**First read.** You land on the page title at top left and the blue button at top right. Then the eye drops into the white cards. After the first screen the navy sidebar, the title-and-button header and the white card panel feel like a template you already know, so you look at the content and not the frame. One screen gets in the way of that: on Invoices (tile 5) the header is smaller and the whole page sits higher, so moving to it feels like the layout slipped.

**What it's for.** As you told me: screens of one invoicing web app for small businesses, judged as a set for consistent elements and shared columns and margins.

**Verdict.** It's a tight, consistent system. Five of the six screens share the same header, card, table and margins almost exactly. Tile 5 (Invoices) breaks it in type size and in vertical position, and it's the screen users will open most.

**What would move it most**
1. **Tile 5's header and card don't follow the program the other five set.** Defect, and a convention break (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; WCAG 3.2.4 Consistent identification).
   - On tiles 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 the page title is the same size and sits at the same height. The subtitle and the primary button share one band, and the first card starts at the same line, about 123 px down in the full-size screens.
   - On tile 5 the title "Invoices" is visibly smaller and set higher. The subtitle, the "New invoice" button (about 42–78 px instead of 47–83) and the card top (about 114 px instead of 123) all move up with it.
   - The card heading "All invoices" is larger than every other card heading ("Recent invoices", "Clients", "Payout history", "Business details"). It now sits almost at the size of its own page title, so the two levels blur into each other (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1).
   - The cost: switching between Overview and Invoices, which show the same table, the header jumps and shrinks. That reads as a different template, and it weakens trust in a money tool.
   - Fix: put tile 5 on the shared page-header component and the shared card heading style. Don't try to match the numbers by hand.

**Smaller things.**
- Every table (tiles 2, 3, 4, 5) and the Top clients list (tile 1) left-aligns money amounts, so the decimals don't line up and comparing sizes takes a read. Align amount columns right, with tabular figures (Bringhurst, 4.4.3).
- The "Revenue by month" bars on tile 1 have no values or scale, so the viewer can see October was the best month but not by how much. Label each bar with its amount (Tufte, ch. 2).
- On tile 2, "Outstanding" and "Overdue" are bare totals with no comparison. "Average days to pay" on tile 1 shows it can be done ("Down from 23 days last quarter"). Add the same kind of comparison to these two (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design").

**What's working.**
- On five screens, the header band (title, gray subtitle, one blue primary button on the right) is identical. It gives every page one obvious action and one place to look for it. Keep it as the single source for tile 5 too.
- Content lines up on one left edge (about 272 px) and one right edge (about 1240 px) on all six screens, with a constant gutter between cards. Even the two-up and three-up card rows on tiles 1, 2 and 4 share those edges.
- Status pills carry a word as well as a color (Paid, Due, Overdue) and look the same on tiles 2 and 5.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: make the page header and the card one locked component each, with no per-page size choices, so a seventh screen can't drift. Taste: for a tool people open daily, sameness is the feature.
2. Let the headline money figure on Overview lead. "Outstanding" and "Paid this month" currently get the same weight. One of them answering the owner's first question ("what am I owed?") would give the dashboard a clear starting point. Taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the nav and buttons, and whether tile 5's longer table keeps the header fixed while scrolling.
