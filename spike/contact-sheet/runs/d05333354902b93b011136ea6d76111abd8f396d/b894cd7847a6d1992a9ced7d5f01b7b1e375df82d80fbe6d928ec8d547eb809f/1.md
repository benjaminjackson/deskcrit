**First read.** The six screens look like one product right away. They share the navy sidebar with the "Ledgerly" wordmark, the gray canvas, white cards, the blue primary button at top right, and a page title with a gray subtitle. Looking across the set, tile 5 (Invoices) stands out. Its title is smaller and sits higher, and its card heading is bigger than the matching heading on every other screen.

**What it's for.** Six screens of a small-business invoicing web app, judged together to see whether they follow one system.

**Verdict.** The system mostly holds. Across all six tiles the sidebar, the content's left edge, the right margin, card padding, table column insets and status pills all match. One screen breaks the pattern: tile 5's page header and card heading use different sizes and positions from the rest.

**What would move it most**
1. **Tile 5's header doesn't match the other screens.** This is a defect. On tiles 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 the page title ("Reports", "Overview", "Clients", "Payments", "Settings") is the same large size and sits about 27 px below the top of the frame, with the button lined up beside it. On tile 5, "Invoices" is visibly smaller and sits about 2 px higher, and the "New invoice" button is about 3 px higher than the buttons on the other screens. Someone moving through the sidebar sees the title shrink and the header jump on this one page, so it feels like a different template. The direction of the fix: use the same page-header component everywhere.
2. **Tile 5's card heading is a size up from the others.** This is a defect. "All invoices" is clearly larger than the matching card headings: "Recent invoices" on tile 2, "Clients" on tile 3, "Payout history" on tile 4 and "Business details" on tile 6. Together with the smaller page title, this squeezes the gap between the two levels on this screen. The card heading starts competing with the page title, which goes against keeping a few type sizes with clear jumps between them (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI). Use the shared card-heading style.

**Smaller things.** None that break the set. Buttons are different widths, but that comes from their labels ("Save changes" against "Add client") and is fine.

**What's working.**
- Every tile shares the same layout. The content's left edge is about 137 px from the frame edge, the cards stop at the same right margin, and table text is inset by the same amount inside its card. That's why the set reads as one product.
- The same kind of element looks the same wherever it appears. The Paid, Due and Overdue pills match on tiles 2 and 5, and the stat cards share a style on tiles 2 and 4 (small label, large amount, small note).

**Where it could go further.** Nothing beyond the findings.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the header difference on tile 5 comes from a separate template (for example, a page that scrolls) or from a stray style override. Check the live page's CSS for the title and heading styles.
