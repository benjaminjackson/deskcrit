**First read.** The eye goes to the dark navy sidebar and then to the bold page title at the top left of each tile. The six tiles read as one calm, conventional SaaS admin app: same sidebar, same title and subtitle pairing, same blue primary button at top right, same white cards on a light gray ground. Scanning across the tiles, tile 2 (Payments) stands out. Its title and cards start noticeably further right than on the other tiles.

**What it's for.** As you told me, these are screens of one invoicing web app for small businesses, judged as a set for consistency of elements and alignment.

**Verdict.** The set is consistent in almost every way, and only tile 2 breaks the pattern. Its content column sits on a different grid from the other five, so moving between Payments and any other page makes the whole page jump.

**What would move it most**
1. **Tile 2's content column is inset more than every other screen (defect).** On tiles 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6, the page title, subtitle and the left edge of the first card start about 20 px right of the sidebar. The primary button and the right edge of the cards stop about 20 px from the right edge. On tile 2, "Payments", "Payouts to your bank", the Next payout card and the Payout history card all start about 36 px from the sidebar. "Request payout" and the right card edges stop about 36 px from the right edge. Everything moves about 16 px inward on both sides. 16 px is too small to look intended and too large to miss, so it reads as a slip, not a choice (Arnheim on placements that are almost aligned). Clicking from Invoices to Payments makes the title and the button jump sideways, and the app feels assembled page by page rather than built from one layout (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one grid governs every page; WCAG 3.2.4 on consistent identification across pages). The fix is to give Payments the same page container and padding the other five screens use. Don't nudge the cards by hand.

**Smaller things.**
- Tiles 1, 2 and 3: the amount columns are left-aligned. Right-align them and use tabular figures so the dollar values line up by place (Bringhurst 4.4.3).
- Tile 1: rows INV-0942 down to INV-0937 repeat the clients, dates and amounts of INV-1042 to INV-1037. This looks like duplicated sample data. Fill it with real rows before the screen is shown.
- Tile 3: Outstanding, Paid this month and Overdue appear as bare figures. Put a comparison next to each, such as the change from last month or the number of invoices (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design").
- Tile 6: the Revenue by month bars have no values and no scale, so August through November can only be compared by length. Label each bar with its amount.
- Tile 4: no field is marked required or optional. Add an asterisk with a short legend, or add "optional" to the optional labels (Budiu, NN/g).
- Tile 4: the input borders look pale against white. Check them against 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
- Tile 1: a list of 21 invoices has no search or status filter. That's the first shortcut a frequent user would reach for (Nielsen heuristic 7).

**What's working.**
- Apart from tile 2, every tile shares one page frame: sidebar, title with a gray subtitle underneath, one blue primary button at top right, and content in white cards. Moving between pages, people always know where the title and the main action are. Keep that template and build every new page from it.
- Each kind of element looks the same everywhere. The status pills (Paid, Due, Overdue) are identical on tiles 1 and 3, the table headers use the same small capitals on every tile, and the active nav item uses the same highlight on all six. The status colors also carry a word, so the meaning doesn't rely on color alone.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Taste: tile 3's three summary cards, Outstanding, Paid this month and Overdue, are all the same size and weight. For a small-business owner opening the app, the number to act on is the money still owed. Making one of these figures clearly lead, and linking it to the invoices behind it, would turn the Overview from a report into a to-do list (Few on dashboards being glanceable).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Target sizes: the sidebar links and the small primary buttons may be close to 24 px tall. Measure them in the live app.
- Focus states, and how the Payments page lays out at other widths. Check in a browser whether the extra inset on tile 2 comes from a different container.
