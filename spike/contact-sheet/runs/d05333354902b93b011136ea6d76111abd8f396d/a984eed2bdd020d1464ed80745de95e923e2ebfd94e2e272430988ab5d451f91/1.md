**First read.** Six screens of one product share a navy sidebar on the left and a light gray work area with white cards. The eye lands on the bold page title, jumps to the blue button at top right, then goes to the table or figures. Tile 4 stands out right away: its top-right button is a green outlined pill, and it's the only one on the sheet. Otherwise this looks like a calm, tidy, plain invoicing tool.

**What it's for.** A small-business owner moves between the screens of an invoicing tool and should feel that each one is the same tool.

**Verdict.** As a set, it holds together well. The sidebar, page header, card style, table style and content margins repeat exactly across all six screens. One tile breaks the pattern: tile 4 (Reports) uses a different button style for its page action.

**What would move it most**
1. **Tile 4's "Export CSV" button breaks the button pattern.** Tiles 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 each have one page action at top right: a filled blue rectangle with white text and small corner radius ("New invoice", "Add client", "Request payout", "Save changes"). Tile 4's action sits in the same spot but is an outlined, fully rounded pill in green. Someone moving through the app learns that "the blue box top right is what I do on this page." On Reports, that cue is gone and a new shape takes its place, so the screen looks like it's from a different kit. The green adds a second problem: everywhere else in the product, green means "Paid" (the status pills on tiles 1 and 2). Using it for an export button borrows a status color for an unrelated job (Apple HIG and Material on semantic color). *Defect* for set consistency (WCAG 3.2.4, consistent identification; Nielsen 4, consistency and standards) and *convention* for the color. Fix: use the same filled blue button as the other five screens. If export is meant to rank below a primary action, use one shared secondary style that will appear on other screens too, in the product's neutral or blue, not green.

**Smaller things.**
- Amount columns on tiles 1, 2 and 5 are left-aligned, so the decimal points zig-zag ("$860.00" under "$1,240.00"). Right-align money columns so amounts can be compared down the column (Bringhurst 4.4.3).
- The tile 2 sample data repeats: rows INV-0942 to INV-0937 copy the clients, amounts and dates of INV-1042 to INV-1036. That puts "Nov 18" on an older invoice number than "Oct 20" and makes the list look out of order. Use believable data that runs in sequence before showing the set.
- The tile 4 "Revenue by month" bars have no amounts or scale, so a viewer can see that October was the best month but not by how much. Put the value at the end of each bar (Tufte; Few).
- The three figures on tile 1 (Outstanding, Paid this month, Overdue) have no comparison, while tile 4's "18 days" has one ("Down from 23 days last quarter"). Add the earlier period or a change next to each (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design").
- The tile 2 list (21 invoices, and it scrolls) has no search or filter by client or status. Even a status filter would speed up the most common lookup.

**What's working.**
- **The shell is truly fixed.** The sidebar width, wordmark, nav spacing and active-item highlight are the same on every tile. The content column starts and ends at the same x on all six (title, cards and button right edge line up). Keep this exactly as it is. It's why the set reads as one product.
- **One header pattern everywhere:** a bold title, a one-line gray subtitle that says something real ("21 invoices this quarter", "Payouts to your bank"), and the action at the right. This makes each screen quick to read.
- **Tables share one style** across tiles 1, 2, 3 and 5: small capital column headers, thin row rules, the same row height and the same inset inside the card. Status pills pair color with a word, so the color never carries the meaning alone.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: make the page-action slot a rule of the system, with one filled blue primary per screen and one shared secondary style. Then every future screen (Reports, exports, filters) has a ready answer, and the blue keeps meaning "do the thing here." *Taste*, following Gerstner's idea of a design as a program rather than one-off layouts.
2. Let the money lead. Overview (tile 1) and Payments (tile 5) open with large figures, but Invoices and Clients open with tables at the same weight as everything else. If the history column on tile 3 or a total on tile 2 got the same large-figure card, every screen would start with the number the owner came for. *Taste*. Which figure belongs on each screen is the maker's call.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Target sizes: the sidebar items and buttons look short at this contact-sheet scale. Check them at 1× in a live browser against the 24 px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8).
- Focus states on the nav and the tile 6 inputs.
