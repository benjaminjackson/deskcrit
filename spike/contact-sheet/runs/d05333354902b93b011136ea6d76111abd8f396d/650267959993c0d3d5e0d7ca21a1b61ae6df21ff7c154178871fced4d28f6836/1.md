**First read.** The six screens read as one product right away: the same navy sidebar on the left, a large title with a gray subtitle, one blue button at top right, and white cards on a pale gray background. Across the set, the eye catches one screen sitting differently. On tile 2 (Payments), the title and cards start further right and stop short of the right edge, so that screen looks narrower than the rest. It feels calm, tidy and generic.

**What it's for.** As you described it: an invoicing tool for small businesses, judged here on whether the screens look and line up the same across the set.

**Verdict.** The set is consistent except for tile 2. The sidebar, nav, active state, title block, primary button, cards, table headers and status pills all look the same on every screen. Tile 2 alone uses a narrower content column, so its title, button and cards jump inward when you switch to Payments.

**What would move it most**
1. **Tile 2 is off the shared column.** On tiles 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 the title, cards and tables run from about x=272 to x=1240 in the full-size screens. On tile 2 the same block runs from about 304 to 1208: about 32 px more inset on each side. The title "Payments", the "Request payout" button and both card rows all move together. Going from Invoices to Payments, everything shifts inward and back again, so this one page looks like it came from a different build. Because every other screen agrees, users will notice this break. It's a defect against the set's own system: one grid for every page (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*), and consistent placement of repeated parts (Nielsen 4, consistency and standards). Fix: put tile 2 on the same content container as the other screens, with the same left and right margins.

**Smaller things.**
- Tile 1, invoice list: rows INV-0942 to INV-0937 repeat the clients, dates and amounts of INV-1042 to INV-1037, and they appear after Oct 20 with Nov dates, out of order. It looks like filler data. Replace it with real-looking sample rows before showing this (common sense).
- Amount columns (tiles 1, 2, 3) are left-aligned. Set them flush right in tabular figures so the dollar values line up by place (Bringhurst 4.4.3).
- Tile 6, "Revenue by month": the bars show no values or scale, so a reader can't tell what August's bar means. Put the amount at the end of each bar (Tufte; Few).
- Tile 6, "Revenue by month": the title only names the chart. Say the takeaway the bars already show.
- Tiles 2 and 3 headline figures ($2,980.00, $4,525.00, $12,870.00, $515.00) stand alone, while tile 6's "18 days" has a comparison ("Down from 23 days last quarter"). Give the others a comparison too, such as the previous period (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design").
- Tile 4, form inputs: the field borders look very pale on white. Measure them against 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11) and darken them if they fall short.

**What's working.**
- The page header is one fixed pattern on every screen: title, gray subtitle that says something useful ("21 invoices this quarter", "Q4 so far"), and a single primary action at top right. Keep it as the template.
- Tables share one style everywhere: small-caps gray column headers, light row rules, the same row height and padding. Clients, Invoices and Payout history all read as the same component.
- The status pills on tiles 1 and 3 pair color with a word (Paid, Due, Overdue), so they work without color vision.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on the comparison line in Smaller things: tile 6's "18 days / Down from 23 days last quarter" is the only number on the six screens that tells the owner whether things are getting better. Taste: a small-business owner comes here to learn "am I getting paid faster?", so making every headline figure carry that before-and-after would make the product feel like it answers questions, not just stores records.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav, buttons and fields.
