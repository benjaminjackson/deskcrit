**New findings**
1. **The headline's line break splits the number from its unit.** The headline at top left (about x=160–750, y=130–240) breaks as "Cross the bay in twelve / calm minutes". The first line ends on a bare "twelve", so on first scan the eye reads "twelve" with no unit and only gets "minutes" after dropping to the next line. That weakens the page's strongest fact, which the crit wants to lead. Bringhurst (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.4.6) says short number expressions should be kept together. The fix is to break by phrase, as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes", using a non-breaking space or a set break so "twelve calm minutes" always stays on one line. That also puts the selling phrase whole on the second line. Convention.
