**New findings**
1. **The full-strength blue fills about half the first screen, so the color can't mark anything** (convention). The hero band runs edge to edge from about y=62 to 462 in fully saturated electric blue. That's roughly half the viewport, and it's the loudest thing on the page. Because of that, the primary button has to be white to stand out. Your one strong brand color ends up as wallpaper instead of the thing that points to the action, and a large field of this intensity tires the eye (Itten, *The Art of Color*, contrast of saturation; Schoger and Wathan, *Refactoring UI*: saturated color marks what matters, large areas use a tint or shade). Fix: fill the band with a deep shade or a light tint of the same blue, and save the full-strength blue for small things like "Try it free". This also gives the product image the crit asks for a calmer ground to sit on.
2. **The feature row is jammed up against the hero** (taste). The feature headings start only about 35 px below the blue band, and the row is only about 64 px wide margins from the page edges. That's less space than the hero leaves between its buttons and its own bottom edge (about 58 px). After the hero's generous padding, the three columns look squeezed in under it rather than starting a section of their own. They read as a footnote to the hero, which undercuts the "Read-only access" reassurance down there. Fix: give the feature row clearly more space above it than any gap inside the hero, roughly the hero's own top padding, so it reads as its own group (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on grouping by proximity).
