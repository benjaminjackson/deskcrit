**New findings**
1. **The headings are in link blue, so they look clickable.** This covers the title "Planning a vegetable bed" and the three subheads at left (y ≈ 240, 368, 494). They are bold and in the same saturated blue that the web uses for links. There are no real links on the page to compare them with, so a reader may well try to tap "Preparing the soil" or "Watering," expecting it to open a section or another page. On a phone, someone with dirty hands who taps and gets nothing loses a moment of trust. The crit's note on the blue is about mood (generic, not gardening). This one is about what the color seems to promise the reader can do. Direction: if you keep a colored accent on the headings, choose a hue far enough from link blue that it can't be mistaken for a link. That way, real links added later still stand out. *Convention* (mild).
2. **Copy, the site step: the size of the bed is never given.** This is in the paragraph under the title, at y ≈ 155–185. "Mark the corners with sticks and a length of string" tells a first-time gardener how to mark out the bed, but not how big to make it. That's the one decision they need before they push the first stick in. It also affects everything after it: how much to dig, how much compost, how many short rows. Direction: add a size or a rule of thumb for size, such as a width you can reach across without stepping on the soil. The actual figure is the maker's to supply. *Taste.*
