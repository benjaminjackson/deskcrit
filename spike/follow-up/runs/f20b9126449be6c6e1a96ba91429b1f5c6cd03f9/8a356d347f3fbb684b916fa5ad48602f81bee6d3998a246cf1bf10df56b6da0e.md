**New findings**
1. **The route only runs one way on the page.** In the first paragraph (about y=187–241), "runs from the North Landing to the Market Quay every forty minutes" never says the boat comes back. A rider can't tell whether there's a return crossing from Market Quay, or whether "every forty minutes" means a boat leaves each landing every forty minutes or the round trip takes forty minutes. Anyone planning a trip there and back needs this before anything else. The page doesn't answer it, and the later mention of "each landing" only suggests it. State the direction or directions the service runs and how often a boat leaves each side. Only the maker knows those facts. *Defect.*
2. **The fare is hinted at but never given.** In the second paragraph (about y=288), "at no extra charge" tells cyclists the bike costs nothing on top of a fare, but nothing on the page says what that fare is or how to pay it. The only money line answers a question about the add-on and skips the main one. Readers planning a crossing find out the cost, and whether they need cash or a ticket in advance, only at the landing. Add a short line with the fare and how to pay, using facts the maker has, or link to where they're published. *Defect.*
3. **"In calm weather" raises a question it doesn't answer.** At the end of the first paragraph (about y=241), the crossing time is qualified by the weather. That tells the reader rough weather changes things, but not whether it means a slower crossing, a reduced timetable, or cancellations, or where to check on the day. A commuter who reads this has a new worry and nowhere to resolve it. Either say what happens in bad weather and where service changes are announced, or drop the qualifier if it only means a few minutes' delay. *Taste, close to a defect if crossings really do get cancelled.*
