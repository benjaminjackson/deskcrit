**New findings**
1. **The subtitle breaks in the middle of its best phrase.** *(Convention.)* In the gray subtitle under the headline (around y=205–240), the line ends at "in the water" and leaves "beside you." alone on a second line. That orphan of two words splits "with a coach in the water beside you", the clause the page most needs a nervous adult to take in. As a result the reassurance lands as an afterthought, and the short ragged second line makes the block look unfinished. Bringhurst says to break lines so that phrases stay whole and to avoid runts at the end of a paragraph. The fix is to set the subtitle measure so the clause stays on one line. You can widen the line slightly, tighten the copy, or force the break before "with a coach" so the reassurance gets a line of its own.
