**New findings**
1. **The only accent color is spent on a label that says nothing.** In the top-left, the blue "New for spring" eyebrow is the one spot of color in the frame, so the eye goes to it, and all it gets is a season. It doesn't name a category, a reason to care, or anything a shopper can act on, and it will be out of date in a few months. Either use that slot and that color for something that helps the pitch (what kind of product this is, or a launch fact only the maker can supply), or make it quieter so it stops competing with the headline. *Taste.*
2. **The subhead's first line ends on an orphaned "a".** In the gray subhead at about y≈360, line 1 ends "opens flat like a" and "suitcase" starts line 2. That splits the phrase that carries the bag's most distinctive claim, so the reader stumbles on it. Break the line before "like a suitcase" or tighten the measure so the phrase stays on one line. This is the subhead, not the headline break the crit already flagged. *Convention.*
3. **"A 22 litre bag" is missing its hyphen.** In the subhead, a number and unit placed before a noun act as a compound modifier, so it should read "22-litre bag" (or "22-liter" if the site uses US spelling). Without the hyphen it reads as sloppy copy on the page's most factual line. *Convention.*
