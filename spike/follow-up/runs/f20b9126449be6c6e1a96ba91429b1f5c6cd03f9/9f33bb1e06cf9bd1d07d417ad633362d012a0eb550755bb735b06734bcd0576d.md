**New findings**
1. **The contact page gives no way to reach the studio except the form.** The whole page has no email address, phone number or studio location: not in the left column under the intro, not in the footer (which has only Privacy, Terms and Careers). Some people would rather write from their own inbox, want to attach a brief or RFP, or want a name to address. On a studio's site, a page with only a form can also feel like a gate rather than a door, and visitors can't tell whether a person or a queue gets the message. This is a **convention**, since a contact page usually offers a direct channel as well as the form. The fix is to add the studio's real contact details, at least an email address and whatever else the maker wants to publish, in the left column under the reply-time line. That way the visitor can choose the form or their own email.
2. **The form asks for an email address and says nothing about what happens to it.** There's no line near the "Email address" field or the Send button saying the address is used only to reply. The only privacy cue is a small "Privacy" link in the bottom-left footer, far from the form. Someone cautious about sharing an email address with a studio they haven't hired yet has nothing reassuring at the point where they decide. This is **taste**: it's a small cost on a low-stakes form. The direction is one short line of light text under the button or the field saying how the address will be used, worded to match the studio's actual policy, and linking to the privacy page if needed.
