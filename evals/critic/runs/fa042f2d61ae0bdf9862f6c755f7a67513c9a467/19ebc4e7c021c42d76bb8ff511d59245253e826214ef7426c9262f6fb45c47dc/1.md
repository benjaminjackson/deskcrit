I've finished the checklist and the deep read, and I'm writing up the critique now.

**First read.** My eye goes to the red tile at top left, then moves across six coloured blocks that all weigh the same, then drops to a grey line at the bottom saying there is "a large feature tile." There isn't one. It reads as a garden-show gallery template where the photos haven't gone in yet: tidy, cheerful, and empty. The footer line makes it feel like the page describes some other design.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming a web gallery page that previews six areas of a garden show so visitors can see what's there and choose what to look at.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. A gallery is its pictures, and here every picture is a flat colour block with a pale circle, so a visitor learns nothing from what they see. Fix that first. Then give the page the focal point its own caption claims it has.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures show nothing.** All six tiles are a solid colour with a centred translucent circle. A rose arch looks the same as a pond edge apart from its colour. The visitor has only the captions to go on, so the gallery is really a list of six labels. This is a defect, judged by common sense (no catalogue rule covers placeholders). If these are images that failed to load, check the live page. If the circles are the intended art, they need to become real photos or illustrations of each area. Every other judgement about this page depends on what goes in these boxes.
2. **Nothing leads, and the copy says something does.** The six tiles are the same size, colour strength and spacing, so no tile is seen first and the eye scans them like a table. The footer line "The large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point" describes a tile the page doesn't have. A reader either goes looking for it or concludes the page is unfinished. This is a convention (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size) and a copy defect. Make one area clearly the first thing seen, such as the show's headline garden, by letting its tile span two columns or the full width, and set the other five back. If you don't want a feature, delete the footer line. Either way, it should not stay as it is.

**Smaller things.**
- Rose arch caption block (top left): its title sits about 2 px higher than Herb bed and Pond edge, and the gap between its title and description is tighter. The baselines across row one don't line up, and one card looks like it came from a different build. Give all six cards the same caption spacing (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- Circles in every tile: they sit exactly on the tile's bottom edge, so the shape just touches the frame. That looks like an accident, not a crop. Either crop it decisively or keep it clear of the edge (common sense). This goes away once real images replace them.
- Six fully saturated hues cover about half the page, and the colours carry no meaning. Nothing stands out because everything is loud (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). If colour coding stays alongside real photos, reduce it to a thin tag or a muted tint.

**What's working.**
- The type is clean and has clear levels. The page title, the bold card titles and the grey descriptions are told apart by weight and colour as well as size. Keep that three-level system.
- The descriptions are concrete and specific: "Tomatoes in late July," "Climbing roses over the east gate." They tell a visitor what they'll actually see and when. Keep writing at that level.
- The three-column grid, the shared left edge and the generous gap between rows make each row read as a band. That's good scaffolding for the real images.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: let the descriptions do more than label. Most already give a place or a time. If each one gave its location ("over the east gate") and its best moment ("late July"), the gallery would double as a plan for the visit, which is what a show visitor needs. Taste.
2. Let the page title "Garden show gallery" name this show and this year. Right now it could sit on any garden site, so a visitor can't tell which show they're looking at. Taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are images that failed to load or the intended design. Check the live page.
- Whether the tiles are links, and what clicking one opens.
