**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the grey subhead, and stops on the dark teal "Get started" button. Then there's nowhere left to go. The whole right half of the frame and the bottom third are bare cream, so it reads as a hero section with its picture missing. It feels calm, tidy and anonymous: a startup landing page that could be selling anything, and nothing about it says travel.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the home page hero for Wayfold, a group-trip planning app. It should make a visitor who is organising a trip with friends get the idea and start.

**Verdict.** The type and the button styling are competent, but the page doesn't do its job. Nothing shows the product or a trip, so half the screen is empty and the promise rests on one generic headline. The missing visual is the single biggest problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half is empty: no product, no trip, nothing to see** (defect). From about x=700 to the right edge, and across the whole band below the buttons, there's nothing. This one gap causes several problems. All the weight sits top-left, so the page tips (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). The empty space doesn't read as a planned pause set against something dense; it reads as space nobody filled (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). And "one place" is a claim the visitor has to take on trust, because they never see that place. If an image or app screenshot was meant to load there, this capture didn't get it; check it in a live browser, and if it loads, most of this finding goes away. Otherwise, fill the right column with the thing the headline promises: one shared trip that already holds flights, a stay and a day plan, ideally with a few friends' votes or comments on it. The picture should prove the "one place" idea, not just decorate it.
2. **The copy could be on anyone's page, and the ask doesn't say what happens** (defect, judged on the copy). "Plan the whole trip in one place" says what every travel or itinerary app says, so a visitor can't tell what's different here. The real difference is in the subhead: it's for a *group* to *agree* without "a dozen chat threads". That's the sharp idea, and it's buried in 18 px grey text. "Get started" doesn't name a first step. "Start a trip with your group" or "Invite your friends to a trip" would tell the visitor exactly what they're committing to. The page also never sets Wayfold next to what the visitor uses now. The group chat is already named, so use it: put "Your group chat vs. one Wayfold trip" in the visual or the headline. Then visitors can judge the value against something they already know, instead of taking the claim on trust.
3. **The eye path ends at the buttons** (defect). After "See pricing", nothing on screen leads anywhere: no next section, no hint of more below, about 300 px of blank cream. A visitor who isn't ready to sign up has nothing to read next. Bring the next section (how it works, or a group's trip) far enough up that its top shows in the first screen, or let the visual from finding 1 run down into that space.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline break: "in" is left hanging at the end of line 1 ("…trip in / one place"). Break as "Plan the whole trip / in one place" so each line is one phrase. This is common sense; the source for heading breaks is unconfirmed.

**What's working.**
- The order of importance is clear. One large, heavy, near-black headline leads, the subhead is plainly secondary in size and grey, and the solid teal "Get started" outranks the outlined "See pricing". Keep that ranking when you add a visual, and don't let the image overpower the headline.
- The type is restrained: one sans family, a few clearly different sizes, display type set tight, and a subhead of about 60 characters per line. Everything shares the same 80 px left edge as the logo.
- The palette (cream, deep teal, near-black) is small and consistent. The teal carries the brand from the logo to the primary button.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: make "without a dozen chat threads" the headline's idea, not the subhead's. Group agreement is the one thing a solo itinerary app can't claim, and leading with it tells the right visitor (the one organising friends) that this page is for them. Taste.
2. Let the deep teal already used in the logo and button mark the group's decisions in the visual, such as the stay everyone voted for. Then the one brand colour also carries the product's point: this is where the group agrees. Taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image or animation should have loaded in the right half. Check in a live browser.
- Focus indicators on the nav links and buttons, and how the two-column layout reflows on a phone.
