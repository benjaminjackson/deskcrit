**First read.** The eye lands on the H1, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then drops straight into the body text. Within a second it reads as a standard SaaS help-centre article: calm, tidy and trustworthy. Then comes a small letdown. The page promises a task, and you find three paragraphs of rules and no steps.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help article for a billing owner or admin who wants to swap the card on a Ledgerly subscription now and know what happens afterwards.

**Verdict.** The typography is competent and calm, but the page doesn't do its job. It's titled as a how-to, yet it never tells the reader how to change the card or takes them to the place where they can. The most important line, about what happens when a card is declined, can be read two ways on the one point people will act on: when they lose their plan.

**What would move it most**
1. **The task is missing (defect).** The H1 promises "Update the card", but the body only says this can be done "from the billing page". That phrase isn't a link, and there are no steps: no menu path, no button name, no sign of what the screen looks like. Meanwhile three other phrases on the page are links. A reader who came to do one thing has to go and find the billing page on their own, which is the exact hunt a help article exists to prevent. This is common sense; the related principle in the catalog, Tognazzini's "anticipation", is off the approved list. **Fix:** make "billing page" a direct link, or add a short prominent "Go to Billing" action under the title, and add a short numbered list of steps (where to click, what the button is called) before the explanation. Then move the rules about open invoices and roles below the steps.
2. **The decline timeline can be read two ways (defect).** Under "When the new card is declined" the text says "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days… If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan."
   - Reading A: the "second attempt" is the day-7 retry, so the downgrade happens on day 7.
   - Reading B: the first charge counts as attempt one, so the "second attempt" is the day-3 retry and the downgrade happens on day 3.

   "After seven days" is also unclear: seven days after the decline, or after the first retry? This is a deadline, and readers will hold Ledgerly to it. A reader who takes the wrong reading loses their plan four days earlier than they expected. **Fix:** give the clock in days from the first decline ("on day 3 and day 7 after the first decline; if the day-7 retry fails, your workspace moves to the free plan"). Also say in one clause what the free plan takes away from them.
3. **The open-invoice rule stops at the fact (defect, copy).** "Any invoice that is already open keeps the old card until it is paid." People usually change cards because the old one expired or was declined, so the reader immediately wonders how that open invoice will ever get paid. The line stops at how the system behaves and leaves the reader to work out the consequence. **Fix:** finish the sentence with what it means for them and what they can do, for example: "if your old card no longer works, pay the open invoice from billing history with the new card."
4. **Nothing shows where you are (convention).** In the left nav, "Update a payment card" is drawn exactly like its four siblings, and "Billing" in the top nav isn't marked either. Someone who arrived from search can't see their place in the section (Nielsen heuristic 1; Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist", guideline 5). **Fix:** mark the current item in both menus with one clear cue: a bolder weight, a side bar or a background.

**Smaller things.**
- Body lines look like about 80–84 characters (for example, the first line, "You can replace … The new card is"). That's over Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Narrow the text column by about 60–80 px. There's plenty of empty space on the right to absorb it.
- There's no search box in the header. On a help centre, search is the main way people get in and the quickest way to get unstuck (Nielsen heuristic 7). Add search to the top bar.
- The names don't line up. The breadcrumb says "Billing / Payment methods", but the sidebar has no "Payment methods" group and calls the page "Update a payment card", while the H1 says "the card that pays for your subscription". That's three names for one place (Norman, conceptual model). Pick one name and use it in all three.
- Paragraph 2 uses "instead" twice in one sentence ("instead of by card… paying by invoice instead"). Cut the second one.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is disciplined: one sans family, a bold H1, a clearly smaller bold H2, and regular body text. Every level differs in both size and weight, so the structure reads at a glance. Keep it that way.
- Links are blue and underlined, and their text says where they go ("roles and permissions", "paying by invoice"). Don't drop the underline when you revise.
- The H2 sits much closer to its own paragraph than to the one above, and the sidebar's first line lines up with the breadcrumb. The quiet grid is what makes the page feel trustworthy.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2. Turn "When the new card is declined" into a small dated timeline: day 0 declined, day 3 retry plus email, day 7 retry plus email, then the free plan. The reader is anxious about a deadline, and a laid-out sequence answers "how long do I have?" faster than any sentence can. This is taste, but for a billing owner under pressure it's the one piece of information worth giving a shape.
2. Use the empty right column for a short "Before you change it" box holding the two conditions the body currently buries: you need the owner or billing role, and bank-transfer customers should use a different page. Readers who can't do the task find out right away instead of after reading two paragraphs. Taste; it keeps the main column about the task itself.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus styles on the nav and inline links, and how the sidebar reflows on a phone. The sidebar is a primary way around the section, so check both on the live page.
