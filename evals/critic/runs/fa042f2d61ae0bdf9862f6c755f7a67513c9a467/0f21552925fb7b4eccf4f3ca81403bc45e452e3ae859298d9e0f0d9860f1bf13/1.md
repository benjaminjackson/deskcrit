**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the subhead, then to the dark "Get started" button. Then it hits "Start now", which says the same thing, and the page runs out: the right half and the bottom third are blank beige. It reads as a calm, tidy, but generic SaaS hero for group-trip planning, with nothing to look at but words.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing-page hero for Wayfold, a group-trip planner. It has to make someone who organises trips with friends see the product and start using it.

**Verdict.** The type and the copy are solid, but the hero doesn't show the product. Half the screen is empty where the proof should be, and the call to action is split across two buttons that say the same thing. As it stands it asks for trust and gives nothing to see.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing shows the product, and the right half is a hole** (defect). The headline promises "the whole trip in one place", but nothing shows that place. There is no itinerary, no shared plan, no group view. The right half of the screen (about x 720–1200) and everything below y 500 are empty. Two things follow. First, the visitor has to take the claim on trust, because they can't see flights, stays and day plans sitting together and judge whether it beats their own group chat. Second, all the visual weight sits in the top-left, so the page tips left and the eye has nowhere to go after the buttons (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). This empty space isn't playing off anything dense; it's what's left over. Fix: put a real product view in the right column, such as one trip with a flight, a stay and a day plan, plus signs that several people are in it (avatars, a vote, a comment). Then the picture shows what the headline says. A view set against something the visitor knows, like a messy chat thread next to the tidy Wayfold plan, would let them judge the value on their own terms.
2. **Two buttons, one action** (defect). "Get started" and "Start now" are synonyms placed side by side. The visitor stops to work out the difference, finds none, and the outlined twin weakens the filled primary (Nielsen heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Each label also has to be clear on its own (WCAG 2.4.4), and these two can't be told apart. Fix: keep one button for the action and either remove the twin or give it a different job, such as "See how it works" or "See pricing". While you're there, make the primary button specific. "Plan a trip with your group" names something the visitor actually does. "Get started" could sit on anyone's page.

**Smaller things.** None beyond the two findings.

**What's working.**
- The subhead does real work. It names what is held together (flights, stays, day plans) and who it's for (a group), and it ends on a pain the reader will recognise ("without a dozen chat threads"). It runs about 70 characters a line at comfortable leading. Keep it.
- The type hierarchy is clean. There's one heavy, tightly set headline, a clearly lighter grey subhead, and small nav. Three levels with big steps and no sizes that nearly collide.
- The palette is restrained: one deep teal on a warm off-white with near-black text. It feels calm and unhurried, which suits travel planning, and the teal clearly belongs to the action.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: make the product view tell the subhead's story. Show the "dozen chat threads" collapsing into one Wayfold trip, so the problem and the fix share the hero. The page exists to make a group organiser switch, and showing their current mess next to the cure persuades better than a claim (taste).
2. Let the teal own the decision moment. It's already the brand and CTA colour, so use it only on the call to action and on the single "agreed" state in the product view (for example, the plan the group voted for). Then the colour means "the group decided", which is the outcome Wayfold sells (taste).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav links and buttons. Also check that the empty lower third isn't content that never loaded or was caught mid-animation; check that in a live browser before acting on finding 1's layout point.
