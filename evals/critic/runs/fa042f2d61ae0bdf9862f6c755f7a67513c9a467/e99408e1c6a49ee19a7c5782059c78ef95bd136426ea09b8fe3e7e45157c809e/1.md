**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title and then on the black grid of 2 px rules, which is the heaviest thing on the page. The times are in a column that starts a third of the way across, set in the same weight as everything else. It reads at once as a plain, competent ferry timetable for local commuters, but it feels like a form to fill in more than a schedule to glance at.

**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is a web or print timetable that lets a rider standing at North landing find the next sailing that runs today, quickly.

**Verdict.** It's legible and correct, but it doesn't help the rider with the one question they bring: "which boat can I catch today?" The days each boat runs are pushed to the last column in the same weight as "Daily", and heavy cell borders take the attention that the times should get.

**What would move it most**
1. **The exceptions are hidden in the "Runs" column** (defect). Four sailings run daily, but 12:40 and 17:10 run on weekdays only and 19:30 runs on weekends only. That information sits at the far right in the same regular weight as "Daily". A Saturday rider has to read across every row to learn that there's no 12:40 or 17:10. A Tuesday rider may not notice that the last boat is 17:10 and not 19:30. Those are the mistakes that strand people. Fix: make the exceptions stand out from the default. One way is to mark only the exceptions (leave "Daily" out and footnote it as the default, then set "Weekdays only" and "Weekends only" in bold). Another is to split the table into a weekday column and a weekend column, so each rider reads only their own day.
2. **The grid is heavier than the data** (convention, Tufte's data-ink; Lupton, *Thinking with Type*). Every cell is boxed in near-black rules about 2 px thick. That's the top of the range for this defect, and it makes the grid the darkest thing on the page after the title. Lupton's line fits exactly: rules should "illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell." Fix: remove the vertical rules and make the horizontal ones thin and pale grey, or replace them with a pale tone on alternate rows. The times then become the darkest marks on the page.
3. **"Spring season" has no dates** (defect, judged from the copy). The subtitle is the only clue to when this timetable applies, and a rider can't check it against today's date. Is it March to May? Until the clocks change? From Easter? A schedule people act on needs a valid-from and valid-to line. Put the exact dates in the subtitle.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Period" column (Morning, Morning, Midday…) repeats what the times already say, and it takes the first column, where the eye starts. Drop it, or make it a light group label spanning its rows, so "Leaves north" becomes the first column.
- The table is stretched to the full content width, which leaves about 300 px between departure and arrival. A narrower table keeps each row together. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- The copy is plain and specific. "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" name exactly what happens. Keep the verbs.
- The type scale is clean: a bold title, a grey subtitle, bold column heads and regular body text, with no near-duplicate sizes. Hierarchy comes from weight, not extra sizes.
- Times are in 24-hour format with leading zeros (06:40), so the column scans cleanly.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: make "Leaves north" the column that leads, in a larger or bolder setting than the rest. The departure time is the one thing a rider at the landing acts on, so it should be the first thing they see. Arrival can drop back to grey, since every crossing takes the same 12 minutes. That fact could become a single line ("Crossing takes 12 minutes") instead of a full column. This is taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the return direction (market quay to North landing) is on another page or tab. The title "River ferry timetable" promises both directions, and only one is shown here.
- Whether the times use tabular figures. "11:00" looks slightly narrower than "06:40", but I can't confirm that at this resolution. Check the font's tabular-figures setting in the live page.
