**First read.** The eye goes to "River ferry timetable", drops to the bold header row, then runs down the Period column: Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday. It reads as a clean, calm, generic web table. It's easy to scan down, but reading across a row takes work, because each row is a long walk from left to right.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a public timetable page. A rider should be able to find the next sailing from North landing, and know whether it runs on the day they're travelling, in a few seconds.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and legible, and it's about halfway to doing its job. The layout treats all four columns as equally important and stretches them across the full width. As a result, the two things a rider has to put together, the departure time and the days it runs, sit about 600 px apart. The exceptions that could strand someone look exactly like "Daily".

**What would move it most**
1. **The table is stretched until its rows come apart** (defect). The four columns are spread over about 1080 px, with gaps of 240–300 px between them, while each row is only about 57 px tall. The space between columns is several times the space between rows, so the eye reads columns, not sailings. To find out whether the 12:40 runs on Saturday, you have to track from x≈360 across to x≈950 along a pale rule. Proximity is supposed to show what belongs together (Arnheim, on Wertheimer's rule of proximity), and here it shows the opposite. Fix: size the table to its content, about 500–600 px, or tighten the column gaps so each row reads as one unit. Put the days next to the departure time.
2. **The exceptions are hidden among the rules** (defect). "Weekdays" (12:40, 17:10) and "Weekends" (19:30) have the same weight, colour and position as the four "Daily" rows. A weekend rider who glances down the times will plan around 12:40 or 17:10, and those boats won't come. The exceptions are the only cells in that column that carry news. Fix: make the exceptions stand out, for example a muted "Daily" with the exceptions in a darker or bolder setting, or mark the exceptional times themselves with a short note. That way a weekend rider can rule out the 12:40 and 17:10 at a glance.
3. **The first column is the one nobody needs** (taste, argued as common sense). "Period" takes the left-most column, where scanning starts, and repeats "Morning" and "Midday" row after row. Anyone can tell 06:40 is morning. It pushes "Leaves north", the column riders actually scan, into second place. Fix: either drop the column and lead with the departure time, or turn the periods into small group headings with extra space between groups. The second option also breaks the seven identical rows into a rhythm and gets rid of the repeats.
4. **The copy doesn't say when this timetable is valid** (defect, since riders plan on it). "Spring season" gives no dates, so a reader can't tell whether this applies today or what happens on public holidays. The column labels "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" also cut the place names the subtitle spells out, so the reader has to work out the stops. Fix: put the date range in the subtitle ("Runs 21 March – 20 June"). Say whether holidays count as weekends, and use the full stop names in the column headings. If there's a return direction, say where to find it.

**Smaller things.**
- The times look like they use proportional figures: "11:00" sits visibly narrower than "06:40", so the right edges of the time columns are ragged. Switch on tabular figures, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` (Bringhurst 4.4.3). The figures are too fine to tell apart for certain from a screenshot, so check this in the font settings.

**What's working.**
- **The row rules.** They're pale and thin, clearly lighter than the text, so they help the eye follow a row without competing with the data. Keep them that faint (Wilke, Tufte on data-ink).
- **The clear title level.** One heavy title, a grey subtitle, and a bold header row in the same family. That's three distinct levels without any extra sizes. Keep that restraint when you add dates and notes.
- **24-hour times with arrival next to departure.** Every row gives the 12-minute trip without anyone having to do arithmetic.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: make the "Runs" information the one place the page uses colour or weight. The page currently has no accent at all, so a single treatment for "not every day" would become the thing riders learn to look for. This is taste, but it serves a timetable's real job, which is to stop wasted trips.
2. Let the next departure lead. If this is a live web page, a line above the table such as "Next boat: 15:20, arriving 15:32" would answer most visitors' only question before they reach the table. This is taste; the table then becomes the reference rather than the answer.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the table behaves on a phone. At this width, four spread-out columns may squeeze or scroll sideways, and the first column is the one to lose.
