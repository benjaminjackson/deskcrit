**First read.** My eye lands on the first line at top left and simply starts reading. Nothing pulls it anywhere. There are four short headings and four answers, all in the same dark grey sans at nearly the same size. You can only tell question from answer by reading them. It reads as a bakery's pickup FAQ. It feels calm and friendly, but it's as flat as a plain text file.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a help or FAQ section for a bakery's click-and-collect orders, where a customer arrives with one question and needs to find its answer at a glance.

**Verdict.** It doesn't yet do its job as a scannable FAQ. The headings are set like the body text, so someone looking for "cancel" has to read every line. The copy is warm and clear, but it leaves the two rules that people will act on (the cancellation cutoff and what to show at the counter) open to more than one reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings aren't headings.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two look to be about 18 px regular, in nearly the same colour as the answers below them, which look to be about 19 px regular. The question is actually a touch smaller than its answer. Two levels within a pixel of each other look like a mistake, not a choice. The only cue to the structure is the gap between groups, so the page reads as eight similar lines instead of four questions. A customer who came about one thing can't skim to it, and the page has nowhere for the eye to start (no title either). This is a **defect** and breaks a **convention**: when two things are meant to differ, the difference has to be obvious at a glance in size, weight, colour or form (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). One step of difference reads as an accident, and the order of importance should be plain (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). Fix: make each heading differ from its answer in a way you see at once. Semibold or bold at the same size is usually enough here ("bolder, not bigger", Refactoring UI). You could also drop the answers to a slightly lighter grey. If this stands alone as a page, add a title above the four.
2. **The rules people will act on can be read two ways.** These are **copy defects**, and they cost money or a wasted trip.
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot or any time on the day before. A customer who cancels the evening before an 8 am pickup may think they're in time and still be charged. State the cutoff as a time ("until 24 hours before your pickup time", or "until 6 pm the day before"), and say what happens after it. Does the customer still pay?
   - "show it at the counter along with a name" could mean their own name or yours. A friend who gives the wrong one gets turned away. Say "along with your name" (or "the name on the order").
   - "holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" doesn't say what happens next. Add a few words so the customer learns the consequence: "after that, the bread goes back on sale".
3. **The allergy answer tells someone ordering online to go to the till.** "Every loaf has a card by the till" helps only someone standing in the shop. The person reading this page is ordering ahead and wants to know before they pay. For an allergy question, that gap matters more than any type choice. This is a **defect** (content). If the ingredients are listed on the product pages, point there. If they aren't, say how to ask before ordering.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: "Give the person your order number…" and "Orders can be cancelled…" run to roughly 105–110 characters, well past Bringhurst's 45–75. The block is about 820 px wide. Narrow it to about 600–650 px at this size.
- Runt: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergy answer. The narrower measure will likely fix it. Otherwise rebreak the line.

**What's working.**
- The voice. It's plain, second person and specific ("the bread has already been baked for you" gives the reason, not just the rule). Keep it when you tighten the wording.
- Spacing groups each question with its answer. The gap between heading and answer (about 10 px) is clearly smaller than the gap between groups (about 35 px), so once the headings carry weight, the structure will read immediately.
- One typeface, one colour and a single left edge. The restraint suits a small bakery. The fix is more contrast within that system, not more elements.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1. Lead with the cancellation cutoff. It's the one number that costs the customer money, so give it its own line in bold rather than leaving it mid-sentence in the last answer (taste). Someone checking whether they can still cancel finds it in a second.
2. Order the questions by how often people ask them. Cancelling and changing the time are probably asked more than collecting for someone else, and the allergy entry should sit where people ordering for others will see it (taste; check against your actual support questions).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether these are expandable accordions, and if so whether the headings look clickable, and how the block reflows on a phone.
