**First read.** The eye lands on "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops through the centred paragraph and the three short lines, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the landing page for a small local ferry, aimed at day-trippers and commuters. It feels calm, clean and friendly, but plain. Swap the words and it could be the hero of any small service's website.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's the homepage hero for a ferry service: it should get a would-be passenger to decide this crossing works for them, then check sailing times.

**Verdict.** It's well put together and the order of reading is clear, but it only does half its job. It sells the mood of the crossing and holds back the facts a passenger decides on: where the boat goes, what it costs, and when the first and last boats leave. The copy is the biggest thing in the way, not the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy withholds what a passenger acts on (defect).** In the paragraph under the headline, "from early morning until late evening" gives no times. "Cross the bay" never says where to: the page names the north landing and nothing else. There's no fare anywhere, though "Bicycles travel free" implies people pay. Someone deciding whether they can catch a boat home at 9 pm, or whether this beats driving round the bay, has to click through before they can answer. The button becomes a chore instead of a next step. Replace the vague phrases with the facts: "First boat 6:40, last boat 22:20", "North landing to [town]", and a fare. The twelve minutes already lands well because it's a real number, so give the rest of the copy that treatment too. This is common sense, not a cited rule: a hero that answers "can I use this?" lets "See the timetable" become the obvious next step rather than the only way to find out.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline break.** "twelve / calm minutes" splits the number from what it counts, so the first line ends on a bare "twelve". Break after "bay" instead ("Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes") so the promise reads as one phrase (Bringhurst 2.4.6 on keeping a number with its unit).
- **Centred paragraph.** The intro is three long centred lines (about 74, 65 and 40 characters). With no shared left edge, the eye has to hunt for the start of each line. Set it flush left, or cut it to one or two short lines if it stays centred (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst 2.1.3).
- **Type sizes too close.** The paragraph (about 20 px), the three feature lines (about 18 px) and the button label (about 18 px) are near enough in size to look like a mistake rather than separate levels. Make the feature lines the same size as the paragraph and set them apart by weight or colour, or make the step between them clearly bigger (Bringhurst 3.1.1; Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).

**What's working.**
- **Headline.** It's a concrete, physical promise ("twelve calm minutes") set big and bold. It owns the page without help, so keep that size and weight gap.
- **Feature lines.** "Bicycles travel free", "Seats on the open deck" and "Hot drinks on board" are specific and in passengers' own words, and none of them could sit on anyone else's page. They're the best copy on the page.
- **One clear action.** There's a single primary button with a label that says exactly what you get. Nothing competes with it.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Builds on finding 1.** Let the copy say what the paragraph already hints at: "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck". The page talks about a view and never shows one. A wide photo of the bay from the deck, behind or above the headline, would make "calm" something the visitor sees for themselves instead of taking on trust. (Taste.)
2. **Let "twelve minutes" lead.** It's already the strongest fact on the page. Pair it with what it replaces, such as "40 minutes by road", so the visitor weighs the ferry against a trip they already know. That makes the case on their own terms. (Taste.)

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Empty bottom third.** The content ends a little below two-thirds of the way down, with blank white underneath. Check in a live browser whether more content starts below the fold or the hero really floats in the top of the screen.
- **Button states.** I couldn't see hover or focus states on the button.
