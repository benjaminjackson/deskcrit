**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers across the top, then drops to the blue line climbing to the right. It reads as a weekly marketing-traffic dashboard for a small SaaS team. It feels calm, tidy and capable, but also stock: nothing on it tells me what to notice.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an at-a-glance dashboard for someone on growth or marketing, who checks whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and mostly well made, but the numbers can't be trusted as shown. The conversion-rate card says it went up when its own inputs say it went down, and the chart doesn't add up to the total above it. On a dashboard, that wrecks the whole point of the screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The conversion card contradicts the other two cards.** *Defect.* Visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups rose 2.4%. Sign-ups grew more slowly than visits, so the conversion rate must have fallen. 3,912 ÷ 184,220 is 2.12% now. The implied previous figures (≈3,820 ÷ ≈173,630) give about 2.20% before. That's a drop of about 0.08 pts, but the card says "+0.1 pts" in success green. Someone who checks finds the dashboard lying about the one figure that measures outcome. Someone who doesn't check walks away thinking conversion improved, and acts on it. Fix the calculation or the data. Then give a drop its own sign and colour, so green means good news only (Apple HIG and Material Design both treat green as success). If the figure really is up, the visits or sign-ups delta is wrong instead. Either way, the three cards have to agree with each other.
2. **"Visits by channel" doesn't add up to Visits.** *Defect.* If the y-axis shows weekly visits, the three lines add up to roughly 138k across the 12 weeks. The card says 184,220. About 46k visits, a quarter of the traffic, aren't in the chart. Maybe Direct, Referral and the rest were left out. Maybe the axis isn't weekly. The viewer can't tell, and either way "which channel drives our traffic" gets a misleading answer. Fix: show every channel, or add an "Other" line in grey, or retitle the chart to say it shows the top three channels. Also put the unit and the time step on the axis ("Visits per week"), so nobody has to guess what 6k means (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few).
3. **Nothing on the screen says what matters.** *Taste, in my view.* The three KPIs are the same size, weight and colour. Every delta is the same green. The chart title only names the topic. So the eye scans and finds no news, and the one real story (traffic up, conversion down) is hidden by the same green as everything else. Give the outcome metric more weight, or let the deltas show direction honestly (finding 1 will do half of this). Have the chart title state the finding, such as "Search drives the growth; Social is volatile". This is argued on its own terms; no approved source states it.

**Smaller things.**
- The legend sits at the top left, while the lines end at the right edge where their labels would go. Put "Search / Social / Email" at the right-hand end of each line and drop the legend (Tufte; Wilke).
- "Last 12 weeks" at the top right looks like plain text, so you can't tell whether you can change the range. If it's a picker, give it a visible control cue such as a chevron or a button outline (Norman, signifiers). If it's static, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI card shows its comparison right under the number ("vs previous 12 weeks"), with the period spelled out. That's exactly the context a headline number needs. Keep that pattern once the maths is fixed.
- The chart's ink is restrained: a zero baseline, faint gridlines, no markers or fills. The three lines sit in separate bands and never cross, so each series is easy to follow even for a colour-blind viewer.
- The grid is strict: three equal cards over a full-width panel, matching gutters, and the same 48 px left edge from the logo down to the chart.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 3. Let the conversion rate lead. It's the number that says whether the traffic is worth anything, and it's the one a growth lead acts on. Give it the dominant spot and size, and treat Visits and Sign-ups as its inputs, so the screen answers "are we converting better?" before it answers "are we busier?" *Taste* (Tschichold's ordering by size difference).
2. Put the previous 12 weeks in the chart as a pale grey ghost of each line. The cards already compare against that period, so letting the chart show the same comparison ties the top and bottom halves together. Then the viewer can see where the +6.1% came from instead of trusting a single number. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a picker, and whether the chart shows values on hover. Without a hover readout, exact weekly figures can only be read off the gridlines.
