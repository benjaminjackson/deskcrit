**First read.** My eye goes to the bold headline at top left, then to the blue "Create your link" button, then along the row of four cards. In about two seconds I can tell it's a scheduling tool. Then I notice the right half of the hero is empty. The page feels tidy and competent, but it looks like any SaaS template, and nothing on it says "this one, not Calendly."

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing homepage for a booking-link product, aimed at small service businesses (the customer strip lists dental, tutoring, physio and vets), and that its job is to get visitors to make a link or try the live example.

**Verdict.** It's clear and well set, but it doesn't persuade. The hero has no product picture: half the first screen is empty, and nothing shows what a Slotwise booking page looks like. The headline could also sit on any competitor's page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half of the hero is empty (roughly x 680–1216, y 100–380).** The headline, subhead and buttons all sit in the left column, and nothing on the right balances them, so the top of the page looks lopsided (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The empty area isn't open space doing a job like a calm margin would. It looks like something is missing, and it may be an image that didn't load. If it is, check the live page; if it isn't, this is the biggest gap on the page. The bigger cost is what the visitor never gets to see: the booking page their clients would use. For a product whose whole pitch is "share one link, let people pick a time", that picture is the proof. Put a real booking page in the right column, ideally one like the customers' own (a physio clinic's slots for the week). *Defect.*
2. **The headline and CTAs don't set this product apart.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" describes the whole category, so a visitor comparing tools learns nothing about why to pick this one. The customer strip suggests small clinics and tutors, which is a sharper audience than the copy speaks to. The subhead also stops at the feature ("keep every meeting in the right place") instead of what the visitor gains, such as no more back-and-forth emails or fewer no-shows. "See a live example" is the strongest ask on the page because it lets visitors judge the product for themselves, but it's styled as the weaker option. "Create your link" is specific, which is good. Direction: name the audience or the problem in the headline, end the subhead on what changes for "you", and give the live example more weight (or make it the picture from finding 1). *Taste, on copy.*
3. **The first card breaks the row.** "Shared calendars" wraps to two lines because its "New" pill is pushed to the right edge. That drops its body text and "Learn more" about 30 px below the other three, so the four cards no longer line up across the row (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*, "Construction of the grid"). That pill is also solid blue where the others are grey, so the row looks like two systems (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). The pills themselves don't help either: "Auto" and "Email" are internal tags that tell a visitor nothing ("Auto" what?) (Nielsen, heuristic 2). Put the badge right after the heading like the others, or shorten the heading so it fits on one line. Keep only meaningful pills ("New", "Beta") and drop the rest. *Defect, but minor.*

**Smaller things.**
- Customer strip (y 770): plain grey names with no heading read like a second menu. Add "Used by" or use real logos so it reads as proof.
- Card icons: the calendar is an outline icon and the clock, mail and form icons are filled. Use one style. The "Intake forms" glyph also looks like a layout grid rather than a form.
- The four "Learn more" links can't be told apart out of context (WCAG 2.4.4). Make each one say where it goes, e.g. "How team routing works".
- "Start free" (header) and "Create your link" (hero) are identical filled blue buttons with different labels, so they look like two different primary actions (Material Design, button emphasis). Use one label, or step the header button down a level.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: the headline, card headings, body and nav are clearly separate sizes, with weight doing the rest of the work. Keep it.
- The card body copy speaks to people ("so nobody has to do the math", "so the first minutes are useful"). Each card ends on what the visitor gets. The hero copy should match that voice.
- The palette is restrained: one blue, kept for actions, with everything else neutral. The CTA is easy to find because of it.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1. Let the blue that marks the buttons also mark the open slots in the booking-page picture. That ties "Create your link" visually to what it creates, so the button and the product read as one idea. *Taste.*
2. The customer names point to one audience: small practices that take appointments. Commit the whole first screen to them, through the example in the picture, the wording of the headline and the order of the cards, rather than speaking to "teams" in general. A clinic owner who sees their own situation acts faster than one who sees a category. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right side of the hero is a hero image that didn't load. Check the live page.
- Hover and focus states on the buttons and links.
