**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%), drops to the green "+x% vs previous 12 weeks" lines, then slides down into a large chart with three rising lines. I look for what the lines are and find nothing: no names, no values, no dates. It reads as a clean, calm SaaS analytics overview for a marketer or founder, and it feels tidy but unfinished, as if half the chart never got drawn.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a weekly-review dashboard telling a marketing or growth owner how traffic, sign-ups and conversion moved over the last 12 weeks, and which channels drove it.

**Verdict.** The KPI row works. The chart, which takes two-thirds of the screen, communicates almost nothing: three unnamed coloured lines with no scale and no time axis. The headline problem is that "Visits by channel" doesn't say which channel is which, how many visits, or when.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read.** *Defect.* This is the large panel from y≈283 to 758.
   - **No series labels:** The title promises channels, but the blue, orange and green lines carry no names and there's no legend. The viewer can see that one channel is biggest and one is jumpiest, but not which ones. Those are the two things the chart exists to answer.
   - **No y-axis values:** The gridlines have no numbers, so you can't tell whether the blue line is 10k or 100k a week. You also can't check whether the bottom gridline is zero, so the gaps between the lines may exaggerate or hide real differences.
   - **No x-axis dates:** "12 weeks" has to be taken on trust, and the dip at point 7 can't be tied to anything.
   - **Leftover space:** The empty strip left of the plot (x≈49–121) and the band below the baseline (y≈685–757) look like room set aside for labels that never rendered. If they fail to load in the live page, that's a bug. If they were never designed, it's a design gap. Either way the viewer meets the same blank.
   - **Colour alone:** Hue is the only thing separating the series (WCAG 1.4.1). Orange and green are the pair most likely to merge for a red-green colour-blind viewer.
   - **Fix:** Put each channel's name at the right end of its line, in the line's colour (Tufte: labels on the graphic itself; Wilke). Add y values with units and week dates on x (Tufte, ch. 2; Few). Direct labels also fix the colour-only problem.

2. **The chart doesn't tell you what it found.** *Taste.* "Visits by channel" names a topic. The KPI cards above already say *what* happened (visits up 6.1%), and the chart should say *why*. Example: "Organic drove most of the 6.1% rise", plus an end-value label on each line. That ties the chart to the card above it, so the viewer doesn't have to compare the two by eye. As it stands, the chart and the cards sit side by side without speaking to each other.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing separately.

**What's working.**
- **Every headline number has a comparison:** Each one has its delta and a stated base period ("vs previous 12 weeks"). Conversion is given in "pts" rather than %, which is the correct and unambiguous unit for a change in a rate. Keep that.
- **The figures agree:** 3,912 ÷ 184,220 = 2.12%, so the three cards check out against each other, and a sharp reader will trust the page for it.
- **Calm, consistent cards:** One grid, one type family, three clear size levels (title, KPI figure, label/delta) and colour saved for meaning. The page is easy to scan, and everything important fits on one screen.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Builds on finding 1.** Let the chart answer the card above it. Make "Visits" the selected card, show its total line in grey behind the three channels, and the screen becomes one connected story instead of two separate widgets. *Taste:* for a weekly review, the job is "what moved and why", and linking the number to its breakdown does that in one glance.
2. **Use the empty space for the other metrics.** Sign-ups and conversion get a number and nothing else, while visits gets a full chart. A small 12-week sparkline in each card (Tufte's word-sized graphics) would show whether +2.4% is a steady climb or one good week, at almost no cost in space. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the missing axis labels and legend are a rendering or loading failure: check in a live browser.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is a control (it doesn't look like one). If it's meant to change the range, it needs a visible affordance.
