**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers in the top row (184,220 · 3,912 · 2.12%), sees green "up" lines under each, and drops to the large chart of three rising lines. It reads as a calm growth overview for a small SaaS team: clean, quiet and competent. A beat later it stops working: the biggest thing on the page, the chart, can't be read at all.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly overview for a growth or marketing lead, to see how traffic and sign-ups are trending and which acquisition channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The top row works. The chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, says nothing you can act on. It has no channel names, no values and no dates, so the "by channel" question the page raises goes unanswered.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart is unlabelled, so it can't be read.** This is a defect. "Visits by channel" shows three lines (blue, orange, green), but there is no legend or direct label saying which channel is which. The gridlines have no y values, and the x axis has no week dates. You can see that "something goes up", but not which channel is biggest, how big it is, whether the gap between blue and orange is 2× or 10%, or which week dipped. You also can't tell whether the y axis starts at zero, so the visual gaps between lines may exaggerate or hide real differences. Because colour is the only thing separating the series, the orange and green lines are also hard to tell apart for viewers with red-green colour blindness (WCAG 1.4.1). The missing labels leave dead bands, about 70 px under the baseline and about 50 px left of the plot, that look like a rendering failure. Tufte (*Visual Display*, ch. 2) and Few both say a reader should never have to guess what a number measures. Fix:
   - Name each channel at the end of its line, with its latest value. Direct labels mean no legend and no reliance on colour (Tufte, Wilke).
   - Add y-axis values with the unit, visits per week.
   - Add week dates on the x axis.
   - Use the dead bands for those labels.
   - If the labels and values are meant to be there and didn't render, check that in a live browser. As captured, this is the main failure on the page.
2. **The chart doesn't connect to the numbers above it and states no finding.** This is common sense, not a cited rule. The cards say visits are up 6.1%, and the chart is where you'd look to learn *why*. But its title is a topic ("Visits by channel"), not an answer. Even with labels, the viewer has to compare three lines across 12 weeks to work out which channel drove the growth. Fix: make the title or a one-line caption state the takeaway, for example "[Channel] drove most of the +6.1%". Point out the one week where the blue line dips, if it matters.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right): if this is the date-range control, it looks like a plain label. Give it a control shape, such as a border or chevron. If it's only a label, it's fine.
- The empty space inside the chart card is leftover rather than chosen (the bands under and left of the plot). Fixing finding 1 takes care of it.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards carry the right context. Each number has a comparison ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"), and conversion uses "+0.1 pts" rather than a misleading percent-of-a-percent. Keep that precision.
- The hierarchy is clear: the large bold figures, small grey labels and smaller green deltas each have one job, with a short, disciplined type scale.
- The chart itself is restrained: thin gridlines, no chartjunk, lines suited to a 12-week series. Keep the restraint when you add the labels.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: make the chart show channels for *sign-ups* or conversion, not only visits. Visits are the vanity metric. A growth lead acts on which channel brings people who sign up, and the 3,912 card is currently a dead end. This is taste: the page would then answer the question it exists for.
2. Let the three KPI cards set the chart's colours. If the headline is the 184,220 visits, highlight the one channel that matters most in blue and grey the others, so the chart's focus matches the page's (Tufte, Few: emphasise one series, grey the context). This is taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the missing axis labels and legend are a load or render failure. Check in a live browser.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its focus state.
