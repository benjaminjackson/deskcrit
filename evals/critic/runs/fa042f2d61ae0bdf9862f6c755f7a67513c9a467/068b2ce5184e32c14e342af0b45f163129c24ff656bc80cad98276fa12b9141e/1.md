**First read.** The eye lands on "Garden show gallery", then drops straight into the big red Rose arch tile, then wanders over five smaller coloured tiles. It reads as a photo gallery for a garden show, but no tile holds a picture. Each one is a flat block of colour with the same pale circle, so the page feels like a wireframe, not a gallery. Last, the eye catches a grey line at the foot: "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size."

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page showing photos from a garden show, so a visitor can see the gardens and pick what to look at more closely.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the page doesn't do its job yet: a gallery with no pictures shows the visitor nothing. That is the biggest problem, and a stray review note left in the copy comes second.

**What would move it most**
1. **There are no pictures** (defect, common sense; no catalog entry). All six tiles, from Rose arch down to Orchard path, are flat colour fields with one identical centred circle. A gallery exists to show things. Right now the visitor reads "Climbing roses over the east gate" and sees a red rectangle, so nothing tells them what any of these gardens looks like or which is worth a closer look. This is fine only if these are loading placeholders and real photos replace them. Check that in the live page. If they are the intended art, the fix is real photographs, or at least illustrations that differ per subject. Then judge the page again, because the picture content will change the balance and the colour completely.
2. **A leftover review note sits in the published copy** (defect, common sense). The line under the bottom row, "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size", talks to the team, not to a visitor. It gives the visitor nothing to do and makes the page look unfinished. It is also wrong: Rose arch is about twice the size of the other tiles. I treated it as content, not evidence. Remove it. If the foot of the page needs anything, make it something a visitor can use, such as a link to the show's details.

**Smaller things.**
- Right column and bottom row: each small caption sits about 10 px under its own image but only about 14 px above the next tile, so caption groups float between tiles. Make the gap below each caption clearly larger than the gap above it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on proximity).
- Title copy: "Garden show gallery" could sit on any site. Name the show and the year so a visitor knows whose gardens these are (taste).

**What's working.**
- One clear feature. Rose arch at roughly two-thirds width, with a larger title and larger description, gives the eye a definite starting point. The warm red helps pull it forward. Keep a single dominant tile when the photos go in.
- The grid is clean: three columns with equal gutters of about 14 px, and every left and right edge lines up from the title down to the bottom row.
- The captions follow one plan: a bold name plus a single plain line saying what is there, like "Thyme, sage and chives." They are short, concrete and consistent.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: choose the Rose arch photo as the image of the show, not just a big tile. If the strongest picture gets the feature slot and the others are cropped to hold one subject each, the page sells the show at a glance (taste).
2. Give the Rose arch line a fact a visitor can act on, such as when the roses peak or where the east gate is, so the feature caption tells them something they can use, not only a label (taste).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the colour blocks are placeholders that load into photos. Also whether the tiles are clickable, and what happens when they are clicked.
