**First read.** Three slabs of equal-weight saturated colour: burnt orange, green and purple. The eye starts at "Learn to swim" because it is top left, reads across the three bold headlines, then drops into the empty lower half of each card and finds nothing there. It reads straight away as a pool's "what we offer" section. It feels friendly, confident and easy to scan, and then it simply stops.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is a section of a public pool's website. It should help adults and families see the three kinds of session and go on to book one or find out more.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job well: the three offers are clear, parallel and quick to read. But the piece leads nowhere. Each card is more than half empty colour and has no time, price or way to act, so a viewer who has picked a session has nothing to click and nothing more to learn.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no next step, and the bottom of every card is the empty space where it should go.** The text ends about 330 px down. Below that, each card is roughly 300 px of plain fill, down to the bottom edge at 632 px. A parent who reads "Family hour" and decides "yes, that one" can't tell whether the card is clickable, and there's no button, link or arrow to suggest it. They also aren't told which Sunday hours, what it costs, or whether they need to book. The eye path runs from the headline to the blurb and into nothing (Arnheim's point about a path with no dead ends). Whether this counts as a defect depends on something a still can't show: if the whole card is a link, the click may work, but nothing tells the viewer that. Direction: use the empty lower half for what the viewer acts on. Put one fact that helps them decide (times, or a price, e.g. "Tue 7–8pm · £X") and a clearly styled action at the foot of each card, named for its offer, such as "Book a Tuesday class", "See lane times" or "Plan a family visit". Then shorten the card to fit what it holds. *Defect.*
2. **Big blocks of colour with no job.** Each card is around 360×560 px of full-strength fill, and the text uses less than half of it. Taken together, saturated colour covers most of the frame. The empty colour doesn't separate anything, because the white gutters already do that, and it makes each card look top-heavy and unfinished, as if content failed to load. Gathered space only works when it's shaped against something dense (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). Here it's just leftover. Direction: fit the card height to its content once finding 1 has filled it, or let the empty area carry something that earns it, such as a picture of the pool. *Defect, with consequences that overlap finding 1.*

**Smaller things.**
- The row of cards isn't centred: it is 56 px from the left edge and 96 px from the right. That reads as a centred layout that slipped. Make the outer margins equal, or clearly commit to a left-aligned page grid.
- There's a loose ~170 px white band under the cards that relates to nothing. Once the cards are resized, tie it to the section spacing.

**What's working.**
- **Copy that's specific and human.** "With a coach in the water beside you", "three speeds to choose from" and "floats and toys for the children" each answer the fear or question that audience actually has. Keep that voice when you add the times and the action.
- **A clean two-level type scale.** Big, tight, bold headlines over regular body text in one family (looks like Inter). The difference is immediate, the two-line headlines hold together, and the white text on all three fills looks clearly readable.
- **One colour per offer.** With three parallel choices, equal weight is right. Giving each its own hue lets the colour carry through to timetables and booking pages as a way to find each programme.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Let each colour become that programme's identity across the site.** Orange is always lessons, green always lanes, purple always family. The same hue would then appear on the timetable, the booking screen and signs at the pool, so a returning swimmer finds "their" session at a glance. This only works if the colours stay reserved for the three programmes. *Taste.*
2. **Builds on finding 2: put the water in the picture.** A tightly cropped photo in each card's lower half, such as a coach beside a swimmer, a lane rope or a child with a float, would show the promise in each blurb rather than just state it. It would also make these cards about this pool and not any pool. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable as whole links, and whether hover and focus states show that.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 7 yes, 1 no (every element does a job)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 10 yes, 3 no (exact alignment or clearly off it; empty space gathered; eye path)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 8 yes, 1 no (strong colour kept to a small share)
Pictures: skipped, none
Charts and data: skipped, none
Interface: 33 lines, 31 yes, 2 no (controls look like what they do; what the user needs next is in front of them)
