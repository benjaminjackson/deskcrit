**First read.** The eye lands on "Garden show gallery", drops straight into the big red Rose arch tile, then works round the five smaller tiles in reading order. It reads as a clean, well-built gallery page for a garden show. It feels calm, orderly and a little cheerful. But it shows no gardens: every "photo" is a flat colour block with a pale circle sinking into its bottom edge. The labels promise roses, herbs and a pond, and the pictures don't show any of them.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page where visitors browse photos from a garden show. Nobody told me, so correct me if that's wrong.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound and could ship once there are real pictures. As it stands it doesn't do its job, because the only thing a gallery exists to show is missing. Apart from that, the one real layout flaw is the feature caption, which floats loose.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders, so the gallery has nothing in it.** This applies to all six tiles. Each one is a single flat colour with an identical centred, half-sunk pale circle. Someone visiting a "Garden show gallery" comes for the roses over the east gate and the water lilies. What they get is a colour key, and the captions have to do all the work. The headline and the images don't form one idea yet, because the images don't show anything. *Defect, by common sense.* This could be a capture where the images never loaded, or it could be the design: if the real photos are wired in, check in a live browser and this goes away. If they aren't, the fix is to drop real photography into the existing frames. Use a consistent crop and grade so the six tiles still read as a set, and make sure the Rose arch shot can carry the feature slot by itself.

2. **The Rose arch caption floats between two tiles.** The "Rose arch / Climbing roses over the east gate." block sits about 15 px below its image and about 17 px above the Bean rows tile. With the gaps that even, the eye can't tell which picture the caption belongs to, and a quick reader may connect it to the brown tile underneath. The small cards get this right: about 9 px from image to caption and about 15 px to the next tile. *Convention:* the gap inside a group should be clearly smaller than the gap between groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on Wertheimer's proximity). Tighten the caption up under its image, or open up the gutter below it, so the feature card has the same caption-to-image ratio as the small cards.

**What's working.**
- **The grid is disciplined.** It uses three columns. The feature spans two columns and two rows, the right-hand stack of Herb bed and Pond edge ends on the same line as the Rose arch caption, and the roughly 14 px gutters repeat everywhere. Keep this when the photos go in.
- **The type hierarchy is clean.** I count about five sizes (about 60, 36, 18, 14 bold and 13 grey), and every step is clear. The two smallest are told apart by weight and colour, not by an almost-equal size, which is the right way to do it.
- **The tile colours match their subjects.** Rose red, herb green, pond blue, bean brown, glasshouse yellow and plum purple. If the colour blocks stay on as loading placeholders, that mapping makes them feel intended.

**Where it could go further.**
1. *Taste.* Let the Rose arch feature change from show to show, and give its dek more of a story than the small captions get. That way the feature slot tells visitors what to see first, instead of just being bigger. The scale contrast already sets up that promise. The copy should keep it.
2. *Taste.* Builds on finding 1: if the flat colours stay as placeholders, use the subject colour as each photo's loading tint. The colour-to-subject mapping then carries over into the finished page rather than being thrown away.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the tiles are links, and what hover and focus look like. Whether the flat blocks are unloaded images: check this in a live browser. How the 2×2 feature reflows on narrow screens.
