**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline, drops to the italic standfirst, and settles into the body. It reads as a long-form magazine feature, calm and literate, with good writing. But for the first few seconds it looks like a well-set text document: nothing on this screen shows the allotment, the bus stop, or the people, even though the byline credits "Photographs by the residents".

**What it's for.** My assumption: the first screen of an online magazine feature, meant to pull a casual reader into a long human-interest story.

**Verdict.** The type is clean and the copy is strong, so it works as a reading page. As the opening of a *feature* it is flat. The one thing that would make a stranger stop is the residents' photographs, and they aren't on this screen. The body line is also too long for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The photographs the byline promises aren't on the first screen** (defect, unless the image failed to load in this capture). The byline at y≈250 says "Photographs by the residents", but the first screen is all text, and below the last paragraph there is a blank band about 200 px tall. The reader is told this is a story about a place and its people, then gets no picture of either, so the page could be any blog post. If an image should be in that blank band, it's a loading problem and you should check it in a live browser. If the layout really puts the first photo below the fold, it is a design choice, and it gives away the piece's best hook. The fix is to let one resident's photo lead, either between the headline and the standfirst or as a full-width image under the headline block. Choose one that does more than repeat the headline: the water-tap queue, say, or a bath-tub bed. Then the picture and the words build one idea together. This is my own argument, not a cited rule.
2. **The body lines run too long** (convention, Bringhurst §2.1.2, 45–75 characters). The column is about 720 px wide, and the body lines look like 85–90 characters. "It started with a letter. Halima Osei, who has lived on the estate since 1998, wrote to" is about 87. Over a long feature, that makes the eye work harder to find each next line. Narrow the column to about 600–640 px, or set the body a step larger, to get close to 66 characters. Measure the count in the browser.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: "line" sits alone on the second line, which leaves a runt and splits "bus line" across the break. Rebalance the break so "bus line" stays together, either with balanced wrapping or a non-breaking space, or by tightening the size or measure (Bringhurst §2.4.2).
- Top navigation: "Features · Food · Places · Subscribe" looks identical for every item, though this page is a Feature. Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- The copy. The standfirst makes a real before-and-after ("a car park nobody used" → forty households), and it lands on a concrete human detail: the queue at the tap as the best place to hear the news. The second paragraph opens on a person and a date, and the one-line third paragraph ("Most of them are still in use.") gives a nice beat. Keep this voice.
- The hierarchy is clear and economical. You have one big bold headline, a standfirst set off by italic and colour rather than another size, plain body text, and a small grey byline. Each level has one job.
- The restraint with colour. The single green "Feature" kicker is the only accent, and it suits a story about growing things.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: let the residents' own photographs set the tone. Run them unpolished and in their own framing, with a caption naming who took each one. In a story about people building something themselves, the authorship of the pictures is part of the point. This is taste.
2. The green of the "Feature" kicker could do more than label the section. It could carry through as the one colour for pull quotes or photo captions, so the piece feels like it belongs to its subject instead of a generic article template (Inter, grey nav, centred column). This is taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty band at the bottom of the screen: check in a live browser whether an image or further text should be there, or whether the capture just ends.
