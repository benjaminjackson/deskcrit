**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", the largest and darkest thing on the page, and then moves right to the white card and down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads at once as a contact page for a small design or dev studio. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: nothing on the screen says anything about Fernwick Studio itself.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the studio's contact page, and its job is to get a prospective client to send an enquiry the studio can qualify and answer.

**Verdict.** It does its basic job. The ask is clear, the form is short and the button is unmistakable. The biggest problem is a mismatch: the intro asks for a budget and timeline, but the form has no place for them. So the details the studio most needs to qualify a lead depend on the visitor remembering them. Second is a layout that stops short of its own grid, so the page looks shifted left and unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form doesn't collect what the copy asks for.** The intro says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The form below has only Name, Email and Message. A visitor reads that line, moves to the form, starts writing about the project, and the request is gone from their mind. Most enquiries will arrive without the two facts the studio wanted, and that costs a round of email before any quote. This is a defect: Nielsen's recognition-rather-than-recall heuristic says the context the user needs should sit at the point of action, not in a paragraph they have already left. Fix: give budget and timeline their own optional fields in the form. Short selects or ranges work well because they're quicker than writing and give you comparable answers. At minimum, put the prompt inside the Message field's label.
2. **The content block doesn't use the page's grid.** The nav runs from 56 px to about 1224 px, but the form card stops at about 1068 px. That leaves a dead strip of about 156 px on the right that no other element uses, so the whole composition looks shifted left. The left column also sits empty under its three lines of copy. The split (text column, gap, card, leftover) follows no proportion you can name. This is a convention (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*: one grid sets every block's edges). Here it costs polish, because the page looks unfinished rather than deliberately spare. Fix: end the card's right edge on the same line as "Contact", or narrow both columns and centre the pair on the page's axis. Either way, the empty space should be one intended area, not scraps left over.
3. **The headline and intro could be on anyone's site.** "Tell us about your project" is the default line for every agency contact page. Nothing here, in words or pictures, says what Fernwick does, who it works for, or who will read the message. A visitor comparing two or three studios gets no reason to pick this one at the moment they decide. This is a defect of the copy. Fix: make the headline or intro specific to the studio's work and audience. "We read every message" would be stronger with a name or role ("Anna, our founder, replies within two working days").

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks the same as the other links, so nothing marks the current page. Give it one cue, such as bold weight or a heavier underline (Laubheimer, NN/g menu-design checklist).
- Field borders: the grey outlines on the three inputs look close to 2:1 against white, below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for. Measure them, and darken them if they fall short.
- Form: no field is marked required or optional. Mark the optional ones, especially once budget and timeline fields exist (Budiu, NN/g).

**What's working.**
- One primary action. "Send message" is the only solid-filled element on the page, so the next step is never in doubt. Keep it the only filled button even after adding fields.
- Labels sit above the fields, in bold, close to their own input and clearly apart from the next. The form is one column with no placeholder-only labels. It's easy to scan and easy to fill in.
- "We read every message and reply within two working days" sets an exact, checkable expectation. That's the strongest line on the page, so keep it.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 3: put a sign of the work next to the ask, such as one small project image or a single line naming two recent clients. A prospective client is deciding whether to trust this studio with a budget, and the empty left column is exactly where that proof could sit. Taste.
2. Treat the "two working days" promise as a feature, not small print. Make it the second thing the eye reads, so the commitment that sets this studio apart isn't left in body-weight grey. Taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the fields show a visible focus ring, and whether they validate and show errors in a helpful way. Check this in a live browser, because this page is nothing but a form.
