**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", drops to the two-day reply promise, then moves right to the white form card and down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads at once as a design studio's contact page. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: apart from the wordmark, nothing on the screen says anything about Fernwick.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a contact page where prospective clients send a project enquiry, so the job is to get a complete, useful message sent with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order is clear, there is one obvious action, and the copy sets a concrete expectation. What holds it back is small but visible: the form card isn't placed on any edge the rest of the page uses, and the form leaves out a few basics (which fields are required, how readable the field borders are, which page you're on).

**What would move it most**
1. **The form card sits on no grid line, so the layout looks unfinished.** **Convention.** The header and footer run from x≈56 to x≈1224. The card starts at about 509 and stops at about 1068. That leaves a strip of about 156 px of grey on the right that belongs to nothing. The card isn't aligned to the right margin and isn't centred, so it looks placed by eye, and the empty strip reads as leftover space rather than chosen space. The column split (about 400 px of text, about 560 px of card) doesn't follow any ratio either. Müller-Brockmann (*Grid Systems*) holds that every block should take its edges from the same column lines that run through the page. To fix it, put the card's right edge on the 1224 margin so the two columns fill the width the header sets up. If you want a narrow form, make that an explicit column and set the left column against it.
2. **Nothing says which fields are required.** **Defect.** None of the three labels ("Your name", "Email address", "Message") has an asterisk or the word "optional". A visitor can't tell whether they can skip their name, and the first time they find out is an error after they click Send. Budiu (Nielsen Norman Group, "Marking Required Fields in Forms") says to mark every required field. If all three are required, the simplest fix is one line at the top of the card ("All fields required") or an asterisk after each label with a short key.

**Smaller things.**
- Input borders: the mid-grey borders on white look like they're below 3:1. That makes the empty fields faint for low-vision users (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border colour against #fff, and darken it if it's under 3:1.
- Navigation: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so the menu doesn't show where you are. Mark the current page with one cue, such as a bolder weight or a bar under the link (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Help placement: the hint "Include a budget range and a rough timeline" sits in the left column, about 450 px from the Message box where people would act on it. Repeat it as helper text under the Message label (Nielsen 10).

**What's working.**
- The subhead does real work. "We read every message and reply within two working days" is a specific, checkable promise, and the budget/timeline hint tells people what a useful enquiry looks like. Keep both.
- The hierarchy is clean. There is one bold headline, one type family, labels above their fields, a single-column form and exactly one filled primary button. The eye never has to search.
- The palette is restrained: neutral greys, plus one blue shared by the links and the button. The button is the only saturated block on the page, so it stands out for the right reason.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Use the empty space under the subhead to make the page Fernwick's (taste).** The left column is empty from y≈300 down to the footer. A visitor who is deciding whether to write wants a reason to pick this studio and someone to picture reading the message. Put something specific there: one recent project, the name and face of the person who replies, or a direct email address for people who'd rather not use a form. This page currently looks like any studio's contact page, so a single specific detail would do more than any change in styling.
2. **Make the headline concrete (taste).** "Tell us about your project" could appear on any agency's site. Ending on what the visitor gets, something like "Tell us about your project and we'll reply with next steps within two days", would carry the promise into the line people read first.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button. This is a keyboard-heavy form, so check that the ring is at least 2 px and at 3:1 contrast.
- Whether the email field uses an email input type, and how errors appear when the form is submitted.
