**First read.** The eye lands on "Learn to swim", which is huge, bold and near-black, then drops to the grey line under it, then drifts right to two small items, "Join the lanes" and "Family hour". Then it stops, because there is nothing else. The bottom 45% of the frame and the right quarter are empty. It reads as the top of a local pool's page. It looks calm and clean, but it feels like a wireframe someone forgot to finish: no picture, no colour, no button.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the landing page for adult swimming lessons at a local pool, and its job is to get a hesitant adult non-swimmer to book a Tuesday class.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The headline makes a promise, and then the page gives the reader nothing to act on: no time, no price, no place, no way to book. The space where that should be goes to two unrelated pool sessions instead.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no ask, so the eye path ends nowhere** (defect). The path runs headline → subtitle → two small items → 360 px of empty white. Nowhere does the page say what to do next. "Tuesday evenings" gives no time. "Small" gives no number. There is no price, no start date, no location and no button. A reader who is persuaded has to go looking for how to sign up, and many won't. The empty bottom and right also make the page feel top-left heavy and abandoned rather than calm (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The fix: put one exact ask straight under the subtitle, styled as the only button on the page, naming a concrete first step ("Book a place on Tuesday 7 pm, 6 swimmers max"). Then give the next band the facts the reader will hold you to: time, group size, cost, where.
2. **The page talks to two other audiences right after addressing a beginner** (defect, common sense; no approved source states it). The headline and subtitle speak to an adult who can't swim. The next two items are for people who already swim ("three speeds to choose from") and for parents ("floats and toys for the children"). At the moment of deciding, the beginner reads about things that aren't for them, and the page stops being about their fear and their class. Either give this band to the lessons themselves (what the first class is like, the coach beside you, shallow end first) or move the other sessions well down the page and label them clearly as "Also at the pool".

**Smaller things.**
- Type levels collide. The subtitle (~22 px, grey) and the card headings (~20 px, near-black, regular) are almost the same size. The darker card headings even out-rank the subtitle, which they shouldn't (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1). Drop the card headings a step, or make them clearly smaller and bolder, so the subtitle is plainly level two.
- The colour doesn't carry the mood. It's all grey on white, so nothing feels like water or reassurance, and it could be any service's page (Itten, *The Art of Color*). One calm water hue on the ask would do it; see "Where it could go further."

**What's working.**
- The headline. "Learn to swim" is three plain words, very large and bold. Nobody misreads what the page offers, and it clearly dominates. Keep that scale.
- The subtitle copy. "With a coach in the water beside you" speaks directly to the fear of an adult beginner. It is the best line on the page, so build the ask around it.
- The left edge is clean. Headline, subtitle and first column share one line at x≈96, and the two columns sit on shared baselines. The structure is sound, just under-filled.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: let the coach line become the picture (taste). One photo of a coach in the water beside an adult learner, set in the empty right side, would show the subtitle's promise instead of only stating it. It would also balance the frame.
2. Make the class size the number that leads (taste). "Small" is vague, while "6 swimmers, 1 coach" is a fact a nervous beginner can weigh against a crowded public session they already know. Set it large, near the ask.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Join the lanes" and "Family hour" are links. They read like calls to action but aren't styled as links. If they're clickable, they need to look it.
