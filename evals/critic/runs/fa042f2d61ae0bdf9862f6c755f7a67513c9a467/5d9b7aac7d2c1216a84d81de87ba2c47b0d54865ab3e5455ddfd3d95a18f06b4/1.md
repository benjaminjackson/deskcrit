**First read.** The eye goes to the purple "Sign in" button, then jumps up to the red-bordered email field and its red message. Then it settles on the "Quillpost" wordmark. It reads as a sign-in card caught with a validation error. It is calm, tidy and familiar, and close to the default SaaS sign-in card.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you gave no context: returning users sign in to Quillpost and fix their typed email when it's wrong. New users should find their way to sign-up.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the form is built correctly, and the error is in words under the field it's about. What holds it back is that the way to sign up is invisible: "Create an account" looks like plain gray text. Next after that are a pale password field edge and an error message that describes a format but doesn't say what the user got wrong.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link** (defect). It's the bottom line of the card, "New here? Create an account". The whole line looks like one gray caption in one weight, so nothing marks the link. A new user scanning for sign-up sees a remark, not something to click. Every other link on the card ("Forgot your password?") is purple, so this one breaks the card's own pattern too. NN/g (Moran, "Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty") found that links styled as static text draw less attention. WCAG 1.4.1 says not to rely on color alone. Here not even color sets it apart. Fix: give "Create an account" the same purple as "Forgot your password?", and add an underline or a heavier weight, so it reads as the second path out of the card.

2. **The password field's edge looks too faint** (convention, WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11). The resting border on the password field is a pale gray-blue on white, and it looks well below 3:1. Next to the strong red border above it, the field almost disappears. For a low-vision user, or on a washed-out laptop screen, the box shape that says "type here" is gone. Measure the border color against white. If it's under 3:1, darken it until it passes, for every resting field and not just this one.

3. **The error message states a format, not the problem** (defect, copy). The input is "dana.ortiz@exmaple". It's missing an ending like ".com", and "exmaple" is very likely a typo of "example". The message, "Enter an email address in the format name@example.com", makes the user hold their entry next to the pattern and spot the difference themselves. A stranger might also read "example.com" as the domain they're meant to type. The rule is met on paper, since it does say how to fix it (WCAG 3.3.3), but it doesn't land on this user's mistake. Fix: name what's missing, in words aimed at the user: "This email is missing its ending, like .com". Where a typo is likely, offer the fix ("Did you mean …?").

**Smaller things.** None beyond the above.

**What's working.**
- There is one clear primary action. The full-width filled purple "Sign in" is the only strong shape on the card, so nobody wonders what to press. Keep the other actions as quiet text links.
- The error is shown in three ways: a red border, a red message in words, and its position right under the Email field it concerns. It doesn't rely on color alone. Keep that pattern for every field error.
- The labels are visible, bold, and sit tight above their fields. There's clearly more space between the field groups than within them, so the form reads as two units plus actions.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1. Treat the card's two exits as a matched pair. "Forgot your password?" and "Create an account" are the two ways out for users who can't simply sign in, so style them identically. That makes the card read as one sign-in, two ways out. (Taste.)
2. Let the purple that is already in the wordmark and the button carry more of the brand. Right now the page could be anyone's sign-in. Even a slightly purple-tinted background instead of the neutral gray-blue would make it read as Quillpost before anyone reads a word. (Taste.)

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the error appears too early (while the user is still typing) or on blur or submit.
- Whether the focus ring on the fields and links is visible and at least 2 px.
- Whether the password field offers a show-password toggle on focus.
