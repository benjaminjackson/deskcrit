**First read.** The eye goes first to the purple "Sign in" button, then up to the "Sign in" heading, then gets pulled sideways by the red border around the Email field. The red says something is wrong, but nothing on the screen says what. It's a clean, calm, generic sign-in card for a product called Quillpost, and right now it shows a failed attempt that doesn't explain itself.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the sign-in screen of a web app (Quillpost) for returning users, captured in its validation-error state, with a route to sign-up for new users.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the one thing this state exists for fails: the form flags the email as wrong and doesn't say why or how to fix it. The user is left staring at "dana.ortiz@exmaple" trying to work out what's wrong.

**What would move it most**
1. **The error has no words.** *Defect, and it breaks a convention.* The Email field has a red border and nothing else: no message, no icon. The user has to work out the problem alone: is the address unknown, is it formatted badly, or is it the password? Here there are two problems, the misspelt "exmaple" and the missing ".com", and a red outline points to neither. Anyone who can't tell red from the grey border of the Password field gets no signal at all. WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.1 (Error Identification) requires the error to be described in text, and SC 3.3.3 (Error Suggestion) requires the message to say how to fix it when the fix is known. Fix: put a short message directly under the Email field that says what is wrong and how to correct it, such as "Enter a full email address, like name@example.com". If the check can spot a likely typo, offer "Did you mean …?". Keep the red border as a second cue. NN/g's error-message guidelines say the same: near the cause, in plain words, with a fix, keeping what the user typed (which this screen already does).
2. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link.** *Defect.* At the foot of the card, "New here? Create an account" is one grey line in a single style, so a new user can't see which words to click, or that any can be clicked. Meanwhile "Forgot your password?" is purple, so the card uses two different link styles. That breaks the one system the page sets up (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). This line is the only way in for new users. Fix: style "Create an account" like the other link, in the brand purple. Also give both links a cue besides colour, such as an underline, so they don't rely on hue alone (WCAG Techniques G183/F73).

**Smaller things.**
- The Password field's border is a pale grey-blue that looks close to or below 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure it, and darken it if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- There is one clear primary action. The full-width purple button is the only filled, saturated element on the card, so nobody has to look for what to do next.
- The labels are visible, bold, and sit above their own fields, closer to them than to anything else. Nothing depends on placeholder text, so the form still reads with values entered.
- The spacing is tight and regular. One left edge runs from the wordmark through the fields to the link, and the empty space is collected around the card instead of scattered.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: make the error message do more than reject the input. A sign-in screen exists to get a returning user in quickly, and "exmaple" is exactly the kind of slip a "Did you mean dana.ortiz@example.com?" suggestion fixes in one click. That turns the error into help instead of a dead end. Taste.
2. Nothing else beyond the findings. The card is intentionally plain, and for a utility screen that's the right call.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus: whether the red border is also how focus looks, and whether a focused field has an indicator at least 2 px thick with 3:1 contrast.
- Target size: the two text links look about 18 px tall. Check that their click areas reach 24×24 px (WCAG 2.5.8).
