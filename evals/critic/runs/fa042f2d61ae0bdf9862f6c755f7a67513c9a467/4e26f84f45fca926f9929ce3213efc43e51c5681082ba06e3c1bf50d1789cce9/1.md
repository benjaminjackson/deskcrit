**First read.** The eye goes to "Create your account", drops down a column of six empty boxes, and stops on the navy "Create account" button. It reads as a signup page for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels tidy, calm and competent, and also anonymous: apart from the wordmark and one line of subhead, it could be the signup screen for any SaaS product.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a first-time visitor signs up for a free notes workspace, and the page's job is to get them through the form with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The page works: the hierarchy is clear, the layout is clean and the primary action is obvious. What holds it back is how much the form asks and how little it explains. There are six fields and none is marked required or optional. Two of them do nothing for the person signing up, and the password field gives no rules. Someone who only wants to take notes gets a longer, more uncertain form than the task needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for too much up front and doesn't say what's optional.** "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" sit between the user and a free account. Nothing marks either one, or any other field, as required or optional. "Workspace name" has no hint about what it is or whether it can be changed later. A new user has to stop, make up a name for something they haven't seen yet, and guess whether they can skip the referral question. Each pause is a chance to leave. *Defect.* The fix has three parts. First, cut or defer: move the referral question after signup, and default the workspace name (for example "Ben's notes" from the first name) or ask for it once they're inside, following Nielsen's progressive disclosure. Second, whatever stays and isn't required gets "(optional)" after its label (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). Third, add a one-line hint under Workspace name if it stays.
2. **The password field gives no rules.** If there's a minimum length or a character rule, the user only finds out when they're rejected after pressing submit. *Defect* (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall; Nielsen 5, error prevention). The fix is a short helper line under the field, such as "At least 8 characters", visible before they type.
3. **"Sign in" isn't marked as a link.** In "Already have an account? Sign in", the link is the same gray, size and weight as the rest of the sentence, with no underline. A returning user who landed here by mistake, which is common on signup pages, has to guess where to click. *Defect.* WCAG technique G183 requires a link that relies on colour alone to differ by 3:1 from the text around it, and this one doesn't differ at all. The fix is to underline it, or set it in the brand navy at medium weight.

**Smaller things.**
- **Blank dropdown with a mismatched arrow.** The "How did you hear about us?" select is an empty box with the browser's default black arrow, which is heavier and darker than anything else on the card. Give it a "Select one" prompt and an arrow styled to match the inputs (Gerstner: one program for repeated elements). This is common sense, not a rule.
- **Faint input borders.** The pale gray-blue borders look below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for between a field's edge and the white card. Measure them, and darken the borders if they fall short.

**What's working.**
- **Labels.** Each label is short, bold, sits above its field and is closer to it than to the field before. The form scans cleanly, and the labels don't vanish as placeholders would. Keep this.
- **One clear action.** The full-width navy button is the only strong colour on the page apart from the wordmark. The eye knows where the form ends and what to press.
- **Name pair and type scale.** Putting first and last name side by side is the right exception to the single column. The type scale is tight: heading, subhead, labels and button text each have a clear job.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Builds on finding 1. Make the subhead say why Quillnest.** *Taste.* "Start a free workspace for your notes" could sit on any notes app's page. One concrete promise, the thing Quillnest does that the user's current notes app doesn't, would give a hesitant visitor a reason to type the first character. The page's job is to convert, and right now the copy does none of the persuading.
2. **Let the navy carry more of the brand.** *Taste.* The wordmark and button already share the navy, while the rest of the page is the pale blue-gray every template uses. A wordmark or small illustrative touch beside or above the card, in that same navy, would give the page an identity without adding fields or clutter.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the inputs and the button: these matter on a form that people tab through.
- When and how validation errors appear.
