**First read.** My eye goes first to the row of three big numbers, starting with $4.82M at top left. Then the large blue bar chart pulls it down, and the heavy black box around the chart holds it there. It reads as a finance overview for a SaaS company. It feels calm, competent and a bit generic. Nothing here is broken at first glance. The problem comes on the second look, when you try to make the numbers add up.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a founder or finance lead opens this to check in a few seconds how revenue is doing this year and whether they can trust the figures.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job, but the numbers don't agree with each other, and on a revenue page that is the one thing it can't get wrong. The chart also draws its frame more heavily than its data and doesn't say what it shows.

**What would move it most**
1. **The figures contradict each other** (defect). The table on the right is headed "Revenue by plan, 2025" and its Total is $4.82M. That is exactly the figure in the first card, "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025". But the twelve monthly bars add up to about $4.49M of recognised 2025 revenue (312+328+…+447 by eye). And December's ~447K × 12 would put ARR near $5.4M, not $4.82M. So a careful viewer sees three possible answers to "how much revenue?" and can't tell which is real. Once one figure looks wrong, every figure on the page is in doubt. The fix: name what each block really measures ("ARR by plan, Dec 2025" if that's what it is), and check that ARR, monthly revenue and the plan split come from the same definition and period. If they really do measure different things, say so in the labels, the way the chart already does with "recognised revenue".
2. **The chart's frame is louder than its bars** (convention). There's a black box around the plot and near-black gridlines at every 100. They are as dark as the blue bars, or darker, so the grid competes with the data and the chart looks caged. The fix: remove the frame, and make the gridlines thin and pale grey or drop them, so the bars are the strongest thing in the chart (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*; Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, on data-ink).
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding** (taste, common sense). "Monthly revenue, 2025" leaves the viewer to work out the story themselves: steady growth from ~312K to ~447K, about +43% across the year, with small dips in April and July. A title like "Monthly revenue grew 43% through 2025" gives the viewer the point before they've read a single axis, and lets them check it against the bars.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Revenue by plan, 2025" heading sits a few pixels lower than "Monthly revenue, 2025" beside it. That's close enough to look like a mistake. Put both on the same baseline.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI card has a comparison right under it ("+18% vs Dec 2024", "+3 pts vs 2024"), in green and with a sign, so the figure has context and its meaning doesn't depend on colour alone. Keep that.
- The chart is labelled honestly: the y-axis starts at zero, there's a units subtitle ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue") and a source line ("closed invoices only"). That's exactly the kind of labelling that will make fix 1 easy.
- The type and colour are restrained: one neutral range, one blue, a few clearly separate sizes. The page reads as one quiet whole.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 3: draw 2025 against 2024 in the chart, with last year's months as pale grey bars or a thin line behind the blue. The cards already make that comparison in words. Letting the chart show it too would make "+18%" something the viewer can see, not just read (taste).
2. Let the blue mean one thing. It's the brand's only hue and it currently fills all twelve bars. Use it only for the current period, or for the latest month, and grey the rest, so the eye lands on "where we are now" (taste; Tufte's case for spending ink only where it says something).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the "Jan to Dec 2025" pill is a date picker or a static label, and whether its states and the nav's hover and focus states are clear.
