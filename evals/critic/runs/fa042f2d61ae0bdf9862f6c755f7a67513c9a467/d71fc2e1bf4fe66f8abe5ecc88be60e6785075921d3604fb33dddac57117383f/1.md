I've opened the reference entries for the lines that failed and am writing the critique now.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the week with your whole team", jumps to the blue "Create account" button, and then works back up through the form. It reads at once as the sign-up page for a team-planning SaaS. It's calm, tidy and competent, but it's also anonymous: with the "Tallyboard" wordmark swapped out it could be any product's sign-up page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop sign-up page for a team weekly-planning tool. Its job is to get a team lead to fill in the form and start a free workspace.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: the form is clear, the button is plain to see, and the hierarchy is right. What holds it back is that the form hides things people need at the moment they need them: which fields are required, the password rule, and the sign-in link. The left half also makes no case for signing up beyond one sentence.

**What would move it most**
1. **The password rule disappears as soon as someone starts typing (defect).** "At least 10 characters" exists only as placeholder text in the Password field. Once the first character goes in, the rule is gone, so people guess and find out on submit. This is the field most likely to cause a failed submit. Nielsen's sixth heuristic (recognition rather than recall) says requirements stay visible next to the field and aren't kept in a placeholder that vanishes. Fix: move the rule into helper text under the field that stays put. The placeholder can then go.
2. **No field says whether it's required (defect).** None of the five fields has a marker. "Company" in particular looks optional for a free workspace of up to five people, but nothing says so. People will either fill in a field they didn't need to or stop to wonder. Budiu (NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms") says to mark required fields. Fix: add "(optional)" after the label of each optional field, likely Company, or put asterisks on the required ones with a one-line key. Better still, ask whether Company belongs on this screen at all.
3. **"Sign In" doesn't look like a link (defect).** In "Already have an account? Sign In" under the button, the link is the same small grey as the question beside it, with no colour change and no underline. A returning user who lands here has no visible way back to their account. Fix: set "Sign in" in the brand blue already used for the wordmark and the button, or underline it.
4. **The left column says too little and stops too early (taste, with a balance cost).** The left side is a wordmark, a headline and two lines of text, ending around y 360. The card runs to y 665, so the bottom-left half of the screen is empty and the page tips to the right (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). More importantly, that empty space is exactly where the page could show why anyone should sign up. "Plan the week with your whole team" could be any planner's tagline. Nothing shows what a planned week in Tallyboard looks like, so a team lead gets no reason to choose this over the tool they already use. Fix: use the space below the subhead to show the product's actual week view, or one concrete before-and-after of how a team's week gets planned. That makes the left side worth the room it takes and balances the card.

**Smaller things.**
- Input borders look pale grey-blue on white, roughly 2:1. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 and darken them if they fall short.
- The placeholders "Enter first name", "Enter last name" and "Company name" just repeat their labels, so they add noise without help (common sense). Drop them, or use the space for an example only where it helps, as "name@company.com" does.
- The left column nearly lines up with the card but doesn't. The "Tallyboard" wordmark sits about 8 px off the "Create your account" heading, and neither column hangs from the card's top edge at y 96. Align the wordmark with the card top or the card heading on purpose.
- "Sign In" is title case, while "Create account" and "Create your account" are sentence case. Make it "Sign in".

**What's working.**
- There's one primary action, and it's unmistakable. "Create account" is the only filled, saturated thing in the card, it runs the full width of the card, and the label says exactly what happens.
- The labels sit above the fields, in bold and close to them, with clear gaps between fields. The form scans in one pass, and pairing first and last name is the right exception to a single column.
- The subhead "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." is good copy. It gives a price, a limit and the next step in plain words, and answers the objection before anyone raises it. Keep it.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 4: give the headline a promise the product can prove. Instead of "Plan the week…", name the specific thing a team gets from Tallyboard that it doesn't get from a shared doc or its current tool, so the viewer compares against what they already have and trust (taste).
2. The brand blue is already in the wordmark and the button. Let it also mark the one or two key words of the subhead, such as "free" and "five people", so the offer and the action read as one thread down the page. Use it sparingly so the button keeps its pull (taste).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and button, error messages and when validation fires, and whether the password field has a show-password toggle.
