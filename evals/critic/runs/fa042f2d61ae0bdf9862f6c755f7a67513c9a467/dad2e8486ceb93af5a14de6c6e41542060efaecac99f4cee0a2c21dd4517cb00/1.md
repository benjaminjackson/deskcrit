**First read.** My eye lands on the bold headline, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", and then drops straight to the filled blue "Save changes" button. It reads as a calm, well-built confirmation dialog in a calendar app, for whoever looks after a group's calendar (here, a choir). The trouble starts when my eye moves left along the button row. "Export as file" and "Discard changes" look like the same kind of thing: two quiet blue words. I have to read both to learn that one of them throws my work away.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a desktop web dialog where someone who has edited a shared calendar decides whether to publish the edits, keep working, or drop them.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The question is clear, the primary action is clear, and the craft is clean. The one real problem is the only action that loses work: "Discard changes" is styled exactly like a harmless export option and sits right next to "Keep editing", so it can be hit by mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" looks harmless and sits where a slip lands on it.** In the button row, Discard is plain blue text, the same style as "Export as file", and about 40 px from "Keep editing", which is the safe choice for someone who isn't sure. Two problems follow. Someone scanning the row reads Export and Discard as a pair of minor options, so nothing warns them that one of them is permanent. And someone reaching for "Keep editing" can easily land one button to the left and lose all their rehearsal-time edits. Apple HIG and Material Design both say a destructive action should be set apart from the safe ones and styled as destructive, and Raskin argues that offering undo beats trusting the user to read a warning. Fix: give Discard its own look (red text, or a red outline) and move it away from the safe pair, usually to the far left of the row with clear space before the right-hand group. Even better, add an undo after a discard. **Defect.**
2. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** The dialog asks a yes-or-no question about publishing. Export answers a different question, it's a rare need, and it adds a fourth button for the user to read before acting. It's also the reason Discard ends up hidden among the quiet options. Under Nielsen's "aesthetic and minimalist design" heuristic and his progressive-disclosure guidance, a rare option shouldn't sit inside the main choice. Fix: take Export out of this dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu. That leaves three actions (discard, keep editing, save), which is the convention people already expect. **Convention.**

**Smaller things.**
- Nothing else failed the checklist.

**What's working.**
- **The headline is a real question about a named object.** "Save changes to the shared calendar?" can be answered with the button labels as written ("Save changes", "Discard changes"), so nobody has to translate between the question and the options. Keep the button labels as the verb plus its object.
- **The body line states the consequence for other people.** "...will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar" tells you exactly what publishing means, and that is the one fact that should make someone pause. It's specific ("choir rehearsal times") and not generic.
- **The primary action is clear.** There's one filled button, on the right. "Keep editing" is a lighter outlined button, and the type uses two sizes and two weights with nothing in between. Keep that restraint when you restyle Discard: red text or a red outline is enough, and it should not be filled.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: make the body line also cover the destructive path, for example "Discarding removes your edits for good." That way both outcomes are spelled out before either button is pressed. Right now the copy only describes what saving does. **Taste**: in a dialog whose whole job is to protect a shared calendar, both outcomes deserve a sentence.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button gets keyboard focus first, and what the focus ring looks like. Focus should go to "Keep editing" or "Save changes", never to Discard.
- Whether a dimmed backdrop sits behind the dialog. The capture shows plain white, which may just be how it was captured. Check it in a live browser.
- The grey border on "Keep editing" looks close to the 3:1 minimum (WCAG 1.4.11). The label already marks it as a button, but measure the border colour to make sure.
