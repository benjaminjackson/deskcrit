**First read.** My eye lands on the bold "Library opening hours" title and then runs down a single column of evenly spaced lines. I can see three bold names in it, but not where one branch's hours end and the next begin. It reads as a plain, calm public-library web page. It is tidy but slow to scan, because everything sits at the same gap and every time is spelled out in words.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a library website page where residents check when a branch or the mobile library is open before they go. Hours are plain facts, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** It doesn't quite do its job yet. The type is clean and readable, but the reader can't quickly match a branch to its hours, and several times can be read two ways or are missing. Both problems matter on a page whose only purpose is to stop someone turning up at a closed door.

**What would move it most**
1. **The groups don't show (defect).** Every vertical gap on the page is about 45 px: title to "Main branch", heading to its first line, line to line, and the last Main branch line to "Harbour branch". So "Harbour branch" sits as close to "Saturday, ten to four" above it as to its own hours. The reader gets one list of eight lines, not three branches. Someone scanning for Harbour's Saturday hours can easily read the wrong Saturday, since there are two lines that start "Saturday". Equal gaps erase the groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on proximity). A heading should sit nearer the text it introduces than the text before it (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.2.2). The fix: tighten the space between a heading and its own lines, and between the lines inside a group, to well under the gap between branches. Alternatively, set each branch as a small two-column block (day on the left, hours on the right) so the days line up for comparison.
2. **The times are worded so they can be misread (defect, copy).** "Nine to eight", "ten to six", "ten to four", and "ten to two" have no am/pm and no numerals. "Ten to four" also reads as 3:50, a time of day, as easily as "10 until 4". This is the part people act on, and the wrong reading sends them at the wrong time. Spelled-out words are also slower to scan than figures. The fix is to set the times as figures in one format with a range dash, keeping the same values. Fact needed from the maker: whether each time is am or pm, if the page or brief doesn't state the convention elsewhere.
3. **The mobile library has no times, and closed days go unsaid (defect, copy).** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" tell a resident where to go but not when, so they can't plan a visit. Main branch never says it's closed on Sunday, and Harbour never says it's closed on Sunday or Monday. The reader has to infer that from what's missing. Stating "Closed" for those days removes the guess. Fact needed from the maker: the hours the mobile library stops at each place.

**Smaller things.**
- I see no addresses or holiday exceptions. That may be fine if they're elsewhere on the site, but a hours page is where people look for them.
- The branch headings look slightly smaller than the hours under them. Weight alone separates them, which works, but making them the same size as the body would read as more deliberate (taste).

**What's working.**
- **The type.** It's one plain sans in dark near-black on white, with about 18 px body text and high contrast. There's nothing to fight through, and it suits a public service.
- **The title.** "Library opening hours" is the clear entry point and says exactly what the page is.
- **The restraint.** There are no boxes, icons, or decoration, so once the spacing is fixed the structure will be carried by space alone.
