**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild", runs down the two lines of intro, then jumps across to the blue "Sign up" button in the card on the right. It reads as a news page from a small ferry operator, written for the people who ride it: calm, plain, and tidy. Then the eye drops to the picture and gets very little back. It is a few flat gray and blue blocks.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this page tells regular passengers about the rebuilt landing and gets them to sign up for timetable-change notes. No claim is at stake. The piece reports plain facts, and its one promise ("one short note… stop the notes at any time") is answered in place.

**Verdict.** The page reads cleanly, but the sign-up form, its only call to action, can't do its job: it asks for a name and a landing, never for where to send the note. The picture comes second. It is meant to show the rebuild, and it shows none of it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form has nowhere to send the note** (defect). The "Get timetable changes" card asks for "Your name" and "Home landing", then promises "We send one short note when the timetable changes." Nothing on the page says where that note goes. Someone who signs up gets nothing, or reads the gap as a sign the form is broken and doesn't bother. The name isn't needed to send a timetable note; the address is. This is fine only if the person is already signed in and their address is on file, and nothing on the page shows that. The fix is a labeled contact field for whatever channel the notes use. Consider dropping the name field to keep the form at two fields (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, ch. 2: the screen should let a first-time user build a correct picture of what happens).
2. **The picture doesn't show what the text is about** (defect, and a template tell). The text and caption talk about wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, a kiosk, and ramps that "meet the deck at every tide". The figure is a gray slab, a lighter block, and a blue rounded bar on a blue band. There's no ramp, no tide line, no shelter you can recognize. The caption, "seen from the water at low tide", points at things the viewer can't find. The strongest line on the page, that prams and wheelchairs now roll straight on, has nothing behind it. A wheelchair user is the reader most likely to want to see it. A generic placeholder in the main image slot also makes the page look like anyone's (Sokolovsky, unslop: the visual should come from the product's world). The fix is a real photo or drawing of the north landing that shows the ramp meeting the deck at low tide.
3. **"Home landing" is a blank text box** (convention). A ferry has a short, fixed list of landings. A free-text field makes people recall and spell the name ("North landing"? "North"? "N. Landing"?), and every variant is a mismatch the system has to catch. Make it a picker of the landings (Nielsen, heuristics 5 and 6: constrain inputs; recognition rather than recall).

**Smaller things.**
- Neither field is marked as required or optional. Once the form is fixed, put "(optional)" after any field that isn't required, or mark the required ones with an asterisk and explain it once (Budiu, NN/g).
- Fact needed from the maker: which channel the timetable notes go out by (email, text message, or something else), so the contact field and the helper line can name it.
- The footer, "Ferry timetables are a sample for a fixture image. All names and places are invented.", reads as placeholder copy. It shouldn't ship on a live page (common sense).
- Taste: "Sign up" says less than the heading above it. Using the heading's own words on the button ("Get timetable changes") would tell people what they get when they press it.

**What's working.**
- The left column holds one clean edge at x≈96 from headline to footer. Body text is about 18 px at roughly 66 characters a line with comfortable spacing, so it reads with no effort.
- The type scale is tight and clear: headline, card heading, body, and one small size for labels and captions. Weight does the work between levels, with no near-duplicate sizes.
- The helper line under the button states the cost of signing up and how to get out of it, in plain words. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the two fields and the button, and what happens after "Sign up" (confirmation, errors).
