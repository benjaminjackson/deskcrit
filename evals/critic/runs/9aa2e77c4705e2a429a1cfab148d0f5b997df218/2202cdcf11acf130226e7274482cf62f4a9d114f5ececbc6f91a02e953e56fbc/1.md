**First read.** The eye lands straight on the huge black capitals "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY", then goes up to the small gray "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" line, then down to the two lines of body text, and stops at "thermos." It reads as a web hero or poster announcing free ferry rides at a harbor open day. It feels clean and confident but cold, closer to a software landing page than a morning out on the water. Then the eye falls into a blank lower half with nothing more to find.

**What it's for.** Assumption: an announcement, as a web hero or poster, that gets members of the local public to show up for free ferry rides at the City Harbour open day. No claim is at stake: it's an event notice, and a free ride asks the reader to believe nothing.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy works, but the piece doesn't do its job yet. It tells you what, where, and how often, but never when, so nobody can actually plan to go. The pale, generic styling and the empty lower half make it look unfinished rather than inviting.

**What would move it most**
1. **The date and hours are missing (defect).** "Open Day" and "every half hour" need a day and a start and end time to be usable. Without them, a reader who is keen to go still has to look elsewhere, and most won't. On an event piece, the date is the line people act on and hold you to. Put it in the second level of the hierarchy, next to "north landing", not hidden in small print. Fact needed from the maker: the date of the open day and the first and last ferry times.
2. **The eye path dead-ends, and the page tips into its top-left corner (convention: Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"; *The Power of the Center*).** All the weight sits in the top 57% and the left half. The lower ~45% of the frame is empty, so it looks like content failed to load, not like planned space. The path also ends on "thermos." with no next step: no date block, no way to find the landing, and nothing to do. Give the lower field a job. The details from finding 1 and any way to find the landing that the maker already has could anchor it and finish the path. Or, if this is a hero with content below, crop the frame so the empty band doesn't read as the design.
3. **The color and type tell you nothing about a harbor or a morning (taste, argued; Itten, *The Art of Color*).** Near-black and one cool gray on white, set in a neutral sans, is the look of a hundred product pages. Nothing here says water, boats, early light, or a free day out, and the copy ("Bring a hat and a thermos") is warmer than the styling around it. The open day's whole job is to make people want to come. Bring in a hue or an image that belongs to this harbor, kept to an accent so the headline still leads.
4. **Two stock template moves weaken the headline's voice (template tells, unslop).** The small, gray, widely tracked uppercase line above the headline ("CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY") is the standard landing-page eyebrow. The headline also looks set at an extra-bold, roughly 800 weight. Readers have seen both everywhere, so the piece reads as anyone's. The event name is also the most important fact on the page, and the eyebrow treatment makes it the faintest thing there. Set the event name in sentence case at normal tracking and give it real presence. Then let the headline earn its rank through size rather than the heaviest weight.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "ALL ABOARD THE / MORNING FERRY", which leaves "THE" stranded away from its noun. Break it at the phrase: "ALL ABOARD / THE MORNING FERRY" (Bringhurst, Hochuli; the source is unconfirmed, so this is common sense).
- The headline is tracked wide at display size, so the word shapes come apart and it reads as a set of letters rather than a phrase. Large type wants tighter spacing, not looser (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80; Bringhurst 2.1.1).
- The gap from the eyebrow to the headline (~48 px) is almost the same as the gap from the headline to the body (~53 px). The event name doesn't visibly belong to the headline, so tighten the first gap (Arnheim, proximity).

**What's working.**
- One left edge at about x = 90 holds every block, so the page reads as one clean column. Keep that edge when you add the date and details.
- The scale is clear: about three sizes (~14, ~20, ~110 px) with big jumps between them, so it's never in doubt what to read first.
- The body copy is specific and human. "Free rides every half hour from the north landing. Bring a hat and a thermos." says who, what, and where in plain words, at a comfortable ~60 characters per line.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether this is the top of a longer page, with the date and a map further down, or the whole poster. If it's a web hero, check that the details appear without scrolling at common laptop heights.
