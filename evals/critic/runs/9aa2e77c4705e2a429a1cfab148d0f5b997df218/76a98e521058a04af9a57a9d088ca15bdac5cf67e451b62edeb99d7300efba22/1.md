I'm done with the checklist and deep reads, and am writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "Landing stage rebuild" headline. Next it goes to the blue "Sign up" button, and then to the flat block figure. This reads as a short notice from a ferry or harbor operator, with a sign-up box for timetable alerts. It feels calm, clean, and municipal. A few seconds in, I notice that the form fields, the figure caption, the note under the button, and the footer line can't be read at all.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public page for local ferry users that tells them about the rebuilt landing and asks them to sign up for timetable changes. To sign up, a reader needs to know what each field wants, what they'll get, and how often. The small note under the button seems to cover that, but at its current size nobody can read it.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't do its job. Its one action is a form whose labels and terms are too small to read, so a reader can't tell what they're typing or what they're agreeing to. The figure is a weaker second problem. It shows none of the improvements the text describes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form labels, the note under the button, the figure caption, and the footer line look about 4 px tall, which can't be read.** You can see that something is written above each field ("Your name"? something like "Home landing"?), but you can't make out the letters even at full resolution. A reader sees two empty boxes and a button. They have to guess what goes in each box, and they can't read the line that seems to say what they'll be sent and that they can stop at any time. That's the reassurance someone needs before handing over details. The same goes for the caption under the figure and the footer at the bottom left. This is a **defect**: these labels are far below any readable minimum, and Apple's HIG puts the floor for text at 11 pt. They're also set in light gray, so they look faint as well as tiny (WCAG 1.4.3). The fix: set the field labels at the body text size or close to it, directly above each field. Set the note, caption, and footer as one small caption size, still clearly legible and dark enough to pass 4.5:1. Once the labels are readable, check that the fields ask for what a timetable alert needs, which is somewhere to send it.

2. **The figure doesn't show the rebuild.** The copy names wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, and a kiosk. The figure shows a light-blue sky, a pale gray box, a dark gray slab, and a blue rounded bar on a water band. None of the things the text promises are visible. It reads as a placeholder or wireframe block, which is a stock move from the template tells (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture"). It costs the reader in two ways. The page's only picture adds nothing to the claim that the landing is better now, and it makes the page look unfinished. This is mostly common sense, and I'm marking it **taste**. The fix: show the actual landing, by photo or by a drawing that clearly shows the ramp meeting the deck and the covered seating. Or, if no such picture exists, cut the figure and let the copy carry the page.

3. **The blue rounded bar in the figure looks like a second button.** It matches the Sign up button's color, fill, and corner radius exactly. On a page with one action, a second shape in the button style catches the eye and invites a click that does nothing (Arnheim on grouping by similarity). This is a **defect**, though minor. The fix: draw the boat, or whatever the bar stands for, in a color or shape the interface doesn't use for controls.

**Smaller things.**
- Neither field says whether it's required. Mark both, or mark the optional one (Budiu, NN/g).
- The article and the sign-up card are only loosely connected. The card heading "Get timetable changes" doesn't say why a story about the landing leads to timetable alerts. A short line tying them together would help, using words already on the page.
- The bottom right of the page and the band below the last paragraph are empty. On a page this short that's fine, but the footer floats far from the content it belongs to.

**What's working.**
- The headline clearly dominates: it's large, heavy, and dark, so the eye knows where to start. Keep that contrast.
- The body copy is good: plain, concrete, and written for the reader. "Prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" lands the improvement on the people it helps. Keep this voice.
- There's one primary button, in a single blue, with a large target and dark field borders. The controls are obvious and the palette is calm.
