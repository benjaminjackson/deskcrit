**First read.** The eye lands on the six color blocks, which are the loudest thing on the page. It drifts across them like a paint-swatch card and only then finds "Garden show gallery" at the top left. It reads as a tidy, calm page for browsing a garden show's spots, but it shows no garden, so it feels like a wireframe rather than a gallery.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where visitors to a garden show (or people planning to go) browse its areas and see what each looks like. It's an informational page with no claim at stake.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. A gallery's whole content is its pictures, and every picture here is a flat color block with a pale circle, so a visitor learns nothing about how the Rose arch or the Pond edge looks. The layout under them is sound. The page title is also too weak to head the page, and the first card breaks the pattern the other five set.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures show nothing.** All six tiles share one stand-in: a saturated rectangle with a lighter circle centered on its bottom edge. The circles are just filler. The captions name concrete things ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Reeds and water lilies") and the image above each one shows none of them. Picture and caption are meant to make one idea, and here the caption does all the work. The same move appears on the template-tells list as "empty box or generic picture" (Sokolovsky, unslop): a generic visual makes the page look like anyone's, so the garden's own character never shows. Defect. This is fine only if these are placeholders in a draft, or if real photos failed to load in this capture. If so, check the live page. Fix: put a real photo of each area in each tile, cropped to a shared ratio and treated alike (one color grade, one crop style).
2. **The page title is no bigger than the card titles.** "Garden show gallery" looks about 20 px bold, and "Rose arch" through "Orchard path" look about 22 px bold in the same face. The title meant to name the page sits a step *below* the items it heads. The two sizes are a couple of pixels apart, so they read as one level, or as a mistake. A difference this small doesn't register as a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8; Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1, sizes from a scale). With six equal tiles below, nothing tells the eye where the page starts. Defect. Fix: make the page title clearly larger than the card titles, by a full step on a scale, so the page has one entry point (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). Or step the card titles down, and keep only bold to set them apart from the descriptions.
3. **The first card doesn't match the other five.** Under "Rose arch", the title sits about 2 px higher than "Herb bed" and "Pond edge". Its description also looks slightly smaller and set tighter, so it ends about 4 px higher than its neighbors. The near-miss is enough that the top row's captions don't sit on one baseline. It looks like a separate component or a leftover override, not a choice (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I). Defect, small in pixels but noticeable in a strict grid like this one. Fix: give all six cards one caption style and one spacing.

**Smaller things.**
- Six unrelated saturated hues (brick red, green, steel blue, olive brown, mustard, violet) fill about half the content area with no system behind them. If they're meant to stay as the tiles' backgrounds or category colors, cut them to a small related set (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). If real photos replace them, the issue goes away. This is taste: as it stands, the palette reads as a toy or swatch card more than a garden.
- The circle centered on the bottom edge of every tile is a habit, not a choice. It repeats identically six times and points the eye nowhere. It goes away with real photos.

**What's working.**
- The grid is clean: three equal columns with even gutters, consistent tile proportions, and generous outer margins. Keep it when the photos go in.
- The grouping is right. Each title sits tight under its picture, the description tight under the title, and a much larger gap separates the rows, so each card reads as one unit without a box around it.
- The captions are concrete and short. "Tomatoes in late July" and "Runner beans on canes" say what is there, which is exactly what a visitor scanning the page needs.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are links, and if so, their hover and focus states. Nothing on them signals that they can be clicked.
- Whether the flat color blocks are intended placeholders or images that failed to load in the capture. Check the live page.
- How the three columns reflow on narrower screens.
