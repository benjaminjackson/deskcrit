**First read.** The eye goes straight to the black four-line headline. It fills the top half of the screen, so this reads as an announcement before it reads as a timetable. Then the eye drops to the paragraph and looks for times, and there aren't any. The small gray "Spring timetable" label is the last thing noticed. The page feels calm, clean, and confident, and it is close to empty of the one thing a rider came for.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page for people who take the river ferry and want to know when they can catch it this season. No claim is at stake. It's practical information, and the reader only needs it to be complete and clear.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The page calls itself a "Spring timetable," but nothing on screen tells a rider when a boat leaves. The headline uses the most space on the page to say the least.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no timetable on the timetable page (defect).** The body says the ferry runs "every forty minutes," but not when it starts. A rider can't turn that into a departure, so they can't plan around it. The headline's "by daylight" makes it worse: daylight hours change a lot between spring and early summer. Does the first boat move with sunrise, or keep a fixed time? A rider who guesses wrong waits on the landing or misses the last boat back. If a table of times sits below this screen, the fix is to bring at least the first and last departures up into this view. If it doesn't exist, the page needs one. Fact needed from the maker: the first and last departure times from each landing, and whether they change across the season.
2. **"Spring through early summer" has no dates (defect).** Season boundaries are what a rider acts on and holds the operator to. "Early summer" could mean the start of June or the start of July, so someone planning a trip in late June can't tell if the service will be running. Fact needed from the maker: the start and end dates of this timetable.
3. **The headline outranks the information (defect).** At about 80 px, bold, over four lines, it takes roughly 330 px of an 800 px screen to say the same thing as the label above it. Meanwhile the facts that matter ("every forty minutes," "about twelve minutes") sit in 20 px body text. Every level of the hierarchy is clear (Refactoring UI), but it's in the wrong order. The rider's question is "when," and the loudest element answers "what." Direction: shrink the headline to a short title that fits in one or two lines, and give the departures (item 1) the dominant spot.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Spring timetable" label is meant as the page's heading but names something the page doesn't show. It becomes true once the times are there (WCAG 2.4.6).
- The headline breaks "early / summer," splitting a phrase and leaving "summer" alone on the last line. Rebreak it or shorten it, which item 3 already asks for (Bringhurst 2.4).
- "weather." sits alone on the paragraph's last line, so both blocks end on a one-word line and the right edge steps down raggedly. Change the measure slightly or rebreak so at least two words carry over (Bringhurst 2.4.2; Hochuli).

**What's working.**
- One sharp left edge at x≈120 for the label, headline, and body. The column reads as one calm, ordered unit. Keep that single alignment when you add the times.
- The body copy is plain and specific: "from the north landing to the market quay," "about twelve minutes in calm weather." It names places and gives an honest caveat, which is exactly the voice a timetable needs.
- A restrained palette of dark gray on white with one gray label. It will leave room for the times to be the strongest thing on the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether a timetable sits below this screen. The empty bottom quarter suggests the page ends here, but check in a live browser. Also how the four-line headline reflows on a phone, where it would likely fill the whole first screen.
