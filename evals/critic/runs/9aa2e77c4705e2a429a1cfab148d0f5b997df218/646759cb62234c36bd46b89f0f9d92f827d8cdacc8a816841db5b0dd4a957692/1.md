**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the gray subhead, and stops on the dark green "Get started" button. Then it drifts right and down into a large empty area and finds nothing. This reads as the homepage hero for a group trip planner. It feels calm, neat and quiet, but so sparse that it looks unfinished, as if the main picture never arrived.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing homepage, meant to get someone who organizes group trips to sign up. Tell me if that's wrong. That person needs to see that Wayfold actually beats a group chat, and right now nothing on the page shows it.

**Verdict.** The type and the call-to-action layout are clean and well ordered. But the hero shows nothing: the whole right half and the bottom third are empty. The page asks to replace "a dozen chat threads" and gives the reader nothing to look at that would make that believable.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half of the hero is empty, so the page has no picture and no proof.** About 560 px to the right of the headline and the area below the buttons hold nothing.
   - **What the reader sees:** all the visual weight is in the top-left corner, and the empty side is too big to read as chosen breathing room. It reads like a missing image (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"; Ruder on space that works only when it's set against something dense).
   - **What it costs:** the subhead promises flights, stays and day plans in one place for a group. The reader has to imagine that, and comparing it to the group chat they already trust is left entirely to them.
   - **Template tell:** an empty hero box is a stock move (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"). It makes the page look like any other startup's.
   - **Fix:** fill the right side with a real product view: one group trip with its flights, stays and day plans together, and some sign of the group agreeing. The headline and the picture then make one argument. A stock travel photo would not do this job.
   - **If an image is supposed to be there:** check whether it failed to load in this capture. If it did, this finding moves to what that image shows.
   - Defect.
2. **The headline is the generic half of the message, and the distinctive half sits in the subhead.** Almost any travel planner could say "Plan the whole trip in one place".
   - **What actually sets Wayfold apart:** "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads" is the specific point, and it is set at a third of the headline's size in gray.
   - **What it costs:** a reader who only skims the headline can't tell this from any other itinerary app.
   - **Fix:** bring the group-agreement idea up into the headline, using words already on the page, such as "group", "agree" and "chat threads".
   - **Line break:** the headline also breaks as "trip in / one place", so line 1 ends on a preposition. Rewriting it is the chance to break it at a phrase.
   - Defect (copy).
3. **"Get started" doesn't say what starts.** The main button is the one thing the reader acts on, and it names no first step.
   - **Fix:** name the action in the page's own terms, such as starting a group trip.
   - **What already works:** "See pricing" is specific and correctly styled as the secondary action. Keep it.
   - Defect (copy).

**Smaller things.**
- The headline looks like it's set around weight 800, which is a stock template move (unslop, "Headings at weight 800 or 900"). At this size, a weight one or two steps lighter would still clearly lead the page and look less like everyone else's.

**What's working.**
- **Clear order:** headline, subhead, primary button, secondary button. There's one obvious primary action, and the outlined "See pricing" sits clearly below it.
- **One left edge:** the logo, headline, subhead and buttons all hang from the same 80 px line, so the left column reads as one unit.
- **Restrained color:** a warm off-white background, near-black text and one deep teal accent. It's calm and fits the planning job, and it isn't a template gradient. Keep this palette when you fill the right side.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is a hero image or animation that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check this in a live browser.
