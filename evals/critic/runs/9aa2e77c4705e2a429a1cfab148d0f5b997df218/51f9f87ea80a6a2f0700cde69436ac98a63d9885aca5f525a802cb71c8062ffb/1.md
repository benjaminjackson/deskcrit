**First read.** The eye lands on "Account settings", then runs down four identical white cards to the blue outlined buttons on the right. Then it catches the pale blue "Billing" pill in the sidebar and stops, because the page under it isn't a billing page. It reads as the settings home of a calm, neutral SaaS tool, Tallis Cloud, for a workspace member. It feels orderly and quiet, and a little generic.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the settings overview a signed-in user of the Harbour Studio workspace sees, meant to let them find and change one account setting quickly on a desktop browser. No claim is at stake: the page asks the user to believe nothing, only to find their way.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and well built, but the navigation contradicts the page. The sidebar says you're in Billing while the content is a general overview, and the overview's sections don't match the sidebar's. A user can't tell where they are or where a setting lives.

**What would move it most**
1. **The sidebar marks the wrong place.** "Billing" in the left nav has the selected treatment (blue-tinted pill, bold blue text). The main area has the heading "Account settings" and shows Profile, Notifications, Billing and Sessions side by side. The user gets two answers to "where am I?" They'll either think the Billing click failed or wonder whether the cards are the billing page. Showing the current location is the point of the selected state (Nielsen heuristic 1; NN/g, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", guideline 5), and the page heading should name the same place the nav marks (WCAG 2.4.6). Defect. Fix: make the nav and the heading agree. If this is the overview, either give it its own nav entry and highlight that, or highlight nothing. If the user really chose Billing, show the Billing page under a "Billing" heading. If the highlight is a leftover from hover or capture, check it in a live build.
2. **The overview and the sidebar describe two different structures.** The sidebar lists six sections: Profile, Notifications, Billing, Members, Integrations and Security. The cards show four, and one of them, "Sessions", isn't in the sidebar at all. A user who sees "Sign out of devices you no longer use" here and later wants it again has to guess whether it lives under Security. Members, Integrations and Security have no card, so the overview looks complete when it isn't. Users build their model of the product from its grouping and names (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, ch. 2), and here the two lists teach different models. Defect. Fix: give each section one name used everywhere. Either rename the card to match its nav home or add Sessions to the nav. Then either give every nav section a card or make it clear the cards are a chosen few.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing card copy: "Your plan renews on 1 March" has no year, no plan name and no amount. That's the line someone opens Billing to check, and they'll hold you to it. Fact needed from the maker: the plan name and renewal amount to show on this card.
- "View invoices" is the only billing action. If changing the plan or payment method lives elsewhere, the card gives no hint. Consider a label that names where the button really goes (taste).
- "Workspace: Harbour Studio" in the top right reads as a static label. If users can belong to several workspaces, it doesn't look like a switcher (taste, depends on the product).

**What's working.**
- The grid is tight. The wordmark and the sidebar pill share a left edge. The card column's right edge lines up with the workspace label. "Profile" in the nav sits level with the "Account settings" heading. Keep these alignments when you fix the nav.
- Each card pairs a plain title with a one-line description that says what the setting does for the user ("Choose which updates reach your inbox and which stay inside the app"). That copy lands on the reader, so keep it.
- All four actions are equal outlined buttons with a specific verb (Edit, Manage, View invoices, Review). On an overview of peer settings, having no primary button is the right call.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus indicators on the nav items and buttons, and whether the Billing highlight is a hover or focus state caught in the capture rather than the selected state. Check in a live browser.
