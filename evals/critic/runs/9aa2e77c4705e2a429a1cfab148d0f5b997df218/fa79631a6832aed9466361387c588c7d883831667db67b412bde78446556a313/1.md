**Verdict.** It does its job: there's a clear place to start, three distinct type levels, and short sections a beginner can scan. The one real flaw is that the text column is too wide. It's minor at this length, but it would cost more if the page grew.

**What would move it most**

Rake the surface level" is a single line. That's well past the 45 to 75 characters Bringhurst gives as comfortable (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). Here every paragraph is only two lines, so the reader barely feels it. But each line return is a long trip back to the left edge, and on a fuller page the reader would start losing their place. Narrow the column to about 60 to 70 characters at the current size (around 600 to 650 px). The 330 px of empty space on the right already shows the page doesn't need the width. *Convention.*

- **The type scale.** I count three sizes with big steps between them: the title at about 52 px, headings at about 30 px and body at about 17 px. The headings also differ from the body in weight (bold) and color (blue), so every level is obvious at a glance. Keep it at three levels if the page grows; don't add an in-between size.
