**First read.** My eye lands on "Changing your pickup time" because it comes first, not because it matters most: all four bold headings are the same size, so I run down them like a list. It reads as the help or FAQ section of a small bakery's ordering site. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, close to a printed notice at the counter.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming it's a help page for customers who already have a bakery pickup order and need a quick answer about changing, collecting, allergens or cancelling. The page states facts and asks the reader to believe nothing, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The layout is good: it scans easily and the grouping is right. What lets it down is the copy. The two lines people will act on, the cancellation deadline and what a stand-in collector must bring, can each be read two ways, and a wrong reading means a lost order or someone turned away at the counter.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways (defect).** Under "Cancelling an order", "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" could mean 24 hours before the chosen pickup slot, or any time before the end of the previous day. Someone who reads it the loose way cancels at 9 pm the night before a 7 am pickup and finds it's too late, because "the bread has already been baked for you." The paragraph also never says *how* to cancel, while the pickup-time paragraph above it does ("Open your order…"). The line needs a fixed cutoff stated against the pickup time. Fact needed from the maker: the exact cancellation cutoff (hours before the slot, or a clock time on the day before) and where the customer cancels.
2. **"Along with a name" doesn't say whose name (defect).** Under "Collecting for someone else", the person collecting will read "a name" as their own name, or the orderer's, or either. If staff check the orderer's name and the collector gives their own, the collector is turned away at the counter. The fix is to say whose name, for example "your name" if that's what the bakery checks. Fact needed from the maker: whose name staff check at collection.
3. **The allergen answer only works once you're in the shop (defect, common sense).** "Every loaf has a card by the till" tells someone ordering online that they can't see ingredients until they arrive to collect bread they've already paid for. Someone with an allergy is the reader who most needs to know before ordering, and this section leaves them nowhere to look. The fix direction is to say where ingredients can be checked before ordering, if anywhere. Fact needed from the maker: whether ingredients can be seen online or before ordering, and where.
4. **The page has no title, so the four questions have no frame (convention).** If this screenshot is the whole page, four headings of equal size and weight compete to be seen first (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). Nothing above them says this is help for pickup orders or whose bakery it is, so a visitor who arrives from search has to work that out from the questions (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Add one clearly larger title above the list and keep the four headings as they are. Wording that is already a category, such as "Pickup orders", would do without new content.

**Smaller things.**
- The body column runs about 820 px, so the one-line answers ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") reach about 95 characters, well past Bringhurst's 45 to 75 (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). Narrow the column to about 600 to 640 px at this size. The empty right band will take the space.
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the Allergies paragraph (Bringhurst 2.4.2). The narrower column will reflow it; otherwise keep the last two words together.
- "Open your order" doesn't say where the order is (an email, an account page). Make it a link, or name the place. Fact needed from the maker: where customers open their order.

**What's working.**
- **The spacing between and within sections.** Each heading sits about 8 px above its answer and about 45 px below the previous answer, so every question and answer reads as one unit without any rules or boxes. Keep those ratios when you add a title.
- **Questions phrased as headings.** "Changing your pickup time" and "Collecting for someone else" match what a customer is trying to do, so scanning the headings finds the answer.
- **Restraint.** One family, two sizes, dark gray on white, nothing decorative. It's right for a help page and it reads like the bakery talking, not a template.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, and whether "Open your order" is a link anywhere on the live page.
