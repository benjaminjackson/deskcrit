**First read.** The eye lands on the big dark headline, "Plan the whole trip in one place," drops to the gray subhead, then to the two green buttons. Then it goes to the right half of the screen and finds nothing. It reads as a landing page for a trip-planning app. It feels calm and tidy, but also unfinished, like a hero section waiting for its picture.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing site for a group trip-planning app, aimed at the person who ends up organizing a trip for friends or family, and meant to get them to sign up. To sign up, that person has to believe that flights, stays, and day plans really live together here and that a group can agree inside the app. Nothing on the screen shows them that.

**Verdict.** The type and the left column are clean and well ordered, but the page doesn't do its job yet. It asks the visitor to take its main promise on trust, with half the screen empty where the proof should be, and then it offers two buttons that say the same thing.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing shows the product, and the right half of the screen is empty (defect).** The subhead promises that Wayfold "keeps flights, stays, and day plans together so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads." That's a claim the visitor doesn't already believe, and the page gives no proof of it: no screen, no example trip, nothing that shows the group agreeing. The visitor has to imagine the product. Meanwhile everything sits in the top-left, and the right half and bottom third are bare. The page looks tipped to the left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), and the empty space looks left over rather than chosen. This is also a common template look: an empty hero area where the product should be (unslop, Sokolovsky). The fix is to fill the right side with a view of the product doing what the subhead says: the three kinds of plans side by side, and some sign of the group making a choice together. Then the picture both balances the page and supports the claim. If an image is supposed to be there and just didn't load in this capture, check it in a live browser. As captured, it's a defect.
2. **"Get started" and "Start now" are the same button twice (defect).** They sit side by side with different styles but labels that mean the same thing. The visitor stops to wonder what the difference is, and the outline button suggests a second path that doesn't exist. That weakens the primary action (Nielsen heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*), and neither label says where the button goes (WCAG 2.4.4). Keep one button for signing up and either remove the other or give it a different job. The nav already offers "How it works," which is a natural second action for someone who isn't ready yet. Also consider naming the action in the page's own words, such as "Plan a trip," instead of a generic "Get started."
3. **The headline is generic, and the group angle is hidden in the subhead (taste, on the copy).** Lots of travel apps could use "Plan the whole trip in one place." What sets this one apart is that *a group* can *agree* on a trip without endless chat threads, and that sits in the smaller gray text that many visitors will skim past. Move the group point up into the headline using words already on the page, or open the subhead with it, so a first glance tells the visitor what's different here.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place," which splits the phrase "in one place." Move "in" down to the second line so the break falls at the phrase boundary.

**What's working.**
- **The left column holds one edge.** The logo, headline, subhead, and buttons all start on the same left line, and the nav ends cleanly on the right margin. Keep that grid when you add the visual.
- **The type scale is clear.** There are four sizes with big steps between them: headline, subhead, nav and button text, and logo. Each size has an obvious job, and the subhead runs about 60 characters a line, a comfortable length to read.
- **The palette is small and holds together.** Warm cream, dark green-gray text, and one deep green for the logo and actions. It fits a calm planning tool and makes the primary button easy to spot.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the buttons and nav links.
