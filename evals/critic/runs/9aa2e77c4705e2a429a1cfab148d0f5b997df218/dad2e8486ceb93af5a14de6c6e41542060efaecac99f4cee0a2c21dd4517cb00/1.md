**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question "Save changes to the shared calendar?", reads the one-line consequence under it, and jumps to the solid blue "Save changes" button. Then it sweeps back left across a row of four actions to work out what the other three do. It reads as a calm, well-made confirmation dialog in a calendar or scheduling app. The only moment of doubt is in the action row.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the dialog someone sees when leaving after editing a shared calendar, here a choir's rehearsal schedule. It has to let them publish, keep working, or throw away their edits, without a wrong click. No claim is at stake. It's a plain decision.

**Verdict.** This is a good dialog with one real flaw. The question, the consequence line and the primary button all agree. But the action row puts a destructive "Discard changes" in the middle, styled exactly like an unrelated "Export as file", so the choice that loses work looks like the safest one in the row.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" is not set apart from the safe actions.** (Defect.) It sits second in the row, about 16 px from "Keep editing". It has the same plain blue link style as "Export as file", which is harmless. Someone heading for "Keep editing", or tabbing through quickly, can hit Discard. Nothing about it says that this one throws away the new rehearsal times. Apple HIG ("Alerts", "Buttons") and Material ("Dialogs") both call for destructive actions to be set apart from the safe ones and styled as destructive. Because Discard and Export look the same and sit as a pair, they also read as one group of minor options (Arnheim on grouping by similarity), when one of them is the most costly choice here. Fix: move Discard away from the other actions, usually to the far left edge of the row on its own, and give it a destructive treatment such as red text. Keep "Save changes" as the only filled button. Its label, "Discard changes", already names the outcome, so keep it.
2. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** (Convention: Nielsen, progressive disclosure.) The question is save or not. Export is a different task, and it adds a fourth choice to a dialog that needs three. Its link style also makes it look like Discard's twin, which is part of why Discard doesn't stand out. Fix: take Export out of this dialog and leave it where export normally lives in the calendar. If it truly has to be offered at the exit, put it behind a quieter control away from the action row. The row then becomes Discard alone on one side and Keep editing / Save on the other.

**Smaller things.**
- The left end of the action row: the text "Export as file" starts about 34 px right of the heading and body's left edge. The row sits nearly flush left without actually being flush left, so it looks like a near-miss. Once Discard moves to the far left, set its text on the heading's left edge.

**What's working.**
- The copy. The heading is a direct question, and the primary button answers it in the same words ("Save changes"). The body line names the specific consequence: the new choir rehearsal times will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar. That is exactly what someone needs to know before publishing. Keep all of it.
- Clear hierarchy. One filled button, one outlined, the rest as text. Large bold heading, plain body, generous even padding. The eye knows where to start and where to finish.
- "Keep editing" is a clear, named way out. It's much better than a bare "Cancel", which could mean cancel the edits or cancel the dialog.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button has default focus and what Enter does. Enter must not trigger Discard.
- The page behind is blank white with no dimming overlay. That may just be how it was captured; check in the live app that the dialog sits over a dimmed calendar.
