**What would move it most**
1. **The headline breaks inside its phrases.** Line 1 ends on "their" and leaves "fields" on line 2. Line 2 ends on "and the" and pushes "birds" to line 3. The second break does the most harm. "And the birds that came back" is the story's turn and its payoff, and the reader meets "and the" at the end of a line, has to carry it down, and gets "birds" late. Breaks that fall at phrase boundaries let each line land as a unit: "The farmers who are letting their fields / flood on purpose, / and the birds that came back", or something close to it. At this size, and for the one line everyone reads, that matters. The usual fix is to balance the headline's wrapping (CSS `text-wrap: balance`, or tune the measure) and check where the phrases fall at each width. This is a convention. Bringhurst and Hochuli are the usual sources, but this entry has no confirmed citation for headline breaks, so treat it as common sense.
