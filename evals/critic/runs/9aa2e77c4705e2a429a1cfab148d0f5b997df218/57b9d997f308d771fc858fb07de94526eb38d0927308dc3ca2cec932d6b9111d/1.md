I've finished the checklist and checked the entries for the few weak spots. Here is the critique.

**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Planning a vegetable bed," then down a calm column of three subheads, each with a short paragraph under it. It reads at once as a beginner's how-to page for a home garden. It feels quiet, plain and trustworthy, like a well-kept notebook. Nothing competes with the text.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web article or help page for first-time home gardeners, read on a laptop while planning, and meant to be skimmed and then followed step by step. It gives instructions and asks the reader to believe nothing they would resist, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** This works. The hierarchy is clear, the grouping is right and the copy is unusually good. The one real cost is the measure: the paragraph lines run too long for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraph lines are too long.** Every paragraph runs about 90 characters a line, for example "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level". On the way back to the left edge the eye has to search for the start of the next line. That matters most on a page people read in short glances between tasks. Bringhurst gives 45 to 75 characters as the comfortable range for a single column, with 66 as the ideal (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). Narrow the text column to about 66 characters at this size. The headings can stay where they are. This is a convention whose breach costs the reader something here, so it counts as a defect.

**Smaller things.**
- The section heads ("Preparing the soil," "Choosing what to plant," "Watering") differ from the body only in size: same weight, same near-black. They read well enough because the size step is clear, but a weight change would let you shrink them a little and still mark the sections (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9). This is a convention, and it costs little here.
- "last year's" uses a straight apostrophe. Set a curly one. The approved source is unverified on this point, so this is common sense rather than a rule.

**What's working.**
- **Grouping by space.** Each heading sits about 12 px above its paragraph and about 40 px below the paragraph before it, so each section reads as one unit without rules or boxes. Keep that ratio if you change the measure.
- **A clean three-step type scale.** Title (~56 px), heads (~32 px) and body (~18 px) are spaced far enough apart that each size has one obvious job. Don't add a fourth size between them.
- **The copy.** Every line says who does what and when: "Water deeply in the morning, twice a week, rather than a little every day." Concrete numbers ("six hours of sun," "a week") and plain verbs give a beginner something they can act on. Leave it alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the column narrows and the type holds up on a phone. Check the line length and heading wraps at about 375 px wide.
