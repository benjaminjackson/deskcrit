**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers across the top, then drops to the blue line climbing across the chart. It reads as a marketing analytics overview for someone who checks traffic and sign-ups each week. It feels calm, clean and trustworthy: everything is green and going up. That trust is the problem, because two of the numbers don't agree with the others.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard for a marketing or growth team, built to show at a glance how traffic and sign-ups are doing and which channel brings the visits. The numbers ask for no belief beyond being right, so no claim is at stake, but they do have to agree with each other.

**Verdict.** The layout and the type are solid, but the piece fails its one job: it reports figures that contradict each other. The conversion card says up when its own inputs say down, and the chart is missing about a quarter of the visits. Anyone who checks once will stop trusting the whole screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The Conversion rate card shows a gain that the other two cards rule out (defect).**
   - Visits are "+6.1%" and sign-ups only "+2.4%". Sign-ups grew more slowly than visits, so conversion must have fallen.
   - Working back from the cards: 184,220 ÷ 1.061 ≈ 173,600 visits and 3,912 ÷ 1.024 ≈ 3,820 sign-ups in the previous period. That's about 2.20% then against 2.12% now, a drop of about 0.08 pts.
   - The card says "+0.1 pts vs previous 12 weeks" and colors it green. The reader comes away thinking conversion improved when it got slightly worse. That is the one metric that tells them whether the extra traffic is worth anything.
   - Fix direction: compute the delta from the same two periods as the other cards, and show a fall as a fall, in its own color. Check the sign and rounding logic behind all three deltas, not only this one.
   - Fact needed from the maker: the previous-period conversion rate and how the delta is calculated.
2. **The "Visits by channel" chart doesn't add up to the Visits total (defect).**
   - Read off the chart, the weekly points sum to about 71k for Search, 44k for Social and 23k for Email: roughly 138k over the 12 weeks.
   - The card directly above says 184,220. About 46k visits, around a quarter, are not in the chart. A chart titled "by channel" with three series makes those three look like the whole.
   - A reader who sees the gap either distrusts both numbers, or misreads the mix and over-credits Search and Social.
   - Fix direction: show the missing channels as their own series or as one muted gray "Other" line, or say plainly in the chart's title or a note that only three channels are shown.
   - Fact needed from the maker: which channels make up the remaining ~46k visits, or whether the two figures come from different sources.
3. **The chart doesn't say what its y-axis counts (convention: Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*; Few).**
   - "0–8k" doesn't say per what. Given the totals it is presumably visits per week, but the reader has to work that out, and the Visits card next to it is a 12-week total.
   - That leaves the reader comparing a weekly figure with a 12-week one without being told.
   - Fix direction: state the unit and the time step in the chart's title or on the axis.
   - Fact needed from the maker: the unit of each point (visits per week, or another interval).

**Smaller things.**
- Legend above the chart: the reader has to match a color in the legend to a line in the plot. Label each line at its right end instead (Tufte; Wilke). Put the name where the line ends and drop the legend.
- "Visits by channel" is a label, not a finding. A title that says what the reader should notice, using only facts the chart shows, would save a read. This is taste.
- "Last 12 weeks", top right: if this changes the date range, it looks like plain text. Give it a control shape, such as a border or a chevron. If it is only a label, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- Every headline number has its comparison right under it, in the same place and the same wording on all three cards. That pattern is the right one to keep; only the delta logic behind it needs fixing.
- The type scale is tight and clear:
  - one heading size ("Overview")
  - one big-number size
  - one label size
  - one small size for deltas and axis ticks

  Weight and color do the rest, so nothing collides.
- The chart is honest in its form. It uses lines for change over time, a y-axis that starts at zero, light gridlines and no decoration. The legend order matches the line order from top to bottom, and the lines never cross, so the series stay easy to follow.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive.
- Whether the chart has hover values. Those would make the unit problem less serious but wouldn't fix it.
- The chart card sits about 17 px from the bottom edge, while the side margins are about 48 px. That's probably just where the capture was cut; check in a browser.
