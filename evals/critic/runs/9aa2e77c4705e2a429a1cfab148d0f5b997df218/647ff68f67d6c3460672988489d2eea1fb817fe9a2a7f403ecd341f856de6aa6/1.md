**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers", then drops into three short paragraphs of calm gray-black text in a left column. This is plainly a service page for people taking a river ferry. It feels quiet, plain, and trustworthy, but a bit like a draft: the text fills the top-left of the frame and the rest is blank.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that tells ferry riders when and how to catch the boat, read by someone deciding when to get to the landing. It gives plain facts and asks the reader to believe nothing, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The page is cleanly set and easy to read, but it doesn't do its main job. A passenger can't plan a trip from it, because none of the times they need to act on are given as times. That gap matters more than anything in the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times a passenger acts on are vague words instead of times** (defect).
   - **Where:** "Between early morning and late evening" and "every forty minutes" in the first paragraph, and "In winter" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" in the third.
   - **What the reader meets:** They are left guessing what time to arrive, which months count as winter, and when the last boat leaves.
   - **Two readings:** "Leaves the quay before dusk" can mean the last boat leaves at dusk, or at some unstated time before it. A rider who takes the first reading and turns up at dusk may be stranded on the wrong bank on a winter evening.
   - **Which way it runs:** "Runs from the north landing to the market quay" also doesn't say whether the boat comes back, or from which landing "the quay" in the third paragraph means.
   - **Fix:** Replace each vague time with a clock time or a date range, and name the landing for each departure.
   - Fact needed from the maker: the first and last departure times from each landing in the regular timetable, the dates the winter timetable runs, the last winter departure time, and whether the ferry runs in both directions.
2. **The timetable is split in two and nothing can be scanned** (common sense, backed by Krug's point that pages are scanned, not read).
   - **Where:** The regular timetable is in paragraph 1, the winter timetable is in paragraph 3, and the bicycles paragraph sits between them.
   - **What the reader meets:** Someone checking "when's the next boat?" reads all three paragraphs to put the timetable together. Even then they can't tell at a glance which rule applies today.
   - **Fix:** Put both timetables together and first. Set the bicycle rules apart below them, under short headings or as a small table of departures, so a rider can find the timetable and the bike rule separately.
   - **Copy edit:** Moving "In winter the timetable thins to one boat each hour" straight after the first paragraph is already most of it.

**What's working.**
- **The setting reads easily.** The column keeps lines to about 65 to 72 characters, the line spacing is comfortable, and the dark text on white is high contrast. Keep this column width when you add structure.
- **The hierarchy has two clear levels.** One bold heading and one body size (Bringhurst's "few sizes, big steps"). Adding subheadings should add one level, not several new sizes.
- **The bicycles paragraph is the model for the rest.** It says who does what and when: riders wheel their bikes on after foot passengers board and lock them to the rail. The timetable copy needs that same plainness.
