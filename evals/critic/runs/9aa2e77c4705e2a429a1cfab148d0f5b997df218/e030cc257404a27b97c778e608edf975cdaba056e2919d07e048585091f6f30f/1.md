**First read.** The eye lands on "See where every dollar goes this month", big and white on navy, then drops to the subhead and the white "Try it free" button. From there it slides across to the right half of the navy band, and nothing is there. It reads as a budgeting app for individuals or households. It looks clean, calm and trustworthy, but sparse, more like a wireframe than a finished pitch.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage of a consumer budgeting app, and its job is to get someone who manages a personal or household budget to start a free trial and connect their bank accounts. That reader has to see the product working (the categories, the limits, the heads-up alert) and get a reason to trust a new app with their account logins. They see neither: the only reassurance is the maker's own word under "Read-only access", and the product never appears.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound and the type is well set, but the page doesn't make its case yet. The biggest problem is the empty right half of the hero: it's the spot where the reader should see the product, and it holds nothing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no visual, so the product is never shown.** This is a defect. About 40% of the navy band, to the right of the headline, is empty. That costs the page two things. First, balance: all the weight sits in the left column, and nothing on the right answers it, so the band looks unfinished rather than deliberately spare (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Second, and more important, the reader is asked to "connect your accounts, set a limit for each category, and get a heads-up" without ever seeing what a category, a limit or a heads-up looks like. An empty hero where a product preview should sit is also a common template tell (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"): it makes the page look like an unfilled starter layout. Fix: put a real product view in that space, such as a category list with its limits or the heads-up alert itself. It should pair with the headline by showing "where every dollar goes", not by repeating the words.
2. **The page asks for trust it doesn't earn.** This is a defect. The reader is asked to accept that they should "Connect your accounts" to an app they've never heard of. The objection they'll surely raise is "is it safe to hand this my bank login, and does it actually work?" The page's only answer is the sentence "The app can see balances and history but can never move money." That's a good line, but it's the maker's word. Nothing shows how the connection works, who stands behind the app, or that anyone uses it. The fix needs that kind of proof, placed near the ask, and the facts have to come from the maker. Fact needed from the maker: how accounts are connected (provider or method) and any proof of security or of real users the product can show.
3. **"Try it free" doesn't tell the reader what they're committing to.** This is a defect on the line the reader acts on. "Free" can mean free forever, a free tier, or a trial that ends and starts charging. A reader who guesses wrong either hesitates or feels misled later. Fix: say what the free part is, next to the button or in its label. Fact needed from the maker: what "free" covers (a trial length, a free tier, and whether a card is required).

**Smaller things.**
- The headline looks extra-bold (around 800). Combined with the tight tracking, it's a common template move. A bold (700) weight would still dominate at this size, so measure the weight and step it down if it's 800 or more.

**What's working.**
- **The copy speaks to the reader.** The headline names a concrete outcome, and the subhead lists three plain steps ("connect… set a limit… get a heads-up before you go over"), each with its verb.
- **The feature row answers real worries in plain terms.** "Read-only access", "can never move money" and "fix any of them in one tap" each end on what they mean for the reader, not on the feature. Keep that voice when you add proof.
- **The hierarchy and grid are clean.** One dominant headline and four clear type levels in one family. Everything hangs on one 64 px left edge, and the three feature columns are evenly spaced. One filled primary button sits next to one outlined secondary.
