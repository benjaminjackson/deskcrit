**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board", then drops into three paragraphs set entirely in capitals. It reads as a plain, calm passenger-info page for a river ferry. The calm goes once you start reading: the capitals make it sound like a posted warning, and the eye works its way through it letter by letter instead of word by word.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page telling ferry passengers the route, how often boats run, the bicycle rules and the winter timetable before they travel. It asks for no belief, just plain facts, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the hierarchy works. But the body text is all capitals, which makes the one part people come to read the hardest part to read. That single choice is the main thing in the way. After it, the copy leaves passengers without the times they need to plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The three body paragraphs are all capitals** (the paragraphs from "THE RIVER FERRY RUNS…" down to "…AT EACH LANDING."). Capitals all have the same height and rectangle shape, so words lose the outlines a reader recognizes them by. The reader has to spell their way through about 70 characters a line, across seven lines. Capitals also shout: a friendly "Before you board" heading sits over text that reads like a warning notice. Readers with low vision or dyslexia pay the most. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says don't capitalize whole paragraphs. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) says text in capitals is especially hard to read. Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for short labels like "PASSENGER NOTES". Defect.
2. **The service times are too vague to plan around.** "Between early morning and late evening" is the line passengers will act on, and it can be read many ways. Someone who reads "late evening" as 11 pm and finds the last boat left at 8 pm is stranded on the wrong bank. "In winter" has the same problem: a passenger can't tell whether their travel date gets the hourly timetable. The page also sends readers to a printed timetable "in the shelter at each landing", which they can't see from where they're reading this page. Fact needed from the maker: the first and last sailing times, and the dates the winter timetable starts and ends. Defect.

**What's working.**
- There's one clear entry point. The heading is the only large, bold element. It has a quieter gray uppercase label above it and plenty of space below it, so the eye knows where to start. Keep that contrast in size and weight when the body goes to mixed case.
- The text is broken into short, single-topic paragraphs (route and crossing time, bicycles, winter). They're split by space alone, flush left, with lines in a comfortable 65 to 75 character range. Keep that structure.
- The palette is restrained: one near-black, one mid-gray for the label, and white. It suits a practical notice and doesn't compete with the words.
