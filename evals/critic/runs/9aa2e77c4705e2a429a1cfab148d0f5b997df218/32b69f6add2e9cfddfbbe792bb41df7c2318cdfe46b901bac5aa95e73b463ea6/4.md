**First read.** The eye lands first on the three big numbers across the top, starting with 184,220. It then drops into the large chart and follows the blue line upward. It reads as a web-analytics overview for a growth or marketing team, and it feels calm and tidy. Then the chart stops you: it shows three colored lines and nothing anywhere says which channel each one is, how many visits they stand for, or which weeks they cover.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard where a marketing or growth team checks traffic and sign-ups over the last 12 weeks and sees which channels drive them. It reports measured numbers, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job, but the chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, can't be read. It promises "Visits by channel" and names no channel, gives no values and gives no dates, so all the viewer can take from it is "three things went up, roughly."

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no series labels, no value axis and no time axis.** You can't read the "Visits by channel" panel at all. The three lines (blue, orange, green) have no legend and no labels. The gridlines have no numbers, so the viewer can't tell whether the blue line means 10,000 visits or 100,000, or whether the gaps between lines are big or small. The x-axis has no week labels, so the dip in the blue line around the middle can't be tied to a date. The empty band under the baseline is where these labels should sit. This is a **defect**: the viewer can't answer the one question the panel's title asks, which channel brings the visits. Tufte (*The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2) and Few require every chart to label its axes, units and series. Wilke and Tufte favor labels placed on the data over a legend. Colors are also the only thing that tells the lines apart, which fails WCAG 1.4.1. Orange and green are close in lightness, so a viewer with red-green color blindness could mix them up even if a legend were added. Fix: put the channel name at the right end of each line, add value labels on the gridlines and week labels along the bottom, and use the empty band under the axis for them.
2. **The chart needs data only you have.** The fix above can't be written from what's on the screen. Fact needed from the maker: the channel name for each of the three lines, the visit counts the gridlines stand for, and the week dates on the time axis.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Overview" page heading looks about 24 px and the KPI numbers about 28 px. They're bold sizes only a few points apart, so they read as near-equals rather than two levels. Shrink the heading or enlarge the numbers so one clearly outranks the other (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- The chart title "Visits by channel" names the topic but not the finding. A title that states what the lines show would save the viewer from working it out. This is taste, and the wording has to wait until the lines are labeled.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI card carries a comparison ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). The viewer gets direction as well as size, so they can tell good from bad without remembering last quarter. Keep that pattern.
- The hierarchy inside each card is right: a small gray label, a big bold number, a small green delta. The number wins at once and the label never competes with it.
- Everything fits on one screen, with one clean grid, even gutters, and a quiet gray ground with white cards. Nothing competes with the data.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- I can't tell whether "Last 12 weeks" at the top right is a date-range control or just a label. If it's the control, it looks like plain text and needs a button or dropdown style to look clickable. Check in the live product.
- Whether hovering the chart shows a tooltip with values. Even if it does, the chart still needs its labels, because a static glance should answer "which channel."
