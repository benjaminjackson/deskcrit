**First read.** The eye lands first on the three big numbers (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%), reads across them, and drops to three colored lines that all climb gently. It reads as a web-analytics overview for a growth or marketing team, and it feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. The trouble starts at the chart: you look for which line is which, and nothing on the page tells you.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is an internal dashboard where a marketing or growth team checks traffic, sign-ups and conversion over the last 12 weeks, and sees which channels drive visits. It shows measured numbers to people who already trust the data, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The KPI row works. The chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, can't be read: it has no channel names, no y-axis values and no dates. It shows that three somethings went up by some amount, and that's all. Fixing the chart's labels is the one change that matters.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Visits by channel" doesn't name the channels and has no scale (defect).** The panel shows a blue, an orange and a green line, but there's no legend, no direct labels, no y-axis values and no week labels on the x-axis. The viewer can't tell which channel is growing, how many visits a line stands for, or which week the dip in the blue line (about two-thirds of the way across) or the orange peaks fall in. The four faint gridlines are scale lines with no numbers on them, so they do nothing. Without values the viewer also can't tell whether the y-axis starts at zero, so the gaps between the lines may look much bigger or smaller than they are. Tufte (*Visual Display of Quantitative Information*) and Few both require labeled axes with units, and Tufte and Wilke prefer labels placed on the data. The fix:
   - Name each line at its right-hand end, in its own color, so the names also cover what the legend would.
   - Add values to the y gridlines and week labels along the x-axis.

   The empty band under the baseline and the gutter left of the plot look like space set aside for exactly these labels. If the labels exist in the build and failed to render in this capture, check that first. As the image stands, it's a defect.
2. **The series are told apart by hue alone (convention, WCAG 1.4.1).** Even with a legend, blue/orange/green with no label at the line is a lookup task. For viewers with red-green color blindness, the orange and green lines get close in hue and are separated only by position. Direct labels at the line ends (finding 1) fix this too. That's another reason to choose them over a legend.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart title "Visits by channel" names a topic, not a finding. Stating what changed (which channel led the growth) would let the viewer act without studying the lines. This is taste, and it only works once the lines are labeled.
- The text inside the chart panel starts about 2 px to the right of the text in the KPI cards (x≈71 vs x≈69). It looks like a near-miss, not a choice. Use the same inner padding for both kinds of card.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards have a clean three-level structure: a quiet gray label, a large heavy number, and a small green change line. You get the figure at a glance and the context on a second look. Keep it.
- Every headline number comes with a comparison ("vs previous 12 weeks"). The conversion rate correctly uses "pts", not "%", for its change. That's a careful detail worth keeping.
- The palette stays restrained: neutral grays, with color only on the data and the deltas. When the lines are labeled, they'll be the clear focus of the panel.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- Whether the chart's axis labels are missing from the design or just didn't render in this capture. Check in a live browser.
