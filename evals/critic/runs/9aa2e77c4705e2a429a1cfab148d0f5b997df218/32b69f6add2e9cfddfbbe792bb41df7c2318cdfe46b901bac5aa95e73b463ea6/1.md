**First read.** The eye lands on "Overview", runs across the three big numbers (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%), then drops into the chart, which takes up about 60% of the screen. There it finds three colored lines and nothing that says what they are. It reads as a calm, tidy internal web-analytics dashboard for a marketing or growth team. The top third feels finished and the chart feels like a draft.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal dashboard where a team checks traffic and sign-up performance over the last 12 weeks and decides which acquisition channel to look at. It reports measured figures, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The stat row does its job, but the chart doesn't. "Visits by channel" has no channel names, no values and no dates, so the biggest element on the screen tells the reader only that three things went up. That one gap decides whether this screen is useful.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read: there are no series labels, no y-axis values and no x-axis dates** (defect). The blue, orange and green lines under "Visits by channel" aren't named anywhere, so a reader can't say which channel leads, which is noisy, or which grew. The four pale gridlines at about y=364–604 have no numbers, so you can't read a value or see how far apart the lines are. The twelve points along the bottom have no week labels, so you can't tie a dip (the blue drop near the 8th point, the orange zigzag) to a date. Tufte and Few both require labeled axes with units, and with no labels at all the chart is decoration. Fix: name each line at its right end in its own color (Tufte and Wilke: label directly, not with a legend). Put values on the gridlines and week dates along the bottom axis. The empty band under the axis (about y=685–758) is already the space for those dates. The channel names and the date format are facts only the maker has. Fact needed from the maker: the name of the channel each of the three lines shows.
2. **Color is the only thing that tells the series apart, and with no labels even color tells you nothing** (defect, WCAG 1.4.1). Even once a legend exists, a color-blind reader has to match hues to names. Orange and green sit close in lightness and could merge for some readers. Direct labels at the line ends fix this along with finding 1. Without them, add a second cue such as line style or marker shape.
3. **Without a y-scale you can't tell whether the chart is honest** (convention). The gaps between the lines look large, but with no values and no visible zero, the reader can't tell whether blue is twice green or 5% above it. A line chart may leave out zero if its axis is labeled (Wilke, Few); this one has no labels. Fix: show the axis values, and say so when the axis doesn't start at zero.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart title names a topic, not a finding ("Visits by channel"). Once the lines are labeled, consider a title or short note that says what changed. This is taste, and no approved source covers it.
- The chart title starts about 2 px right of the stat-card labels (x≈71 vs ≈69). It looks like a near-miss rather than a choice, so match the padding (Arnheim on ambiguous placement).
- The three green deltas are small, about 12 px. They look like they pass contrast, but measure the green against white to confirm 4.5:1.

**What's working.**
- Each stat card gives the number with a comparison ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"), so the reader gets a judgment along with the figure. Keep that pattern.
- The hierarchy is clear: a small gray label, then a large dark number, then a small colored delta. Three steps with distinct jobs and no sizes that nearly collide.
- It's restrained. Gridlines are pale, and the cards use only thin borders on a light gray ground, so the data marks are the strongest ink. Keep that once labels go in, so they don't add clutter.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top right could be a date-range control or a static caption. If it's a control, it needs a button or dropdown look, because right now it reads as plain text.
- I couldn't check hover tooltips on the chart. If values only appear on hover, the chart still fails at a glance and on touch screens.
