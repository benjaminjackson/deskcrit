**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", drops to the gray subtitle, then runs down the time columns. It reads as a clean, calm departure board for one route, in one direction. It feels tidy and generic: a default web table, competent and with no character.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a printed or on-screen timetable for riders who want to know when the next ferry leaves north landing on the day they're traveling. It asks them to believe nothing beyond the plain times, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** It works as a lookup table, but it isn't built around the rider's real question, "does a boat run today, and when?" The day each sailing runs is pushed to the far right edge with the same weight as everything else, and the column that gets the most space, Period, tells the reader nothing new.

**What would move it most**
1. **The day each sailing runs is the deciding fact, and it sits last and as quiet as everything else.** Defect. Three of the seven sailings don't run every day (12:40 and 17:10 are weekdays only, 19:30 is weekends only). Today a Saturday rider has to read the time, then carry the eye about 600 px right across empty space to "Runs" to learn whether the boat exists for them. Someone who stops at the time column will turn up for a 12:40 that isn't coming. The four short columns are spread across the full 1080 px width, so each row reads as four separate words rather than one sailing (Arnheim on grouping by proximity). Fix direction: tighten the columns so a row reads as one unit, and make the exceptions stand out from "Daily" by weight or placement, not by color alone. Better still, let riders find their own day first, for example by splitting the table into weekday and weekend sailings.
2. **The Period column takes the most prominent position and adds nothing.** Common sense. "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday…" restates what 06:40 and 11:00 already say. It sits in the first column, where the eye starts each row, so the first thing a rider reads on every line is the least useful. Fix direction: drop it. If the time-of-day bands matter, use them as group headings, with extra space between groups instead of seven equally spaced rows.

**Smaller things.**
- The times are set in proportional figures. "11:00" and "17:10" come out narrower than "06:40", so the right edges of both time columns are ragged. Switch to tabular figures so the columns line up (Bringhurst, 4.4.3).
- "Spring season" in the subtitle doesn't tell a rider whether the timetable applies on the date they're traveling. Fact needed from the maker: the start and end dates of the spring season.

**What's working.**
- There's one clear entry point: the bold title, a quiet gray subtitle, then the table, and nothing competes with them. Keep that restraint.
- The row rules are pale enough to guide the eye along a row without boxing in the cells (Lupton on over-ruled tables). Keep them that light.
- The subtitle names the route and direction up front ("North landing to market quay"), so no one misreads this as the return sailings.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell how the four-column table reflows on a phone, which is where many riders will check it.
