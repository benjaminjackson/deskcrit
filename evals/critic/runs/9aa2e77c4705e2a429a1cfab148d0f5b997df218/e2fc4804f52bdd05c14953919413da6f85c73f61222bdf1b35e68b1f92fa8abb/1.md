**First read.** The eye goes first to the big dark box at top left, finds a gray avatar silhouette where a face should be, then drops to the bold two-line headline and the black "Book a free seat" button. The whole right half of the screen is blank white. It reads as a bookshop's event page that hasn't finished loading. The type is calm and decent, but the page feels like a wireframe.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a local bookshop's desktop page for a free author evening, and its job is to get readers to book a seat. No claim is at stake: the event is free and the page states plain facts.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The heaviest thing on the page is a placeholder where the author should be, and with the empty right half the event looks unfinished. The copy and type underneath are good enough to work once those two things are fixed.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture is a placeholder, and it's the first thing anyone sees.** The 600×330 dark box at top left is the largest, darkest element on the page, so the eye lands there first. What it finds is a generic gray head-and-shoulders avatar in brown-gray on near-black, which barely shows against the background. On an author evening the face is the draw: it's how a reader recognizes Ines Varga and decides the evening is worth a trip. Here the box only says "someone." Image and headline should make one idea together; at the moment the image adds nothing to the headline. Whether this is unfinished work or an image that didn't load, the viewer meets an empty hero either way. It's also a stock template move (unslop: "empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"), so the page looks like any other event template. Fix direction: put in a real photo of the author, or the cover of *The Salt Year*, or an image of the island the talk is about. Anything specific to this evening works. If no photo exists, shrink the box rather than give the top of the page to nothing. Defect.
2. **The page tips left, and the empty half looks like a mistake.** Everything sits in one column about 600 px wide, from x=48 to x=648. From the image down, nothing at all is in the right half of a 1280 px screen. White space works when it's clearly chosen and set against something dense (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). This space is just left over, so it doesn't read as calm, it reads as broken. The weight is also lopsided: the dark image and black headline sit hard left with no counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Fix direction: use the width on purpose. For example, put the image on the right with the date, headline, text, and button on the left. Or widen the image across the page. Or, if a narrow column is the point, center it so the space around it looks chosen. Defect, since the page looks half-built.

**Smaller things**
- In the navigation, "Events" looks the same as the other links, so nothing tells a visitor they're on the Events page. Mark the current page with one cue, such as bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The page gives a date and time but not where the evening is held. "Find us" in the menu suggests the shop itself, but a reader shouldn't have to guess before booking. Fact needed from the maker: the venue or branch address for this event.

**What's working**
- The text block in order: rust date line, then headline, then one plain paragraph, then one button. Each level is clearly different in size, weight, or color, and the date sits tight against the headline it belongs to. Keep this order and spacing when you rebuild the layout.
- The copy. "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor" says exactly what happens. "Book a free seat" names one specific action and takes away the cost worry in the same three words.
- The restrained palette: warm near-black, warm gray, and one rust accent. It suits a bookshop and keeps the button as the only strong dark block in the text.
