**First read.** The eye goes to the purple "Sign in" button, then up to the email field with its red border. You can tell right away that this is a sign-in card for a product called Quillpost. It feels calm, tidy, and familiar. The second look is where it breaks: something is clearly wrong with the email, but nothing on the screen says what.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the sign-in screen of a web app, shown in the state after a failed submit, for a returning user who wants to get in fast. No claim is at stake; the user only needs to sign in.

**Verdict.** The layout is solid and conventional. The one state this image exists to show fails: the email field is marked wrong by a red border alone, with no message. The user can't tell what's wrong (the address `dana.ortiz@exmaple` has a typo and no ending like .com) or how to fix it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The error has no words.** The only sign of the error is the red border on the Email field. There's no message under it, no icon, and no line at the top of the card. Someone who looks at it quickly may think the field is just focused, or that the password was the problem, so they press Sign in again. A color-blind user may see no error at all. WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.1 (Error Identification) and SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) both require the error to be stated in text. SC 3.3.3 (Error Suggestion) adds that the message should say how to fix it. Fix: put a short message directly under the Email field, in the gap above "Password". It should say what's wrong with the address and what a valid one looks like. Keep the red border as a second cue. Also give the message more room than the label gap gives it now, so it reads as belonging to Email and not to Password. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The two text links don't match. "Forgot your password?" is purple, while "Create an account" is the same gray as "New here?", so it doesn't look clickable. Neither one has an underline. Give both links one style, with a cue besides color (an underline), and make "Create an account" visibly different from the question before it (WCAG technique G183; Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*).
- The default input border on Password looks pale gray against white and may be under 3:1. Measure it against WCAG 1.4.11 and darken it if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean: one filled primary button, a clear "Sign in" heading, and bold labels above each field (not placeholders), all in one column on one left edge. Keep this structure when you add the error message.
- The type scale is tight: about four sizes, each with a clear job. The wordmark, heading, labels, and helper text separate by weight and color, not by sizes that are almost the same.
- There's generous, gathered space inside the card, and the card is centered on a quiet gray background. For a one-task screen, centering is the right choice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus: whether the red border is also standing in for the focus ring, and whether focus is visible on the links and button.
- Whether an error message appears after a delay or is announced to screen readers.
