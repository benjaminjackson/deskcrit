**First read.** The eye lands on "Help for ferry passengers," drops to the two bold section heads, then works down the left column. It reads as a plain, calm help page from a small ferry service, written for people at the landing or planning a trip. It's quiet and clear, and a little bare.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web or printed help page that tells ferry passengers what to do before they board and how to report a problem, read by an ordinary passenger with no special knowledge. It asks the reader to believe nothing they would doubt; it gives instructions, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: one clear headline, two well-named sections, readable text and plain-spoken copy. Two things hold it back. Both lists are set so their markers don't line up with the text, which makes the items blur together. And the "goes wrong" steps send the reader somewhere else for the one fact they need to act.

**What would move it most**
1. **The list markers don't hang, so the items run together (defect).** In "Before you board," each bullet sits about 25 px left of its text, but the wrapped lines go back to the margin under the bullet (x≈90). The bullets don't form a column of their own, so the left edge is ragged and every item looks like the start of one long paragraph. The numbered steps on the right do the same: "1." "2." "3." sit inline, and the wrapped lines start under the number (x≈660). At a glance you can't see that there are three steps. A reader standing on a quay, scanning for "what do I do," has to read whole lines to find where each item starts. Bringhurst (§4.4.2) says lists should be made clear by position, with numbers hung in the margin. Fix: hang both lists so the bullets and numbers sit in their own column, with wrapped lines aligned under the first word. Close the oversized gap after the bullet while you're at it.
2. **Step 3 doesn't give the address it tells you to send to (defect).** "Send it to the address on the noticeboard in the shelter" means anyone reading this away from the landing (at home, after the trip, which is when most people complain) can't do the step. This is the one place on the page where the reader has to act, and it ends in a dead end. Fix: print the address right in step 3 in place of the reference to the noticeboard, or put it beside the step. Fact needed from the maker: the postal or email address that complaints should go to.

**Smaller things.**
- "If something goes wrong" stops at handing in the note and never says what happens next or when the reader will hear back. That leaves them unsure whether it was worth doing. Fact needed from the maker: who reads the notes and how soon passengers get a reply.
- In the first bullet, "the last boat leaves before dusk" is the line a passenger plans around, and it can be read two ways: a fixed time, or one that changes with the season. Someone who guesses wrong misses the last boat. Fact needed from the maker: the time of the last winter sailing, or a note that it changes with the season and where to check it.
- The fourth bullet ends on "clear." alone on its last line. Rewrap it or trim a word so it doesn't end on a runt (Bringhurst §2.4.2).
- Across the two columns the lines drift off a shared baseline after the first item. For example, the first line of right-hand step 2 sits level with the gap between lines in the left column. Space the list items in whole line steps so the rows line up (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). This is minor on a web help page.

**What's working.**
- The headings say exactly what each section holds ("Before you board," "If something goes wrong"), so a reader picks the right column without reading anything else. Keep that wording.
- The copy gives a reason with each instruction ("since the machine is slow to give change…", "so that the gangway is kept clear"). That's why people follow it. Don't trim those clauses away in an edit.
- The type is simple and enough: one family, three clearly separate sizes, bold only on headings, dark text on white, and lines of about 60 characters. Nothing needs adding.
