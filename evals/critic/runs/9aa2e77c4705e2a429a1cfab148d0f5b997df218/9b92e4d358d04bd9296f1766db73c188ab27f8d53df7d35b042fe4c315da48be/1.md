**First read.** The eye goes to "Project notes" at the top left, then down a stack of five boxes with heavy outlines. The third box, "Sketches," sits about 14 px to the right of the other four, and that small step is the most noticeable thing on the page after the title. It looks like a slip, not a choice. The page reads as a plain, orderly list of project stages. The near-black borders make it feel heavier than five one-line notes need.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal page that a project team reads to see the stages of its own plan, from kickoff to handover. No claim is at stake: it's a team's own plan, so it asks the reader to accept nothing.

**Verdict.** The page does its job: the title, the stage names and the descriptions are clear and easy to read. But one card is out of line, which makes the whole thing look unfinished. The heavy box around every card makes the borders louder than the words inside them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Sketches" card is out of line with the other four (defect).** Its left edge sits about 14 px right of the column, and its title and text move right with it. Its right edge still lines up with the others. Fourteen pixels is too little to read as a choice and too much to miss. The reader stops to wonder whether Sketches is a sub-step of Research or whether something broke. Either way the clean list now looks unfinished. Arnheim: a placement that is neither clearly here nor clearly there makes the eye waver. Gerstner: items of one kind follow one rule, and a variant appears only where the rules call for it. Fix: put it back on the shared left edge. If Sketches really is a sub-step of Research, show that clearly, with a much deeper indent and a visible link to its parent, so it can't be read as a slip.
2. **Every card has a thick near-black border (convention).** The outlines look about 3 px wide and nearly as dark as the headings. Five of them stacked turn the page into a column of hard compartments. The borders are now the strongest pattern on the page and compete with the stage names. The gaps between the cards already separate them, so the borders add no information. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*): boxes should show how things relate, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell. Tufte: ink that carries no information. Fix: take the borders out and let the space between stages do the separating, or replace them with a pale tone or a light hairline, so the stage names lead.

**Smaller things.**
- These are stages in order (Kickoff → Handover), but nothing shows the order except their position. That's taste: a step number, or a stated "Stage" next to each name, would show the reader at a glance that this is a sequence and not loose notes. That only matters if the order matters to the team.

**What's working.**
- The type is clear. The page title, the bold stage names and the regular descriptions are three clearly different levels, with weight doing the work between the names and the descriptions. Keep that.
- The copy is good. Each description says who does what in one sentence with a real verb ("Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need"). Keep that plain, active wording.
- The spacing inside each card is tighter than the gap between cards, so each name clearly belongs to its own line of text.
