No claim is at stake: the page states plain facts (hours, class sizes, delivery terms) and asks the reader to believe nothing they'd doubt.

**Verdict.** The page is calm and well built below the hero, but it doesn't do its job yet. The headline is hard to read over the pattern, and once someone has read the page there's nothing to click, book, or shop.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is hard to read over the pattern.** "Plants for shade and small spaces" is white type set straight on a busy pattern of light and dark leaves. The worst spots are "small," whose letters cross pale yellow leaves and white dots, and "spaces.", where a white dot sits against the period. Readers have to piece the words together, and the one line that says what the store is offering this week costs the most effort on the page. WCAG F83 measures contrast against the lightest parts of the image behind the letters, not the average, and those parts fail here. Fix: put a dark scrim or a solid panel behind the headline, or move it off the image onto the white area where the intro line already sits. *Defect.*
2. **The page has no next step.** It has no button and no link in the content. The cards for Saturday workshops ("ten places in each class") and Delivery ("orders over $60") both describe something to sign up for or buy, but neither leads anywhere. The headline names a range of plants, and you can't browse it from here. The eye goes headline → intro → cards and hits a dead end. A reader who's interested has to guess that "Plants" or "Workshops" in the small gray nav is the way forward. Fix: add one main action tied to the headline, such as a way into the shade and small-space plants, styled clearly above everything else. Give the workshop card its own lower-weight link to book (Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis). *Defect.*
3. **The hero image shows none of the plants on offer.** The headline promises plants for shade and the intro names ferns, hostas, and compact shrubs. The image is a flat pattern of bright yellow-green leaves with red and orange dots, which reads more like a sunny tropical print than a shady corner, and it shows none of the named plants. So the image only decorates and adds nothing to the message. It's the stock "generic picture as the hero visual" move (unslop): readers have seen it on many other sites, so this nursery looks like any nursery. It is also the busy pattern that causes finding 1. Fix: use a picture from this store's world, such as the actual ferns and hostas, in shade, and calm enough to hold the type. *Template tell, and taste on the mismatch with "shade."*

**Smaller things.**
- The headline's left edge sits about 16 px to the right of the logo, intro, and cards (around x=72 against 56). It looks like a near-miss rather than a choice. Line it up on 56 or move it clearly off.
- The headline breaks as "small / spaces," which splits the phrase "small spaces." Break before "small spaces" or widen the text box so the phrase stays together.

**What's working.**
- The area below the hero is well judged. The intro line runs about 70 characters, and the three cards share one grid, one left edge, and one rhythm of bold title over plain text. Each card title says exactly what the card holds. Keep this structure.
- The card copy is concrete and complete: "Open every day from 8 am to 6 pm, until 8 pm on Thursdays" and "Free within ten miles on orders over $60" each say who does what and when, with units, so a reader can act on them without guessing.
- The serif wordmark against the plain sans text gives the store a voice without crowding the page.
