**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal Growth page that a marketing or growth team uses to see where Q3 accounts came from, what changed since Q2, and what that means for Q4 spend. It reports facts to the team that owns them, so no claim needs proving.

**Verdict.** This is a solid, honest chart, and the numbers add up. But the page says it is about change, and the chart shows only one quarter. The reader has to take the "what changed" on the panel's word, and the chart is no help with the Q4 budget decision the panel announces.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't show change, but change is the page's point (defect).** The panel is headed "What changed this quarter" and its first claim is "up from a quarter in Q2". Every bar is Q3 only. The one Q2 figure is a fraction in the text, for one channel. Did referral grow? Did paid social fall? Were events always this small? Nothing on the page says, and you can't judge that cutting events is right without knowing whether events were trending up or down. A number with no earlier period can't be read as good or bad (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). The fix: show Q2 next to Q3 for every channel. A pale gray Q2 bar or tick behind each blue bar would do it, or a change figure under each value label. Then the panel's claims can be seen in the chart instead of taken on trust.
2. **The chart and the commentary don't point at each other (taste).** The panel makes two points: organic search is up, and podcast ads plus events are small. On the chart, all six bars are the same blue, and the title only names the topic. So the reader has to match the text to the bars on their own, and nothing tells a skimmer which bars matter. The common-sense fix: color only the bars the panel discusses (organic search, and podcast ads with events) and drop the rest to gray. You could also move the panel's first finding into the chart title or a short note on the Organic search bar, so the chart says what changed by itself.

**Smaller things.**
- The y-axis ticks and gridlines repeat the value labels already printed on every bar. They are pale, so they don't hurt, but either set could go, and the chart would be quieter without them.
- The panel says "a third" and "a quarter" while the chart is in counts. If you show Q2 (finding 1), the counts and the fractions will be easier to check against each other.

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest and direct: bars start at zero, are sorted largest first, have values printed on top, and use one color for one series. Nothing needs decoding.
- The source line is exact. It gives the dates and the 5,400 total, and the panel's "a third" and "fewer than 700" check out against the bars. That earns trust. Keep it.
- The type has a clear order: a bold title, a gray subtitle that defines what is counted ("by the channel of the first visit"), body-size commentary, and small gray detail. Each level has one job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the bars have hover detail, and how the two columns stack at narrower widths.
