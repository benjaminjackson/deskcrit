**First read.** The eye goes first to the bold "Tell us about your project" at top left, then across to the white form card, then down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads as the contact page of a small design studio. It feels calm, tidy, and neutral, and a bit anonymous: apart from the "Fernwick Studio" wordmark, nothing on the screen tells you whose studio this is.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: this is a studio website's contact page, and a prospective client uses it to send a first project inquiry. The only claim is the promise to "reply within two working days". That is a plain commitment the reader accepts or doesn't, so no claim here needs proof.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: the page is clear, the form is short, and the order of importance is right. The biggest problem is that the page asks for a budget range and a timeline, but it asks in the left column, far from the box where people type. Many inquiries will arrive without the two things the studio says it wants.

**What would move it most**
1. **The request for budget and timeline is in the wrong place.** The paragraph under the headline says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." It sits in the left column, about 450 px from the Message box, and it is read before anyone starts typing. By the time someone is in the Message field, the request is out of sight and forgotten, and the box itself gives no hint. The studio then has to write back and ask, which slows down the very two-day reply the page promises. Help belongs next to the step it concerns (Nielsen 10, *Help and documentation*). **Fix:** put the budget-and-timeline request at the Message field, either as helper text under its label or as two short optional fields for budget and timeline. Keep the reply promise in the intro. *Defect.*
2. **The field borders look too faint.** The outlines of the name, email, and message boxes are a pale gray on white and look under the 3:1 that WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 (Non-text contrast) requires for the edge of a control. Someone with low vision, or using a dim screen, sees bold labels floating over boxes they can barely make out. Measure the border color against white. If it is under 3:1, darken it until it passes; the labels and the layout can stay as they are. *Defect (convention, WCAG).*

**Smaller things.**
- **Nav doesn't show the current page:** "Contact" in the top nav looks exactly like Work, Services, and About, so nothing says which page you are on. Mark it with one clear cue, such as bold or a heavier underline (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **No required or optional markers:** none of the three fields says whether it must be filled in. Add an asterisk to each required label and explain it once, or add "(optional)" to the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- **Card is off the header's grid:** the card's right edge stops at about x 1068, while the header runs to a right margin at about 1224. That leaves a 156 px strip that matches nothing else on the page and makes the content look placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Either widen the card or the columns out to the header's right margin, or bring the header in to match the content's width.
- **Generic headline:** "Tell us about your project" could sit on any agency's contact page, so nothing here says what this studio does or who it is for. *Taste.* This is a minor point on a contact page, but it is the only large line of text on the screen.

**What's working.**
- **The intro copy sets an expectation the reader can hold you to.** "We read every message and reply within two working days" names who does what and by when. Keep that sentence exactly as it is.
- **The form is short and clearly labeled.** Three fields in one column, each with a bold label just above its box, and the space between fields clearly larger than the space between a label and its box. Nothing to puzzle over.
- **There is one primary action, and it is unmistakable.** "Send message" is the only filled button and the only block of solid color in the main area. The underlined blue links stay clearly a lower level.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and the button. On a keyboard-driven form these matter; check in a browser that each one is at least 2 px with 3:1 contrast.
- Validation and error messages, for example after an invalid email address.
