**First read.** The eye goes to "Tell us about your project" at top left, then across to the white form card and down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads right away as the contact page of a small design studio: calm, plain, and competent. It also feels a little unfinished at the edges. The form stops well short of the right margin, and the bottom third of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the contact page of a design studio's website, for prospective clients who have already seen the work and now want to start a conversation. No claim is at stake. The only promise is "reply within two working days", which a visitor takes at face value, and anyone on this page has already decided to get in touch.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The headline, the short intro, the three labeled fields and the single strong button make the action obvious, and nothing gets in the way of sending a message. The biggest gap is between what the copy asks for and what the form gives: it asks for a budget and a timeline but has nowhere to put them. After that, the layout's right edge comes from nothing on the page, so the page looks a little lopsided.

**What would move it most**
1. **The intro asks for a budget range and a rough timeline, but the form has no place for them.** The visitor reads "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them," then sees only Name, Email and a blank Message box. They have to remember the request and work it into their message. Most won't, so the studio loses the two details it said it wanted. The direction: either give these two items their own short, clearly optional fields between Email and Message, or put the prompt inside the form next to the Message label. Either way the request sits where the visitor is typing. This is common sense, not a cited rule.
2. **The form card's right edge lines up with nothing.** The header runs from 56 px to 1224 px, but the card stops at about 1068 px. That leaves a strip roughly 156 px wide on the right that no other element uses. Together with the empty space under the short left column, the page looks pulled left, and the content seems placed by eye rather than on a grid (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The direction: take the columns from the header's margins, so the card's right edge sits on the 1224 px line or on a clear column line, and give the text column and the card a deliberate ratio. This is a convention.

**Smaller things.**
- The navigation doesn't mark the current page: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About. Give the current item one clear cue, such as bolder weight, a bar, or a background (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- No field is marked as required or optional. Put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or add "optional" to the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). This matters especially if you add the budget and timeline fields above.
- The gray input borders on white look like they fall a little under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border color against white, and darken it to at least 3:1 if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- **One clear action.** "Send message" is the only filled, saturated element on the page, so nothing competes with it. Keep it that way.
- **Real labels.** Each field has a bold label above it, close to its own field and not a placeholder. They're easy to scan and they don't disappear when you start typing.
- **Honest copy.** "We read every message and reply within two working days" is a specific promise, and it answers the visitor's first worry better than a generic "Get in touch."

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the fields and button, and what error messages look like when a field is left empty or the email is invalid. On a form, both are worth checking in a live browser.
