**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline "Help around the house, on your schedule", then drops to the row of four cards. In that row the window-cleaning photo grabs attention first: a dark, wood-paneled hall with black window panes, sitting next to three bright flat illustrations. It reads as a friendly local home-services homepage. It feels calm, warm and trustworthy, except for that one card, which looks like it came from a different site.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage of a local home-services company, for homeowners deciding whether to book a crew into their home. That reader has to believe the crew is safe to let in and the price is what they'll pay. Right now they get the word "vetted" and four "From" prices with no unit, and neither carries that.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound and the hierarchy is clean, but the page doesn't yet earn a stranger's booking. The biggest problems are the mismatched, off-message photo in the first card and a trust claim ("vetted crew") with nothing behind it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The window-cleaning photo breaks the set and shows the wrong thing.** This is a defect.
   - **Style:** The first card uses a photograph while the other three use flat teal and coral illustrations at the same size and crop. The row reads as put together from unrelated sources rather than designed as one family. That's common sense, not a published rule. The photo is also the darkest, busiest thing on the page, so it pulls the eye to the cheapest-feeling spot first.
   - **Content:** It shows an ornate, dim hall (it looks like a library or college lounge at night) with windows that read as black panes, not clean glass. A homeowner looking for window cleaning sees neither a home nor a result.
   - **Fix:** Replace it with an illustration in the same flat style as the other three. If you'd rather use photos, switch all four to photos with one shared treatment.
2. **"Vetted crew" asks for trust the page never backs up.** This is a defect against the job. The subhead asks the reader to accept that "a vetted crew arrives". For a service that sends strangers into someone's home, that's the claim that decides the booking, and the page gives the maker's word only. The reader will ask who these people are and what "vetted" means, and nothing visible answers that.
   - **Fix:** Put proof this reader would accept on sight close to the headline or the cards, such as what the vetting consists of, or reviews or ratings from named customers. It has to come from the maker. Don't fill it with made-up content.
3. **The prices don't say what they're for.** This is a defect. "From $89", "From $189", "From $24" and "From $65" give no unit, so the reader can't tell what they'd pay. Is $24 per bag or per load? Is $89 per window or per house? Prices are what a customer holds you to, so a figure that can be read two ways sets up a dispute or a bounce to the Pricing page. Fact needed from the maker: the unit each "From" price covers (per visit, per load, per window, per hour, etc.).

**Smaller things.**
- The only booking control is "Book a visit" up in the nav. The subhead says "Pick a service, choose a day", but the cards have no action, so picking a service leads nowhere. Give each card a clear way to book that service, or make the whole card visibly clickable (Norman, natural mapping).
- The hero has no action of its own. Once the reader has read the promise, the next step is a trip to the top-right corner.

**What's working.**
- The type pairing works: a warm serif for the logo and headline, a plain sans for everything else. It's friendly without being cute. Keep it.
- The three illustrations (basket, shirt, plant) share one palette, one level of detail and one tinted panel. That's the system the photo should join.
- The card copy is concrete and in the customer's own words. "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday" tells the reader exactly what happens and when. Bring the other lines up to that standard rather than down.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- About 150 px of empty background sits below the cards. Check in a live browser whether the page continues (trust, process, areas) or really ends there. If it ends, the reader gets no proof and no next step below the cards.
- I couldn't check hover and focus states on the nav links or the cards.
