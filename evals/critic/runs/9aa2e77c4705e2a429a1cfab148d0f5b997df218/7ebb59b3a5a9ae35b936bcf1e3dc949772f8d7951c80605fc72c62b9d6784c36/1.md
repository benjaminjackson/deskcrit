**First read.** The eye lands on the leaf confetti before anything else. The whole frame is one even scatter of bright green ovals. The white headline comes into view a beat later, and the subhead and outlined button flicker in and out of the pattern beneath it. It reads as a plant nursery's homepage hero, and it feels busy, sunny and springy rather than cool, shady or fern-like.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage hero for a local nursery, meant to tell home gardeners what Fernhill sells and get them browsing or visiting. To believe "ferns and shade plants for every corner, ready to plant this weekend", that reader needs to see the actual plants, healthy and for sale, and this page shows none.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't do its job yet. The background is generated green ovals, not the ferns and shade plants the headline promises. It shows nothing the nursery sells and sits under the text with no calm ground, so the background is both the biggest missed chance and the main thing hurting readability.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture shows no ferns and no shade plants.** The full-bleed background is flat ovals in about a dozen greens. Ferns have fronds, not ovals, and nothing here looks like a product, a greenhouse or a hill. The headline makes a specific promise ("Ferns and shade plants"), and the picture should prove it. Right now it only says "green", which any garden site could say. The reader gets no sense of what they'd actually take home, which hurts a nursery more than most businesses, because people buy plants by how they look. Direction: replace the pattern with real pictures of Fernhill's own ferns and shade plants, ideally the greenhouse the button points to, so the picture and the headline make one case. *Taste / common sense, and the most important point.*
2. **The text has no ground to sit on.** The subhead "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend." is white regular text at roughly 20 px. Behind "Grown", "hill" and "weekend." are pale lime leaves, so at those points the letters look well under 3:1 and the line has to be read twice. The ghost button has the same problem: its thin white border and white label cross light leaves, so the edge that makes it look pressable drops out in places (WCAG 1.4.11, 3:1 for component edges). The headline survives on size alone. WCAG's F83 technique says contrast counts at the worst point behind the text, not the average. Direction: put a dark scrim or solid panel behind the text block, or use a picture with a calm, dark area where the text sits. Also consider a filled button, so the one action doesn't depend on a hairline border. Measure the subhead and the button border against their lightest leaves once the background is chosen. *Defect.*
3. **The pattern competes with the headline and spreads out the color.** Nearly the whole frame is saturated mid-to-light green at one even density. Nothing is quieter anywhere, so the eye has no resting spot and no path. It scans the confetti instead of going headline → subhead → button. In grayscale, the leaves and the white text sit too close in value for the structure to hold. Itten and *Refactoring UI* both argue that saturated color should mark what matters, with large areas muted. The bright lime also works against the subject: shade plants are about cool, deep, low-light greens. Direction: whatever picture goes in, give it a dark, muted range overall, with a clearly quiet area behind the text, and save the brightest green for one accent. *Convention (saturation, value) plus taste (mood).*

**Smaller things.**
- The headline leaves "corner" alone on its third line. Rebreak it as "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner" or widen the measure slightly (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The wordmark's left edge (about x 64) and the hero text's left edge (about x 96) are close but don't line up, so it looks like two containers. Either align them or offset the hero clearly. *Taste.*
- The navigation shows no current page. That's fine on the homepage, but check that the other pages mark where you are.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and sounds like a person. "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" gives a place and a time, and "Browse the greenhouse" names a concrete place instead of "Shop now". Keep both.
- The layout is simple: one left-aligned text block, one action and a plain wordmark, with no badge, extra buttons or stock hero clutter. Once the background stops fighting it, this layout will read cleanly.
- The headline is set big and bold in one sans with tight line spacing, so its three lines hold together as one unit and clearly lead the subhead.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states. Whether the leaf field moves or animates; if it does, the readability problems get worse.
