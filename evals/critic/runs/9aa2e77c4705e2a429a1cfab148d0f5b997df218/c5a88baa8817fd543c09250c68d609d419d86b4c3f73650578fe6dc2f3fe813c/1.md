**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild," takes in the first paragraph, and drops to the blue "Sign up" button in the card on the right. It reads as a news or notice page from a local ferry service, written for the people who ride it. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. The figure looks like a wireframe placeholder, not a picture of a landing.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this tells regular ferry riders about the rebuilt landing and gets them to sign up for timetable-change notes. The only ask is to sign up. To do that, a rider needs to trust that the notes will be few and will actually reach them. The helper line covers the first part ("one short note… stop the notes at any time"). Nothing on the form covers the second.

**Verdict.** The page reads well, but the sign-up card can't do its job: it asks for a name and a landing and never for a way to reach you. The figure is the other weak point, because it doesn't show the ramp change the article is about.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form has no contact field, so the notes have nowhere to go.** The card in the upper right asks for "Your name" and "Home landing," then "Sign up," and promises "one short note when the timetable changes." A rider who fills it in will reasonably ask, "a note to where?" Either they don't trust the button or they sign up and never hear anything. The name field is also the one that does the least for delivery. The fix: the form needs a field for the channel the notes go out on, labeled as plainly as the others. Cut or demote the name field if delivery doesn't need it, so the form stays at two or three fields. *Defect* (common sense; the checklist has no line for it). Fact needed from the maker: the channel the timetable notes are sent by (email, text message, or something else).
2. **The figure doesn't show what the text and caption describe.** The caption says "The new north landing, seen from the water at low tide." The text's main point is that "the ramps now meet the deck at every tide." The picture is a gray slab, a pale box and a blue rounded bar on a flat band of water. There's no ramp, no benches, no covered area, and nothing that shows the tide. A reader can't check the one change that matters to people with prams and wheelchairs, and the flat blocks look like a placeholder nobody replaced. If it's a stand-in for a real photo or drawing, it's fine once the real image is in. Otherwise the figure needs to show the ramp meeting the deck at low water, because that's the claim it illustrates. *Defect* (the picture reads as an empty placeholder). The headline-and-picture point is *taste*: no approved source covers it, but here the image and the text should carry one idea and they don't.

**Smaller things.**
- "Home landing" is a free-text box, so riders will type the same landing in several spellings. The service has a fixed list of landings, so give them a picker (Nielsen, heuristic 5: error prevention).
- Neither field says whether it is required. Mark the required fields with an asterisk and explain it once, or put "(optional)" after any optional label (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- The footer line "Ferry timetables are a sample for a fixture image. All names and places are invented." reads as test copy. Remove it before this ships.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean. There's one bold headline, body text around 18 px at roughly 66 characters a line, and a small gray caption. Each level is clearly different, so the page reads in one pass.
- The helper line under the button ("We send one short note… You can stop the notes at any time") answers the obvious worry about being spammed, in the rider's own words, right where they decide. Keep it.
- The body copy speaks to riders: "prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" says what the change means for them, not just what was built.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The focus states of the two fields and the button, and what the form shows after "Sign up" (confirmation or errors).
