**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the Conversion tab of an internal analytics tool, where a product or growth team compares trial-to-paid conversion across plans for Q3 2025. It reports measured figures and asks the reader to believe nothing beyond them, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The page is well made, but the chart misleads. Its bar axis starts at 2.5%, so a gap of 0.9 points looks like one plan converts three times better than another. Fix the scale before anything else, because every decision taken from this chart inherits the distortion.

**What would move it most**
1. **The bar chart's value axis starts at 2.5%, not zero** (left panel, y-axis 2.5% to 4.5%). In the data, Growth (4.0%) converts about 1.3 times as well as Starter (3.1%). But the visible bars are 1.5 and 0.6 axis units tall, so Growth looks 2.5 times as tall. That's a lie factor of about 5 (Tufte, ch. 2). Team (3.6%) and Enterprise (3.4%) are only 0.2 points apart, yet they look clearly different. A reader who glances at this concludes "Starter converts terribly", and anyone who carries the picture into a planning meeting without the labels carries the wrong size of effect. The value labels on the bars are correct, but the bar lengths say otherwise, and people remember lengths. Bar length encodes value, so bars must start at zero (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, 17.1; Few, pitfall 8). Fix direction: start the axis at zero so the lengths keep the true proportions. If the small differences are the point, switch to dots on a labeled non-zero scale, where the eye reads position, not length. **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- **No comparison for any rate** (chart and table). There's no previous quarter or target, so the reader can't tell whether 3.1% is good or bad for Starter, only that it's lower than the others. Fix: put the change from Q2 or the target next to each plan's rate (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Fact needed from the maker: the prior-period rates or targets per plan.
- **The title names the topic, not the finding** ("Trial to paid conversion by plan"). The reader has to work out the result themselves. Fix: state the result the data already shows in the title or subtitle, for example which plan converts best and by how much. This is taste and common sense; no approved source covers it.

**What's working.**
- **The subtitle and the Definition block** ("Share of trials started in Q3 2025 that became paid accounts within 30 days", "Trials still open on 30 October are counted as not converted"). They pin down exactly what the number means, which is rare on dashboards and worth keeping word for word.
- **Direct value labels on each bar, plus the raw counts in the table.** The reader gets exact values without a legend, and can check the rates against the counts (286/9,210 = 3.1%; all four match). The table also shows the sample size, so a reader can see Enterprise rests on only 38 conversions.
- **One restrained accent and a quiet gray structure.** The nav marks the current tab with bold and color, the gridlines are faint, and the only strong color on the page is the data.
