**First read.** The eye lands first on "Plan the week with your whole team", the biggest and darkest type. It drops to the blue "Create account" button and then moves up through the form. It reads as the sign-up page of a team planning tool. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but it could belong to any SaaS product. The lower-left half of the screen is a blank gray area with nothing in it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the sign-up page for a team weekly-planning tool, and that the reader is a team lead who came from a landing page or ad and is deciding whether to start a workspace. To give up five fields and a password, that reader needs to see what they're signing up for, such as the product itself or proof that teams like theirs use it, and this page shows neither.

**Verdict.** The form is clean and works. The page as a whole asks for commitment and gives nothing back. The biggest problem is the empty left half: it's the one place that could show the product, and it shows nothing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The left column makes a promise and then stops, leaving a big empty area where the case should be.** Under the subhead, the whole left half from about y=370 down is bare gray (defect). The page asks the reader to accept "Plan the week with your whole team". The only things that back that up are "free" and "up to five people", which lower the cost but don't show any value. A team lead will ask "what does this actually look like, and will my team bother using it?" Nothing on the page answers that, so they're asked to fill in a password and company name on trust. The split layout is also a stock move: a headline on the left and a card on the right, with no product preview (Template tell, unslop: "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"). That makes the page look like anyone's sign-up. On top of that, the weight tips right. The dense white card runs from y=96 to y=665, while the left text block ends at about y=360 with nothing to balance it, so the page looks top-heavy on the left and bottom-heavy on the right (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The space isn't a deliberate rest area either; it's left over (Ruder). **Fix direction:** put a product preview from the real product in the lower left, such as a real week-planning view, or proof that this reader trusts. That one change gives the page a counterweight and answers the objection. Don't add a stock illustration.

2. **Input borders look too pale to show where the fields are.** On the five text fields, the boxes are white on a white card, so a thin light-gray stroke is the only edge. It looks under the 3:1 minimum for component boundaries (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11), and low-vision users will have trouble finding the fields (defect). Measure the border color against #fff. If it's under 3:1, darken the stroke until it passes. Keep the white fill.

**Smaller things.**
- No field is marked required or optional. "Company" in particular reads as possibly optional, so people will hesitate or submit and hit an error. Mark the required fields with an asterisk and a one-line legend, or add "optional" after the optional ones (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- "Sign In" in the footer line looks like the gray text around it, with nothing marking it as a link. Underline it or give it the brand blue (WCAG technique G183).
- "Sign In" is in title case, while everything else ("Create account", "Create your account") is in sentence case. Make it "Sign in".
- The placeholders "Enter first name", "Enter last name" and "Company name" just repeat their labels (Nielsen heuristic 8). Remove them or replace them with a format hint like the email and password fields have. The password field does this well already.
- The "Tallyboard" wordmark sits about 8 px lower than "Create your account" across the gutter. That's close enough to look like a miss. Either line them up on a baseline or move them clearly apart (Arnheim).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight and clear: headline, card title, body, labels and the small footer line, each in a clearly different size or weight. Keep it.
- The form is laid out correctly. It's one column, the only side-by-side pair is the short first/last name pair, the labels sit above the fields, and there's one strong primary button. The password field states its rule ("At least 10 characters") before the user can get it wrong.
- The subhead copy earns its place. "free", "up to five people" and "invite the rest of the team later" answer the cost question before the reader asks it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button, and the error states after a failed submit. These matter on a form like this, so check them in a live browser.
