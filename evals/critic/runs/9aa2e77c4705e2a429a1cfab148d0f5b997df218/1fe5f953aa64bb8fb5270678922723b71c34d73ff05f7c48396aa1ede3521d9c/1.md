**First read.** The eye goes to the three big white headlines, "Learn to swim", "Join the lanes", and "Family hour", reading left to right as equal choices. It's clearly a pool or leisure center's program row, and it feels bold and friendly. Then the eye drops into the large empty bottom half of each card and finds nothing there, no time, price, or link, and the read ends.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a section of a public pool's website that lets local adults and parents pick a swimming program and go on to book or find out more. No claim is at stake here. These are plain offers (when, where, for whom), so the reader isn't asked to believe anything.

**Verdict.** It's a good start for the top half of a card, but it doesn't do the job yet. Each card says what the program is and then stops, with no next step and no details to plan by. About 45% of each card is dead color where those should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no next step.** The bottom of each card is empty, and nothing on the row is visibly clickable: no button, link, arrow, or underline. A parent who wants Family hour has nowhere to go. They might guess the whole card is a link, or they might not. If the cards are links, nothing on screen says so. The fix is to give each card one clear action that names what happens next, styled as a button or an underlined link, and to put it at the bottom of the card where the eye already ends up. *Defect* (common sense: the main action must look like something to click, and the screen should put the next step in front of the user). Fact needed from the maker: what each card leads to (booking, timetable, or signup) and what the action should say.
2. **The empty half of each card is wasted, and the details a swimmer plans by are vague.** Below the text there's roughly 300 px of solid orange, green, or purple that holds nothing. It isn't resting space. It reads as unfinished, as if the content didn't load. The copy also leaves gaps a swimmer needs filled before they act: "Tuesday evenings" and "Sunday afternoons" give no hours, "from six" has no end time, and nothing says what it costs or whether you need to book. That space is where those details belong. Either put the details there, set as a short, quieter block above the action, or shorten the cards so the color fits the content. *Defect* (common sense). Fact needed from the maker: the session times, the price, and whether booking is needed for each program.
3. **The row is off-center and has no heading.** The cards start 56 px from the left edge but end about 96 px from the right, so the row leans left on a white page. There's also no section heading or venue name, so a viewer arriving here can't tell whose pool this is or what the row is called. The fix is to center the row or align it to the page's real grid, and to add a heading that names the section. *Convention* (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"; the heading point is common sense).

**Smaller things.**
- Three unrelated, fully saturated hues fill most of the frame. Strong color is everywhere, so it marks nothing, and once an action button is added it will struggle to stand out. Tie the colors to one family, or tone down the large fields and keep the strongest color for the action (*Refactoring UI*, "Define your shades"). This is taste.
- "Join the lanes" is the one headline a newcomer has to decode. The body text explains it, but "Lane swimming" would say it in the heading itself. That's an editorial edit using words already on the card.

**What's working.**
- The headlines are short, verb-led, and set large and bold with tight leading. Each one reads as a unit and tells you who the card is for in two or three words. Keep that.
- The body copy speaks to the reader in plain terms ("with a coach in the water beside you", "floats and toys for the children"). That's specific and human, and it makes the programs feel real.
- White text on all three colors looks comfortably above 4.5:1, and the 18 px body size reads easily.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable or have a hover state, and how the three columns stack on a phone.
