**First read.** The eye goes to "Project notes" and then straight to the five heavy black frames below it, which carry more ink than any of the words. It reads as an internal page listing a project's stages from Kickoff to Handover, for the team doing the work. It feels plain and orderly. It also feels a little like a form or a wireframe, because of the boxes.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal reference page that shows a project team the stages of the work in order and what each stage produces. No claim is at stake: the team is being told its own plan, not persuaded of anything.

**Verdict.** It does its job. You can read the five stages and their tasks at a glance. The main thing in the way is the heavy border around every item: it's the loudest thing on the page and it carries no information, so the page reads as a stack of boxes rather than as one plan.

**What would move it most**
1. **The five dark frames compete with the content.** Each stage sits inside a near-black outline that looks about 3 px thick, with only about 18 px between frames. That gives roughly 15 thick horizontal lines down a column whose content is ten short lines of text. The eye reads the frame edges before the words, and the stages look like separate compartments, not steps in one sequence. The space between the stages already separates them. The heading-and-line pairs would group themselves with nothing but space, or at most a pale tone or a hairline divider. Fix: take the borders out, or swap them for space or a light tint, so the stage names lead (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, on rules and boxes that "trap each entry"; Tufte on ink that carries no information). *Convention.*
2. **The title doesn't name what's under it.** "Project notes" suggests loose notes, but the page is an ordered plan: five stages, each with one task, from Kickoff to Handover. A reader skimming a list of pages can't tell from this title that it's the stage plan. Fix: change the title so it names what the page is: the project's stages, or its plan. Use words already on the page, not a new label (WCAG 2.4.6, Headings and Labels). *Defect, minor.*

**Smaller things.**
- The order is carried only by position. Nothing marks the five boxes as a sequence, and dropping the boxes will make that easier to show. This is taste: if order matters to the team, a quiet step number or a connecting line would say "sequence" without extra words.
- "Agree on the scope, the dates, and who signs off each stage" is missing the word "on" after "signs off", so it reads clipped. A copy edit: "who signs off on each stage".

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three sizes (title, stage name, task) with clear steps between them, all in one sans family, and bold doing the work of setting the stage names apart. Keep it.
- Each task line is a verb-first sentence that names what gets done ("Visit three sites…", "Pick one direction…"). A team member knows what each stage produces without having to work it out.
- One left-aligned column with consistent left edges and generous margins. The empty space is gathered on the right, not scattered between items.
