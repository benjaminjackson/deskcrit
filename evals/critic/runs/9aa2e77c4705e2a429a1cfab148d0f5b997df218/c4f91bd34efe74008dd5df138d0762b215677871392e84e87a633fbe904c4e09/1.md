**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big serif quote, the only large thing on the page. It drops to the bold line "said a volunteer…" and only then finds the small gray paragraph. It reads as the lead of a library newsletter: calm and warm, with a lot of open space. The news, that the reading room reopens on Monday, is the last thing you read and the faintest.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the lead of a library newsletter, in email or on the web, telling regular patrons that the reading room is reopening, with the volunteer's quote for color. No claim is at stake: this is an announcement, and readers don't need persuading.

**Verdict.** This is a well-made page with its priorities backwards. The quote takes the eye, but read cold it says almost nothing, while the one thing a patron can act on ("reopens on Monday") is set in the smallest, lightest text. Swapping those two jobs would move it most.

**What would move it most**
1. **The news is buried under the color.** The headline is a quote of about 96 px that takes up most of the page. The fact readers act on, "reopens on Monday", is in the gray paragraph at the bottom left, roughly a fifth the size and the lowest-contrast text there. Someone skimming the email gets the mood and misses the date. The page should carry one main message, with details clearly smaller and grouped (Müller-Brockmann, *The Graphic Artist and His Design Problems*). Here the message and the detail have traded places. The fix: make the reopening the first thing the eye lands on. Either let the reopening line lead and give the quote a supporting size, or keep the quote large and lift "reopens on Monday" into a strong second level, in the dark ink and bold weight the attribution uses now, rather than gray body text. *Defect.*

2. **The headline doesn't land for a stranger.** "It's the 'old' reading room—nobody calls it anything else" relies on context the page never gives. The scare quotes on 'old' suggest the room now has another name, or that "old" no longer fits after the renovation. Nothing on the page says which, so the reader has to guess what the joke is, and the largest text on the page ends on a shrug. Two directions that don't add content: tie the quote to the news by putting the reopening right next to it at a level you can't miss, or say in the body what the room is officially called now, if that's the point. Fact needed from the maker: whether the reading room has a new official name that the quote is reacting to. *Defect.*

3. **"Monday" can be read two ways.** A patron who reads this on a Tuesday, or forwards it a week later, can't tell which Monday is meant. A date is exactly what people hold you to. If the wrong Monday sticks, someone turns up to a closed room or stays away a week too long. Fact needed from the maker: the calendar date of the Monday the room reopens. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The opening quotation mark of the headline sits on the text edge, so the "I" of "It's" starts about 40 px to the right of "room" and "anything" below it. At this size the ragged left edge is plain to see. Hang the opening quote mark in the margin so the letters line up with the kicker and the body (convention; the source is only partly confirmed, see Bringhurst 4.4.2).
- The headline breaks between "reading" and "room", splitting the name of the place the story is about. Rebreak so "reading room" stays on one line, for example by letting "the 'old'" sit on line one alone or by adjusting the measure (common sense; the source is unconfirmed).

**What's working.**
- The type pairing is good. A bookish serif for the human voice, a plain sans for the facts, and a single rust accent on the kicker suit a library and feel warm rather than corporate. Keep the pairing and the restraint.
- The attribution is a nice touch: a lowercase "said…" in bold sans carries the quote's sentence on. It shows it belongs to the quote by weight and position, not by an extra size. The detail "stamped returned books for thirty years" gives the quote its warmth.
- The space is deliberate. Everything sits on one left edge at about 110 px, and the open space is gathered to the right and bottom rather than scattered.
