**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable," reads the gray subtitle, then drops into a heavy black grid. The grid takes over: 32 boxed cells in thick near-black rules, all the same weight, so the eye scans row by row with nothing to guide it. It's clearly a plain, functional departure table for people taking the ferry. It feels clean but stiff, more like a spreadsheet than a timetable.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a printed or on-screen timetable for riders on the north landing to market quay run, used to answer "when is the next boat I can actually take today?" No claim is at stake. This reader needs times they can trust, not persuading.

**Verdict.** It works as a lookup table, and the times are easy to read. The biggest problem is that the one thing that can strand a rider is set exactly like the routine information: which boats skip which days. It's in the last column, in the same type as "Daily," inside a cage of heavy rules that make every cell look equally important.

**What would move it most**

1. **The day exceptions are hidden among the "Daily" entries.** Two departures run weekdays only (12:40, 17:10) and one runs weekends only (19:30). Those three cells in the Runs column look exactly like the four "Daily" cells, and they sit in the column read last. A Saturday rider who scans the left side for a time, sees 17:10, and walks to the landing has misread the table. Nothing on the page warned them. **Defect** (common sense; no catalog line covers it). Direction: make the exceptions the thing that stands out. Let "Daily" recede (lighter, or implied by leaving the cell empty under a stated default) and mark Weekdays and Weekends so they catch the eye. Or split the table by day type so a rider reads only the boats that run on their day. Either way, a rider should be able to tell at a glance that a time doesn't apply to them.

2. **Every cell is locked in a heavy box, so the rules shout louder than the times.** The 2 px near-black border around all 32 cells is as dark as the numbers. The page reads as compartments, not as departures in order. Lupton: table rules "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell"; Tufte calls this non-data ink. **Convention**, and it costs something here: the grid flattens everything to one level, which is part of why finding 1 goes unnoticed. Direction: remove the vertical rules, and replace the horizontal ones with thin pale lines, space, or a pale tint on alternate rows, so the times are the darkest thing in the table.

3. **The Period column repeats itself and splits its own groups.** "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday, Afternoon, Afternoon" puts the same word on each row, and each row has its own box, so the two morning boats look no more related than morning and evening. The column takes about a fifth of the width to say something the times already show (Palmer / NN/g on common region; Arnheim on proximity). **Convention.** Direction: either drop the column, or give each period one label with more space between periods than within them, so it acts as a section heading.

4. **"Spring season" doesn't say when the timetable applies.** A rider acts on dates. "Spring season" could mean March to May, or the operator's own season dates, and someone reading it in early June won't know whether these times still hold. **Defect.** Fact needed from the maker: the first and last dates this timetable is valid.

**Smaller things.**
- The times use proportional figures. "11:00" and "11:12" are visibly narrower than "06:40" and "15:32," so the right edges of both time columns wobble. Switch to tabular figures (Bringhurst 4.4.3).
- The column widths (about 240 / 300 / 295 / 248 px) look set by eye rather than by content. Once the rules are gone, size the columns to what they hold and bring the two time columns closer together so a departure and its arrival read as a pair.
- Only one direction is shown. If the ferry also runs quay to north landing, riders will look for those times here. If this sheet is deliberately one-way, the subtitle already says so, which is fine.

**What's working.**
- The type is all one family with clear jumps in size: a bold title, a lighter gray subtitle, bold column headers, and regular body text. There's no muddle of near-identical sizes. Keep that scale.
- The column headers are plain and specific ("Leaves north," "Arrives quay"). A rider knows which end of the trip each time belongs to without a legend.
- Departures are in time order and the page is otherwise uncluttered, with generous margins and nothing competing with the table.
