**First read.** The eye goes first to the big dark rectangle at the top, and it finds a generic gray person silhouette. From there it drops to the bold headline "An evening with Ines Varga on her new novel, The Salt Year", then back up to the rust date line, then down to the dark "Book a free seat" button. It reads as a bookshop's page for a single author event. The type and spacing feel calm and well kept, but the top of the page feels unfinished, as if the photo never loaded.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an event page for Margin Books that gets local readers to book a free seat at the reading. The ask is free and small, so the reader mostly needs to see that the evening is worth going to and where it is. They get the who, what, and when, but not the face, the book, or the place.

**Verdict.** The text half of the page does its job well. The top 40% of the page is a placeholder avatar, though. The largest thing on the page shows nothing, and it takes the first look away from the headline. Replacing it is the one change that matters.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture is a placeholder silhouette, and it dominates the page.** The 600×330 dark box shows a stock "no photo" person shape. It's the first thing the eye lands on and the biggest element on the page, but it says nothing about Ines Varga, The Salt Year, or the evening. On a book event page, the author's face or the book cover is the strongest reason to come. Here the page instead looks unfinished or broken, and the headline has to win attention back from an empty frame. It's also a stock template move ("Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual", Sokolovsky, unslop): readers have seen this shape on countless profile pages, so the page looks like anyone's. **Defect.** Fix: put a real picture from this event's world in the slot, such as the author photo or the cover of The Salt Year. If none exists yet, remove the box and let the headline lead the page. Either way, the headline or a real image should be the first thing seen. If the silhouette is a fallback for an image that failed to load, check the page in a live browser. The fallback still needs fixing, because this is what visitors see whenever the image fails.

2. **The page never says where the event is.** The copy gives the day, the time, the drinks, and the program, but no venue. A reader deciding whether to book a seat needs to know where they're going. "Find us" in the nav suggests the shop, but they'd have to leave the page to confirm it, or guess which branch (Nielsen, heuristic 6: recognition rather than recall). This is the line they'll act on, so a gap here is a defect, not a style point. **Defect.** Fix: put the place next to the date in the rust line, where the reader already looks for when. Fact needed from the maker: where the event takes place (venue or branch, and address).

**Smaller things.**
- The nav doesn't mark "Events" as the current page. All four items look the same. Give the current one a single cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g menu-design checklist).
- The date "Thursday 13 November" has no year. On a page that may stay up after the event, add the year or make past events read as past. Fact needed from the maker: the year of the event.
- The silhouette is brown on near-black with little light-to-dark contrast, so even as a placeholder it reads as a muddy box. This goes away once a real image replaces it.

**What's working.**
- The text block has a clear order: a small rust date, a large bold two-line headline set tight, then plain body text, then one dark button. Each level has an obvious job, with no near-duplicate sizes. Keep this scale.
- The body copy is concrete and reads like a person talking: "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor. Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm." The reader knows exactly what the evening involves.
- "Book a free seat" names the action and its cost in four words. It's the one primary button, and it's styled plainly. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the silhouette is a deliberate placeholder or an image that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- What happens after "Book a free seat" (a form, a confirmation, or a seat limit), and the focus states of the nav and button.
