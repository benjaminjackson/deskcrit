**First read.** The eye lands on the huge cream italic "Harbour Lights Festival", jumps up to the amber date, then drops to the two-line description. It reads as the hero of a small coastal town's festival page or poster, made for locals and day-trippers. It feels calm and evening-like, a little literary: the navy reads as night water and the amber as lamplight.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the poster or landing-page hero for a free local festival, and its job is to get people to turn up. The piece doesn't need the reader to believe a claim, only to know when and where to come.

**Verdict.** As a piece of type it's well made and does most of its job. But nowhere on it does it say where the festival is, and for an event that's the one missing fact that stops anyone coming.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no place on the piece.** The date line says when ("Saturday 14 June, from noon"). The headline says what. The description mentions "the quay" and "the old net shed", but no town, harbor, or address appears anywhere. A local might fill that in, but anyone who sees this shared online or on a poster out of town can't tell where to go, and "the old net shed" means nothing to them. This is a **defect**: it stops the reader from acting, and an event piece exists to make people act. It's a fact only you have, so it can't be fixed with words already on the piece. The usual fix is to put the place on the line with the date, so when and where read as one unit in the amber line the eye already visits second. Fact needed from the maker: the town or harbor name and, if useful, where in it the festival happens.

**Smaller things.**
- The amber date line is set in small uppercase with wide letterspacing above the hero headline. That's a stock template move (Sokolovsky, unslop, "Uppercase labels with wide tracking"), so this line looks like the line on many other pages. Setting it in sentence case at normal tracking would keep its color and its job and make it look less borrowed.

**What's working.**
- **One clear focal point at a confident scale.** The italic serif headline is roughly seven times the size of the body text. That gives the piece an unmistakable place to start, and the italic gives it warmth and occasion where an upright sans would look corporate. Keep that scale jump when you add the location; don't shrink the headline to make room.
- **A tight, three-level type scale on one left edge.** The date, headline, and paragraph share one left margin and use three clearly different sizes, plus a contrast in form (sans vs. italic serif) and in color (amber vs. cream). Nothing competes, and the order of reading is obvious.
- **The copy is specific.** "Lantern boats on the water at dusk, music on the quay, and a fish supper at the old net shed. Free entry for everyone." This is concrete, sensory, and couldn't sit on any other festival's page. The paragraph runs about 60 characters per line with comfortable line spacing.
