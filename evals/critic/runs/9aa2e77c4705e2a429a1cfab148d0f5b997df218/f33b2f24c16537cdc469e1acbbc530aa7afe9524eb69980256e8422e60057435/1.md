**First read.** The eye goes to the big black "Garden show gallery," then drops onto the large red tile at top left, then moves around a grid of flat colored blocks: green, blue, brown, yellow, purple. Each block has the same pale half-circle rising from its bottom edge. It reads as a tidy, well-built photo-gallery layout with no photos in it. It feels like a wireframe or a UI-kit demo, not a garden.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page where visitors to a garden show, or people who missed it, browse pictures of the show gardens. It asks the viewer to believe nothing; it just has to show the gardens, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** The layout works, but the page doesn't do its job: there isn't a single garden to look at. Every "photo" is a colored rectangle with a circle, so the gallery has nothing in it. Everything else on this list is minor next to that.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are stand-ins, so the gallery shows nothing (defect).** All six image areas, from the red "Rose arch" feature to the five small tiles, are flat color fills with a lighter circle clipped by the bottom edge. A visitor comes to see the rose arch and gets a red box. The captions ("Climbing roses over the east gate," "Reeds and water lilies") describe things the viewer can't see, so text and picture never make one idea. The caption is left doing the picture's job. This is also the stock "empty box or generic picture" move (Sokolovsky, unslop): it looks like any gallery template, not this show. If this is a capture where the images failed to load, it's fine once real photos appear; check it in a live browser. If the blocks really are the design, the fix is real photographs of each garden, cropped to these frames. The feature image needs the strongest one, since its size promises the best view on the page. Fact needed from the maker: the photographs of each garden.
2. **Six saturated hues fill most of the grid and drown the type (taste, and it only matters if the blocks stay).** Red, green, blue, ochre, yellow, and purple each get equal weight, so the page reads as a color chart and the small captions get lost underneath. Real photos fix this on their own. If you keep illustrated stand-ins, pull them toward one muted, garden-toned range so the type and the feature tile set the order, not the hue.

**Smaller things.**
- The pale circle in each block is a tint of its own ground, so the shape barely separates. On the yellow "Glasshouse" block it nearly disappears. This goes away with real photos.
- The small-tile captions (bold title plus gray line, about 13 px by eye) are readable but at the low end for a 1280 px page. Measure them, and consider a step up if they come in under 14 px.
- "Garden show gallery" doesn't say which show or when. A stranger landing on the page can't place it. Fact needed from the maker: the show's name and date, if the page should carry them.

**What's working.**
- The grid holds. There are three equal columns with even gutters of about 14 px, the feature spans two columns and two rows, and the "Rose arch" caption bottom lines up with "Pond edge" beside it. Keep this structure when real images go in.
- The type hierarchy is clean. The page title, the feature title (about 40 px), and the small bold titles are clearly distinct levels, and each caption sits closer to its own image than to the next tile. You can tell at a glance which words belong to which garden.
- Making one garden the large feature gives the page a place to start instead of a flat wall of equal thumbnails.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the image areas are failed loads or meant as placeholders; check in a live browser. Also hover or click behavior on the tiles, and how the grid reflows on a phone.
