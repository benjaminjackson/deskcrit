**First read.** The eye goes first to the three large figures, 3.8%, €64.20 and 12.4k, then drops to the orange bars and settles on the Saturday peak and the short Sunday bar. It reads as an owner's overview screen for a small bakery that sells online and by subscription. It feels calm and plain, and it's easy to trust at a glance.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home screen of an internal dashboard for the bakery's owner or operations lead: a quick check on how the shop is doing and which days carry the delivery load. It shows measured facts to people who already trust their own data, so no claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** This is a sound, well-built dashboard. The biggest problem is the chart, which is also the largest thing on the page: it adds up raw September orders per weekday, and September 2026 has five Tuesdays and five Wednesdays but only four of every other day. The midweek climb it shows is partly a calendar effect, and an owner who plans baking or delivery staff from it will plan for the wrong days.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday bars compare months with unequal days (defect).** In "Orders by delivery day", each bar is a September total. September 2026 starts on a Tuesday, so the Tuesday and Wednesday bars each include one more delivery day than Monday, Thursday or Friday. Reading the bars by eye and dividing by the number of days, Tuesday (about 710 ÷ 5 ≈ 142 a day) is actually below Monday (about 640 ÷ 4 ≈ 160), and Wednesday (about 760 ÷ 5 ≈ 152) is well below Thursday (about 820 ÷ 4 ≈ 205). The smooth Monday-to-Saturday rise the viewer sees isn't the real pattern. This is a sample problem, not an axis problem: the axis starts at zero and the ticks are even. To fix it, show the average orders per delivery day, or sum over whole weeks so every weekday counts the same number of times. Either way, say which you did on the chart. The source line under the chart is already the right place for it.
2. **The time frames don't line up, and nothing on the screen says how they relate (defect, copy).** Two cards say "Last 30 days" and compare "vs previous 30 days". The subscriber card says "As of 30 September 2026" and compares "vs 31 August". The chart says "orders delivered in September 2026". A reader can't tell whether the conversion rate and order value cover September or the 30 days before today. If today isn't 30 September, the cards and the chart describe different periods but sit side by side as if they were one. Dates are what an owner reports and acts on, so this needs to be unambiguous. Put every block on one stated period, or label each with its exact dates in the form the subscriber card already uses. Fact needed from the maker: the exact date range "Last 30 days" covers on this screen.
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding (taste, common sense).** "Orders by delivery day" makes the owner work out the point themselves: Saturday is the peak, Friday is close behind, and Sunday is about a third of either. On a screen meant for a quick check, one line that states the finding saves that work. Write it after fix 1, though, because the true pattern may differ from what the bars show now.

**Smaller things.**
- The bars have no value labels, so getting Saturday's real count means estimating against the 900 and 1,200 gridlines. Putting the number at the top of each bar would answer the question most owners will ask.
- The KPI labels ("Checkout conversion rate", regular weight, about 15 px) and the chart title (bold, about 17 px) do the same job as card headings but look different. Pick one treatment (taste).

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards are disciplined. In each one the figure is clearly the dominant element, the unit (%, €, k) is set lighter and grayer so it doesn't compete, and every figure has a comparison marked three ways: a ▲, a + sign and green. Keep all three, so the comparisons still read without color.
- The page has one clear grid. Logo, title and cards share the 48 px left edge, the text inside the cards and the chart shares an inner edge, and the gaps are even. Nothing is nudged off its line.
- The chart is restrained: one series in the brand orange, faint gridlines, a zero baseline, days in calendar order, and a source line. Those are the right choices. Only the data under it needs fixing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus styles and the click area of the nav links ("Orders", "Subscribers", "Products"). Check in a browser that each link's target is at least 24 px tall.
