**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold "River ferry timetable" heading, then drops to a small gray patch under it. At normal viewing size that patch is a texture, not words. Everything else is empty white, so the page reads as broken or half-loaded rather than as a timetable.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a public web page that tells ferry riders when the boats leave and how the service works. No claim is at stake.

**Verdict.** It fails. The only content is set too small to read, and it is prose where riders need times. The heading promises a timetable, and nothing on the page delivers one.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text can't be read** (defect). The three paragraphs at top left, about 120–225 px across and 140–195 px down, have letters about 4 px tall. That is far below any reading size, and much smaller than the 16 px that Material 3 uses for body text. At this size the thin strokes blur to gray, so the text also looks faint, even if its color would pass. A rider sees a smudge. The page title is the only thing they can read. Set the body at normal reading size, about 16 px, in a regular weight with dark text. Then check the contrast against the WCAG 1.4.3 minimum of 4.5:1, because at the current size it can't be judged.
2. **There is no timetable** (defect). From what I could make out, the copy describes the service in sentences: how often the boat runs, how long a crossing takes, the winter schedule, bicycles, and where printed timetables are posted. A rider who comes for "when is the next boat" gets a paragraph to decode. They have to work out departure times themselves, and the copy even sends them to a printed timetable at the landing. The heading names something the page doesn't hold (WCAG 2.4.6, headings and labels). Exact departure times are a lookup task, and Few says lookup belongs in a table. Put the times in a table: routes or landings across the top, departures down the side, and the winter schedule as its own column or block. Keep the prose short and under it as service notes. Fact needed from the maker: the actual departure times and last-boat times for each landing and season.
3. **The page has no scale and tips into one corner** (convention). The heading is about 40 px and the body about 4 px, with nothing in between. That 10:1 jump makes the body look like a caption or a mistake, not the page's content. All of the content sits in a strip about 100 px wide in the top-left corner, and the other 95% of the 1280 × 800 frame is empty. That empty space isn't working against anything (Arnheim on balance; Ruder on space set against density). It reads as missing content. Fixing items 1 and 2 will mostly fix this: give the body and the table a real column, about 45–75 characters wide, and let the table be the large block the heading leads into.

**Smaller things.**
- The body text looks like a light or thin weight, which turns to mush at small sizes (Bringhurst 6.2.3). Use a regular weight once it's resized.

**What's working.**
- The heading is plain, says what the page is in three words, and clearly comes first. Keep its weight and its spot at the top left.
- The left edges of the heading and the body line up on one vertical, which gives the page a clean anchor to build the table on.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- I couldn't proofread the copy at this rendering size. Check it for typos at full size. If the tiny text comes from a zoom or scaling bug and not from the design, check it in a live browser at 100% zoom.
