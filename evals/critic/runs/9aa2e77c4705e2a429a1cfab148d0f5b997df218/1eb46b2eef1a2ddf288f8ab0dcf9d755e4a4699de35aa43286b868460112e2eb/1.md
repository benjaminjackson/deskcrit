**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "About the trust" heading, then drops into one tall gray-black block of text and has nowhere to go after it. It reads as the About section of a small local conservation charity's website: plain, calm, and honest, but it's a wall. The most useful line for a visitor, that anyone can come and help on a Saturday morning, is buried in the eighth line of the block.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the About section of the trust's website, for local walkers and residents, and that it does two jobs: say who the trust is, and get people to come to a work day. No claim is at stake here. The facts (miles of path, 1987, staff and member numbers) ask the reader to believe nothing they'd doubt. The open question is whether a reader can act on the invitation.

**Verdict.** It tells the trust's story well enough, but it fails its second job. The volunteering invitation is buried mid-paragraph, and it doesn't say when or where to turn up, so a willing reader can't act on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation is buried inside the history.** One unbroken 12-line paragraph (lines 1–12, under the heading) runs three topics together: what the trust looks after and how it began, how big it is today, and the work days. A skimming visitor reads the first line or two and leaves before "anyone is welcome to join for the morning." Nielsen Norman Group's research on web reading shows people skim, and a long dense block loses them. That makes this a **defect** here, because the most actionable sentence is the one most likely to go unread. Fix: split the text where the topic changes. Start a new paragraph at "Work days run…" so the invitation has its own block. Better still, give it a subhead or a stronger position, so a skimmer finds "you can help" without reading the history first.
2. **"Every second Saturday" can be read two ways, and it's the one line a volunteer acts on.** It could mean alternate Saturdays or the second Saturday of each month. A reader who picks the wrong meaning turns up on a day with no work party. The paragraph also never says where people meet, what time "the morning" starts, or whether they need to sign up first, so even a reader who understands the date can't turn up. This is a **defect**: dates and commitments are what readers act on and later hold you to. Fact needed from the maker: which Saturdays the work days run on, where and at what time they meet, and whether volunteers need to book first.

**Smaller things.**
- The page has no next step after the paragraph: no link or contact for joining a work day or becoming a member. Once the facts above are supplied, a plain line or link under the work-days paragraph would give readers somewhere to go.
- The right-hand ~40% of the frame is empty. That's fine if a picture or sidebar sits there on the live page. If not, a photo of the paths or a work day would give the reader something specific to the trust (taste).

**What's working.**
- The reading setup is right. Body text looks about 20 px, with lines of about 60–66 characters, generous leading, a gently ragged right edge, and near-black on white. Keep all of it when you split the paragraph.
- The copy is concrete and local: "eleven miles of footpath, two small woods, and the old quarry," "clearing drains, mending stiles, and cutting back gorse." "We provide tools, gloves, and a hot drink… nobody needs any experience" already answers the newcomer's worries. Don't trade it for generic charity language.
- The hierarchy is simple and clear: one bold heading clearly above one body size. Nothing competes.
