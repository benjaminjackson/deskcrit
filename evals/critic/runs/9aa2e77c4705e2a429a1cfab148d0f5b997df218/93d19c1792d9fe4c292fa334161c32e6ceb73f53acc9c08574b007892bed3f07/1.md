**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a confirm dialog in a shared-calendar web app. The person who just edited choir rehearsal times uses it to decide whether to publish the change to all followers. No claim is at stake: the dialog asks for a choice, not belief.

**Verdict.** The question and its consequence are clear and well written, but the action row doesn't do its job. Four equal primary buttons, with the destructive one sitting among them, make the user read every label before they can answer a yes/no question, and put "Discard changes" one misclick from "Keep editing."

**What would move it most**
1. **Every button is styled as the primary action.** "Export as file," "Discard changes," "Keep editing," and "Save changes" are all filled blue with white bold text. The dialog asks one question, and its answer, "Save changes," should be the one thing the eye goes to. Instead the eye has four stops of equal weight, and the user has to read all four labels to find the yes. Because the four look identical, they also read as four equal options rather than one answer and some ways out. Defect, against convention (Material Design 3, "Buttons"; Apple HIG, "Buttons"): a view has one high-emphasis action. Fix: keep "Save changes" as the only filled button. Make "Keep editing" an outlined or text button, and style the rest lower still.
2. **"Discard changes" is not set apart from the safe actions.** It sits second from the left, between "Export as file" and "Keep editing," and looks exactly like them. It throws away the user's edits, and nothing about it says so. A user aiming for "Keep editing," or reaching quickly across the row, can lose their work with one click. Defect (Apple HIG, "Alerts"; Material Design, "Dialogs"): style destructive actions as destructive and keep them physically apart from the safe ones. Fix: move Discard to the far left, away from the Keep editing/Save pair. Give it a destructive treatment, such as a red text button. Add undo after it if the product can.
3. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** It answers a different question from "save or not?" and adds a fourth choice to a dialog that only needs a yes, a no, and a way back. Each extra option slows the decision and weakens the main answer (Nielsen heuristic 8, aesthetic and minimalist design; Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). Defect: it costs every user reading time to serve the few who want a file. Fix: take it out of the dialog and offer export where the calendar is managed, or at most as a quiet text link apart from the button row.

**Smaller things.** None. The type, color, spacing, and contrast all pass.

**What's working.**
- The body line "The new choir rehearsal times will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar" spells out exactly what Save does and who will see it, in the user's own words. Keep it word for word.
- The headline asks a real question with a specific object ("the shared calendar"), and the main button echoes it ("Save changes"), so question and answer match. Keep that pairing when you restyle the buttons.
- The type is restrained: one bold heading, one regular body size, generous padding, and a light border and shadow. Once the buttons have a hierarchy, the dialog will look calm and finished.
