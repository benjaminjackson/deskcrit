**First read.** My eye goes straight to the big serif headline "The finest bread in the valley." It takes in the brown line above it, drops to the two-line subhead, and then has nowhere to go. It reads as the first screen of a small local bakery's website. It feels calm, warm and old-fashioned in a good way, but also unfinished: the lower third and the right side are empty cream.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of Hartley's Bakery's website, aimed at people nearby who are deciding whether to come in or buy. A first-time visitor who might come in needs to see the bread and know when and where to get it. The subhead's specifics (wood-fired, every morning, Mill Street, since 1962) partly back up "finest." But they never see the bread, and nothing tells them what to do next.

**Verdict.** The type is handsome and the hierarchy is clear, but this is a hero for a bakery with no bread in it and no next step. The empty half of the screen is where the piece stops doing its job.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page asks the visitor to accept "the finest bread in the valley" without showing any bread.** The headline makes a claim about something you can see and almost taste, and nothing on the screen shows it. The subhead's facts help, but the doubt any local will have ("better than where I buy now?") is a judgment people make with their eyes, and there's no picture to make it with. The headline and an image should be two halves of one idea. This is my taste, not a published rule: the headline here is doing both halves alone. Fix: put a real photograph of these loaves (or the oven) in the empty right side or under the text, so the headline points at something the visitor can judge. *Defect.*
2. **The eye path dead-ends after the subhead, and the visitor gets no ask.** There's no hours line, no "visit us / order" link and no address beyond "Mill Street." Someone persuaded by the headline has to go looking for how to act on it. The path should run from the focal point to an ending you can act on (Arnheim, *The Power of the Center*). Fix: end the stack with one specific action and the practical facts a walk-in customer needs. Fact needed from the maker: opening hours, the full address, and whether people can order ahead. *Defect.*
3. **All the weight sits in the top-left 60%, and the rest of the frame is unused cream.** The bottom ~300 px and the right ~550 px are empty, with no shape or job. They don't read as chosen breathing room, so the screen looks like it's waiting for something to load rather than resting. A heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), and empty space only works when it's set against dense areas in a deliberate shape (Ruder, *Typography*, "Contrasts"). Fix: let the image from finding 1 take up that space, or move the text block down and in so the emptiness is shaped. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "The finest bread in the / valley," which leaves one word on its own second line and splits the phrase "in the valley." Break it as "The finest bread / in the valley," either by narrowing the measure or with a forced break (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The eyebrow "HARTLEY'S BAKERY · SINCE 1962" is the stock uppercase, letterspaced label above a hero (unslop, "Uppercase labels with wide tracking"). It's the move every landing page makes, so the bakery's name and its 1962 date get less credit than they deserve. Set it in sentence case at normal tracking, or show the name as the bakery's own wordmark.

**What's working.**
- The pairing is a serif display face for the headline against a plain sans for the text. It reads as traditional and handmade without being twee, and the headline is clearly in charge (three sizes with big steps between them). Keep that type scale.
- The subhead is specific and concrete: three named loaves, "every morning," "wood-fired," "Mill Street." It's the most convincing thing on the page. Keep its wording as it is.
- The cream ground, near-black text and one brown accent make a small, warm palette that fits a bakery.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The empty right half might be a picture that didn't load when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser. If a picture belongs there, findings 1 and 3 become checks on that picture instead.
