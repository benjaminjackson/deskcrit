**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, "Booking links that fill your calendar", then drops to the "Create your link" button, then slides across four identical blue-outlined cards. It reads as a scheduling SaaS for small service businesses. It feels clean and competent, but very blue and very familiar: the right half of the hero is blank, so after the headline there's nothing about this product to look at.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a landing page for owners of small appointment-based practices (clinics, tutors, coaches) that should get them to create a booking link. To act on "fill your calendar", that reader has to see proof that the link brings in bookings, or at least the link itself working. They don't see either. The only evidence is an unlabeled row of business names.

**Verdict.** The page is tidy and readable, but it doesn't make its case. The headline promises more bookings, and the only support is a gray list of names with no label. Around that, the page is a stock template: an empty hero half, four look-alike badged cards, and one blue on everything.

**What would move it most**

1. **The promise has no proof (defect).** The headline asks the reader to accept that these links "fill your calendar". That's a claim about demand, and a booking link doesn't create demand. A practice owner will object right away: "people already book me by phone or with the tool I have. Why would this bring more?" What the page shows doesn't carry the claim. The four cards describe features (time zones, reminders, forms), and only "cut down on no-shows" touches on filled slots. The strip of names at the bottom (Harbor Dental, Northfield Tutoring…) has no heading, no number, and no outcome, so it reads as decoration, not as customers who got results. Three things are missing: shown evidence that bookings go up (or that no-shows go down), a link from the names to that result, and an answer to "why not my current tool?" The fix direction: move the proof up near the headline and tie it to the claim. Or, if there is no proof, narrow the headline to something the page can show. Fact needed from the maker: what the row of business names stands for (customers, and what result they got).

2. **The hero's right half is empty and shows no product (defect).** The headline and subhead take the left ~640 px, and the right half above the cards is bare white. The page tips left, and that open area isn't composed space (Ruder on gathered versus leftover space; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). It's also the template tell of a hero with no visual (unslop). The bigger cost is that the reader never sees what a Slotwise link looks like to their guests, and that's the one thing that would make "share one link, let people pick a time" concrete. "See a live example" admits that a picture of the product is the persuasive move, then hides it behind a click. The fix direction: put a real preview of the booking page there.

3. **One blue on everything, so nothing is marked as the thing to click (convention).** The same saturated blue covers the headline, the nav links, all four card titles, the 2 px card borders, the icons, every badge, every "Learn more", and both buttons. The accent stops meaning "act here". "Create your link" competes with the headline and with four bright card frames, and non-clickable titles look exactly like links (Apple HIG and Material 3 on accent color; the unslop tells "Every element highlighted" and "Neon or bright colored borders"). Approved fix direction: keep the blue for the main action and at most one key highlight, and draw the headings, icons, card borders, and badges in neutrals. A hairline gray border will hold the cards fine.

4. **Stock feature-card row with a badge on every card (taste, template tell).** Four equal boxes, each with an icon, a bold blue title, a pill, three lines of text, and "Learn more", is the row a viewer has seen on hundreds of SaaS pages, so Slotwise looks like anyone's. The pills make it worse. "New" and "Beta" are real status, but "Auto" and "Email" aren't status at all: they restate the title. With all four badged, none stands out (unslop, "PRO, New, and Beta badges everywhere"). The fix direction: drop "Auto" and "Email", keep at most one real status badge, and consider whether all four features need equal boxes or whether one belongs to this product's pitch more than the others.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1, "Shared calendars", wraps to two lines while the other titles fit on one, so its body text and "Learn more" sit about 30 px below the other three. The rows across the cards break (Müller-Brockmann on lines aligning across columns). Shorten the title, or give every card room for a two-line title.
- The "New" pill in card 1 is centered on the two-line title instead of sitting on its first line, which looks like a near-miss.
- "Shared / calendars" breaks inside a two-word name. Keep the name on one line.
- The icons mix styles: the calendar is an outline and the clock, envelope, and layout icons are solid (unslop, "Icons in many sizes and colors"). Use one style. The "Intake forms" icon is a layout grid, not a form.
- Two filled primary buttons with different labels ("Start free" in the header, "Create your link" in the hero) for what is presumably the same action. Use one label for both.
- The customer row has no heading, so a reader can't tell what the list is (WCAG 2.4.6). This overlaps with finding 1.

**What's working.**
- The headline and the button are clearly the top two elements. There's one size jump from the headline to the subhead and another to the card text, and nothing in between to muddy the scale. Keep that.
- The subhead copy is concrete, says who does what ("Share one link, let people pick a time that works…"), and runs a comfortable ~60 characters per line.
- The left edges are disciplined. The logo, headline, subhead, button, cards, and divider all hang from the same 64 px margin and close on the same right edge at 1216 px.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the four "Learn more" links carry distinct accessible names for screen readers, and the hover and focus states of the buttons and links.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (main image and headline make one idea; every element does a job; each heading names its section)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 9 yes, 4 no (lines of text align across columns; no near-miss alignment; no accidental groups; empty space gathered; visual weight balances: the last two count as one problem)
Text: 30 lines, 28 yes, 2 no (paragraphs break cleanly; headings break at phrase boundaries)
Color: 9 lines, 7 yes, 1 no (saturated color kept to a small share), 1 skipped (dark theme)
Pictures: 12 lines, 8 yes, 2 no (one rendering style; icon stroke and fill consistent), 2 skipped (photos; scene)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 31 yes, 1 no (one primary action), 1 unsure (focus indicator)
Template tells: 10 lines, 5 yes, 5 no (no stock bright borders; color scheme has character; icons from one set; badges kept for real status; hero and feature row not the stock template, with no empty hero)
