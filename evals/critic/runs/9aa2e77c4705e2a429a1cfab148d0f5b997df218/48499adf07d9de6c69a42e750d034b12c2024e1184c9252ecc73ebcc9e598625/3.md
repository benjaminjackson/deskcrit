**First read.** I see the big blue headline first, then "Create your link", then the row of four blue-outlined cards. It reads at once as a scheduling tool for small service businesses, and it looks clean and competent. Then the eye stalls. The right half of the hero is empty, and below the headline nearly everything is the same blue, so after the headline nothing pulls ahead.

**What it's for.** This is my assumption: a SaaS landing page that should get owners of small appointment-based businesses (dentists, tutors, physios, coaches) to create a booking link. Before trusting "Booking links that fill your calendar", that reader needs to see the booking page their clients would actually use, and some sign that businesses like theirs use it. The page offers only a link to an example and five unlabeled business names, so they see neither.

**Verdict.** It's tidy but doesn't yet do its job. The page claims a lot ("fill your calendar") and shows nothing of the product. Its one accent color is spread over every element, so the page has no second focal point after the headline.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no product in it, and the empty right half shows it.** The headline and subhead take the left ~650 px. The other half of the first screen, from x≈660 to the right edge, is blank. A first-time visitor is asked to believe "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "let people pick a time that works" without seeing the booking page their clients would land on. That proof sits behind "See a live example", which is a click most people won't make. The empty half also tips the hero left and reads as space left over, not space someone chose (Ruder on gathered against leftover space). A hero with no product is also a stock template move (unslop: empty box as the hero visual). *Defect.* Fix direction: put the booking page itself, as clients would see it, in that right half, so the visitor can judge it at a glance and "See a live example" becomes the deeper look.
2. **Blue is on everything, so the main action doesn't stand out.** The headline, nav links, four card titles, four icons, four badges, four 2 px card borders, four "Learn more" links, and both buttons are all the same saturated blue. "Create your link" is the one thing the page wants clicked, but it's just one more blue shape in a field of blue. The bright borders also make the cards louder than the hero's buttons (Apple HIG and Material 3 on accent color; unslop's "Every element highlighted" and "Neon or bright colored borders"). *Convention, and a template tell.* Fix direction: keep blue for the main action and at most one highlight. Draw the headline (or at least the card titles), icons, and borders in neutrals, with the borders as quiet hairlines.
3. **A badge on every card.** "New", "Auto", "Email", and "Beta" sit next to all four card titles. Only "New" and "Beta" are a status. "Auto" and "Email" just repeat the title in a pill. With four pills, none stands out, and the row looks like many other template pages (unslop: badges everywhere). The badge also takes space from "Shared calendars", which wraps to two lines. That pushes card 1's text and "Learn more" one line below the other three cards, so the rows across the cards no longer line up (Müller-Brockmann on lines aligning across columns). *Template tell plus a visible misalignment.* Fix direction: drop "Auto" and "Email" and keep one quiet status at most. That lets the first title fit on one line and puts the four cards back on shared lines.
4. **The customer names don't say what they are.** "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" sit in small gray text under a full-width rule, so they read like footer links. They are the page's only social proof, and right now the visitor has to guess that they're customers. *Defect.* Fix direction: label the row as customers. Set it apart from the cards with space rather than a rule, which currently makes it look like the start of the footer.

**Smaller things.**
- Two filled primary buttons on one screen use different words for what looks like one action ("Start free" in the header, "Create your link" in the hero). Use one label so visitors don't wonder whether they're two different things.
- The icons mix styles: the calendar is an outline, while the clock, envelope, and layout icons are filled. Use one style.
- "Shared / calendars" breaks inside a two-word name. Fixing item 3 fixes this too.
- Four "Learn more" links pass WCAG 2.4.4 because each sits in its own card. Read alone, they don't say where they go, so naming the feature in each would help scanning.

**What's working.**
- The headline is clearly dominant, sized well above everything else, and set close to the subhead, so the page's point is legible in a second. Keep this scale step.
- The subhead is plain and concrete ("Share one link, let people pick a time that works"). It says who does what, and it runs at a comfortable ~70 characters per line.
- "See a live example" as the secondary action is the right kind of ask: it lets the visitor judge the product for themselves, and it's correctly styled lower than the main button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the buttons and links. Whether the empty right half of the hero is a visual that never loaded: check it in a live browser. If it is a visual that failed to load, item 1 becomes a question of what that visual shows.
