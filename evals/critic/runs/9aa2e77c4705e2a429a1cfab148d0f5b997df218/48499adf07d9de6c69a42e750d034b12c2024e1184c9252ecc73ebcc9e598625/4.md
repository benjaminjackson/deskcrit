**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, "Booking links that fill your calendar," then drops to the blue "Create your link" button. After that it slides along a row of four blue-bordered cards that are all equally loud. The right half of the hero is empty white. It reads as a homepage for a scheduling tool for small service businesses. It feels tidy, competent and anonymous, like many SaaS pages you've already scrolled past.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage hero of a booking-link product. Its job is to get small-business owners and solo practitioners (clinics, tutors, coaches, going by the names at the bottom) to click "Create your link." To believe the headline's promise that it will *fill* their calendar, that reader needs to see proof that it brings in more bookings than what they use now. They don't: the only evidence is five gray business names that nothing says are customers.

**Verdict.** It's clean but generic, and it doesn't do its job. The headline makes a big promise, the page shows nothing to back it up, and half the hero is empty where the proof or product could go. The same blue on almost every element means nothing stands out after the headline, so the page looks like anyone's.

**What would move it most**
1. **The promise has no proof, and the hero has no picture (defect).** The headline asks the reader to believe the links will "fill your calendar." That's an outcome claim, and a skeptical owner will ask whether it works better than their current booking page or the phone. Nothing on the page answers that:
   - The right half of the hero, about 560 px of white beside the headline, is empty. It reads either as an image that didn't load or as a stock template layout.
   - The strip at the bottom, "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets," has no label, so the reader doesn't know these are customers. Even if they guess, a name doesn't connect to "fill."
   - The four cards describe features (time zones, reminders), not filled calendars. Only "cut down on no-shows" comes close to the claim.

   The page is missing two things: a shown result that ties to the "fill" claim, and a view of the product itself. The obvious place for both is the empty right side: what a guest sees when they open the link, which also makes "See a live example" concrete. Label the name strip so it says what these businesses are to Slotwise. If the empty area is a failed image load, see "Couldn't judge" below. Tells: "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual."
2. **One blue on everything, so the accent marks nothing (convention plus template tell).** The same saturated blue is used on:
   - the headline and the four nav links
   - both filled buttons and the underlined links
   - all four card titles, icons and badges
   - the 2 px borders around every card

   Because the color marks everything, it can't point to the one thing to do, "Create your link." The four bright-bordered cards are the second-loudest thing on the page and pull the eye away from the CTA. Fix: keep the blue for the main action and at most one highlight. Make the card borders hairlines or plain tints, and set the card titles, icons and nav in neutrals (Apple HIG and Material 3 on accent color; unslop, "Every element highlighted," "Neon or bright colored borders").
3. **Stock hero layout: a left-aligned headline, a blank right half, and a row of four icon-and-bold-title cards with a pill on each (template tell).** Readers have seen this exact layout on many product pages, so Slotwise's own point gets lost in it. It's worst in the badges. "New" and "Beta" are real statuses, but "Auto" and "Email" are just features dressed up as badges. With four pills in one row, none of them signals anything (unslop, "PRO, New, and Beta badges everywhere"; "Feature cards with an emoji and a bold title"). Fix: keep at most one badge where status matters (Beta, probably). Then let the hero's right side show this product, so the hero belongs to Slotwise rather than the template.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1, "Shared calendars": the "New" badge forces the title onto two lines. That pushes its body text and "Learn more" about 30 px below the other three cards, so the rows don't line up across the cards (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Removing the badge fixes it.
- Four identical "Learn more" links can only be told apart by the card around them. Screen-reader link lists will read "Learn more" four times (WCAG 2.4.4). Name the feature in each link, or give each a label that does.
- Headline: "calendar" sits alone on the second line. A slightly narrower or wider measure would break it into more even lines (taste, after Bringhurst on lone last words).
- The gray customer names look light and small. Measure them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- The page is unbalanced: everything sits on the left and the top right is empty, so it tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). This goes away once finding 1 fills that space.

**What's working.**
- The subhead is good copy. "Share one link, let people pick a time that works, and keep every meeting in the right place" says who does what, in plain words. Keep it.
- The card body copy speaks to the reader's own problems ("so nobody has to do the math," "to cut down on no-shows," "so the first minutes are useful"). That is the clearest writing on the page; keep that voice when the cards are restyled.
- The type scale is disciplined: one sans family, a big headline, a clear body size, one small size. The CTA pairing is also right: one filled button, with "See a live example" as an underlined secondary link.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half of the hero is a design choice or an image or animation that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the buttons, nav and links.

**Checklist.**
- The whole piece: 8 lines, 6 yes, 2 no (main image and headline work together; headings name their sections)
- What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
- Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (lines align across columns; balance)
- Text: 30 lines, 28 yes, 2 no (clean line breaks; text contrast)
- Color: 9 lines, 8 yes, 1 no (saturated color kept to a small area)
- Pictures: 12 lines, 11 yes, 1 no (no empty image area)
- Charts and data: skipped
- Interface: 33 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (link purpose), 3 unsure (states, focus visible, focus not obscured)
- Template tells: 10 lines, 5 yes, 5 no (stock effects; color character; badges; hero layout; specific pictures)
