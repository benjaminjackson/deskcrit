**First read.** The eye lands on $4.82M, the largest and boldest figure on the screen, then moves across the three stat cards and drops into the big blue bar chart. The black box around the chart is the darkest ink on the page, so it pulls attention away from the bars. It reads as a SaaS company's annual revenue dashboard: calm, orderly and believable at a glance.

**What it's for.** My assumption is that this is an internal finance or leadership dashboard that a founder, finance lead or board member uses to check how 2025 revenue went. No claim is at stake because it reports figures and asks for no belief. It does depend on the figures agreeing with each other, though, and that is where it slips.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job: there is a clear place to start, each headline number has a comparison, the chart is honest and the source line is there. But the plan table and the chart contradict each other, and a dashboard whose numbers don't reconcile loses the reader's trust in all of them. Fix that first. The heavy chart frame comes second.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Revenue by plan, 2025" table doesn't match the chart above it.** This is a defect.
   - The table's Total is $4.82M, the same figure as the "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" card.
   - The 12 bars of "Monthly revenue, 2025" read by eye as roughly 310 + 330 + 340 + … + 447 thousand, which adds up to about $4.5M of recognized revenue.
   - So the table is either year-end ARR split by plan under a revenue label, or one of the two numbers is wrong. Anyone who adds up the bars, and finance people do, will find a $0.3M gap and start doubling down on every other number on the page.
   - Fix: the heading has to name what the table actually holds. If it is ARR by plan, label it that way, matching the card ("Annual recurring revenue by plan, Dec 2025"). If it really is 2025 revenue, its total has to match the chart. Fact needed from the maker: whether the plan figures are Dec 2025 ARR or 2025 recognized revenue.
2. **The chart frame and gridlines outweigh the data.** This breaks a convention (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*; Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*).
   - The plot sits in a near-black box about 1–2 px thick, and the gridlines at 100–400 are dark gray to black. They carry no data, yet they are the strongest lines in the lower two-thirds of the screen.
   - What it costs: the chart reads as a heavy black box before it reads as a steady climb, and it competes with the stat cards for first look.
   - Fix: remove the frame and make the gridlines thin and pale gray, so the blue bars are the darkest marks in the chart.

**Smaller things.**
- **Lower row is off the card grid:** the chart ends around x=960 and the plan table starts around x=1012, but the cards above split at about 907 and 925. Put the chart and table on the same column lines as the cards, for example chart under the first two cards and table under the third.
- **Section headings miss by a few pixels:** "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025" sit about 4 px apart vertically. Put them on one baseline.
- **Mixed units:** the chart is in thousands of dollars, while the cards and table use $M. A reader has to convert to compare a Dec bar with the totals. Use one unit across the screen (Numbers in mixed formats).
- **Total row looks like a plan row:** "Total" has the same weight and rule as Starter, Team and Business, so it can read as a fourth plan. Set it apart, for example with a heavier rule above it.
- **Plan table has no comparison:** every stat card shows change from 2024, but the plan figures have none, so you can't tell which plan drove the 18% growth. Add the prior-year comparison the cards already use.
- **Neutral chart title:** "Monthly revenue, 2025" names the chart but not the finding. On a live dashboard whose data changes, this is acceptable, so it is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The stat cards:** each has a plain-language label with its period ("Dec 2025", "2025"), a large figure and a green change against 2024 that carries a + sign, so the meaning doesn't depend on color alone. Keep that pattern exactly.
- **The chart's honesty:** bars start at zero, there is one color and a natural month order, the subtitle gives the units, and there is a source line ("closed invoices only"). That source note is the kind of detail that earns a finance reader's trust.
- **Restraint:** one blue, gray neutrals, one typeface, and empty space gathered into the sidebar and the right column rather than scattered.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the "Jan to Dec 2025" chip is a date picker. It doesn't look like a control. If it is one, it needs a visible cue such as a chevron.
- Hover values on the bars, and how the layout reflows at narrower widths.
