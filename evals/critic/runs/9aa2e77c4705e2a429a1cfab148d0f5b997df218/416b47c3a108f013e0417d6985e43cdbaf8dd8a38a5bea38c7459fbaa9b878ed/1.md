**First read.** The eye lands straight on the big italic "Harbour Lights Festival". It's warm cream on deep navy and clearly the center of the piece. Next it goes up to the amber date, then down to the two-line description, and there it stops. It reads as an evening event at a small harbor: calm, a little nostalgic, inviting. It's handsome, but after the last line the viewer is left asking "so where is it?"

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero of a web page or a digital poster for a free local harbor festival, read by locals and visitors deciding whether to go. It's an event notice with no claim to prove, so nothing here needs the reader's belief.

**Verdict.** The type and color are well judged, but as an event announcement it fails at the one job it has: it never says where the festival is. It also gives the reader nothing to do next, so the eye runs out after the body text.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no location anywhere.** The only places named are "the quay" and "the old net shed" in the body text (lower left). Those mean something to someone who already knows the harbor and nothing to anyone else. A reader with the date in hand still can't plan to go, and a poster or link shared beyond the village loses exactly the people it's meant to bring in. This is a **defect** in the copy. The direction is to put the place next to the date, where the reader already looks for the "when and where", at the same amber label level. Fact needed from the maker: the name of the harbor or town where the festival is held, and the address or meeting point if there is one.
2. **The eye path ends with no next step.** The piece goes headline, date, two lines of description, then about 200 px of empty navy at the bottom with nothing in it. There's no link to a program, map, or times, so the viewer who is now interested has nowhere to go. The empty band reads as space left over rather than space set aside for something. This is a **defect** for a web hero, and **common sense** for a poster too (Arnheim, *The Power of the Center*, on a clear eye path ending somewhere). The direction is to end the path with one specific next action below the body text, using the space already sitting empty there. "Free entry for everyone" means it shouldn't be a ticket button. It should be whatever the reader needs to actually turn up, such as the program or how to get there, and the maker supplies that.

**Smaller things.**
- The date "Saturday 14 June, from noon" has no year. Today, 14 June 2027 falls on a Monday, so a reader who checks a calendar will find the weekday and date don't match and won't know which is right. Fact needed from the maker: the year of the festival.
- The date line above the headline is set in small uppercase letters with wide tracking. That's a stock landing-page move readers have seen on countless heroes, and it makes this piece look more generic than the rest of it deserves (unslop, Sokolovsky, "Uppercase labels with wide tracking"). Set it in sentence case at normal or slightly open tracking, keeping the amber so it still reads as the key detail.

**What's working.**
- **The headline face.** The large serif italic, kept tight over two lines, has the soft, hand-lettered warmth of an evening harbor event, and it dominates without any help from boxes or effects. Keep it as the single focal point.
- **The palette.** Deep navy, cream, and one warm amber feel like dusk and lanterns. They match the subject without illustrating it, and the amber is rationed to one line, so it pulls the eye to the date.
- **The body copy.** It's short and concrete. "Lantern boats on the water at dusk, music on the quay, and a fish supper at the old net shed" tells the reader what the evening is actually like, which most festival blurbs don't. The measure of about 60 characters and the line spacing are comfortable.
