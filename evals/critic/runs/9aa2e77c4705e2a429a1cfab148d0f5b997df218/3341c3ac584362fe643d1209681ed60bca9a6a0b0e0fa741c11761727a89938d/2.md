**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", reads down through the paragraph and the three short lines, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. It's clearly the landing page for a small ferry service, aimed at locals and day-trippers. It feels calm, tidy and plain. It also feels unfinished: everything sits in the left 60% of the frame and the right side is bare white.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a ferry operator's website, for people deciding whether to take the crossing and wanting to know when it runs. No claim is at stake: the page states plain facts (twelve minutes, every forty minutes, bikes free) and asks the reader to believe nothing more than that.

**Verdict.** The copy and type are clean and the page does its basic job: you know what it is and what to click. Two things hold it back. All the weight is on the left, with nothing on the right to balance it and nothing to show the bay. And the one fact a traveler acts on, when the boats run, is vague.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page tips left, and the bay is never shown.** The headline, paragraph, list and button all sit between about x=160 and x=790. The right ~490 px and the bottom ~230 px are empty, and that space isn't doing anything. On a 1280-wide screen it reads as a picture that didn't load, not as a choice. A heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). It also costs the page its mood: "calm" and "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck" are the selling feeling, and right now they exist only as words. Fix direction: put real weight on the right that belongs to this ferry, most naturally a picture of this crossing or this boat. If the page is meant to be text-only, widen and scale the type block so it fills the frame on purpose. *Convention (balance) and taste (the missing picture).* If an image is supposed to be there and failed in the capture, check it in a live browser.
2. **"From early morning until late evening" leaves out the times people plan around.** This is the line a traveler acts on, and it can be read two ways: is the first boat at 6 or at 8? Is the last one at 8 pm or at 11? Someone who guesses wrong misses the last crossing home. The vagueness also pushes every visitor through the timetable button just to answer "can I go now?" *Defect (common sense; the anticipation principle isn't on the approved list).* The fix is to replace the vague phrase with the actual first and last departures, kept just as short. Fact needed from the maker: the first and last departure times from the north landing.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline break: "twelve / calm minutes" splits the number from its unit across lines (Bringhurst 2.4.6). Rebreak as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes", or adjust the measure so "twelve calm minutes" stays together.
- The feature list (~18 px, regular, dark gray) is only a step or two smaller than the paragraph (~20 px, same weight and color), with no bullet or other marker. The three lines read as stray leftovers of the paragraph rather than a list (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). Give them an obvious difference, such as a marker, weight, or clearly smaller size, or fold them into the paragraph.
- The list repeats the paragraph: "Seats on the open deck" echoes "from the open deck", and "Bicycles travel free" half-repeats "Bring a bicycle". Cut the repetition from one place or the other so each line adds something.
- "The bay" and "the north landing" are never named, so a stranger can't tell which crossing this is. Fact needed from the maker: the names of the two landings (or the bay).

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a concrete promise with a number in it ("twelve calm minutes"). It's specific to this service and is the right thing to have dominate. Keep it as the largest element.
- The plain, single-face type with one blue accent (eyebrow and button) is a restrained palette, and the button is the only strong color on the page. Keep that discipline when adding anything to the right side.
- The single primary action, "See the timetable", names exactly what you get when you click it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty right half may be an image that didn't load. Check it in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states of the button.
