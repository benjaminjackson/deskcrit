**First read.** My eye lands on "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then drops through the paragraph, the three short lines and the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a website for a small local ferry, for day-trippers and locals. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, but sparse to the point of looking unfinished: everything sits in the left 60% of the frame, and the right side and bottom are empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the top of the home page for a passenger ferry, meant to get someone planning a trip across the bay to open the timetable. No claim is at stake here: a route, a crossing time and what you can bring are plain facts, not things the reader has to be persuaded of.

**Verdict.** It's clean and almost works: the order of importance is right, the type is well set and the next step is specific. But the copy never says where the ferry goes, which is the first thing a traveler needs, and the empty right half makes the page look like it's still loading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page never says where you arrive (defect).** The headline says "Cross the bay" and the paragraph says boats leave "the north landing", but nothing on the screen names the other side or what town or place the north landing is in. A reader checking whether this ferry fits their trip has to click through to the timetable just to learn the route, and someone comparing ways across the bay may give up first. Put both ends of the route somewhere the eye passes in the first second: in the headline or in the opening of the paragraph ("Boats leave the north landing for …"). Fact needed from the maker: the name of the landing the ferry crosses to, and where the north landing is.
2. **The right half of the screen is empty, so the page looks unfinished rather than calm (taste; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance).** All the weight sits in a column at the left, from about x 160 to 790. Nothing balances it on the right (about 490 px wide) or below the button (about 230 px). For a page selling a pleasant crossing ("watch the harbour slide past from the open deck"), the reader is asked to imagine the very thing the space could show. A viewer may also wonder whether an image failed to load. If a picture is meant to go there, check it in a live browser. If not, either give that area something specific to this ferry (the boat, the bay or the route) or let the text column grow and center the group so the empty space looks deliberate.

**Smaller things.**
- The three lines "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" are set at about 18 px, just under the paragraph's ~20 px. Two text sizes this close read as a mistake, not as two levels (Bringhurst, 3.1.1). Use one size, or set the list apart by weight or a marker.
- The list has no bullets or other markers, so at a glance it reads as a fourth, ragged paragraph rather than a set of perks. Add simple markers or a clearer indent so it scans as a list.
- The list repeats the paragraph: "open deck" appears in both, and the bicycle comes up twice. Cut the overlap so each line adds something new (common sense).

**What's working.**
- The headline clearly leads: it's large, bold and tightly set over two lines, with clear steps down to the paragraph and the button. Keep that order of importance.
- "See the timetable" is a specific action that matches what a traveler wants next, and it's the only button, so there's nothing to choose between.
- The small palette (dark gray text, one blue for the brand name and the button) suits a calm public service. The blue keeps the brand name and the action visibly linked.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty right side is meant to hold a picture that didn't load in this capture; check in a live browser. I also couldn't check the button's hover and focus states, or how the page reflows on a phone.
