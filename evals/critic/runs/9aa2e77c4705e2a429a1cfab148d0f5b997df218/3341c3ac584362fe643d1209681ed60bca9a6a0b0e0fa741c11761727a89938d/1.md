**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then drops through the paragraph, the three plain lines and the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads at once as the top of a small ferry company's website. It feels calm and tidy, but bare. All the content is in the left 60% of the frame, the right side is blank white, and nothing on screen shows a bay, a boat or a place.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a local ferry operator's website, on desktop, for locals and day-trippers deciding whether to take the boat and when. Hardly any claim is at stake: the page states times and onboard facts and asks the reader only to look up the timetable.

**Verdict.** It works as a clear, readable hero with one obvious action. It is weak as a page for a place, though. The biggest problem is the empty right half with no picture of the crossing. The page looks like any template and leaves the "calm" promise unshown. Close behind: it never says where the boat goes.

**What would move it most**
1. **Empty right half, no visual of the crossing.** About 500 px of the right side of the frame is blank. The type block sits heavy on the left with nothing balancing it, so the page looks unfinished or half-loaded, not deliberately spare. The headline promises "twelve calm minutes" and the paragraph asks you to "watch the harbour slide past", but nothing lets the viewer see either. They have to take "calm" on the word alone. Text on white with an empty hero slot is a stock template move, so the page looks like anyone's and the place, which is this company's whole offer, never appears. Visual weight needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), and a hero visual should come from the product's own world (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"). Fix direction: fill the right side with a picture of this crossing, such as the boat, the deck or the harbour seen from it, so image and headline make one idea. Otherwise, recompose the type so the empty space is gathered on purpose rather than left over. If an image was meant to sit there and didn't load, check it in a live browser. Defect (balance) plus template tell.
2. **The page never says where the boat goes.** "Cross the bay" and "the north landing" give the starting point but not the destination. The viewer has to click into the timetable to learn something the first screen should tell them, and it is the first thing a day-tripper checks. The headline's promise only lands once they know what's on the other side. Fact needed from the maker: the name of the landing or town the ferry arrives at. Defect.
3. **The three-line list repeats the paragraph and barely differs from it in size.** The paragraph already says "Bring a bicycle" and "the open deck". The list then says "Bicycles travel free" and "Seats on the open deck", so two of the three lines are repeats. The list also looks a couple of pixels smaller than the paragraph (around 18 px against 20 px), with no markers and line gaps close to the paragraph's own leading. It reads as a looser, slightly shrunken continuation of the paragraph, not a separate level, which makes the difference look like an accident (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). The only new facts, "free" and "hot drinks", are the ones that get lost. Fix direction: cut the overlap. Either fold the new facts into the paragraph (an edit like "Bring a bicycle (it travels free), a pram or a picnic…") or keep a list of only the new items and make it plainly its own level, through weight, markers or a clear size step. Defect (wasted reading) plus convention.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks "twelve / calm minutes", splitting the number from its unit. Break after "bay" instead: "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" (Bringhurst 2.4.6).
- "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" is vague about the hours. The reader acts on those hours, though the timetable button covers this. Fact needed from the maker: the first and last sailing times, if they should appear on this screen.

**What's working.**
- There is one clear focal point. The headline far outweighs everything else, so the eye knows where to start, and it states a concrete benefit (twelve minutes) instead of a slogan.
- "See the timetable" is the right single action. It is specific and names exactly what the reader gets, and it is the only blue block besides the small brand label, so nothing competes with it.
- The palette is restrained, near-black plus one blue on white, and the left-aligned text column has a comfortable line length (about 65–70 characters) and loose leading. It reads easily and fits the calm tone. Keep this when you add the image.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The button's hover and focus states.
- Whether the empty right side is intended or an image that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
