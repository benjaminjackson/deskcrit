**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours" at the top left, then drops down three bold branch names, each with two lines of detail. It reads as a plain public-library hours notice: calm, tidy, and not trying to be clever. Then you hit "Saturday, ten to four" and have to stop and work out whether that means 10:00 to 4:00 or 3:50.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a library web page or posted notice that tells local residents when and where each branch is open, so they can turn up while it's open. No claim is at stake; this is plain information.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and well grouped, but it fails at its one job: telling people the hours at a glance and without ambiguity. Times written out in words read two ways, are slow to scan, and the mobile library has no times at all.

**What would move it most**
1. **Times in words read two ways (defect).** "Saturday, ten to four" at Main branch, and "Tuesday to Friday, ten to six" and "Saturday, ten to two" at Harbour, can each be read as a single clock time: in British usage, "ten to four" is 3:50. The piece uses British forms ("Harbour", "school gates"), so its readers are exactly the people most likely to hear it that way. Opening hours are what people act on. A resident who reads "ten to two" as a time may not know when the branch opens or closes, and either arrives at a locked door or doesn't come at all. Even read correctly, words make the eye decode each line instead of glancing at it. Write the times as numerals with a dash, keeping the times the piece already gives: "Saturday, 10–4" (or "10am–4pm" if the maker wants am and pm spelled out).
2. **The hours are set as prose, so they can't be scanned or compared (convention).** Each branch is two sentence-like lines, so to answer "who's open Thursday evening?" you have to read all six lines. Few, *Show Me the Numbers*: when readers need to look up exact values, use a table with aligned values. Set the days and the times as two aligned columns under each branch name: days left-aligned, times lined up with each other (Bringhurst, 4.4.3, on aligning columns of figures). Keep the current light, rule-free look.
3. **The mobile library gives a day and place but no time (defect).** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" tell a resident where to go, not when. A van that's only there for part of the day is the entry where a missing time costs the most. Fact needed from the maker: the times the mobile library is at the market square on Wednesday and at the school gates on Thursday.

**Smaller things.**
- Harbour branch lists Tuesday to Saturday only. The reader has to infer that it's closed Sunday and Monday, and Main branch is silent about Sunday too. If they're closed, saying so heads off the "is it just missing?" doubt. Fact needed from the maker: whether each branch is closed on the days not listed.

**What's working.**
- **Grouping by space alone.** Lines within a branch are about 33 px apart and the gaps between branches are about 75 px, so the three groups separate cleanly with no boxes or rules. Keep this when the times go into columns.
- **A clear, short hierarchy.** A large bold title, then branch names that differ from the text by weight rather than size, then regular text. That's three levels with one face and no extra sizes (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). Each branch name sits right on top of its own lines.
- **Restraint.** Dark gray on white, one family, no decoration. That suits a civic notice and puts nothing between the reader and the hours.
