**First read.** In version 1 the eye goes first to the three tinted boxes and the bold card titles under them. The headline "Hardy plants grown on our hillside" is set smaller than "Perennials" and "Fruit trees", so you find it on a second look, and the page reads like a category index with no lead. In version 2 the headline is about twice the size, so the eye lands on it, drops to the intro, and then moves along the three cards. It reads as a small, calm, outdoorsy plant nursery that sells by mail. It feels honest but plain.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page of a small nursery's online shop. Its job is to tell a gardener in a cold area what makes these plants different and send them into the catalogue.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the headline size, and that one change gives the page a starting point and puts the nursery's main claim first. Version 1 hides that claim under its own category labels. Both versions still have two problems: the pictures are empty colour blocks, and there's nothing that tells the visitor what to do next.

**What changed, and why it matters**
Version 1's headline (about 26 px) is smaller than the card titles (about 28 px), so the hierarchy runs backwards. The three card titles and the headline compete to be seen first, and the cards win. The visitor sees "Perennials / Fruit trees / Workshops" before they read why this nursery is worth buying from. Version 2 sets the headline at about 60 px. That makes it plainly the first thing seen, with clear steps down to card titles, intro and body text, as Tschichold asks in *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size. It's a defect in version 1, and version 2 fixes it.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **The three picture boxes are empty tints.** Under each card title there's a flat green rectangle where a photo should be. This is a plant nursery, and the photos are the product. Without them the visitor can't see the plants, the hillside or the class, and the cards look unfinished. The headline's claim ("grown on our hillside") has nothing to back it up. If the photos simply failed to load in this capture, that needs checking. As built, it's a defect. Fix: put real photos in, ideally ones that prove the headline, such as plants in rows outdoors and the trees on dwarf roots. That way the picture and the headline make one argument.
2. **There's no next step.** The page has no button and nothing marked as a link apart from the top nav. The cards don't look clickable (no arrow, no "Shop perennials", no underline), so the visitor has to guess whether to click a card or go to the nav. This is a defect: it slows the one thing the page exists to do. Fix: give each card a specific link such as "Browse 300 perennials" or "See spring classes", and consider one main action under the intro. Material and Apple HIG both say a view should have one clearly primary action.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline in version 2 breaks as "on our / hillside", leaving one word alone on the second line and splitting the phrase. Narrow the headline box or rebreak it as "Hardy plants / grown on our hillside" (Bringhurst 2.4.2 covers the lone last word; the phrase-break point is common sense).

**What's working.**
- The copy has substance. "Raised outdoors in Fernhill, so they are ready for a cold garden on the day they arrive" gives a reason and tells the buyer what it means for them. The card lines are specific: "Over 300 kinds", "dwarf roots", "each spring". Keep that voice.
- The three-column grid is clean: shared left edges, equal gutters, and headings sitting close to their own text. Keep it.
- The palette is muted greens on a pale green-white with dark green type. It suits a hardy-plant nursery, and the contrast is comfortably strong.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are links, and whether the blank bottom third of the screen is just the end of the capture or a gap before more content.
