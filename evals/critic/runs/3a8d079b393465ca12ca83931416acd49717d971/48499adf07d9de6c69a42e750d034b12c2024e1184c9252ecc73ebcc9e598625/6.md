**First read.** My eye goes to the big blue headline, then the blue "Create your link" button, then along the four blue-outlined cards. It reads as a booking-link SaaS landing page: clean, competent and generic. The right half of the hero is empty white, and after the first few seconds everything looks the same shade of blue, so nothing pulls you further in. The row of grey names at the bottom reads like leftover footer text, not customers.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the first screen of a marketing page. It should persuade small appointment-based businesses (the dentists, tutors, physios and vets in the bottom row) to create a booking link.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and legible, but it doesn't sell. It describes the product and never shows it, and the generic headline gives these customers no reason to pick this over the booking tool they already know. The biggest problem is the empty right half of the hero, where a real booking page should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero tells and never shows.** The headline and subhead take the left half and the right half (about x 680–1216, y 110–380) is empty. The viewer is asked to "Create your link" without ever seeing what their customers would book through. The best ask on the page, "See a live example", lets the viewer judge the product for themselves, but it's demoted to an underlined text link and there's no example in sight. The empty right side also makes the hero tip left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": the heavy side has no counterweight). Fix: put a real booking page in the right half, ideally one of the named customers' (say Harbor Dental's slot picker in the guest's time zone). That shows the product, balances the hero, and makes "See a live example" a click into something the viewer has already seen. *Defect.*
2. **The headline could be anyone's.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" is a claim every scheduling tool makes, so nothing on this screen says what's different here. It's also slightly off for this audience: a physio or vet doesn't need a link to *fill* their diary, they need fewer phone calls and no-shows. The card copy already has sharper lines ("Send a note the day before and an hour before to cut down on no-shows"). Fix: write the headline in the customer's terms and end it on what changes for them, for example that clients book themselves without calling you, and set it against what they do now. *Taste, argued: generic copy gives the viewer nothing to act on.*
3. **One blue does every job, so it marks nothing.** The headline, nav links, both buttons, every card border, card title, icon, badge and "Learn more" link are the same saturated blue. The button gets no colour contrast from the headline above it, and the clickable and non-clickable parts look like one group. Apple HIG and Material both say to keep the accent for the main action and interactive state. Fix: keep the blue for the buttons, links and at most one highlight. Make the headline and card titles near-black, the card borders light grey (or drop them for a pale tint), and the icons a neutral or muted tint. *Convention, and here it costs emphasis.*
4. **The customer row isn't social proof yet.** "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" at the bottom are plain grey words under a thin blue rule, with no label, logos or quote. Without a label (WCAG 2.4.6, headings and labels) a stranger can't tell whether these are customers, partners or links. Fix: label it ("Booking with Slotwise:") and give it logos or one short result from one of those businesses. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The "New" badge squeezes the "Shared calendars" title onto two lines, splitting the name ("Shared / calendars"). That pushes card 1's body and "Learn more" about 30 px below the other three cards, so the rows don't line up (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Move the badges above or below the title, or drop them.
- The badges mix kinds of label. "New" and "Beta" are product status, but "Auto" and "Email" just repeat the card title. Keep only the status badges or remove them all.
- "Start free" (nav) and "Create your link" (hero) look like the same action under two names. Pick one label.

**What's working.**
- The type is disciplined: one sans family, a clear scale (headline, subhead, card title, card body, small grey row) and a tight two-line headline. Keep that scale when you recolour.
- The card body copy is concrete and ends on what the viewer gets ("so nobody has to do the math", "so the first minutes are useful"). That's the voice the headline should use.
- The grid holds: the logo, headline, card edges and rule all share the 64 px left edge, and the four cards have equal gutters.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav, the buttons and the four "Learn more" links. The "Learn more" links pass WCAG 2.4.4 only because each card title gives them context, so check that screen readers announce that context.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (visual and headline make one idea; every element does a job; headings name their section)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 10 yes, 3 no (lines align across columns; only related elements look alike; visual weight balances)
Text: 30 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (headings break at phrase boundaries)
Color: 9 lines, 8 yes, 1 no (strong color kept to a small share)
Pictures: 12 lines, 12 yes
Charts and data: skipped
Interface: 33 lines, 31 yes, 1 no (puts what the user needs next in front of them), 1 unsure (focus indicator)
