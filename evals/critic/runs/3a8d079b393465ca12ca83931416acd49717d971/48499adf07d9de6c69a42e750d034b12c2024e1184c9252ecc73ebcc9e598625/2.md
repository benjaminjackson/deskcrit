**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline and then drops to the four blue-outlined cards, which pull nearly as hard as the "Create your link" button between them. The right half of the hero is empty. The row of business names at the bottom reads like a footer menu, not like customers. It reads as a booking or scheduling SaaS homepage. It feels clean and competent, and it could belong to anyone.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the hero of a marketing homepage for a booking-link product. It should get small appointment-based businesses (clinics, tutors, coaches, vets) to click "Create your link".

**Verdict.** It's tidy, the copy is clear, and nothing is broken, but it doesn't sell. Nothing on screen shows the product, and the one blue is spread over every element, so the call to action doesn't stand out. A visitor learns what Slotwise is without seeing a reason to choose it over the booking tool they already know.

**What would move it most**
1. **The product is missing from the hero.** The right half of the top section, beside the headline, is blank. "Booking links that fill your calendar" is a promise you're asking visitors to take on trust. One picture of a booking page with open slots, or a week of filled calendar, would prove it and make the headline concrete. Right now the blank space reads as a picture that never loaded, not as a choice. It also leaves the hero heavy on the left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Put a product view there that shows the result the headline promises. If this is a picture that failed to load in the capture, then the finding is the left-heavy hero, not the missing product view. *Defect.*

2. **The accent blue is on almost everything, so the main button gets lost.** The same saturated blue is used for the headline, nav links, four card borders, four icons, four card titles, four badges, four "Learn more" links, the divider and both buttons. When everything is the accent colour, the eye has no cue for where to act. The 2px blue card borders also make the cards the second-loudest thing on the page, so they compete with "Create your link". Keep the blue for the primary button and at most one highlight. Make the headline near-black, the card borders pale grey or a pale tint (or remove them), and the titles and icons neutral (Apple HIG, Color; Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, on replacing rules with a pale tone). *Convention, and it costs something here.*

3. **Two different primary buttons.** "Start free" in the top right and "Create your link" in the hero are both solid blue buttons with different labels. That raises the question of whether they do different things. Use one label for one action, or make the nav button lower-emphasis. Of the two labels, "Create your link" is the better one because it names exactly what you get (Material Design 3, button emphasis). *Convention.*

4. **The proof doesn't read as proof.** Harbor Dental, Northfield Tutoring, Kestrel Physio, Lumen Coaching and Orchard Vets are set in small grey text with no label, under a full-width blue rule. They look like a footer menu. These names are your best argument, because they show the viewer businesses like their own. Give the row a label that says what it is (for example "Booking with Slotwise:"), and use their logos or one number with a unit (e.g. "fewer no-shows since…"). That lets a viewer compare against a business they recognise rather than take your word for it. *Defect (the copy and grouping mislead the viewer).*

**Smaller things.**
- In the first card, the "New" badge forces "Shared calendars" onto two lines. That pushes its text and "Learn more" about 30px below the other three cards, so the lines don't sit level across the row. Shorten the title or move the badge so all four titles fit on one line.
- "Shared / calendars" breaks a two-word name across lines. The same fix applies.
- The "Auto" and "Email" badges are styled like "New" and "Beta" but don't mark status. They describe the feature. Drop them so that "New" and "Beta" mean something.
- Four identical "Learn more" links don't say where they go. Name each one ("How reminders work") or make the whole card the link (WCAG 2.4.4).

**What's working.**
- The copy is plain, and it speaks to the viewer. "So nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows" and "so the first minutes are useful" each end on what the viewer gets, not just on what the feature does. Keep that pattern when you edit.
- "Create your link" with "See a live example" beside it as a text link is a good pair. The first names the exact thing you'll make. The second lets a sceptical visitor judge the product for themselves before signing up. It's the strongest ask on the page, so give it the visual priority it deserves.
- The type scale is clean: headline, subhead, card title, body and a small label. The steps between sizes are clear, and the cards sit neatly on a four-column grid with even gutters.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half of the hero is a picture that didn't load. Check this in a live browser.
- Focus states on the buttons and links.
