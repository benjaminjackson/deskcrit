**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "Changing your payment card", drops into the intro, then runs down the three bold subheads like a contents list. It reads as a plain help-centre article for a paying customer. It feels calm, competent and slightly bare, which suits the job.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop help article for an account owner who needs to swap their card, or who wants to know what happens when a payment fails.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: the hierarchy is clean and the article is easy to scan. The biggest problem is the copy in the highest-stakes section. "If a payment fails" leaves the reader unsure when their account will lock and how to stop it. After that, the subheads float midway between sections instead of sitting with their own text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The failed-payment timeline can be read two ways** (defect). This is the last paragraph. "We try again after three days… After a third failed try the account moves to read-only." Does the original charge count as the first try? Then read-only comes after about 6 days. Or are there three retries? Then it comes after about 9. A customer planning around payday will hold you to whichever reading they made. The missing comma also makes the sentence trip ("a third failed try the account moves"). The paragraph also never says what read-only stops the reader doing, and it never tells them the fix, which is in this very article. Say it as a timeline in the reader's terms, for example: "If a payment fails, we try again 3 days later, and again 3 days after that. If the third try fails, your account becomes read-only: you can view but not edit. To restore it, add a new card above." This is the paragraph people arrive at worried, so it has to be the most exact one on the page.
2. **The subheads float between sections** (convention). "Add a new card", "Remove an old card" and "If a payment fails" each have about 30 px of space above them and about 30 px below. So each heading belongs as much to the paragraph before it as to the one it introduces. Scanning down, the three sections don't separate cleanly; the page reads as an even stream of text. Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style* §2.2.2) sets subheads with more space above than below. Roughly double the space above each subhead and tighten the space below, so each heading visibly owns its text.
3. **"Remove an old card" sends the reader to a step it doesn't explain** (defect). The section says a card can be removed only "once another card is set as the default". It never says how to set a default. A reader whose Remove is greyed out hits a dead end on the page that was meant to help them (Nielsen heuristic 10: help sits at the step it concerns). Add a sentence before "Select the card": how to make a card the default, and where that control is.

**Smaller things.**
- Lines run about 78 to 85 characters ("A card can be removed once another card is set as the default. Select the card, then" is 84). That is past Bringhurst's 45 to 75. Narrow the column by about 80 to 100 px.
- "days." sits alone on the last line of the hold paragraph. Tighten the measure or rebalance that line.
- Taste: the UI path "Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card" is buried in prose with the labels unmarked. Setting the labels in bold, or making the steps a short numbered list, would let the reader follow along with the app open.
- "nothing already paid is moved" in the intro is slightly odd. "Invoices you've already paid stay on the old card" says what it means.

**What's working.**
- The title is a plain task in the reader's words ("Changing your payment card"), and the subheads are the three things people actually come here for. Keep that structure.
- The intro answers the first worry straight away: when the change takes effect and what happens to past charges. Lead every help article this way.
- The type system is restrained and clear: one family, three sizes with real steps between them, and weight doing the heading work. Don't add more.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, and whether the breadcrumb links get an underline or focus ring on hover and focus.
