**First read.** The eye lands straight on the huge widely spaced headline, "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY", then goes up to the small grey "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" and down to the two-line paragraph. Then it stops: the bottom 40% and the right third are blank. It reads as a clean, calm announcement for a harbour event, and the first question it leaves you with is "when?"

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web hero or poster telling the general public about free ferry rides at a city harbour open day, so they turn up.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy and typesetting are tidy, but the piece fails at its job. It invites people to an event without saying when it is, so nobody can act on it. The empty lower half is where that information should go.

**What would move it most**
1. **No date, no times, no next step. (Defect.)** The copy says "every half hour" but gives no day, no first or last sailing, and no sign of whether you need to book. A viewer who wants to go can't plan anything and has to look it up somewhere else, which most won't do. "Free rides every half hour" can also be read two ways: walk on, or reserve a free ticket. The wrong reading means either a wasted trip or a missed one. "North landing" only helps someone who already knows the harbour. The fix: make the day and the hours the second-loudest thing on the page, for example "Sat 14 June · 9:00–16:00, first boat 9:00, last 15:30". Then add one plain line that settles booking ("No booking, just walk on") and one exact pointer to the north landing, such as a street, a landmark, or a map link.
2. **The page is top-heavy and the eye path dead-ends. (Convention, Arnheim on balance; Arnheim/Dondis on eye path.)** All the weight sits in the top-left 60% and nothing below it pulls back, so the eye finishes the paragraph and drops into empty space. The emptiness isn't doing anything here. It isn't framing the headline, it's just where the copy ran out. Fix 1 fixes this too: putting the date, time and place block low on the same left edge gives the page a counterweight and the eye a place to end.
3. **It could be anyone's announcement. (Taste.)** Grey-black sans caps on plain white, with no colour, image or mark to say harbour, water or day out. On a poster wall or a busy events page, nothing makes it stand out from a council notice. I'd keep the strong headline and add one ownable element, such as a single sea or flag colour or a ferry or wave mark, so it feels like an invitation and not a memo.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline is tracked wide at a very large size (letter gaps look about half the word gaps), so "ALL ABOARD THE" loosens into separate letters and takes a moment longer to read. Large type usually wants tighter spacing, not looser (Bringhurst 2.1.1; Lupton p. 80). Pull the tracking back towards 0.02–0.04em and tighten the word spaces to match. This is a convention, and a mild one: the airy signage feel may be intentional.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear three-level scale: a small bold grey caps eyebrow, a dominant headline and a plain body. Each level differs in more than size, so nothing competes. Keep this when you add the date block, and give that block its own weight (bold, or the accent colour) rather than a fourth near-miss size.
- "Bring a hat and a thermos" is a lovely line. It's specific and human, and it tells you what the morning will feel like. More of the copy should sound like that.
- There's one hard left edge with a generous margin, and the paragraph runs about 65 characters per line with comfortable leading.
