**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The type is handled well, but someone who opens a "Spring timetable" leaves without a single time or date they could act on, and the headline uses a whole screen to say less than one line of the timetable would.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable isn't there, and the copy hides the facts a rider needs.** *(Defect.)* The small label at top left says "Spring timetable", and the headline and paragraph talk around a schedule without giving it. The headline says "by daylight", but from when to when? "Spring through early summer": which dates? "Every forty minutes": from what first departure? A rider can't work out when to be at the north landing, and "by daylight" can be read two ways: a fixed timetable, or one that shifts with sunset. A rider who guesses wrong misses the last boat. These are exactly the details people act on and later hold the operator to. A label should name what its section holds (WCAG 2.4.6), and this one names something the screen doesn't show. Fix: put the real numbers in the first screen. Give the date range ("20 March to 30 June"), the first and last departure from each side ("First boat 7:00 from North Landing, last 19:40 from Market Quay"), and then the times themselves, as a table or a short list that people can scan.

2. **The headline costs a whole screen and says little.** *(Taste, argued.)* It is a 12-word sentence set at display size over four lines, and it takes up about 330 of the 800 px. Size isn't the problem: one dominant element is right. The problem is what it's spent on. "Quayside ferry crossings by daylight, spring through early summer" reads like a title for a document, not an answer for someone waiting to cross. Fix: keep the scale and cut the words to the name ("Quayside Ferry", or "Spring timetable"). Move the dates and hours into a line just below, where they can be exact. That frees the top half of the screen for the times.
