**First read.** The eye goes straight to the row of four buttons, because each is a different strong colour: teal, purple, orange, blue. It then goes up to "Low-light houseplants" and across four plant drawings that look like the same plant. It reads as a tidy, calm plant-shop category page, but the coloured buttons make it feel like a template, and the drawings tell you nothing about what you would be buying.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a shop category page where someone with a dim room compares four plants and adds one to the cart.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and well aligned, but it doesn't help the shopper choose. The drawings don't show the actual plants, the cards don't say how each plant copes with low light, and the four button colours suggest differences that aren't there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show the products.** All four tiles hold the same leaf-in-a-terracotta-pot icon with the leaves rearranged. The snake plant has no upright blades. The pothos is listed as "trails to 6 ft" but is drawn standing up. On a shop page the picture is the main way people choose, and here a shopper can't tell what any of these plants looks like, so the only real difference between the cards is the price. *Defect* (common sense; no approved source states it). Fix: use a real photo or a faithful drawing of each plant, shot or drawn the same way so the set stays consistent. Keep the pale tile background, which works.
2. **Four colours for one button.** The four "Add to cart" buttons do exactly the same thing but are teal, purple, orange and blue. A shopper reads different colours as different meanings: is the orange one a warning or a sale, is the blue one information? They also pull the eye down to the buttons before the plants. They are the loudest, most saturated colours on the page and have nothing to do with the muted green-and-terracotta of the illustrations. The orange is warm, so it jumps ahead of the rest for no reason. Amber and blue are status colours (Apple HIG, Material), and here they mean nothing. *Convention* (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: elements of one kind share one style; Refactoring UI: a small, defined palette). Fix: one button colour for all four. Pick a single hue taken from the plant palette, such as the leaf green or a deep forest green, so the colour supports the calm mood instead of fighting it.
3. **The cards skip what the page promises.** The headline and subhead promise plants that do well "more than six feet from a window". The cards then give only pot size and mature size. They don't say how much light each plant tolerates, how often to water it, or whether it's safe for pets, which is what someone picking a plant for a dark corner wants to compare. Right now the shopper has to go to "Care guides" and remember what they read there. *Defect* (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). Fix: add one line to each card that ties the plant to the promise in the shopper's own terms, such as "Fine in a north-facing room; water every 2–3 weeks". Set it in the same small grey style as the size line.

**Smaller things.**
- White text on the orange button looks to be about 4.3:1, which is under the 4.5:1 WCAG 1.4.3 asks of 15 px bold text. Check it with a contrast tool. Fixing item 2 makes this go away.
- The "Houseplants" item in the top navigation looks the same as the other items, so nothing shows that you're in that section. Mark the current item with one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Nielsen NN/g, menu-design checklist).

**What's working.**
- The grid holds. The four cards share edges, gutters and inner padding, and the plant name, size line, price and button sit on the same baselines across all four, so you can compare the cards side by side at a glance. Keep that when you add the extra line.
- The subhead "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window" is concrete. It gives the shopper a measurement from their own home instead of a vague "low light". The other copy should be held to the same standard.
- The order inside each card is clear: a bold name, a smaller grey size line, then a bold price. The price stands out without needing a colour.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether keyboard focus on the buttons and nav links is visible.
