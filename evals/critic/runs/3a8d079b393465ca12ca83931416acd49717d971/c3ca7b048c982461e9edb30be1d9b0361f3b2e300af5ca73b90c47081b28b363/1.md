**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then down the centred column to the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a website for a small local ferry, aimed at day-trippers and commuters. It feels calm, clean and tidy, but also plain: Inter type, one blue, no picture. It could be the top of almost any small service's website.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top section of a ferry operator's homepage. Its job is to tell a local or a visitor that the crossing is quick and pleasant, and to get them to plan a trip.

**Verdict.** It is clear and well built, but it doesn't give a would-be passenger what they need to decide. It never says where the boat goes, when the first and last boats leave, or what a ticket costs, so the only way forward is a click. The craft is fine. The content is what holds it back.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts a passenger needs are missing or vague** (defect; common sense, since no approved source covers it). The paragraph says boats leave "the north landing" but never says where they go, and the headline's "the bay" doesn't name the other side either. "From early morning until late evening" is a timing promise people will act on, and it can be read two ways. One reader hears a 6 am start and plans a commute. Another hears 8 am and doesn't. Whoever planned on the wrong one misses a boat and blames you. There's no fare at all. Right now the viewer has to click "See the timetable" just to learn whether this ferry suits them at all.
   - Fix: put the hard facts on this screen in one short line under the headline, with the destination, first and last departure, and fare (for example "North landing to [south town] · first boat 6:40, last 22:00 · £3"). Then the button is for planning a specific trip, not for basic questions.
   - Better still, make the button more specific, such as "Next departures from North landing", so it points at the viewer's own next trip.
2. **The three best selling points read as more lines of the paragraph** (defect, from a size and spacing convention). "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" sit at about 18 px under a body paragraph of about 20 px. That size gap is too small to read as a separate level (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1, on choosing from a modest set of distinct sizes). The list is centred like the paragraph and has no markers. The gap above it is only a little bigger than the space between lines, so it doesn't read as its own group (Arnheim, on grouping by proximity). On top of that, two of the three items repeat the paragraph ("Bring a bicycle", "the open deck"), so a skimming reader sees them twice and takes in neither.
   - Fix: make the list clearly its own group. Give it more space above, plus a marker, icon or weight change, and a size that is either the body size or clearly smaller.
   - Trim the paragraph so each fact appears once. "Bicycles travel free" is the strongest line on the page and is currently the easiest to miss.
3. **There's no picture, for a product sold on its view** (taste). The paragraph's best line is "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck", but the page shows only white space and a blue button. On a leisure crossing, a photo of the deck or the bay is the argument that makes people want to go. Without one, the page looks generic and could be any company's. My view is that one strong photo of the crossing, beside the text or behind it with safe contrast, would do more for this page than any change to the type.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** it breaks as "twelve / calm minutes", which splits the number from its unit. Rebreak it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" (Bringhurst 2.4.6, on keeping a number with its unit).
- **Everything centred:** every block, including the paragraph, the list and the button, is centred on one axis. That is fine for a short hero, but it is part of why the list merges into the paragraph. If you keep it centred, keep the paragraph to two lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*, on centring by default).

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes" is concrete, measurable and specific to this service, and "calm" sets the mood. Keep the number in it.
- **The order of the page:** one dominant headline, a small blue company name above it, and exactly one primary button. The hierarchy at the top and bottom of the column is clear.
- **Restraint:** a single blue shared by the company name and the button, and a comfortable paragraph line length of about 74 characters.
