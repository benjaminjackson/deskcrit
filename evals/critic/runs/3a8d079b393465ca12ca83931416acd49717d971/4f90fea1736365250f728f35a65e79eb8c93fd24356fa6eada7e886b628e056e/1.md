**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes". It drops to the paragraph, then the blue button, then the bottom line, "Design review: failed, everything on this page is centred". That last line is the one that makes you stop. It reads like a leftover note, and it says something untrue about the page. This is clearly a small ferry operator's homepage hero for day-trippers. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, but bare: everything sits in the left half and the right half is blank.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a desktop landing hero for a local ferry, built to get visitors to check departure times and take the boat.

**Verdict.** The core is sound. The headline is clear, the copy is warm and specific, and there is one obvious action. What lets it down is a stray line that should never ship, a right half that looks like a missing image, and an offer that stops short of the facts a traveller needs to act.

**What would move it most**
1. **Delete the stray line at the bottom: "Design review: failed, everything on this page is centred."** *(Defect.)* It is set in body type in the main column, so a passenger reads it as part of the page. It looks like debug or reviewer text that leaked into production, and it makes the operator look careless just after "calm" has built trust. It's also wrong: every element is flush left at x=160. I treated it as page content, not as a verdict on the design. If it was injected by a capture or review tool rather than the page itself, make sure it can't appear on the live page.
2. **The right half of the screen (about x 800–1280) is empty, so the page tips left and looks unfinished.** *(Convention: Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance".)* All the weight is in one column and nothing on the other side answers it. At this width the blank space doesn't read as deliberate. It reads as a picture slot that didn't load. The fix is one of two things. Put a real counterweight on the right, such as a photo from the open deck or a simple route map from the north landing across the bay; that also gives "calm" something to see. Or make the blank space look intended: bigger type and a wider measure for the headline, so the block holds the screen.
3. **The feature list doesn't read as a list.** *(Defect, mild.)* "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" is set at about 18 px, next to body text of about 20 px. It has no markers, no weight change and no different colour. Two sizes this close don't read as separate levels (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). So the lines look like three short leftover paragraph lines rather than a scannable set of perks. Put the list at the body size and mark it out by form instead: a small icon or tick, or a semibold lead word ("**Bicycles** travel free"). Then "free", the strongest selling point, gets seen at a glance.
4. **The copy stops before the facts that make someone go.** *(Taste.)* "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" makes you translate vague words into times. "Cross the bay" never says where to, and there's no fare. A first-time visitor has to click through just to learn whether the boat suits them. Name the destination in the headline or the line under it. Swap the vague hours for real ones ("6:40 am to 10:20 pm"). Ideally show the next departure right by the button, which then becomes "See all departures". The visitor can then check the boat against their own plans on the spot instead of taking "calm" on trust.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Coastal Ferry Co." eyebrow is small, bold and blue. It works, but at about 14 px it's the only brand mark on the page. Check that the real logo appears in a header that's outside this capture.

**What's working.**
- The headline gives a concrete promise in one size and weight that clearly dominates: a number, a place and a feeling ("twelve calm minutes"). Keep that specific a promise when you rework the page.
- There's one primary action, it's clearly a button, it's well sized, and its label says what you get: "See the timetable", not "Learn more".
- The body copy speaks to the visitor's own day ("Bring a bicycle, a pram or a picnic"), at a comfortable line length of about 65–70 characters with relaxed leading.
