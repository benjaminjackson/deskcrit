The headline and layout are sound, but the picture and the button work against the page. The background shows no plants, and its bright half draws the eye away from the one action.

**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline on the left. Within a second it is pulled right into a bright scatter of lime and kelly-green ovals, which is the loudest thing on the screen. It goes up and off the top-right corner and doesn't come back to "Browse the greenhouse". It reads as a plant nursery's home page and feels fresh and green, but it looks like a template: a stand-in pattern where the product should be.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home-page hero for a local nursery. It should make a gardener want these ferns and shade plants and send them into the catalogue.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page sells ferns for shady corners but shows no fern, no shade and no plant at all. Its brightest half is decoration that takes attention from both the headline and the only action.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture contradicts the headline and stands in for the product** (defect). The headline promises "Ferns and shade plants", but the right half of the screen is a flat, sunny scatter of generated ovals. There are no fronds, no deep shade and no real leaves. A shopper looking for plants learns nothing about what this nursery grows. The image doesn't back up the claim, so the page looks like it could belong to any green brand. My view as a designer: the picture should be the other half of the headline, not wallpaper. The obvious fix is a real photograph of their own ferns in shade, such as a dim corner or a frond lit from the side. That would also make the cool, low-light mood the copy describes.
2. **The bright right half overpowers the left and pulls the eye off the page** (convention: Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The headline and button sit on the dark left. The right ~45% is the lightest, most saturated and busiest area in the frame, so the page tips right. The leaves also climb up and to the right, so the eye's path runs from the headline into the bright mass and out at the top corner. It skips the button on the way. Calm the right side: darken it, cut its saturation, or give it one clear subject, so it supports the headline and the eye settles back on the button.
3. **The only action is styled as a secondary button** (convention: Material Design 3 and Apple HIG on button emphasis). "Browse the greenhouse" is a thin white outline with no fill, sitting on a field of busy green. It is the one thing the page wants the visitor to do, yet the subhead outweighs it. Make it a solid button in a colour the background doesn't use, so it is the second thing the eye finds after the headline. The label is fine: it names a real place and a real action.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline runt: "corner" sits alone on the third line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rebreak so "every corner" stays together, or widen the measure so it sets in two lines.
- The saturated greens cover nearly half the frame. Once the image is calmer, keep strong colour for the button so it has something to stand out from.

**What's working.**
- The headline is clearly dominant: big, bold, tightly leaded and flush left on the darkest part of the image, so you know where to start. Keep that.
- The subhead "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend." is the best line on the page. It is specific, local and tells the visitor what they can do and when. It does more selling than the headline. Consider bringing that line's "this weekend" urgency into the button or the headline.
- The type is restrained: about four sizes (logo, nav, headline, subhead/button) with big steps between them, one family, and a quiet white nav bar. It's clean and needs no work.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the outlined button. Once it is filled, check that the focus ring still shows clearly against the green.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 6 yes, 2 no (image and headline one idea; colour mood suits the message)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 14 lines, 12 yes, 2 no (visual weight balances; eye path stays on the page)
Text: 30 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (no runts or widows)
Color: 9 lines, 8 yes, 1 no (saturated colour kept to a small share)
Pictures: 12 lines, 7 yes, 3 no (subject stands apart from background; texture doesn't compete; no false stand-in for the product), 2 skipped (no photos, no icons)
Charts and data: skipped
Interface: 33 lines, 28 yes, 1 no (one primary button), 3 unsure (states shown; focus indicator; focus not obscured), 1 skipped (phone thumb zone)
