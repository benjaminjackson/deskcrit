**What would move it most**
1. **The bottom sentence is a stray comment, and it's wrong.** "The heavy black lines around every cell…" sits where a footnote would go, but it describes borders that aren't there. The table uses thin, pale row rules and no cell boxes. A rider reads it as part of the timetable and is left wondering what they're missing, or whether this is the right version. It's defect, common sense: a published timetable can't carry a remark about its own styling. Fix: delete it. That spot is worth using for what riders actually need, such as the season dates, the 12-minute crossing, or where to check for cancellations.
2. **The days a boat runs sit at the far right, so weekend riders have to read every row.** Days are the first filter a rider applies, and "Weekdays" and "Weekends" are spread down a column at the opposite end of the row from the departure time. On a Saturday, a rider must cross-check all seven rows to find that 12:40 and 17:10 don't run. It's defect: it slows the main task and invites a missed boat. Fix: put "Runs" next to "Leaves north" so time and days read together. Better still, split the table into "Every day", "Weekdays only" and "Weekends only", or mark the exceptions so "Daily" stays the quiet default.
3. **"Spring season" has no dates.** The subtitle is the only thing that tells a rider whether this timetable applies today, and "spring season" could mean March to May or a ferry operator's own calendar. A date is something a rider acts on, so an unclear one is a defect. Someone turning up in early June won't know if these times still hold. Fix: state the dates, e.g. "1 March – 31 May".
4. **The table is padded with columns that say almost nothing.** "Period" takes the leftmost, most-read position but repeats what the time already says ("Morning" for 06:40). "Arrives quay" is always departure plus 12 minutes. Four short columns spread across about 1080 px also leave long gaps inside each row, and the eye has to bridge them to match a time to its days. This is taste, close to Tufte's case against ink that carries no information. Fix: lead with the departure time. Drop Period, or turn it into a light group label over its rows. Either keep Arrives and say "12-minute crossing" once in the subtitle, or keep Arrives and narrow the table so each row reads as one unit.
