**First read.** My eye lands on "Before you board", bold and dark at the top left, and drops straight into a grey-black block of capitals. It reads as a short help or info page for a river ferry. The heading feels calm and modern. The body feels like a teleprinter notice or a warning sign, and it reads like someone is shouting a timetable at me.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web info page telling ferry passengers, including cyclists, how the service runs, so they can plan a crossing and know what to do when they board.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the two things that decide whether it works both fail. Every sentence of the body is set in capitals, which makes the only real content slow to read. And the times a passenger would plan around ("early morning", "late evening", "in winter") are too vague to plan with.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set in capitals. Defect, and a published convention.** All three paragraphs (eight lines, about 75 words) are uppercase. Capitals strip out the shapes of words that readers use to recognise them, so every line here has to be read letter by letter. The page is short, but it is also the whole message, and passengers will often read it on a phone at the landing. Capitals also make a friendly note sound like a warning. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says caps suit headings shorter than one line and that whole paragraphs should never be capitalised; Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls text in capitals particularly difficult to read. Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, which already uses them well.
2. **The times passengers act on can't be acted on. Defect (copy).** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" doesn't say when the first or last boat leaves, and that is what decides whether someone makes the last crossing home. "Every forty minutes" doesn't say from which minute, so nobody can tell whether the next boat is at :10 or :35. "In winter the timetable thins to one boat each hour" doesn't say which dates count as winter or on what minute the boats leave. The last sentence then sends people to "the shelter at each landing" for the printed timetable, which means walking to the quay to find out whether walking to the quay was worth it. These are the lines people will hold the operator to, so vagueness here costs missed boats. Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing, the departure minutes, and the winter dates, or link to the timetable from this page.

**Smaller things.**
- The heading promises boarding guidance, but only the bicycle paragraph is about boarding; the other two are about the timetable. Either retitle the page (for example "Using the river ferry") or split it under two short subheads, "Timetable" and "Bicycles". This is my judgement as a designer, not a catalogued rule.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three levels (small tracked grey label, large bold heading, regular body) with big steps between them and nothing in between. Keep it when you switch the body to mixed case.
- The text is held to a column of roughly 70 characters instead of stretching across the 1280 px width, and the gaps between paragraphs mark the breaks clearly. Keep that measure.
- The label, heading and paragraphs share one tight left edge with comfortable line spacing and a quiet single-neutral palette, so nothing competes with the words.
