**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board", the only large, bold line, and then drops into three blocks set entirely in capitals. The calm, plain page suddenly sounds like a posted regulation or someone shouting. By the second paragraph I'm reading letter by letter, not word by word. It looks clean but generic: a system sans in one grey column that could belong to any service.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web info page for ferry passengers: when the boat runs, how bikes work, and what changes in winter, read before they set off.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the body text is set all in capitals, which makes the one part that carries the information slow and hard to read. The page also never gives the times a passenger actually needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body is all capitals** (defect). All three paragraphs, about eight lines in total, are in capitals at body size. Capitals take away the word shapes readers rely on, so every line here is slower to read. Running text set this way also reads as a warning, not as help. That clashes with the friendly "Before you board" above it. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says not to capitalise whole paragraphs and to keep capitals for headings shorter than one line, labels and captions. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls text in capitals "particularly difficult to read." The fix is to set the paragraphs in upper and lower case. Keep capitals only for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, which already uses them correctly: short and letterspaced.
2. **The timetable has no times** (defect, copy). "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" is the line passengers will act on, and it gives no first or last sailing. One person reads "early" as 6:00, another as 8:00, and the one who guessed wrong misses the boat or waits at an empty landing. "Thins to one boat each hour" has the same gap: winter isn't given as dates, and the hour isn't given as a departure time (on the hour? at :20?). The fix is to state the real times, e.g. "first boat 6:20 from North Landing, last 22:40". If they change, say where to check them, beyond "printed timetables in the shelter," which only helps someone already standing at the landing.

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- The type has three clear levels: a small tracked grey label, a bold heading about twice the body size, and the body. Each has an obvious job, and weight and colour do the work, not extra sizes. Keep this scale as it is.
- One shared left edge at x≈120 and a line length of about 70 characters make for a comfortable column once the caps are gone. Don't widen it to fill the empty right side.
- Each paragraph covers one subject (schedule, bikes, winter). That makes the page easy to scan. If it grows, those topics are ready to become subheadings.
