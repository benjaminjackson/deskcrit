**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board", then hits three paragraphs set entirely in capitals and slows to a crawl. It reads as a passenger-information page for a river ferry: plain, neutral, a system-UI look. The heading is calm, but the body reads like a notice that is shouting.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web info page that lets ferry passengers plan a crossing: when boats run, how bikes work, and what changes in winter.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The structure is sound, but the all-caps body makes the part people actually came for slow to read. On top of that, the copy never gives the times a passenger needs to catch a boat.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set in all capitals.** All three paragraphs (rows ~180–425) are in capitals, about 18 px, running to two or three lines each. Capitals take away word shapes, so every word has to be read letter by letter. The text is grey-black and even, with no ascenders or descenders to guide the eye. The page that looks quickest to scan becomes the slowest. It also sounds like shouting, which jars with the calm heading. Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps": capitals suit headings shorter than one line, not whole paragraphs. Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, also calls capital text "particularly difficult to read". **Convention, and a defect here.** Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case. Keep capitals for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, which already uses them correctly: short and letterspaced.
2. **The copy leaves out the facts a passenger acts on.** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing and no departure minute, so nobody can work out when to be at the landing. "In winter" doesn't say which months. "One boat each hour" doesn't say at what minute past. The last line sends people to "the shelter at each landing" for the printed timetable. They only find out the times once they're already standing at the quay, which is the moment this page should have saved them. **Defect** (times are what passengers plan around and later hold the operator to). Fix: give the first and last sailings, the departure minutes, and the winter dates. Or put the timetable, or a link to it, right on this page rather than in the shelter.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing. Type sizes, spacing and alignment are clean.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clear.** There are three sizes with big steps between them: the small tracked grey label, the bold heading at about 38 px, and the body at about 18 px. Each has an obvious job, and the heading differs in weight as well as size.
- **The label-then-heading pair is tight and well grouped.** "Passenger notes" sits close to "Before you board". Paragraph spacing is clearly bigger than line spacing, so each topic (times, bikes, winter) reads as its own short chunk.
- **The measure is right.** The lines run about 65 to 75 characters in one left-aligned column, inside Bringhurst's comfortable range. Keep that width when you switch to mixed case, which will make the lines a little shorter.
