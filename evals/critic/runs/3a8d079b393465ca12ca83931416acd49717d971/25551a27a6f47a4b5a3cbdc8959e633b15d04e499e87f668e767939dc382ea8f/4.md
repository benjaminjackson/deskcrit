**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: a web info page that tells ferry passengers the route, the bike rules and the winter changes before they travel.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the piece fails at the one thing it exists to do, which is being read quickly. Every sentence is in capitals, and the copy leaves out the operating times a passenger actually needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is all capitals** (defect, and a broken convention). All three paragraphs, nine lines in total, are set in capitals at body size. Capitals all have the same height and a squared-off outline, so the shapes that let readers take in whole words are gone. People have to spell their way through, and it reads like shouting. On a page people skim on the way to a pier, that defeats the purpose. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says not to capitalise whole paragraphs, and Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls text in capitals particularly difficult to read. Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for the short "PASSENGER NOTES" label, where they already work.
2. **The copy leaves out the facts a passenger acts on** (defect). "Between early morning and late evening" gives nobody a first or last sailing to plan around. That is exactly the line a reader would rely on and then blame you for. "In winter" gives no dates either, so a passenger in late October can't tell which timetable applies. And "printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" only helps someone already standing at the pier, not someone reading this before they leave. Fix: give the first and last sailing times, the dates of the winter timetable, and where to see the timetable before travelling.
