**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable" in bold at the top left and drops into three paragraphs of grey-black prose. I expect a grid of departure times. I get a short essay about the ferry, and the right half of the screen and the bottom third are empty. It feels calm and tidy, but I come away not knowing when the next boat leaves.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that lets someone who rides the ferry, or is thinking about it, find out when boats leave and plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. It's called a timetable but has no times in it. The typesetting is clean, but a rider can't plan a single trip from this page, and the page itself sends them to a noticeboard at the landing for the information it was meant to give.

**What would move it most**
1. **The title promises a timetable and the page has no times.** *Defect.* "River ferry timetable" leads the reader to expect departures. The body gives only "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". Someone standing at the north landing at 5:50 pm can't tell whether a boat is coming, when the first one leaves, or whether they've missed the last. WCAG 2.4.6 asks that a heading describe what its section holds, and this one describes something that isn't on the page. Fix: put the timetable where the empty right half of the screen is now, or right under the title. Make it a real table of departure times from each landing, set in tabular figures, with a separate winter table. If the times truly can't go on the page, rename it to say what it is, such as "About the river ferry". The table is the better choice.
2. **The details a rider acts on can be read more than one way.** *Defect, copy.* A rider will hold the operator to these lines, and several of them are vague:
   - "Leaves the quay before dusk": dusk moves by over an hour across winter. One rider reads it as about 4 pm, another as 5 pm, and the second one is left on the wrong bank. Give a clock time, or one per month.
   - "In winter": which dates?
   - "One boat each hour": on the hour, or at some other minute past?
   - "Runs from the north landing to the market quay": does it also run back? The page never says it does, so a rider on the quay side may assume it doesn't.
   - The page never mentions the fare, only that bicycles cost nothing extra.

   Fix: answer each of these with a time, a date, or a direction, phrased as what it means for the rider, e.g. "The last boat back from the market quay leaves at 4:20 pm from November to February."
3. **The facts are buried in sentences, so the page can't be scanned.** *Defect, and it follows from 1.* Frequency, crossing time, the bicycle rule, winter changes and where to find updates are all written as running prose. A rider has to read every line to pull out the one number they came for. The paragraphs are short enough to read comfortably, so the problem isn't the text block. The problem is that a timetable is the kind of content you look things up in, and prose is the wrong form for that. Fix: once the table is there, cut the prose to a few labelled lines under it, such as "Crossing time", "Bicycles" and "Changes this week". Keep the bicycle boarding note as a sentence, because it's an instruction and reads well as one.

**What's working.**
- The type is set well for reading. The body looks about 18 px with roughly 1.5 line spacing, lines run about 65 to 72 characters, and the ragged right edge is gentle. Keep all of that for any prose that remains.
- The hierarchy is plain and honest: one bold title, one body size, contrast from weight and size, and dark text on white. It needs no extra levels.
- The bicycle paragraph is good instruction copy. It tells riders who does what, in what order, and where: wheel the bike on after foot passengers, then lock it to the rail.
