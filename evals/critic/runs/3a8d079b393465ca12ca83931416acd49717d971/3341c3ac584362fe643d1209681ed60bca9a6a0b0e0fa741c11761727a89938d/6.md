From there the eye drops through the paragraph, slides past three loose lines and stops on the blue button. It reads as a small ferry operator's landing hero. **What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a ferry company's homepage, there to get a local or visitor to catch the boat, with "See the timetable" as the next step.

**Verdict.** The core is sound: one clear message, one clear action, and good reading type. What's holding it back is that it doesn't give a rider the facts they'd act on (where the boat goes, when, and what it costs), and it leaves half the frame empty with nothing that says "bay" or "harbour". The result reads as a pleasant template rather than this particular crossing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy leaves out the facts a rider acts on.** "Cross the bay" never says from where to where. "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" gives a frequency but neither end of the day, so someone planning a 7 am commute or a 10 pm return can't tell if the ferry works for them. There's no fare anywhere. Those three facts are what a rider remembers and holds the operator to, and right now every one sits behind the button. **Defect.** The fix: name both landings in the headline or just under it ("North Landing to [town]"), give the real first and last sailings ("6:20 am to 11:00 pm, every 40 minutes"), and add the fare. Then "See the timetable" becomes a way to check a time the rider already half knows, not a way to find out whether the ferry is any use to them at all. Better still, show the next departure on the page.
2. **All the weight sits top-left and the right half is empty.** Every element lives in a column about 620 px wide starting at x≈160. The right 40% of the frame and the bottom quarter hold nothing. Arnheim's point on balance is that a heavy side needs a counterweight, and here nothing answers the text block, so the page looks unfinished rather than airy. It also wastes the one thing a ferry sells on sight, which is the water. The copy promises "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck" and the page shows a white void. **Convention (balance), plus taste on the missed picture.** The fix: give the right side something with weight. A photo of the boat or the deck view would do it best, a map of the route would also work, or you could widen the measure and centre the composition on purpose. Whichever you choose, the empty space should be a decision, not something left over.
3. **The three perks read as stray lines of body text.** "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" are set at about 18 px, barely smaller than the 20 px paragraph above. They're in the same weight and colour, with no marker. Two sizes that close don't read as two levels (Bringhurst, §3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). They look like a paragraph that broke badly, so the viewer doesn't take them in as a list of reasons. "Bicycles travel free" is a real selling point and it's getting lost. **Defect.** The fix: make the list visibly different. Use the paragraph's size with a marker or icon per line, or set the items as short bold labels in a row. Either way, drop the near-miss size. On the copy, the paragraph already says "bring a bicycle" and the list then says bicycles go free. Pick one place to say it.

**Smaller things.**
- No navigation, no wordmark beyond the small blue "Coastal Ferry Co." line, and no link to fares, routes or contact. If this is the whole top of the page, a rider has nowhere to go except the timetable. Add a minimal header, or check whether one was left out of the capture.

**What's working.**
- The headline. It's specific and promises one thing ("twelve calm minutes"). It's set tight and heavy, and the two lines hold together. Keep both the line and the treatment.
- The reading text. The paragraph is flush left at about 70 characters per line, with generous leading and dark-on-white contrast. It's comfortable to read and needs no change.
- One clear action. There's a single primary button in the only saturated colour on the page, and its label names what you get. That's the right restraint, so don't add a competing button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus and hover states on "See the timetable". It's the only action, so check them in a browser.
