**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the first screen of a small ferry operator's website. Its job is to tell a local or a visitor that they can cross the bay easily, and when the next boat leaves.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and readable, but it doesn't yet do its job. It holds back the one fact a ferry passenger comes for (when the next boat leaves, and what it costs), and it leaves half the screen empty. So it reads as an unfinished template rather than a place you'd actually catch a boat from.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule is hidden behind a button and blurred in the copy (defect, common sense).** The paragraph says boats leave "every forty minutes from early morning until late evening". A passenger can't plan with that. They can't tell whether the first boat is at 6:00 or 8:30, or whether "late evening" means 20:00 or 23:00. So they have to click "See the timetable" just to learn what the page could have told them. Times are exactly the kind of thing people act on and later hold you to, so vague wording there is a real cost, not a style note. "The north landing" also assumes the reader already knows the place. Fix: put the actual first and last departures and the next departure on this screen, e.g. "Every 40 minutes, 06:20 to 22:40 · next boat 14:20". Add the fare if you can, and name the landing in terms a visitor would recognise. Then the button becomes "Full timetable and fares" rather than the only way to get the basics.
2. **The right half of the screen is empty, so the page tips left and looks broken (defect).** All the weight sits in a column from x≈160 to 790 on a 1280-wide screen. Nothing on the right (x 790 to 1280) balances it. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") is right that a heavy side needs a counterweight. Here, though, the bigger cost is that the gap reads as a missing image rather than chosen space, and the piece loses the one thing that would sell "calm minutes" and "watch the harbour slide past": seeing the boat and the water. If an image was meant to load there, this is a capture problem; check it in a live browser. If not, add a photo of the deck or the crossing, or put the next departures panel there. Either one balances the page and makes the case at the same time.
3. **The copy could be any ferry's (taste).** "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" are pleasant, but they're the minimum the viewer already expects of a ferry. "Twelve calm minutes" is the only line that's really yours. Lean on it. Set the crossing against the viewer's alternative, e.g. "12 minutes across, or 45 round the bay by road". That lets them judge the value themselves instead of taking "calm" on trust.

**Smaller things.**
- The intro paragraph (~20 px) and the three feature lines (~18 px) are almost the same size, so they look mismatched rather than like two levels (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Set both at one size and separate the list by spacing or a light marker, or make the step clearly larger.
- The headline breaks as "twelve / calm minutes", which splits the number from what it counts. Rebreak it to "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes", or keep "twelve calm minutes" on one line with a non-breaking space (common sense; Bringhurst 2.4.6 applies this to numbers and units).

**What's working.**
- There's one clear entry point. The headline is far heavier and larger than everything else, and the single solid blue button is plainly the only action. Keep that one-thing-to-do clarity when you add times and an image.
- Everything aligns on one left edge at x≈160, with tight grouping (brand label close to the headline) and even, generous gaps between groups. It reads as calm, which suits the message.
- The paragraph is easy to read: Inter at a comfortable size, about 68 characters per line, with open leading.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether something (likely a photo) was meant to fill the right half and didn't load; check the live page. Also the button's hover and focus states.
