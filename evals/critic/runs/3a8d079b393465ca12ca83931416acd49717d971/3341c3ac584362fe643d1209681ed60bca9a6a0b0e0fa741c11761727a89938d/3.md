**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops to the paragraph, skims past the three plain lines and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a small ferry company's landing page. It feels calm and clear, but also half-finished: everything sits in the left 60% of the frame, and the right side and the bottom third are blank, as if a picture never loaded.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero of a ferry operator's website. It should get day-trippers and locals to trust the crossing and get them to the next departure.

**Verdict.** The copy and the type do their job well. The layout does not. It looks like a page waiting for its image, and it holds back the two facts a would-be passenger came for, which are when the next boat leaves and what it costs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The empty right side makes the page look broken, not calm.** All the weight (the headline, the paragraph, the button) sits in a column from about x=160 to x=790. Nothing on the right balances it, so the page tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Gathered white space is good, but this space has no shape and no job, and it reads as leftover, which Tschichold and Müller-Brockmann both treat as a fault. For a ferry, that blank area is exactly where the viewer expects the water, the boat or the harbour, the thing that would sell "calm" better than the word does. *Defect* if an image failed to load, *convention* if this is the intended design. Either way, the fix is to give the right side a counterweight: a photo of the open deck or the bay, or a strong block of real information (see 2). Failing that, centre the column and make the emptiness look chosen.
2. **The answer is behind the button instead of on the page.** "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" makes the viewer do the work. Early is when? Is the next boat in 5 minutes or 35? It also gives no fare and doesn't say where the north landing is. Someone deciding whether to walk to the pier needs the next departure time, the first and last sailings, and the price. They now have to click through to a timetable to get them. *Defect* (common sense; there is no approved source for this). Put the next departure and the fare on the page, for example "Next boat 14:20 from North Landing · £4 adult, bikes free". Then make the button the next step ("Full timetable" or "Buy a ticket"). That same block can also be the counterweight finding 1 needs.

**Smaller things.**
- The paragraph (about 20 px) and the three-line list (about 18 px) are near-identical sizes, so the list reads like a smaller paragraph rather than its own level (Bringhurst, §3.1.1). Set the list at body size and mark it out with an icon, tick or weight instead, or make the size step bigger.
- The headline breaks "twelve / calm minutes", which splits the number from its unit (Bringhurst §2.4.6, on keeping a number with its unit). Break after "bay" or "in", or keep "twelve calm minutes" together with a non-breaking space.
- "Bicycles travel free" partly repeats "Bring a bicycle" in the paragraph above. Let the list carry the facts and the paragraph carry the feeling.

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and makes a promise you could hold them to: a place, a duration and a feeling in six words. It is far better than generic copy; keep it.
- The type is disciplined. One family, a strong step from the bold headline to the regular body, a comfortable line length (about 60–70 characters), dark text with good contrast, and one left edge that everything shares.
- There is one clear primary action, sized generously (about 209×55 px), and its colour matches the small brand label above the headline, so the colour scheme stays tight.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank right half is a hero image that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- How the button behaves on hover and focus, and how the layout reflows on a phone, where the empty side would disappear and the balance problem would go with it.
