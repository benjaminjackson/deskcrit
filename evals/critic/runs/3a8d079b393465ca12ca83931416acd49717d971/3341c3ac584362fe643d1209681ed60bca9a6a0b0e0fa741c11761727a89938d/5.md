**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", reads down through the paragraph and three loose lines, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top section of a small local ferry's website, for day-trippers and locals. It feels calm and tidy, but also bare: all the content sits in the left 60% of the frame, and the right side is empty white, so it looks like a picture failed to load.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a landing page that gets someone thinking about the crossing to check the times and plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The headline and the single button work. But the page holds back the facts a traveller needs to plan: there are no departure times, no price, and no named places. And the empty right half makes it look unfinished rather than calm.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy leaves out the facts you need to plan.** In the paragraph, "every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" says how often the boat runs but not when. "Early" and "late" could mean 6 or 8, and 8 pm or 11 pm. "The north landing" doesn't say north of what, or where the boat goes. There's no fare anywhere. A visitor can't answer "can I make it on Saturday at 9, and what will it cost?" without clicking, and the button only promises more reading. *Defect (common sense, not a published rule).* Fix: put the real numbers in the copy, for example "First boat 6:40, last 22:20, every 40 minutes, from North Landing to [town]. £4 one way." Then the button can say what it adds, such as "See today's departures". "See the timetable" is already a concrete ask, so keep it specific.
2. **The page is weighted entirely to the left.** Text and button fill x≈160–790, and the right ~490 px carry nothing. Nothing on the right balances the heavy left column (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), so the empty space reads as missing content rather than chosen calm. It also wastes the one thing a ferry page has going for it: the bay itself. *Defect if an image was meant to load there; taste if the empty space is on purpose.* Fix: either fill the right side with something that carries the promise, such as a photo of the open deck on the water or a small "next departures" panel, or centre the column and make the empty space clearly deliberate. If a hero image should be there, check that it loads.
3. **Nothing makes this ferry stand out.** The name "Coastal Ferry Co.", Inter on white, a blue rounded button: this could be any operator's template. The headline is the only line with character ("twelve calm minutes" is specific and good). *Taste.* Fix: let the headline's mood carry into the visuals, through an image, a sea colour, or the actual place names, so the page feels like this bay and not just any bay.

**Smaller things.**
- The paragraph (~20 px) and the feature list (~18 px) are almost the same size, so they look like a mismatch rather than two levels (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1). Set the list in the paragraph's size, and set it apart with weight or a marker instead.
- The three features ("Bicycles travel free", etc.) have no bullets or icons, so they read as stray lines and not a list. Bringhurst (4.4.2) says to make a list clear with positioning and with markers such as bullets or dashes. "Seats on the open deck" also comes straight after the paragraph's "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck", so it repeats it. Replace it with something new, such as the fare or accessibility.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a specific promise with a number ("twelve minutes") and a feeling ("calm"), set large, bold, and tightly leaded, so it clearly comes first. Keep it.
- There's one primary action, with high contrast and a good size (~210×55 px). Nothing competes with it.
- The left edge is clean: the brand name, headline, paragraph, list, and button all line up at x=160, so the column reads as one ordered block.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button. Whether the empty right side is a picture that failed to load: check it in a live browser.
