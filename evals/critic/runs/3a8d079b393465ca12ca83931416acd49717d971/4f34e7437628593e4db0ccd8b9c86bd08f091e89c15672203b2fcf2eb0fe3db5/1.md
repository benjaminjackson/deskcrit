**First read.** The eye goes to "Revenue overview" and then the three KPI cards, starting with $4.82M. From there it's pulled into the big block of blue bars, which sits inside a heavy black frame. It reads as a calm, competent, fairly standard SaaS revenue dashboard. It looks trustworthy until you add up the numbers.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a founder or finance lead uses this to see in a few seconds where 2025 revenue stands and which way it's heading.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job: clear KPIs with comparisons, a clean scale and a sensible chart. But the figures on the screen don't agree with each other, and on a revenue screen that undermines everything else. The heavy chart frame is the second problem, well behind the first.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Revenue by plan, 2025" totals the ARR figure, not 2025 revenue.** The table on the right adds up to $4.82M (0.61 + 1.94 + 2.27), which is exactly the "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" card. Its heading says 2025 revenue. The monthly chart next to it is recognised revenue, and its twelve bars add up to about $4.5M by eye. A finance reader who checks will find two different "2025 revenue" totals side by side and stop trusting the whole page. The December bar (~$447k) also doesn't obviously square with $4.82M ARR (~$402k a month). There may be a real reason, such as non-recurring revenue in recognised revenue, but the screen doesn't say so. **Defect** (a heading that doesn't name what its section holds, WCAG 2.4.6, plus contradictory numbers). Fix: label the table for what it actually shows ("ARR by plan, Dec 2025"). If it really is 2025 revenue, make its total match the chart. Either way, add a one-line note where recognised revenue and ARR are meant to differ.
2. **The chart's frame and gridlines are as dark as the data.** The plot has a full black box and near-black gridlines at every 100. They compete with the bars, and the frame makes the chart the heaviest thing on the page, louder than the KPIs above it. **Convention** (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*; Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, data-ink). Fix: remove the frame and make the gridlines thin and pale grey, so the bars are the darkest marks in the chart.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart title "Monthly revenue, 2025" only names the topic. A title that states the finding, such as "Revenue grew every quarter, ending at $447k in December", saves the reader the work of finding it. Taste.
- The plan table has no comparison. Every KPI has a "vs 2024" line, but the plans don't, so you can't tell which plan is driving the +18%. Add a change column or each plan's share (Few).
- The "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025" headings sit a few pixels off a shared top line. Align them exactly.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with its period and a comparison ("+18% vs Dec 2024", "+3 pts vs 2024"), and each green figure also carries a sign and words, so colour isn't carrying the meaning alone. Keep this pattern.
- The chart is honest: bars start at zero, ticks are even, it has units ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue") and a source line ("closed invoices only"). That's exactly what a finance reader needs to trust it. Once the totals agree, it will earn that trust.
- The page is restrained: one blue, neutral greys, a tight type scale (title, KPI numbers, section headings, body, captions) and right-aligned money in the table. It reads quickly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the "Jan to Dec 2025" chip is a working date-range control, and focus states on the side navigation.
