**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the big serif title "Leek and potato soup with brown butter". It then drops through a large gap to the two columns, and "Ingredients" and "Method" share a baseline. This is a recipe page on a small food site, for someone about to cook. Version 1 feels warm, like a cookbook page. Version 2 feels cooler and plainer.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a recipe page someone reads while cooking. It has to tell them what to buy and what to do, in order, and be easy to scan.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job well. Version 1 is a little better. The only change is the background: cream in version 1, white in version 2. The ingredient divider rules stayed cream-tinted, so on white they read as faintly yellow smudges. On cream they read as quiet structure. Everything else is pixel-identical.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's rules lost their surface (defect, small but visible).** This is the only difference between the versions.
   - In version 1 the six rules under the ingredients, at x 120–420 and y 428–613, are a darker tone of the cream ground. They separate the rows and you barely notice them.
   - On version 2's white ground the same warm rules look yellowish and slightly dirty. Next to them, the header rule at y 63 is a neutral grey, so the page now has two rule colours that don't seem related.
   - A colour reads against what surrounds it (Albers, *Interaction of Color*; simultaneous contrast). A tint chosen for cream doesn't carry over to white.
   - The white also drops the warmth that made version 1 feel like a cookbook, and that warmth suited a soup recipe.
   - Fix: keep the cream ground. If white is required, retint the rules to a neutral grey that matches the header rule.
2. **The serving line floats with nothing to hold it (defect, both versions).**
   - "Serves 4 · 45 minutes" sits about 100 px below the title and about 90 px above the columns. It belongs to neither, and the page has two empty bands where it should have one.
   - The gap between groups should be clearly bigger than the gap within a group (Arnheim; Wertheimer's proximity).
   - Fix: pull the line up under the title as its subtitle. That leaves one deliberate gap above the ingredients and method.

**Smaller things.**
- Navigation at top right: nothing marks the current section. Make "Recipes" bolder or underline it (Nielsen Norman Group, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Method step 4: "chives." sits alone on the last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Adjust the measure or rewrite the line slightly.
- Ingredient rows (about 37 px apart) and method lines (about 28 px apart) sit on different baselines. It's minor, because the two columns are read separately.

**What's working.**
- The title clearly comes first, and the faces have plain jobs. The serif is for the title and the instructions, and the sans is for labels, the navigation and the ingredient list, which makes the ingredients easy to scan as a shopping list.
- The method's numbers hang outside the text, and the lines run about 70 characters, which is comfortable reading.
- The copy is concrete and cook-friendly: "until the potatoes break when pressed", "until it smells of hazelnuts". Each step names a cue you can check rather than a timer alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- How the two columns stack on a phone.
