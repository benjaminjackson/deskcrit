**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild", drops into the intro paragraph, then gets caught between two blue shapes: the rounded blue bar in the illustration and the blue "Sign up" button to its right. It reads as a calm, public-service news item from a small ferry operator, with a sign-up panel for timetable alerts beside it. It feels tidy and trustworthy, and a little like a wireframe that was never finished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a ferry operator's page for regular passengers. It reports the rebuilt landing stage and asks them to sign up for timetable-change notices.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are competent. The page fails at its one action, though: the sign-up form promises to "send one short note" but never asks where to send it. Nobody can actually get timetable changes through it as shown.

**What would move it most**
1. **The sign-up form has no email or phone field (defect).** The panel asks for "Your name" and "Home landing", and the small print says "We send one short note when the timetable changes." There is nowhere for that note to go. A passenger who fills it in either gets an error they didn't expect or believes they've signed up when they haven't. Then they miss the change the form was for. If the contact details come from a logged-in account, the screen needs to say so, because a stranger can't see that. Fix: add the contact field as the first field, and label it with the channel ("Email for timetable changes"). Make it clear that one landing's changes go to that address. "Your name" adds nothing to a timetable alert, so drop it unless something uses it.
2. **The illustration has a fake button (defect).** The rounded blue bar at the right of Figure 1 has the same blue, corner radius and rough size as "Sign up". Shapes that look alike get read as related (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), so the eye pauses on it as a second control, and some viewers will try clicking it. It also weakens Sign up as the page's single blue action. Fix: keep that exact blue for controls only. Draw the boat (or whatever it is) in the picture's own muted blues and greys, and give it a shape that doesn't look like a button.
3. **The picture doesn't show what the text claims (taste/common sense).** The copy sells wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, a kiosk and step-free access at every tide. Figure 1 is three flat rectangles on a blue band, and you can't find a ramp, a roof or a bench in it. The caption says "seen from the water at low tide", but nothing in the drawing shows the tide or the ramp-to-deck join, which is the actual news. As it stands it reads as a placeholder. Fix: use a photo or drawing that shows the ramp meeting the deck. That's the part that matters to pram and wheelchair users. Or drop the figure.
4. **"Home landing" is a free-text field (convention).** Passengers will type "North", "north landing", "N. stage" or a misspelling, and a mismatched value means no alert. Nielsen's error-prevention heuristic calls for limiting input to valid values. Fix: use a select or radio list of the actual landings.

**Smaller things.**
- Neither field is marked required or optional. Mark the required ones, or add "(optional)" to the others (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- The footer line "Ferry timetables are a sample for a fixture image. All names and places are invented." is scaffolding text. Remove it before this ships.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clear.** I count four sizes: headline, panel heading, body, and caption/label. The headline and panel heading are bold, so the levels differ in weight as well as size. Keep it this lean.
- **The body copy is concrete and talks about the passenger.** "Prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" turns a construction fact into what changes for the reader. The rest of the copy should match that.
- **The form's small print sets honest expectations:** one short note, only when the timetable changes, and you can stop at any time. That's the right reassurance in the right place, right under the button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus and hover states on the fields and button.
- What happens after submit (confirmation or errors). That matters here, given the missing contact field.
