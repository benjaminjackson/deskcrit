**First read.** The eye lands on "Learn to swim" because it comes first in reading order. It then slides across to "Join the lanes" and "Family hour", because all three headlines are the same size and nothing ranks them. It reads as the programmes block of a pool or leisure centre site, and it feels confident at the top. Then it goes dead: the bottom half of every card is empty colour, and there's nothing to click, book, or check.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a section on a local pool's website that should help adults, lap swimmers, and parents each find their session and go on to book or check times.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job yet. The top 45% of each card is well set, but the cards end with no next step, and more than half of each card is empty colour. The viewer learns that the sessions exist and then has nowhere to go.

**What would move it most**
1. **No next step anywhere.** None of the three cards has a button, link, price, or "see times", and there's no section heading or venue name above them. A parent reading "Sunday afternoons… floats and toys" wants to know which hours, what it costs, and whether to book. The card doesn't say, and if the whole card is a link, nothing shows that it is. The viewer has to guess what's clickable and has to go elsewhere for the facts they'd act on. *Defect* (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: a page should make clear within seconds what you can do and where to click; NN/g on clickable elements; Nielsen's recognition rather than recall). **Fix:** give each card one specific, visibly clickable ask at the bottom, such as "See Tuesday class times" or "Book a family slot". Put the facts people act on next to it: exact times, price, and whether to book or turn up. Add a section heading that says where this is.
2. **The lower half of each card is dead colour.** The text ends at about y=330 and the cards run to y=630, so roughly 300 px × 3 of saturated rust, green, and violet carries nothing. That weight drags the eye down into an empty block, and it makes the cards look like they're still loading. The page itself has no room to breathe, with only 56 px of margin. *Defect/convention* (Ruder: empty space should be gathered and set against dense areas, not left as leftover; Arnheim on balance). **Fix:** either put the space to work by filling it with the facts and ask from finding 1, or cut the cards down to their content. If you want tall colour blocks for impact, give them a job, such as a photo of each session or a large time ("6am").
3. **Three loud, unrelated hues, filling nearly all the area.** Rust, green, and violet are each fully saturated and fill almost the whole composition. They don't share a family, and none of them suggests water, calm, or a place to swim. It reads as a generic card template that could advertise anything, so nothing tells the viewer this is *their* pool. *Taste*, backed by Refactoring UI on defining a small palette and Itten on colour matching the mood. **Fix:** keep the idea of telling the three sessions apart by colour, but pull the colours from one family that suits the subject, or tone down the large fields. Then a brand accent can mark the actions.

**Smaller things.**
- The row of cards isn't centred: there's 56 px on the left and about 96 px on the right of a 1280 px frame. That near-miss looks like an error. Centre it, or align it to a real grid.
- The cards look about 360 × 560 px, a height that follows neither the content nor any proportion. Size them from their content once the facts and ask are added.

**What's working.**
- The headlines are tight, heavy, and short. They break as phrases ("Learn to / swim", "Family / hour"), are set close enough to read as one unit, and name the session in the swimmer's own words. Keep the type and the copy.
- The body copy is specific and human: "a coach in the water beside you", "three speeds to choose from". It speaks to each audience's real worry. Keep that voice, and add the times and price in the same plain way.
- The type scale has two clear levels, with a big step in size and weight between them. Don't add a third size near either one when you add the facts and the ask.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are clickable as a whole, and what their hover and focus states look like. If they are links, they need a visible focus ring that shows against all three colours.
