**First read.** Three strong colour slabs hit first: orange, green, purple. The eye lands on "Learn to swim" because it sits on the left and the orange is warm, then moves across "Join the lanes" and "Family hour". Within a second it reads as a swimming pool's three programmes. Then the eye drops into about 300 px of empty colour under each blurb and stops. Nothing comes next. The mood is bright and friendly, but it doesn't feel like water, and it feels unfinished.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a section of a local pool or leisure centre's website that sends adults, lap swimmers and families to the right session and gets them to book or come along.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job yet. The headlines are clear and the blurbs are well written, but each card stops halfway down with no detail to act on and no way forward. The bottom half of every card is empty colour where the useful part should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no next step, and not enough information to act on.** None of the three cards has a link, button, price, time or place. "Tuesday evenings", "every morning from six" and "Sunday afternoons" tell people roughly when, but not what time it ends, what it costs, whether to book, or which pool. Krug says a page should make clear within seconds what you can do and where to click. Here a keen viewer has nowhere to go. *Defect.* Fix: each card ends with one specific ask tied to its session, such as "Book a Tuesday class" or "See this week's lane times". Add the one or two facts people decide on: the time window and the price or "free for members". A small line naming the pool above the row would also help, because right now this could be any pool's page.
2. **The bottom half of every card is empty colour.** From about y=330 to 630, each card is a solid block of saturated colour with nothing in it. It doesn't read as breathing room. It reads as content that's missing: a photo that didn't load or a button that's gone. It also makes the cards top-heavy, and the eye path ends there with nowhere to go (Arnheim on balance; Ruder on empty space doing work). *Defect.* If photos or buttons belong there and didn't render, check the live page. If not, fill the space with item 1 (details plus the ask, pinned to the foot of the card) or shorten the cards to fit their content.
3. **The colours don't say "swimming", and they cover most of the content area.** Burnt orange, forest green and purple make three loud, unrelated slabs. The colour-coding works, and each colour is labelled by its heading. But nothing here suggests water, calm or a pool, and at this size the colours drown the words (Itten, Block: colour mood should support the subject). *Taste, argued.* Keep the three-way coding if it's used elsewhere on the site. Otherwise either move to related water tones (blues and teals) or keep the hues and shrink the area they cover, for example a colour band or photo on top with the text on white below.

**Smaller things.**
- The row isn't centred. It has about 56 px of margin on the left and about 96 px on the right, so the block leans left. Centre it or line it up with the page's content column.
- "Small adult classes" can be read as classes for small adults. Try "Adult classes in small groups".
- "Join the lanes" is the one heading that needs its blurb to explain it. "Lane swimming" or "Swim lengths" says it straight away.

**What's working.**
- The headings are big, bold and tightly leaded. Each two-line heading reads as one unit, and the two-size type scale (heading and body) is clear and consistent.
- The blurbs are written for a person: "with a coach in the water beside you" and "floats and toys for the children" each make a concrete promise to a specific person. Keep that voice when you add the details and the ask.
- White text holds up on all three colours. By eye it's roughly 5:1 or better on each, so legibility isn't a problem.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are clickable as a whole, and whether they have hover or focus states. If they are links, they need a visible signal.
- Whether images in the lower half of the cards failed to load. Check the live page.
