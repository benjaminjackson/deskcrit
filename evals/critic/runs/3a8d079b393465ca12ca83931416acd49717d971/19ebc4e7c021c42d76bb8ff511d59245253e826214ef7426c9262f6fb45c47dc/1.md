**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. Six equal tiles in a 3×2 grid each pull the same amount. It settles briefly on the red one at top left, only because it's warm and comes first, then wanders across the row. It reads as a garden show gallery, but every "photo" is a flat colour block with a pale circle in the middle, so the page feels like a wireframe, not something to browse. The note at the bottom says a large feature tile gives the gallery its focal point. No such tile exists.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where garden show visitors browse the exhibits and choose one to open.

**Verdict.** This doesn't do its job yet. A gallery is there to show the garden, and this one shows none of it: all six images are placeholders. Behind that, the layout has no lead exhibit. Its own footer says there's a feature tile, so the design it describes and the one on the page don't match.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders (defect).** All six tiles are one flat colour with a centred pale circle. A visitor learns nothing from them. "Rose arch" next to a red rectangle doesn't show a rose arch, and in a garden gallery the picture is the whole reason to click. The six arbitrary hues (red, green, blue, ochre, mustard, purple) also make up the page's palette and fill about half the screen with saturated colour that means nothing. This is common sense, not a cited rule. If this is a capture where the images failed to load, check the live page. If it's the design, it isn't finished. Fix: put real photos of each exhibit in the tiles, with one consistent crop and colour treatment. The colour will then come from the gardens, and the arbitrary fills can go.
2. **No tile leads, and the copy says one does (defect).** Six tiles of the same size, weight and spacing compete equally to be seen first, so the eye has no starting point and no path through the page (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size). The footer line "The large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point" describes something that isn't there. A visitor who reads it will look for the feature and not find it, and it reads like a design note that leaked into the page. Fix: choose one exhibit to lead (the show's highlight, or this week's best) and give it a tile two columns wide or a full row, with the other five set back as smaller tiles. Then delete the sentence, or swap it for something the visitor can use, such as the show dates or how to see the exhibits.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Rose arch" title and description sit a few pixels higher than the other five captions, so the baselines across the first row don't line up. This near-miss reads as a mistake (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). Give every caption the same offset below its image.
- The descriptions are fine as labels but generic. If there's room, say when or where to see each exhibit ("Tomatoes in late July" already does this; the others could too).

**What's working.**
- The caption type scale is clean: a bold dark tile name over a regular grey description, a clear two-level step that's easy to scan. Keep it.
- The grid is regular, with even gutters and generous outer margins, and each caption clearly belongs to the tile above it, because the gap between rows is much larger than the gap between image and caption.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are clickable and show a hover or focus state. Nothing in the image marks them as links. If they open exhibit pages, check in a live browser that they look interactive.
- Whether the placeholder images are the design or images that failed to load.
