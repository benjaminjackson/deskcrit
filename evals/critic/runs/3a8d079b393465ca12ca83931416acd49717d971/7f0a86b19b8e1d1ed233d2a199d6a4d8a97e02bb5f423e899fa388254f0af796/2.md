**First read.** The eye lands on "Learn to swim" right away, then drops to the grey line under it ("small adult classes on Tuesday evenings"), then to the two small items, "Join the lanes" and "Family hour". Then it stops: no button, no time, no price, no place, no name, and the bottom 45% of the screen is empty. It reads as a calm, tidy first screen for a local pool, but it has nothing to act on.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming this is the first screen of a local pool's or swim school's website, meant to get adults who can't swim to sign up for the Tuesday class.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The type is clean and the headline is hard to miss, but someone who reads "Learn to swim" and wants to go has no next step and no details to decide with. The two items below also pull them away from the lessons.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing to act on (defect).** The page makes a promise and then stops. Someone who wants lessons still has to find out when on Tuesday, where, how much, how many people are in a class, whether beginners who are afraid of water are welcome, and how to book. None of that is here, and there's no button. The empty lower half (roughly y 450–800) is where it belongs. That emptiness also leaves all the weight in the top-left, so the screen looks unfinished rather than calm (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). Krug's test is that a visitor can tell within seconds what they can do and where to click, and this screen fails it. **Fix:** put the decision facts for the Tuesday class right under the subtitle, such as "Tuesdays 7–8 pm · 6 adults per class · £X for 6 weeks", and add one button that names the exact thing, such as "Book your first Tuesday lesson", not "Get started". If the class has a free or cheap first session, say so on the button. That lets the viewer judge the class by trying it instead of taking it on trust.
2. **The headline and the items below sell different things (defect).** "Learn to swim" plus "adult classes" tells a nervous beginner that this page is for them. But the only other content is open lane swimming at three speeds, which is for people who already swim, and a children's session. A beginner reads the cards and wonders whether this is really a lessons page. A lane swimmer never looks past "Learn to swim". Either way, one of them leaves. There's also no pool or school name anywhere, so the viewer can't tell whose page this is. **Fix:** decide what this screen is. If it's the lessons page, cut the two cards or move them lower under a heading like "Also at the pool". Make the space below the subtitle answer a beginner's questions (shallow end? afraid of water? what to bring?). If it's the pool's homepage, the headline has to cover all three offers, and each card needs its own link. Put the name at the top either way. (One message per screen is my judgment here, not a cited rule.)
3. **The copy leaves gaps the viewer has to fill (defect, mainly where it touches decisions).** "Tuesday evenings" gives no time. "Every morning from six" doesn't say until when or whether weekends count. "Three speeds to choose from" sounds like a feature, but it never says what it means for the reader: you won't get stuck behind someone slower or hold anyone up. "Join the lanes" is a label for a category, not a description of what happens. A visitor will hold the pool to these times, so give exact times on every line and end each card on what it means for the reader.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle (looks ~22 px, grey) and the card headings (looks ~20 px, near-black) are almost the same size, so they read as one level with two colours. Make the subtitle clearly bigger or the card headings clearly smaller and bolder (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale").
- The headline looks set at the face's text spacing, so at this size the letters and word spaces look a little loose. Tighten the tracking slightly (Bringhurst, section 2.1.1; Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80).

**What's working.**
- The headline is plainly in charge: one big bold line with a single obvious job. Keep that dominance when you add the booking details; don't let a new price or button match its weight.
- The subtitle earns its place. "With a coach in the water beside you" speaks straight to an adult's fear of drowning in front of strangers. It's the best line on the page. Keep it, and build the call to action around that reassurance.
- The setting is well judged: lines of about 60 to 65 characters, comfortable line spacing, real dark-grey text with plenty of contrast, one typeface, one clear left edge at 96 px, and a gutter between the two columns that matches.
