**First read.** The eye lands on the "Notifications" title, then goes down the four bold row labels and across to the blue toggles. The page reads at once as an email-preferences screen in a work-management app. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. In version 1 the descriptions under each label read easily. In version 2 they fall back to a pale grey, and the eye skips them unless it makes an effort.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a settings page where a signed-in user decides which emails they receive and needs to understand what each one is before switching it.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change between the two is the colour of the description text, and version 2 makes it too pale. That matters here because the descriptions are what tell the user what they are agreeing to receive: every Monday, after an hour unopened, at 8 am, at most once a month.

**What would move it most**
1. **The descriptions in version 2 are too faint (defect).** The helper line under each setting, such as "A short email every Monday…" and "We send no more than one a month", is set in a mid-grey that looks like about 3:1 on white. That is below the 4.5:1 that WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 requires for text this size. Helper text gets no exemption; only disabled or purely decorative text does. On this page the descriptions are not secondary detail. They are the actual terms of each setting: when the email arrives, what triggers it and how often. Turning them down reverses the page's priorities. The user can easily read "Mentions" but has to squint at "you have not opened it within an hour", and that line decides whether they turn the setting off. Low-vision users and anyone on a glare-washed laptop lose those terms entirely. Version 1 uses a darker slate grey, which still reads as one step below the labels and clearly passes. That is the right fix, and version 2 should go back to it. If you want more separation between label and description, get it from the labels' weight, not by lightening the descriptions further (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger; lighter, not smaller). Check the version 1 value with a contrast checker to confirm it is 4.5:1 or above.

**Smaller things.**
- Both versions: the off toggles ("Due dates", "Product news") have a very pale grey track on white, which looks well under 3:1 (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11). The knob position still shows the state, but the control's outline almost disappears. Darken the off track or give it a border.

**What's working.**
- The copy, in both versions. Every description says who does what and when ("sent at 8 am in your time zone", "no more than one a month"). The intro says up front that account and security emails can't be switched off. A user can decide without guessing, and that is the hardest part of a preferences page to get right.
- A tight, clear structure. There is one title, one intro line, and four ruled rows with bold labels. The type scale has three distinct sizes and each has a clear job.
- The single blue accent is kept for the on state and nothing else, so you can see at a glance which emails are on.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus indication on the toggles, and whether a screen reader announces each toggle with its label.
