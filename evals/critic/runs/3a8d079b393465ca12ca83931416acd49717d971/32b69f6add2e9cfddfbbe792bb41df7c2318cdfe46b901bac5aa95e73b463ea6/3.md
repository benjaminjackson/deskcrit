**First read.** The eye lands on the row of three big numbers and reads them left to right, since none outweighs the others. It then drops to the chart: three coloured lines all rising, with no names, no values and no dates. It reads as a standard SaaS analytics overview. It looks calm, tidy and templated, and the good news is told mostly by green text and lines going up.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a weekly health dashboard for a marketing or growth team: how much traffic is coming in, from where, and whether it is turning into sign-ups.

**Verdict.** The top half works, but the dashboard can't be trusted or read as it stands. The chart has no labels at all, so its main question ("which channel is driving this?") can't be answered. The conversion card reports a gain that its own neighbours show to be a loss.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read. Lines have no names, the y axis has no values and the x axis has no dates.** In "Visits by channel" the blue, orange and green lines carry no legend or labels. The gridlines have no numbers, and there is an empty band about 75 px deep under the baseline where week labels should be. The viewer can see that three things went up but not which channels they are, how many visits each one means, or which week dipped. That makes the panel decoration. It is the biggest element on the page and it answers nothing. Without a scale the viewer also can't tell whether the gaps between lines are 2× or 20%, or whether the axis starts at zero. **Defect** (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few: label axes, units and series). Fix: put each channel's name at the right-hand end of its line. Label the y axis with visit counts and the x axis with week dates, using the empty band that is already there. Label directly rather than with a legend (Tufte; Wilke). Telling the series apart by name also stops the chart depending on hue alone (WCAG 1.4.1). Right now orange and green are the only difference between two series.
2. **The conversion delta contradicts the figures beside it, and the green colour tells the viewer it's good news.** Visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups rose 2.4%, so the rate can't have gone up. Working back from those numbers, it was about 2.20% before and is 2.12% now, a drop of about 0.08 points. The card says "+0.1 pts vs previous 12 weeks" in success green. This is the figure a growth team acts on. Shown this way, they'd conclude that funnel efficiency is improving when visits are growing faster than sign-ups. **Defect**: a number the viewer will act on that is wrong, coloured with the wrong meaning (Apple HIG, *Color*: green is for success). Fix: check the calculation, show the true delta with its sign (it should round to −0.1 pts), and colour it as a decline. If "+0.1" is really measured against a different baseline, the label has to say which one.
3. **Nothing on the page says what happened.** The title "Overview" and the chart title "Visits by channel" name topics. The viewer has to work out the story themselves, and with the chart unlabelled they can't. *Taste* (the source for this point isn't on the approved list), but it matters for a glance screen. Once the chart is labelled, have its title or a one-line note state the finding in plain terms, e.g. "Paid search drove most of the growth; sign-ups lagged traffic."

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks" in the header (top right) looks like static text. If it's the period picker, give it a control shape (outline or chevron) so the viewer knows the range can be changed. If it's only a label, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- The KPI row: each card has a label, a large figure and a comparison to the previous period in a fixed order. The figures sit at one clear size above small, plain labels. Keep this pattern exactly.
- The layout is calm and consistent. One margin, one gutter and one card style frame everything, and all the key figures fit on one screen. That makes it easy to scan once the content is fixed.
- The gridlines are quiet and there's no chart clutter, so labels can be added without the chart getting busy.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a picker, and whether hovering the chart shows values. A hover tooltip would not fix the missing labels, because the overview still has to read at a glance.
