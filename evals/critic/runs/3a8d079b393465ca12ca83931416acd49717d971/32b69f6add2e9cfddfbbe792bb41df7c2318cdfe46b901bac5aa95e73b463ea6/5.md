**First read.** My eye goes to 184,220, then across the three KPI numbers, then down to the big chart: three coloured lines all rising gently. Then I start looking for what the lines are, and there's nothing to find: no legend, no axis values, no dates. It reads as a tidy, calm, fairly generic SaaS analytics overview. It looks finished until you try to read the biggest thing on it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly dashboard for a growth or marketing team, used to check whether traffic, sign-ups and conversion are healthy over the last 12 weeks, and which channels are driving them.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The chart takes up two-thirds of the screen and tells the viewer almost nothing, and the one KPI that matters most for a decision, conversion rate, is reported as improving when the numbers beside it say it fell.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart can't be read: no series names, no values, no dates.** This is a defect. "Visits by channel" has three lines and you can't tell which colour is which channel. The y-axis has no numbers and the x-axis has no weeks. The gridlines divide a scale nobody can read. The viewer can see that "three things went up a bit" and nothing else: which channel leads, by how much, or when the dip in week 8 happened. Without y values you also can't tell if the axis starts at zero, so the gaps between lines and the size of the slopes could be overstated. That matters because the chart's whole point is to compare channels. Tufte (*Visual Display of Quantitative Information*) and Few: every chart labels its axes and units, so nobody has to guess what a number measures. Put the channel name at the right-hand end of each line rather than in a legend (Tufte, Wilke). Add week dates along the bottom, add visit counts on the y-axis, and state the baseline.

   The empty strip about 70 px wide down the left of the plot and the empty band at the bottom look like space reserved for labels. If so, the labels may have failed to render, and that's the first thing to check in a live browser. Either way, what ships here is a chart without them.

2. **The conversion delta contradicts the other two cards.** This is a defect, and the worst kind, because it's the number people act on. Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups up 2.4%. When sign-ups grow more slowly than visits, conversion has to fall. Working backwards, the last period had about 173.6k visits and 3,820 sign-ups, which is about 2.20% conversion. So 2.12% is down about 0.08 pts, yet the card says "+0.1 pts" in green. A viewer who trusts the card will think the funnel is improving when it's getting worse. Either the delta is computed wrong (sign flipped, or measured against a different baseline) or the other two deltas are. Fix the calculation, and make sure a fall shows in a different colour from a rise.

3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** This is taste, but it's the cheapest win here. Even once it's labelled, "Visits by channel" makes the viewer work out the story themselves. A title like "Organic drove most of the 6% visit growth; paid flat since week 6" (or whatever is actually true) tells them in one line, and turns the chart from decoration into the answer to their question.

**Smaller things**
- Green means "up/good" in the KPI deltas and is also one of the chart series colours. Use a non-status colour for that series so "green" keeps one meaning (Apple HIG: Color).
- With no labels, colour is the only thing that tells the series apart. Orange and green can be hard to separate for colour-blind viewers. Direct labels (finding 1) solve this; otherwise add a second cue such as line style or markers (WCAG 1.4.1).
- The chart card has dead space: the empty left gutter and a roughly 70 px band below the baseline. Once labels are in, tighten the card so the plot fills it.
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) sits exactly where a date-range picker usually goes but is styled as plain text. If it's a control, give it a chevron or border. If it isn't, fine, but users will click it.

**What's working**
- The KPI row is well built: three equal cards, a clear label → big number → comparison order, and every number has a "vs previous 12 weeks" comparison, which is exactly the context a headline metric needs.
- The type scale is disciplined: page title, card value, card label and delta are clearly separate levels with no near-miss sizes, and weight does the fine work.
- It's restrained: light borders, quiet gridlines, one neutral background, and everything fits on one screen. Keep that calm when you add the labels.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the chart's axis labels and legend are missing by design or failed to render (check in a live browser). Also hover tooltips on the lines, which would partly but not fully excuse the missing labels.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its focus and hover states.
