**First read.** The eye goes to the three bold KPI numbers first, mainly 184,220 on the left, and then down to the large chart. The chart shows three lines climbing gently, and nothing says what any of them is or how big it is. The page reads as a calm, well-made marketing analytics overview. The trust it builds in the top strip drains away in the bottom 60%.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a dashboard for a growth or marketing team to check traffic and sign-up health over the last 12 weeks, and to see which channels are driving them.

**Verdict.** The top strip does its job well. The chart, which takes most of the screen, tells the viewer almost nothing. It has no series names, no values and no dates, so "Visits by channel" can't answer the one question it asks. Until the chart can be read, the page amounts to three numbers and a decoration.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read (defect).** In the "Visits by channel" card, the blue, orange and green lines have no names: there is no legend and no direct label. The y-axis has no values and the x-axis has no dates. A viewer can see that one channel is biggest and one is the most volatile, but can't say which channels they are, how many visits each brought in, or which week the dips happened (the blue dip around the 7th point, the orange swings). They also can't check whether the three lines add up to the 184,220 above, or how much of the vertical scale is really zero-based. Every chart needs labeled axes with units and a readable scale (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*; Few). Series should be labeled at the data rather than decoded from colour (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). The card leaves an empty gutter on the left (about x 48–120) and an empty band at the bottom (about y 690–760), exactly where tick labels would go. So the labels may exist and simply not have rendered when this was captured. If so, check them in a live browser. As it ships in this image, it fails. Fix: put the channel name at the right end of each line (there's room before the card edge), add y values with a unit and 3–5 date ticks along the x-axis, and show each channel's 12-week total next to its name.
2. **The series are split by colour alone (defect, WCAG 1.4.1).** Even with a legend added, blue, orange and green would be the only thing telling the lines apart. Viewers with a colour-vision deficiency would have to match swatches to lines. Direct labels at the line ends fix this as well as finding 1, so use direct labels rather than a legend.
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding (taste, I can't cite an approved source).** "Visits by channel" describes what is in the chart. On a glance-and-go dashboard, the useful thing is what changed: for example "Organic drove most of the +6.1% growth" (whatever is true). It's also worth asking whether visits by channel is the right chart at all. The KPI strip already says visits went up. What a growth team can't see anywhere on the page is which channels bring sign-ups, and that is the decision this screen should support.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is set like a plain label. If it's the date-range control, give it a border or a chevron so it looks like something you can change (NN/g). If it really is only a label, it's fine.
- "Overview" (about 24px bold) and the KPI numbers (about 26px bold) are almost the same size, so they read as one level by accident. Either shrink the page heading or make the numbers clearly bigger. "Overview" is also a heading that could sit on anyone's dashboard, and dropping it would cost nothing.

**What's working.**
- The KPI strip is exemplary. Every number comes with a label and a comparison with the previous period. "+0.1 pts" correctly uses percentage points for a rate, and the three figures agree with each other (3,912 ÷ 184,220 = 2.12%). Keep this exactly as it is.
- The chart is restrained: hairline pale gridlines, no frame, no fills. The data lines are the darkest thing in the plot, so once it's labeled it will read cleanly.
- Margins and gutters are consistent: one 48px outer margin, equal gaps between the cards, and card edges on a shared grid. The page feels calm and trustworthy.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the axis labels and legend are missing from the design or just failed to render in this capture. The reserved empty space suggests the latter, so check in a live browser.
- Hover tooltips on the lines, and whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a picker.
- Focus indicators.
