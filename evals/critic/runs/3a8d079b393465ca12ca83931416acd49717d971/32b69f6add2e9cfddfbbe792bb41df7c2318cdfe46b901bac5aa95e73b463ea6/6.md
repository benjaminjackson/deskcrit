**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top (184,220, 3,912, 2.12%), reads the green "+x% vs previous 12 weeks" lines under them, then drops to the three coloured lines below. That's where it stalls. The chart has no axis values, no dates and no key, so you can see three lines going up but can't tell which channel is which or how big the gaps are. It feels clean, calm and competent, and it reads as a default SaaS dashboard.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a marketing or growth analytics overview. A team checks it to see how traffic and sign-ups did over the last 12 weeks and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The top row works. The chart, which takes up two thirds of the screen, tells the viewer almost nothing. With no axis values, no dates and no series labels, "Visits by channel" shows three lines going up and nothing more. That one gap is the big problem: the largest element on the page is close to useless.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart has nothing to read it by.** The "Visits by channel" panel (y ≈ 280–760) has gridlines but no y-axis values, no week labels on the x-axis, and nothing naming the blue, orange and green lines. The viewer can't say which channel is biggest, how many visits a line stands for, or what week a dip falls in. The point of a by-channel breakdown is to compare channels, and they can't. Because there's no y scale, they also can't tell whether the axis starts at zero. So the gaps between lines, which look like big differences, might be large or tiny. Tufte (*The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2) and Few both say a chart needs labelled axes with units, so no one has to guess what a number measures. **Defect.** Fix: add y-axis values (visits per week) and week labels or dates on the x-axis. The empty gutter left of the plot (x ≈ 50–120) and the dead band under the baseline (y ≈ 685–758) already look reserved for them. Then put each channel's name at the right end of its own line rather than in a legend. That's Tufte's direct-labelling principle (Wilke says the same), and with only three well-spaced lines it's easy to do.

2. **The series are told apart only by colour.** Even if you know the channel order, blue, orange and green are the only way to tell the lines apart. For anyone with a colour-vision deficiency, or anyone printing this in greyscale, it fails. This is WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1, Use of Color. **Defect.** The direct labels from finding 1 fix this too. That's another reason to choose them over a coloured legend.

3. **Neither the chart title nor the page says what happened.** All three KPIs say "up vs the previous 12 weeks", and the chart then gives the viewer no help answering the obvious next question: *which channel drove it?* "Visits by channel" names the topic, not the finding. The viewer has to work out the story from unlabelled lines, which right now they can't do at all. **Taste**, argued on its own terms: a dashboard someone opens weekly should give them the answer they came for. Fix: once the lines are labelled, use the title or a one-line caption to state the takeaway, for example "Organic drove most of the growth; paid flat since week 6". Or mark the line that moved most and let the others recede.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count about six sizes. Two pairs sit too close to read as different levels. "Overview" (~24 px) is only a step below the KPI figures (~26 px), and the "Brightpath Analytics" wordmark (~17 px) is barely bigger than the "Visits by channel" title (~16 px). Keep four sizes: KPI figures, page title (clearly smaller, or made distinct by weight), panel title, and labels/deltas.
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top right looks like a plain caption. If it's the date-range control, which is where users will look for one, give it a button or dropdown shape. If it's only a label, this is fine as it is.
- The space under the plot (y ≈ 685–758) and the gutter left of it are leftover gaps rather than deliberate space. Filling them with the axis labels from finding 1 solves this.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with a comparison to the previous period, with a clear unit ("+0.1 pts", not "+0.1%", for the conversion rate). That's exactly how a headline metric should be shown. Keep it.
- The palette is restrained: neutral grey and white, colour only for data and the up/down status. It stays readable, and the chart's colours will stand out once they mean something.
- Everything fits on one screen, on a simple three-column grid with even gutters, so you take in the whole picture at a glance.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and whether the chart has hover tooltips. Tooltips would help, but they don't replace axis values on a dashboard people glance at.
