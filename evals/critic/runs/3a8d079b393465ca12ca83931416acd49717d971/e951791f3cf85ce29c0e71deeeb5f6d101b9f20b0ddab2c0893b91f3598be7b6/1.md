**First read.** My eye goes straight to the bold sans headline, then drops into three narrow columns. Before I've read a word, the gaps between words jump out: holes in lines like "twelve minutes in calm / weather, and a little" and "bicycle during the". It reads as a short information page about a river ferry. The mood is plain and calm, but the whole block hangs in the upper left with a big empty L of white around it.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an information page or panel telling riders how the ferry runs: the route, bicycles and winter service.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The text is meant to be read, and the justified setting in columns this narrow makes reading lumpy and slow. That one choice is most of the problem. The off-balance placement and a headline that promises more than the text delivers come after it.

**What would move it most**
1. **Justified text in columns about 30 characters wide breaks into holes and rivers.** Every column has lines stretched to two or three normal word spaces: "minutes between early", "weather, and a little", "Riders wheel them on", "bicycle during the", "In winter the timetable", "each landing, and the". The gaps line up down the columns into white channels. The reader stumbles on each wide gap, and the even grey that makes text easy to read is gone. Hochuli says that under about 50 characters "even wordspacing becomes difficult, if not impossible… large 'holes' appear" (*Detail in Typography*). Bringhurst calls the result "white acne" and gives 40 to 50 characters as the measure for text in several columns (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). These columns look about 28–35 characters wide. **Defect.** Fix: set the text flush left, ragged right. That alone removes the holes. If you want to keep justification, widen the columns toward 40–50 characters (two columns rather than three would do it) and turn on hyphenation.

2. **The block is stuck top-left and the page tips.** All the content sits in about the left three-quarters and top 60% of the frame. Roughly 270 px is empty on the right and 290 px below. The three columns also end at three different depths. The empty area reads as space nobody used, not as a margin you chose, so the piece looks unfinished or cropped wrong (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). **Defect** of placement. Fix: either make the text area fill the frame's width, or own the asymmetry. For example, use the empty right column for something that pulls weight: a timetable, a map, or a pull quote.

3. **The headline promises seasons and the text gives one.** "The river ferry through the seasons" sets the reader up for spring, summer, autumn and winter. Instead they get the route (column 1), bicycles (column 2) and winter (column 3). They'll hunt for the other seasons and feel short-changed. WCAG 2.4.6 asks headings to describe their content. The catalog marks this entry as reading content, so take it as common sense rather than a strict convention. Fix: retitle to what's there, something like "Riding the river ferry". Better still, give each column a short subhead (Route and times / Bicycles / Winter service), since each column already covers a separate topic and the reader is likely scanning for one answer.

**Smaller things.**
- Column 1 is the only place a time is given in clock terms ("every forty minutes between early morning and late evening"), and it gives no hours. A rider will act on this, so "early morning" needs a real time, as does "before dusk" in column 3 (copy, common sense).

**What's working.**
- The headline and body form two clearly different levels: a bold grotesque against a regular text serif. The contrast is in face and weight, not just size, so the hierarchy is clear immediately. Keep that pairing.
- The baselines line up across all three columns and the gutters are equal. The grid underneath is sound, so fixing the setting won't need a new structure.
- The serif body is a sturdy, comfortable reading face at a decent size and dark enough. The problem is the setting, not the type choice.
