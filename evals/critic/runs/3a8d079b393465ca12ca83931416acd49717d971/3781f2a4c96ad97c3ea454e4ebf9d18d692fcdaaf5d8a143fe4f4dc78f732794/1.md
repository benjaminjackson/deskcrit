**First read.** My eye goes first to the headline "Tell us about your project", then across to the white form card, then down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads straight away as the contact page of a small design studio. It feels calm, competent and tidy, and also generic: apart from the name, nothing on it says what this studio is like.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the contact page of an agency's website, meant to get prospective clients to send an enquiry the studio can act on. Correct me if it's something else.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the form is easy to fill in and the copy is friendly. The biggest weakness is that the page asks for a budget and a timeline but doesn't help people give them, so many enquiries will arrive without them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The request for a budget and timeline is in the wrong place and has no field to go in.** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them" sits under the headline in the left column, about 450 px from the Message box it refers to. People read the headline, move to the form, and type into an empty Message box with that advice out of sight. Even someone who remembers it has to write a budget into free text, which is a small task many will skip.
   - Help should sit next to the step it's about (Nielsen, heuristic 10).
   - **Fix:** if budget and timeline really matter to the studio, give each its own short optional field (a budget menu with ranges and a timeline menu), so the answer is one click. At the very least, move the line under the Message label as hint text.
   - **Type:** defect. It costs the studio the information it says it wants.

**Smaller things**
- **Navigation:** "Contact" looks the same as Work, Services and About, so nothing shows which page you're on. Mark the current page with one cue, such as a bolder weight or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Required fields:** none of the three fields shows whether it's required. Put an asterisk after each required label with a one-line key, or add "(optional)" to any optional field (Budiu, NN/g). This matters more if you add the optional budget and timeline fields.
- **Field outlines:** the grey borders on the three inputs look lighter than 3:1 against white. Measure the border colour. If it's under 3:1, darken it one step (WCAG 1.4.11).
- **Headline line break:** "Tell us about your / project" splits the noun phrase and leaves "project" alone on line two. Break it as "Tell us about / your project", or widen the column a little. This is taste: the reference entry for this rule has no confirmed source for headings.
- **Right edge:** the form card ends around x=1068, but the nav ends around x=1224. The card lines up with nothing, which leaves a stray strip on the right and makes the content look placed by eye. Take the card's right edge from the header's right margin, or from a column line the page uses elsewhere (Müller-Brockmann).

**What's working**
- **Order of importance:** there is one clear starting point (the bold headline), one primary button, and labels set in bold above every field. Nobody has to guess where to look or what a field is for. Keep that.
- **The intro paragraph:** "We read every message and reply within two working days" is a specific promise that answers the visitor's real worry, which is whether anyone will reply. That's much better than a generic "We'd love to hear from you." Keep the concrete number.
- **Restraint:** one blue carries the links and the button. Links are underlined, not marked by colour alone, and the body text is a comfortable size at about 50 characters a line.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Focus rings on the fields and button.
- Input types (for example, whether the email field rejects a malformed address).
- Validation and error messages.
- How the two columns stack on a phone.
