**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves right to the white form card, and drops to the blue "Send message" button. It reads as the contact page of a small design studio. It feels tidy, calm and anonymous: the layout and the words could belong to any agency, accountant or SaaS company.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: get a prospective client to send an enquiry the studio can act on, with scope, budget and timeline.

**Verdict.** It works as a form: it's clear, labelled and has one obvious button. It falls short as a studio's pitch. The intro asks for budget and timeline but the form gives them nowhere to go. And nothing on the page says who Fernwick is or why a client should write to them rather than anyone else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form ignores its own request.** The intro says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them", but the card has only Name, Email and one small Message box. The visitor has to remember that line and work it into free text. Most won't, so the studio gets a vague "we need a website" and spends its first reply asking for the budget. The Message box, about 120 px tall, also signals that a short note is enough. This is a defect (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). Fix: add a budget-range picker and a timeline field (or a start-date choice), both marked optional. Make Message taller and give it a prompt about what the project is. Then the intro line can go or shrink.
2. **Nothing on the page is specific to this studio.** "Tell us about your project", a grey background, a white card and a default blue button are the standard contact-page template. A client comparing three studios learns nothing here that makes them write to this one. The design gives no hint of how Fernwick works. This is taste, but for a design studio the page itself is a work sample. Fix: put one concrete reason to write next to the form, such as a named person who replies, a line about the kind of work you take on, or a recent project. Make the headline say something only you would say. The reply-within-two-days promise is good; give it more weight.
3. **The layout stops short and leaves leftover space.** The card's right edge, at about 1068 px, lines up with nothing. The nav runs to about 1224 px, so a strip about 160 px wide sits empty on the right, plus a band about 180 px tall under the card. Everything weighs on the upper middle, and the empty areas look accidental rather than chosen. This is a convention (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Ruder on gathered white space). Fix: run the card to the same right edge as the nav, or make the open space clearly deliberate, for example with a wider left column that holds the content from point 2.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing shows the visitor which page they're on. Mark the current item with one cue, such as bold or a bar (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- Form: no field is marked required or optional. If you add the optional budget and timeline fields, mark them "(optional)". If all three current fields are required, say so once (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- Input borders: the grey field outlines look pale against white, possibly under 3:1. Measure them, and darken them if they fall short (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- Labels sit above the fields, in bold, close to their own field and well away from the next. Keep this when you add fields.
- The reply-time promise ("reply within two working days") is concrete and useful to the visitor. It's the most persuasive line on the page.
- There's exactly one primary action, clearly styled, and the heading, body and label sizes are distinct, with no near-duplicate sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the email field validates its format, and what errors look like.
- Focus rings on the fields and the button.
