**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves right to the white form card, and comes to rest on the blue "Send message" button. It reads straight away as a small studio's contact page. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: apart from the wordmark, nothing on the screen is particular to Fernwick.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop contact page where a prospective client sends a design studio a project enquiry.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The ask is clear, the form is short and conventional, and the reply promise is concrete. Two things hold it back. The form doesn't collect the budget and timeline that the copy asks for. And the card sits off the page's grid, so the layout looks unfinished on the right.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy asks for budget and timeline, but the form gives them nowhere to go** (taste / common sense). The intro says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The form then offers only one blank Message box with no prompt. Visitors have to remember that request while writing freely, and most won't. The studio then gets enquiries without the two facts it needs to qualify them, which costs an extra round of emails and works against the "reply within two working days" promise. Fix: either add two short optional fields (a budget-range picker and a timeline field), or at least put a prompt in the Message box that names both. Then the request sits where the visitor is typing.
2. **The form card sits off the page grid** (convention: Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim on near-miss placement). The header content runs from x≈56 to x≈1224, but the card stops at about x≈1068. That leaves a ~156 px strip on the right that is neither margin nor gutter. The left column's text ends around x≈440 and the card starts at x≈509, and neither edge matches a column line. The ~450 / 560 / 156 split looks like it was placed by eye. The result is that the page looks slightly lopsided and less finished than the rest of the craft deserves. Fix: put both blocks on one column grid. For example, run the card to the 1224 edge, or split the content width into clear columns and keep the leftover space as one deliberate area, not a strip.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the current page. Mark it with one cue, such as bold or a heavier underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Form labels: no field is marked required or optional. If all three are required, put an asterisk after each label and explain the asterisk once at the top of the form, or mark Message as optional if it is (Budiu, NN/g).
- Input borders: the pale grey field outlines look below 3:1 against white. Measure them and darken them to at least 3:1 if they fall short (WCAG 1.4.11).
- Headline: the break "Tell us about your / project" leaves "project" alone and splits "your project". A slightly wider measure or a deliberate break after "us" would keep the phrase together (common sense).

**What's working.**
- The intro copy makes a concrete promise ("We read every message and reply within two working days"). That gives the visitor a real reason to trust the form and send. Keep it word for word.
- The form itself is what a form should be: one column, labels above the fields, bold labels sitting close to their own fields with clear gaps between groups, and one unmistakable primary button. There's nothing to learn and nothing to misread.
- The restraint holds up: one blue used consistently for links and the action, neutral greys elsewhere, and a three-level type scale (headline, body, labels) with clear steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and button, error and validation states, and the confirmation after "Send message". On a contact form these decide whether the visitor knows the enquiry arrived, so check them live.
