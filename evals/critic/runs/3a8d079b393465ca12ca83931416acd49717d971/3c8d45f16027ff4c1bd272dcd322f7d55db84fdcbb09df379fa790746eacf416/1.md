**First read.** The eye goes to "Book a workshop seat" and then down four identical empty boxes. Then it stops. There is no button and no next step, only blank white below the last field. It reads as a plain, competent booking form that someone stopped building halfway.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: a web page where someone picks a workshop and books seats on it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The form has no way to submit, so nobody can book anything. Behind that, every field is an open text box. That makes the person remember and type things the page should be offering them: which workshop, which dates exist, how many seats are left.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no submit button.** The page ends after "Notes for the host", with about 250 px of empty white where the action should be. Someone fills in four fields and then has nothing to press. They can't book, and they can't tell whether the booking already happened. This is the single defect that stops the page working, and it also leaves the eye with nowhere to end up (Arnheim). Add one filled primary button directly under the last field, aligned to the fields' left edge. Have it name what happens: "Book 2 seats" or "Request booking", not "Submit" (Material Design, "Buttons"). If this screenshot simply cut the button off, check the live page. As captured, though, the form is a dead end. *Defect.*
2. **The fields ask people to remember things the page should show them.** "Workshop name" is a blank box, so the user has to recall and spell the exact name. A typo or near-match ("UX intro" vs "Intro to UX") gives the host a booking they can't match to a workshop. "Preferred date" is free text, so "next Tues", "3/4" and "April 3" will all come in. It also never shows which dates the workshop actually runs, and "preferred" makes it unclear whether this is a booking or a request. "Number of seats" accepts any text, has no default of 1, and gives no limit. Use a list or radio choice of the real workshops, ideally showing date, time, price and seats left. Then offer the date as a choice of that workshop's sessions, and make seats a number stepper starting at 1 with the maximum shown (Nielsen 5 and 6). If someone arrived from a workshop's own page, prefill that workshop. *Defect.*
3. **Nothing says what's required.** The four fields look exactly alike, so the user can't tell that "Notes for the host" is presumably optional and the other three aren't. Put "(optional)" after "Notes for the host", or asterisk the required fields and explain the asterisk once at the top (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). *Defect, minor next to the first two, but cheap.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Notes for the host" is a single-line box. Free-text notes take a multi-line text area, and a one-line box tells people to keep it to a few words. Make it about 3–4 lines tall.

**What's working.**
- Each label sits close above its own field, with about four times that gap between field groups. Every label clearly belongs to its box, and the eye moves down the single column with no doubt. Keep that spacing.
- The type is quiet and disciplined: one family, a clear title size and one bold label size, all dark on white. Keep it this restrained when you add the button, so the button is the only strong element after the title.
- The fields are a generous height (about 42 px) with visible borders, so they read as typeable at a glance.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The focus ring on the fields, and whether the screenshot cut off a button below the fold. Check both in a live browser.
