**First read.** The bold black "Garden show gallery" lands first. The eye then drops onto the big terracotta tile and its "Rose arch" caption, and from there runs around the five smaller tiles. It reads as a photo gallery index for a garden show, and it feels tidy and clear. It also feels like a wireframe: what you see is six blocks of flat colour, each with a pale half-circle, not gardens.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where visitors browse photos of the exhibits at a garden show, with one featured garden.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound. The grid, the featured-tile hierarchy and the type all work. But the piece doesn't do its job yet, because no garden is shown: every picture is a placeholder. Once the photos are in, the main problem left is that captions sit closer to the next tile than they should, so it's unclear which tile each caption belongs to.

**What would move it most**
1. **Every picture is a placeholder (defect, common sense).** All six tiles are a flat colour with a centred half-circle. A gallery that shows no gardens gives the visitor nothing to browse, so the page fails at its one job. The swatches also cause a second problem. Six fully saturated hues (red, green, blue, ochre, yellow, purple) cover about 70% of the page. That reads as a UI colour set, not as roses, herbs and ponds. And because a large area is at full saturation, the featured tile gets no extra emphasis from its colour (Itten, *The Art of Color*, contrast of saturation; Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). If these are images that failed to load, check that in a live browser. Either way, the fix is the real photographs. Treat them consistently: one crop ratio and a similar grade, so the set holds together the way the swatches do now. If the flat tiles are meant to be the final look, give them muted tints, not full-strength hues, and expect the page to still say nothing about the gardens.
2. **Captions sit almost as close to the next tile as to their own (defect).** In the right column, "Herb bed / Thyme, sage and chives." sits about 10 px below its picture and about 16 px above the Pond edge tile. On a quick scan the caption floats between the two, and with real photos (busier than flat colour) the pull toward the tile below gets stronger. "Rose arch" has the same problem: its subtitle ends about 15 px above the Bean rows and Glasshouse tiles, so the big caption reads as half a heading for the bottom row. Group by proximity: the gap inside a group should be clearly smaller than the gap between groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on Wertheimer's proximity). The fix is to keep the caption tight to its image and open the space between tiles, for example to about twice the image-to-caption gap, or more under the hero.

**Smaller things.**
- The page title "Garden show gallery" could head any show's page. Name the show and its year ("Hartley Garden Show 2026: the gardens") so a visitor arriving from search knows they're in the right place. The name and year here are only an example. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- The grid. The hero spans two of three columns, the three-column bottom row picks up the same column lines and the same gutter (about 14 px), and the right-column stack ends level with the hero caption. That gives the page one calm structure. Keep it when the photos go in.
- The hierarchy. There is one clearly featured exhibit, and the caption levels are distinct: 36 px "Rose arch", then 18 px grey subtitle, then 14 px bold names, then 13 px grey descriptions, with weight and colour (not near-identical sizes) carrying the small steps.
- The captions themselves. Each one is concrete and specific ("Climbing roses over the east gate.", "Tomatoes in late July."). They tell the visitor what they're looking at and where.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are links, and whether they have hover and focus states. If they open larger photos, they need a visible focus ring and an obvious clickable look.
- Whether the flat tiles are deliberate placeholders or images that didn't load. Check this in a live browser.
