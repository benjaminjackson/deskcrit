**First read.** My eye goes to "Garden show gallery" and then drops straight onto the big red block, where it finds nothing to look at: a flat swatch with a pale circle. It moves on to the other five blocks and sees the same thing in five more colours. It reads as a gallery template for a garden show, with the photos not yet in. It feels tidy, cheerful and empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web gallery page where visitors to a garden show (or people thinking of going) browse its displays, with one display featured.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't do its job yet, because a gallery with no pictures has nothing to show. All six images are placeholder blocks, and the whole page depends on what goes in them. The grid and type structure under them are sound.

**What would move it most**
1. **None of the six images shows anything (defect, common sense).** Each picture area, from the 770 px "Rose arch" block to the five small tiles, is a single flat colour with a centred circle. The viewer gets no roses, beans or pond. The only thing that tells the exhibits apart is the colour, which is arbitrary (a purple orchard, a gold glasshouse). The captions do all the work, and they're the smallest text on the page. These placeholders also cause the colour problems. Six strong hues fill well over half the page, so the palette has no system (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*, "Define your shades"; Material Design colour roles) and the colour pulls attention away from the content. Put in real photographs before judging anything else. If the blocks are images that failed to load in this capture, this finding goes away, but check the photos then for a shared crop, light and colour grade. Six mismatched snapshots in this grid would look worse than the swatches.
2. **Captions float between their own picture and the next one (defect).** "Herb bed / Thyme, sage and chives." sits about 9 px below its image and about 15 px above the "Pond edge" image. The hero caption "Climbing roses over the east gate." ends about 15 px above "Bean rows". With the gap that close to the 14 px gutter, image and caption barely read as one card, and the eye has to work out which picture a caption belongs to. This is worst in the right column, where cards stack. Space should show the groups: the gap inside a card has to be clearly smaller than the gap between cards (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on proximity). Keep the caption tight to its image and roughly double the vertical gap between rows, to about 28–32 px.

**Smaller things.**
- The hero's grey subtitle and the "Pond edge" subtitle beside it miss a shared baseline by about 2 px (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Either align them exactly or move one clearly off.
- "Garden show gallery" doesn't say which show or which year, so the copy could sit on any garden site. Naming the show (for example "Hartfield Garden Show 2026") tells the visitor what they're looking at. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The grid is real.** It has three equal columns. The hero spans exactly two columns plus the gutter, and its caption ends level with the "Pond edge" card, so the asymmetric layout looks deliberate rather than leftover. Keep that relationship when the photos go in.
- **The type levels are clear.** I count about five sizes (title about 60 px, hero title about 40, hero subtitle about 18, card title about 14 bold, card text about 13 grey). The two smallest are told apart by weight and colour rather than by a near-identical size, which is the right way to do it (*Refactoring UI*: bolder, not bigger).
- **The featured item clearly dominates.** It's four times the area of the other tiles, with a larger title, so the eye knows where to start.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are clickable and what they open. If they are, check that hover and focus states exist.
- Whether the blocks are deliberate placeholders or photos that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- How the three columns reflow on a phone.
