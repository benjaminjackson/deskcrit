**First read.** The eye lands on "Crossing the river", then jumps right to "Good to know" and its blue button before it comes back to the left column. It reads as a plain, calm passenger guide for a small local ferry: clean, trustworthy, a bit generic. Once I'd read it, I still didn't know when the next boat leaves.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web info page for passengers on one ferry route. Its job is to tell them when they can cross and what they can bring, and to point them to the full timetable.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and easy to read, but it doesn't answer the question a passenger came with, and the sidebar is louder than the main content. The biggest problem is the copy. A schedule page with no times on it sends every reader to another page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The guide never says when the boats run.** Under "When the boats run", the copy says "between early morning and late evening", "thins to one boat each hour" and "before dusk". A passenger can't plan a crossing from any of those. "Before dusk" moves by hours across the winter, and "in winter" has no dates. These are the lines people act on and later hold the operator to, so vagueness here costs real trips: someone reads "late evening" and turns up at 9pm. This is a defect (common sense; no approved source covers it). Fix: give the first and last sailing times, say which months count as winter, and give the actual last winter sailing. If the times change too often to print, say "first boat 6:40, last boat 21:20 (see the timetable for winter times)" so the page at least anchors the reader.

2. **The sidebar shouts over the main content.** "Good to know" looks about 34 px, larger than the section headings (about 28 px). The card's body text (about 20 px) is larger than the main body (about 18 px). So the side note outranks the guide it supports, and the eye goes there second. The scale is muddled too. I count about seven sizes: 56, 34, 28, 24, 20, 18 and 16. The grey subtitle (about 24) sits close to the H2s (about 28), and the two body sizes are only a couple of pixels apart. Steps that small look like accidents rather than levels (Bringhurst, 3.1.1 "Don't compose without a scale"; Dair on weak contrast). This is a convention break, and here it costs you: the reading order runs backwards. Fix: set the card heading at or below the H2 size and the card text at the body size or smaller. Aim for about four levels: title, subtitle/H2, body, button.

3. **"Good to know" names nothing.** The card is about where to find printed timetables and notices, but its heading could sit on any website. A scanning reader skips it, or opens it expecting fares or rules (WCAG 2.4.6). This is a defect. Fix: name the content, e.g. "Printed timetables at each landing". The button also links to the online timetable while the text talks about the printed one. Either say that ("Or see the full timetable online") or bring the two together.

**Smaller things.**
- Card right edge (about x 1150) matches no other edge. The nav ends at about 1184. Put the card on the same right margin as the nav (Müller-Brockmann, one grid).
- Card top (about y 147) sits about 10 px below the H1's cap height. It looks almost aligned but misses. Align it to the H1 cap line, or drop it clearly lower.

**What's working.**
- The main column is set well. Lines run about 65–70 characters, the leading is comfortable, and each H2 sits close to its own paragraph with clear space above it, so the two topics read as separate chunks at a glance. Keep it.
- Restraint: one typeface, one blue used only for the single primary action, and a lot of calm white space. That suits a public-service page and makes the button easy to find.
- The bicycle paragraph is the right kind of copy: concrete and procedural ("wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"). The timetable section needs the same treatment.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the two columns reflow on a phone. The card will probably stack, and if it lands above the guide the sizing problem gets worse.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 7 yes, 1 no (headings name their section)
What matters first: 3 lines, 2 yes, 1 no (distinct levels)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (one grid; no near-misses)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: skipped, none
Charts and data: skipped, none
Interface: 33 lines, 30 yes, 1 no (anticipation: puts the needed information in front), 2 unsure (focus indicator, reflow)
