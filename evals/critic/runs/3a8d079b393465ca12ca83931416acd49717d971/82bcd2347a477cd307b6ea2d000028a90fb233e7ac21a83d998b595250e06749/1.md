**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold black headline, "Update the card that pays for your subscription". It then drops into a calm, well-set column of grey-black body text with a few blue links. It reads as a help-centre article for a SaaS billing product: quiet, competent and trustworthy. Then a gap shows up. I got to the bottom without learning how to change the card. The page explains rules and edge cases but never gives me the steps.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article for a customer, often a stressed one whose card just failed. The job is to get them to a working card fast and tell them what happens to their account in the meantime.

**Verdict.** The typography and layout are clean, and they're not what holds this page back. It fails its job as a how-to because it has no steps and no link to the place where you do the task. Its most consequential sentence, about when you get moved to the free plan, can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **A how-to with no "how" (defect).** The headline promises to show how to update the card. The body gives only rules (who can do it, which invoice is charged) and a decline policy. The only directions are "from the billing page", and that phrase isn't even a link, while four lesser phrases are. The reader has to go and hunt for the billing page and then work out the screen on their own. That's the one thing they came here to avoid (Nielsen, heuristics 6 and 10: put what the user needs to act in front of them). Fix: put a short numbered list of steps right under the headline, naming the real menu path and the button label. Make "billing page" a direct link. Move the rules about who can do it and which invoice is charged below the steps.
2. **The retry and downgrade sentence can be read two ways (defect, copy).** It says "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days… If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan." Is "the second attempt" the day-3 retry, since it's the second charge overall, or the day-7 retry, the second *re*try? And is "seven days" counted from the first decline or from the day-3 retry? The penalty is losing your paid plan, so a customer who picks the wrong reading will act too late. They'll hold you to the reading they picked. Fix: give one dated timeline, e.g. "We retry on day 3 and day 7 after the first decline. If the day-7 retry fails, you move to the free plan that day." Also say what the free plan takes away from them, in "you" terms.
3. **Links are marked by colour alone (convention, WCAG 1.4.1 / G183).** "check your billing history", "roles and permissions", "paying by invoice" and "restore your previous plan" are mid-blue with no underline, set inside near-black text. The blue and the body colour look closer than 3:1 to each other in lightness, so readers with low vision or colour-blindness may not see the links at all. That's a real cost on a page where the links carry the next steps. Fix: underline inline links by default, or measure the link colour against the body text colour and make sure it reaches 3:1.

**Smaller things.**
- **Left sidebar:** "Update a payment card" looks exactly like its neighbours, so nothing marks the page you're on. Mark it with one cue, such as bold weight, an accent bar or a tint (Laubheimer / NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Measure:** the body column runs about 80–85 characters a line, judged by eye, past Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Narrow the column by about 15%, or step the size up slightly.
- **No search:** a help centre with no search field in the header makes every visitor browse the menus (Nielsen heuristic 7). Add search to the top bar.

**What's working.**
- **A clean type scale:** I count about four sizes (H1, a bold subhead, body, and small nav/sidebar text). Each has an obvious job, and the subhead stands out from body text through weight rather than by being slightly bigger. Keep that.
- **The headline is task-phrased:** it's in the reader's words ("the card that pays for your subscription"), not a system label like "Payment methods". Keep writing headings this way.
- **Quiet, roomy layout:** one strong left edge for the breadcrumb, headline and body, with the subhead sitting closer to its own paragraph than to the one above. The page feels calm, which suits someone dealing with a payment problem.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether links get an underline or focus ring on hover and keyboard focus. Check that in a live browser, since links are currently told apart by colour alone.
