**What would move it most**
1. **Hang the opening quotation mark.** At top left, the “ sits on the 100 px margin. That pushes "The" about 35 px to the right of "just", "leaves" and "the" on the lines below. At this size the quote's left edge reads as a notch, and the quote no longer lines up with the masthead, attribution and caption, which all share that edge. Hang the mark into the margin so the T aligns with the j, l and t below. The whole column then shares one sharp left edge.
   This is taste, close to common sense: the catalog's source for the quotation-mark point is unconfirmed (only Bringhurst on hanging numerals in lists partly supports it), so I'm not calling it a convention. The reason is the size. In body text the shift is a few pixels and nobody sees it, but at about 80 px the shift is the width of a letter. It's the first thing the eye crosses, and on a piece this spare it's the only thing out of place.

**What's working.**
- **One clear focal point and three distinct levels below it.** The large serif quote dominates. The bold dark sans names the speaker, and the lighter grey sans gives the facts. Each level differs in size, weight or colour, so the reading order never stalls. Keep this restraint when the issue number or a call to action gets added.
- **The copy.** "That is when the gulls get bold" is specific and local; no other newsletter could have run it. The caption then gives concrete facts (5:40, bicycles, bread deliveries) that ground the quote without repeating it. Quote and caption make one idea together: mood, then the facts.
- **Gathered white space.** The empty right side and bottom are one deliberate area, not leftover gaps. Leading in the quote is tight enough that its four lines read as one unit, and the caption runs about 73 characters a line, within the 45 to 75 range (Bringhurst). The "fi" ligature in "first" is in place.
