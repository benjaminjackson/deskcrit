**First read.** The eye lands on the leaf pattern before it reaches the words. Hundreds of bright lime and dark green ovals fill the whole frame, and the headline is big and white but has to fight its way out of them. It reads as a plant shop's homepage and feels lively, even loud. It doesn't look shady, ferny or local. The subline and the outlined button sink into the leaves, and I only found the button on a second look.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage hero for a small local nursery that specialises in ferns and shade plants. Its job is to tell visitors what Fernhill grows and send them to browse the stock.

**Verdict.** The words and the structure are sound, but the background undermines them. A loud, cartoonish leaf pattern pulls as hard as the headline, makes the subline and button hard to pick out, and shows no ferns, no shade and no nursery. As it stands, the hero sells "green" and nothing more specific.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pattern competes with everything and holds up nothing (defect).** Saturated lime ovals at full contrast cover about 90% of the frame. The headline, subline and button all have to fight it, so the eye has no calm ground to settle on. The type should be the one dominant element, and here it isn't (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on one focal point). Fix: push the background far back. Darken and desaturate it, or cut it to a narrow value range, so the headline clearly wins. Better still, follow finding 2.
2. **The picture doesn't make the headline's point (taste, argued).** The headline promises ferns and shade plants, but the image is flat, evenly scattered ovals with no fronds, no shade and no greenhouse. Image and headline should add up to one idea. Here the image only says "leaves," and its bright, sunlit lime works against the word "shade." It also looks like a generated placeholder (if a photo was meant to load here, check that first). The cost is that this hero could belong to any garden shop, and Fernhill's specialty and place never show. Fix: use a real photograph of your ferns, ideally on the hill or in the greenhouse. Pick a darker, shaded image with a calm area where the type can sit.
3. **The subline and button sit on the busiest part of the frame (defect).** "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend." is small white text with lime leaves running behind its letters. The "Browse the greenhouse" button is only a 1–2 px white outline on that same background, so its edge breaks up and it reads as faint (WCAG 1.4.3 via F83 for the text; 1.4.11 for the button edge). This is the one action on the page, and it is the hardest thing to see. Fix: put a dark scrim or solid panel behind the text block, or move the text onto a calm area. Make the button a solid fill, such as white with dark green text, so it is the second thing the eye finds after the headline.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline break:** "corner" sits alone on the third line, a runt that leaves a long-long-short shape. Rebreaking or widening the measure slightly would give "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner."
- **Left-edge near-miss:** the logo sits at about x 64 and the hero text at about x 96. Align them, or offset them clearly.
- **Navigation:** nothing shows which page you're on. This is minor on a homepage, but it matters on inner pages.

**What's working.**
- **Copy:** "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" is specific and concrete. It gives provenance and a reason to come now. Keep it.
- **Button wording:** "Browse the greenhouse" names a real place and a real action, not "Shop now."
- **Type:** the headline is set big, bold and tight, with three clear levels (headline, subline, button/nav) and no near-duplicate sizes. Once the background is calmed, the type system will carry the page by itself.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the leaf pattern is a placeholder for a photo that didn't load, or animates; check this in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the outlined button.
