**First read.** My eye goes straight to "Crossing the river", then down the left column through the three bold subheads. It reads as a short feature or guide page about a ferry crossing, set as two text columns. It feels calm and bookish, a little plain. A second later I notice the two columns don't quite sit together: the right one starts slightly higher and its lines don't line up with the left.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short piece of reading on a web page or in a printed booklet, meant to be read for pleasure from start to finish.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the type is comfortable to read and the copy is good. What holds it back is craft at the level of single lines: the two columns don't share a top edge or a line grid, and every paragraph after a heading is indented. A careful reader will see the page as almost finished rather than finished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The two columns don't line up with each other.** The right column's first line ("Stallholders arrive…") sits a few pixels above the line of "Early boats". It is neither aligned nor clearly offset, so it reads as a miss. From "Going home" down, each right-hand line sits level with the gap between two left-hand lines. Scanning across the gutter, you see two pages slid against each other instead of one spread. The cause is that the space around the headings isn't a multiple of the line spacing, so each heading pushes the column off by part of a line. Fix: set the space above and below each subhead so heading plus space adds up to a whole number of body lines (or use a baseline grid), and start both columns on the same top line. *Convention* (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid"; Arnheim on placements that are almost but not quite aligned).
2. **The first paragraph under each heading is indented.** "The first ferry…", "The market quay is…" and "In the evening…" all start with an indent, even though a heading already marks the new section. The indent separates nothing there, so each section opens with a small hole in its left edge right under the bold subhead. Fix: set the first paragraph after a heading flush left and keep indents only between paragraphs. The current indent size is fine. *Convention* (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.3.1).

**Smaller things.**
- Short last lines: "flowers." and "the engine." are single words alone on a last line, and "the last boat." closes the piece on a near-runt. Rewrite or adjust the line breaks so each paragraph ends with at least two words (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- The type scale has three clear levels: a large bold serif title, bold subheads and roman body text. Each size has an obvious job, and the subheads differ from the body in weight as well as size. Keep it.
- The body text is easy to read. Lines run about 60 to 65 characters with generous line spacing, and the dark grey text on white has strong contrast.
- The space works: each subhead sits much closer to its own text than to the paragraph above, and the empty space is gathered at the foot of the page rather than scattered.
