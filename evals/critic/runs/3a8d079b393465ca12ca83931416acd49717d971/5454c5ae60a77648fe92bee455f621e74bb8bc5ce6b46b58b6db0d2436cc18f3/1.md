**First read.** The eye goes straight to the hero, a full-width spread of bright illustrated leaves, and then hunts for the white headline inside it. The letters keep dissolving into the pale yellow and cream leaves. A leaf runs through "small" like a strikethrough, and the full stop after "spaces" merges with a white berry. Then the eye drops to the intro line and three tidy cards, and stops there. It reads as a friendly local garden centre homepage that has nothing for you to do next.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's the homepage of a local garden centre, meant to get people with shady or small gardens to browse the plants or come in this weekend.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The calm, well-spaced lower half is sound. But the headline is hard to read against its own background, the picture doesn't show the idea the headline states, and the page gives you nothing to do. A visitor understands the offer slowly and then has nowhere to go.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline dissolves into the pattern** (defect). "Plants for shade and small spaces" sits directly on the busiest, lightest part of the leaf pattern, with no scrim or panel. White letters lose their edges wherever a pale yellow or cream leaf passes behind them: across "small", the "P" of "Plants", and the full stop. Where contrast fails is the worst point behind the text, not the average (W3C, *Techniques for WCAG 2.2*, F83). The fix is to put a dark scrim, a see-through overlay or a solid panel behind the text, or to move the headline onto a calm, dark area of the illustration. Every letter should stand out from what is directly behind it.
2. **There is no action anywhere on the page** (defect). The intro promises ferns, hostas and shrubs "ready to pot this weekend", and then the page ends in three information cards and blank space. There's no "See the shade plants" button, no way to book the workshop and no directions link. The order of reading, headline then intro then action, stops before the action. Wanting to buy is exactly the moment the page drops the visitor. Add one clearly primary button under the intro that names the specific thing, like "Browse ferns, hostas and shrubs for shade". Keep any second action ("Plan your visit") visibly lower in weight (Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis levels).
3. **The picture doesn't show the headline's idea** (taste). The headline is about shade and small spaces. The image is a sunny, generic confetti of leaves that could sit on any plant shop's site, and it shows neither shade nor a small space. The picture takes up half the screen and adds nothing the words don't already say. In fact it works against "shade" with its bright yellow-green. Use a picture of the actual idea: a shady corner, a balcony or a narrow side bed planted with the ferns and hostas named below. Then the image proves the claim instead of decorating it, and you get calm areas for the headline as a bonus.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline alignment: its left edge sits at about 72 px, while the logo, intro and cards all line up at 56 px. That's a near-miss that looks like a mistake. Line it up at 56, or move it clearly off that edge.
- Headline line break: it breaks as "…and small / spaces", which splits the phrase and leaves one word alone on line two. Break it as "Plants for shade / and small spaces".
- Type scale: I count about four close sizes below the headline. They are the intro (about 19 px), the card headings (about 17 px bold), the nav (about 16 px) and the card text (about 15 px), so they read as sizes picked one at a time (Bringhurst, §3.1.1). Pick three clear steps and separate the card headings by weight only.
- Workshop card: "ten places in each class" makes places sound scarce, but the card gives no time, date, price or way to book, so the visitor has to go and look for them. Add the next date and a booking link.

**What's working.**
- The lower half is calm and disciplined. The three cards are equal, evenly spaced and share one edge, and each heading sits tight to its two lines of text. Keep this structure.
- The copy is concrete and local: "Free within ten miles on orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box" and "Open every day from 8 am to 6 pm, until 8 pm on Thursdays". These tell you exactly what you get, with no filler. The intro line names real plants rather than "great greenery".
- The green serif logo against the plain sans text is a clear, warm pairing that suits a family garden centre.
