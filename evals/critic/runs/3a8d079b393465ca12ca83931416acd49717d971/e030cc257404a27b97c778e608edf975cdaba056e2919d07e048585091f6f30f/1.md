**First read.** The eye lands straight on the white headline, "See where every dollar goes this month". It drops to the subhead and then to "Try it free". Then it slides right into a large empty navy block with nothing in it, and down to three plain feature columns over a blank white band. It reads as a budgeting app for individuals and households. It feels calm and trustworthy, but templated, as if the picture hasn't loaded yet.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a home page for a consumer budgeting app, meant to get first-time visitors to start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The words do their job and the type is clean, but the hero sells an app the visitor never sees. The empty right half of the navy band is the biggest thing in the way. Next is an ask that doesn't say what "free" means.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no product in it, and its right half is dead space.** About 40% of the navy band (x ≈ 790–1280) holds nothing. All the weight sits on the left, so the band tips and looks unfinished (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). The bigger cost is what's missing. The headline promises you'll "see where every dollar goes", but the visitor sees nothing. For a money app, a picture of the actual screen is the proof: categories, a limit, the "heads-up" alert. If an image failed to load in this capture, then it's fine as designed and you should check it live. If it was left out on purpose, it's a **defect**. Fix: put one real product view on the right that shows the promise in the headline, such as a month's categories with one close to its limit and the alert showing.
2. **"Try it free" is the line people will hold you to, and it can be read two ways.** It could mean a free plan or a time-limited trial that then charges a card. A visitor who assumes "free" and gets billed on day 15 feels tricked. One who assumes a trial and would have stayed on a free tier may not bother. Nothing nearby, not even a line under the button, settles it. It's also an ask that could sit on any SaaS page: it says nothing about what happens when they click. **Defect.** Fix: name the terms and the first step, e.g. "Start free – 30 days, no card" or "Connect your bank free", with a short line under the button giving price and cancel terms.
3. **The strongest trust point is buried.** "The app can see balances and history but can never move money" answers the first fear anyone has before linking a bank account. Right now it's the third of three equal, unillustrated feature columns below the fold line of attention. **Taste.** Pull it up next to the buttons, for example as a short reassurance line under them ("Read-only. We can never move your money."), so it's there at the moment of decision.

**Smaller things.**
- The feature row is three plain text pairs with no picture, number, or icon, and a blank band runs underneath. It looks like default template output, so nothing in it sticks. If the page continues below, give each feature one concrete visual or example. If it doesn't, the page ends too soon.

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and speaks to the viewer: "every dollar" and "this month" put it in the viewer's own terms and time frame. The subhead says exactly what happens in three verbs (connect, set a limit, get a heads-up). Keep both.
- The buttons are ranked clearly: a filled white primary next to an outlined secondary, so it's obvious which one to press.
- Spacing and alignment are disciplined. There's one 64 px left edge, three even columns, and headings sit tight to their text. The calm navy-and-white palette suits a product that asks for bank access.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image failed to load (check the empty right half in a live browser). Also hover and focus states on the two buttons and the nav links.
