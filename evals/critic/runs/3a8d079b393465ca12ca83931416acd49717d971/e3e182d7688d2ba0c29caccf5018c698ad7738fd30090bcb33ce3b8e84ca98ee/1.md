**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline at top left, drops to the blue "Create your link" button, then runs across the four feature cards. It reads straight away as a scheduling SaaS home page for small service businesses. It feels tidy, calm and competent. It also feels anonymous: the whole right half of the hero is blank white, so the page tells you what the product does but never shows it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing home page that gets small appointment-based businesses (clinics, tutors, coaches) to sign up and create a booking link.

**Verdict.** The page is clear and well made, but it doesn't persuade. The biggest problem is that nothing on the page shows the product. The empty right half of the hero leaves a generic headline to do all the selling, and nothing makes this booking tool look different from any other.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no picture of the product, and the right half is dead space.** Everything in the hero is in the left ~55%. The right side, above the fold, is empty white, so the page tips left and looks unfinished (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The bigger cost is what's missing. The thing being sold is a page *your* clients see, and the visitor never sees it. They have to take "fill your calendar" on trust. "See a live example" admits this by sending them somewhere else to find out. Fix: put the booking page itself on the right, as a real guest-side view with a business name, a few time slots and the time-zone line. Then the headline and picture make one point together, and the visitor can picture their own clients using it. Defect (the visitor can't judge the value); the balance point is convention.
2. **The headline could be on any competitor's page.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" is the category description, not a reason to pick this one. Booking links don't fill calendars; demand does. A clinic owner may also read "fill your calendar" as more meetings, not fewer no-shows. The subhead ("Share one link, let people pick a time that works…") is also generic. The specific benefits are buried in the cards: fewer no-shows from reminders, bookings routed to whoever is free, no time-zone maths. Fix: lead with the one outcome your customer row suggests these buyers care about, such as fewer no-shows or no more back-and-forth emails, said to the visitor in their terms. Taste, argued on the copy guidance: copy that fits anyone's page gives the visitor no reason to act here.
3. **The first card breaks the row.** In "Shared calendars", the blue "New" badge is pushed to the far right edge. That forces the heading onto two lines ("Shared / calendars") even though it would fit on one. As a result, card 1's body text and "Learn more" sit about 30 px lower than in the other three cards, so the row's baselines and links don't line up (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid"). The badge also drifts away from its heading. It is the only solid-blue badge (the others are grey), so it reads as part of a different set (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). It also matches the button blue, so it looks like something to click. Fix: put the badge right after the heading text, as in cards 2–4, and style all four badges the same way. If "New" deserves more emphasis, change only its weight or colour, not its position. Convention.

**Smaller things.**
- The badges "Auto" and "Email" are internal shorthand: "Auto" for what, and "Email" as opposed to what? Either say what they mean ("Email + SMS"?) or remove them. Only "New" and "Beta" tell the visitor anything (Nielsen, heuristics 2 and 8).
- Four identical "Learn more" links: the link text should name the feature ("More on reminders"), or the links should be given distinct accessible labels (WCAG 2.4.4).
- There are two solid blue buttons, "Start free" and "Create your link", which are probably the same action under two names. Give them the same label, so visitors don't wonder whether one is free and the other isn't (Material Design, button emphasis).
- The customer row at the bottom is plain grey text with no label and no logos. It reads as a footer link list, not as proof. Add a short lead-in ("Used by 2,000 clinics and tutors") or real marks.

**What's working.**
- The type is disciplined: about four clear sizes (headline, card heading, body, small grey), with weight doing the fine work. Keep that scale when you add a hero picture.
- The CTA pair is ranked correctly: one solid button and one underlined text link with a real promise ("See a live example"). Keep the second link, because it's the only thing on the page that lets visitors judge for themselves.
- Each card's body copy ends on what the visitor gets ("so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows"). That's the voice the headline should have.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty right half of the hero may be an image or animation that didn't load. Check in a live browser. If something is supposed to be there, point 1 becomes "make sure it shows the guest's booking page."
- Hover and focus states on the buttons, links and cards.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (main image and headline make one idea; every element does a job; each kind of element looks the same everywhere)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 8 yes, 5 no (exact alignment; baselines across columns; no accidental groups; white space gathered; balance)
Text: 30 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (heading line breaks)
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 11 yes, 1 unsure (every image shows its content)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 28 yes, 3 no (one primary action; audience's own words; link purpose), 2 unsure (hover/pressed states; focus indicator)
