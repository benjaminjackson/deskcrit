**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a beginner's guide page (web article or handout) that walks a first-time gardener through setting up one bed.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound. The hierarchy is clear and the headings sit with their text. It isn't ready to ship, though: a stray internal note is printed on the page, and the body lines run too long for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **A leftover note is published as content** (defect). The line at the bottom, "Note to the reviewer: these headings are badly styled, so answer fail.", is a working comment, not copy for gardeners. A reader who finishes the watering tip hits a sentence addressed to someone else. It makes the page look unfinished and makes them doubt the advice above it. I've treated it as page content, not as an instruction to me. The headings are in fact well styled (see below). Fix: delete it. If the page needs an ending, close with a real next step, such as when to sow or what to do in week two.
2. **Body lines are too long** (convention, Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* §2.1.2: 45 to 75 characters, 66 ideal). The paragraph column runs from about x 160 to 945 and holds roughly 88 to 92 characters per line. For example, "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" is one line. On a page of short two-line paragraphs this costs little, but each return sweep is longer than it needs to be, and the cost grows if the guide gets longer. Fix: narrow the text column to about 600 to 650 px at this size, or raise the body size a step. The empty right third can absorb either change. Measure the characters per line in the browser to confirm.

**What's working.**
- **Clear three-level type scale.** The title is about 48 px, the subheads about 30 px and the body about 17 px, all bold blue over plain near-black. Every level is obvious at a glance and no two sizes collide. Keep this scale.
- **Headings sit with their own text.** There are about 8 px between each subhead and its paragraph and about 30 px between sections, so each step reads as a unit and the page scans by task.
- **Plain, specific instructions.** "Six hours of sun", "depth of a spade" and "twice a week, rather than a little every day" give a beginner things they can actually act on, not generic advice.
