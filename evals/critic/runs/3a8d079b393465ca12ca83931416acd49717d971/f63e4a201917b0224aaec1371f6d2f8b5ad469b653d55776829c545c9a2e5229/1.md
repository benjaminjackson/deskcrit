**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, drops to the orange "Start your free week" button, then gets pulled straight down into the purple "Keep your streak going" box, which is as loud as the button and looks like a second, bigger one. The large teal panel on the right holds two outline chat bubbles; it fills the space but adds nothing to the headline. The three steps at the bottom read cleanly. It's a language-learning app's landing page, tidy, but in three unrelated colours.

**What it's for.** Assumed: the first screen of a consumer language app's landing page, meant to get a first-time adult visitor to start the free week.

**Verdict.** The headline and the steps do their job, but the hero works against its own button. A purple message meant for existing users sits right under the CTA and looks like a competing button, and a big teal panel of clip art takes the space where proof should go. Remove the purple card and replace the teal panel with something that shows the product, and this becomes a good page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple streak card competes with the button and talks to the wrong person.** *Defect.* It sits directly under the orange CTA with the same rounded corners, a fill just as strong, and bold white text, so it reads as a second, larger action (Material Design 3 and Apple HIG: one primary action per view is dominant). The eye goes button → purple box and stops there, because the box isn't clickable and leads nowhere. Its copy also assumes the visitor already has a streak, which nobody on an acquisition page has. It uses the product's own words, not the visitor's (Nielsen, heuristic 2). The fact inside it is useful, but it's a retention message. Fix: remove it from the hero. If you want the statistic, turn it into plain grey text below the steps, written for a newcomer ("Five minutes a day is enough: people who practise five days in a row finish twice as often"). The orange button should then be the only filled colour shape in the left column.
2. **The teal panel says "conversation" again and shows nothing.** *Taste, argued.* The headline already promises conversation; two empty speech bubbles repeat it in clip art. That makes the panel the largest and most saturated block on the screen, and it carries no information. A visitor deciding whether to give you ten weeks wants to see or hear the thing: a real lesson screen with the word-by-word feedback from step 2, a short clip of a learner speaking at week 1 and at week 10, or the tutor responding. That panel is your best chance to back up "ten weeks", and right now nothing on the page supports the promise. Fix: replace the illustration with a product screenshot or a before/after sample, so the visitor can judge the claim for themselves.
3. **Three loud hues, no system.** *Convention.* Orange (CTA), purple (card), and teal (panel) are three saturated accents on one screen. Apple HIG and Material reserve one accent for the main action, and Refactoring UI asks for one or two brand hues. Because each colour does a different job, the orange button stops being special, and the page looks assembled rather than designed (taste). Fix: keep orange for the action only. Making the purple card plain (finding 1) gets you most of the way. Then either tone the right-hand panel down to a neutral or very light tint, or let the product image supply the colour.
4. **The copy leaves gaps the visitor has to fill.** *Defect for the ask, taste for the rest.* "A tutor that listens": a person or software? That changes the price the visitor expects and how much they trust "ten weeks", so say which. "Finish with a short chat about your day": with whom? "Start your free week" is a decent ask but generic. Naming the language they'll pick ("Start Spanish free for a week") or offering one lesson now ("Try a 5-minute lesson") gives them one exact thing to do and lets them judge the value themselves. Also say what happens after the week (card required? auto-renew?), because that is the line they'll hold you to.

**Smaller things.**
- The teal panel's right edge stops about 150 px short of the right edge that the nav and the third step column share (~1224 px). It looks like a near-miss, not a choice. Run it to that edge or onto a column of the same grid (Müller-Brockmann).
- The bottom third of the screen is empty below the steps. Fine if more page follows, but check in a browser that the fold doesn't land on blank space at common laptop heights.

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and outcome-shaped: "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks" names a result and a time frame. Most competitors' headlines could sit on anyone's page; this one couldn't.
- Type is disciplined: one family, about four sizes with clear jobs, and bold rather than size separating the step heads from the lede. Keep that.
- The three steps (Listen / Speak / Use it) under thin rules explain the method in seconds and line up on a clean three-column grid. They're the clearest part of the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus and hover states on the CTA and nav links.
- Whether the hero reflows sensibly on a phone, where the purple card would push the steps further down.
