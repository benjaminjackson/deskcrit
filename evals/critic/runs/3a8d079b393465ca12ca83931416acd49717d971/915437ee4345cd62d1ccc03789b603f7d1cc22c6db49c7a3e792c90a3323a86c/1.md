**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web or printed notice that tells passengers when the ferry runs, how long a crossing takes, and what to do with a bike.

**Verdict.** The setting is clean and easy to read, but the page doesn't do its job well. Every fact a passenger comes for is buried mid-sentence and given only vaguely, with no clock times, so the one question they have ("when is the next boat?") can't be answered from this page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable is written as prose, and its numbers are vague** *(defect)*. A passenger skims for times, but the facts sit in the middle of sentences. "Every forty minutes" comes after a route description. "Between early morning and late evening" gives no hour. In winter it "thins to one boat each hour", but winter has no dates, and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" gives no time either. Someone standing at the landing at 18:30 in November can't tell from this whether a boat is still coming. The page even sends them elsewhere: "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing." So this page admits it isn't the timetable. Times and dates are exactly what readers act on and later hold the operator to, so a line that can be read two ways here is a defect, not a matter of style. Direction: pull the service facts out of the prose into a short scannable block or small table: first and last departure from each landing, how often boats run, crossing time, and the winter dates and hours. Keep the prose only for the background (tide, sandbank). Pages are scanned, not read; they need short blocks and obvious answers (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web").
2. **The first paragraph's second sentence runs on and hides its point** *(defect, copy)*. "Each crossing takes about twelve minutes… and a little longer when the tide turns… on most mornings at low tide" is a 49-word sentence. It ends on a sandbank, not on what it means for the passenger, and "a little longer" has no number. Direction: say it to the reader with a figure, for example "At low tide, usually in the morning, allow about N minutes", and cut the harbour-wall detail or move it to the end.

**Smaller things.**
- Rag, all three paragraphs: the right edge looks hand-broken. The last line of paragraph 1 ("sandbank… low tide.") runs about 40 px past every other line. "quay" would fit on line 1, and "and" would fit after "boarded,". This makes a lumpy edge rather than a gentle rag (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.3; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, "The line"). Remove the forced breaks and let the column wrap at one width.

**What's working.**
- The type is set right for reading. A plain sans at a comfortable size, lines of about 50 to 65 characters, generous leading and dark-on-white contrast make the prose effortless to get through. Keep all of this when you add the timetable block.
- The hierarchy is clean: one bold headline that is clearly bigger and heavier than the text, then nothing competing with it. Paragraphs are marked by space alone, and the headline sits close to the text it introduces.
- The empty space is gathered into one large open area to the right and below, not scattered in small gaps. If you add a timetable, that area could hold it without crowding the text.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, and whether the odd line breaks are hard-coded (they would get worse at other widths).
