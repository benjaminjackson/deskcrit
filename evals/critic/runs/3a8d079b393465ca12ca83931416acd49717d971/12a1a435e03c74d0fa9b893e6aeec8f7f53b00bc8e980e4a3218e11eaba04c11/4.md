**First read.** The eye goes straight to the three big numbers along the top, 3.8, 64.20 and 12.4, and then down to the orange bars, where Saturday is the tallest and Sunday is low. It reads as the admin home page of a small bakery's online shop. It feels calm, tidy and well made. Then you hit a problem: none of the three numbers says what it counts. 3.8 of what? 64.20 in which currency? And you can't have 12.4 subscribers.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the shop owner's overview, checked daily or weekly to see how the business is doing and to plan baking and delivery.

**Verdict.** The layout is good, but the page doesn't do its job yet. The three numbers it puts first have no units, no time period and nothing to compare them against, so the owner can see them but can't tell what they mean or whether they're good.

**What would move it most**
1. **The three headline numbers can't be read as they stand (defect).** They're in the cards in the top row.
   - "3.8" is probably a percent, but it could be a ratio.
   - "64.20" has no currency.
   - "12.4" under "Active subscribers" can't be a head count. It's probably 12,400 with the "k" dropped, but the viewer has to guess. A misread here leads to a wrong decision, and these are exactly the numbers an owner remembers and repeats.

   Fix: put the unit right on each value: "3.8%", "£64.20" (or whichever currency), and "12,400" or "12.4k". This is the first thing to fix, and it's a quick one.
2. **No number tells the owner whether things are good or bad (convention).** The cards show a value and nothing else: no last period, no target, no trend. The owner can't tell whether 3.8% is a good week or a slump, so the overview never gives them a reason to act. Few's "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design" (pitfall 2) names this exact gap. Fix: next to each number, add the time period it covers and one comparison, such as "▲ 0.4 pts vs August" or a small sparkline.
3. **The time period is only in the small print (defect).** The page doesn't say what period it covers. The only date is the grey source line under the chart ("September 2026"), so the owner has to assume the cards cover the same month. That also leaves no visible way to look at a different period. Fix: show the period once, near "Shop overview" (for example "September 2026 ▾"), and make it the control that changes the period.

**Smaller things.**
- **Chart title (taste).** "Orders by delivery day" names the topic, not the finding. Something like "Saturday is the busiest delivery day; Sunday has a third of its orders" says what the chart shows, so the owner gets it at a glance.

**What's working.**
- **The chart is honest and plain.** Bars start at zero, ticks are evenly spaced, there's only one colour, the light gridlines stay in the background, and the days run Monday to Sunday. Keep it this clean.
- **The type sizes are clear.** I count four levels: big numbers, page title, card and chart titles, and small labels. Each has an obvious job and no two sizes nearly collide.
- **Space and alignment are consistent.** The cards and the chart panel share edges and gaps, and the bold tab in the top menu shows clearly which page you're on.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the menu tabs.
- How the three cards rearrange at narrower widths.
