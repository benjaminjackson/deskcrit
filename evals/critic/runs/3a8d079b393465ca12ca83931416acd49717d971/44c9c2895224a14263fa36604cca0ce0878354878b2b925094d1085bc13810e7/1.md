**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, "Notes for ferry passengers", then drops into three calm, well-set serif paragraphs. It reads as a plain information page for people who take a small river ferry. It feels quiet and trustworthy, like a well-made leaflet. When you finish reading, you notice you still don't know when the next boat leaves.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page telling ferry passengers when and how to use the service, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** The typography is sound and I found no visual defect. The page still fails its job, because the copy never gives a time. A passenger can't plan a crossing from it, and it sends them to a printed timetable in a shelter at the landing, the one place they would need to already be.

**What would move it most**
1. **There are no times anywhere on the page, and the only timetable it mentions is physical** (defect, judged on the copy). "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" and "before dusk" are all a passenger has to plan with. None of them answers "can I catch a boat at 7:10?" or "what's the last boat home in January?". The last paragraph then says "printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing", so the answer is somewhere you have to travel to first. Times are what a passenger acts on and later holds the operator to. Being vague here makes people miss the boat. Fix: give the first and last departures from each landing, for summer and winter, with the dates the winter timetable starts and ends. Make the 40-minute pattern concrete ("from the north landing at :00 and :40…"). Mention the printed copy only as a backup.
2. **Two lines can be read two ways, and both concern when you can travel** (defect). "The last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" doesn't say which quay. The page talks about the "north landing" and the "market quay", so a reader at the north landing can't tell whether this means their side. "Before dusk" moves by more than an hour across the winter, so someone reading it as "around dusk" may arrive after the last boat. Fix: name the landing and give a clock time or a rule ("last boat from market quay 16:00, December to February"). Use one name for each stop every time it appears.
3. **The schedule is split in two, with bicycles in between** (taste). Paragraph 1 gives the summer pattern and paragraph 3 the winter one, so someone checking times reads past the bicycle rules to get the full picture. With only three paragraphs there's no reflow problem, but the page reads like a letter rather than something to scan. Fix: put all timing first, ideally as a small two-column summer/winter table, and give "Bicycles" its own short labelled section. A couple of plain subheads would also make the vague heading "Notes" less of a burden.

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- The type pairing does its job. The bold grotesque heading and the open, upright serif body differ clearly in form and weight, so there are exactly two levels and no confusion between them. Keep that when you add subheads: make them a third, clearly smaller step of the heading face, not a new size of the body face.
- The body setting is comfortable. Lines run about 66–70 characters at about 20 px with generous line spacing and a soft rag, so the paragraphs read easily. Keep this measure if the page gets wider.
- The restraint suits the subject. Near-black on white with no ornament feels like a public notice you can trust.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, and whether the right-hand white space is intended at this width or holds something that didn't load. Check this in a live browser.
