**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the big serif headline, "Hold a real conversation in Italian by summer", then drops to the orange actions. In version 1 it stops at three identical orange buttons and moves back and forth between them. In version 2 it goes straight to "Start your first lesson" and treats the other two as asides. The chat panel on the right reads as a real conversation you could be having. The page looks like a warm, calm language-learning product for adult beginners.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the hero of a landing page for Parla, an Italian course. Its job is to get a new visitor to start the first lesson.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the call-to-action row, and it fixes the one real defect in version 1: three actions shown as equally important. Apart from that row, the two versions are pixel-identical, and the rest of the page is strong.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1 gives all three actions top billing** (defect, plus convention). The buttons at y≈450 ("Start your first lesson", "See the course plan", "Gift a course") are the same filled orange, the same height and the same weight. A visitor who has just been told what they'll get has to choose between three things that look equally important, and the gift option, which almost nobody arriving here wants, gets as much pull as the real ask. Three orange blocks also triple the accent area and pull weight away from the headline. Material Design 3's "Buttons" guidance says a view has one high-emphasis action and the others use lower-emphasis styles, such as outlined or text buttons. That is exactly what version 2 does: one filled button, with "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" as underlined text links. The eye now has one next step, and the secondary links are still easy to find and clearly clickable. Keep version 2's treatment.

**Smaller things** (these apply to both versions):
- Chat panel, lower third (y≈410–555): about 150 px of empty beige under the last message. It looks like a conversation that stopped loading. Either fill it (a fourth message, or a reply field that shows *you* answer) or shorten the panel to fit the messages. This is common sense, not a sourced rule.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a concrete promise with a deadline ("by summer"). The subline says exactly how: fifteen minutes a day, a weekly video call, a teacher in Bologna who corrects you as you speak. Specific copy like this sets the product apart from generic "learn a language" pages.
- The chat panel shows the outcome rather than describing it. It works with the headline instead of repeating it, and the reply bubble in your colour lets the viewer picture themselves in it.
- "Start your first lesson" is a good ask: one exact, low-commitment action that lets the viewer judge the product for themselves.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The hit areas of the text links in version 2 and of the dot-separated nav items (WCAG 2.5.8 asks for 24 px). Check them in the browser. It's also worth checking whether the chat panel animates in, which would explain the empty space.
