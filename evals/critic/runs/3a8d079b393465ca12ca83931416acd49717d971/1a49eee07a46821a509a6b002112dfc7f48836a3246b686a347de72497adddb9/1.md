**First read.** The eye lands first on the large dark block at the top, finds only a grey head-and-shoulders silhouette there, and drops to the bold headline: "An evening with Ines Varga on her new novel, The Salt Year." From there it goes down to the black "Book a free seat" button. It reads as a single event page for an independent bookshop. It feels calm, competent and fairly anonymous, and the dead image makes it feel unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an event page for a local bookshop. Its job is to get readers who already know the shop, or the author, to book a free seat for a reading.

**Verdict.** The type, copy and button do their job. The page fails at the top, though: the largest and heaviest thing on it is a stock avatar placeholder, so the first thing a visitor sees says nobody has finished the page, and there's no author, book or place to look at.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image is a placeholder** (defect). The 600×330 block takes about 40% of the content area and holds a generic person icon in brown on near-black. Because it's the darkest, largest shape on the page, the eye goes to it first and gets nothing. It doesn't show who Ines Varga is, what the book looks like, or what the evening will feel like. It also adds a heavy, sombre tone to an invitation that mentions wine and an island. The silhouette barely separates from its background either, so even as a placeholder it reads as a dark smudge. If the real photo failed to load, this isn't a design fault. As shipped, though, it's the headline problem. Fix: put in a real author portrait or the book's cover. Ideally use something that ties to the island setting the copy mentions, so the picture and the headline make one invitation. If no picture is available, remove the block and let the headline lead. The type is strong enough to carry the page alone. Arguing this as common sense: an image that could be removed with no loss is a fault, and this one costs the first second of attention.

**Smaller things.**
- Date, top of the text block: "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. 13 November was a Thursday in 2025 but is a Friday in 2026, so the weekday or the date is wrong for this year, or the event has already passed. Dates are what people act on, so check the date and give the year or the next correct date.
- Navigation, top right: "Events" looks the same as the other links, so the page doesn't show where you are. Mark the current page with one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Body copy: the page never says where the event happens or when it ends. "From the floor" suggests the shop, but a visitor deciding whether to come shouldn't have to click "Find us" to find out. Add "at Margin Books, [street]" next to the date, and say whether seats are limited. That also makes "Book a free seat" more urgent without inventing pressure.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and human. "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor. Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm" tells you exactly what the evening is. "Book a free seat" names one exact action and removes the cost question in three words. Keep both.
- The type hierarchy is clean: a small rust-coloured bold date, a big bold two-line headline set tight, a quiet grey paragraph, and one solid dark button. That's four clear levels, and colour does the job a fifth size would have done.
- The single narrow column, left-aligned to the image edge, with generous empty margins. Nothing competes with the one action.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the hero image is a real photo that failed to load in this capture. Check it in a live browser before treating finding 1 as a design decision.
