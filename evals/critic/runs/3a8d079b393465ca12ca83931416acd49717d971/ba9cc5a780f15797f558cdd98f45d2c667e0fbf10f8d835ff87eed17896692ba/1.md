It reads as a plan of the stages of a design project, probably an internal doc page for a team. It feels tidy but heavy. **What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an internal page that shows a team the stages of a project, in order, so they know what happens and when.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: it's clear, readable and well spaced, with a sensible three-level type scale. Two things hold it back. The heavy borders out-shout the content, and the page never says that these five things happen in order, which is the main thing it exists to tell you.

**What would move it most**
1. **The borders are louder than the text (convention).** Each of the five cards has a near-black outline about 3 px thick. That makes it darker and heavier than the 16 px body text inside it. The eye reads five frames first and the words second, and the column looks like a stack of form fields or five "selected" items rather than a calm list. Lupton warns about this in *Thinking with Type*: rules and boxes should "illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell." Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 4–5, makes the same point with ink that carries no information. Fix: remove the borders, or replace them with a pale tint or a hairline in light grey. The outlines do all the grouping now, so if you remove them, widen the gap between stages (now about 18 px, smaller than the padding inside each card) until it is clearly bigger than the space between a stage name and its line.
2. **Nothing shows the sequence (defect).** The copy describes a process: Kickoff leads to Research, then Sketches, then Review, then Handover. But the five boxes are visually identical and fully separate, so they read as five equal, unordered notes. A viewer has to infer the order from the words, can't tell where the project stands now, and can't see when anything happens. The title "Project notes" makes this worse, because it names a kind of document, not a plan. Fix: make the order visible. Number the stages (1–5) or join them with a vertical line, and if this is a live plan, mark the current stage and add a date or owner to each. Then retitle the page to say what it is, for example "Project stages" or "How this project runs". This is common sense: no approved source states it for this case.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing. The page is simple enough that the two points above cover it.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale.** There are three sizes with clear steps (title about 34 px, stage names about 20 px bold, body about 16 px regular), and weight separates the names from the lines below them. Each level has an obvious job. Keep it.
- **The copy.** Every line names a concrete action with a verb and an object: "Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need", "Pick one direction and list the open questions". A stranger knows exactly what each stage involves. Don't trim these into labels.
- **The line length.** Lines are about 55 characters, with one shared left edge and generous margins. It's easy to scan.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable. If they are, they need a visible clickable cue that doesn't depend on the heavy border, plus hover and focus states. If they aren't, the button-like outline is one more reason to drop it.
