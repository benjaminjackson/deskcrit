I compared the two screens. The only change is the paragraph under the greeting: version 1 sets it all in capitals, version 2 in normal upper and lower case. Everything else is identical, so the verdict turns on that one change.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Good morning, Dana" in the bold serif, then drops to the paragraph. In version 1 the four-line all-caps paragraph is a dense grey block that reads like terms and conditions. You slow down there before you reach the two classes and the dark "Book a class" button. In version 2 the same paragraph reads as a friendly note, and the eye moves on to the class list without effort. Both feel calm and warm, like a boutique studio app.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home screen of a yoga studio's member app, telling a pass holder what's left on their pass and getting them to book one of today's classes.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The paragraph carries the rules a member will be held to (six sessions left, valid until 30 November, book at least two hours ahead, cancel by 8 pm the night before), and version 2 makes it quick to read where version 1 makes it a chore. In both versions the biggest remaining problem is the empty hero box, which takes a third of the screen and shows nothing.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Version 1 sets a four-line paragraph in all capitals** (convention, and a defect given what the text says). Capitals have no ascenders or descenders, so every word becomes the same rectangle and the reader has to spell their way through. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says don't capitalise whole paragraphs. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls text in capitals particularly difficult to read. This paragraph holds the deadline and the cancellation rule, which are exactly what a member later gets charged for missing. Making it the hardest text on the screen to read is the wrong way round. The caps also make the paragraph look louder than the class names below it, which blurs the order of the levels. Version 2 fixes both by setting it in upper and lower case.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **The hero image is an empty beige gradient** (defect). It runs from 64 to 274 px, about a third of the first screen. It shows no photo, no studio and no teacher, and it pushes the classes and the button down. If a photo failed to load in this capture, this is fine. If not, either put a real picture there that says something (the studio, today's teacher) or remove the box and move the class list up. Common sense; no approved source.
2. **The class list doesn't say when it is or connect to the button** (defect, common sense). "Slow flow 7:00 am" and "Mobility 12:15 pm" have no heading or date, so the viewer has to guess they mean today. "Book a class" under two specific classes doesn't say which one it books, so the viewer has to work out whether to tap a row or the button. Add a heading such as "Today, Mon 23 Nov", and make the action name the class, for example a "Book" control on each row or a button that books the selected class.

**Smaller things.**
- "Book a class" is copy that could sit on any studio's app. A version tied to the list ("Book Mobility, 12:15") gives the viewer one exact thing to do.

**What's working.**
- The serif greeting over a plain sans for everything else gives one clear starting point and a warm, personal tone. Keep it.
- The class rows put the name in bold, the time and room underneath, and the spaces on the right, separated by hairline rules. They scan in a second.
- The single dark pill button is clearly the primary action and sits low enough to reach with a thumb.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the hero is a failed image load. Check it in a live build.
- Whether the class rows can be tapped.
