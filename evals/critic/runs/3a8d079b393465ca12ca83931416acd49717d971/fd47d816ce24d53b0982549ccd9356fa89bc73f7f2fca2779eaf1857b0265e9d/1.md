**First read.** In version 1 the eye hits the five black-outlined boxes before it reads a word. The page looks like a wireframe, and "Your account" has to compete with the frames. In version 2 the eye lands on "Your account", moves to the four bold card headings, and reads down the content. It's the same account overview, but it feels calm and finished rather than blocked out.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the account home of a small online shop, where a returning customer checks their details, last order, address and card, and jumps to edit one of them.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the borders: version 1 draws every card and the nav panel in a near-black 2 px outline, version 2 uses a hairline pale-grey edge. With the heavy outlines gone, the content is the strongest thing on the page again. Version 2 is a competent, plain account page. The main thing left to fix is the fixed-height cards, which leave a lot of dead white space.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1's heavy borders (the change that decides this comparison).** All five boxes are outlined in a dark rule about as heavy as the headings. The frames become the pattern the eye reads first, the page looks like a grid of compartments, and the 15 px content inside feels fenced in. Lupton, *Thinking with Type*: rules and boxes "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell"; Tufte calls this kind of ink non-data ink. Version 2 makes exactly the approved fix: it replaces the borders with a pale edge and lets white-on-off-white do the grouping. Keep version 2's treatment. *Convention.*
2. **Fixed-height cards leave the bottom third to half of each card empty (both versions).** "Payment method" ends at about y 550 in a card that runs to 680, and the nav panel is hollow below "Sign out". This space isn't resting room. It's leftover space, and it pushes the second row lower than it needs to be, so the page looks emptier than it is. Let the cards fit their content (equal height within a row is fine), and let the nav panel end after its last item or drop the box altogether. *Taste, argued from Ruder: empty space should be gathered and shaped, not left over.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Sign out" sits in the list as an ordinary nav item. A small gap or a divider above it would set the session action apart from the page links (taste).
- The current nav item ("Account") is marked only by bold. That's enough, since the page title confirms it, but a background tint would make it easier to spot (taste).

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete and needs no decoding: "Order 48213, placed 12 October / Two linen pillow cases / Delivered 15 October" tells the customer everything at a glance. Every link names its action ("Change card", "Manage addresses").
- The type is cleanly limited: a bold title, bold card headings, regular body text and blue links. Each level is told apart by weight and size, not by many near-identical sizes.
- One grid: two equal columns with equal gutters, the card text inset the same in every card, and the nav and title tops aligned.
