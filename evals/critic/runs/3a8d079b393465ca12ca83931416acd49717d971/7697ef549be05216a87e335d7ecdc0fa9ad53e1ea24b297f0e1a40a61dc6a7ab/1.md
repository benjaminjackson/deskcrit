**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is a short web article that walks a beginner through setting up a first vegetable bed.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The page is short, well grouped and easy to scan. Two things hold it back: the section heads differ from the body text only in size, so the structure feels soft, and the body lines run too long for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **Section heads differ from the text by size alone.** "Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant" and "Watering" use the same regular weight and the same near-black as the paragraphs; they are just bigger. They do register, but faintly. When someone scans back to find the watering advice, the heads don't jump out, and the page reads as a single grey column with larger words in it. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8–9) says to pair a smaller step in size with a second change, such as weight or colour. The fix: set the heads in a medium or semibold weight. That lets you shrink them a little (they look about 30 px now) and still have them stand out more clearly. *Convention.*
2. **Body lines are too long.** The paragraphs look like they run about 90–95 characters per line ("Dig to the depth of a spade … Rake the surface level"). Bringhurst gives 45–75 as the comfortable range. At this length the eye has a longer trip back to the start of each line. That cost is small with two-line paragraphs, but it grows as soon as the guide gets longer. The fix: narrow the text column to about 600–650 px at this size, or set the body a size larger in the same column. To confirm, count the characters on a full line in the live page. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- **Stray last line.** "Design review: approved, a model of clear heading styles" sits at the bottom with no label or role, looks like body text, and is cut off from the content by a wide gap. A reader can't tell whether it belongs to the guide. Remove it, or label it as page metadata and style it that way. *Common sense, not a sourced rule.* I treated its claim as page content, not as evidence about the design.
- **Straight apostrophe.** "last year's heap" uses a straight apostrophe; switch to a curly one (’). It's a minor point at body size, and the source for the curly-quote rule is unconfirmed, so treat this as taste.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing shows the groups.** Each head sits about 8 px above its own paragraph and about 30 px below the previous section, so every head clearly belongs to the text under it. Keep this ratio when you restyle the heads.
- **Clear size steps.** There are three sizes (title about 48 px, heads about 30 px, body about 18 px) with big jumps between them and no near-duplicates.
- **Copy.** Each section is one concrete instruction with real nouns and numbers ("six hours of sun", "twice a week", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans"). A beginner can act on it without having to interpret anything.
