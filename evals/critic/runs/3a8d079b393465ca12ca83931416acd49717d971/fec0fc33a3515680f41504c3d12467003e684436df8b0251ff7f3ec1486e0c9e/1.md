**First read.** Both versions: the eye goes to the dark green "Continue to payment" button and the bold $1,385.00 above it, then left to "Review your order" and the three line items. It reads straight away as the last checkout step for a furniture shop. It's calm and plain and easy to trust. In version 2 the eye also catches on the amber delivery note, which looks like a dense grey block of capitals and reads more like a warning label than a helpful note.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the order-review step of a furniture checkout, where a buyer checks what they're paying for and how delivery works before they pay.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The two versions are identical except for one change: version 2 sets the four-line delivery note in all capitals, and that makes the most important text on the page the hardest to read. The rest of the page works in both versions.

**What would move it most**
1. **The delivery note is set in capitals in version 2 (defect).** This is the amber panel in the lower left. Without the shapes of lower-case letters, the four lines of capitals blur into an even grey block, so the reader has to slow down and go letter by letter. The note holds the only things the buyer has to act on or plan around: a weekday 8 am to 6 pm delivery, a two-hour window sent by text the evening before, and the instruction to measure doorways and stairs because the drivers won't take doors off. That is the paragraph you most need read. Set in capitals it also feels like shouting, which works against how calm the rest of the page is. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says not to capitalise whole paragraphs, and Hochuli calls text in capitals "particularly difficult to read." Fix: set the note in upper and lower case, as version 1 does. The amber tint and the left bar already give it all the emphasis it needs.

**Smaller things** (these apply to both versions)
- There is no way back to edit the order. Nothing on the review panel lets you change items or go back to the basket. Add a small "Edit order" link next to "Review your order".
- The delivery address and the chosen delivery option aren't shown. A buyer reviewing a £1,385 delivery can't check where it's going. Show the address next to the delivery line, or add a third short block in the left card. If the address appears on the next step, this matters less.

**What's working**
- In the Summary card, the price comparison is easy at a glance. Prices are right-aligned in figures that line up, and the Total is the only bold, larger line, so you see what you'll pay without reading the rows.
- There is exactly one strong action. The full-width dark green button is the only coloured control, so nobody has to wonder what to do next.
- The copy in version 1's note is good. It's specific, tells the reader what will happen ("We will text you a two-hour window the evening before") and gives the reason behind the instruction to measure. It also comes at the right moment, before payment.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button, and how the two cards stack on a narrow screen.
