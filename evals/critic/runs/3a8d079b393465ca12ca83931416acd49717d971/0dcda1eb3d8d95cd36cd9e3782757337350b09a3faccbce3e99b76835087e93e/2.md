**First read.** The bold title "River ferry timetable" lands first, then the eye drops into a wide, evenly ruled table and moves down the Leaves column. It reads as a clean, generic web table: calm and legible, but every cell has the same weight. The restriction in the far-right "Runs" column is the last thing you see, and it's the one that decides whether a boat is any use to you.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since none was given: a public timetable people check to catch the north-landing ferry to market quay on a given day, on a screen or printed at the landing.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and mostly correct, but it fails the rider in the one place that matters. Which days a sailing runs is the last column you read, and it's set exactly like everything else, so someone checking on a Saturday can pick the 12:40 or 17:10 and miss the boat.

**What would move it most**
1. **The day restrictions are hidden where the eye arrives last.** "Weekdays" and "Weekends" sit at the far right of rows 4, 6 and 7, in the same regular weight as "Daily". A rider looks for a time, finds 12:40 under Leaves, and may never travel 600 px to the right to learn it doesn't run today. **Defect**: it leads to missed boats, and an exception set in the same weight as "Daily" is easy to read past. Direction: make the day you're travelling the first thing you choose, not the last thing you read. The usual fix is two tables or two column groups, "Weekdays" and "Weekends", each listing only the boats that run. At the very least, mark the exceptions so they stand out from "Daily" (weight, a symbol, or a footnote) and move that column next to the times.
2. **The table is stretched to the full page width, so each row comes apart.** Four short columns are spread over about 1080 px. There's roughly 300 px between a departure and its arrival, and the gap between columns is about five times the row height. Proximity says rows should hold together and columns sit apart; here the columns are the far-apart groups, and on rows that look alike the eye can slip from 12:40 to the wrong arrival line. **Convention** (Arnheim on grouping by proximity). Direction: size the table to its content so Leaves and Arrives sit close together and each row reads as one trip. The leftover space can stay empty.
3. **The times use proportional figures, so the colons don't stack.** Down the Leaves column, "11:00" and "17:10" are visibly narrower than "06:40" and "19:30", so the colons and minutes step left and right. Scanning down a column of times is the whole task here. **Convention** (Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* 4.4.3: columns of figures use tabular figures). Direction: turn on the face's tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) for both time columns.

**Smaller things.**
- The Period column (Morning, Midday…) repeats what the hours already say and takes a quarter of the width. Drop it, or turn it into small group labels between the row bands (taste).
- The copy leaves gaps a stranger has to fill. "Spring season" gives no dates. Riders act on dates, so say "Valid 1 March to 31 May" or the real dates. "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" have lost their nouns; "Leaves North Landing" and "Arrives Market Quay" read like the place names they are.
- The return trip, quay back to north, isn't shown, and no line says where to find it. If this is the only timetable at the landing, people will ask.

**What's working.**
- The rules are pale and thin, with one slightly heavier rule under the header. The table is ruled lightly enough that the lines never compete with the text. Keep that restraint.
- There's one typeface with a clear three-step scale (title, bold column heads, regular body), and the grey subtitle is still readable. There's nothing to fix in the type system itself.
- The 24-hour times are consistent and unambiguous, with no am/pm to misread.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the table reflows on a phone, where people at a landing will mostly read it. Four spread-out columns are likely to scroll sideways or wrap, so check at 375 px wide.
