**First read.** The eye goes first to the white serif "How can we help?" on the navy band, drops to the white search field, then spreads across the eight topic cards. It reads straight away as a help center for a software product. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also so close to the standard help-center template that nothing about it says "Harbor".

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the landing page of a product's help center: a customer arrives with a problem and should reach the right article fast, mainly by searching and otherwise by topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The search is obvious, the topics scan in seconds, and the layout is disciplined. The biggest problem is in the words, not the layout: the topic list describes two different products, so a first-time visitor can't tell what Harbor is or which half of the help center applies to them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The topics don't agree on what Harbor sells** (defect). Six cards describe workspace software: "Set up your workspace and invite your team", "Plans", "Connect calendars, chat tools, and storage", "sync problems". But "Orders and shipping: Track a delivery or start a return" describes a shop that ships physical goods. A stranger reading the grid has to stop and work out whether Harbor is a SaaS tool, a store, or both. Someone with a parcel question may doubt they're in the right help center at all, and a workspace user wonders whether "Orders" is about their subscription. The grouping and names should show what the product is and how its parts fit together (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, ch. 2, conceptual model). The fix depends on which is true:
   - If Harbor does both (hardware plus an app, say), make that explicit. Split the grid into two labelled groups, for example "Using Harbor" and "Your orders", or rewrite the shipping card so it names what is shipped.
   - If shipping is a template leftover, replace the card.

**Smaller things.**
- **Two-pixel steps between headings:** "Browse by topic" (about 18 px) and the card titles (about 16 px) are the same weight and colour, so the section heading barely reads as a level above them. Make it clearly bigger, or set it apart another way, such as a lighter colour or small capitals (Bringhurst, 3.1.1, on sizes from a scale).
- **Lone words on last lines:** four card descriptions end with one word alone: "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages." (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Widen the text box slightly or rewrite those lines so the last line carries two or more words.
- **Search field has only a placeholder:** "Search for articles" disappears as soon as someone types, and there's no magnifier icon or submit button. Add a magnifier inside the field as a permanent signal, and a visible or accessible label (NN/g, Sherwin, "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful").
- **Generic headline (taste):** "How can we help? / Browse a topic or search the guides." could sit on any company's help center. That convention helps people recognise the page, so it's fine to keep. But nothing on the page, apart from the name in the header, tells the visitor whose help this is. A subline that names the product would anchor them.

**What's working.**
- **Disciplined grid.** The cards' outer edges line up with the logo on the left and the "Contact us" link on the right, and the gutters are even. Keep that alignment when you add sections.
- **One clear starting point.** The navy band with a serif headline over a sans-serif page gives the eye a single place to start, then hands it straight to the search field. Keep the serif for that one headline only.
- **Matched icons and good copy.** All eight icons share one stroke weight and style. Most descriptions name concrete tasks ("Passwords, two-step sign-in, and recovery", "Track a delivery or start a return"), which is exactly how people scan a help center.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the search field and cards show a visible focus ring and a hover state.
- What sits below the fold. A popular-articles list or a "still stuck? contact us" path would matter here, and the screenshot ends on an empty grey band.
