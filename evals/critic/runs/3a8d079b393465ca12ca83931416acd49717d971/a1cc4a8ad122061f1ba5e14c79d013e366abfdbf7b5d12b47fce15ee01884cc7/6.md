**First read.** The eye goes straight to "How can we help?" in white serif on the navy band, drops to the white search bar, then moves to the eight topic cards below. It reads at once as a product's help centre: calm, tidy and trustworthy. It also looks like every other help centre. Nothing on it is specific to Harbor or to the problem someone came with.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page of a product help centre, for an existing customer who has a problem and wants an answer fast, mostly on desktop.

**Verdict.** It works. The structure is clear, the hierarchy is right and the craft is clean. What holds it back is that the page only routes. It sends every visitor to search or to a category and never shows an answer, so the person with the most common problem still needs two or three clicks, and the page could belong to any product.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page offers no answers, only directions** (taste / common sense; the usual source for this point is not on the approved list). Below the hero there are eight categories and nothing else: no popular articles, no top issues and no suggestions under the search bar. Most visits to a help centre are about the same few problems, such as a sign-in failure, an invoice download or a sync error. Those visitors have to guess which of eight cards holds their issue. "Troubleshooting" and "Account and sign-in" overlap, for one. Then they have to scan a list on the next page. The copy adds to this. "How can we help?" and "Browse a topic or search the guides." could sit on any help centre, and the second line only describes the two things already in view. Fix: put the five or so most-read articles, worded as the customer's problem ("Reset your password", "Download an invoice"), either as suggestions under the search bar or as a short list beside or above the grid. Use the subline to say something only Harbor can say, or cut it.
2. **The search bar, the main action, is a blank white box** (convention: Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on signifiers; NN/g on placeholder-only labels). It has no magnifier, no submit button and no persistent label. The only cue is the grey placeholder "Search for articles", and it disappears as soon as someone starts typing. It still reads as a field because it is white on navy, so this is not fatal. But search is the first thing most help-centre users reach for, and here it is the weakest-signalled control on the page. Fix: add a search icon inside the left edge, and a visible button or an "Enter" hint on the right. Keep a real accessible label; the headline can serve as that label if it is properly tied to the field.

**Smaller things**
- Card descriptions: six of the eight end on a short last line of one to three words ("recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages.", "your team."). That's a run of short last lines (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rewrite each to fit one line or fill two, or set a slightly narrower measure so they break more evenly.
- "Browse by topic" is almost the same as the card titles under it: it looks about 18 px bold against about 16 px bold, in the same colour. So it reads as one more label rather than the heading of the section. Make it clearly bigger, or set it apart by colour or case, or drop it, since the grid explains itself.

**What's working**
- The hero does one job. A single dominant headline, the contrast between the serif headline and the sans text, and a search bar centred on the same axis make it obvious where to start. Keep the serif for this one line only.
- The card grid is disciplined. It uses four equal columns on the same margins as the header, the same gutters across and down, one consistent line-icon set, and a bold title plus a single grey sentence in each card. The group spacing is right: the icon sits well above the title and the title sits tight to its description.
- "Status" and "Contact us" stay visible in the header, so a customer whose problem the guides can't fix has a way out without searching for it.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the search box shows a strong focus ring on the navy band, and whether it suggests results as the user types.
- How the four-column grid reflows on narrow screens.
- The placeholder grey on white looks close to 4.5:1. Measure it.
