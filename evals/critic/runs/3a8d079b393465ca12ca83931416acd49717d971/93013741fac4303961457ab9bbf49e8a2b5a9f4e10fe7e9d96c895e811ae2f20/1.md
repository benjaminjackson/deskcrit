**First read.** Both versions show the same thing: a bold serif headline, "Throwing on the wheel for beginners", then two paragraphs, then a tan box with the price and a rust "Book a place" button. In version 1 the eye drops straight down one left edge, from the logo to the headline to the price to the button, and the whole read takes seconds. In version 2 the headline still lands first, but every line of the paragraphs starts somewhere different. Reading it feels slower and more formal, and the logo sits on its own at the far left.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the booking page for a beginner pottery evening course at a small studio. Its job is to tell adult beginners what they'll learn, what it costs and when it runs, and to get them to book a place.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change in version 2 is that everything is centred, and that makes the two description paragraphs (the part that sells the course) harder to read while gaining nothing. Version 1 is clean and complete, and nothing in it stops someone from booking.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2 centres paragraphs of several lines** (defect, and a convention). The first paragraph runs four lines and the second runs two. With both edges ragged, the eye has to hunt for the start of each new line. The four-line paragraph is the one that explains what you'll actually do (centre, open, pull walls, make mugs and bowls), so it's the one that most needs an easy read. Its last line, "at your own wheel.", also hangs alone in the middle and looks like a caption. Tschichold (*The New Typography*) and Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3) both keep centring for a few short lines. Fix: set the paragraphs flush left, as version 1 does.
2. **Version 2 puts everything on a centre axis out of habit, and the logo is left off it** (convention). The eyebrow, headline, body, box, button and even the details inside the box are all centred. Nothing in the content asks for a formal, symmetrical layout. Meanwhile "Kiln Yard Studio" stays at the left margin, so the page has two unrelated alignments and the header looks like it belongs to another page. Tschichold calls central-axis setting the rigid default; Müller-Brockmann asks that every element share an edge or axis with another. Version 1 solves both at once: every block hangs from the logo's left edge at about x=88, so one line runs through the whole page and leads the eye to the button. Fix: go back to the single left edge.

Everything else is the same in both versions. Apart from the centring, version 1 has no real failure on the checklist.

**Smaller things.** None in version 1. (Taste: at this width the right ~45% of version 1 is empty. It reads as a deliberate open field next to a comfortable reading column, so I'd keep it. A photo of a thrown pot there would add warmth, but the page doesn't need one to work.)

**What's working** (keep these in version 1)
- **One left edge from the logo down to the button.** It ties the page together and carries the eye straight to the booking action.
- **Clear type scale and pairing.** A bold serif headline, serif body text at a comfortable ~70 characters per line, and small sans labels. The rust eyebrow and button are the only accent colour, so the button is clearly the one thing to do.
- **The copy speaks to the reader.** "You will learn to centre clay…" and "You take home everything you make" say what the reader gets. The tan box answers price, time, start date and class size before anyone has to ask, and "Book a place" names exactly what clicking does.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the centred version reflows on a phone. Narrow screens might make the centring matter less, but that's a guess.
