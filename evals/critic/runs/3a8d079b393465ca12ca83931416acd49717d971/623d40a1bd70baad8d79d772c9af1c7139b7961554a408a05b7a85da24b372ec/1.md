**First read.** My eye has nowhere to land. Every line is the same grey, the same regular weight and about the same size, so I start at the top-left and read straight down like a plain text file. The headings come through only because they're short lines. It reads as the order-help or FAQ page for a small bakery: calm and tidy, but flat. Near the bottom, the line "Each heading here stands out clearly, so the page is easy to scan" claims the opposite of what I just saw.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help page that lets a bakery customer quickly find the one answer they came for (pickup time, someone else collecting, allergies, cancelling) and act on it correctly.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job as a page people scan. The headings look exactly like the body text, so finding your question means reading everything, and two of the answers that commit the customer to something can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings don't look like headings.** Defect. "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients" and "Cancelling an order" are set in the same regular weight and colour as the answers. They even look a pixel or so *smaller* than the body (~16 px against ~17 px). Someone who came with one question can't pick it out at a glance. They have to read each block's first line to know where they are, which is the reading a help page is meant to save them. A one-step difference looks like an accident rather than a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8 and 8a–8g). Fix: make each heading differ from its answer in a way you notice instantly, most simply a clearly heavier weight, with or without a real step up in size. The page also has no title: no bakery name and no "Your order: questions". So nothing is seen first and nothing tells a new visitor where they are (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order by differences of size). Put one dominant title above the four questions.
2. **Two answers the customer will act on can be read two ways.** Defect. "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" doesn't say a day ahead of what, or how that's counted: 24 hours before the pickup slot, or by some cutoff the day before? A customer who reads it the generous way and cancels the morning of pickup day will be told no, and they'll feel misled, because the bakery has to stand by this line. "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name" doesn't say whose name: the collector's or the person who ordered? A friend who brings the wrong one gets held up at the till. Fix: state the cutoff exactly ("Cancel any time up to 24 hours before your pickup slot") and name the name ("along with your name, as it appears on the order").
3. **The last line has no business being on the page.** Defect (common sense). "Each heading here stands out clearly, so the page is easy to scan" speaks about the page, not to the customer. It reads like a leftover note or placeholder, and it's false: see finding 1. Remove it. If anything goes in that spot, make it a way to reach the bakery for questions the page doesn't answer.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: "Give the person your order number…" and "Orders can be cancelled…" look like about 95–100 characters per line, well past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column to roughly 600–650 px.
- Runt: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer. Rewrap it, or let the narrower column fix it.
- Allergies copy (taste): "a card by the till" only helps someone already in the shop. A customer ordering online can't check before they buy. Say where they can see the ingredients before ordering.

**What's working.**
- The space between blocks is clearly bigger than the space between a heading and its answer, so the four questions already sit as four clear groups. Keep that ratio when you add weight to the headings.
- The answers are short, plain, and in the customer's words ("choose a new slot from the list", "the bread has already been baked for you"). The cancellation line gives the reason, which makes a refusal easier to accept.
- Dark grey text on white in one plain sans, set flush left with comfortable line spacing, reads easily. The page needs no more colour or ornament.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether these blocks expand or collapse, or link anywhere ("Open your order" suggests there's a place to go). If "your order" can be a link, it should be.
