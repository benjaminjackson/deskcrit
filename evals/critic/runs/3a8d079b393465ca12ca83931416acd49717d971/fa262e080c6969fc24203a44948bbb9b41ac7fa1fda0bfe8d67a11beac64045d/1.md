**First read.** Both versions hit the same way: the eye lands on "Change the card you pay with", reads the one-line answer under it, and then scans down. In version 1 the scan has three bold stops: add, make default, declined. In version 2 the stops are gone. After the title, the page reads as six lines of plain text the same size, and you have to read every line to find the part you came for. Both feel calm and trustworthy, like a plain help article.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help-centre article for a customer who wants to swap their payment card, or whose card was declined. Most will scan for their own case rather than read top to bottom.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change is the three subheads. Version 1 sets them bold and a step larger. Version 2 sets them regular at about body size, so they stop working as headings. That matters most for someone who came for "If the card is declined" and now has to read the whole page to find it. Version 1 itself is a clean, working article with nothing serious in the way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The subheads in version 2 have become body text (defect).** "Add a new card", "Make it the default" and "If the card is declined" look the same size, weight and colour as the paragraphs under them. Each one reads as a short first line of its paragraph, not as a label for it. A reader scanning for their problem has no anchors, and the page loses its three-part structure at a glance. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8) says the difference between a heading and its text must be obvious in at least one kind of contrast, and here there is none. Nielsen ("How Users Read on the Web") notes that help readers skim. Fix: make each heading clearly different from its text, in size, weight, or colour, not by one step. Version 1 already does this with bold at a step up, so keep that. Nothing else changed between the two versions, so nothing in version 2 makes up for this.

For version 1, nothing else rises to the ranked list.

**Smaller things** (version 1)
- The UI names in the steps (Settings, Billing, Add card, Use for payments, Save) are set in plain body text, so they blur into the sentence. A reader following along with the app open has to pick them out by reading. Setting them in bold or another clear treatment would make each step easy to match to the screen. This is common sense, not a catalogued rule.

**What's working** (version 1)
- The title is a task in the reader's own words ("Change the card you pay with"). The one-line answer under it settles the two worries people have before they act: you can change it any time, and nothing is charged when you change it. Keep that lead.
- The headings sit close to their own text, with clearly more space above them than below, so each heading and paragraph reads as one group. Version 2 keeps this spacing, but without the weight it isn't enough to carry the structure.
- The reading column is narrow and the lines look to run about 70–80 characters with comfortable line spacing. The copy is concrete: it names each control and ends with a real next step ("call the number on the back of the card").
