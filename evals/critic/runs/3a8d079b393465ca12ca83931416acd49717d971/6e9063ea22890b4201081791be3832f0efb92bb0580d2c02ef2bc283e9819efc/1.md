**First read.** The eye goes to "Get the weekly show list", then down to the purple Subscribe bar. It reads as a newsletter sign-up for a local radio station: calm, tidy and clear. The two versions differ in one thing only, the corners. In version 1 the field and buttons are softly rounded like the card around them. In version 2 the buttons are almost square and the field is only slightly rounded, inside a card that is still soft.

**What it's for.** My assumption: getting listeners of Riverside FM to sign up for a weekly email of the schedule, with a way to see a sample first.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job. The headline, the one-line promise, the single field and one primary button make the task obvious. Version 1 is slightly better because its corners match. In version 2, three different corner sizes sit inside one small card, so the parts look like they came from different kits.

**What would move it most**
1. **The corners in version 2 don't match (taste / common sense).** The card has about 8 px rounding, the email field about 4 px, and Subscribe and "Read last week's list" about 2 px. On a card this small, with every control stacked on the same left and right edges, the mismatch is easy to see. The buttons look sharper and harder than the box they sit in, and the field doesn't match either of them. The viewer won't name it, but the form feels slightly less put-together, and a sign-up form depends on looking trustworthy. Version 1 uses about 6 px on the field and both buttons inside an 8 px card, so it reads as one family. Fix for version 2: give all controls one radius, either the softer one from version 1 or square corners on the card as well. Pick one rule and apply it throughout. No approved source covers this. My argument: a shared shape is the cheapest way to make a few parts read as one whole.

**Smaller things.**
- In both versions, the borders of the email field and the "Read last week's list" button look like pale grey on white, likely well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on component edges. The field's edge is the main cue that you can type there. Darken both borders until they reach 3:1, and measure them to confirm.

**What's working.**
- There is one clear primary action. Subscribe is the only filled, coloured button, and the secondary "Read last week's list" is an outline at the same width. The order of actions is obvious, so keep it.
- The copy is concrete. "Every Friday we send the next week's programmes, guest names, and any changes to the schedule" says when it arrives, what's in it and why it's useful. The secondary button lets the viewer judge a real issue before giving their email, which is the strongest kind of ask.
- The spacing is clean. There is a visible label above the field (not only a placeholder), everything shares one left edge, and the gaps make clear groups: station and headline, promise, then the form.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus and hover states on the field and buttons, and the error message for a mistyped email.
