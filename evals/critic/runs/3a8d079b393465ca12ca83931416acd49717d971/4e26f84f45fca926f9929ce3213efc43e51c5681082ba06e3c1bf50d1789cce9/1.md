**First read.** The eye goes to "Create your account", drops down the stack of fields, and stops on the dark blue "Create account" button. It's a signup page for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous. Apart from the wordmark, nothing on it belongs to this product.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop signup page that gets a new user into a free notes workspace with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The visual craft is sound: clear hierarchy, one primary action, even spacing. Where it falls short is the form itself. It asks six questions where three would do, and it gives no guidance on any of them, so the page does more to block the signup than to invite it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for too much before the user gets any value.** Last name, workspace name and "How did you hear about us?" all sit in front of a free notes app. Each one is another field to fill before the user writes a single note. The attribution question serves the company, not the user. The workspace name asks the user to decide something they can't judge yet. This is a defect: every extra required-looking field costs signups (Nielsen, heuristic 8, aesthetic and minimalist design; Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). Cut the form to email and password, plus a name if you need one. Ask for the workspace name after signup with a sensible default, and move the attribution question to the onboarding flow or make it clearly optional.
2. **Nothing says which fields are required or what the password must be.** There are no asterisks, no "optional" labels and no password rules. The user finds out what's wrong only after they submit and get an error. This hits hardest on the password field and the dropdown: a user who skips "How did you hear about us?" can't tell whether that will block them. This is a defect (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"; Nielsen, heuristics 5 and 6). Put "(optional)" after every optional label, and show the password rules as hint text under the Password field before the user types.
3. **The "Sign in" link looks like the rest of its sentence.** In "Already have an account? Sign in", the link is the same grey, weight and size as the words around it, with no underline. A returning user who lands here by mistake has nothing to show them that it's a link (WCAG 2.2, Technique G183; NN/g, Moran, "Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention"). This is a defect for the one user it's meant to rescue. Give "Sign in" the brand blue and an underline.

**Smaller things.**
- The dropdown under "How did you hear about us?" is an unstyled native control: blank, with a heavy black chevron. It looks like it comes from a different design than the inputs above it. Style it to match, and give it a placeholder such as "Choose one (optional)". This is a convention (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*).
- The input borders are a pale grey-blue on white and look below the 3:1 contrast needed for a field outline. On a low-quality screen the fields fade into the card. Darken the borders (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.11). Measure the actual border colour to confirm.
- "Workspace name" has no hint saying who sees the name or whether it can be changed later. Add one line of help text if the field stays (Nielsen, heuristic 10).
- There's no "Continue with Google" or other single sign-on option. For a free notes app, that's the shortest route in and the one users expect. This is my taste, as a common-sense call about this category of product.
- The copy is generic. "Start a free workspace for your notes" could sit on any notes app. One line on what Quillnest does differently would give the user a reason to finish the form.

**What's working.**
- One clear primary action. "Create account" is the only filled, high-contrast element, so there's no doubt about what ends the task. Keep that.
- Labels sit above the fields and close to them, with even vertical rhythm. The groups are easy to read and nothing depends on a placeholder.
- Pairing First name and Last name side by side is the right exception to a single-column layout. If you keep both fields, keep them paired.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and button, how and when validation errors appear, and how the card reflows on a phone width.
