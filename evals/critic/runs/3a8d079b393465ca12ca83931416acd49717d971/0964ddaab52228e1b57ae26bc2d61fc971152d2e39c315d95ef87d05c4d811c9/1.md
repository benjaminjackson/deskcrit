**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", bold and dark at top left, and then has nowhere to go. Under it is a grey smudge of three paragraphs so small they look like a texture, and the rest of the 1280×800 frame is empty white. It reads as a page that failed to render, not as a timetable.

**What it's for.** Assumed: a public web page that tells riders when the ferry leaves and how long the crossing takes.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job. The only content under the title can't be read, and the times a timetable exists to give aren't on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text can't be read** (defect). The three paragraphs under the title (about x 120–222, y 140–196) are set at about 4 px. Even a full-resolution crop leaves them as specks. Every rider, not only those with low vision, gets nothing from the page apart from its title. The type scale has broken. The title looks about 40 px and the text about 4 px, so the step between them is roughly tenfold, with no level in between and the lower level below anything readable. Bringhurst's rule is a modest set of related intervals (*Elements*, 3.1.1). Measure the computed size: this looks like a CSS bug, such as a stray `font-size` in em or a scale transform, rather than a choice. Set the body at least as large as the page's other UI text (Material 3 body is 16), at a normal reading weight, and check it still reaches 4.5:1 contrast once it's large enough to measure.
2. **The "timetable" has no times** (defect). The heading promises a timetable but the section holds only prose. From what can be made out, it describes how often the ferry runs and how long a crossing takes, and mentions bicycles and notices. A rider wants to know when the next boat leaves, and that needs exact values for looking up. Few (*Show Me the Numbers*, ch. 3) says that job belongs in a table, not in sentences. The heading also stops naming what the section holds (WCAG 2.4.6). Fix: lead with a table of departures from each landing, with tabular figures aligned in columns. Put the prose (crossing time, bicycles, how changes are announced) below it as notes.
3. **The page is mostly leftover space** (defect, a consequence of 1 and 2). About 85% of the frame is empty because the content has collapsed. This empty space has no shape or purpose (Ruder: space works by contrast with dense areas). It will mostly sort itself out once the text is readable and the table exists. Then check that the table gets the room and the title doesn't stand alone.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing before the above is fixed.

**What's working.**
- The title is clear and plain: it names the service and the document in three words.
- The single left edge at x≈120, shared by the title and the text, is a sound start for a grid.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiny text is the design or a rendering fault (zoom, a failed stylesheet, a mid-animation scale). Check the computed `font-size` in a live browser.
- How the page reflows on a phone, where most riders will open it.
