**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the masthead, then slides along a row of five same-sized tinted boxes and their headlines without stopping anywhere. The bottom half of the screen is blank. In version 2 the eye goes straight to the big blue block and the large "New footbridge over the Soar opens after two years of work" headline, then moves to the four smaller cards on the right. Both read as the front page of a local paper. Version 2 looks like a front page; version 1 looks like a list.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage of a local news site for Ashby residents. Its job is to show today's main story first and let readers scan the rest quickly.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job and version 1 doesn't. Version 1 gives all five stories equal weight, so it never tells the reader what matters today. Version 2 fixes that by making the footbridge story dominant. What's left in version 2 is craft: the bottom edges of the two columns don't line up, and the pictures are flat colour boxes.

**What changed, and why version 2 wins**
1. **Version 2 has a lead story; version 1 has none (defect, fixed in version 2).** In version 1 the five cards match in image size, headline size and weight, so the reader has to pick a starting point. A front page exists to make that choice for them. Tschichold asks that order of importance be shown through differences of size, and version 2 does exactly that. The footbridge story gets an image several times larger than the others and a headline about twice the size, so it's plainly the first thing seen and the other four rank below it.
2. **Version 2 uses the screen; version 1 leaves half of it empty (defect, fixed in version 2).** Version 1 puts everything in a band across the top and leaves about half the 800 px height empty. That empty area isn't a planned rest for the eye, just space that ran out of content, so the page looks top-heavy and unfinished. Version 2 fills the screen with a wide column and a 2×2 grid, which balances it and shows the same stories within one screen.
3. **The wider columns in version 2 also improve the text (convention).** Version 1's narrow columns break the short summary lines badly: "Farm.", "out." and "November." each sit alone on a last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2), and "shoot-out" is split at its hyphen. Version 2's wider cards fit each summary on one line.

**Remaining findings for version 2**
1. **The bottom of the lead story doesn't line up with the bottom of the card grid (convention).** The lead summary ("Walkers can now cross…") sits about 10 px higher than the summaries under "Ashby Town win cup…" and "Library extends…". The lead image ends at about 534 px, which lines up with nothing on the right. A near-miss like this looks like a mistake rather than a choice (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Fix it by setting the lead image height so the lead headline and summary end on the same line as the lower cards' summaries, or by offsetting them clearly.
2. **Every picture is a flat colour box (defect, unless images simply failed to load).** For a news page the photo carries much of the story, and the big blue field in the lead is only strong because of its size. If these are placeholders, a real photo will change how the lead reads, so recheck the hierarchy with real images in place. If images failed to load in the capture, this isn't a design problem.

**Smaller things.**
- Gap under the first row of cards: about 90 px separates the "Traders pay half rent…" line from the next row's images. That's much looser than the gutters, so the lower cards look like a separate band. Tighten it toward the gutter size.
- Nav (News · Sport · Business · Weather): nothing marks the current section. On the homepage that's acceptable, but give section pages one clear cue such as bold or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g).

**What's working.**
- **Type pairing:** the bold serif headlines over small grey sans-serif summaries give every card a clear headline-then-detail order. Keep it.
- **Lead headline:** it's about twice the size of the card headlines, a big enough jump that the two levels can't be confused.
- **Copy:** the headlines are specific and local, with verbs and numbers ("falls below 100", "half rent until March"), and each summary adds a fact rather than repeating the headline.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the colour boxes are placeholders or images that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- How the wide-plus-2×2 layout reflows on a phone.
