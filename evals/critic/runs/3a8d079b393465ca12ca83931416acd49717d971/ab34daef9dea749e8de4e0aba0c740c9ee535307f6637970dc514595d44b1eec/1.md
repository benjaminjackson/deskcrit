**First read.** The eye lands on the bold sans heading, drops into the first column, and then hesitates across three equal, narrow columns of serif text with no signposts. It reads as a quiet, orderly info page for a local ferry. The heading promises a story about the seasons, but the page delivers route, bicycles, and a winter timetable, so the first expectation isn't met.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page telling ferry passengers how the crossing works: route and frequency, bikes, and winter changes. It should be read quickly by someone about to travel.

**Verdict.** The page is calm and competently set, but it slows down the reader it's for. The columns are too narrow to read comfortably, and nothing tells a passenger which column answers their question. The heading also points the wrong way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The columns are too narrow to read comfortably (convention, with a real cost).** All three columns hold about 23–30 characters per line (for example "market quay every forty", "In winter the timetable thins"). Bringhurst gives 45–75 for a single column and 40–50 even for text set in several columns. At this width the eye has to jump back every three or four words, so sentences break into fragments. Column 1 runs to 13 lines for one paragraph, and the ragged right edge becomes most of what you see. Meanwhile about 265 px of the 1280 px frame sits empty on the right. The width is there; it just isn't being used. Fix: fewer, wider columns. Two columns, or one column of about 60–70 characters, would fit in the space the page already has.
2. **The heading and the content tell different stories, and the sections have no labels (defect).** "The river ferry through the seasons" leads the reader to expect a summer-versus-winter comparison. Only column 3 is about a season. Column 1 covers route and crossing time, and column 2 covers bicycles. A passenger who came to check whether they can bring a bike, or when the last boat leaves, has to read every paragraph to find out (WCAG 2.4.6, headings describe their content). Fix: give the page a heading that says what it is, such as a practical guide to using the ferry. Give each column a short label in the heading face at a small size, such as "Route and times", "Bicycles", "Winter timetable", so the page can be scanned.
3. **The facts people will act on are vague (defect, copy).** "Between early morning and late evening" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" are the lines a passenger plans around, and neither gives a time. Dusk falls at very different times across a winter. A reader can take "before dusk" as roughly 4 pm or as "a while before it gets dark", and the wrong reading leaves them on the wrong bank. The page doesn't say when the hourly winter timetable starts either. Fix: give first and last sailing times and the dates of the winter timetable. Or, if they change, tell the reader exactly where to get today's times ("check the noticeboard at either landing" is already halfway there).

**Smaller things.**
- Column 2 ends with "stairs." alone on its last line (a runt, Bringhurst 2.4.2). Re-flowing for the wider measure will probably fix it on its own; check it afterwards.

**What's working.**
- The type pairing is clear: a bold sans heading over a readable serif body. The two levels differ in face, weight, and size, so there's no doubt about what is title and what is text. Keep the pairing and keep the scale to these few sizes when you add column labels.
- The alignment is clean. The heading and column 1 share the 120 px left edge, the gutters are equal, and the first lines of all three columns sit on the same baseline. The grid is sound; it just needs wider columns.
- The copy is plain and concrete where it counts ("at no extra charge", "about twelve minutes in calm weather"). It reads like a person explaining the service, which suits a local ferry.
