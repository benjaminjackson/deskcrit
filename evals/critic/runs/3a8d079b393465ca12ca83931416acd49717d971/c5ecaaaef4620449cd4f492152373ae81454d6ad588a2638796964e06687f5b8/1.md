**First read.** The eye lands straight on "Harbour Lights Festival", the large cream italic that fills the middle of the frame. It then flicks up to the amber date and settles on the two-line blurb. It reads as the opening screen of a festival website or a poster for a free waterfront event. It feels calm, warm and a bit nostalgic, like dusk on the water, which is the right mood.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a web page for a free local festival. Its job is to tell people what it is, when it is and where, so they turn up.

**Verdict.** As a typographic composition it's strong and nearly clean: clear focal point, a tight three-level type scale and a palette that suits the event. What stops it working is the copy. It never says where the festival is, and the date may be wrong.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page never says where this is (defect).** The blurb says "the quay" and "the old net shed", and the title says "Harbour", but no town or harbour is named. Someone sent a link or passing a poster has the what and the when but can't plan the trip. Locals may know, but anyone else has to search for it, and "Harbour Lights Festival" is a name many towns could use. Add the place to the amber date line, e.g. "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour". It's already the line people read for facts, so the headline stays untouched.
2. **The weekday and the date only match in 2025 (defect; check it).** 14 June was a Saturday in 2025. In 2026 it falls on a Sunday and in 2027 on a Monday. The date is what people act on, so a mismatch makes some readers come on the Saturday and others on the 14th. If this is for 2025, fine. If it's for a future year, fix the day or the date, and think about adding the year, since a page with no year can't be dated once the event has passed.

**Smaller things.**
- "Free entry for everyone" comes straight after "a fish supper at the old net shed", so it can be read as "the supper's free too". If the supper costs money, say so ("Free entry; supper from £X"). People hold organisers to price lines. (Copy, common sense.)
- "From noon" has no end time, and the best part, the lantern boats, happens at dusk. Give the boats a time ("lantern boats at 9 pm") so people know when to come for the main event.
- There is no next step on this screen, such as the programme, a map or getting there. It may be below the fold, but a first screen that ends on open navy leaves the reader with nothing to do. Taste, given that the screen only shows 800 px.
- Taste: this is all type with no picture, so the "lights" in the name have no echo on the page. A single warm point of light (a photo or a small lantern motif) in the empty lower right would make it this festival rather than any one. It's optional, because the type does carry the mood on its own.

**What's working.**
- The headline wins clearly. At roughly seven times the body size, set in a warm italic serif, the eye has no doubt where to start. The two lines break at the natural phrase "Harbour / Lights Festival" and hold together as one unit. Keep that size gap.
- The type scale is tight: headline, body, and a small letterspaced amber caps label. The label and body are close in size but differ in case, weight and colour, so each has an obvious job. Don't add a fourth size.
- Navy, cream and one amber accent give dusk and lantern light without a picture. The amber is only on the date, which is the right thing to pull forward. The blurb runs about 58 characters a line with easy leading, so it reads in one pass.
