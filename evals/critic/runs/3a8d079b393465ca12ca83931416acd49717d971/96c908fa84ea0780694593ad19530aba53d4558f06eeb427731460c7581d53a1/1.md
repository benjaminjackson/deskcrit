**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the inbox illustration, drops to "No open conversations", reads the two-line explanation, and stops on "Open customer list". It's the empty-inbox screen of a customer-support tool, made for the agents who use it. It feels calm and tidy. The only change between the versions is the fill of the inbox illustration: soft green in version 1, soft blue in version 2. Every other pixel looks the same: layout, type, copy, button and sidebar.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it tells a support agent that the queue is empty and points them to the one useful thing they can do, which is start a conversation from a customer's profile.

**Verdict.** Both versions do their job well, and they do it equally well. The fill colour doesn't change what the agent understands or does. Green reads as a calm "all clear", which suits an empty queue. Blue reads as neutral information, which also suits it. Neither colour breaks a status convention, and neither changes the hierarchy, because both are pale tints of the same lightness under the same grey outline.

**What would move it most**
Nothing would noticeably change how well either version works. Picking between green and blue is a brand decision, not a design fix. If the product's accent colour is blue, use blue. If green elsewhere in the product means "resolved" or "done", green adds a small, useful hint. (Taste.)
