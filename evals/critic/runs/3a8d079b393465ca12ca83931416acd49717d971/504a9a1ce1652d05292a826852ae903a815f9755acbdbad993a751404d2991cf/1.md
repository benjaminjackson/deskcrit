## Critique: "Walking routes" page (three route cards)

**First read.** The eye lands on "Walking routes", drops to the three bold route names, then to the three blue "View route" buttons. Then it falls into the large empty area that fills the lower half of every card and keeps looking for whatever is supposed to be there. It reads as a list of local walks on a council, tourism or trail site. It feels clean, tidy and anonymous, and slightly unfinished.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this: a web page where walkers compare a few routes and pick one to open.

**Verdict.** The page is legible and orderly, but it doesn't help anyone choose. The facts a walker decides on are buried in three sentences of prose. And each card is about 200 px taller than its content, so it looks as if a picture or map failed to load.

**What would move it most**

1. **The empty lower half of every card.** Each card is about 440 px tall, but its content ends at the button, around 230 px down. That leaves an empty, outlined area below the button in all three cards. The viewer will read it as a missing photo or map, or as a broken page, not as breathing room. The white space here is scattered into dead areas inside boxes instead of being gathered where it helps (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). It also makes the whole page top-heavy.
   - Fix: if a route image or map belongs there and didn't load in this capture, restore it. A picture of the coast, the beech wood or the canal would also give each card a reason to look different. If nothing belongs there, let the card height follow its content.
   - Kind: defect. It looks broken unless something outside this image explains it.

2. **The deciding facts are buried in prose, so the routes can't be compared at a glance.** Distance, terrain, climbs, surface, bikes and amenities are all there, but each sits in a different place in a different sentence. To compare the walks you have to read all three blurbs and keep them in your head, and still can't line up "two steep climbs" against "flat" against "paved".
   - Fix: pull the same few facts out of the sentences into the same place on every card, such as a short line of label-and-value pairs under each route name (6 km · 2 steep climbs · café; 4 km · flat · shaded · benches; 10 km · paved · bikes OK). Then let a shortened sentence carry the character of the walk.
   - Add duration or difficulty only if you have it. The current copy has no time estimate, and for walkers that is often the deciding number.
   - Kind: copy and hierarchy defect. Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: make the page scannable.

3. **It could be anyone's page.** The look is a stock blue button, grey text and pale-outlined cards. Nothing in the colour, type or imagery says coast, woodland or water, or tells you who runs these walks.
   - Fix: the cheapest gain is one strong image per route, which also solves finding 1.
   - Kind: taste. The cost is that nothing about this page makes a walker want to go.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1: "end." sits alone on the last line of the blurb. Rewrap it, or trim the sentence so the paragraph doesn't end on one short word (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- **Type scale.** There are three clear levels (page title, route name, body) with the button in the same family. The steps are distinct and nothing collides, so keep it.
- **Consistent cards.** The three cards share one grid, with equal gutters and identical internal structure. That is the right setup for comparison; it just needs comparable content in it.
- **Three equal blue buttons.** This is correct here. They are the same action on three parallel items, so giving all three the same weight serves the choice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether an image or map was meant to fill the empty lower part of each card. Check the live page.
- The focus indicator on the "View route" buttons.

- Text: 30 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (clean paragraph breaks)
- Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
- Pictures: 12 lines, 11 yes, 1 no (no empty placeholder)
- Charts and data: skipped, no chart
- Interface: 33 lines, 32 yes, 1 unsure (focus indicator)
