**First read.** The eye goes straight to the large black headline "Travel lighter with the Wayfarer pack". It drops to the grey subline, then to the dark "Shop the pack" button, and stops. It reads as the opening section of a launch page for a travel backpack. The type is clean and confident, but the piece feels unfinished: there is no bag, no brand, and the right half and bottom third of the frame are empty.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a product page meant to make a carry-on traveller want this bag and click through to buy it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. It sells a physical object without showing it. The subline makes concrete promises: a bag that opens flat, fits under an airline seat, and weighs under a kilo. The viewer has to take all of that on trust, while half the frame sits empty where the proof should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **No product image (defect).** The whole right half (roughly x 860–1280) and the band below the buttons are blank. A buyer can't see what a 22 litre bag looks like, what "opens flat like a suitcase" means, or whether they like it. People buy bags by eye, so the page asks for a purchase before it gives them anything to want. It also leaves the page lopsided: all the weight sits in the top-left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
   - **Fix:** put the bag in that empty right half. Ideally show it open flat, or under a seat, so the picture proves the subline's claim rather than repeating the headline. That pairing is my judgement as a designer, not a cited rule.
   - **To check:** if an image was supposed to be there and failed to load, look at it in a live browser. If the space is empty by design, this is the finding.
2. **No brand and no store frame (convention).** There is no logo, header, navigation or cart anywhere. "Wayfarer" could be the product or the company, and a shopper can't tell whose store this is or how to reach the rest of it. People expect a storefront to have a header with the brand and a cart (Nielsen, Jakob's law). Leaving it out gains nothing here and costs trust right at the moment you ask for money.
   - **Fix:** add a slim header with the brand mark and the cart. If this frame is a crop of a fuller page, ignore this finding.
3. **The ask leaves out the price, and the headline is generic (defect and taste).** "Shop the pack" asks the buyer to commit without knowing the cost, so the one number they will act on is missing from the screen (Nielsen, recognition rather than recall). "Travel lighter" could head any luggage brand's page, so the specific line is buried in the grey subline. The subline is good: it sets the bag against things the viewer already knows (the seat in front of them, a suitcase, a kilo), which lets them judge its value for themselves.
   - **Fix:** put the price on or beside the button, for example "Shop the pack, £120". Then pull one of the subline's concrete claims up into the headline, for example "A suitcase that fits under the seat". That headline suggestion is taste.

**Smaller things.**
- The text column stops at about 860 px, and the space to its right has no job yet. Once the product image is in, size the two halves to each other so the split looks intended.

**What's working.**
- **One dominant headline.** It is huge, heavy and tightly set, with a clear step down to a regular-weight grey subline and a small blue eyebrow. Nobody has to guess where to start. Keep this hierarchy when you add the image.
- **A concrete subline.** "22 litre… fits under an airline seat… weighs less than a kilo" measures the bag against things the buyer already knows. It is the strongest copy on the page, at a comfortable line length of about 60 characters.
- **Clear primary and secondary actions.** There is one solid primary button and one plainly underlined secondary link. "Compare sizes" is a good second ask because it names exactly what happens next.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is an image that failed to load; check in a live browser.
- Focus styles on the button and link.
