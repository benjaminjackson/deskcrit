**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It starts at the top-left line, "Changing your pickup time", and reads straight down as if it were one paragraph. It only becomes clear on the second block that each short line is a question and the line under it is the answer. It reads as a bakery's help or FAQ panel. It feels tidy but flat and anonymous: grey type on white, with nothing that says bakery.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help section for customers who ordered bread online and want to change, hand off, check or cancel a pickup.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well. The headings look like body text, so the viewer can't scan for their question. Two of the four answers also leave out the facts a customer would act on: how to cancel and when the cutoff is, and whose name to give at the counter.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings look the same as the answers, so the list can't be scanned** (defect). "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two headings are in the same regular weight and the same dark grey as the answers. They're also a pixel or so *smaller* than the answer text. The only thing that sets them apart is the space above them. A customer who has come with one question has to read every line to find it. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8) says a difference that isn't obvious at a glance looks like an accident. Tschichold asks that order of importance be shown plainly. Fix: make each question clearly different from its answer in weight, colour or both, for example a semibold heading and a slightly greyer answer, rather than adding another size. Also add a page title such as "Your order: questions" so the list has a start point and something sits above the four questions.
2. **The answers people act on leave out the key facts** (defect, copy). "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" can mean 24 hours before the pickup time or any time the day before. A customer who reads it the second way and cancels at 9 pm for a 9 am pickup will be told it's too late. The line also never says *how* to cancel. "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name" can mean the pickup person's own name or yours, so the friend may stand at the till giving the wrong one. "Open your order" doesn't say where the order is: an email link, an account page? Fix: state the cutoff exactly ("until 24 hours before your pickup time"), give the action ("open your order and tap Cancel"), and name the name ("your name, as on the order"). "After that the bread has already been baked for you" is a good line: it gives the reason in the customer's terms. Keep it.
3. **The allergy answer doesn't help someone who is still ordering** (defect, copy). "Every loaf has a card by the till" only helps people who are already in the shop. Someone with an allergy who is ordering online learns they can't check ingredients until pickup, after they've paid. Fix: link to the ingredients from here, or say where they're listed on each product. Keep "ask the baker" as the backup.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the answers run to about 820 px, which looks like roughly 90–95 characters per line, past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column to about 600–640 px.
- Runts: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergy answer. A narrower column will probably fix this; check after resizing.

**What's working.**
- Grouping by space: the gap from each question to its answer (about 12 px) is much smaller than the gap between pairs (about 38 px), so the pairs hold together. Keep that ratio when you restyle the headings.
- The copy is plain, warm and short, and it gives reasons ("the bread has already been baked for you"). That makes the rules feel fair instead of arbitrary.
- One plain sans-serif typeface, set flush left and with comfortable line spacing. It's easy to read once you're in it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Open your order" or the questions are links or expanding sections, and how they behave.
- How the column reflows on a phone, where most pickup changes will probably be made.
