**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It starts at the top-left line, "Changing your pickup time", only because that is where reading starts, then runs down eight lines that all have the same weight, colour and near-identical size. It reads as a help or FAQ page for a bakery's order-ahead service, but I only got that from the paragraphs, not from anything on the page that says so. It looks calm and honest, and also unfinished, like a plain text file.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a help page where a customer who has ordered bread online finds a quick answer to one specific question, then leaves.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job well yet. The headings look the same as the answers, so a customer can't scan for their question. That's the whole point of a page like this. Two answers also leave out a fact the customer will act on.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings don't read as headings.** The four questions ("Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients", "Cancelling an order") are set in the same weight and colour as the answers under them, and they look a pixel or two *smaller*. A customer who came to cancel has to read the page from the top instead of jumping to "Cancelling". The only signal is the extra space above each group, and that marks a group, not a heading. *Defect.* Dair says a difference between two kinds of text must be obvious at a glance in at least one way: size, weight, form or colour. One step doesn't count (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). Here there is less than one step, and it goes the wrong way. Fix: make the headings clearly different from the body. Bolder is the cheapest way to do it (semibold at the body size, or a step larger). Also give them a clearly smaller size or a quieter colour than the answers, not the other way round. That leaves two levels, each with an obvious job.

2. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways.** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead." A day ahead of what: the pickup time, or the day you placed the order? And is that 24 hours before pickup, or any time the day before? A customer picking up at 9 am Saturday could reasonably think Friday evening is fine. If the rule is "24 hours before your pickup time", they'll find out they've lost their money for a loaf they no longer want. The page also never says *how* to cancel, while the pickup-time answer does tell you to "open your order". *Defect*, because this is a deadline the customer will hold the bakery to. Fix: give the exact cutoff in the customer's own terms, plus the action. For example: "You can cancel until [time] the day before your pickup. Open your order and choose Cancel."

3. **"Along with a name" leaves the person collecting guessing.** Whose name: theirs or yours? The person who gets turned away at the counter is the one who didn't write this page. *Defect* (common sense: this is an instruction someone will follow at the counter). Fix: say which name, e.g. "…along with your name, as it appears on the order."

4. **Nothing says what page this is.** There's no title, so the four questions float with no context, and there's no first element for the eye to land on. It should be self-evident what a page is within seconds (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*); here you have to infer it from the answers. *Convention, and it costs something here.* A customer who arrives from a link or search can't confirm they're in the right place. Fix: add a title above the questions that names the page (something like "Questions about your order"). It should clearly be the largest thing on the page. That single addition gives the page its starting point.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the full-width lines ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") run about 95–100 characters, well over Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column to roughly 65 characters. The page has plenty of room to give up.
- Runt: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrowing the column will probably fix it. If not, rewrap or rephrase.
- Allergies copy (taste): "a card by the till" only helps someone already in the shop. A customer ordering online can't see the card before they order. If the ingredients exist online, link to them. If not, say how to ask before ordering.

**What's working.**
- The grouping is right. The heading-to-answer gap (~10 px) is clearly smaller than the gap between questions (~30 px), so each Q&A already reads as one unit. Keep that rhythm when you change the headings.
- The answers are short, plainly worded and written to "you". "The bread has already been baked for you" gives the reason behind a rule in one human sentence, which makes a strict policy feel fair. Keep that voice.
- The single typeface and near-black text on white are easy to read. No styling needs taking away.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Open your order" and the other references go anywhere (links, or a way into the order screen), and how the column behaves on a phone.
