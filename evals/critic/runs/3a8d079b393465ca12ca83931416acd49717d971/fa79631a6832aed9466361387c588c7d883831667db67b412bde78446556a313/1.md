**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue title, then drops down the three blue subheads like rungs on a ladder. It reads as a short beginner's how-to article about starting a vegetable garden. It feels calm, clear and plain, close to a default stylesheet. The structure shows at a glance, but nothing besides the title gives it any character.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a short web article that walks a beginner gardener through setting up their first vegetable bed.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clean, the spacing groups each heading with its own text, and the page is easy to scan. Two things hold it back. The body text runs too wide to read comfortably, and the first step is hidden in an untitled intro, so the sequence of steps doesn't hold together.

**What would move it most**
1. **The lines are too long** (convention). Every paragraph runs from x≈160 to about x≈945, roughly 90 characters a line ("Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level"). At that width the eye has to work to find the start of the next line, and on a page meant to be read slowly while planning, that is the main source of friction. Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, §2.1.2, gives 45 to 75 characters, with about 66 as the ideal. Fix: narrow the text column to about 66 characters, roughly 620 px at this size, and keep the headings on the same left edge. The line spacing already looks generous enough for that width.
2. **Step one has no heading, so the "steps" start at step two** (defect). The opening paragraph ("Choose a spot that gets six hours of sun… Mark the corners…") is a real step with actions in it, but it looks like an intro. Someone scanning the blue subheads, which is what this layout invites, sees soil → plants → watering and skips where to put the bed and how to mark it out. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.6 asks that headings describe their sections, and here one section has none. Fix: give that paragraph a heading of its own at the same level as the others, such as "Choosing the spot". If you want a true intro, write a separate one-line lead under the title.

**Smaller things.**
- Copy: "six hours of sun" leaves the reader to fill in "direct" and "a day". This is the one number the reader will act on, so spell it out: "six hours of direct sun a day".
- Copy: the article ends at watering. A short closing line (when to expect the first radishes, or what to do next) would stop it ending on a dangling instruction. This is taste.
- "last year's" uses a straight apostrophe. Use the curly one (’). This is common sense; the catalog has no confirmed source for it, and at body size it's minor.
- Look: it's taste, but the saturated corporate blue plus default sans could be any software docs page. A colour drawn from the subject (a deep green or soil brown for the headings) would make it feel like it belongs to gardening without costing any clarity.

**What's working.**
- **A clear type scale.** I count three sizes, about 48 / 30 / 17 px, with big steps between them, and the headings also differ by weight and colour. Each level has an obvious job (Bringhurst; *Refactoring UI*). Keep it.
- **Spacing that groups.** Each subhead sits about 10 px above its paragraph and about 30 px below the one before, so each heading belongs to its text without a single rule or box. Keep that ratio when you narrow the column.
- **Plain, concrete copy.** "Things that forgive mistakes: radishes, lettuce, and bush beans" names real choices a beginner can act on straight away, with no filler.
