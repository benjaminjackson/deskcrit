**First read.** The eye lands on "Project notes", then moves down a stack of five identical boxes with heavy borders. Halfway down, the third box ("Sketches") sits about 14 px to the right of the others. That jog draws more attention than any of the content. The page reads as an ordered list of project stages and feels plain and stiff, more like a form than a plan.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal page that shows a team the stages of a design project in order.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The type is clean, readable and well-scaled, and the copy is unusually clear. But one misplaced card makes the page look broken, and the heavy outlines make a simple list feel boxed in. Fix the "Sketches" card first.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Sketches" card is out of line (defect).** Its left edge sits about 14 px inside the other four cards, and its right edge still lines up with them, so it is also narrower. An offset that small reads as a mistake. Any viewer who does take it as deliberate faces two readings: either Sketches is a sub-step of Research, or it is a fifth stage that was placed carelessly. In a list of stages, that is a question about the plan itself. Every other card follows one pattern, and this one breaks it (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, explains why a near-miss makes the eye waver. The fix: put it back on the 160 px edge with the others. If it really is a sub-step, indent it clearly, by a full step of 40 px or more, with a lighter treatment, so the nesting is obvious.
2. **Every card has a dark outline about 3 px thick (convention).** Five near-black frames are the heaviest marks on the page, heavier than the 16 px body text inside them. The structure shouts and the content whispers, so the page looks like a grid of separate compartments instead of one sequence. Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, warns against trapping each entry "inside a heavily guarded cell". The fix: remove the borders, or swap them for space or a pale tint, so the stage names carry the page.
3. **The page doesn't say it's a sequence, or where things stand (taste, and the copy).** "Project notes" names a category, not what is on the page, which is five stages in order. Nothing marks the order, and there are no dates, owners or status, although "Kickoff" itself promises "the dates, and who signs off each stage". A team member who opens this can't tell which stage the project is in or what is due next. The fix: name the page for what it is, for example "Project stages: from kickoff to handover", and show the order with numbers. If the page is meant to be used while the project runs, give each stage its date or owner.

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- **A clear type scale.** There are three distinct sizes: a title around 34 px, stage names around 20 px semibold, and body around 16 px regular. Each level is obvious at a glance and none come close to colliding. Keep it.
- **Copy that sounds like a person.** Every description starts with a verb and says exactly what gets done ("Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need"). Nobody has to fill a gap. Keep that voice whatever else changes.
- **Restraint.** One neutral palette, strong contrast, and the empty space kept in one open area on the right rather than spread thin. The page is calm and easy to scan.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable, and if so, what their hover and focus states look like.
