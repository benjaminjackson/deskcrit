**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The two versions are the same except for the headline's line spacing. In version 1 the two headline lines collide. Version 2 fixes that, and what's left is a clean, competent hero with only small gaps.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline lines collide in version 1. This is the whole difference, and version 2 fixes it.** In version 1 the descenders of "days" and "building" hang into the tops of "quieter, slower software". The y and g touch the letters below, so the two lines fuse into one dense block, and the first thing anyone reads is the least comfortable thing on the page. That is also odd for a conference about *quieter* software. Bringhurst allows heading lines tighter than text, "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide" (*Elements of Typographic Style*, p. 37). Hochuli faults the same thing (*Detail in Typography*, p. 28). Version 2 opens the line spacing just enough: the lines are still tight enough to read as one unit, and every letter is clear. It also adds a little more space between the headline and the subhead, so the headline, subhead and button read as three steps rather than a stack. Keep version 2's setting. (Defect.)

**Smaller things** (for version 2)
- **The button doesn't say what you'd pay.** "Get tickets" is a clear ask, but nothing near it gives a price or "from £…", so someone deciding whether to go has to click away to find out. Add the price, or a ticket tiers line, under or beside the button. (Taste and copy.)
- **The date line has no year.** "14 to 16 April" relies on "2027" in the wordmark in the top corner. Adding the year to the date line makes the one fact people put in their calendar complete on its own. (Common sense.)
- **The bottom third gives no hint of what follows.** The space below the button is a large, plain navy band with nothing pointing to the programme or speakers. A glimpse of the next section, or a speaker line, would give the eye somewhere to go. Check this against the live page, because the next section may simply start below the fold. (Taste.)

**What's working**
- **Clear order of importance.** The headline is the only large element, the date line and button share one yellow accent, and the paler subhead and nav step back. That gives the eye an easy first, second and third.
- **Copy with a point of view.** "Three days on building quieter, slower software" says something no other conference would, and the subhead names who it's for. Keep it.
- **Restraint.** There is one typeface, two hues and one left edge for every block, and the whole look fits the theme.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The hover and focus states of the button and nav links.
