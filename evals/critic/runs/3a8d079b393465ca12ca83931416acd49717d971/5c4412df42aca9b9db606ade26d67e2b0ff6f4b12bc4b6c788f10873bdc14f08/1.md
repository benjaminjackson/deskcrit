**What it's for.** Assumption, since you told me nothing: the pricing page of a scheduling product, meant to get a solo user or small-team buyer to pick a plan and start.

**Verdict.** It looks finished, but it doesn't do its job. It shows prices, but you can't choose one from where you're reading. With no per-plan action and no recommended plan, the page ends at the bullet lists and the buyer has to find their own way to sign up.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plans have no action** (defect). None of the three cards has a button. The only action is "Start free", top right in the nav, far from the plans, and it doesn't say which plan it starts or whether "free" means a trial of Team or something else. A buyer who has just decided "Team" has nothing to press. The eye path ends at "Reports" (Arnheim, on the eye travelling from focal point to the end without dead ends). Control and thing controlled are far apart (Norman, natural mapping). Pricing cards with a per-card button are the pattern buyers expect (Nielsen, Jakob's law). Fix: put a button at the foot of each card that names the outcome in the buyer's terms, such as "Start Team free for 14 days" if that's what happens. Make it the primary style only on the plan you want most people to pick.
2. **No plan leads** (convention). After the H1, three prices at the same size, weight, and card treatment compete to be seen first. No answer to "which one is for me?" stands out, so the buyer has to compare all three cold (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance by differences). Fix: make one plan, probably Team, clearly first. Give it the accent border or tint, a "Most teams pick this" label, and the one filled button, and set the other two back.
3. **The plans can't be compared as written** (defect: copy). Each card lists three different features, so the buyer can't tell:
   - whether Team includes Solo's booking page and email reminders, and whether Business includes Team's reports;
   - whether "$29 / month" is per person or for the whole team, on a page whose subtitle is "for teams of every size";
   - what "Reports" or "Shared availability" actually do for them.

   The price is the line people hold you to, so this matters. Read "per seat" when it's per account and the buyer thinks it's expensive. Read "per account" when it's per seat and they feel misled at checkout. This also forces recall across cards (Nielsen 6) and leaves the tier structure unclear (Norman, conceptual model). Fix: say "per user / month" or "for your whole team", whichever is true. Open higher tiers with "Everything in Solo, plus:". Give each feature a verb and a consequence, such as "See which booking pages fill up" instead of "Reports".
4. **The copy is generic** (taste). "Simple plans for teams of every size. Change or cancel whenever you like." could sit on any SaaS page and tells the buyer nothing about Tidewell. Make the subtitle carry something they act on: trial length, whether a card is needed, or a comparison to what they use now.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle leaves "you like." alone on its second line. Rebalance the break or widen the measure (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight and every step reads: H1, price, plan name, body. The big bold figure with a small regular "/ month" makes the prices scannable in one sweep. Keep that.
- The palette is restrained: near-black, one gray, one blue. That leaves the blue free to mark the one plan and button you want chosen.
- The three cards share one grid, equal gutters, and aligned baselines across columns, so adding buttons and a highlight won't need a re-layout.
