**First read.** Nothing stands out. The eye lands on six flat colour blocks of equal weight, pulled first by the red at top left and the yellow at bottom centre. Each has a pale circle clipped at its bottom edge, and they read as stand-ins, not pictures. The heading "Garden show gallery" is small and easy to miss. It reads as a gallery template with the photos missing: tidy, but there's nothing to look at.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a web gallery for a garden show, where visitors browse the show's areas and pick one to look at more closely.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. A gallery is its pictures, and here every picture is a placeholder, so the viewer has nothing to choose between except a coloured rectangle and a name. The second problem is that the page has no clear first element.

**What would move it most**
1. **The images are placeholders** (defect, common sense; the checklist has no entry for this). All six image areas are a flat tint with a pale circle cut off by the bottom edge. A visitor can't see the roses, the herbs or the pond, so the gallery shows nothing. The saturated rainbow (six strong hues filling about half the page) is also a side effect of the placeholders. It brings in red, green and amber with no meaning behind them, and it's louder than the garden photography would be. If these are real assets that failed to load, then the capture is the problem. Either way, the fix is real photographs in one consistent crop and colour treatment. Then the page's colour comes from the gardens and not from six arbitrary fills.
2. **Nothing is seen first** (convention). Six cards of identical size and strength sit under a title that looks about 20 px bold, close to the size of the card titles ("Rose arch", "Herb bed"…). The eye has nowhere to start, and the page doesn't say what the show is or when it's on. Tschichold's rule (*The New Typography*) is that logical order is shown by differences of size. Make one element clearly first. The usual choices are a page title that is clearly a level above the card titles, or one lead image (the show's headline garden) given more space than the rest. Two heading sizes that nearly match read as a mistake (Bringhurst, §3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale").

**Smaller things**
- The "Rose arch" caption is out of step with the other five. Its title and description sit about 2 px higher, so it misses the baselines of "Herb bed" and "Pond edge" next to it. Set all six captions from one spacing rule (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*).
- The card titles sit about 4–6 px under the image bottoms, nearly touching. Give them a little more space so they don't look jammed against the image (common sense). Keep that space clearly smaller than the 14 px gutter so each caption still belongs to its picture.
- The circles in the placeholders are centred and clipped at the bottom edge, so the crop looks accidental. This goes away with real photos.

**What's working**
- The three-column grid is clean, with even gutters and shared column edges top to bottom. Keep it when the photos go in.
- Grouping by space works. Each caption sits tight under its image, and the large gap between the two rows separates the rows clearly (Arnheim on proximity).
- The captions are short, concrete and in plain words ("Tomatoes in late July", "Reeds and water lilies"). Each gives the viewer one specific thing to look for.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cards are links, and whether they show a hover or focus state. If they open a detail page, nothing in this image tells the visitor they're clickable.
- Whether the placeholders are the design or images that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
