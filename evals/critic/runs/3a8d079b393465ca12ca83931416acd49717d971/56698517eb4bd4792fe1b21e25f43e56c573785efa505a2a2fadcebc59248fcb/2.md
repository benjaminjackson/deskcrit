**First read.** The eye goes straight to the six blocks of colour, mostly the yellow "Glasshouse" tile and the red "Rose arch" tile, and never settles on the page title. There are no plants, paths or ponds anywhere; each tile is a flat colour with a pale circle sitting on its bottom edge. It reads as a wireframe or a page whose photos failed to load, not as a garden gallery. It feels tidy and empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page showing visitors the six areas of a garden show, so they can see what's there and pick what to look at.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet: a gallery with no pictures in it gives visitors nothing to look at. The grid and type underneath are sound, but until each tile shows the place it names, this is a wireframe rather than a gallery.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures show nothing (defect; common sense, no catalog rule).** All six tiles are a flat colour with a lighter centred circle, which reads as an "image goes here" placeholder. The captions promise climbing roses, water lilies and plum trees, and nothing above them delivers. The circles are faint against their tiles and their bottoms sit exactly on the tile edge, so they look like stubs rather than something drawn on purpose. If the real photos simply didn't load in this capture, check it in a live browser. If these stand-ins are the design, replace them with a photo or illustration of each area that matches its caption. A picture of a rose arch does more than a red block can.
2. **The page title is no bigger than the card titles (convention).** "Garden show gallery" looks about 20 px semibold. "Rose arch", "Herb bed" and the other card titles look about 22 px bold, so the page's own title is the quieter of the two. These two levels are only a couple of pixels apart, so they look like a mistake rather than a choice (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). With nothing setting the order, the coloured tiles win, and the viewer starts in the middle of the grid. Make the page title clearly the largest text on the page, a full step above the card titles, so the order of importance is plain (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). If the gallery has a date or venue, it belongs there too.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Rose arch" card, top left:** the title sits about 2 px higher and the caption about 4 px higher than in "Herb bed" and "Pond edge". The baselines across the first row don't line up, and the first card looks like it came from another build. Give it the same image-to-title spacing as the other cards.

**What's working.**
- **The grid:** three equal columns with even gutters and the same left edge from the title down. Keep it when the photos arrive.
- **The card text:** a short bold name over a one-line grey caption gives a clear two-level label that's quick to scan.
- **The captions:** they're concrete and specific to this garden ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Tomatoes in late July"). They're already the most garden-like thing on the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are placeholders by design or photos that hadn't loaded yet. Load the page live.
- Whether the cards are clickable and how they show hover and focus.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (main image and headline make one idea; every element does a job; each kind of element looks the same)
What matters first: 3 lines, 1 yes, 2 no (one element dominates; text levels distinct)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (exact alignment, no near-misses; baselines across columns)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 9 yes, 3 no (every image shows its content; crop looks intended; subject stands apart from background)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: skipped, no visible controls
