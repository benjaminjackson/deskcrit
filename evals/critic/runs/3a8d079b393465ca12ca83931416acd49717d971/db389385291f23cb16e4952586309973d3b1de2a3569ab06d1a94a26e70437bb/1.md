**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: this is the first screen of a marketing home page meant to get team leads or engineering managers to start a trial.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy and type are clean, but the page doesn't do its main job yet. It promises a picture of your week and never shows one, so the viewer has to take the claim on trust and sign up without seeing the product.

**What would move it most**
1. **The product is missing, and its absence leaves the page lopsided.** The subhead's whole pitch is visual: "one honest picture of the next five days". Nothing on the screen shows that picture. All the content sits in the upper-left two-thirds. The right side and the roughly 270 px below the button are blank, so the weight tips up and to the left and nothing on the other side balances it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The viewer can't tell this tool from any other planner, and can't check whether "honest" means anything. *Defect.* Fix: put a real view of a planned week on the right or just below the fold, built from the three inputs the subhead names: tickets, meetings, and someone on leave. Make it show the thing a sprint board hides, such as a day that's already full. That one picture is the argument the page is missing, and it fills the empty side too.
2. **The ask makes the viewer grade the product on faith.** "Start a free trial" could sit on any SaaS page. It doesn't say what you'll get or how long it takes, and it doesn't set Weekplan next to anything you already trust. *Defect (copy).* Fix: make the ask a comparison on the viewer's own terms, such as "See next week from your Jira and calendar" or "Connect your calendar and see your real week". That way the first thing a trial gives them is their own week shown back to them, and they can judge it against what their current board claims.
3. **The headline is tracked loosely.** In "Plan the week your / team actually works" the letters and words look spaced for text size, not for an ~80 px heavy display weight. The words drift apart, and the line reads as five heavy blocks instead of one phrase. At this size the spacing should tighten, not stay at text spacing (Bringhurst §2.1.1; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 34). *Convention.* Fix: tighten the tracking slightly negative at this size, and close up the word spaces with it, until each line reads as one phrase.

**Smaller things.**
- Under the button: nothing answers the questions a trial button raises (how long it lasts, whether a card is needed). A single quiet line such as "14 days, no card" would remove the hesitation. This is common sense, not a published rule.

**What's working.**
- The headline speaks to a real pain in the viewer's own words. "Actually works" quietly admits that plans and reality differ. Keep the line and only fix its spacing.
- The subhead is concrete: it names three inputs and the output, about 60 characters per line, in a readable grey. It's the best copy on the page, so build the missing image from it.
- There's one clear primary action and one shared left edge at x=120 for the logo, headline, subhead, and button. The structure is right, and it just needs content on its open side.
