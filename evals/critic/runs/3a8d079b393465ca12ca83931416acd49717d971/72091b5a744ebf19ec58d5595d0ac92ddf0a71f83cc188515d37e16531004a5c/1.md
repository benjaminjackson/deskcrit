**First read.** The eye goes straight to the black slab of a headline, then drops to the grey line. I read it as the top of a web page announcing free ferry rides at a harbour open day, for locals and families. It feels loud and clipped, more like a sports-brand sale than a slow morning on the water. Reading it a second time I notice there's no day, no time and nothing to click, and the bottom 40% of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: the top of a page or a social card that gets the public to turn up for free ferry rides at the City Harbour Open Day.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The hierarchy is clean, but the headline is crushed until its letters collide, and the page never says when the open day is. Someone who wants to go can't.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no date, time or way to act. *Defect.*** The eyebrow says "Open Day" and the body says "every half hour from the north landing", but nothing says which day, what hours, or where the north landing is. There's no button or link either. Free rides every half hour are worth nothing if people don't know which day to come. The empty lower half shows the space is there.
   - **Fix:** add a dated, timed line next to the body copy, such as "Saturday 14 June, 9am–1pm".
   - Point "north landing" to something people already know, like a street or a map link.
   - If this is a web page, add one specific action, such as "See the ferry times" or "Get directions to the north landing", rather than a generic "Learn more".
2. **The headline is tracked so tight the letters crash. *Defect, and against convention.*** In "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY":
   - The two Ls fuse, R runs into D, N-I-N-G merges into one shape, and the Rs in FERRY touch.
   - The word spaces have nearly closed, so it reads "ALLABOARD" and "MORNINGFERRY". Reading slows and the words lose their shape at a glance, which is the one thing a display line is for.
   - Big type does want tighter spacing than text. But Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 30) says letters should not touch, and a small hole between two letters is less bad than a collision. Bringhurst says to kern "consistently and modestly".
   - **Fix:** ease the tracking back until no letters touch, and open the word spaces so each word stands alone. It will still look tight and bold.
   - *Taste:* the heavy, airless setting also fights the message. "Bring a hat and a thermos" is unhurried, and a little air in the headline would carry that mood better.

**Smaller things.**
- The eyebrow "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" is small capitals tracked negative, so it reads as a cramped grey smudge. Letterspace it positively, around 5–10% of its size (Bringhurst 2.1.6).

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy.** There are three distinct levels (eyebrow, display, body) with big jumps between them, so the eye never wonders where to start. Keep that scale as it is.
- **The body copy.** It's set well: a comfortable size, roughly 60 characters a line, easy leading, flush left on the same edge as everything else.
- **"Bring a hat and a thermos."** It's specific and human, and it paints the morning. That's the kind of copy the rest of the piece should match.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the headline wraps at narrower widths. At this setting a mobile break could strand "THE" or split "FERRY" badly. Check it at phone widths.
