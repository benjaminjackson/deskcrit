**First read.** The eye goes to the bold black title, "Notes for ferry passengers", then slides into a block of pale grey text that's tiring to read. Below that is a smaller grey line that looks like a footnote. It reads as a plain, calm information page, but faded, as if everything under the title were switched off. The text keeps to the top-left, and the rest of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page telling ferry riders how the service runs, so they can turn up at the right time and know the rules for bikes.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. All the useful information is in pale grey that looks well under readable contrast. The one line that could leave a passenger stranded (winter timetables) is set as the least important thing on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraphs and the note are too faint to read comfortably (defect).** All three text blocks under the title are a light grey on white, and they look about 2.5:1, well short of the 4.5:1 WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 asks for normal text. Readers with low vision, or on a phone in sunlight at the landing, will struggle. Everyone else reads more slowly. The page also has only two values, a black title and pale grey for everything else. In grayscale it's a title over a haze. Fix: darken the text until it reaches at least 4.5:1. A dark grey close to the title colour is the usual answer. Check the exact ratio with a contrast checker on the live colour.
2. **The most important instruction is styled as the least important (defect).** "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel." is the only line that tells the reader to do something, and the only one whose miss costs them a wasted trip. It's set smaller than the paragraphs, in the same grey, at the bottom. It also sits only a few pixels below the body size (it looks like about 17 px against about 20 px), so it doesn't read as a separate level either. It looks like a size slip. Fix: drop the in-between size and give the warning more emphasis, not less. Make it bolder or darker at body size, or move it up beside the schedule it changes (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
3. **The schedule copy is too vague to plan a trip by (defect).** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" gives the interval but no first or last sailing. The reader can't tell whether they'll catch a 06:30 crossing or a 22:00 one home. This is exactly the line people act on and later hold the operator to. "Early" and "late" could mean 5am or 8am, 9pm or 11pm. Fix: give the first and last departure from each side, with real times. Then the winter note can say what actually changes.

**Smaller things.** None beyond these.

**What's working.**
- The title is clearly dominant through weight and colour, so the eye knows where to start. Keep that contrast and make the body darker. Don't make the title lighter.
- The single flush-left column at about 70–75 characters a line, with generous line spacing and clear gaps between paragraphs, is a comfortable reading setup. Once the text is dark enough, it will read easily.
- The bike paragraph puts its steps in the order you'd do them (board after foot passengers, lower deck, lock to the rail). That's good instructional copy.
