**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Harbour Lights Festival", a big cream italic filling the top two-thirds. From there it goes up to the amber date, then down to the two lines of body copy, and stops. It reads as the hero of a small-town festival website, and it feels calm, warm and a bit literary, like dusk on the water. Then comes the question it doesn't answer: where is this?

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a festival website for local people and day-trippers. Its job is to get them to put the date in their diary and turn up.

**Verdict.** As a piece of type and mood it's strong and almost faultless. As an invitation it falls short: it gives the date but not the place, and it gives the viewer nothing to do next. Someone who isn't already local can't act on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no place.** The page never names the town or harbour. The only location is "the old net shed", which only someone who already knows the place can find. A visitor learns what's happening and when, but not where, and that's the one thing they need in order to come. This is a defect: it leaves out the people a free public festival most wants to reach. Put the place next to the date in the amber line, e.g. "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour". The amber line is already the "when"; make it "when and where".
2. **The page ends in a dead end.** After the body copy there's no ask: no map or directions link, no "add to calendar", no programme. The eye path ends at "Free entry for everyone." and then reaches 200 px of empty navy. In a still image the bottom third looks like space a button or link was meant to fill. This is my judgment, not a cited rule (the eye-path entry's sources aren't on the approved list). Add one specific ask in the body's left column, below the copy. "Get directions to the quay" or "See the evening's programme" names what happens, which "Find out more" doesn't. Being free is the reason to come, so put the ask right after it.

**Smaller things.**
- "From noon" with no end time: lantern boats "at dusk" suggests the evening, but people planning a trip will want to know when it finishes. Add "until late" or the actual time. This is copy, not a checklist line.
- The year is missing from the date. That's fine on a live site this season, but it becomes ambiguous once the page is archived or shared later.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** The large italic serif is set tight and breaks at a natural phrase, "Harbour Lights / Festival", so it reads as one shape and carries the whole mood with no picture. Keep it this size and this dominant.
- **The palette.** Deep navy, cream and a single lantern amber, with the amber kept to one small line. Colour alone says "evening by the water", and the date stands out without competing with the title.
- **The body copy.** It names concrete things you'd go for (lantern boats, music on the quay, a fish supper) instead of generic festival language. It's set at a comfortable size and line length, all on the same left edge as everything above it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether there's navigation or an ask further down the page or in a header that wasn't captured. If there is, finding 2 shrinks to "bring it up into this first screen".
