**First read.** In version 1 the eye goes first to the large, light "Recent work", then down to the wide tan block for Coldharbour Lane library, then right across the two narrower tiles. It reads as a calm, confident portfolio page for an architecture practice. In version 2 the eye lands on the row of three equal blocks and moves across them like a table. "Recent work" is small enough to pass for a caption, and nothing tells you where to start.

**What it's for.** My assumption is that this is the projects page of a small architecture practice. Prospective clients use it to see what the practice builds and to pick a project to look into.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. It has an order: a page title and a lead project. Version 2 flattens both, and the heading ends up the same size as the project names. Both versions show flat colour blocks where the photographs should be. For an architecture site that is the biggest gap of all.

**What changed between the versions**
1. **The lead project lost its lead (defect, in V2).** V1 makes Coldharbour Lane library twice as wide as the other two (a 2:1:1 split). The newest and largest public building comes first, and the row reads as "our headline project, then more". V2 gives all three the same width, so a 2,400 m² library and a ferry canopy look equally important, and the viewer has no entry point. Tschichold says the order of importance should show through differences of size (*The New Typography*). V1 does that and V2 does not. If V2's equal grid is a deliberate choice, make one thing dominant another way, such as a larger title for the lead project. As it stands, nothing dominates.
2. **The heading fell to the same level as the project titles (defect, in V2).** In V2, "Recent work" looks about 22 px, the same as "Coldharbour Lane library" and the other names. Two different jobs share one size, so the page title reads as one more label (Bringhurst: a few distinct sizes, not near-repeats). V1 has three clear levels: a heading of about 56 px in a light weight, titles of about 22 px in regular, and grey captions of about 14 px. The light weight also keeps the big heading from fighting the pictures. Keep V1's scale.

**What would move it most (in version 1)**
1. **The pictures are flat colour blocks (defect).** All three tiles are plain fills with no photograph. On an architects' portfolio the buildings are the product. With no images the viewer can't judge the work, and the warm tones are standing in for content. This may be a capture where the images hadn't loaded, or unfinished placeholders. Either way, the page only works with real photographs in place. Once they're in, give the lead image the strongest picture and give the set one grade and one crop style.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav links (Projects · Studio · Contact) all look the same, so nothing marks Projects as the current page. Add one cue, such as an underline or a heavier weight (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- In V1, "Two houses on Weir Road" fills its column almost to the gutter. It fits now, but a longer name will wrap or crowd the next tile. Allow for wrapping or a shorter title.

**What's working.**
- V1's light-weight display heading against regular-weight titles: size and weight work together, so the heading leads without shouting.
- The captions say what each project is, how big it is and when it was finished ("Public library, 2,400 m², completed 2025"). That is concrete and in a client's own terms. Give the other two captions sizes as well, as the library's has.
- One restrained warm palette, with generous margins and one clean grid, suits a practice that wants to look careful.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blocks are images that hadn't loaded yet. Check in a live browser.
- The nav targets' hit area. They look about 20 px tall, so measure them against WCAG 2.5.8.
