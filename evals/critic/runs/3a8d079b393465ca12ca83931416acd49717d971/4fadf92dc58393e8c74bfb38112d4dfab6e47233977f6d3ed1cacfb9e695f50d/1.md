**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a product detail page on a homewares shop. Its job is to tell a shopper what the scale does and get them to add it to their basket.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is that the two body paragraphs go from flush right (version 1) to flush left (version 2), and that change fixes the one real typographic defect between them. Both versions are still held back by the same bigger problem: the product image is an empty placeholder, so the page sells a scale the shopper never sees.

**What changed, and why version 2 wins**
- In version 1 both paragraphs (four lines and three lines) are set flush right. Every line starts at a different point, so the reader has to search for the start of each new line. That slows reading of the copy that makes the case for buying, such as the tare button, the 5 kg limit and the one-year battery life. It also breaks the strong left axis that the "Kitchen" label, the title, the price and the button share, so the column looks less tidy than it is. Version 2 sets both paragraphs flush left: one crisp left edge, a soft rag on the right, and the whole column on one axis. **Defect and convention** (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3: set paragraphs of several lines flush left and keep centring for a few short lines).

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **The product image is empty.** The right half of the page is a beige panel with a faint off-white circle and no product. The shopper is asked to pay $34.00 for something they can't see. The circle also barely stands out from the beige, so even as a placeholder it nearly disappears. If this is a picture that hasn't loaded, the finding becomes "check it loads." As designed, it's the biggest thing between this page and a sale. Fix: put in a sharp photo of the scale. Ideally show it on a counter with the display visible, because the copy sells the glass top and the unit switching. This is common sense, so I'm marking it a **defect**.

**Smaller things.** None for version 2. In version 1, the flush-right paragraphs (above) are the only other failure.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clear: a small amber category label, a big bold title, readable body text, a bold price, and one dark primary button. Each level is a distinct step, and there are about four sizes with no near-collisions.

- Lines run about 70 characters with generous leading, so the body text is comfortable to read.
