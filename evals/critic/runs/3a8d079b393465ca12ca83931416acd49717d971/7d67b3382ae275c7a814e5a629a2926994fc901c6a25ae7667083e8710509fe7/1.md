I compared the two versions. **Version 2 is better.** The only change is the colour of the body text, and in version 1 that colour is too pale.

**First read.** In both versions the eye goes to the big, heavy serif headline, then the byline, then into the text. It's a calm, plain article page for a cycling route guide. In version 1 the paragraphs look light grey, and the eye slides off them. They look like a preview or a quote rather than the article itself. In version 2 they look like text you're meant to read.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a route guide that cyclists read to plan a ride. It needs to be easy to read for the full six minutes and leave them with the practical facts (gravel after the third bridge, cafés close at four, bring lights).

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job. It's a clean, well-proportioned reading page with nothing seriously wrong. Version 1 fails on the one thing a reading page must get right: the body text looks too faint to read comfortably, and it is the palest text on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **Body text contrast (version 1 to version 2).** In version 1 all three paragraphs are a mid grey on white. By eye that's somewhere around 3.5 to 4:1, below the 4.5:1 WCAG 1.4.3 asks for normal-size text. Readers with low vision, readers on a phone in sunlight (likely for a ride guide), and anyone reading the full six minutes will tire or skim. It also turns the order of importance upside down. The small sans byline ("By Tom Arden · 6 minute read") is darker than the article, so the page tells you the metadata matters more than the route. Version 2 darkens the body to a deep grey that looks well past 4.5:1. The text now holds up as the main content and sits clearly below the near-black headline. This is a **defect**. Version 2's fix is the right one, so keep it. To confirm, measure the exact ratio of the version 2 body colour in a browser.

**Smaller things.** None worth a line. Paragraph lines run about 78 to 80 characters, just over Bringhurst's 45 to 75. That's borderline in a single centred column, and pulling the column in by about 30 px would land it at about 70.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** It's a heavy serif at a size clearly above everything else, set tight over two lines that break on a phrase ("…Brindle Lock / to the estuary"). The eye has an obvious place to start.
- **Paragraph spacing and leading.** Short paragraphs, generous line spacing, and paragraph gaps clearly bigger than the gaps between lines. The text reads as three clear chunks.
- **The copy.** It's specific and useful to a rider: the surface change and when it happens, the 32 mm tyre threshold, café closing times, why to bring lights. Keep that voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the text column narrows and reflows sensibly on a phone, where this guide will most likely be read.
