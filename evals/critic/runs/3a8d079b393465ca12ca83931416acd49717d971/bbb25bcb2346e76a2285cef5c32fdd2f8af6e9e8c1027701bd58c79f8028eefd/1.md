**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on "Maps of the inland sea", moves down the left column, then drifts to the large tan block on the right, which turns out to be empty. It reads as a museum exhibition page: calm, scholarly and warm. In version 2 the bold sans title and subheads grab the eye at once, and the page scans as three labelled chunks. In version 1 everything is in the same regular serif, so the subheads blend into the body text and the left column reads as one block of prose.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an exhibition page that tells a museum visitor what the show is, what they'll see, and when and how they can go.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the headings, and it is the right change: it gives the column a hierarchy you can scan, and the practical facts (times, free entry) can be found at a glance. Both versions share a bigger problem: the main image is an empty gradient. On a page about hand-coloured maps, no map is shown.

**What would move it most**
1. **The image is an empty placeholder (both versions, defect).** The 420×560 block on the right is a plain tan-to-brown gradient. It takes nearly half the page and draws the eye, then gives nothing back. For an exhibition of hand-coloured charts, the picture is the reason to come, and the headline promises maps the page never shows. If an image failed to load in the capture, this is fine once it loads. If not, put in a real chart from the show, cropped so the line work and colour can be read at this size.
2. **Version 1's headings differ from the body only by size (v1 only, convention).** "What you will see" and "Opening times" are the same serif, the same regular weight and the same colour as the 18 px text below, just a few sizes up. Dair says a contrast must be obvious in more than one way, and a weak contrast reads as an accident (*Design with Type*, ch. 8–9). Here a visitor who is skimming for opening hours has to read rather than scan. Version 2 fixes this the way Dair recommends, by pairing the size step with a change of structure and weight (bold sans against serif text). The bold sans also matches the "Calder Museum" wordmark, so the page now uses one plan of two clearly different faces. Keep version 2's headings.

**Smaller things.**
- The navigation doesn't mark the current page. "Exhibitions" looks the same as the other links. Mark it with a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- The image's right edge (about x=1220) overshoots the navigation's right edge (about x=1184) by roughly 36 px. That is close enough to look like a miss. Either align them or make the break clearly deliberate.

**What's working.**
- In version 2, bold sans headings over serif body text give three distinct levels (title, subhead, body) and tie into the wordmark.
- The copy is specific and concrete: "Forty charts drawn between 1620 and 1790", "a brass sounding lead found in the harbour mud in 1958". The practical line gives days, hours, end date, price and access in one sentence a stranger can read. Keep it.
- Heading spacing groups well: each subhead sits tight to its paragraph, with clearly more space above it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the image is a deliberate placeholder or one that didn't load. Check it in a live browser.
