**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the bold title, then steps down the three bold section heads: "Check the deposit", "Take photos on day one", "Know your notice period". You get the whole guide from those four lines before reading any body text. In version 2 the title still catches the eye first, but it is lighter and greyer. The section heads are regular weight in the same slate grey as the body, so the page reads as one grey block, and you have to read the paragraphs to find the structure. Both are calm, plain and trustworthy in tone.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short guide page for someone about to rent. They need to scan the three or four things to check before signing, and later come back to find one of them.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change is in the headings: version 2 sets them in regular weight and in the body's colour. That takes away the scannable outline, which is the main thing a checklist page like this has to offer. Version 1 works. What remains is minor: the measure runs a little long.

**What would move it most**
1. **The section heads in version 2 differ from the body text only in size, and only by one small step.** "Check the deposit" and the other heads look about 24 px against body text of about 17–18 px. They share its regular weight and slate grey. In grayscale they almost merge with the paragraphs beneath them. Someone scanning for "what about my deposit?" can't jump to it at a glance and has to read down the page. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8–9) says a contrast must be obvious, and a small step in size should come with a change of weight or colour. Version 1 does exactly that with bold and near-black, and it is the version to keep. *Convention, with a real cost here (defect):* the page only works if it can be scanned. Fix direction: restore the heavier weight and darker colour on the headings, and on the title too.

**Smaller things.**
- Both versions: the body lines look like about 85–90 characters ("A tenancy is a contract… explain any"), above Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column a little, to about 600 px at this size, to land near 66.
- Both versions: the copy is good. Each section opens with a verb and speaks to "you", and the dates and counts ("thirty days", "one month… two from the landlord") are specific. Keep it.

**What's working.** In version 1, bold dark heads paired with a modest size step give a clean four-level outline with no extra sizes. The spacing is right: about 36 px above each head and about 10 px below, so each head clearly belongs to its paragraph. And the headings are instructions in their own right ("Take photos on day one"), so even a skim of the heads alone teaches the reader something.
