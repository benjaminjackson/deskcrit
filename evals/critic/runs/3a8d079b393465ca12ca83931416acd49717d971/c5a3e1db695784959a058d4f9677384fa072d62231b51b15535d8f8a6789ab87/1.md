**Verdict.** It works. There is one clear focal point, a clean order of reading and type set with care. The only thing that noticeably holds it back is where the block sits in the frame: it rides at the top-left, so the frame looks like a page that ran out of content rather than a composed card.

**What would move it most**
1. **The block sits too high, so the frame looks unfinished.** This is taste, though the balance point comes from Arnheim. The text runs from about 60 px down to about 590 px, which leaves only about 60 px above the label but about 210 px of empty band at the bottom, plus about 250 px empty on the right. A viewer sees a heavy top-left and an empty bottom-right with nothing pulling against it, so the card reads as a web page that stopped rather than a frame someone chose. Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception* ("Balance"): weight comes from size, darkness and where things sit, and a heavy side needs a counterweight. Asymmetry is fine here, and I'm not asking you to centre it. Commit to one of two choices:
   - Lower the block so the open space below it looks chosen. Roughly a third of the leftover height above and two thirds below works.
   - Use the bottom band on purpose, for example by moving the masthead label or an issue date down to the bottom-left so it anchors the corner.
   
   Either way, make the frame's split look chosen, not left over.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line break in the quote:** "the first ferry / leaves" splits the subject from its verb, so line 3 opens on a lone verb. Rebreak at phrase boundaries, for example "…quietest just / before the first ferry leaves, / and that is when / the gulls get bold." This is common sense; the reference entry's sources are unconfirmed for headings.
- **Spacing of the quote:** at around 80 px it looks set with text spacing, and the word spaces look wide ("is quietest", "that is when"). Tighten the word spacing and, a little, the letter spacing, so the four lines read as one solid block (Bringhurst 2.1.1; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 34).

**What's working.**
- **The opening quotation mark hangs outside the left edge.** That keeps the quote, label, attribution and caption on one crisp left line at x≈100. Keep this when you reflow.
- **The quote is the only large element.** The three small lines are told apart by weight and colour rather than more sizes: bold dark name, regular grey caption, bold grey label. That gives a short, clear type scale with an obvious job for each level (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- **The caption earns its place.** It grounds a mood line in hard facts: 5:40, bicycles, bread deliveries. The type pairs a serif with a plainly different sans and uses a greys-only palette, which keeps everything in one quiet register that suits a harbour at dawn.
