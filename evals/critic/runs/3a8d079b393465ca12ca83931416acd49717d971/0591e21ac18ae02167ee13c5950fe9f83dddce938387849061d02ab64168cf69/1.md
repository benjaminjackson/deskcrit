**First read.** The eye lands on the huge black headline and right away sees that it is broken. Each line's descenders run into the next line: the g's of "crossings" hit "spring", the g of "daylight" hits "through", and the y of "early" hits "summer". The block reads as a smear of letters before it reads as words. The eye then drops to a short intro paragraph and stops dead with half a screen of white below it. It feels like a page that broke and was never finished.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the top of a web page telling local riders and visitors when the river ferry runs this spring.

**Verdict.** It doesn't work yet. The headline's line spacing is so tight that its lines collide, so the most important text on the page is the hardest to read. And a page labelled "timetable" gives no times or dates, so the rider leaves without the one thing they came for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline lines collide** (defect, convention). In all four headline lines, descenders cut into the letters below: "crossings" into "spring", "daylight" into "through", "early" into "summer". The line spacing looks to be well below the type size. The viewer has to untangle each line before reading it, and the biggest, most confident element on the page looks like a rendering bug. Bringhurst allows display type set tighter than its size only "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide" (*Elements of Typographic Style*, p. 37), and Hochuli faults exactly this (*Detail in Typography*, p. 28). Fix: open the headline's line spacing until no letter of one line touches the next. Keep it tighter than the body text so the four lines still read as one block. Roughly 1.0–1.1× the type size will likely clear it in this face; check the g/y-over-cap pairs.
2. **It promises a timetable and gives none** (defect). The eyebrow says "Spring timetable" and the headline says "spring through early summer". The page shows no departure times, no first or last boat, and no start or end dates. "Every forty minutes" without a first sailing doesn't let anyone plan a trip. "By daylight" leaves the rider to guess whether the last boat is at 6 pm or 9 pm, and those hours shift across the season. The viewer will plan around these, so the vagueness matters. The eye path also dead-ends at the intro, with nothing below it. Fix: put the actual times and dates right under the intro: first and last departure from each landing, the season's start and end dates, and how evening times change. That's what fills the empty lower half. Also make the headline carry one fact the rider can use (when the service runs) rather than a scene-setting phrase.

**Smaller things.**
- The intro paragraph ends on "weather." alone on its last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rewrap the text or adjust the measure so the last line carries more than one word.
- The headline is long for its size: four lines at this scale. Once the times exist, a shorter headline, or the same one a step smaller, would leave room for them above the fold. (Taste.)

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three sizes with big steps (small bold grey eyebrow, very large heavy headline, regular body), and the eyebrow is set apart by colour and weight as well as size. Keep this structure.
- The intro paragraph is well set: comfortable size, line length near the top of the 45–75 range, open leading, and plain words that state the route and crossing time precisely.
- The single strong left edge at 120 px holds all three elements together. Hang the timetable from the same edge.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The headline collision could be a line-height value that only breaks at this viewport width or with a fallback font. Check it at other widths and confirm the intended font loaded.
