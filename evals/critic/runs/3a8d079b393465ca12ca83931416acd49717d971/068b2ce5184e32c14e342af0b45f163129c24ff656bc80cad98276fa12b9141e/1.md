**First read.** My eye lands on the big red tile with "Rose arch" under it, then moves up to "Garden show gallery" and across to the smaller tiles. It reads as the photo index for a garden show, with one featured exhibit and six smaller ones. Then it stops making sense: none of the tiles holds a picture. Each one is a flat block of colour with a pale circle, so this feels like a wireframe of a gallery, not a gallery.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page where visitors browse the garden show's exhibits by photo and pick one to look at.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but as shipped the piece fails its job, because a gallery with no pictures shows nothing. Fix the images first. Until real photos are in, nothing else on this page can be judged properly.

**What would move it most**
1. **There are no pictures.** All seven tiles are a flat fill with a centred circle. The viewer can't see a rose arch, an herb bed or a pond edge, so they have nothing to choose between except the captions and the colours. On a page whose only content is images, that's the whole job missing. It's either placeholder art or images that didn't load. It's only fine if this is a deliberate wireframe. If it's meant as the finished page, put real photography in every tile, cropped to the tile's shape. *Defect* (common sense; the checklist has no catalogued rule for this).
2. **A stray line at the foot of the page: "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size."** It reads as an internal note left in the build. It also tells the visitor something untrue, since the Rose arch tile is clearly larger. A public visitor who reads it learns that the page is unfinished. Delete it. *Defect* (common sense: a finished page carries no internal notes, and every element should do a job).
3. **Six strong, unrelated hues cover most of the grid** (red, green, blue, brown, mustard, purple). With no photos, these blocks are the loudest thing on the page. They're also arbitrary: green suits "Herb bed", but nothing about "Glasshouse" says mustard. Large areas of full-strength colour tire the eye and weaken emphasis (Itten, *The Art of Color*; Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). If the fills are stand-ins for photos, this goes away once the photos arrive. If they're meant to stay as backgrounds or loading states, use one light neutral tint for all of them, so the photos and the featured tile carry the colour. *Convention*

**Smaller things.**
- Hero tile: "Rose arch" over "Climbing roses over the east gate" is a good pairing of title and image in principle. With a blank red block, though, the image half of that idea is missing. That's solved by finding 1.

**What's working.**
- **One clear lead.** The Rose arch tile spans two of the three columns and two rows, and it gets the one large caption (bold heading plus grey line). The eye knows where to start and then moves naturally to the right column and down. Keep this one-dominant-plus-even-supporting structure when the photos go in.
- **A clean grid.** Three equal columns with the same narrow gutter run throughout. The hero's caption block ends level with the Pond edge caption, and every small tile repeats the same pattern of image, bold title and grey line. It reads as a system, not a collage.
- **Specific captions.** "Thyme, sage and chives", "Tomatoes in late July" and "Plum and pear trees" each tell a visitor what they'll actually see. They aren't generic labels, so keep writing them that way.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank tiles are images that failed to load. Check in a live browser and on a slow connection.
- Whether the tiles are links, and how they look on hover and focus.
- How the layout reflows on a phone, where the 2×2 hero needs a defined order.
