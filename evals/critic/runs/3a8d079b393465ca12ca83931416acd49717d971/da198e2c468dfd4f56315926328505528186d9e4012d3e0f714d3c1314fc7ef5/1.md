**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline "Plans for every size of business", drops to the three large prices ($9 / $24 / $58), then settles on the blue "Start free trial" button in the middle card. It reads straight away as a SaaS pricing page for an accounting or invoicing tool, aimed at small businesses. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. Both versions give the same first impression, because they differ in only one thing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it should help a small-business owner find the plan that fits and start a trial, ideally of the middle plan.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The layout, copy and hierarchy are the same in both. The only change is that the plan descriptions and feature lists are a darker grey in version 2. That is the text people use to choose a plan. In version 1 it looks pale enough to fall below the WCAG contrast minimum, and in version 2 it reads easily. The underlying page is solid, and version 2 has no major problem left.

**What would move it most**
1. **Feature and description text contrast (fixed in version 2).** This covers the grey text under each price ("For one person who sends a few invoices a month.") and the four feature rows in each card. In version 1 it looks like a mid-grey of roughly #7b7f88 on white, which I'd put around 4:1. That is under the 4.5:1 minimum for normal text in WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3 (Contrast (Minimum)). This is the content that tells someone which plan to pick, such as "Payroll for up to five people" or "Three bank feeds". Faint text makes it a chore to compare, and it fails readers with low vision or anyone on a glary screen. Version 2 darkens it to what looks like about #4b5563, comfortably above 4.5:1, and still lighter than the plan names and prices, so the hierarchy holds. **Defect** in version 1. Keep the version 2 colour, and check the exact ratio in the browser to confirm.

**Smaller things** (these apply to both versions)
- **Empty band at the bottom of each card.** There are about 60 px of empty space below each button inside the cards. It is space with no job, and it pulls the buttons away from the card edge. Trim it so the bottom padding matches the top, or use it for a line like "No card needed for the trial".
- **Runt in the "Growing firm" description.** "currencies." sits alone on the last line. Reword it, for example "For firms with several sites or currencies.", or rebalance the line so the paragraph doesn't end on a single word (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- **One clear primary action.** Only the "Small team" button is filled blue. The other two are quiet grey, so the page steers people to the middle plan without shouting.
- **Copy in the customer's own words.** Lines like "Payroll for up to five people", "Access for your accountant" and "cancel when you like" name concrete things the owner recognises. "Everything in Small team" saves people from re-reading the list.
- **A tight type scale and shared baselines.** There are about four levels: headline, price, plan name, and body. Prices, rules and buttons sit on the same lines across all three columns, so comparing plans across the cards is effortless.
