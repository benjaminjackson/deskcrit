**First read.** My eye goes first to the dark rectangle top left, then down to the bold headline, then to the black button. It reads as a bookshop's event page for an author reading. The bottom-left block is clear and warm, but the whole thing feels unfinished: a stock grey-person silhouette fills the biggest, darkest area, and the right half of the screen is blank.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop event page for a local bookshop, meant to get readers to book a free seat at an author evening.

**Verdict.** The copy and the text block do the job well, but the page as shown isn't ready to ship. The heaviest thing on it is a placeholder avatar, and the layout is one narrow column dropped onto a wide screen with nothing on the right.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture is a placeholder, and it's the loudest thing on the page.** The 600×330 near-black block at the top left has the strongest contrast on the screen, so it's seen first. All it shows is a generic brown head-and-shoulders silhouette, which is also low in contrast against the near-black behind it. So the first thing a visitor sees tells them nothing about Ines Varga, the novel or the island. The image takes the first glance from the headline and gives nothing back. *Defect.* If a real photo failed to load, this is fine once it loads; if it's the design, it isn't. Fix: use an author portrait or the cover of *The Salt Year*. Better still, use a picture of the island, so the image adds what the headline can't say: the setting the talk is about. If nothing is available, drop the block and let the headline lead.
2. **A phone column stranded on a desktop canvas.** Every piece of content sits in a column about 600 px wide on the left. The nav runs the full 1280 px, so nothing in the content area relates to its right edge. The roughly 630 px on the right and the band below the button are leftover space, not a composed open area. The page tips hard to the left and reads as if a sidebar failed to load. *Defect* (it looks broken), backed by convention: Arnheim on balance (a heavy side needs a counterweight) and Müller-Brockmann on one grid governing the page. Fix: give the right side a job. The obvious one is to put the image and the text block side by side (picture left, date, headline, body and button right), so the event fits in one balanced band. A second column with the practical details would also work (see 3).
3. **The facts someone needs to commit are missing.** The page gives the date, the start time and when drinks begin. It doesn't say where (which branch, an address, or even "at the shop"), how long the evening runs, or whether seats are limited. To find the venue, the reader has to leave for "Find us" and remember the date while they do. *Defect*, by Nielsen's recognition rather than recall. These are exactly the details people act on and hold you to. Fix: add a short details block with the place, the end time and the number of seats, ideally in the empty right column.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Events" looks the same as the other items, so nothing shows which section you're in. Mark it with one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Headline: *The Salt Year* is set in the same roman as the rest of the headline, so a stranger may not read it as a book title. Italicise it. Some house styles skip this, so it's taste.

**What's working.**
- The copy. "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor" tells you exactly what the evening is, and "Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm" answers the next question before anyone asks. Keep this plain, specific voice.
- "Book a free seat" is a strong ask. It names one exact action, and putting "free" on the button removes the main reason to hesitate. It is the only strong button on the page, which is right.
- The text stack has a clean scale: a small bold rust date, a big tight two-line headline, a body line of about 70 characters, then the button. Each level is clearly distinct and the spacing groups them well.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states; how the layout reflows at tablet width.
