**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands straight on **$184,320**. It's the one oversized figure, set in a double-width card, and from there the eye drops to the six smaller tiles and then the chart. Version 2 has no such entry point: seven equal tiles in a 4+3 grid, all at one size, so the eye scans left to right as if reading a table. It also catches on the empty eighth slot in the top right of the second row. Both read as a calm, clean monthly sales summary for a small retailer.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an at-a-glance month-to-date sales dashboard for the owner or manager of Tidewater Supply, where revenue is the number they check first.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. It decides that revenue matters most and shows it, and its card grid fills its rows cleanly. Version 2 makes every metric equal and leaves a hole in the grid. In both versions the remaining weak spot is the same: the weekly revenue chart has no axis or week labels.

**What would move it most**
1. **What changed between the versions: one figure dominates (V1) vs. seven compete (V2).** In V2, Revenue, Orders, Average order, Returns and the other three tiles share one size and weight, so nothing tells the viewer where to start. The headline number gets no more emphasis than "Items per order 3.4". V1 makes Revenue span two columns at roughly twice the type size, so the order of importance is plain (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: logical order shown by differences of size). V1's 3+4 layout also fills both rows. V2's 4+3 leaves an empty slot that looks like a missing metric or a failed load. *Defect* in V2, fixed in V1. Keep V1's structure.
2. **The chart can't be read as numbers (both versions).** "Revenue by week" has no value axis, no week labels and no dollar amounts. The viewer sees a shape but can't say what any week earned, or which weeks these are. There are eight bars, yet the page says "October so far", which is at most five weeks. With no baseline shown, the viewer also can't tell whether the bars start at zero; if they don't, the week-to-week rises look bigger than they are. Add week dates and a light value scale or direct value labels, start the bars at zero, and make the title match the range shown (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, 17.1). *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The chart's bars fill only the left half of a full-width card, leaving a dead right half that tips the lower page to the left. Either spread the bars across the card or narrow the card and use the space for something else. Taste/common sense (Arnheim on balance).
- The chart title says what the chart shows but not what it means. Something like "Revenue up 4 of the last 5 weeks" would let the viewer get the point without reading the bars. Taste.

**What's working.** Every tile gives its delta in context, such as "+12% on September" and "-0.4 points", so each number has a comparison. Keep that. The grey label, black figure and green delta in each card make a three-level scale with no near-duplicate sizes. The muted palette leaves green as the only signal colour.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Below the fold, check whether the chart's x-axis or week labels are simply cut off before treating finding 2 as confirmed.
