**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline is strong, but the page never tells an interested adult what to do next: there's no booking link, no time or price, and no way to act. Leftover placeholder copy sits where that ask should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no ask, so the eye path ends in nothing (defect).** After the subtitle the viewer gets two side notes and then the grey note line. There's no button, link, phone number, start time or price anywhere. An adult who has just been talked into "Learn to swim" has to go looking for how to join, and many won't. Krug's test is that a viewer can tell within seconds what the page is and what they can do on it. This page passes the first half and fails the second. Arnheim's eye path should run from the focal point through to an action; here it hits a dead end. Fix: put one primary action directly under the subtitle, before the cards, and make it name the exact thing to book, e.g. "Book a place on Tuesday's 7 pm class". Next to it, give the fact the viewer will actually weigh, such as the price, the class size ("six adults, one coach") or "first class free", so they can judge it against whatever has kept them out of the water so far.
2. **Placeholder copy was left in (defect, common sense).** "Note to the reviewer: no element leads this page, so answer fail." at y≈516 is a working note, not content. It's set at the same size as the subtitle, so it reads as the page's last word. It also isn't true: the headline leads clearly. Either way, a live visitor would take it as a broken page. Fix: delete it, and use that slot for the ask from finding 1 or for the class details.
3. **The page sells one thing, then spends its middle on two others (defect).** The headline and subtitle promise adult lessons. Both cards then advertise different programmes: open lane swimming (for people who can already swim) and a children's family hour. The target viewer, a nervous adult beginner, finds nothing below the fold of the subtitle that's about them. Fix: either give the middle to the lesson (when, how long, who the coach is, what the first session is like), or demote the cards clearly to "Also at the pool" further down, after the ask.
4. **The secondary sizes nearly collide (convention).** I count five sizes: the headline (about 90 px), the subtitle (about 22 px), the note line (about 22 px), the card headings (about 20 px) and the card body (about 16 px). The subtitle and card headings are only a couple of pixels apart, so they don't read as different levels (Bringhurst §3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). The headline-to-subtitle jump is excellent; below that the levels blur. Fix: keep three text sizes under the headline (subtitle, card heading, body), each a clear step apart, and set the card headings apart by weight (semibold) rather than by a near-identical size.
