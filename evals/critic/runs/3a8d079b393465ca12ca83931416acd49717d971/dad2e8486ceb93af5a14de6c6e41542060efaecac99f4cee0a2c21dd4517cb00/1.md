**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question "Save changes to the shared calendar?", reads the one line under it, and drops straight to the filled blue "Save changes" button at bottom right. It reads at once as a confirmation dialog in a calendar or community-scheduling app. It feels calm, clean and competent. On the way to the buttons the eye passes two blue text links, "Export as file" and "Discard changes", that look the same, so it reads them as two harmless options of equal weight.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop web confirmation dialog for someone who edits a shared calendar, so they can publish their edits knowingly or back out safely.

**Verdict.** This mostly does its job. The question, the consequence and the main action are clear and in the right order. The one thing in the way is "Discard changes". It throws away the user's work, yet it sits in the action row looking just as harmless as "Export as file", so one mis-click loses every edit.

**What would move it most**
1. **The destructive action looks harmless and sits among the safe ones (defect).** "Discard changes" is the second text link in the button row, one gap from "Keep editing". It has the same blue as "Export as file" and nothing marks it as the action that destroys work. A user who wants out and reaches left of "Keep editing" can lose all their rehearsal-time edits. Apple HIG and Material Design both say destructive actions should be visually separated from safe ones and styled as destructive. Raskin ("Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo") argues for offering undo instead of relying on a warning. Fix: make discard look different from export, with a destructive colour or a clearly distinct treatment, and move it away from the save/keep-editing pair, for example to the far left with a clear gap. Make sure it is never the default action. If you can, back it with undo.
2. **A side task sits in the decision row (convention, Nielsen on progressive disclosure).** The dialog asks one yes/no question: publish these changes or not. "Export as file" answers neither, but it gets the first slot in the row. That makes four choices where two or three are needed, and it puts the export link right beside the destructive link, which feeds finding 1 (two look-alike links read as a pair of equal, safe options, so the similarity makes a group that isn't real). Fix: take export out of this dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu. That leaves Discard / Keep editing / Save, which is easier to separate.

**Smaller things.**
- The label "Export as file" starts about 30 px to the right of the left edge shared by the heading and body text (x≈280), because of the text button's padding. It looks like a near miss, not a choice. Either align the first label's text with that edge or move the destructive action somewhere clearly different, which finding 1 suggests anyway.
- Taste: "Save changes" undersells what the button does. The body says the times go out to everyone who follows the calendar, so a label like "Save and share" would say so at the moment of commitment.

**What's working.**
- The copy does its job. The heading is a direct question the buttons answer, and the body line tells the user the consequence in their own terms ("shown to everyone who follows this calendar"). Keep that sentence.
- There is a clean three-step hierarchy: the bold heading, a regular-weight body line of about 75 characters, then a single filled primary button. Only one action is styled as primary, so nobody has to hunt for it.
- The restraint: one blue, one neutral range, a light shadow, generous padding. Nothing competes with the question.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Default focus and focus styling: check that keyboard focus opens on "Save changes" or "Keep editing", never on "Discard changes", and that the focus ring is visible on the text buttons.
- The page behind the dialog is blank white with no dimming overlay. That may be how it was captured. In a live browser, check that the dialog sits over a backdrop that shows it is modal.
