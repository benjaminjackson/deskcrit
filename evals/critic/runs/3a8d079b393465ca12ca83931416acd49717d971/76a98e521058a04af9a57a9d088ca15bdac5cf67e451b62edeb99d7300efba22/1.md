**First read.** The eye goes to "Landing stage rebuild" at top left, drops through the paragraph to the blue-grey illustration, then jumps right to the blue "Sign up" button. It reads as a news page from a ferry operator with a timetable-alerts sign-up next to it. It feels calm, tidy and sparse. Then it stalls on the form: two empty boxes with nothing readable above them, so you can't tell what to type.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public service page from a ferry operator that tells passengers about the rebuilt landing stage and gets them to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** The article half works, but the sign-up form doesn't. Its field labels, its helper line, the figure caption and the footer are set so small (about 3–5 px) that nobody can read them, so the one thing the page asks people to do comes down to guessing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form fields have no readable labels.** This is a defect. Above each of the two fields in the right-hand card sits a label only a few pixels tall. Even at full resolution its letters can't be told apart ("Your name" for the first, maybe; the second can't be read at all). So the viewer sees two blank boxes and has to guess whether they want a name, an email, a phone number or a stop name. Anyone who guesses wrong either gives up or sends the wrong thing. Captions and labels need to stay readable (Apple HIG puts the minimum at 11 pt), and each field needs a persistent visible label (Nielsen Norman Group on placeholder-only fields). Fix: set the labels at body size or one step below, in the body text colour, directly above each field. Because the boxes have no placeholders either, the label is the only clue, so make it say exactly what goes in ("Email address").
2. **All the supporting text is unreadable, at about 3–5 px.** This is a defect. It covers the caption under the illustration, the line under "Sign up" (it seems to say something like "We send one short email when the timetable changes. You can stop at any time"), and the footer line at bottom left. The helper line is the one that tells someone what they're agreeing to and how to stop, which is exactly what makes a cautious person willing to give a contact detail. At this size it does nothing, and it may even look like hidden small print. Fix: set the caption, helper and footer at one small size the eye can read, roughly 12–14 px. The type scale would then be H1, H2, body, one small size, with nothing below that.
3. **The ask is vague.** This one is about the copy. "Get timetable changes" and "Sign up" don't say which route, how the changes arrive, or how often. Read cold, "timetable changes" could mean the whole schedule or just disruptions. Fix: say what the viewer gets and for which crossing, for example "Email me when this crossing's times change", and make the button repeat that action rather than say "Sign up".

**Smaller things.**
- The boat in the illustration is a rounded rectangle in the same blue as the Sign up button, so it reads for a moment as a second button. Give the boat a colour of its own, or keep that blue for actions only.
- The illustration is flat grey and blue blocks with no detail. It reads as a wireframe placeholder, and it doesn't show what the text boasts about (wider ramps, covered seating, the kiosk). Use a photo of the new stage, or a drawing that shows the ramp meeting the deck. If the blocks are a stand-in for an image that hasn't loaded, check the live page.
- "Get timetable changes" names a benefit but not the scope (which route, by email or SMS). A heading that names the service would tell people what the box holds before they read the fields.
- Neither field is marked required or optional. Mark the required fields with an asterisk and a one-line legend, or mark the optional field "(optional)".

**What's working.**
- The article copy is concrete and speaks to the passenger: "so prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" turns a building fact into what it means for the reader. Keep that voice.
- The two-column split is clean. The article and the sign-up card each hold one group, sit on a clear grid, and leave generous margins. The big empty area below is calm, not unfinished.
- The Sign up button is the only saturated element on the page, so it's plainly the primary action.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the second field restricts input (email or phone type) and how it validates. I can't tell because its label can't be read.
- The focus and error states of the fields.
