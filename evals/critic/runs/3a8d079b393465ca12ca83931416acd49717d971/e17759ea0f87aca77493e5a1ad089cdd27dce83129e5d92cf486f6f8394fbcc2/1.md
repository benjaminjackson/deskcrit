**First read.** The serif headline lands first, then the eye drops to the tallest bar, 705 in Q3 2026, and reads the bars left to right as two humps, one per year. It looks like a page from a board deck: clean, calm, competent, a bit generic. The last bar, Q4 2026, reads as a real result. Nothing on the bar tells me it hasn't happened yet.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is one page of a board sales review. It shows directors the seasonal shipment pattern and where Q4 is heading, so they can discuss inventory and planning.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The chart type, labels and finding-as-title are right, and the page reads in seconds. The one real problem is that the forecast bar is drawn exactly like the actuals, so on a board page a projection reads as fact.

**What would move it most**
1. **The Q4 2026 forecast looks like an actual (defect).** The far-right bar (560) has the same solid blue and the same bold label as the seven measured quarters. The only signal is in the subtitle's parenthesis and the footnote, which most directors won't read. They will remember "Q4 came in at 560" and later hold the team to a number nobody measured. A forecast should look different from observed data (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). Draw that bar as an outline or hatched fill, put "Forecast" right under or above its label, and add a range (for example 530–590) if the September forecast has one.
2. **The headline tells the weaker half of the story (taste, on the copy).** "Shipments peak each third quarter" is true, but the bars show something a board cares about more: every 2026 quarter beats the same quarter of 2025. That's 410→455, 520→590, 610→705, and 480→560 forecast, about 11–16% growth. The headline leaves directors to work that out by matching bars across a gap of four, and many won't. Also, "each third quarter" rests on only two Q3s, which is thin evidence for a pattern. Lead with the point the board should act on, for example "Every quarter is up on last year; Q3 is still the peak." Or put the year-on-year change on the 2026 bars so directors don't have to compare them by eye.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle says "Q4 2026" twice in one sentence ("eight quarters to Q4 2026 (Q4 2026 is the forecast)"). Rewrite it as "Q1 2025 to Q4 2026; Q4 2026 is a forecast."
- If the claim is meant as a pattern, it rests on only two years. If earlier years are available, add them or show them as faint context. Otherwise soften the claim to "both years."

**What's working.**
- The values are written on the bars, the y-axis starts at zero, and the gridlines are faint. Directors get exact figures without having to read the axis. Keep that.
- The title states the finding and the chart title plus "Thousands of units" give the unit. The page explains itself, which is right for a document read cold before a meeting.
- The type scale is tight: one serif headline, one sans for everything else, and weight rather than extra sizes separating the chart title from the subtitle.
