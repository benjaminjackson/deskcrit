**First read.** In both, the eye lands on the bold title "Departures from Corn Exchange", drops to the bold times in the left column, then catches the two red words, "4 min late" and "Cancelled". It reads as a bus stop departure board for a screen or web page. It feels calm and plain. In version 1 the black grid of boxes comes forward before the data does. Version 2 reads as a list of departures, and version 1 reads as a spreadsheet.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a live departure board for people waiting at Stand C. They need to find their bus, see when it leaves, and spot any disruption at a glance.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The two are the same in text, type, colour and spacing. The only change is that version 2 drops version 1's black outline round every cell and keeps pale horizontal rules between rows. So the times, destinations and status are now the darkest thing on the page, rather than the lines. Both versions still make the viewer do sums for a delayed bus, and they give a cancelled bus the same weight as one that is running.

**What would move it most**
1. **What changed: the cell grid (version 1 → version 2).** Version 1 draws a near-black, roughly 1 px box round every cell. The vertical rules add nothing the column gaps don't already show, and they break each row into five compartments. Your eye stops at each line instead of running across from time to destination to status, which is the one movement this table exists for. The rules are about as dark as the text, so they compete with it. Version 2 makes exactly the fix the canon asks for: it removes the vertical rules and turns the row dividers pale grey. Lupton, *Thinking with Type*: the rules in a table "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell". Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 4–5, on non-data ink. *Convention, and in version 1 a defect that slows the read.* Keep version 2's treatment.
2. **The delay makes the viewer do the arithmetic (both versions).** The 14:11 row says "4 min late" but the Time column still says 14:11. Someone waiting has to add 4 minutes in their head, and anyone who only scans the bold time column will think the bus is due at 14:11. The time is what people act on, so this is a *defect*. Show the expected time ("Expected 14:15") in the status or in the time cell, and keep the scheduled time as secondary.
3. **A cancelled bus looks like a running one (both versions).** The 14:40 route 19 to Northgate row has the same bold time, destination and weight as every running bus. Only the red word at the far right says it isn't coming. A viewer who scans the left two columns will plan around a bus that doesn't exist. *Defect.* Make the whole row read as cancelled: mute it or strike through the time, so the left side of the row carries the news too.

**Smaller things.** None beyond the above.

**What's working.**
- **Version 2's pale row rules.** They group each departure as one line without fencing it in. Keep them.
- **Bold times in the first column.** They give a clean scan line down the left, and the figures line up.
- **Red used only for the two disruptions, always with words.** Colour is never the only signal, so colour-blind viewers get the same message. The colour stays reserved for news.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the board refreshes itself and how stale "Updated 14:02" gets. If the data is not live, the viewer can't trust the status column.
