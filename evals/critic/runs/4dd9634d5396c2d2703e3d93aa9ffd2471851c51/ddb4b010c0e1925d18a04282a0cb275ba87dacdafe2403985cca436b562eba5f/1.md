**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: the basket page of a small online shop, where a shopper checks what they're buying, adjusts it, and goes to checkout.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the checkout path is obvious, but the core job of a basket isn't done: the shopper can't see how many of each item they have, and the remove button sits flush against the + button. Fix the row controls and this is a solid page.

**What would move it most**
1. **No quantity is shown on any line (defect).** Each row has − and + buttons with nothing between them, so the shopper can't tell whether they have one tin of lapsang or three. They also can't tell whether "$24.00" is the price of one or the line total. They have to work it out from the summary: $24 + $14.50 + $32 + $9 = $79.50, so presumably one of each. A basket's main job is to show its current state (Nielsen, heuristic 1 on visibility of system status and heuristic 6 on recognition rather than recall). Fix: put the quantity between − and +, ideally as an editable number. Where the quantity is above 1, show the unit price under the line total, e.g. "2 × $12.00".
2. **Remove (×) is fused to the + button (defect).** In each row the × is the third segment of the same small bordered control, the same size and weight as + and −, with no gap. Reaching for + to add a second tin and slipping one segment removes the item. Destructive actions should be set apart from safe ones and labelled with their outcome (Apple HIG "Buttons"; Raskin, "Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo"). Fix: take remove out of the stepper and make it a separate text link, "Remove", under the product name or at the end of the row. Then give an undo ("Sencha removed. Undo") rather than a confirmation dialog.
3. **The stepper buttons are tiny and touching (convention).** Each segment looks about 24 px square, the bare WCAG 2.5.8 floor, with zero spacing. On a phone that is well short of 44 pt (Apple) or 48 dp (Material). This compounds finding 2. Fix: larger targets with space between them. Measure the rendered size to confirm.

**Smaller things.**
- **Product thumbnails are flat colour squares.** They show no product, so the shopper can't confirm by sight that this is the teapot they picked. If they're placeholders or images that didn't load, the fix is real product photos. If they're deliberate, they are spending space without doing a job.

**What's working.**
- **One unmistakable primary action.** The solid near-black "Check out" button is the only filled element on the page, so there's never a question about what to do next. Keep it the only one.
- **The summary panel.** The pale tint holds exactly one group. Total is bolded rather than enlarged, and shipping is itemised, so the total has no surprise in it.
- **Clean row structure.** The name is set heavier than the size or pack line, prices are right-aligned to one edge, and the hairline rules are just strong enough to separate the rows. Keep this pattern when you add the quantity.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the quantity updates the total live.
- Whether there's an undo after removing an item.
- How the two columns stack on a phone, where the target sizes matter most.
