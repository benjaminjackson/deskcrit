**First read.** The eye lands on "Your basket", runs down four tidy rows, then jumps to the black Check out button on the right. It's clearly a tea shop's basket page, and it looks calm, neat and trustworthy. The second look catches the problem: none of the rows says how many of the item you're buying.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop basket page where a returning or first-time shopper checks what they're buying, adjusts it, and moves on to checkout.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the path to checkout is obvious. But a basket's one job is to confirm what you're buying, and this one never shows the quantity. Fix the quantity control before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no quantity anywhere on the page (defect).** Every row has a − + × cluster (around x 620–690) with no number in it, and the rows have no "Qty" anywhere else. The shopper can't tell whether they have one tin of lapsang or three. They also can't tell whether $24.00 is the unit price or the line total, because the four prices add up exactly to the $79.50 Items figure. That only works out if every quantity is 1, and nothing on the page says so. Pressing + changes a number you can't see, so the only feedback is the price changing, which can read either way. This breaks Nielsen's first heuristic (visibility of system status) and the convention shoppers know from every basket: − [n] +. Fix: put the count between − and +, and if quantities can go above 1, show "$12.00 each" under the line total.
2. **Remove is fused to the quantity buttons (defect).** The × sits flush against + in one bordered strip, all three the same size and weight. Someone reaching for + to add a second sencha is one small slip away from deleting the line, and nothing on the page suggests there's an undo. The HIG, Material and Raskin's "Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo" all say the same thing: set destructive actions apart from safe ones, name them, and offer undo. Fix: move remove out of the stepper, as a text link ("Remove") under the product name or a separate icon with clear space around it, and show an undo line in the row after removal.
3. **The controls look tiny (defect).** The − + × buttons look about 24 px square and touch each other, which is at the WCAG 2.5.8 minimum with no spacing to spare. They're also the only controls on a page whose whole purpose is editing. Measure them in the browser. Either way, the fix for #1 should make them bigger: a stepper around 32–36 px tall with the number in the middle.

**Smaller things.**
- Thumbnails (x 120–192) are flat colour squares, not product photos. If the images didn't load, check that. If they're placeholders, ship real photos, because shoppers use the picture to confirm "yes, that's the teapot I picked".
- The Total row in Summary is the same size as Items and Shipping and is set apart only by bold. Give it a step up in size, or a rule above it, so the number you're about to pay is the most prominent figure in the panel.

**What's working.**
- There's a single primary action: the dark, full-width Check out button is the only filled control, so there's never any doubt about the next step. Keep it that way when you restyle the stepper. The stepper should stay outlined.
- The rows are well set: bold name, quiet variant line ("100 g tin", "600 ml") and right-aligned prices separated by thin rules. They scan quickly, and the variant line answers "which size did I pick?" before anyone asks.
- The two-column split with the summary in a light grey panel keeps the money in one place, and its top lines up with the heading's band.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether pressing + or × gives feedback (the total updating, an undo prompt). Also focus visibility on those small buttons.
- Whether the summary panel stays in view when the basket scrolls.
