**First read.** The eye goes to the black "Check out" button, then to "Your basket", then down the four rows. It reads as a tidy, ordinary checkout basket for a small tea shop. It feels calm and competent, and also generic. On a second look the rows have a gap: there is a − + × control but no number in it, so I can't tell how many of anything I'm buying.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the basket page of a small online tea shop, where a shopper checks what they're buying and how many, adjusts it, and goes to checkout.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the checkout path is obvious, but the page doesn't do its main job. It never shows the quantity of each item, and it hides "remove" inside the quantity stepper. A shopper can't confirm the one thing a basket exists to confirm.

**What would move it most**
1. **No quantity is shown anywhere.** *Defect.* Each row's stepper (about x 620–690) is just −, +, × with no value between − and +. Nothing in the price column shows a count either. The only clue is arithmetic: $24 + $14.50 + $32 + $9 = $79.50, so everything is presumably one of each. The shopper has to do that sum to find out, and once they press + they can't tell what changed except by watching the price. The basket fails to show its own state (Nielsen, heuristic 1: visibility of system status). It also breaks the convention every shopper knows, where a stepper shows its number. Fix: put the current quantity between − and +, ideally as an editable field. If the price on the right is a line total, add the unit price under the product name ("$12.00 each") once the quantity can be above one.
2. **"Remove" is fused onto the quantity control.** *Defect.* The × sits in the same bordered strip as + and −, the same size and weight, with no gap. That makes it look like a third quantity step, and a slightly missed click on + deletes the item instead (Gestalt grouping, Wertheimer/Arnheim; Apple HIG and Material on destructive actions). Fix: move remove out of the stepper, as a text link ("Remove") under the product name or at the far right with clear space around it, and offer an undo after it fires rather than a confirmation dialog.
3. **The stepper targets look tiny and touch each other.** *Convention.* Each segment looks about 24 px square, right at the WCAG 2.5.8 minimum with no spacing to spare. On touch screens that's far below 44 pt (Apple) and 48 dp (Material), and it makes the mis-tap in finding 2 likely. Measure them in the browser. Fix: larger segments, which you get anyway once the quantity field from finding 1 sits in the middle.

**Smaller things.**
- The product images are flat colour tiles with no product in them. They look like placeholders. If they're real, they tell the shopper nothing. A small photo of each tin, pouch and teapot lets the shopper check the item at a glance. *Common sense.*
- "Items" in the summary is vague next to a dollar figure. "Subtotal" is the word shoppers expect. "Single" as the scoop's variant reads oddly; "1 scoop" or nothing at all would be clearer.
- The summary doesn't say what shipping covers or when it arrives. One line ("Standard, 3–5 days") would make the $5.00 something the shopper can judge. *Taste.*
- The look is competent but generic: apart from the wordmark, nothing says *tea shop*. That costs little on a basket page but nothing here is memorable. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- There is exactly one strong action. "Check out" is the only dark, filled element on the page, so the next step is never in doubt. Keep it the only one.
- The type is plain and the scale is tight: roughly four sizes (page title, summary/product names, prices, details), with weight rather than extra sizes doing the fine work. Prices are bold and right-aligned on one edge, which makes them easy to scan down.
- The two-column layout follows the convention, with the item list on the left and the totals and action in a tinted panel on the right. The rows are evenly spaced by hairlines rather than boxes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What happens when + or × is pressed: whether the total updates, and whether remove can be undone.
- Keyboard focus on the stepper segments.
- How the right-hand summary reflows on a phone, and whether "Check out" stays within reach there.
