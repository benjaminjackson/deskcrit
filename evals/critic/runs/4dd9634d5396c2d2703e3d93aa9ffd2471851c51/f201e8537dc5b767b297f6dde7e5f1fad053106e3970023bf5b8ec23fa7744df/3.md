**First read.** My eye goes first to the two black buttons: "View site" in the top right, then "Publish" below the editor. It reaches "Edit post" and the body text after that. It reads as a plain, tidy editor for whoever writes the parish newsletter. It feels calm, competent and generic, like a template's editing screen.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the screen where a parish volunteer, probably not technical, writes a newsletter post and sends it out to parishioners. Correct me if that's wrong.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly works, but it doesn't make publishing feel safe. It never says what this post is, what state it's in, or what "Publish" will do. For a volunteer about to put something in front of the whole parish, that's the gap that matters, and the equal-weight "View site" button makes it worse.

**What would move it most**

1. **The screen doesn't show the post's state or what "Publish" commits to (defect).** "Edit post" names no post, and there's no title field. Nothing says whether this is a draft or already live, or when it was last saved. "Publish" can be read two ways on a *newsletter*: it puts the post on the website, or it emails every subscriber. The volunteer can't tell which, and the email reading can't be undone. They'll either hesitate or send something by mistake. This is Nielsen's first heuristic (visibility of system status): the screen should say what is happening and where the user is. Fix: show the post's status and save state near the heading, such as "Draft, saved 2 minutes ago". Make the button say what it does, such as "Publish to site" or "Publish and email 240 subscribers", whichever is true.

2. **There's no title field (defect, unless the title is set elsewhere).** A newsletter post needs a headline, and every list or email preview will show one. Here the editor opens straight into body text with no title and no label. Either the volunteer has to remember to set it on another screen, or the post goes out untitled. A labelled field should sit above the editor (Wroblewski and NN/g: every field gets its own visible label). If the title really is set elsewhere, this screen needs to show it.

3. **Two primary buttons compete (convention, and it costs something here).** "View site" in the header and "Publish" are the same filled black, so the page has two calls to action. The header one is higher, so it's seen first, and it leads *away* from unsaved work. Material Design and Apple HIG both say one action per view is the filled one. Keep Publish filled. Make "View site" a text link or outlined button, and consider naming it "Preview post" if that's what people actually need before publishing.

4. **The editor's lines are too long (convention).** Body text runs the full 860 px width of the editor, about 110 characters a line. Bringhurst's comfortable range is 45 to 75. The writer reads their own draft back in this box, and long lines make proofreading slower and less reliable. Cap the text column at roughly 65 to 70 characters inside the editor. The box can stay wide.

**Smaller things**
- Toolbar targets look about 20 px tall and are packed together. "H1" and "H2" sit with no gap and read as one control. Measure them against WCAG 2.5.8's 24 px, then give each button its own hit area and some spacing.
- The toolbar labels are developer shorthand: "</>", "H1 H2", a bare "S" for strikethrough, and a lone curly quote. A parish volunteer won't know what "</>" or "H1" does (Nielsen 2). Use "Heading" and "Subheading", drop the code button, and add tooltips at the least.
- The screen doesn't show how this post fits into the rest: there's no "All posts" link or breadcrumb back to the list (Norman, conceptual model). Add one beside or above "Edit post".

**What's working**
- Save draft is outlined and Publish is filled, sitting together at the bottom right where the eye ends up after writing. That pairing is right, so keep it.
- The calm type and plain palette keep the focus on the volunteer's words. The body text is a comfortable size and leading with good contrast.
- The editor's tall, empty writing area invites people to write. That empty space is deliberate, not left over.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether autosave exists, and what Publish does after the click (a confirmation, a preview).
- Hover and focus states on the toolbar and buttons, and whether the toolbar has keyboard shortcuts.
