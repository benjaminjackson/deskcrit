**First read.** The eye lands on "Edit post", drops to the dark Publish button bottom right, then gets pulled back up to the matching dark "View site" button in the header. The text in the middle reads easily. It looks like a calm, plain editor for a small community newsletter, run by volunteers, and it feels tidy and harmless. What's missing only shows on the second look: there's nothing that says which post this is, whether it's live, or what Publish will send out.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a simple CMS screen where a parish volunteer, probably not technical, writes a newsletter post and publishes it.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and the writing area is pleasant, but it doesn't tell the writer what they're about to publish. There's no title field and no draft/live status, and the header link is styled as heavily as Publish. Fix that before polishing anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The writer can't tell what this post is or what state it's in.** Defect. "Edit post" (`h1`) doesn't name the post, and there's no title field, only a body box. Nothing says whether this is a draft or already live, or when it was last saved. A volunteer who clicks Publish doesn't know what headline readers will see, or whether they're overwriting a live post. Someone who comes back later can't confirm their draft saved. This breaks Nielsen's heuristics: show the current state (1), and keep context on screen instead of making people remember it (6). Fix: add a labelled title field above the body. Add one quiet status line near the heading or the buttons, such as "Draft · saved 2 minutes ago" or "Published 3 May". Let the heading carry the post's name.
2. **"View site" looks as important as "Publish".** Convention (Material Design, Apple HIG, NN/g: one filled primary action per view). Both are filled #1f2328 buttons with white 15 px bold text. Save draft, the outlined button, is correctly lower. So two buttons claim "main action", and the one that just leaves the page sits first in reading order, top right. A nervous volunteer looking for the safe next step gets two equally loud answers. Fix: drop View site to a text link or outlined button. If it's meant as "preview this post", say so ("Preview post").
3. **The formatting toolbar is too small, doesn't look clickable, and is labelled in symbols.** Defect for this audience.
   - **Too small:** all ten toolbar buttons are 20×20 px (`div.bar > button`), under the WCAG 2.5.8 minimum of 24 px. The facts file flags nine of them, and the tenth (</>) is the same size. They sit 2 px apart, so people who are older or less steady with a mouse will hit the neighbouring button.
   - **Don't look clickable:** they're bare bold glyphs with no button outline.
   - **Symbols:** B, I, U and S are all set in the same bold, so I isn't italic, U isn't underlined and S isn't struck through, and they don't preview what they do. "1.", "•", "“" and "</>" are developer shorthand. A parish volunteer won't know that "</>" means code, and has no reason to need it. That goes against Nielsen 2, matching the audience's words.

   Fix: make the targets at least 24 px with a visible button shape or a pressed state. Draw I, U and S in their own styles. Swap the cryptic glyphs for clear icons with labels or tooltips, and drop or hide H1 and the code button.

**Smaller things.**
- Body line length is about 110 characters across the 860 px editor. Bringhurst's range is 45–75, and long lines make your own text harder to proofread. Narrow the writing column to about 680 px, or add more side padding.
- The editor's border looks very pale grey, likely under the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on input edges. Darken it a step. The toolbar band partly makes up for it.

**What's working.**
- Calm, readable writing area: 16 px Inter at 1.6 line height, dark text on white (15.8:1). Keep the type and the generous padding inside the box.
- Save draft (outlined) next to Publish (filled) is the right ranking for that pair. Keep it once View site is toned down.
- Toolbar grouping: thin dividers split inline styles, headings, lists/quote and code. That logic is right; it just needs bigger, clearer buttons.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the toolbar and buttons. Check them with the keyboard.
- Whether keyboard shortcuts (Ctrl/Cmd+B) work and whether the toolbar has tooltips.
- The body field is measured as a plain `textarea`, which can't show bold or headings. Check that the toolbar actually formats text rather than inserting markup.
- What Publish does: whether it asks for confirmation or shows a preview first.
