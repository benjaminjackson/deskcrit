I ran the full checklist on the parish newsletter's "Edit post" screen. The page is tidy and calm, but it has gaps that will catch out a non-technical editor.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Edit post", drops into the two paragraphs about the spring fair, then goes to the black Publish button. The black "View site" button in the top-right corner pulls almost as hard. This reads as a simple publishing tool for a village or church newsletter. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. But a few seconds in I still can't tell which post this is, what it's called, or whether it's already live.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop screen where volunteer parish editors, who aren't technical, write a newsletter post and publish it.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and quiet, but it doesn't fully do its job. A post with no title, and no sign of whether it's a draft or live, leaves the editor publishing blind. Fix that before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The post has no title field, and nothing on the page says which post this is.** *(Defect.)*
   - The only field is the body box. "Edit post" names the screen's job, not the post.
   - An editor coming back to this has nothing to confirm they opened the right post. When they click Publish, they can't tell what headline readers will see, or whether one exists at all.
   - Every field needs its own visible label (Wroblewski; NN/g), and a heading should say what its section holds (WCAG 2.4.6).
   - **Fix:** add a labelled "Title" field above the body, and make the page heading carry the post's name (e.g. "Editing: Spring fair on the green").

2. **Nothing shows the state of the post.** *(Defect, Nielsen heuristic 1: visibility of system status.)*
   - "Save draft" and "Publish" sit side by side with no status anywhere: no "Draft" or "Published" tag, no "Saved 2 minutes ago".
   - The editor can't tell whether readers can already see this text, whether Publish will push changes to a live post, or whether their last edit was saved. For a volunteer who edits now and then, that's the thing they'll worry about most.
   - **Fix:** put a status line near the heading or next to the buttons, showing draft or published and when it was last saved.
   - If the post is already live, relabel the main button "Update post" so it says what it will do.

3. **Two buttons both look like the main action.** *(Convention: Material Design, Apple HIG, NN/g on button emphasis.)*
   - "View site" in the header is filled with the same `#1f2328` as Publish (`background`, `a` and `div.foot > button:nth-of-type(2)`), at the same 15 px bold.
   - On first look the eye goes to it as often as to Publish. Leaving the editor is styled as strongly as committing the work.
   - **Fix:** make "View site" an outline or text link, so Publish is the only filled button on the screen.

4. **The formatting toolbar is too small, and its labels are hard to read for this audience.** *(Defect, WCAG 2.5.8 and Nielsen heuristic 2; the third point is taste.)*
   - **Size:** each button is 20×20 px (`targets`, `div.bar > button`). That breaks the 24 px minimum for nine buttons (`violations`), and they're packed only 2 px apart. Older parish volunteers using a trackpad will hit "I" when they mean "U".
   - **Jargon:** "H1 H2" and "</>" are web jargon. A parish editor thinks in "Heading" and "Subheading" and has no use for a code button.
   - **Letters don't show their effect:** "I" isn't italic, "U" isn't underlined, and "S" looks like any other bold letter. "S" in particular could mean strikethrough, small, or anything.
   - **Fix:**
     - Make the buttons at least 24 px (ideally about 32 px).
     - Show each letter in its own style, or add tooltips and labels.
     - Rename H1/H2 in plain words, and drop the code button unless someone here actually needs it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line length:** text in the body box runs about 110 characters per line (I counted the first line by eye; the facts file couldn't measure the textarea). That's well past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column inside the box, or the editor will lose their place re-reading their own draft.
- **Near-miss alignment:** the toolbar "B" starts about 6 px left of the body text. Line them up on one edge so it doesn't look like an accident.
- **No way back:** there's no Cancel or back link to a list of posts; "View site" is the only exit. Add a "← All posts" link.
- **Pale border:** the body box's grey outline looks well under 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11; this was judged by eye, not measured). The toolbar tint and the text still mark the box, so it costs little here. Darken it a step anyway.

**What's working.**
- **One family, few colours:** everything is Inter in a single near-black, at 15.8:1 contrast. That gives the calm, no-fuss feel a parish tool should have. Keep it.
- **Save draft vs Publish:** they're paired correctly, one outlined and one filled, right-aligned under the box where the eye ends up after writing.
- **Body text setting:** 16 px with 1.6 line height is comfortable to write in. The plain sentence case throughout suits the audience.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus:** I can't see whether the 20 px toolbar buttons show a visible keyboard focus ring.
- **Rendered output:** I can't tell whether formatting shows in the box as you type or only after publishing. If the box is plain text and the toolbar inserts markup, that's a much bigger problem for this audience. Check it live.
