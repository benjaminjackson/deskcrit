**First read.** The eye lands on the bold black headline. Then it drops to the byline, which looks crisper and darker than anything below it. The three paragraphs read as a pale grey block. It reads as a calm, well-made blog article for people who work on old walls. It feels quiet and trustworthy, but the article text itself looks washed out.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a how-to article page for hobbyists and smallholders, who should be able to read it start to finish and follow the steps.

**Verdict.** The page does its job, apart from one real defect. The text people came to read is the faintest thing on the page. The grey body text falls below the contrast minimum, and it makes the byline outrank the article.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is too pale to read comfortably. Defect.** All three paragraphs are set in #818181 on white, which is 3.9:1 contrast (`contrast`, `article > p:nth-of-type(2–4)`). They are flagged as violations against the 4.5:1 minimum for normal text in WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3. At 17 px regular, this is not large text, so the lower 3:1 limit doesn't apply. A reader following repair steps, maybe on a phone outside in daylight, has to work to read every line, and readers with low vision lose it. Darken the body text until it reaches at least 4.5:1. A dark grey around #4a4a4a–#555 keeps the soft feel and passes.
2. **The byline outranks the body. Defect.** The byline "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" is 15 px in #1f2328 (`p.by`), and the body is 17 px in pale grey. Only 2 px separates the two sizes, so size doesn't tell them apart. Colour does, but it points the wrong way: the metadata reads as more important than the article. This is the inverse of the usual step down from headline to text to metadata (Müller-Brockmann; Bringhurst on a deliberate scale). The fix for item 1 solves most of this. Then make the byline the quieter line: keep it at 15 px but give it a mid grey that still reaches 4.5:1, so the levels go headline, then body, then byline.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav (Stories / Guides / About) doesn't mark the current section, so a reader can't tell whether this piece sits under Stories or Guides. Mark the current item with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The byline date "March 14" has no year. For a practical guide, readers judge how current it is by the date. Add the year.

**What's working.**
- **The reading column.** It runs 64–65 characters per line (`characters_per_line`) with line height 1.65, inside Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Keep this measure when you darken the text.
- **The structure is disciplined.** There is one typeface (Inter), one left edge at x=370 shared by the headline, byline, and paragraphs, and plenty of space. The headline at 34 px bold clearly comes first, with nothing competing with it.
- **The copy is specific and well written.** The headline says exactly what the piece covers. The paragraphs give concrete actions in order ("mark every bulge with a stick", "set a through stone every metre") and tell the reader why ("the cheapest repair you will ever make"). Nothing here could sit on anyone else's page.
