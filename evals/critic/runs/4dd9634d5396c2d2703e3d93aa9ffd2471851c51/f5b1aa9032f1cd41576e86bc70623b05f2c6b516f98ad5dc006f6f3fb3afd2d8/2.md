**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline, as it should. Then it drops to the byline, which is the darkest small text on the page, and only after that reaches the body, which reads as a pale grey block. It feels like a calm, unfussy blog post about hands-on craft. The article itself, though, looks less important than the line telling you who wrote it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a long-form how-to article on a small editorial site, *Field Notes*, read on desktop by people who will actually go and fix a wall.

**Verdict.** The setting is sound: one clear headline, a good column width, generous leading and plain copy. But the body text is too faint, and that one choice drags down both readability and hierarchy. Darken it and this page is in good shape.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is too pale, and it ranks below its own byline. Defect.** All three paragraphs are set in #818181 on white, which measures 3.9:1 (`contrast`, `article > p:nth-of-type(2–4)`). That fails the 4.5:1 minimum for 17 px text (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3), and the measurement flags all three paragraphs. Readers with weaker eyes, a dim screen or glare outdoors will strain over the instructions they came for. Older readers are likely here, and the subject is outdoor work. The second cost is hierarchy. The byline is smaller (15 px) but near-black (#1f2328, 15.8:1), so the metadata outweighs the article. The order reads as headline, then byline, then the actual content, when it should run headline, body, metadata (Müller-Brockmann: each level should be distinct, and in the right order). Fix: bring the body close to the headline colour, around #1f2328 to #3a3f45, so it is at least 4.5:1 and in practice nearer 10:1. If the byline needs to step back, make it the grey one.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Stories / Guides / About" all look identical, so nothing shows which section this article belongs to. Mark the current section with one cue, such as bolder weight or an underline (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- Byline, "By Ellen Marsh, March 14": the date has no year. On a how-to, readers use the date to judge whether the advice is current, and without a year they can't tell if it's from this spring or six years ago. Add the year.

**What's working.**
- **The reading column.** It's 540 px wide, giving about 64–65 characters per line (`characters_per_line`), with a 1.65 line-height (`line_height_ratio`). That sits right in Bringhurst's 45–75 range, and the spacing suits the measure. Keep both when you change the colour.
- **One clear starting point.** The headline is 34 px bold against 17 px body. That's a decisive step, and its two lines are set tight (1.25), so it reads as one unit. With four sizes in total (34, 18, 17, 15), the type scale is simple.
- **The copy.** It's concrete and speaks to the reader ("Before you move a stone, walk the full run of the wall and mark every bulge with a stick"), and every paragraph ends on something you can act on. It's specific to its subject, not filler.
