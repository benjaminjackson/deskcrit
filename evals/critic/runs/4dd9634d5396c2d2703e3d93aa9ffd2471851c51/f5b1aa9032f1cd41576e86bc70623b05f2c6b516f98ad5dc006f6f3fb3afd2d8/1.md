**First read.** My eye goes to the bold black headline, then straight to the small black byline under it. Only after that does it reach the three paragraphs, which are the article itself. The page reads as a calm, well-made how-to post on a personal or small-publication blog. It looks quiet and trustworthy, but the body text looks washed out, as if it were greyed out or still loading. (Note: the facts file was opened at the same time as the image, so this first read wasn't fully blind. It describes the image alone.)

**What it's for.** My assumption: a long-form article page whose whole job is to be read comfortably to the end by someone repairing or curious about dry stone walls.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound and the writing is good, but the page fails its one job: the body text is set in a grey too pale to read comfortably. Because of that, the article sits below the byline in the order of what matters.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is too faint, and it is the article.** All three paragraphs are #818181 on white, measured at 3.9:1 (`contrast` for `article > p:nth-of-type(2)`, `(3)` and `(4)`; all three appear in the file's `violations`). That is below the 4.5:1 that WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 requires for 17 px regular text. People with low vision, readers on a bright phone outdoors, and anyone on a cheap screen meet a page that looks greyed out or disabled. Everyone else just has to work harder for 500 words. On a reading page this is not a side issue; it is the main surface. *Defect.* Fix: darken the body text until it clears 4.5:1. A near-black close to the #1f2328 already used for the headline and byline is the natural choice, with grey kept for genuinely secondary text.
2. **The byline outranks the article.** The byline "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" is 15 px in the dark #1f2328 (`p.by`). The body below it is 17 px but pale grey. The smaller, less important line looks stronger than the text it introduces, so after the headline the eye stops on the metadata, not the first sentence. Sizes don't help sort this out: the page uses 18, 17 and 15 px for logo, body, and byline/nav (`type_sizes`). These steps are too small to read as levels (Bringhurst, §3.1.1; Dair on weak contrast). *Defect* (the hierarchy is upside down below the headline). Fix: once the body is dark (finding 1), make the byline clearly quieter with a lighter colour than the body, so the order runs headline, body, metadata. Don't add another size.

**Smaller things.**
- Navigation: "Stories", "Guides" and "About" all look the same, so nothing tells the reader which section this article belongs to. Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Byline date: "March 14" has no year. On a how-to people will find through search for years, readers can't tell whether the advice is current. Add the year.
- Copy: "through stone" is used without explanation. A first-time waller won't know it means a long stone laid across the full width of the wall. Add a short clause the first time it appears.

**What's working.**
- The text column: 64–65 characters per line (`characters_per_line`) at 1.65 line height. That is right in Bringhurst's 45–75 range, with comfortable leading. Keep the measure and spacing exactly as they are when you change the colour.
- The headline: 34 px bold at 1.25 line height holds its two lines together. It says exactly what the piece is and when you'd need it ("after a hard winter"), so the right reader knows in one glance that this is for them.
- The writing: concrete, ordered, and in the second person ("Before you move a stone, walk the full run…"). It tells the reader what to do and why, and that is a big part of why the page works once it's readable.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav links, and how the column and header behave on a narrow screen.
