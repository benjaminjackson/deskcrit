**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is lighter than the byline (taste, supported by convention).**
   - **What I see:** The byline "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" is set near-black at what looks like about 15 px. The three paragraphs below it are a mid-grey, roughly #666, at about 17 px.
   - **What the reader gets:** The small metadata line looks firmer than the article itself, so the reader is pulled back to it. The grey body reads a shade soft and washed out over a long read.
   - **Contrast:** It probably still passes the 4.5:1 minimum (WCAG 1.4.3), so this isn't an accessibility failure. Check the exact value in a browser.
   - **Why it matters:** Each level should differ clearly from the next, and in one direction (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Dair on contrast). Here size says "body above byline" while darkness says the opposite. Refactoring UI's advice is to use lighter colour to push down secondary text such as metadata, not the main text.
   - **Fix:** Darken the body to near the headline's colour, say #222 to #333. Then make the byline the lighter, quieter line: the grey, smaller size, or both.
