**First read.** The eye lands on the row of four big numbers and moves across them evenly, because nothing stands out. 412, $9,860, 287 and 3 all look equally important, so the refunds number, which is the only one that asks for attention, gets the same weight as the good news. Then the eye drops to a calm, tidy list of orders. It reads as a bakery owner's weekly back office: quiet, competent and generic. The pale script logo almost fades into the white.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an admin home screen where the owner or counter staff of a small bakery (Larkspur) check how the week is going and see what has to be made or handed over next.

**Verdict.** It's clean and legible, but it reports instead of directing. Every number gets the same voice, and the order list leaves out what staff need to act on it (who, when, in what order). The biggest problem is that nothing tells the viewer what matters or what to do next.

**What would move it most**
1. **The four stat cards compete equally, and the one that needs attention is lost** (top row, y≈180–336). All four numbers share one size, weight and colour, so the eye has no place to start. Refunds "3 — All for late delivery" is the only thing on the page asking for action, yet it looks exactly like the healthy numbers. *Defect.* Make one thing lead (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size). On an ops screen, the first thing seen should be what needs attention: give the refund card a status treatment that doesn't rely on colour alone. Alternatively, make revenue the clear lead and set the other three back.
2. **Most headline numbers have nothing to compare against** (Revenue, Pickups, Refunds cards). Only Orders says "Up 38 on last week". "$9,860" and "Average order $23.93" don't say whether the week is good or bad, and "70% of all orders" is a breakdown, not a trend. *Convention* (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Put a change from last week, or a target, next to each number, the way the Orders card already does.
3. **"Recent orders" is the wrong label, the list is out of time order, and it's missing what staff need** (lower panel). The rows are upcoming pickups and deliveries, not recent orders. They run today 4:30 pm, Saturday, tomorrow 8:00 am, Friday 9:00 am, so whoever is preparing them has to sort them in their head. If today is Thursday, "tomorrow" and "Friday" are the same day written two ways. No row has a customer name or order number, so staff have to look up who the birthday cake belongs to somewhere else. *Defect* (WCAG 2.4.6 for the heading; Nielsen's recognition rather than recall for the missing customer). Fix:
   - Rename it to say what it holds, e.g. "Coming up".
   - Sort it by due time.
   - Write every date the same way (absolute day plus time).
   - Add the customer's name to each row.
4. **The nav doesn't show where you are** (top right). Orders, Menu and Staff all look the same, and none of them is this page. *Convention* (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist"; Nielsen heuristic 1). Add a Dashboard or Home item and mark the current item with one clear cue, such as weight, an underline or a background.

**Smaller things.**
- The refunds card states a cause ("All for late delivery") but gives no way to act on it. Link it to the three orders or the delivery schedule. This is common sense, not a cited rule.
- The logo is pale grey-blue script and looks washed out next to the near-black type, so the brand barely registers (taste). Give it a stronger tone if that's not its intended colour.

**What's working.**
- The plain-language order lines ("Birthday cake, lemon, serves twelve") read like a person talking, not a database. Keep that voice.
- "Up 38 on last week" is exactly the right pattern for a stat card. Repeat it on every card.
- There's one sans-serif face, a clear three-level type scale (page title, card label, number) and a shared left edge at 48/71 px. Keep that structure.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the stat cards and order rows can be clicked through to details. Nothing in the styling suggests they can, so check in the live app.
