**First read.** I see "Northwind Ferries" and a big headline, but the headline is grey and light, so it looks dimmed. The black "See the timetable" button pulls my eye down past it. It reads as a quiet, tidy homepage for a small ferry company, calm and believable, but generic. After a few seconds I still don't know where this ferry goes, and I'm not sure whether "six" and "eleven" mean morning or evening.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the desktop homepage of a local car-and-foot-passenger ferry, and it should tell someone arriving when the boats run and get drivers to book.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and well built, but the page doesn't do its job yet. The headline promises a timetable that the page doesn't show, never names the route, and gives its times without morning or evening. On top of that, the headline is styled to look switched off.

**What would move it most**

1. **The facts people act on are missing or can be read two ways (defect).** Everything a visitor would act on is in the copy, and it doesn't hold up for someone new to it:
   - "From the first boat at six until the last at eleven" has no am or pm. "Six" is surely morning, but "eleven" could be 11 am to a visitor who assumes winter service is short. Someone who reads it that way will plan around a ferry that has already stopped running.
   - The route is never named, so the page doesn't say *from where to where*.
   - "Winter timetable" gives no dates, so people can't tell whether it applies to the day they're travelling.
   - "Book a car space up to a day ahead" can mean that booking opens only 24 hours before sailing, or that you must book by the day before. A driver who reads it the wrong way either misses the window or doesn't book at all.

   The fix: name the route in the headline or the kicker, write the times as 06:00 and 23:00 (or 6 am and 11 pm), give the date range for the winter timetable, and rewrite the booking line so it has only one meaning, e.g. "You can book a car space from 24 hours before you sail."
2. **The headline doesn't lead (defect).** The h1 is the largest text (44 px, `font_size`, `h1`), but it's weight 400 in #7d858d grey (`color`, `h1`, contrast 3.74). Every other piece of text on the page is #1f2328 at 15.8:1. That makes the headline the lightest thing in view. The solid black button and the bold "Winter timetable" kicker out-pull it, so the eye skips the main message and lands on the button. The contrast does pass WCAG's 3:1 for large text, so this isn't a compliance problem. The problem is the hierarchy: one element should clearly be seen first (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). Set the headline in the same near-black as the text, and consider a medium weight, so that size and value both put it first.
3. **The page shows no sailings and gives drivers no way to book (common sense; my judgment, not a cited rule).** The headline is about times, but the next step is a click to another page. The page also tells drivers to book, yet the only button is "See the timetable", and the menu (Timetable, Fares, Help) has no Book link. That leaves a driver with nowhere to act. Two things would fix it: show the next two or three departures, or today's first and last boat, right on the page; and give drivers a direct "Book a car space" action. If booking is the main goal, it should be the primary button.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count six sizes (44, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15 px, `type_sizes`). Five of them sit within 4 px of each other, so they don't read as separate levels (Bringhurst, §3.1.1). Keep 44, 18 and 15, and separate the rest by weight.
- The headline breaks "the last at / eleven", leaving "eleven" alone on line three. That splits the phrase and leaves a one-word last line (Bringhurst §2.4.2). Rebreaking, or changing the width, would fix both.
- In the Dogs card, "lounge." sits alone on the second line. Tighten the copy or widen the measure.
- The 44 px Inter headline looks set at text tracking and reads slightly loose. Tighten the letter spacing a little (Bringhurst §2.1.1).

**What's working.**
- The grid is real. The blocks share three left edges (120/480/840, `left_edges`), and the rule over each card makes it read as one group without needing a box. Keep this structure through the revision.
- The card copy is concrete and answers questions people really ask: "Up to 2.4 metres high", "Free, and racks on both decks", which deck dogs can use. More of the page should be written like this.
- The hero paragraph is easy to read: 18 px, 70 characters per line, line height 1.6.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the menu and the button, and how the hero and the three columns reflow on a phone.
