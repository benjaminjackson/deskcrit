**First read.** The eye lands on the big grey headline, then drops straight to the black "See the timetable" button, which is darker than anything above it and wins on contrast. It reads as a ferry operator's homepage: calm, tidy, almost a wireframe. The headline looks faded, more like a subtitle or placeholder than the main message. Nothing on the page says where the ferry goes.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage of a small ferry operator. It's for drivers and foot passengers who need to know when boats run and whether they need to book.

**Verdict.** It's clean and nearly does its job, but it's held back by three things. The headline is set in the faintest colour on the page. The copy never names the route. And the one action that needs planning, booking a car space, has no button.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headline is the palest text on the page, so the message loses to the button.** The headline is 44 px regular weight in #7d858d, 3.74:1 contrast (`color`, `contrast`, `h1`). Every other piece of text is #1f2328 at 15.8:1. It passes WCAG's 3:1 for large text, so this isn't an accessibility violation. It's a hierarchy defect. Size makes the headline dominant, but its lighter value pulls it back, and the solid black button becomes the darkest, heaviest thing on the screen. Viewers take in "See the timetable" before "every forty minutes", and the grey makes the key fact look secondary or unfinished. Seen in greyscale, the structure runs button, then kicker and body, then headline, which is the wrong order (Albers; Refactoring UI on letting lightness carry structure). Fix: set the headline in the same near-black as the body. If it then feels too heavy, use a lighter weight, not a lighter tint. **Defect.**

2. **The copy never says where the ferry goes, and the times can be read two ways.** "Crossings every forty minutes" doesn't say from where to where, and neither do the logo, the kicker or the cards. A first-time visitor can't confirm they're on the right ferry's site. "The first boat at six until the last at eleven" has no am or pm and is spelled out in words. People will act on these times, so they need to be exact. Most people will assume 06:00 to 23:00, but someone planning a late return has to guess. The arithmetic doesn't quite work either: 40-minute departures from 06:00 hit 22:40 and 23:20, not 23:00. "Winter timetable" also gives no dates. Fix: name both ports in the headline or kicker, give times as numerals with a 24-hour clock or am/pm ("06:00 to 23:00"), and give the winter dates. **Defect.**

3. **The page tells drivers to book but gives them no way to do it.** The lede says "Book a car space up to a day ahead", but the only button is "See the timetable", which repeats the "Timetable" link in the nav. A driver reads that they should book, then has to guess that booking lives under Fares or Help. Fix: make the primary action the thing the lede asks for. Use "Book a car space" as the button, and if the timetable link is still needed, show it as a secondary text link next to the button. The headline already answers the timetable question at a glance. **Defect (common sense; no approved source).**

4. **It looks like a template, so it could be any operator on any coast.** The page is pure grey and black Inter with no picture, no colour and no sense of a place or a boat. Nothing tells the viewer this is a particular ferry, which also adds to the "am I on the right site?" doubt from point 2. Fix: one real image of the boat or the crossing, or one brand colour, would make it unmistakably this company. **Taste.**

**Smaller things.**
- The type sizes are muddled: I count six sizes (44, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15 in `type_sizes`). Five of them are within 4 px of each other, and the logo (19 bold) and card headings (17 bold) read as almost the same level. Keep about three sizes, 44 / 18 / 15, and get the remaining distinctions from weight (Bringhurst, 3.1.1).
- Two lines end on a lone word: "eleven" sits alone on the headline's third line, splitting "the last at / eleven", and "lounge." sits alone in the Dogs card. Rewrite or rebreak so the headline breaks at a phrase boundary and no line ends on a single word (Bringhurst, 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- There's one clear left edge at x=120 that runs from the logo through the kicker, headline, lede, button and first card. The three cards sit on an even 320 px grid with 40 px gutters, and their right edge lines up with the nav. Keep that grid.
- The lede is well set: 18 px at 70 characters per line and 1.6 line height.
- The three cards are short and useful. The rule above each card groups it cleanly, and each heading names exactly what its card covers (Cars and vans, Bikes, Dogs).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the nav and button, and how the layout reflows on a phone, where most foot passengers will check times.
