**First read.** The eye goes to the big headline because of its size, but it lands softly: the headline is grey, and the solid black "See the timetable" button and the bold black "Winter timetable" kicker pull at least as hard. It reads as a small ferry operator's homepage. It feels calm and tidy, but faint at the top and close to a template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a ferry company's homepage, for travellers who want to know when boats run, whether their vehicle, bike or dog can come, and how to travel. The main task is getting them to the timetable or to a booking.

**Verdict.** The page is clear and well spaced, but its most important line is its weakest. The headline is set lighter than everything around it, and the copy leaves a traveller unsure about the route, the dates and the booking rule. Fix the headline's value and the words before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is the lightest text on the page, so the page has no strong starting point.** The h1 is `#7d858d` at 3.74:1 (`color`, `contrast`, `h1`). Everything else is `#1f2328` at 15.8:1. It passes WCAG's 3:1 for large text at 44 px, so this isn't an accessibility failure. The problem is hierarchy. In grayscale the headline sinks behind the 15 px bold kicker and the black button, so the eye bounces between three things instead of reading the headline first and moving down. One element should clearly lead and the rest should step back (Tschichold, *The New Typography*), and value should carry the structure on its own (Albers; Refactoring UI). Fix: set the headline in the same near-black as the text, or darker. If you want it to feel lighter, drop it to a lighter weight rather than a lighter colour. *Defect.*
2. **The copy leaves a traveller unsure about things they'll act on.** Read as a stranger would:
   - The page never names the route. "Crossings every forty minutes" doesn't say from where to where.
   - "Winter timetable" gives no dates, so a visitor in March can't tell if it still applies.
   - "Book a car space up to a day ahead" has two readings. One is "bookings open no earlier than 24 hours before sailing". The other is "you can book any time until the day before". A driver who picks the wrong one either shows up without a space or tries to book a week out and finds they can't.
   - "six" and "eleven" are probably 6 am and 11 pm, but these are the times people plan around. Write them as 06:00 and 23:00.

   Fix: name the crossing in the headline or kicker, put dates on the timetable, and rewrite the booking line so it has one meaning, e.g. "Car spaces open for booking 24 hours before each sailing." *Defect.*
3. **The page tells drivers to book but gives them nowhere to do it.** The only action is "See the timetable". A driver who has just read "Book a car space…" has to guess that booking sits under Timetable or Fares. A ferry homepage's visitor most likely wants the next sailing and a way to book. Fix: give car booking its own visible route, a secondary link or button next to the primary one, styled lower. Showing the next departure in the hero would go further. *Common sense; no approved source.*
4. **Five text sizes sit within 4 px of each other.** The sizes are 15, 16, 17, 18 and 19 px (`type_sizes`): the cards' body text, the button, the card headings, the hero paragraph and the wordmark. Next to them is one 44 px jump. Steps that small don't read as levels, they read as accidents (Bringhurst, §3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). The 17 px bold card headings only differ from the 15 px text through weight anyway. Fix: keep about three sizes: 44, 18 for the lead paragraph, 15 for everything else. Let weight do the remaining work. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- The headline wraps to leave "eleven" alone on a third line, breaking inside the phrase "the last at eleven". Widen the measure slightly or rebreak it after "minutes," so it runs on two lines or breaks at phrase boundaries.
- In the Dogs card, "lounge." sits alone on a second line, while the other two cards stay on one line. Trim the copy ("Welcome on the open deck and rear lounge") or let the columns breathe.
- The 44 px headline looks like it uses Inter's default text spacing, so it reads slightly loose at display size. Tighten the tracking a little.
- In the header, the right edge of the "Help" text (~1146 px) misses the right edge of the card rules (1160) by about 14 px. That's a near-miss rather than a choice. Align the nav text to the content edge.

**What's working.**
- One left edge at 120 px carries the logo, kicker, headline, paragraph, button and first card, and the three cards sit on a clean 320/40 grid with matching baselines. The page feels orderly without a single box. Keep that.
- The three facts in the cards are concrete and useful: 2.4 metres, bikes free with racks on both decks, dogs on the open deck and in the rear lounge. That's the right kind of copy, and the headline should match it.
- The hero paragraph runs at 70 characters per line with 1.6 leading (`characters_per_line`, `line_height_ratio`, `section.hero > p:nth-of-type(2)`). That's comfortable reading.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus visibility on the nav links and the button. These are the main ways around the page, so check them by keyboard in a browser.
