**First read.** The eye goes straight to the row of four big numbers, landing hardest on "$9,860", then drops to the Recent orders list. It reads as a small bakery's weekly admin screen. It is calm, tidy and readable, but it looks like any dashboard template. The pale grey script logo looks switched off rather than branded.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since nothing was given: this is an internal dashboard for the bakery's owner and counter staff. It should show how the week is going and what has to go out the door next.

**Verdict.** It's clean and legible, and it does the "how's the week" half of its job. It fails the "what do I do next" half. The order list isn't sorted by when orders are due and doesn't name who they're for, and three of the four numbers can't be judged good or bad. It's a tidy report rather than a working tool.

**What would move it most**
1. **The order list doesn't tell staff what's due next or who it's for (defect).** The rows in "Recent orders" run today 4:30 pm, Saturday, tomorrow 8:00 am, Friday. That's neither time order nor obviously the order they were placed in. Someone at the counter has to read all four and sort them in their head to find the next job. No row shows a customer name, an order number or a price, so when someone walks in for "two sourdough loaves", nothing on screen confirms it's their order. The heading "Recent orders" names when orders arrived, but what the rows actually show is when they are due. **Fix:** decide what this list is for. If it's the work queue, call it something like "Due next", sort it by due time with the soonest at the top, and give each row the customer's name and an order number. That's common sense; Few and Wilke on ordering by a natural sequence such as time support it.
2. **Three of the four numbers have nothing to compare against (convention, Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design").** Only Orders says how it's doing: "Up 38 on last week". Revenue's sub-line ("Average order $23.93") is a different metric, not a comparison, so $9,860 could be a great week or a bad one. Pickups has a share but no trend. Refunds "3" has no earlier figure, which matters most here because it's the only card that suggests a problem. **Fix:** give every card the same kind of comparison as Orders: change against last week, or against a target.
3. **"This week" is ambiguous on the numbers people will act on (defect, copy).** The page doesn't say which days count as this week. That makes "Up 38 on last week" readable two ways: a partial week compared with last week's full total (which would make "up" understate things), or the same days compared with the same days. It's also unclear whether Saturday's delivery falls inside the totals. **Fix:** put the date range next to the heading (for example "Mon 16 – Sun 22 Nov") and say what the comparison covers ("38 more than the same days last week").

**Smaller things.**
- **No current-page marker in the nav.** "Orders", "Menu" and "Staff" all look the same, and this page ("This week") matches none of them. Mark the current item, or add a "This week" or "Overview" item and mark that (Nielsen 1; NN/g menu checklist).
- **The type sizes are too close together.** I count five: 34, 26, 17, 15 and 14 px (`type_sizes`). The "Recent orders" heading (17 px bold) and the card labels (15 px bold) are only 2 px apart. So are the card sub-lines (14 px) and the row text (15 px). Each pair reads as a mistake, not as two levels. Merge 17 into 15 and 14 into 15, and use weight or a grey to separate them where needed (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- **The page title is smaller than the numbers below it.** "This week" (26 px) sits under the 34 px figures, which is defensible, but at that size it barely registers as the page title. Either drop it to a quiet label or make it clearly the title.
- **The Refunds card hits a dead end.** "3 … All for late delivery" is the one alarm on the page, with no visible way to see which orders they were. Make the card or the line link to those orders.
- **The logo looks disabled.** The Larkspur mark is #a8b4bf at 2.11:1 (`a.logo`). It's exempt from the contrast rule, but it's the page's only colour and it reads as greyed out. Use the brand's real colour, or full-strength ink (taste).
- **The nav's right edge just misses the cards' right edge.** The "Staff" text ends around x 1218, while the cards end at 1231. Align the text, not the link's padding, to the card edge (Arnheim on near-misses).
- **Times are given with mixed precision.** "Saturday morning" sits next to exact times. If the bakery promises a delivery window, say the window ("Saturday, 8–11 am") so staff and customer mean the same thing.

**What's working.**
- **The four-card row has a consistent structure.** Each card goes label, big number, one plain-language line, on a shared grid (left edges at 71, 372, 673 and 974 px). That makes the week scannable in two seconds. Keep it.
- **The order rows are written in plain language.** "Birthday cake, lemon, serves twelve" is what a baker actually needs to read, with no SKUs or codes. Keep that voice when you add names and order numbers.
- **The text is easy to read throughout.** All text is 14 px or larger at 15.8:1 contrast, the hairline row rules are quiet, and the empty space is gathered at the bottom rather than scattered.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the order rows and the KPI cards are clickable; hover and focus states on the nav.
