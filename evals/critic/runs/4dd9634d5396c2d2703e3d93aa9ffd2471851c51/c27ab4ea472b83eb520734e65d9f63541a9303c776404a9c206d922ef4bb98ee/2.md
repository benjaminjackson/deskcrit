**First read.** The eye lands on the row of four big figures and moves left to right. $9,860 is the widest, so it holds the eye a moment longer. Then the eye drops to the order list. It reads as a small bakery's weekly overview for the owner. It is calm, tidy and easy to read, but it could be any shop's admin page, and nothing on it asks you to do anything.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: this is the home screen of a bakery's back office. The owner or a shift lead checks it each morning to see how the week is going and what has to go out the door next.

**Verdict.** It's clean and legible, and as a weekly report it nearly works. As the screen someone opens before a shift, it falls short. The order list is in no useful order and misses what staff need to hand an order over. The headline numbers mostly can't be read as good or bad. Those two things are the job, and both need work.

**What would move it most**

1. **The order list doesn't work as a to-do list.** The rows under "Recent orders" (y 435–600) run: today 4:30 pm, Saturday morning, tomorrow 8:00 am, Friday 9:00 am. That isn't sorted by due time, so staff can't scan top to bottom for what's next. The dates mix relative words ("today", "tomorrow") with weekday names ("Friday"). If today is Thursday, "tomorrow 8:00 am" and "Friday 9:00 am" are the same day, but the list makes you work that out. "Saturday morning" has no time, and this is a promise to a customer. No row has a customer name or a status (baked, packed, paid), so at the counter you can't tell whose two sourdough loaves these are. The heading is also wrong: these aren't recent orders, they're the next orders to fulfil.
   - Defect. Headings should name what their section holds (WCAG 2.4.6). Putting the next task in front of the user is common sense.
   - Fix: sort by due time, earliest first. Use one date format with a time on every row. Add the customer name and status. Rename the section to something like "Due next". The item and its due time also sit about 700 px apart across a 1138 px row, so the eye has to travel along the divider lines to pair them. Bringing the time closer to the item would help (taste).

2. **Three of the four headline numbers have nothing to compare against.** Only Orders has a comparison ("Up 38 on last week"). Revenue gives an average order value, Pickups gives a share of orders, and Refunds gives a reason. None of them says whether this week is better or worse.
   - The Refunds card is the one that should prompt action. It shows "3" at the same 34 px weight as everything else and offers no way to open those three late deliveries. Is three a lot? Was it zero last week?
   - Defect (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"): a bare number can't be read as good or bad.
   - Fix: give each card a change against last week, as Orders does. Let Refunds stand out, and make it clickable, when it rises above normal.

**Smaller things.**
- **Too many close type sizes:** I count five sizes (34, 26, 17, 15, 14 px). The 17 px "Recent orders" heading, the 15 px card labels and list rows, and the 14 px card subtitles sit too close together to read as separate levels (Bringhurst, scale). Drop the 14 and 17 sizes and use weight to tell the levels apart.
- **No current-page marker:** the nav (Orders, Menu, Staff) draws every item the same, so nothing shows where you are. This page isn't in the nav at all. Mark the current item, or add an "Overview" item (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g).
- **Washed-out logo:** the "Larkspur" script is pale grey (`#a8b4bf`, `contrast` 2.11, `a.logo`). Logos are exempt from the contrast rule, but this looks faded, not branded. It's also the only touch of identity on an otherwise anonymous page. Show it in its real brand colour, or darker (taste).

**What's working.**
- **Type and colour:** one family (Inter), near-black text at 15.8:1 contrast throughout, and no stray colour. The screen is calm and every word reads on first glance. Keep that.
- **"Up 38 on last week":** this is the right model for every card. It's a plain sentence with a comparison built in.
- **Layout:** the four-card grid lines up exactly with the list panel below (left edges at 71, 372, 673 and 974 px), and the whole overview fits on one screen without scrolling.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the order rows and KPI cards are clickable, and what hover or focus states they have. This matters for the Refunds card.
