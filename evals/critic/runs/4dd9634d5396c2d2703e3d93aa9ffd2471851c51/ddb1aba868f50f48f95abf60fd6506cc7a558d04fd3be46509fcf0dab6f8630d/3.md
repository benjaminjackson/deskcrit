**First read.** The eye goes to the two solid black buttons first: "View site" at the top right, then "Publish" at the bottom right. Only after that does it settle on "Edit post" and the text. It reads as a clean, plain editor for a small parish newsletter. It feels calm and tidy, and generic in a harmless way.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a screen where a parish volunteer, probably not technical, writes a newsletter post and publishes it.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The layout is calm, the text is easy to read and nothing is broken. What lets it down is that it doesn't tell the volunteer where they are or what "Publish" will do: there is no post status, no save state, no title and no way back to the other posts, and the most important button looks exactly like a navigation link.

**What would move it most**
1. **The screen doesn't show the post's state, which post it is, or where it lives (defect).** "Edit post" names no post. Nothing says whether this is a draft or already live, or when it was last saved. Nothing links back to a list of posts; the only way out is "View site". Someone who comes back to finish a post can't tell whether parishioners have already seen it, and can't easily get to their other posts. Nielsen's first heuristic (visibility of system status) asks that saved/unsaved and current state be visible, and Norman's conceptual model asks that the screen show how its parts relate. To fix it, put a status line by the heading (for example "Draft · saved 2 min ago" or "Published 3 May"), add a "← All posts" link, and show the post's title. I can't see a title field at all, which looks like a gap, because the newsletter list will need one. If the title is set somewhere else, show it here.
2. **"View site" and "Publish" are the same solid black button (convention, and it costs something here).** Both are filled #1f2328 with white 15 px bold text, so the first thing the eye catches is a navigation link, not the action the page exists for. A volunteer heading for "Publish" might hit "View site" and leave mid-edit, or simply not see that one action matters more than the other. Material, Apple's HIG and Nielsen Norman Group guidance all say one filled primary per view. Make "View site" a text link or an outline button and leave "Publish" as the only filled one.
3. **"Publish" doesn't say what it does (defect, copy).** On a screen called "Parish Newsletter", "Publish" can mean "put it on the website" or "email it to everyone on the list". That button commits the volunteer, and the email reading can't be undone. If the wrong reading wins, a half-finished post lands in every parishioner's inbox, or a post they thought was sent sits unread on the site. Name the result on the button or right next to it, for example "Publish to site" or "Publish and email 240 subscribers".

**Smaller things.**
- The formatting toolbar sets B, I, U and S all in the same bold face, so none of them previews its effect, and the bold "I" reads as another divider bar beside the real dividers (Norman, signifiers). Set each letter in the style it applies: bold B, italic I, underlined U, struck S.
- The toolbar uses developer terms (H1, H2, `</>`) for what is probably a non-technical audience (Nielsen 2). Use "Heading" and "Subheading", and drop the code button unless parish posts really need code.
- The writing area is 860 px wide, so the post runs at about 110–115 characters per line (the first line is ~114). That's well past Bringhurst's 45–75, which makes proofreading tiring and doesn't match how the post will read on the site. Narrow the text area, or its inner padding, to about 66 characters.

**What's working.**
- The centred single column, with the heading, editor and buttons all on the same 210/1070 edges, keeps the screen calm and points the volunteer at one task. Keep that discipline.
- "Save draft" (outline) and "Publish" (filled) sit together under the editor, right-aligned to its edge, with the safer action on the left. Once "View site" is demoted, this pair is exactly right.
- The type is one family (Inter) in a small, distinct scale (26/18/16 px plus 15 and 13 for controls), all dark on white at about 15.8:1 contrast. Nothing is faint or fussy.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The focus indicator on the toolbar buttons and the editor. Check in a browser that it's visible, since keyboard users will tab through ten small buttons.
- Whether the toolbar buttons show tooltips or keyboard shortcuts (⌘B and so on) on hover.
