**First read.** The eye goes first to the two dark buttons, "View site" at top right and "Publish" at bottom right. It then settles on "Edit post" and two short paragraphs about the spring fair. It reads as a clean, plain editor for a small church newsletter. It is calm and uncluttered but anonymous: it could be any CMS, and it tells you almost nothing about the post you are editing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the screen where a parish volunteer, probably not technical, writes or edits a newsletter post and then saves or publishes it.

**Verdict.** It works as a plain writing surface, but it doesn't do the editor's real job. It never tells the writer which post this is, whether it is live, or what "Publish" will do. A volunteer who is unsure whether pressing the button emails the whole parish will hesitate, or press it by mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **The screen says nothing about the post or its state.** "Edit post" is the only context. There is no title field, no "Draft" or "Published" status, no last-saved time, and no link back to a list of posts. The header holds only the newsletter's name and "View site". So the writer can't tell which post they are in, whether readers can already see it, or whether their changes are saved. A newsletter post with no headline is also odd in itself. Will the fair item go out untitled? This is a defect (Nielsen 1, visibility of system status; Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall; Norman, conceptual model). Fix: put a title field above the body, show a status line near the heading ("Draft · saved 2 min ago"), and add a way back to the posts list in the header.
2. **"Publish" commits the writer to something the screen never names, and "View site" shouts as loudly.** On a newsletter, "Publish" can mean two things: put the post on the website, or send it to everyone on the mailing list. The writer acts on the second reading only if they think it is safe. Get it wrong one way and they hold back a post that should go out; get it wrong the other way and they email the parish a half-finished draft. Meanwhile "View site" has exactly the same dark fill as "Publish", so the page has two primary actions, and the first one the eye meets is the navigation link. This is a defect (Material Design, button emphasis levels: two equal filled buttons is a fault). Fix: name the outcome on the button ("Publish to website", or "Publish and email subscribers"), and drop "View site" to a text link or outline button so that "Publish" is the only filled button.
3. **The toolbar speaks web-editor jargon to parish volunteers.** B, I, U, and S are all set in the same bold, so none of them shows what it does: the I isn't italic and the S isn't struck through. "H1", "H2", "“", and "</>" are developer terms. "</>" (code) has no plausible use in a fair notice, and H1 clashes with the page's own heading. This is a defect for this audience (Norman, signifiers; Nielsen 2, the audience's own words). Fix: draw each button in its own style (an italic *I*, an underlined U, a struck-through S), use word or icon labels such as "Heading", "Subheading", and "Quote", and drop code.

**Smaller things.**
- Editor text runs about 110 characters per line across the 860 px box, well past Bringhurst's 45–75. Long lines make it harder to proofread a draft. Narrow the text column inside the editor to about 65 characters, or pad it in.
- There is no visible way to leave without saving, such as Cancel, Discard changes, or Back to posts. "View site" is the only exit, and it leaves the edit in an unknown state. Add a quiet Cancel next to Save draft. (Nielsen 3)

**What's working.**
- The single centred writing column, with the toolbar attached to the editor box and the actions directly under its right edge. Everything sits where the writer expects it, and the space is gathered rather than scattered.
- The restraint: one typeface (Inter), one near-black (#1f2328, 15.8:1 contrast on white), and 16 px body text at 1.6 line height (`font_size`, `line_height_ratio`, `textarea`). It is comfortable to read and write in. Keep this when adding the status line and title field.
- The two actions are correctly paired. "Save draft" is an outline button and "Publish" a filled one, placed side by side at the bottom right, so the safe action sits before the committing one.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the toolbar formats anything. The facts file reports the body as a plain `textarea`, which can't show bold or headings. Check in a browser that the buttons do what they show.
- Whether "Publish" asks for confirmation, and whether there are focus rings and keyboard shortcuts for the toolbar.
