**First read.** The eye goes straight to the two solid black buttons, "View site" at top right and "Publish" at bottom right, and moves between them because they look identical. Then it drops to "Edit post" and the two paragraphs. It reads as a clean, quiet editor for a small parish newsletter: calm and tidy, but anonymous, and something is missing. The post has no title, and nothing says which post this is.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a non-technical parish volunteer uses this to write a newsletter post and publish it.

**Verdict.** It's a solid, uncluttered base, but it isn't ready for the volunteer's job. A post can't be given a title, the screen doesn't say whether this post is live, saved or a draft, and the one action you can't undo (Publish) is styled exactly like a nav link.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no title field, and nothing says which post this is.** "Edit post" sits over a bare body field. A newsletter post needs a headline, and the volunteer has nowhere to type one, or they'll try to fake one with the "H1" button inside the body. The page heading also doesn't name the post being edited. The fix is a labelled title field above the body, set larger, so it previews as the post's headline. The body field should also carry its own label, or a clear "Write your post…" prompt when empty (Wroblewski; NN/g, "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful"). *Defect.*
2. **The screen hides the post's state.** Nothing says whether this post is a draft, already published, or has unsaved changes, and there's no "last saved" time. For a volunteer, the two scariest questions are "did I lose my work?" and "is this already live in the newsletter?". Right now both mean clicking and hoping (Nielsen 1, visibility of system status). Put a small status line near the action buttons or the heading: "Draft · saved 2 min ago" or "Published 3 May · unpublished changes". *Defect.*
3. **Two buttons share the primary style.** "View site" in the header is the same filled black button as "Publish". The more important action has no more weight than navigation, and the matching pair splits the eye on first glance. Publish is the step you can't take back; "View site" is a way out of the page. Keep Publish as the only filled button, and make "View site" a text link or outline button (Material Design, button emphasis levels; NN/g on visual hierarchy). *Convention, and here it costs something.*

**Smaller things.**
- **Editor line length:** body text runs about 110 characters per line across the 860 px editor. That's far past Bringhurst's 45–75, and it also misleads the writer about how the post will read on the site. Narrow the text column or match it to the published width.
- **Toolbar glyphs:** B, I, U and S are all set in the same bold weight, so none of them previews its effect, and they float without button shapes. "•" and "“" are cryptic. Show each letter in its own style (bold B, italic I, underlined U, struck S) and give the buttons visible hit areas or tooltips (Norman, signifiers).
- **Jargon for this audience:** "H1", "H2" and "</>" are developer terms. A parish volunteer would understand "Heading", "Subheading" and probably doesn't need a code button at all (Nielsen 2).
- **No way back:** there's no "Back to posts" or "Cancel" link. "View site" is the only exit, and it leaves the editor without saying what happens to unsaved text. Add a back link by the heading (Nielsen 3).

**What's working.**
- The layout is calm and well aligned. The heading, editor and buttons share one left and right edge, and the empty space sits at the bottom, not scattered. Keep that restraint; for this audience it's the right tone.
- Body text in the editor is a plain, readable sans at a comfortable size with good leading. It's easy to write in.
- "Save draft" as outline and "Publish" as filled, placed side by side at bottom right, is the right pairing. Keep it once "View site" is demoted.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Toolbar target size:** the glyphs look about 20 px with roughly 30 px between centres. Measure the real hit areas against WCAG 2.5.8 (24 × 24 px).
- **Focus indicators, toolbar active states** (does B show as on when the cursor is in bold text?), and whether leaving with unsaved changes asks for confirmation.
