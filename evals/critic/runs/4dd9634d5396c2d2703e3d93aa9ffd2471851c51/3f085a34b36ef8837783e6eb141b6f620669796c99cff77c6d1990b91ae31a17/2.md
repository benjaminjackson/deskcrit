**First read.** My eye lands on the bold "Edit post", then jumps to the black "View site" button in the top right corner. After that it drops to the sample text and ends on "Publish". It reads as a plain blog-style editor for a small parish newsletter. It feels calm, tidy and a little anonymous, like the default screen of any content system.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a parish volunteer, probably not technical, writes a newsletter post and publishes it with confidence.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly well built, but it doesn't yet do its main job. The volunteer can't tell which post this is, what state it's in, or what Publish will do. There's also no place for a title. The biggest problem is that the screen hides the facts someone needs before pressing a button they can't take back.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no title field, and no hint of where the post goes (defect).** The editor has a single unlabelled body box. There's nowhere to type a headline, and nothing says whether the first line becomes the title. A newsletter post without a headline is either impossible or publishes looking broken, and the volunteer only finds out afterwards. "Newsletter" plus "reply to this post" also suggests Publish might email people. Nothing near the button says whether it sends an email, posts to the site, or both, or who will get it. Publish commits the volunteer to something public, so this matters most. Add a labelled Title field above the body (Wroblewski; NN/g on visible labels). Put one plain line next to Publish that says what will happen, such as "Publishes to the website and emails 240 subscribers". Users expect a title field in any post editor (Jakob's law).
2. **Nothing shows the post's state (defect; Nielsen 1, visibility of system status).** "Edit post" never names the post. Nothing says whether it's a draft or already live, or whether the changes are saved. A volunteer who comes back to this screen can't tell if the fair notice is already public, so they may publish it twice or think it went out when it didn't. Put the state and save time near the heading or the buttons, for example "Draft · saved 2 min ago". Use the post's title in the heading or breadcrumb.
3. **"View site" looks the same as "Publish" (convention: one primary action per view, Material Design, NN/g).** Both are solid black buttons. "View site" sits in the top right corner, where the eye checks first, so it takes the weight that belongs to Publish. It also invites the volunteer to leave mid-edit. Make View site a text link or outline button so Publish is the only filled button on the screen.
4. **The toolbar uses technical shorthand (convention: Nielsen 2, match the user's own words).** H1, H2, </>, U and S are web-developer terms. A parish volunteer won't know what H1 or </> does. The code button has no place in a parish notice, and underlined text on the web looks like a link. Cut the toolbar to what these posts need: bold, italic, heading, list and link. Use plain labels or tooltips ("Heading", "Bulleted list").

**Smaller things.**
- **Line length:** text in the editor runs about 95–100 characters per line, wider than Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the editor's text column to about 65–70 characters so it reads like the published post.
- **Way back:** there's no "Back to posts" or "Cancel" link, so the only route out is the header. Add a breadcrumb or back link beside "Edit post".

**What's working.**
- The layout is quiet. It has one centred column for one task, generous margins, and no clutter. Keep it that restrained when you add the title and status.
- The action buttons sit right under the editor at its right edge, with Save draft as the outline button and Publish as the filled one. That's the right order and the right difference in weight.
- The body text is a comfortable size with relaxed line spacing, so the post is easy to read while editing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the toolbar buttons have clickable areas of at least 24 px. The letters look small and close together, so check the hit areas in the browser.
- Focus states on the toolbar and the editor.
