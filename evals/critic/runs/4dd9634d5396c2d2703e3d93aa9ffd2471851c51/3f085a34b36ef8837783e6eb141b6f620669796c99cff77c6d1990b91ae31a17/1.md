**First read.** The eye lands on "Edit post", then gets pulled between two identical black buttons: "View site" at top right and "Publish" at bottom right. It reads as a simple editor in a small CMS for a parish newsletter, the kind a volunteer uses once a week. It feels calm, tidy and generic: there's nothing to trip over, and nothing that tells you where you are.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the screen where a non-technical parish volunteer writes or edits a newsletter post and then publishes it.

**Verdict.** The basics are sound: the type is readable, the palette is restrained and the save and publish actions sit in the right place. But the screen doesn't tell the volunteer which post this is, whether it's already live, or how to get back. That is the biggest problem, because the main decision here is "Save draft or Publish", and the user can't make it confidently without knowing the post's state.

**What would move it most**
1. **The post has no name, no status and no exit.** "Edit post" is the only heading, and it doesn't say which post. There's no title field and no "Draft" or "Published" marker. There's no "last saved" time, no Cancel, and no link back to a list of posts. The only way out is "View site". So a volunteer looking at "Save draft" and "Publish" can't tell whether the post is already live, which means they can't tell whether "Save draft" would take it down. They also can't tell whether leaving now loses their changes. That's a **defect** against Nielsen's first heuristic (visibility of system status) and his third (user control and freedom), and against WCAG 2.4.6, since the heading should name what the section holds. The heading should name the post, a status badge should sit beside it, and a "Saved 2 min ago" line should sit near the buttons. Add a plain way back, such as "← All posts" or a Cancel next to Save draft. If posts have titles on the site, give the title its own field above the body.
2. **Two buttons fight to be the main action.** "View site" and "Publish" are the same filled black, the same size and the same weight, so the eye splits between them. Publish is what this screen exists for. "View site" is a side trip and, judging by its style, the more prominent of the two, since it sits first in reading order at top right. That's a **convention** break (Material Design and Apple HIG: one primary action per view, with two equally weighted filled buttons counted as a fault), and here it costs something: it pulls attention away from the commit action and offers a tempting way to leave mid-edit. Make "View site" a text link or outline button so Publish is the only filled button.
3. **The toolbar is written for developers.** "H1", "H2" and "</>" are web-developer terms a parish volunteer may not know. B, I, U and S are all set in the same bold sans, so the "I" reads as a vertical bar rather than "italic". The "•", "1." and "“" glyphs are tiny and leave you guessing. This is a **defect** for this audience (Nielsen's second heuristic: use the user's words; Norman's signifiers). Draw each letter as what it does: **B** bold, *I* italic, U underlined, S struck through. Rename H1/H2 to "Heading" and "Subheading", or put them in a style menu. Drop the code button unless someone actually needs it.

**Smaller things.**
- The editor's body text runs about 105 characters per line, well past Bringhurst's 45–75. That makes longer posts harder to proofread, and it won't match how the post reads on the site. Narrow the text column, or match the site's own measure.

**What's working.**
- Save draft (outline) and Publish (filled) are paired correctly: they're adjacent, ranked by emphasis and sit right under the editor's bottom-right corner, where writing ends. Keep that pairing when you demote "View site".
- The editor text is set at a comfortable size with generous leading, flush left, in one plain sans. Writing in it will feel easy.
- The palette is one neutral range with black for the actions. Nothing competes with the content, which suits a plain community tool.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the editor shows a visible focus ring or caret when active, and whether the toolbar buttons show hover or pressed states and tooltips. Tooltips especially would ease the toolbar-labelling problem above.
