**First read.** My eye goes to the headline, then the byline, then hits a block of small, pale, thin grey text that looks like a caption or a placeholder rather than the article itself. It reads as a calm, literate blog post about a practical craft, and the tone is right. But the article text, which is the reason anyone is on the page, is the faintest thing on it. The byline and the menu both look more important than the writing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a long-form how-to article on a small editorial site, read on desktop by people who want to learn the craft.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but it doesn't do its main job yet: the body text is set too small, too light and too grey to read comfortably at article length. Fixing the body setting would change this page more than anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is the weakest text on the page (defect).**
   - **What's wrong:** The three paragraphs are 13 px (`font_size`, `article > p`) in Inter Light 300 (`font_weight`), in #727272 (`color`).
   - **Compared with the rest of the page:** The byline and nav links are 15 px in dark #1f2328 (`p.by`, `nav > a`). So the menu labels, "Stories / Guides / About", are bigger and darker than the article.
   - **The contrast number hides the problem:** At 4.81:1 (`contrast`) the body grey passes WCAG 1.4.3 on paper, which is why `violations` is empty. But stacking small size, a light weight and a mid grey makes the text look fainter than the ratio suggests. In the crop the thin strokes break up and the paragraphs blur into a grey band.
   - **Why it matters:** Bringhurst notes that very light weights "do little to increase readability". Material 3 sets body text above button and label text, not below it.
   - **What the reader gets:** For an article meant to be read through, that means squinting, or giving up after the first paragraph.
   - **Fix:** Make the body the page's main reading text. Set it at least as large as the nav, around 16–18 px, in Regular 400, close to the heading colour. Keep grey for metadata if you want it anywhere.
2. **The type scale is crowded at the bottom and in the wrong order (convention).**
   - **The sizes:** There are four: 34 / 18 / 15 / 13 (`type_sizes`).
   - **Where they collide:** 18, 15 and 13 are only a couple of pixels apart, so they don't read as separate levels (Bringhurst 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale").
   - **The ordering is upside down:** The byline (15, dark) ranks above the body (13, grey). Metadata should sit below the text, not above it.
   - **What to keep:** Three levels will carry this page: a title around 34, body around 17, and one small size for byline and nav. Tell the byline apart from the body by colour or weight, not by being bigger.

**Smaller things.**
- **Navigation:** Stories / Guides / About all look the same, so nothing tells the reader which section this piece belongs to (probably Guides). Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Nielsen Norman Group, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- **The reading column:** The measure is about 71 characters (`characters_per_line`), set flush left with a gentle rag. Keep the column width when you enlarge the type: going up to around 17 px will cut the characters per line, so widen the column a little to keep it in the 60s.
- **The headline:** It is the clear entry point at 34 px bold, with tight 1.25 leading (`h1`), so the two lines hold together. It breaks at a natural phrase, "…wall / after a hard winter".
- **The copy:** It is specific and talks to the reader: "the cheapest repair you will ever make" says what is in it for them. The opening paragraph raises a real problem before the instructions start.
