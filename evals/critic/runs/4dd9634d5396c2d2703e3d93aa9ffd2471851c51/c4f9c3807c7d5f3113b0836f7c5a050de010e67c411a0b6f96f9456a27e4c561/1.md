**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set below the page's own chrome, so reading takes effort.** Defect.
   - **What's measured:** the three paragraphs are 13 px Inter at weight 300 in #727272 (`font_size`, `font_weight`, `color` on `article > p`). The nav links are 15 px regular in near-black #1f2328, and so is the byline (`nav > a`, `p.by`).
   - **What the reader gets:** 531 of the page's roughly 620 characters sit at the smallest size, the lightest weight and the palest colour on the screen. The ratio is 4.81:1 (`contrast`), so it technically passes WCAG 1.4.3 and the measurement lists no violations. But a 13 px light weight has thin strokes, so in practice it reads paler than that number suggests. The reader squints at the instructions while "Stories / Guides / About" sits comfortably legible.
   - **Hierarchy:** it is inverted below the headline. The byline is a footnote-level element, but it outranks the text it introduces. Material's type scale puts body text above button and menu text, at 16 against 14, for this reason.
   - **Fix:** do three things together.
     - Bring the body up to about 16–17 px, at or above the nav.
     - Use regular weight. Light weights help large type, not small (Bringhurst 6.2.3).
     - Darken the text toward the heading colour.

   Then let the byline step down in colour or size, so the order runs headline → body → byline. At 16–17 px the 430 px column holds roughly 60 characters per line, which is a better measure than today's ~71. Keep the 1.7 line height or tighten it a little.

**Smaller things.**
- **Current section not marked.** Nothing in the nav (`Stories`, `Guides`, `About`) shows which section this article belongs to, and all three look identical. Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g).
- **Type scale.** There are four sizes: 34, 18, 15 and 13 (`type_sizes`). The 15 and 13 are only 2 px apart, so they read as two near-identical small sizes rather than two levels. Once the body is enlarged, let the byline differ by colour or weight instead of keeping a separate size.
- **Date has no year.** The byline reads "March 14" with no year. For a seasonal repair guide, readers will want to know which winter. Add the year.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** 34 px bold, two lines broken at a natural phrase, 1.25 line height. It holds together as one unit and clearly dominates. Keep it.
- **The reading column.** One narrow, centred column on a single left edge (x = 425), with generous space around it. That is the right frame for long reading, and it frames the text well once the text is strong enough.
- **The copy.** It's concrete and specific: "mark every bulge with a stick", "a through stone every metre". It speaks to "you" and tells the reader why each step matters ("the cheapest repair you will ever make"). Don't let a type fix dilute it.
