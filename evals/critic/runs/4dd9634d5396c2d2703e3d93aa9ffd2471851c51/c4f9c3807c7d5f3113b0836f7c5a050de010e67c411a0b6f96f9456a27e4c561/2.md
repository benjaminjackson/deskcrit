**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an article page on a small editorial site ("Field Notes"), meant for someone who wants to read and follow a practical how-to on repairing a stone wall.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the one thing the page exists to deliver, the article text, is set as the weakest element on screen. It is smaller, lighter and greyer than the byline and the menu. Fix the body text and this is a good page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is styled as the least important text on the page.** This is a defect. The three paragraphs are 13 px, weight 300, in #727272 (`font_size`, `font_weight`, `color`, `article > p`). The byline above them is 15 px in near-black #1f2328 (`p.by`), and so are the menu links (`nav > a`). So the ranking below the headline runs backwards: the author's name and "About" carry more weight than the instructions the reader came for. The light, grey body text makes the page feel finished. For anyone reading a set of steps on a bright screen, it is tiring to read, and the eye keeps getting pulled back to the darker byline. Contrast is 4.81:1, which passes WCAG 1.4.3, but only just, and pairing the thin 300 weight with grey at 13 px makes it look fainter than that number suggests. Very light weights do little for readability at small sizes (Bringhurst, 6.2.3). Paragraph text should be at least as large as the interface text around it (Material Design 3 sets body at 16, labels at 14). How to fix it: set the body at 16–17 px, regular weight (400), in the same dark #1f2328 as the headline. If the byline needs to sit lower than the body, make it smaller or grey instead. Keep the 1.7 line height and check the measure afterwards. At 16 px the 430 px column will hold about 58 characters per line, still inside 45–75. Widen the column a little if you want to stay near 66.

**Smaller things.**
- The menu (Stories, Guides, About) doesn't mark which section this article belongs to, so readers can't tell where they are. Give the current item one cue, such as weight or an underline (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- The byline "March 14" has no year, so a reader can't tell whether this advice is from this spring or from years ago. Add the year. This is common sense, not a published rule.

**What's working.**
