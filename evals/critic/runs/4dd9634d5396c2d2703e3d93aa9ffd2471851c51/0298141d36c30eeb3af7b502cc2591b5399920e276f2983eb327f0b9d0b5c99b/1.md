**First read.** The eye goes to the bold headline, "Why the second day of a stew is better", then down through the byline into three paragraphs. It reads as a short, calm essay on a home-cooking blog. Nothing competes with the headline, and the page feels quiet and trustworthy. After "catches on the pan." the page simply stops, leaving a blank band across the lower third.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a short editorial note on a cooking site. Its job is to get read to the end, and ideally to lead the reader on to cooking or to more of the site.

**Verdict.** This is a sound, readable page with one real flaw in the setting and one in the flow. The lines are too long for comfortable reading, and the article ends at a dead stop even though its last paragraph tells the reader to go and cook.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraph lines are too long.** All three paragraphs run 91 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `article > p:nth-of-type(2–4)`), set at 16 px in a 700 px column. That is well past the 45–75 range and the 66 ideal (Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* §2.1.2). On every line return the eye has a long trip back, and it is easy to drop onto the wrong line. That matters most in a piece that exists to be read slowly. The 1.65 line height (`line_height_ratio`) helps, but it does not fix the problem. To fix it, bring the measure to about 65–75 characters. Either narrow the text column to roughly 560–600 px, or keep the column and raise the body text to about 19–20 px. The larger size would also suit the relaxed tone. *Convention.*
2. **The article ends with nowhere to go.** The last paragraph says "So cook it a day early when you can", but there is no link to a stew recipe, no "more notes", and nothing in the empty band below. A reader who was persuaded has to go to the nav and hunt for what to do next, and most will just leave. Put the next step right after the last line, and make it specific, such as "The shin-and-red-wine stew this is about →", not a generic "Related posts". *Common sense / taste; no approved source covers it.*

**Smaller things.**
- The nav doesn't show where the reader is. Recipes, Notes and Contact all look the same, though this page presumably sits under Notes. Mark the current section with one cue, such as bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The byline "January 9" has no year. On an article people will find through search months later, add the year so readers can tell how recent it is.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** it is clear and specific, it makes a promise, and it is the only heavy thing on the page (32 px bold against 16 px body), so the eye knows where to start without any decoration.
- **Spacing that shows the groups:** the byline sits 10 px under the headline and 24 px above the body, and paragraphs are separated by space with no indents. The headline and byline read as one unit, and the body reads as a separate one.
- **Restraint:** one typeface (Inter), one near-black for all text plus one grey for the byline (`text_colors`), and every block on one left edge at x=290. Contrast is strong throughout (15.8:1 body, 8.99:1 byline). Keep this when you add the next-step link, so it doesn't arrive as a loud card.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus styling on the nav links, and how the column and the lines reflow on a phone.
