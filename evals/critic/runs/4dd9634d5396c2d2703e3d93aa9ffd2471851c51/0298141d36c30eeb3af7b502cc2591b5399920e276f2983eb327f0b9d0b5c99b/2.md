**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold headline, "Why the second day of a stew is better", drops to the byline, and settles into three short paragraphs. It reads at once as a personal food essay on a small blog. It feels calm and tidy, but also neutral and a bit clinical, close to a documentation page. Then the text ends a little over two-thirds of the way down and the page just stops.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a short article on a home-cooking blog. It should be a pleasure to read and should leave the reader wanting to cook, or to read more from Slow Kitchen.

**Verdict.** It works as a clean, readable article. Nothing is broken at the top level. Three things hold it back: the reading column is too wide for its type size, the styling is so neutral that nothing says "food", and the page ends without offering the reader anything next.

**What would move it most**
1. **The text lines are too long.** All three body paragraphs run about 91 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `article > p:nth-of-type(2)` through `(4)`), at 16 px in a 700 px column. The comfortable range is 45 to 75 characters (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.2). At 91, the eye has to travel a long way back to the start of each line and sometimes picks up the wrong one. The generous 1.65 line height is partly making up for that. This is a convention break, and it costs something here: the piece is meant to be read slowly and enjoyed, and this is the one thing that makes reading it work. Fix: narrow the column to about 560–600 px, or raise the body to about 18 px in the same column. Either brings it near 66 characters. Then the line height can come down to about 1.5–1.6.
2. **The styling says nothing about food, so it could be anyone's blog.** The type is all Inter, the text is two cool greys (#1f2328 and #424a53), and there is no hue, image, or texture anywhere. The writing is warm and full of detail (shin bones, the cold pantry, "catches on the pan"). The page dresses it like a README. A reader gets no feel for Slow Kitchen and nothing to remember it by. This is taste, but I'd argue it: colour and type should carry the mood the message calls for (Itten, *The Art of Color*). Fix: give the page one sign of its subject. A text face with some warmth, a warm off-white or a single kitchen-toned accent, or one photograph of the stew would do it. Keep the calm restraint, and drop the anonymity.
3. **The article ends at a dead end.** The last paragraph tells the reader to "cook it a day early", which is the moment they want a stew recipe. The page gives them nothing: no recipe link, no next essay, no footer, just white space below y≈543. The site has a Recipes section, so the obvious next step is one click away but not shown. This is common sense rather than a cited rule. Fix: end the article with one specific next step, such as "The shin-and-red-wine stew this is about →", plus a short "more notes" line.

**Smaller things.**
- **Header navigation:** Recipes, Notes and Contact all look identical, so nothing marks the section you are in, which is presumably Notes. Mark the current item with one cue, such as bolder weight or an underline (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu-design checklist).
- **Type sizes:** I count five sizes: 32, 18, 16, 15 and 14 px (`type_sizes`). Logo, body, nav and byline sit within 4 px of each other. Use one small size for both nav and byline, and let weight and colour separate the rest.
- **Byline date:** "January 9" has no year, so a reader can't tell how old the piece is. Add the year.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** it is the one clear focal point, at 32 px bold against 16 px body. It is also a real promise ("why") that the text keeps.
- **Spacing:** header to title, title to byline, and byline to body step up in a clear order, and the paragraph gaps are distinct without breaking the flow. Keep these steps when you resize the column.
- **Contrast:** text contrast is strong throughout (15.8:1 body, 8.99:1 byline). Keep that if you add warmth to the colours.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column and header behave on a phone. Check that the measure stays near 45–75 characters at narrow widths and that the nav doesn't crowd the logo.
