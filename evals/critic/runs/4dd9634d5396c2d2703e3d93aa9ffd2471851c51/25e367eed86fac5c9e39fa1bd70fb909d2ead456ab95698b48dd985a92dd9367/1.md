**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, which is big, black and tight. It drops to the byline, then stops. The three paragraphs below it are small and grey and look like a caption rather than the article, so the page feels like a title card with fine print under it. It reads as a quiet, well-made blog post on rural craft, but the part you came to read is the hardest part to read.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a long-form how-to article on a small editorial site, which people should read comfortably from start to finish.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the body text is too small and too pale for an article that exists to be read. It is the one thing in the way. Every other choice here is calm and correct, so the size and colour of the body text decide whether this page works.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set below the page's own metadata and menu (defect).** The paragraphs look about 13 px. The byline ("By Ellen Marsh, March 14") and the nav links (Stories, Guides, About) look about 15 px and are darker. That inverts the order of the page: the date and author outrank the article, and the reader's eye treats the body as secondary text. Body text should be at least as large as the menu and button text around it; Material 3 sets body at 16 and labels at 14. The hierarchy also needs distinct levels (Müller-Brockmann), and here the level under the title is the byline, not the text. Fix: set the body at about 16–18 px, which is the largest size after the headline. Then make the byline smaller or lighter than the body, so it reads as metadata. The line length is about 70 characters now, so widen the column to match when you enlarge the type, or you'll fall to about 55. That is still fine.
2. **The body text looks faint (defect).** It is a mid-grey in what looks like a light weight, on white, at a small size. In the crop it looks noticeably washed out next to the black headline. It may pass 4.5:1 on paper, but in practice the thin strokes and grey colour make it shimmer. Measure the colour against WCAG 1.4.3 (4.5:1). Even if it passes, darken it to a near-black like the byline's, because this is reading text, not supporting text. Light weights are for large sizes (Bringhurst, 6.2.3). Fixing finding 1 removes most of the problem. Darkening the text finishes it.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: Stories, Guides and About all look identical, so nothing tells the reader which section this article belongs to. Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- The headline is excellent. It is concrete and specific ("after a hard winter" tells you exactly whose problem it solves), set in a heavy weight with tight lines, and it breaks at a phrase boundary. Keep it as it is.
- The copy speaks to the reader. "The cheapest repair you will ever make" lands the point on the reader, and every paragraph opens on an action. Don't let a redesign trim this voice.
- A single centred reading column with generous white space and no ornament is the right shape for this job. Once the body text is fixed, the restraint will look deliberate rather than empty.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus styles on the nav links.
