**First read.** The eye lands on the headline straight away. It is the only dark, heavy thing on a white page, and it reads in a second as a how-to article in a quiet editorial journal. Then the eye drops to the byline, which looks darker and larger than the text under it. The body text comes last: three small grey paragraphs that look like captions or a summary, not the article. It feels calm and well spaced, and also slightly faint, as if the text were still loading.

**What it's for.** Assumption: an article page on a small editorial site, meant to be read from start to finish by someone who wants to fix a wall.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the one thing this page exists for, the article text, is set as its weakest element. The body is smaller and paler than the byline and the menu, so the page works against long reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is the smallest and palest text on the page** (defect). The three paragraphs at x≈425–852 look about 13 px in mid-grey. The menu links ("Stories", "Guides", "About") look about 15 px and near-black, and the byline "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" is larger and darker than the paragraphs it introduces. Below the headline the order of importance runs backward: the reader's eye settles on metadata and navigation and treats the article as secondary. Small grey text also tires the eyes over a long how-to, and older readers will lean in or leave. A guide about outdoor work will draw plenty of those readers. Two conventions back this up. Body text should be at least as large as the menu text around it (Material Design 3: body 16, labels 14). Text should also clear 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3). By eye the grey looks borderline, so measure it. The fix:
   - Set the body at 16–18 px, at or above the menu size.
   - Darken it to near the headline's colour, so it clearly passes 4.5:1.
   - Let the byline step down to a smaller or lighter caption level, so the order runs headline → body → metadata.
   - Widen the column to match the bigger type. It already holds about 70 characters per line, so the larger body will need roughly 560–640 px to keep 60–75.

**Smaller things.**
- The navigation menu doesn't mark which section this article belongs to (presumably "Guides"). Mark the current section with one cue, such as bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- The headline works well: it's bold, tightly leaded, breaks on a natural phrase ("…wall / after a hard winter") and clearly dominates. Keep it as the single heaviest element.
- The writing is concrete and speaks to the reader ("the cheapest repair you will ever make", "set a through stone every metre"). It tells the reader what to do and why it pays off. Don't let a redesign water it down.
- The single centred reading column, the shared left edge for headline, byline and body, and the generous margins suit a long read. The empty space sits in deliberate places, not scattered gaps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the menu links.
- How the column behaves on a phone once the body size goes up.
