**First read.** The eye lands on the bold two-line headline, drops to the byline, then reaches a small, pale grey block of text. It reads as a calm how-to article on a small editorial site, and it feels quiet and careful. The article itself comes across as the least important thing on the page, as if it were a caption under the byline.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a long-form practical article, a "Guides" piece meant to be read start to finish, often by someone about to do the repair.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but it fails at its main job, which is being comfortable to read. The body text is set smaller and paler than the menu and the byline, so the part the page exists for looks the least important and is the hardest to read.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text ranks below the meta text** (defect). The paragraphs look about 13 px in mid-grey. The menu ("Stories, Guides, About") and the byline ("By Ellen Marsh, March 14") look about 15 px in near-black. The page has four levels (headline, menu, byline, body), but they run in the wrong order: the byline outranks the article. A reader who came for the method has to strain more for it than for the date. Paragraph text should never be smaller than the interface text around it (Material Design 3 type scale: Body Large 16, Label 14), and each level should be clearly different from the next (Müller-Brockmann). To fix it, bring the body to at least the menu size, around 16–18 px for an article. Then make the byline the quieter line, using a smaller size or grey, so the order goes headline, then body, then meta. The column at about 425 px wide will need to widen to keep about 65 characters per line at the larger size.
2. **The body text looks faint** (defect, WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3). The grey on white looks close to the 4.5:1 minimum, and possibly under it. Small text makes this worse, and so do readers glancing at a phone outdoors beside the wall. Sample the hex value and measure it. Either way, darken it to near the byline's colour, because thin, small, light-grey type is the most tiring combination there is for a long read.

**Smaller things.**
- The menu doesn't show where you are. "Stories, Guides, About" all look the same, so a reader can't tell this is a Guide. Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- The headline is a real headline. It's specific, concrete, and says who it's for ("after a hard winter"). It's set tight and bold enough that the two lines read as one unit and clearly lead the page.
- The copy is very good. Each paragraph says who does what, to what, and when ("walk the full run of the wall and mark every bulge with a stick"). It also tells the reader why it matters to them ("the cheapest repair you will ever make"). Keep that voice.
- The single centred column at about 70 characters per line, with generous paragraph spacing and nothing competing in the margins, is the right setting for a long read. The only thing to change is the size and colour of the type in it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column and type scale reflow on a phone, which is likely where this gets read on site.
