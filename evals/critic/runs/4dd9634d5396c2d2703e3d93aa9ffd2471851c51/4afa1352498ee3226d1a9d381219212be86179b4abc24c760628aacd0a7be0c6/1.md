**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold two-line headline, drops to the byline, and then trips: the byline is crisp near-black, while the paragraphs under it are a soft mid-grey. It reads as a quiet, well-made article page for a small rural-craft publication. It feels calm and trustworthy, but the article itself looks faded, as if it were secondary to its own credit line.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a how-to article page that someone reads from start to finish, often to follow the steps.

**Verdict.** The page is mostly sound. Layout, measure, spacing and type choice are all right. But it fades out the one thing people come to read. The grey body text is the single thing to fix, and fixing it is a one-value change.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is too pale, and paler than the byline.** All three paragraphs (x≈370–908, y≈280–640) look like about #777 on white. That is at or just under the 4.5:1 WCAG minimum for normal text (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3), and it's a **defect**. Over a long how-to, pale grey text tires the eyes faster. Readers with low vision, older eyes, or a phone in daylight lose it first, and they are a big share of the people who repair walls. It also turns the order of importance upside down. The byline "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" is smaller but darker, so in greyscale it outranks the text it credits (Albers; Refactoring UI: value should carry the structure). The fix is to darken the body to the near-black of the headline, or close to it (something like #333 or darker), so it clears 4.5:1 with room to spare. If the byline then needs to recede, make the byline grey. Measure the actual hex before you sign it off. By eye it sits right on the threshold, and on a reading page you don't want to be on the threshold.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav ("Stories, Guides, About") doesn't mark where you are. If this article belongs to Guides, give that item one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- **The reading column.** It's about 540 px wide at roughly 60 characters per line, with generous leading and paragraphs marked by space alone. That is textbook measure (Bringhurst) and is why the text reads easily despite the grey. Keep it.
- **The headline.** It's tightly leaded, tightly spaced, and broken at a phrase ("…after / a hard winter"). It holds together as one unit and clearly dominates.
- **The copy.** It's concrete and spoken to the reader ("Before you move a stone, walk the full run of the wall and mark every bulge with a stick"), with real quantities like "a through stone every metre". It tells the reader exactly what to do, which is the whole job of a guide.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus styling on the nav links, and how the column and header reflow on a phone.
