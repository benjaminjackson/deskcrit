**First read.** My eye goes straight to the bold two-line headline. Next it drops to the byline, not the body, because "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" is set in near-black while the paragraphs under it are a mid grey. It reads as a calm, well-made how-to article on a small editorial site, and it feels quiet and competent. The grey body text also makes it feel a little washed out, as if the article is the least important thing on the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a long-form practical article on a web page, for someone who wants to read the whole repair method and then follow it.

**Verdict.** Mostly it does its job. The structure is sound, the measure is right and the type is calm. The one thing in the way is the body text: it's the reason the page exists, and it's the palest thing on it. That makes it harder to read and turns the hierarchy upside down under the headline.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is too pale, and the page's darkness runs the wrong way round** *(defect, partly convention)*. All three paragraphs (from about x 370, y 290 to 640) look like a grey around #777 on white. That's on or just under the WCAG 1.4.3 minimum of 4.5:1 for text this size. Meanwhile the byline above them, which is smaller and less important, is near-black. So the reader's eye lands on the date and author before the content. Over a long article the faint grey tires the eyes, and readers with low vision or a dim screen will lose it first. If you render the page in greyscale, the darkest things are the headline, the byline and the nav, and the article is the faintest. That's backwards (Albers; Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*, on value carrying structure). The fix: darken the body text to a near-black at or close to the headline's colour, at least 4.5:1 and comfortably above it. If the byline should step back, make it lighter grey or smaller, not darker. Measure the actual hex of the body text to confirm the ratio.

**Smaller things.**
- Navigation, top right: Stories, Guides and About all look the same, so nothing tells the reader which section this article belongs to. Mark the current section with one clear cue, like a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", NN/g; Nielsen heuristic 1).

**What's working.**
- **The reading column.** Lines run about 65 to 70 characters, with generous leading (roughly 1.6). The paragraphs are flush left with a soft rag, and they're separated by space alone, with no indents as well. Keep this exact column and setting when you change the colour.
- **The headline.** It's one clear dominant element: a large, heavy, tightly set two-line heading that holds together as one unit. It sits close to its byline and well apart from the header, so the page has one obvious place to start.
- **Restraint.** One typeface, no ornament, a single hairline under the header, and plenty of open space at the sides. Nothing competes with the article.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the nav links look on hover and keyboard focus, and how the column reflows on a phone.
