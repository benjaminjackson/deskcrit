**First read.** The eye goes straight to the black **Sign out** button. It's the darkest, heaviest thing on the screen, darker than the "Profile" heading. Next it goes to "Profile", then down the three fields. The page reads as a calm, competent account-settings screen for a SaaS product, but the one button that shouts is the one that ends your session.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web app's profile settings page, where a signed-in user checks or edits their name, email and time zone and saves the changes.

**Verdict.** The form itself is clean and nearly right. The action row is wrong. "Sign out" is styled as the primary action and sits as a pair with "Save changes", so the page's main call to action ends the session instead of saving the form. Fix that row and this is a solid screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **Sign out is styled as the main action and placed inside the form.** This is a defect and breaks a convention. In the button row under Time zone, "Save changes" is pale grey (disabled, since nothing has been edited) and "Sign out" is solid black with white text right next to it. That causes three problems:
   - The strongest element on the page is an action that has nothing to do with the form.
   - The two buttons read as a pair: two actions for these fields, of the same kind.
   - Someone who edits a field and reaches for the button they've learned to treat as "commit" may sign out and lose the edit. Once Save becomes active, there will also be two filled buttons side by side competing for first place.

   Material Design and Apple HIG both say a view has one visually dominant primary action and everything else steps down. Norman's natural mapping says a control should sit next to the thing it changes, and Sign out doesn't change anything on this form. The fix:
   - Let **Save changes** be the only filled button.
   - Move Sign out out of the form, either to the foot of the left sidebar or the account menu, or to a separate "Session" area lower down.
   - Style it as a quiet text or outline button.

2. **Time zone looks like a free-text box for a value that has to be exact.** This is a defect. "Europe/Lisbon" sits in a field drawn exactly like Display name and Email: no chevron, no search icon, nothing that says "pick one". If it really is free text, people will type "Lisbon", "WET" or "GMT", and since "Reminders and reports follow this time zone", a typo means reminders arrive at the wrong hour. Nielsen's error-prevention heuristic says to constrain inputs like this. Make it a searchable picker and draw it that way (chevron or search affordance). If it already is one, the drawing needs to say so. Better still, list options in the user's own words, such as "Lisbon (GMT+0)", instead of the raw system ID "Europe/Lisbon" (Nielsen: match between the system and the real world).

**Smaller things.**
- Time zone label is a system term ("Europe/Lisbon"). Show the city plus its offset, as in finding 2.
- Disabled "Save changes" gives no hint of why it's off. That's fine if it lights up on edit, but check it does (see below).

**What's working.**
- **Spacing does the grouping.** Each label, field and helper line sits tight together, with clearly larger gaps between the three fields. You can scan it as three units without any boxes. Keep those intervals.
- **The helper copy is specific and talks to the user:** "We send receipts and sign-in links here", "Reminders and reports follow this time zone." Each line tells you what the setting does to you, which is exactly what helper text is for. The subhead "Changes apply to every workspace you belong to" heads off a real question before it's asked.
- **The type scale is restrained.** There's one heading size, bold labels, regular values and smaller helper text. Distinct levels, no near-miss sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Save changes" turns active (and becomes the clear primary) as soon as a field is edited.
- Focus rings on the fields and buttons.
- Whether Time zone opens a picker when clicked.
