**First read.** My eye goes straight to the black "Sign out" button. It is the darkest, heaviest thing on the screen and pulls harder than the "Profile" heading. Next it goes up to "Profile", then down through three tidy, evenly spaced fields. The page reads as a calm, clean account settings screen in a SaaS product. The only place it feels uneasy is that button row.

**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is the profile page of a team or workspace web app, where a signed-in user checks and edits their name, email and time zone.

**Verdict.** The form is well built: clear labels, useful helper text, a sound rhythm. The action row lets it down. "Sign out" is styled as the main action and sits right beside "Save changes", so the one action that has nothing to do with this form is the one the screen tells you to press.

**What would move it most**
1. **Sign out is styled as the primary action and grouped with Save.** This is a defect.
   - **What's wrong.** At the bottom of the form, "Save changes" is greyed out (fair enough, since nothing has been edited) and "Sign out" is a filled black button 12 px or so to its right. The two look like one pair of form actions, and the filled one wins.
   - **What it costs the user.** Someone who has just edited their display name looks for the strong button to finish the job. Until Save lights up, the strong button is Sign out. Even after Save turns on, the two sit as equals. One wrong click logs them out and probably throws away the edit.
   - **Why it's a problem.**
     - Sign out isn't a profile setting, so it's in the wrong place (Norman, natural mapping).
     - It is styled above the real primary action (Material Design and Apple HIG button emphasis levels).
     - It sits right next to a safe action without being set apart (Apple HIG on destructive actions).
   - **Fix.** Move Sign out out of this form, to the bottom of the sidebar or into an account menu, and give it a quiet style such as a text or outline button. Then the only strong button in the form is Save.
   - **One more thing to check.** Save's enabled style needs to be clearly strong once there are changes. This still can't show that.

**Smaller things**
- **Time zone looks like a free-text field.** Its box is identical to Name and Email, with no chevron, so nothing signals a picker and nothing stops a typo like "Lisbon" or "WET". If it really is a select, give it the chevron. If it's typed, make it a searchable list (Norman, signifiers; Nielsen 5, error prevention).
- **"Europe/Lisbon" is a system ID, not how people name a time zone.** Show something like "Lisbon (GMT+0, Western European Time)" (Nielsen 2).

**What's working**
- **Each field is a tight unit.** The bold label sits close above its field and the helper text right under it, with clearly more space before the next label. Keep that spacing.
- **The helper text earns its place.** Each line tells you what the field is used for: "We send receipts and sign-in links here." The intro line, "Changes apply to every workspace you belong to", tells you the scope before you edit anything. That is copy that answers a real question.
- **The palette is restrained.** It's neutral throughout, with one pale tint marking the current sidebar item. That's why the page feels calm, and why fixing the button weights will be enough to make the hierarchy read clearly.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- What Save looks like once enabled, and whether it's clearly stronger than everything else then.
- Whether there is a visible focus ring on the fields and buttons.
