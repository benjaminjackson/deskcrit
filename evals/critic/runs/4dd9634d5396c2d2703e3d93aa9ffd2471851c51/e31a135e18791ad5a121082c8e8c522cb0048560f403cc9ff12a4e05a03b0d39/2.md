**First read.** The eye goes first to the black "Check out" button, then to "Your basket", then down the four rows. It reads as a tidy basket page for a small tea shop, calm and well spaced. A second look shows the rows aren't finished: plain coloured squares where the product photos should be, and a − + × control with no number in it.

**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming it's a desktop basket page where a shopper checks what they're buying and how many, adjusts it, and checks out.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but it doesn't do the main job of a basket: nowhere on the page does it say how many of each item the shopper has. Close behind, the remove button is pressed right up against the + button, so one slip deletes an item.

**What would move it most**
1. **No quantity shown, so the basket can't be checked (defect).** Each row's stepper (−, +, ×, at x 619–692) has no number between − and +. The shopper can't tell whether they have one tin of lapsang or three. Pressing + changes nothing they can see on the row. They can't even tell whether "$24.00" is the price of one tin or the total for the row. That price is exactly what they'll hold you to at payment. The items add up to $79.50 only if each quantity is 1, but nobody should have to do that sum. This breaks Nielsen's first heuristic, visibility of system status: the screen has to show the current state. Fix: put the quantity as a number in the middle of the stepper. If the quantity can be more than 1, show the unit price next to the row total ("2 × $12.00").
2. **Remove sits right next to add, so items get deleted by accident (defect).** The × button touches the + button (both 25 px, `targets`) and shares its border and style, so it looks like a third step in the stepper rather than a delete. The shopper's hand is already aiming at +, and the next button over throws the item away. Nothing marks the × as destructive, and nothing in this still shows an undo. Apple HIG and Material both say a destructive action should be set apart from safe ones and labelled with its result. Raskin argues for undo over a confirmation dialog. Fix: move remove away from the stepper, for example a "Remove" text link under the size line or at the far end of the row. Then show an undo ("Removed Bamboo tea scoop · Undo") instead of making the item vanish.
3. **The thumbnails are empty colour squares (defect, unless the images just didn't load).** The four 72 px squares in the left column are flat colours. A basket is the last place a shopper confirms they picked the right thing, and "Sencha first flush, 50 g pouch" is easier to confirm from a picture of the pouch than from a sage-green square. If these are placeholders, put the real product photos in. If the capture missed them, ignore this point. As it stands, the squares also make the page look like a template more than a tea shop.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count seven sizes: 28, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14 px (`type_sizes`). The logo (19), "Summary" (18), item names (17), prices (16) and summary rows (15) are each a pixel apart, so they look picked one at a time (Bringhurst, 3.1.1). Weight and colour are already doing the work, so cut to about four sizes, e.g. 28 / 17 / 15 / 14, and stop there.
- "Items $79.50" in the summary could be read as a count of items. "Subtotal (4 items)" says what it is.
- Taste: apart from the swatches, the palette is a generic code-tool grey (`#1f2328` on `#f6f8fa`). Nothing here says "tea", so the page could belong to any shop. This matters little on a basket page. It's worth one small brand touch, e.g. the header or the button colour.

**What's working.**
- There's one clear primary action. "Check out" is the only filled button, full width in the summary panel, so the next step is never in doubt. Keep everything else styled lower than it.
- The summary panel sits at the top right, next to the list it totals, and the total is bolded where the eye ends up. The figures are right-aligned on one edge (x 1136), so they're easy to compare.
- The rows are calm and even: names in bold 17 px, the size line in muted grey (`#424a53`, 8.99:1), with rules between the rows. Keep this rhythm when you add the quantity and the photos.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** What happens when you press ×: whether there's an undo, a confirmation, or the item just disappears. Also whether the stepper buttons show visible focus, since they're small and sit right next to each other.
