**First read.** The eye lands on "Your basket", then drops to the black Check out button. It reads as a basket page for a small tea shop: calm, tidy, competent, and generic. On a second look the eye stops at the small − + × clusters. They contain no number, so you can't tell how many of anything you're buying.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop basket page where a shopper checks what they're about to pay for, changes quantities or removes items, and goes to checkout.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound and the path to checkout is clear. But the page doesn't show the one fact a basket exists to confirm, which is how many of each item you're buying. It also puts "remove item" touching "add one". Until those two are fixed, the page fails its basic job, however clean it looks.

**What would move it most**
1. **No quantity anywhere (defect).** Each row's stepper (x 619–692) is only −, + and ×, with no number between them, and no row says "Qty". So the shopper can't confirm the order. They also can't tell whether $24.00 is the unit price or the line total. The two readings only agree at quantity 1, and this is a money figure they'll hold you to. The only way to check is to add up the lines against "Items $79.50", which is work a basket should never ask for (Nielsen, heuristic 6: recognition rather than recall). It also breaks the stepper pattern every shopper knows, where the count sits between − and + (Nielsen, Jakob's law). Fix: put the current quantity in the stepper, and if the quantity can go above 1, show the unit price under the line total (for example, "2 × $12.00").
2. **Remove is fused to "add one" (defect).** The × is the third segment of the same bordered control as + and −, the same size and weight, with no gap. In a row you scan for "+", one slip past it deletes the line. As drawn, × also reads as one more step of the stepper rather than "remove". Destructive actions should be set apart from safe ones and labelled with what they do (Apple HIG, "Buttons"; Raskin, "Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo"). Fix: take × out of the stepper, make it a quiet "Remove" text link or icon set apart under the item name or at the row's end, and offer undo instead of a confirm dialog.
3. **The product pictures are flat colour swatches (defect, unless a feed is still loading).** The four 72 px tiles at x 120 are solid tan, sage, terracotta and sand blocks. A basket thumbnail is there so the shopper recognises what they picked at a glance. These carry no information, and they make a premium tea shop look unfinished. If these are placeholders, put the real product photos in. If the swatches are a deliberate brand choice, they still need to tell tin from pouch from teapot, and right now they don't.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count seven sizes. The six text sizes besides the 28 px heading all sit within 5 px of each other: 19 (`header`), 18 (`div.sum > h2`), 17 (item `h2`), 16 (`div.cost`, button), 15 (summary rows) and 14 (`p`, controls). These steps read as accidents, not as levels (Bringhurst). Keep about four sizes, for example 28 / 18 / 15 / 14, and let weight and colour do the rest (Refactoring UI).
- Summary copy: "Items" doesn't say whether it means a subtotal or a count. Use "Subtotal". "Total" doesn't say whether tax is included. Say so, because the shopper will compare this figure with the card charge.

**What's working.**
- One clear primary action: the full-width dark Check out button (`a`, 272×50 px) is the only filled element on the page and sits right under the total. Keep it that way.
- The two-column layout puts the money where the decision is made. The summary panel's top lines up with the heading at y 106, and prices are right-aligned in one column. That makes the numbers easy to scan and compare.
- Contrast is strong throughout: body text is 15.8:1 and the grey detail lines are 8.99:1 (`contrast`), with no faint text.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether − is disabled at quantity 1, and what happens to the row and the totals after you remove an item (undo or nothing).
- The hover and focus states on the 25 px stepper buttons, and how the row reflows on a phone. There, 25 px segments that touch each other would fall well short of the 44 pt Apple guideline.
