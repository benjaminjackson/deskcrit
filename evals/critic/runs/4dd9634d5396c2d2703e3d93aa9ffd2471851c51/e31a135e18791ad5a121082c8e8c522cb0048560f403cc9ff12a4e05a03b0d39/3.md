**First read.** The eye goes to "Your basket", then across to the dark Check out bar, then down the four product rows. It reads as a tidy basket page for a small tea shop: calm and well spaced. On the second look something is missing. Each row has a −/+ control but no number, so you can't see how many of anything you're buying.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the desktop basket page of a small online tea shop. Its job is to let a shopper confirm what they're buying, adjust it, and move on to checkout.

**Verdict.** The layout and the route to checkout are sound. But the page fails at the one thing a basket must do: it never shows how many of each item is in it. Next to that, a remove button glued to the + button turns a routine edit into an easy mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **No quantity is shown anywhere** (defect). Each row's stepper (around x 619–692) is −, +, × with no number between them, and the summary says "Items $79.50" with no count. The shopper can't confirm the order or see what pressing + did. They have to keep the count in their head or work it out from the line price ($24.00: is that one tin or two?), which is exactly what Nielsen's "recognition rather than recall" rules out. Line prices that reach the total only if every quantity is 1 suggest these are line totals, but the page never says so. Fix: put the current quantity, as a number, between − and +. Where a quantity is above 1, show the unit price next to the line total ("2 × $12.00"). Make the summary line read "Items (4)".
2. **Remove sits flush against Add** (defect). The × shares a border with + in one 73 px strip, so the most common edit (add one more) and the destructive one (drop the item) are 24 px apart with no gap. A slip of the mouse, or of a thumb on a tablet, deletes the line instead. Apple HIG and Material both separate destructive actions from safe ones and give them specific labels. Fix: take remove out of the stepper. Make it a text link such as "Remove" under the product name or at the far right, set apart from the stepper, and offer undo rather than a confirmation dialog.
3. **The steppers are small and crowded for what they do** (convention). The facts file has each button at 25×25 px (`targets`, `div.qty > button`) with the buttons touching. That clears WCAG 2.5.8's 24 px floor, so the file lists no violation, but it's well under 44 pt / 48 dp if this layout reaches touch screens, and Material asks for about 8 dp between neighbours. These are the only controls on the page apart from Check out. Fix: grow them to at least 32–40 px. Once the quantity number sits between − and + (finding 1), the buttons are spaced apart as a side effect.

**Smaller things.**
- **The type scale is muddled.** The facts file lists seven sizes: 28, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14 (`type_sizes`). The logo (19), "Summary" (18), product names (17), prices (16) and summary rows (15) are each one px apart and read as the same level picked five times (Bringhurst, §3.1.1). Keep 28 / 17 / 14 and let weight handle the rest.
- **The product thumbnails are flat colour swatches**, not pictures of tea, a teapot or a scoop. If these are placeholders or images that failed to load, they aren't a finding. If they're the intended design, the shopper loses the quickest way to recognise each line. Put real product photos in.

**What's working.**
- **Check out** is the only filled, high-contrast element on the page. Its white-on-#1f2328 is 15.8:1 at 272×50 px, and it sits directly under the total, so the next step is obvious. Keep it the only primary action.
- **The summary card** is short and well ordered: Items, Shipping, then Total in bold, all right-aligned so the figures line up for checking. Shipping is shown before checkout, not sprung on the shopper later.
- **The row structure is clean.** Thumbnail, name with a plain-language size underneath ("100 g tin", "50 g pouch"), then controls, then price, on consistent left edges (212 px for text) with right-aligned prices. It scans in one pass.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover, pressed and focus states on the stepper and Check out, which matter here because the stepper buttons are tiny. Also whether pressing × removes at once or offers undo.
