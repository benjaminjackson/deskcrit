**First read.** The eye goes to the photo before the words: the green valley and the river curving through the lower middle. The headline comes second, and only just, because its white letters sit on pale sky and partly wash out. The white line below it runs across the dark ridge and treeline, so some words read and some don't. It reads as a small lodge site, quiet and outdoorsy. When the eye reaches the bottom of the river there is nothing to do next.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage of a small lodge, meant to make a traveller want to stay and then book.

**Verdict.** The page has a real idea and good, specific copy, but it fails at two basic things: the white headline is hard to read on the bright sky, and there is no way to book except a plain nav word. It sells the view and then gives the viewer no next step.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero text is hard to read on the photo (defect).** "Wake up above the river" (40 px bold white, `h1`) sits on pale, hazy sky, where white on near-white barely holds. The sentence below it (17 px white, `div.copy > p`) runs across the far ridge and the treeline, so "each with a porch facing east" switches from light ground to dark mid-line. The facts file marks both blocks "not measurable: background image", so the code check can't vouch for them, and by eye the headline is the faintest text on the page. WCAG (Techniques F83) asks for contrast against the worst point behind the letters, not the average. Fix: put a dark scrim or gradient behind the top-left text, or darken the sky in the image, so every letter stands out from what is directly behind it. Keep the photo itself. It's the best thing on the page.
2. **No booking action, and nothing leads to one (defect).** The only way to book is "Book" at the top right, set exactly like "Rooms" and "Trails" (15 px regular, `nav > a:nth-of-type(3)`). The hero has no button. The three cards below are plain text, and there's no price, dates, or availability anywhere. A traveller who is sold by the headline has to hunt for what to do. The river also leads the eye down and off the bottom edge, with nothing waiting there (Arnheim on the eye path). Material and Apple HIG both say a view should have one primary action that clearly stands out. Fix: put one filled button under the hero copy, and make it say exactly what happens, e.g. "Check cabin dates" rather than "Book". Give the nav "Book" the same emphasis so it matches. If you can show a starting price per night, put it next to the button, because that's the number a traveller compares against other places.
3. **The cards below the photo tell you facts but don't say what they mean for you (taste, copy).** "Three marked walks start at the lodge door" and "The shuttle meets the morning train" are concrete, and that's good. But they stop at the fact, and they lead nowhere: Rooms and Trails don't link anywhere visible, and Getting here doesn't say which train or how far the station is. Make each card end on the payoff for the visitor ("no car needed: the shuttle meets the 9:40 train") and on a link to more.

**Smaller things.**
- Type sizes: there are four close sizes: 19 (logo), 17 (headings and the hero sentence), 17 again, and 15 (body). The 17 px hero sentence and the 15 px card text are both plain paragraphs a size step apart that doesn't mean anything. Use one paragraph size and get the difference from weight or colour instead (Bringhurst 3.1.1).
- Single words alone on a last line: "stove." under Rooms and "station." under Getting here. Rewrap or adjust the copy (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The 40 px headline looks set at text tracking, so it reads slightly loose. Tighten it a little (Bringhurst 2.1.1).

**What's working.**
- The headline and photo make one idea: "Wake up above the river", and the picture is that view from above. Keep that pairing.
- The copy is specific in a way most lodge sites aren't: twelve cabins, porches facing east, breakfast from six, a wood stove in every cabin. It sounds like a real place run by a person.
- The layout below the photo is clean: three equal columns on the 64 px margin, headings sitting tight on their text (6 px), and 15.8:1 contrast. Don't add boxes or ornament to it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus styles on the nav links; whether the hero crop keeps the text clear of busy areas on narrower screens; whether the photo's slight softness is the source file or the capture (check it at full size on a high-density screen).
