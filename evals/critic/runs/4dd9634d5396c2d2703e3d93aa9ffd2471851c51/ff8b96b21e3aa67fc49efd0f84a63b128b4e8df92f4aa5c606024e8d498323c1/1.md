**First read.** My eye goes straight to the river curling through the valley, then up to the headline. The headline sits in white on the brightest part of the sky, so it looks faded until you look for it. After that the eye drops to the three short columns along the bottom and stops. It reads as a small, calm, unfussy lodge website. It's pleasant, but nothing on the page invites you to book.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage for a small lodge, meant to get people choosing a trip away to want to stay and then book.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't do its job yet. The picture and the copy set a real mood, but the headline is hard to read against the sky, and the page never gives a way to book. The only route is a "Book" link in the menu, styled the same as "Rooms" and "Trails".

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline and subhead are washed out by the photo** (defect). "Wake up above the river" is white bold 40 px (`font_size`, `h1`), set over pale cloud and haze at rows 120–168. Some letters nearly vanish into the sky, so the line you most need to read is the one you have to work at. The facts file can't measure this (`contrast`: "not measurable: background image"). By eye, the first line of the subhead ("…porch facing east.") is also weak where it crosses the bright ridge and cloud. WCAG F83 says contrast must hold at the worst point behind the text, not on average. Fix: put a dark scrim or gradient behind the top-left text block, or move the text to a darker, calmer part of the image. Every letter should stand clear of what's directly behind it.
2. **There's no way to book** (defect). The page has no button. "Book" is a 15 px regular link in the top-right menu (`nav > a:nth-of-type(3)`), looking exactly like "Rooms" and "Trails". Someone won over by the photo has to hunt for the next step, and nothing marks it as the one that matters. Material Design and Apple HIG both call for one primary action per view that stands above the rest. Fix: add one filled button under the subhead, and name the exact thing it does ("Check dates for a cabin"), not just "Book". Restyle the menu's "Book" so it stands apart from the other links too.
3. **The facts a booker needs are missing** (common sense, not from a cited source). There's no price, no "from £X a night", no dates, no availability. "Cabins sleep two to four" is the only number that helps someone plan. A visitor weighing this against somewhere else can't compare without clicking away. Fix: put a starting price and a dates or availability cue next to the new button. A guest compares lodges on price and dates, so give them those up front.

**Smaller things.**
- Card text breaks: "stove." and "station." sit alone on the last lines of the Rooms and Getting here cards (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Fix: adjust the wording or column width so they don't strand.
- Type scale: below the 40 px headline, the sizes are 19 (logo), 17 (card headings and subhead) and 15 (body and menu), only 2 px apart. The headings rely on bold to stand out, which works, but the steps are small enough to look picked one at a time (Bringhurst 3.1.1). Fix: drop to two sizes, headline and text, and let weight do the rest.
- Copy: "the sun reach the far wall". A stranger doesn't know what wall, and the photo shows an open valley, not a wall. Fix: name it ("the canyon wall") or cut it. "Above the river" also appears in both the headline and the next sentence. Spend that second mention on something new.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and sounds like a real place: twelve cabins, porches facing east, breakfast from six, a shuttle that meets the morning train. Keep that voice. It's what stops this from looking like a template, so put the price and the button in the same voice.
- The headline and photo make one idea. "Wake up above the river" over a high view of the river is exactly the promise.
- The lower section is tidy: three equal 357 px columns on the 64 px margin, headings 6 px from their text, plenty of air between groups, and body text contrast of 15.8:1.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus visibility on the menu links, and how the top-left text and the photo crop hold up on a phone.
