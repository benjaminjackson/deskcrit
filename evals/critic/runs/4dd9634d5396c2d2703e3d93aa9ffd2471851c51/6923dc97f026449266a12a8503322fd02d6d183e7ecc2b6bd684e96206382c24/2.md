**First read.** The eye lands first on the black **Sign out** button, the only solid dark shape on the screen. Only after that does it go up to "Profile" and down the three fields. It reads as a tidy, ordinary account-settings page in a SaaS product. It feels calm and competent but generic. The one loud thing on the page is the action you'd least want someone to press here.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a desktop web settings screen where a signed-in user checks or edits their name, email and time zone, then saves.

**Verdict.** The form itself is clean and almost right. What gets in the way is the button row: the page's strongest visual weight sits on "Sign out", 12 px from "Save changes". So the action that ends the session looks like the main one, and the action the page exists for looks like an afterthought.

**What would move it most**
1. **Sign out is styled as the primary action and parked next to Save** (button row, y≈501–543). Sign out is filled near-black (#1f2328 with white text, `div.row > button:nth-of-type(2)`), and nothing else on the page is that dark. Save changes is pale grey on grey (#a3a3a3 on #eef0f2), so it disappears even though it's the reason the page exists. That causes three problems:
   - Someone who has just edited a field looks for "the button" and finds Sign out.
   - The two buttons are the same height and 12 px apart, so a mis-click throws away unsaved edits and the session.
   - Sign out doesn't change anything on this form. It doesn't belong in the form's action row at all.

   **Convention** (Material Design 3, "Buttons": one high-emphasis action per view; Norman, natural mapping; Nielsen/Jakob's law: users expect sign out in the account menu or at the foot of the side nav). **Defect** in consequence: it misdirects the eye and invites a costly slip. **Fix:** make Save changes the only filled button and give it the filled style once it's enabled. Move Sign out out of the form, either to the bottom of the sidebar or to an account menu, as a quiet text-style action. If it has to stay on this page, put it in its own section well below the form.

2. **The Time zone field looks like free text and shows a system ID** (third field, y≈410). "Europe/Lisbon" sits in a plain box with no chevron, the same as the Name and Email fields. That tells the user to type, so they'll type "Lisbon" or "GMT" and get an error or a wrong zone. "Europe/Lisbon" is also the IANA identifier, not how a person names their time. Reminders and reports depend on this field, so a wrong value has a real cost. **Defect** (Nielsen 5, error prevention; Nielsen 2, the user's own words). **Fix:** make it a searchable picker with a visible dropdown sign. Show values the way people say them, for example "Lisbon (GMT+0, Western European Time)".

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale is muddy at the small end.** I count five sizes: 28, 18, 16, 15 and 14 px (`type_sizes`). The lede (16), the labels and nav (15) and the help text (14) are each one pixel apart, so they read as near-misses, not levels (Bringhurst §3.1.1; Dair). The labels already work because of their bold weight. Collapse 14/15/16 to two sizes and let weight or a softer grey carry the help text.

**What's working.**
- **The field groups are well built.** Each one is a bold label, then the field, then a one-line help note. The gap inside a group is about 6 px and the gap between groups is about 24 px (`spacing`), so each label clearly belongs to its own field. Keep that rhythm.
- **The help copy is the best thing on the page.** "Shown on your comments and in the team list", "We send receipts and sign-in links here" and "Changes apply to every workspace you belong to" each tell the user what the setting does to them, not what the field is. That's rare. Keep it.
- **Save changes is disabled until there are edits.** That's a sensible guard. It's exempt from contrast rules as a disabled control, and it's clearly different from an active button. It just needs to become the strong button once it's enabled.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Save's enabled look.** Whether Save becomes filled and prominent once a field changes. If it does, finding 1 shrinks to the placement of Sign out.
- **Focus indicators** on the fields and buttons.
- **Unsaved-changes warning.** Whether leaving the page (or clicking Sign out) with unsaved edits warns the user.
