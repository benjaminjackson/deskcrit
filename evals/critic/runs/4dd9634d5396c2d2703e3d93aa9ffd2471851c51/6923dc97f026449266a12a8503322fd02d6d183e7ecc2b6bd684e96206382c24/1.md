**First read.** The eye goes to "Profile", then straight down to the black **Sign out** button, the heaviest thing on the screen. Next to it, "Save changes" is so pale that it looks like part of the background. It reads as a plain, tidy account-settings page for a SaaS team tool, calm and competent. The one loud note is on the wrong button.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: a desktop web settings screen where a signed-in user checks and edits their name, email and time zone, then saves.

**Verdict.** Most of this is clean and it nearly works. The one serious problem is the action row: it makes leaving the page look like the main thing to do, and puts that exit 12 px from Save. The free-typed time zone field is a smaller, real risk.

**What would move it most**
1. **Sign out is styled and placed as this form's primary action.** *Defect.* In the action row (324–587 px across, 501 px down), Sign out is the only filled, high-contrast button: white on #1f2328 at 15.8:1. "Save changes" is #a3a3a3 on #eef0f2 at 2.21:1 (`contrast`, `div.row > button:nth-of-type(1)`). Being disabled exempts Save from the contrast minimum, so that isn't the issue. The issue is what the viewer sees:
   - The only strong button on the page ends their session.
   - It sits where the submit button belongs, 12 px from Save.
   - Someone who has just edited their name and goes for the "big button" may sign out instead of saving. Depending on how unsaved edits are handled, they may lose those edits.

   Sign out isn't a change to this form, so it also breaks natural mapping (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*). It belongs to the account or session, not to the Profile fields. Material Design and Apple HIG both say one action per view is dominant, and secondary or exit actions are styled lower. The fix:
   - Make Save the only filled button. Once a field changes, give it the dark fill.
   - Move Sign out out of the form row entirely, for example to the foot of the sidebar or an account menu, as a text or outline button.
   - Keep Save's disabled state, but make it look like a resting button rather than an empty grey slab. Today a first-time viewer can't tell Save is a button at all.
2. **Time zone is a free-text field showing a system ID.** *Defect.* The third field (324, 410) looks like the other two text inputs, with no chevron or picker cue, and holds "Europe/Lisbon". A user will type "Lisbon", "WET" or "GMT", and either get an error or a time zone that's quietly wrong. The helper line says that time zone is what reminders and reports follow, so a wrong value means reminders at the wrong hour. Nielsen's heuristic 5 (error prevention) asks for constrained input here. "Europe/Lisbon" is also the IANA system name, not the user's own words (Nielsen's heuristic 2). The fix: a searchable select that shows a human label with the offset, such as "Lisbon (GMT+0)", and ideally detects the time zone by default.

**Smaller things.**
- The type sizes sit too close: 16 px lede, 15 px labels, nav and buttons, and 14 px helpers (`type_sizes`). They are all the same weight-400 #1f2328 except the bold labels, so the lede and the helper lines read as the same level. Drop the three-way 14/15/16 split to two sizes, and set the helper text apart by colour: a mid grey still above 4.5:1 (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1; Dair, ch. 8).

**What's working.**
- **The form is easy to scan.** Each field is a bold label, a 40 px input and a one-line helper on one left edge at x=324, with tight spacing inside each group and clear gaps between groups. Keep that rhythm.
- **The helper copy is unusually good.** "Shown on your comments and in the team list" and "We send receipts and sign-in links here" each tell the user what the field means for them, in their own terms. The lede "Changes apply to every workspace you belong to" answers the scope question before anyone has to ask it.
- **The sidebar always shows where you are.** "Profile" is the current page, shown by a tint plus bold, so it doesn't rely on colour alone. The 43 px rows are generous targets.
