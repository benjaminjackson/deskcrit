**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a ferry company's home page. It's for people planning a crossing who need to know when boats run, whether to book, and how to do it.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound and the writing is unusually human, but the page doesn't yet do its job. The headline holds the most important fact and is the weakest thing in value. That fact (the times) can be read two ways. And the one thing car drivers are told to do, booking, has nowhere to happen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is greyed out, so it loses to smaller elements.** *Defect.* "Crossings every forty minutes…" is by far the biggest type, but it's set in mid-grey. The small bold label above it and the button below are solid black, so they outweigh it, and the eye lands on them first. The headline then reads as secondary or disabled. It looks like it just clears the 3:1 minimum for large text, so this isn't mainly a legibility problem. It's a problem of order: the page's main claim is the quietest thing on it. Tschichold asks that the order of importance be plain. Fix: set the headline in the same near-black as the body text so size and value agree. If you want a quieter accent, use it on the small label, not the headline.
2. **The times can be read two ways, and the route is never named.** *Defect.* "From the first boat at six until the last at eleven" gives no am or pm. 6am to 11pm is the likely meaning, but someone planning around the last boat can't be sure. Mistaking a late boat for a morning one, or the reverse, means a missed crossing. Nowhere does the page say where the ferry goes from and to. A first-time visitor can learn how often boats run but not between which places. Fix: write the times so they can't be misread ("6am … 11pm") and name the crossing in the headline or the label ("Winter timetable · Port A to Port B").
3. **Drivers are told to book, but there's nothing to book with.** *Defect (common sense; no approved source).* "Book a car space up to a day ahead" is the one instruction with a deadline in it. Yet the only button is "See the timetable", and the menu offers Timetable, Fares and Help, with no Book. A driver has to guess where booking lives. The sentence is also ambiguous. It can mean "you can't book more than a day ahead" or "book no later than the day before", and the two lead to opposite behaviour. Fix: say exactly when booking opens or closes, and put a booking action where drivers will look for it, either next to that sentence or as the primary button. Keep "See the timetable" as the secondary action.
4. **The page looks like a template.** *Taste.* It's all black, white and grey, with an empty right half and three thin-ruled feature cards. Nothing marks it as a ferry company or this ferry company in particular, so it gives the visitor no sense of place or trust beyond "competent". The copy already has warmth (bikes on racks, dogs in the rear lounge). Fix: add one thing specific to this service, such as a photo of the boat or harbour, a map of the route, or a single colour from the brand, in the empty right half.

**Smaller things.**
- "eleven" sits alone on the third line of the headline. Rebreak it or widen the column so the last line isn't a single word. Fixing the times in point 2 may solve this anyway.
- "lounge." sits alone on the second line of the Dogs card. Trim the copy or widen the column.

**What's working.**
- The copy talks like a person ("can simply turn up and buy a ticket at the gate", "Welcome on the open deck"). Keep that voice when you add the missing facts.
- The left edge is clean and the three cards sit on a clear grid. Each card has a heavy rule, a bold heading close to its line, and clearly more space around it than inside it, so the cards scan instantly.
- The type scale is tight: a headline, a lead paragraph, card headings and small text, with no sizes fighting each other.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button and menu links. How the empty right half and the three cards reflow on a phone.
