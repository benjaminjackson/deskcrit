**First read.** The eye lands on the big headline, but because it's pale grey it doesn't stay there. It moves down to the near-black paragraph and then to the black button, which is the darkest thing on the page. It reads as a ferry operator's home page: calm, tidy and plain. It could be any small transport company. Nothing tells you where the boat goes.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: the home page of a small car-and-foot-passenger ferry. Visitors want to know when the boats run, whether they can bring a car, bike or dog, and how to get on.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and mostly works, but the words let it down. The page never says where the ferry goes or when "winter" runs. Its one commitment, about booking, can be read two ways. It also tells drivers to book but gives them no way to do it. The headline holds the most useful fact on the page, yet it's the faintest text there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy leaves out what a traveller acts on** (defect).
   - **No route.** Nothing in the header, eyebrow or headline names the two ports. Someone arriving from a search can't confirm this is their crossing.
   - **No dates.** "Winter timetable" doesn't say when winter starts or ends, so someone planning a March trip can't tell whether these times apply.
   - **The booking line reads two ways.** "Book a car space up to a day ahead" can mean "you can book at most one day ahead" or "you must book at least a day ahead". A driver who reads it the wrong way either misses the booking window or worries for nothing.
   - **The times are spelled out.** "From the first boat at six until the last at eleven" makes the reader turn words into times and assume am and pm.
   - Fix: name the route ("Portsea ⇄ Hollin Isle", or whatever the ports are). Give the season dates. Write the times as numbers ("every 40 minutes, 06:00 to 23:00"). Make the booking rule state one meaning ("You can book a car space from 24 hours before you sail").
2. **Drivers are told to book and given no way to do it** (taste and common sense, not a published rule). The intro mentions booking, and the cards say cars fit on every crossing. But the only button is "See the timetable", which repeats a nav link and a timetable the headline has already summed up. The header has no "Book" link either. A driver who has decided to go has to go looking. Fix: make the primary button the action drivers came to take ("Book a car space"). Keep the timetable as a secondary link next to it.
3. **The headline is the faintest text on the page** (taste). It's mid-grey, while the eyebrow, intro, card text and button are near-black. Being large, it still clears the 3:1 contrast threshold for large text (it looks about 4:1). But the most important sentence recedes behind the explanatory one under it, and the black button wins the first look. That's why the eye slides off the headline in the first read. Fix: set the headline in the same near-black as the text, or darker. If you need contrast, make the eyebrow or the intro lighter instead.
4. **"eleven" sits alone on the third headline line** (convention: Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* §2.4.2). The break after "the last at" separates the time from its phrase. The headline gains a whole extra line, and the time that matters lands as an afterthought. Fix: break the lines by phrase, or tighten the copy (fix 1 does this) so it fits in two lines.

**Smaller things.**
- "Help" in the nav ends about 14 px short of the right edge of the cards (about 1146 against 1160). It looks like a near-miss. Align the nav's right edge to the card column.
- Text sizes sit close together: intro about 18 px, card headings about 18 px bold, card text and eyebrow about 16 px. Use one body size and let weight do the rest (Bringhurst §3.1.1).
- "lounge." sits alone on the last line of the Dogs card. Rewrap it, or shorten the line.

**What's working.**
- The three cards (Cars and vans, Bikes, Dogs) answer the questions people actually ask before a ferry trip, in one short line each, on a clean three-column grid with baselines that line up. Keep them.
- The quiet single-column hero with plenty of space suits a simple service. Don't fill the empty right side just for the sake of it.
- There's one clear primary button with a large, obvious target.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the button and links look on hover and focus, and how the three cards stack on a phone.
