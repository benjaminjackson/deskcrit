**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, then drops to the byline, which looks sharper and blacker than anything below it. The three paragraphs come next and look washed out by comparison, like secondary text or a summary in front of the real article. It reads as a calm, well-made blog or how-to post for someone who does practical rural work. It feels quiet and trustworthy, but the article text feels pushed back.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a long-form how-to article on a small editorial site, and its job is to be read start to finish by someone who has a wall to repair.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The measure, leading and spacing are good. The one real problem is the value order: the article text is the palest text on the page, so the byline and the menu outrank the content the reader came for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is lighter than everything that supports it.** *(Defect.)* The paragraphs are #6d6d6d (`color`, `article > p:nth-of-type(2–4)`). The byline, nav and logo are all #1f2328 (`p.by`, `nav > a`, `strong`). So the 15 px byline looks heavier than the 17 px text under it, and the eye reads "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" as more important than the instructions. The body passes WCAG at 5.17:1 (`contrast`), so this isn't an accessibility failure. It is a hierarchy failure: the levels below the headline run backwards (Müller-Brockmann; Lupton and Phillips, distinct levels of importance). Over 500 characters of reading, grey text also tires the eye more than it needs to. **Fix:** make the body the darkest text after the headline, at or close to #1f2328. If the byline needs to sit apart, make it the grey one: metadata is the thing that should recede.

**Smaller things.**
- **Sizes too close together:** the logo is 18 px, the body 17 px, and the byline and nav 15 px (`type_sizes`). They are close enough to look picked one at a time (Bringhurst, §3.1.1). Use 34 / 17 / 15, set the logo at body size and keep it bold, and let colour rather than a near-identical size separate the byline from the body.
- **No current section in the nav:** "Stories", "Guides" and "About" all look the same, so the reader can't tell which section this article belongs to. It reads like a guide. Mark that item with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- **Byline date has no year (copy):** "March 14" with no year leaves a reader of a repair guide unable to tell whether the advice is current or several winters old. Add the year.

**What's working.**
- The reading column is right: about 64–65 characters per line (`characters_per_line`) at 1.65 line height on 17 px Inter. Keep that measure and leading.
- The headline is set tight at 1.25, breaks cleanly at "after / a hard winter", and is the one clear dominant element.
- The copy is very good: concrete, ordered, and spoken to the reader ("Before you move a stone, walk the full run…"). Every paragraph tells you what to do and why it matters to you. Don't let a redesign break it into bullet points.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the nav links.
