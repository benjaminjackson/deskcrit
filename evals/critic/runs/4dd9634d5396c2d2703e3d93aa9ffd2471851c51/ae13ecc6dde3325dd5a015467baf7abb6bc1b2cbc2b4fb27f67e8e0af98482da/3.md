**First read.** The eye lands on the bold two-line headline. The next thing it finds is the byline, and only after that the article, which sits in a pale grey block below. It reads as a calm how-to article on a small editorial site. It is tidy and easy to read, but the darkest small text on the page is the author's name and the date, not the instructions. The look is anonymous: Inter on white, a centred column, a thin header rule.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: an article page that people read to learn how to repair a dry stone wall, viewed on desktop.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The measure, the leading and the spacing are well judged and nothing gets in the way of reading. The one thing holding it back is that the emphasis runs backwards: the article is set lighter than the metadata around it, so the part people came for reads as the secondary part.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is greyer than everything around it** (defect). All three paragraphs are #6d6d6d (`color`, `article > p`). The byline, the nav and the site name are near-black #1f2328. The byline is also smaller: 15 px against the body's 17 px (`font_size`, `p.by`). So "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" outweighs the instructions, and in grayscale the page's darkest small text is its least important. The body passes contrast at 5.17:1, so this is not a legibility failure. The cost is that the article looks washed out and slightly faded, and it is the thing the reader has to stay with longest. Refactoring UI's advice to use lighter colour, not a smaller size, for less important text points the other way: the de-emphasised colour belongs on the byline, not the body. Fix: set the body in the dark text colour, or close to it, and move the grey to the byline and the nav. Then the size and colour steps agree: headline, then body, then metadata.
2. **The type scale is crowded at the small end** (convention). There are four sizes: 34, 18, 17 and 15 px (`type_sizes`). The 18 px site name and 17 px body are a pixel apart, and the 15 px byline and nav sit only two steps below the body. None of those three steps reads as a separate level, which is the kind of near-collision Bringhurst warns about when sizes are picked one at a time (*Elements*, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Dair makes the same point: a contrast of one step looks like an accident. Fix: keep the headline and the body, then make the byline and the nav clearly smaller or clearly lighter, but do it with one deliberate step. The site name can stay bold at body size.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "after / a hard winter", splitting the phrase. Break before "after" so that "Repairing a dry stone wall / after a hard winter" reads as two units (this is my judgment; the source on heading breaks is unconfirmed).
- The 34 px headline looks set at text tracking and reads a little loose. Tighten the letter spacing slightly at that size (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80; Bringhurst 2.1.1).
- Nothing in the nav (Stories, Guides, About) marks the current section, and the page doesn't say whether this is a Story or a Guide. Mark the current item with one cue, such as weight or an underline (NN/g, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Copy: "March 14" has no year. On a how-to, readers want to know how old the advice is. Add the year.
- Taste: the template is so neutral that nothing on the page feels like stone, fields or hand work. The cost is that it could be anyone's blog. One typographic choice with some character, such as a serif for the headline or the text, would give Field Notes a voice without touching the layout.

**What's working.**
- The reading column is right. Body lines run 64 to 65 characters (`characters_per_line`) at 17 px with 1.65 leading, right in Bringhurst's range, so long reading will be comfortable. Keep this exactly.
- The spacing groups things correctly: 12 px from the headline to the byline, about 27 px to the first paragraph, and about 18 px between paragraphs. The headline belongs to its byline, and the article starts as a clear block.
- The headline and the copy are specific and concrete. "Frost does most of its damage out of sight" is a strong opening line, and the instructions say who does what and when ("walk the full run… mark every bulge with a stick").

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The article stops at "every metre", with about 150 px of empty white below and no ending, footer or next step. Check in a live browser whether the page really ends there. If it does, the reader is left with nowhere to go, such as a related guide or the next part.
- Focus states on the nav links.
