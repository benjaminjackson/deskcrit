**First read.** The eye goes to the bold two-line headline, drops to the byline, then into a column of grey body text. It reads as a quiet, plain article on a practical craft topic, probably for landowners or hobbyists. It feels calm and tidy. The one off note is the byline: it looks darker and firmer than the article under it, so the article itself feels faded.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is an article page on a small editorial site, and its job is to get someone to read a how-to guide on repairing a dry stone wall from start to finish.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The headline leads, the column has a comfortable width and spacing, and the copy is clear and specific. The main problem is that the article text is the palest thing on the page. The byline, the nav and the site name are all drawn darker than the paragraphs you came to read.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is lighter than the metadata around it, and the smaller sizes are too close together** (defect, minor). The three paragraphs are 17 px in `#6d6d6d` (`font_size`, `color`, `article > p`). The byline is 15 px in near-black `#1f2328` (`p.by`), and so are the nav links. The site name is 18 px, one pixel from the body (`strong`). So the eye sees "By Ellen Marsh, March 14" as firmer than the article, and the body reads as secondary text. Body contrast is 5.17:1, which passes WCAG 1.4.3. This is a problem of ranking, not legibility: the most important text has the least ink. The type also uses four sizes (34/18/17/15), and the lower three are one to two pixels apart, so they look like accidents rather than levels (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1: "a modest set of distinct and related intervals"). Dair's point applies too: a difference has to be obvious or it reads as a mistake. **Fix:** give the body the dark ink (`#1f2328` or close to it), and make the byline the grey, quieter level. Then settle on three sizes, for example 34 headline, 17 body, 14–15 meta. The site name should take its weight from bold, not from a one-pixel size step.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: Stories, Guides and About all look the same, so a reader can't tell which section this article belongs to. Mark the current section with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist", guideline 5).
- Copy, my judgement: "March 14" has no year. For seasonal how-to advice, readers can't tell how current it is. Add the year.

**What's working.**
- The column is set well for reading. Lines run 64–65 characters (`characters_per_line`), right in Bringhurst's 45–75 range, with a 1.65 line height and 18 px between paragraphs. Keep that measure and spacing.
- The spacing groups things correctly. The byline sits 12 px under the headline and 28 px above the body, so headline and byline read as one unit. All the article blocks share one left edge at 370 px.
- The copy is concrete and speaks to the reader: "mark every bulge with a stick", "the cheapest repair you will ever make". It starts with the reader's own problem, a section sagging toward the lane, which is exactly what a how-to should do.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav links.
