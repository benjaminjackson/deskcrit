**First read.** This looks like the home page of a small plant nursery: one headline, a short line about what they grow, then three cards (Perennials, Fruit trees, Workshops). The only thing that changes between the two versions is the headline size. In tile 1 the eye has no clear place to land. It moves between the headline and the three card titles, because they all look like the same bold size. In tile 2 the headline is plainly the first thing you read, and the cards follow as the next level down.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a landing page for a local nursery that should tell a visitor in a glance what makes these plants different (grown outdoors and hardy), then send them to a section.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The headline is set far larger than everything else, so the page's one idea, "hardy plants grown on our hillside," lands first and the cards read as the follow-up.
2. Tile 1: The headline looks the same size and weight as the card titles under it, so four bold lines compete and the selling point reads as just another label.

**What decides it.**
- **Which element comes first.** In tile 1 the headline is about 26 px tall and bold. "Perennials," "Fruit trees," and "Workshops" are about the same size and weight, in the same dark green. Four elements are equal and none wins, so the visitor scans all of them before learning what the page is about. In tile 2 the headline is about 60 px, a cap height roughly two and a half times the card titles. The order of importance is obvious right away. These sizes are by eye from the full-size images. This is a **defect** in tile 1 under "One element dominates" (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show logical order with differences of size; Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*: contrast of much against little).
- **Levels of text.** In tile 1 the headline and the card headings are one size apart at most, so the page title and the section headings read as the same level. Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8, says a contrast that small looks like an accident. Tile 2 clearly separates the headline, the card headings, and the body text. This is a **convention**, and breaking it costs tile 1 the start of its hierarchy.
- **What tile 2 gives up.** At this size the headline breaks and leaves "hillside" alone on the second line, and it pushes the cards about 95 px further down. The lone word is a small fix: widen the measure a little or rebalance the break. It doesn't change the ranking, since the cards are still fully visible in the first screen.

Best: tile 2
