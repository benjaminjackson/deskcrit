I compared both versions at full size and checked the deciding differences against the canon. Tile 1 is the better version.

**First read.** This looks like an internal sales dashboard for Tidewater Supply. Someone on the team opens it to answer one question: how is October going? Both versions have the same eight metrics and the same weekly revenue chart. Only the top two rows of cards differ. In tile 1 the eye goes straight to Revenue. In tile 2 it has to scan a row of seven equal cards to find a starting point.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an at-a-glance month-to-date sales summary for staff, where revenue is the headline number and the other metrics explain it.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: Revenue gets a double-width card and a much larger figure, so the page starts with the number that matters most. The eight cards fill a clean 4-column grid with no holes.
2. Tile 2: All seven cards are the same size and weight, so no metric leads. Moving Returns into the top row leaves an empty card-sized gap at the right end of the second row.

**What decides it**
- **Which number gets seen first (defect in tile 2).**
  - Tile 1: the Revenue card runs from x 48 to 630, about 582 px wide. That is two columns, against 281 px for every other card. Its "$184,320" stands about 50 px tall, against about 35 px for "1,406", "$131" and the rest.
  - Tile 2: Revenue is the same 281 px card and the same ~35 px figure as Orders, Returns or Items per order.
  - What the viewer gets: in tile 2, seven equal figures compete, so finding how the month is going takes a scan instead of a glance. Tile 1 shows the headline first and lets the six supporting metrics follow at a lower level.
  - Canon: layout.md "One element dominates" (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*). Its Look for describes tile 2's problem exactly: "three or more elements of the same size and weight competing to be seen first."
- **The hole in row two (taste, with some convention behind it).**
  - Tile 2: the empty slot at about x 951 to 1232, y 333 to 489, is exactly one card wide. It reads as a missing card or a failed load, not as deliberate space. It also makes the right side of the grid lighter than the left.
  - Tile 1: both rows run edge to edge.
  - Canon: this is close to layout.md "White space is gathered, not scattered" (Ruder): empty space should look planned. The entry doesn't name a leftover grid cell, though, so treat this as my judgment. It's also secondary to the hierarchy point.
- **What's the same in both and doesn't affect the pick:** the header, the "October so far" title, the green change lines, and the chart. One caution for whoever reviews the winner: the chart only fills the left half of its card, which may be a capture issue. Returns at "-0.4 points" is also shown in green, the same color as gains. That's correct if lower returns are good, but it's worth confirming.

Best: tile 1
