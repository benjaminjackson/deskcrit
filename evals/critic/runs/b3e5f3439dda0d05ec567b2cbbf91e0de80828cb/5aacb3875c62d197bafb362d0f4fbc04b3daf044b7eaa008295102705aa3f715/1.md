**First read.** This looks like the "about" section of a local food bank's website, aimed at neighbors, donors, and possible volunteers. In both versions the eye lands on the "What we do each week" headline and then drops into the two paragraphs. The two versions are the same in every way except one: in tile 1 the two body paragraphs are centered, and in tile 2 they are set flush left.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The headline, paragraphs, and stats all start from one left edge at x≈96. You read straight down a single column, and every line starts in the same place.
2. Tile 1: Each paragraph is four lines long and centered. Every line starts at a different spot, and the text block floats away from the left edge that the headline and the stats share.

**What decides it.**
- **Where the paragraphs start (defect).** In tile 1, the lines of the first paragraph start anywhere from about x≈102 to x≈337, and the last line of each paragraph is a short stub in the middle ("and growers have some to spare.", "parcel to your door."). On every line the eye has to hunt for the start, which slows reading. This is the paragraph that tells someone in need how to get food: referral, four centres, home delivery. In tile 2 every line starts at x=96, so the text reads in one pass. This is the entry "Paragraphs are not centered or right-aligned": paragraphs of several lines go flush left, and centering is kept for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3).
- **Shared left edge (convention).** In tile 1 the headline and the 412 / 96 / 4 stats are left-aligned, but the centered text between them sits on its own axis. That breaks the page's one strong alignment, so the column looks like it was assembled from separate pieces. Tile 2 keeps all three groups on one edge ("Alignment: every element has a visible relationship", Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).

Nothing else differs between the two. The nav, type sizes, colors, line length, and the stats row are the same in both.

Best: tile 2
