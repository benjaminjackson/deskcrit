I compared the two versions at full size, read the canon entries that cover the deciding difference, and Tile 1 is the better version.

**First read.** This looks like a sales page for a pottery studio's 8-week evening wheel-throwing course for beginners. The goal is to get someone to book. The two versions have the same copy, type and price card. The only change is alignment: tile 1 is set flush left on the same edge as the "Kiln Yard Studio" wordmark, and tile 2 centers every block on the page.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: The two description paragraphs read easily with a straight left edge. The eyebrow line, headline, paragraphs and price card all hang from the wordmark's left edge, so the page reads top to bottom as one column.
2. Tile 2: The two description paragraphs (four lines and two lines) are centered, so each line starts somewhere new. The centered column also leaves the wordmark alone on a second, left-hand axis.

**What decides it**
- **Centered paragraphs (defect).** In tile 2, the opening description runs four lines and each one starts at a different x. "at your own wheel." sits alone in the middle, and the second paragraph ends on a short centered line too. The reader has to find the start of every line, which slows the one block that explains what you get for $320. In tile 1 the same text is flush left and reads at normal speed. Source: "Paragraphs are not centered or right-aligned" (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst 2.1.3), which says to keep centering for a few short lines.
- **Everything on one central axis, with the wordmark off it (convention, with a real cost).** Tile 2 centers the eyebrow line, headline, paragraphs, price card, card contents and button. The wordmark stays at the left, about 88 px in, so the page has two unrelated axes and the header looks detached from the content. In tile 1, every content block starts on the wordmark's edge, about 88 px in. Source: "No central axis by default" (Tschichold), which says centering is a choice for short lines, not the default for a whole page. Nothing in this content calls for a symmetric, formal layout.
- **What tile 2 has going for it (taste).** At 1280 px wide, tile 1 leaves the right half of the page empty and its weight sits to the left. Tile 2 looks more balanced (Arnheim, "Balance"). But that empty space sits together in one calm area, and a left-aligned reading column is normal for a web page. It doesn't outweigh the slower reading and split axes in tile 2. A fair fix for tile 1 would be to put something on the right, such as a photo of the work. That would be new content, so it's the studio's decision.

Best: tile 1
