**First read.** This is a long-read article page for a cycling site, a route guide meant to be read start to finish. The two versions are the same layout, type, measure, and spacing. The only difference is the body text color. In tile 1 the paragraphs are a mid gray. In tile 2 they are a dark gray, close to the byline.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: the body text reads firmly against white, so the reader never strains over the part of the page they came for.
2. Tile 1: the body text looks faint. On a page that is almost all body text, that weakens the whole piece.

**What decides it.**
- **Body text contrast (defect).** I compared both versions at full size (1280 px wide). The body copy looks about 19 px. That is below WCAG's large-text threshold, so it needs 4.5:1 against white.
  - **Tile 1:** the paragraphs are a light, cool gray, visibly paler than the byline above them. By eye it sits near or under 4.5:1, in the "reads as faint" range of WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 (Contrast Minimum). The page is six minutes of continuous reading, so that faintness costs the reader on every line, and most of all on a phone in daylight, which is likely for a ride guide.
  - **Tile 2:** the same paragraphs are a dark gray that looks well clear of the threshold, and they still sit a step softer than the near-black headline.
  - **What to check:** a still can't give the ratio, so sample the tile 1 text color in the live CSS. If it is 4.5:1 or above, it passes the rule but still reads weaker than tile 2.
- **Hierarchy (taste, favors tile 2 slightly).** You might expect the paler body in tile 1 to push the headline forward. It doesn't need to. The headline already dominates by size and weight in both versions, so dropping the text value only takes away readability.

Best: tile 2
