**First read.** This is an account overview for a shop. The user scans four summary cards (details, recent order, address, payment) and follows a link to change one. The two versions are the same layout, copy, type and spacing. The only real difference is the card and sidebar borders. Tile 1 draws every box in a near-black outline about 2 px wide. Tile 2 uses a pale gray hairline. In tile 1 the eye goes to the five black frames before it reaches what's inside them. In tile 2 it goes straight to the title and card headings.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The pale hairlines keep the cards as groups without fighting the content. The headings and the blue links carry the page, as they should.
2. Tile 1: The heavy dark outlines are the loudest marks on the screen. They split the page into compartments and push the headings and links down a level.

**What decides it.**
- **Border weight on the four cards and the sidebar (only deciding difference).**
  - **Tile 1:** Each box has a near-black outline about 2 px wide with rounded corners. These dark lines are heavier than any text except "Your account". The page reads as a grid of boxes, and each card's heading competes with its own frame. The large empty lower half of each card (about y 280–380 and 580–680) feels like an empty fenced area rather than open space.
  - **Tile 2:** The same boxes have a light gray 1 px edge on white over an off-white page. The grouping is just as clear. "Personal details", "Recent order" and the other headings are now the strongest marks in each card, and the blue action links stand out.
  - **Rule:** This is the checklist's "No rules, boxes, or ornament where space would do". It describes exactly what tile 1 does: every card enclosed in a near-black border 1 to 2 px wide. Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 157: rules and boxes "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell". The approved fix is to replace heavy borders with space or a pale tone, and tile 2 is that fix.
  - **Kind:** Convention, with a real cost on this page. Every card in tile 1 looks equally important, and the content has to compete with its own frames.
- **Not deciding:**
  - Tile 2's pale borders are not a contrast problem. The card edges only group content and are not controls, so WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 doesn't apply to them.
  - The links, type sizes, copy and grid match pixel for pixel in both, apart from a 1 px inset shift that comes from the thinner border.

Best: tile 2
