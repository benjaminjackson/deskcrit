**First read.** Both tiles are the same short tenant checklist: a page title, an intro paragraph, and three headed sections telling a renter what to do before signing. They share the layout, copy, sizes, and measure. The only difference is the headings. On tile 1 the title and the three section headings are bold and near-black, so you can scan straight down the page. On tile 2 they are regular weight in the same slate gray as the body text, so the page reads as one even gray block.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: the bold, darker headings stand clearly apart from the body, so a renter can find "Check the deposit" or "Know your notice period" without reading the paragraphs.
2. Tile 2: the headings differ from the body only by a modest step in size. Each heading reads as a slightly larger sentence instead of a signpost, and the title has less pull.

**What decides it.**
- **Section headings ("Check the deposit", "Take photos on day one", "Know your notice period").** On both tiles they look about 24 px against about 18 px body text, so the size step is the same. Tile 1 adds a bold weight and a darker, near-black color. Tile 2 uses the body's own regular weight and slate gray, so the size step is the only thing marking a heading. This is a scannable checklist, and a renter checks a page like this to find the one point they need. On tile 2 the eye has to read to find the breaks, and on tile 1 it can jump between them. This is a convention, from the "Contrast from more than size" entry: pair a smaller size step with a change of weight, form, or color (Dair, *Design with Type*, chapters 8a to 8g and 9). Tile 2 breaks it and tile 1 follows it.
- **Page title ("Before you sign a tenancy").** It is the same size on both. On tile 1, bold near-black makes it clearly the first thing you see. On tile 2, light weight in slate gray leaves it large but pale. It still leads on size, but with less pull, and it looks a lot like the section heads beneath it. This falls under "One element dominates" (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). It is a weaker difference than the section headings, and preferring the thin title look in general would be taste. Here it costs tile 2 because the page has no other focal point.

Best: tile 1
