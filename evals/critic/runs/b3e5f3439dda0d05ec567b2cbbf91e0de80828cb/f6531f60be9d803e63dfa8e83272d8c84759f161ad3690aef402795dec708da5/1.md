**First read.** This is a newsletter signup for a radio station's weekly show list. The two versions are almost identical: same copy, layout, type, color and spacing. The only difference is the corner rounding on the controls.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: The email field, the Subscribe button and the "Read last week's list" button all have the same soft rounding (about 6 to 8 px), which matches the card's corners. The form reads as one set of parts.
2. Tile 2: The email field keeps a small radius (about 4 px), but the two buttons are almost square (about 2 px) inside a rounded card. That gives three different corner treatments in one small form.

**What decides it.**
- **Corner radius on the controls.** At full size (crop of x 400–580, y 340–570), tile 1's field and both buttons share one radius close to the card's. In tile 2 the field is slightly rounded, the buttons are nearly sharp, and the card is rounded. Up close, a viewer sees the field and the Subscribe button as two different kinds of object even though they belong together. The form then looks less finished, and a sign-up form needs to look trustworthy. This is a craft and consistency point that I'm judging as taste. I didn't open a checklist entry for it, because this is the only difference and it's small.
- **How much it matters.** Not much. Hierarchy, copy, the main action and contrast are the same in both, so they do the job about equally. Tile 1 wins only because it's more consistent. If the station's brand uses square corners elsewhere, the fix is to make tile 2's field and card match its buttons, not to pick tile 1 automatically.

Best: tile 1
