**First read.** This is a settings screen for notifications, built for people to scan the four email types, read what each one does, and switch it on or off. The two versions are the same in layout, type sizes, toggles and copy. The only difference is the gray of the one- and two-line descriptions under each setting name.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: The descriptions are a mid-dark gray. They stay clearly below the bold setting names in the hierarchy and are still easy to read.
2. Tile 2: The descriptions are a pale gray. They look faint on white, so the copy that explains each email is the hardest thing on the screen to read.

**What decides it.**
- **Description text contrast.** These are the lines under "Weekly summary", "Mentions", "Due dates" and "Product news". They are small text, about 15 px, which is not large text under WCAG.
  - **Tile 1:** a dark gray that looks comfortably above 4.5:1.
  - **Tile 2:** a light gray that looks to be around 3:1, which is below the 4.5:1 that normal-size text needs (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3; *Text does not read as faint*). I judged this by eye. Measure the exact ratio of tile 2's gray on white to confirm it.
- **What the viewer meets.** These lines are what a person reads before deciding, for example "every Monday", "within an hour" and "8 am in your time zone". In tile 2 they read as faint, so people with low vision or a dim or glare-washed screen strain to read them or skip them. They then switch emails on or off without knowing what they agreed to.
- **What tile 2 gains.** A softer gap between the names and the descriptions. Tile 1 already gets that gap from weight (bold names, regular descriptions) and size, so the lighter gray buys nothing. Refactoring UI makes the same point: use weight and color for hierarchy, but not at the cost of legibility.
- **Verdict on the difference.** It is a defect, not taste.

Best: tile 1
