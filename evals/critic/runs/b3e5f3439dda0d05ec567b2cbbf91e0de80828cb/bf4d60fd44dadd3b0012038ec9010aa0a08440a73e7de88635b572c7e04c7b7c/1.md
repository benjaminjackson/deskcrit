**First read.** This is a three-plan pricing section for invoicing and accounting software, aimed at small business owners comparing plans. The eye goes to the headline, then the three prices, then the blue "Start free trial" button on the middle plan. The two versions are the same in layout, type sizes, copy, and buttons. The only difference is the color of the small gray text: the plan descriptions and the feature lists.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The descriptions and feature lists are a mid-dark gray that reads easily. A buyer can compare what each plan includes without straining.
2. Tile 1: The same text is a pale gray that looks faint on white. That makes the features, which are what someone uses to choose a plan, the hardest part of the page to read.

**What decides it.** It comes down to how readable the text is. I compared crops of the first card at full size.
- **Tile 1:** Lines like "For one person who sends a few invoices a month" and "Up to 20 invoices a month" are a light gray that looks roughly #888 on white. That's probably around 3.5:1, below the 4.5:1 that text this small needs.
- **Tile 2:** The same lines look roughly #555, well above 4.5:1. Both readings are by eye, so measure the two text colors to confirm.
- **Why it matters:** These lines are where buyers learn what $9, $24, and $58 actually get them, things like invoice limits, bank feeds, and payroll seats. In tile 1 they fade back, so comparing plans takes more effort. That's worse in bright light, on a low-quality screen, or for people with weaker eyesight.
- **Source:** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), from the "Text does not read as faint" and "Body text contrast (WCAG AA)" entries. This is a defect in tile 1, and tile 2 fixes it.
- **Hierarchy still holds in tile 2:** The prices and plan names are still clearly heavier than the darker body text, so the plan names and prices still stand out first.

Best: tile 2
