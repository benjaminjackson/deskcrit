**First read.** Both versions are the opening screen of a long-read magazine feature: a small red "Long read" kicker, a large bold serif headline, a two-line standfirst, and a byline under the "Northing" masthead. The eye lands on the headline in both. In tile 1 it reads straight through. In tile 2 it snags on the headline itself, because its three lines are packed so tightly that letters from one line run into the next.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: the headline's three lines stay clear of each other and still read as one block, so it does its job of pulling the reader in.
2. Tile 2: the headline's lines collide in several places. The only thing it gains is moving the standfirst and byline up by about 40 px, which does not matter on a screen with this much empty space below.

**What decides it.** The two versions differ only in the line spacing of the headline. Everything else is the same: type, sizes, colors and left edges.
- **Tile 1:** the type is about 64 px and the lines are about 70 px apart (y ≈ 187, 257, 327). The descenders in "people", "keeping" and "forgotten" clear the ascenders below them, and the heading still reads as one unit.
- **Tile 2:** the lines are about 54 px apart (y ≈ 190, 243, 297), which is less than the type size. At full size:
  - the "p" of "people" and the "p" and "g" of "keeping" run into "lighthouse" and "lit" on line 2;
  - the "g"s of "forgotten" and "lighthouse" press down onto "Fair Skerry".

  Where letters touch, the word shapes blur together. The reader has to work to separate the lines of the one element meant to pull them in, and the page looks like a typesetting mistake, not a tight, confident setting.
- **Source:** this is a **defect** under "Heading lines hold together" (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, p. 37: a title may be set with negative leading "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide"; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 28).
- **Fix for tile 2:** open the line spacing until no letter touches the next line, which is roughly back to tile 1's setting.

Best: tile 1
