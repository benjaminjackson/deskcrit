Tile 2 is the better version. The two tiles are identical except that tile 1 sets its five-line intro in capitals. I compared them on the contact sheet only and did not open the full-size images; the difference is plain at that size.

**First read.** This is the home screen of a yoga studio's booking app on a phone. It greets the member, tells them the terms of their class pass, then lists today's classes with how many spaces are left. The two versions match in every way but one: tile 1 sets the paragraph under "Good morning, Dana" in capitals, and tile 2 sets it in normal sentence case.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The pass terms read as a normal sentence. The member can quickly pick out the facts they will be held to: six sessions left, valid until 30 November, book two hours ahead, cancel by 8 pm the night before.
2. Tile 1: The same terms in capitals take longer to read, and they look like a legal notice or a warning. The paragraph also runs to five lines instead of four, which pushes the class list further down.

**What decides it.** In tile 1, the paragraph under the headline is five lines of capitals at the size of the text around it. With every letter the same height, words lose their shape, so the eye has to read letter by letter. That paragraph holds the booking and cancellation rules, which the member will later be held to, so slower reading means a bigger chance of missing the "cancel by 8 pm the night before" rule. It also clashes with the calm tone the rest of the screen sets. The extra line pushes the class list down: "Slow flow" starts about 40 px lower in tile 1, and in this view tile 2 shows the next class ("Mobility") while tile 1 does not. Tile 2 sets the paragraph in sentence case, which fixes all of this. This is a convention, and here breaking it is a real defect: Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps", says to keep capitals for headings shorter than one line and not to capitalize whole paragraphs; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, says text in capitals is particularly hard to read. The source is the checklist entry "Long text is not set in all capitals".

Best: tile 2
