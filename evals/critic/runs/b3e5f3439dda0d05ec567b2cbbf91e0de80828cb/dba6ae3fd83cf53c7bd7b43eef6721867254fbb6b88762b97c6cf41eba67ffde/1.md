**First read.** This is the landing page for a wedding venue: a restored barn, aimed at couples deciding whether it's worth a visit. The two versions are the same at every pixel except the two body paragraphs. Tile 1 sets them in the same brush script as the "Hollins Barn" logo. Tile 2 sets them in a plain book serif. That one change decides the pick.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The two paragraphs hold the facts a couple uses to choose a venue: what the barn has, who can cater, and how many nights they get. Here those facts read at a normal pace, and the script stays on the logo, where it gives the page its warmth.
2. Tile 1: Eight lines of connected brush script slow the reader down just where the page makes its case. The script also competes with the logo, so nothing on the page stands out as the brand mark.

**What decides it.**
- **Paragraph face (defect).** In tile 1, both paragraphs (y ≈ 335–580) are in a heavy connected script with tight lines. The lowercase letters look alike, and words like "florist" and "caterers" need a second look. A couple skimming for "up to 120 guests," "your own caterer," and "two nights" has to read every word with effort, and many will skim past the details that sell the venue. Tile 2 sets the same words in an upright serif with open line spacing, about 55–60 characters per line, and they read at a glance. This falls under Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 6.2.1: set paragraphs in a face made for reading, and keep script for a few words of display. Tile 2 does exactly that, with script only on the logo.
- **Hierarchy and brand (taste, secondary).** In tile 1 the body is as dark and as busy as the headline, so the headline loses some of its lead. Using the logo's script for the body also uses it up: it stops marking the name "Hollins Barn." In tile 2 the steps are clear, from bold serif headline to regular serif body to sans-serif button. A side effect is that tile 2's paragraphs run one line longer, so the "Book a visit" button sits about 29 px lower. That costs nothing here, because the button still sits well above the fold.

Both versions share the same remaining issues, so none of them changes the pick. The image panel is a plain gradient, probably a photo that didn't load. "Book a visit" also names no time or way to book. Each version's own crit covers these.

Best: tile 2
