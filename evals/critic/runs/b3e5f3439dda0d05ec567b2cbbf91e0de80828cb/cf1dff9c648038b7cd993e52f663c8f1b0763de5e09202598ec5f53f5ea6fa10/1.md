**First read.** Both versions are the same empty state for a support inbox: the line-drawn inbox tray in the middle, then "No open conversations", two lines of help text, and an outlined "Open customer list" button. I assume the piece is there to tell an agent that nothing is waiting and point them to the one useful next step. At full size the only difference is the fill color of the tray illustration and its shadow: pale mint green in tile 1, pale blue in tile 2. Type, copy, layout, spacing, button and sidebar are the same in both.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1 and tile 2 tie. The color swap doesn't change where the eye lands, what the screen says, or what the agent does next. Both tints are pale, sit behind the same gray outline, and are the only hue in an otherwise gray UI, so neither one clashes or pulls focus.

**What decides it.** Nothing about how well the screen works. This is taste.
- **Status-color meaning** (Apple HIG Color, "Semantic color conventions"). Green usually means success and blue usually means information. Here both are used as decoration on an illustration. Green leans toward "all done", which fits an empty inbox. Blue reads more neutral, which fits copy that also tells the agent to go and start conversations. Neither breaks the convention: there's no status message here for the color to contradict.
- **Mood** (Itten, *The Art of Color*, "Color mood matches the message"). Both tints are low in saturation and calm. Neither works against the message.
- **What would actually decide it:** the product's accent or brand color, which isn't shown anywhere on this screen. Pick the tint that matches it, so the illustration looks like part of the product rather than a stock asset. If the product's accent is blue, choose tile 2. If green has no other role in the product, tile 1 is just as good.

Best: tie tile 1, tile 2
