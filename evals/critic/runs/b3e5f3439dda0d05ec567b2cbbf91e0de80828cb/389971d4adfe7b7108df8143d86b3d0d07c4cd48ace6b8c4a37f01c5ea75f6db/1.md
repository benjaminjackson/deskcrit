**First read.** Assumption, since I wasn't told: this is the hero of a landing page for a weekly dinner-planning service, meant for busy home cooks, and its job is to get them to click "Plan my first week." The two versions have the same copy, sizes, colors and button. The only difference is alignment. In tile 1 the whole hero is flush left on the logo's edge (x≈100). In tile 2 everything is centered on the page's middle line.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 1: The flush-left setting makes the two three-line paragraphs and the feature list read cleanly. The hero starts on the same edge as the logo, so the page holds together.
2. Tile 2: It centers paragraphs of two to three lines and a three-item list, so each line starts somewhere new. The centered column also lines up with nothing in the header.

**What decides it**
- **Paragraphs are centered (tile 2).** The body copy ("Tell Weekplate who you cook for…" and "Swap any meal…") runs about 670 px wide over three and two lines. In tile 2 each line starts at a different x (≈306, 313, 537 in the first paragraph), so the eye has to search for the start of every line. That slows the paragraph that explains how the product works, which is the one that sells it. Tile 1 starts every line at x=100. This breaks a convention, and it costs something here (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst 2.1.3: set paragraphs of several lines flush left and keep centering for a few short lines). The centered headline on its own would be fine. The paragraphs are what cost tile 2.
- **The feature list (both).** "Recipes tested in a home kitchen / Portions for one to six people / Lists you can send to a partner" reads as a list in tile 1 because the items share a left edge. Centered in tile 2, the three ragged lines read more like loose captions. This is a smaller version of the same defect.
- **Relationship to the header.** In tile 1 the headline, body, list and button all sit on the logo's left edge at x=100, so the header and hero share one edge (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: each element aligns to an edge it shares with another). In tile 2 the centered column (≈306–974) shares no edge with the logo or the nav. It isn't broken, but it is looser. Tschichold's "no central axis by default" names exactly this, centering every block on one axis, and nothing in the piece gives a reason for it here.
- **What tile 1 gives up.** The right half of tile 1 is empty, and tile 2 looks more balanced as a thumbnail. That is taste. The empty space doesn't slow the reader, and an image or product preview placed there could fill it. The reading cost in tile 2 is a real defect, so it outweighs the balance.

Best: tile 1
