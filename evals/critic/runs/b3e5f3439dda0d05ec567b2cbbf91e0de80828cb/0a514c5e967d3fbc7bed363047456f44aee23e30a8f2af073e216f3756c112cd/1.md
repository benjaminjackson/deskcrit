**First read.** Both tiles are the same dark-navy landing hero for a conference called Fieldwork 2027. On both, the eye goes first to the big white headline, then the amber date line, then the amber "Get tickets" button. The only real change is the headline's line spacing. Everything below it sits 24 px lower in tile 2 to make room.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a conference website. It needs to tell someone, at a glance, what the event is, when and where it is, and how to buy a ticket.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: The two headline lines are clearly separate but still read as one heading. Since the headline is the first thing anyone reads, this is what decides the pick.
2. Tile 1: The headline lines are set so tightly that letters from the top line touch letters in the line below. That makes the most important text on the page look like a mistake.

**What decides it.**
- **Headline line spacing (defect).** In tile 1, the baselines are about 55 px apart, for type with capitals about 55 to 60 px tall. That puts the line spacing below the type size. The tails of the "y" in "days" and the "g" in "building" drop into the tops of the tall letters in "quieter, slower software" below them, and the two lines merge into one dark block.
  - A visitor reads that as a rendering bug rather than a design choice. It also slows the first read of the one line that says what the event is about.
  - In tile 2, the baselines are about 70 px apart. The two lines are close enough to read as one heading, and no letter touches another.
  - This is the "Heading lines hold together" rule: a heading may be set tighter than body text only "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide" (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, p. 37). Tile 1 breaks it; tile 2 meets it.
- **Everything below the headline moves down 24 px in tile 2.** The intro text and button keep the same spacing from each other. The button still sits well inside the first screen, so this costs nothing. The page has a lot of empty space at the bottom of the screen in both versions, so neither tile gains anything there.

Nothing else differs: the date line, intro text, button, navigation and colors are identical. The choice rests entirely on the headline.

Best: tile 2
