**First read.** This is the hero of a landing page for an Italian course. A visitor should see the promise and then start a first lesson. The two versions are the same pixel for pixel except for the row of actions under the subhead. In tile 1 there are three filled orange buttons of equal weight. In tile 2 only "Start your first lesson" is a filled button, and the other two actions are underlined text links. (I'm assuming the page's job is to turn a first-time visitor into someone who starts a lesson.)

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: It has one clear next step. The eye goes from the headline to the subhead and lands on the one filled button, which is the action that matters.
2. Tile 1: Three identical filled buttons make all three actions look equally important. "Gift a course" competes with "Start your first lesson" as loudly as it can.

**What decides it.**
- **Which action is primary (convention, and a defect in tile 1).** In tile 1, "Start your first lesson", "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" are all the same filled orange button. They are the same height (about 52 px), sit at x≈100–688, y≈425–477, and differ only in width. The viewer has to read all three labels to work out which one is for them, and the main conversion gets no more pull than a side path for gift buyers. Tile 2 keeps the one filled button and turns the other two into underlined links of the same size and weight. The secondary paths are still there, but they step back. Material Design 3, "Buttons" (in the entries "Primary versus secondary action" and "Secondary actions are not styled as strongly as the primary one"), says a view should have one high-emphasis action and give the others lower-emphasis styles. Two or more equal filled buttons is the named fault, and tile 1 has three.
- **The links in tile 2 still look clickable (no cost).** "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" are underlined as well as colored dark orange, so they don't rely on color alone. The orange-brown looks dark enough on the cream background, though I'd measure it to be sure. Demoting them doesn't hide them.

Everything else is identical: the headline, the subhead, the chat panel, the nav and the spacing. Nothing else affects the pick.

Best: tile 2
