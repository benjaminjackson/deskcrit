**First read.** This is a product page for a kitchen scale. Your eye goes from the bold title to the price and the black "Add to basket" button, with a placeholder image on the right. Both versions are the same pixel for pixel except for one thing: in tile 1 the two description paragraphs are right-aligned, and in tile 2 they are flush left.

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: Every line on the left starts on the same edge as the category label, title, price and button, so the description reads straight through.
2. Tile 1: The paragraphs are set flush right, so each line starts at a different place. This makes them harder to read, and the column's left edge breaks up partway down.

**What decides it.**
- **Paragraph alignment (convention, and a defect for the shopper).** Tile 1 sets two paragraphs of four and three lines flush right, at x≈588. Each line starts at a different point, from x≈68 to x≈211, so after every line break the eye has to hunt for where the next one begins. The description is the part a buyer reads to decide whether to buy (capacity, steps of one gram, tare, units, battery life), so the misalignment slows down the most important reading on the page. Tile 2 sets the same text flush left at x=68. See "Paragraphs are not centered or right-aligned": paragraphs of several lines are set flush left, and centering or right alignment is for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3).
- **One left edge for the whole column (follows from the above).** In tile 2, the label, title, body, price and button all hang from one left edge at x=68. The eye can drop straight down from the title to the price and the button. In tile 1, everything shares that edge except the body, which pushes the ragged edge to the left, so the column looks split into two alignments at the point where the buyer is reading.

The rest is the same in both versions, including the gray placeholder image, so nothing else affects the pick.

Best: tile 2
