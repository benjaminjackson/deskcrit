**First read.** This is a council page that tells residents which bins go out on which day. The top of the page is the same in both versions: one clear headline, a highlighted "Next collection: Thursday" box, then three short paragraphs with links. The only difference is the link underlines in those paragraphs. In tile 1 the underline sits well below the text, below even the descenders, so it looks like a separate rule floating between lines. In tile 2 the underline sits just under the baseline and breaks around descenders (the g in "working", the y and p in "yearly permit").

**Ranking**
1. Tile 2: Each underline sits tight to its words, so every link reads as one underlined phrase. The paragraphs also keep an even texture.
2. Tile 1: The underlines hang loose. Where a link wraps over two lines ("full / calendar for the year", "report it within two / working days", "replaced / for a fee of $25"), each underline sits nearly as close to the line below as to its own words. The body text looks striped and busier than it needs to be.

**What decides it.**
- **How far the underline sits from the text** (all five links, in the lower half of the page). At full size (390 px wide):
  - Tile 1's underline looks about 4–5 px below the descenders.
  - Tile 2's looks about 1–2 px below the baseline and skips the descenders.
- **What the viewer meets.** In tile 1 the eye has to work out which line each rule belongs to. That is a small hesitation, but it happens on the links that matter most here: reporting a missed bin, the $25 replacement fee and the yearly permit. In tile 2 the underline is clearly part of the link, which is how a reader expects a link to look, and the paragraph stays calm.
- **Kind of claim.** This is taste, argued on common sense. No checklist entry covers underline offset; the only underline line is about paragraph text set in a decorative style, which doesn't apply here. It isn't an accessibility defect either: both versions keep the underline and the blue color, so a link is still marked by more than color alone.

Nothing else differs: the headline, the next-collection box, the copy, the colors and the spacing match. So this is a small win for tile 2, not a big one.

Best: tile 2
