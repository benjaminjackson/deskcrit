**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "Notes for ferry passengers" at top left, then drops into three centered paragraphs below it. Reading them takes work, because every line starts in a different place. The piece reads as a plain information page for a small river ferry. It feels calm but unfinished: the heading is set flush left, the text under it is centered, and the right 40% and bottom third of the frame are empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that tells riders when the ferry runs, how long it takes and how bicycles work, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The centered paragraphs make the most important text the hardest part to read. The copy also puts the times riders plan around into soft phrases ("early morning", "before dusk"), so a rider can't actually plan from it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraphs are centered under a flush-left heading.** All three paragraphs (four lines each) are centered. Each line starts at a different point, from about x 125 to x 305, so the eye has to look for the start of every line. Short lines like "the tide turns against the boat." and "changes for the coming week." float loose in the middle. The heading starts at about x 120, and the nearest body lines start at 125 to 135. That's a few pixels off, so it looks like a mistake rather than a choice. This is a defect for reading and breaks a convention: set paragraphs of several lines flush left, and keep centering for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3). Fix: set the body flush left on the heading's left edge. Line length (about 60 to 68 characters), size and leading are already right, so this one change fixes most of the page.
2. **The copy hides the times riders act on.** Frequency, service hours, crossing time, the winter timetable and the last boat are the facts a passenger plans around and later holds the operator to. Two of them aren't stated. "Between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing. "The last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" leaves the rider to work out when dusk is, and some will turn up for a boat that has gone. The paragraph also points to printed timetables "in the shelter at each landing", which a rider who is planning ahead can't see. This is a defect. Fix: put the actual first and last departure times, and the winter last crossing, in place of the vague phrases. Only the operator has those facts. Also consider pulling the schedule facts out of the prose so they can be scanned, for example one short line each for summer and winter.
3. **The empty space looks left over.** All the content sits in a column across the left half. The whole right side and the area below the text are blank, so the page looks like it is tipping left, or like something failed to load. This breaks a convention: a heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), and empty space should be shaped on purpose, not left over (Tschichold; Müller-Brockmann). Fix: either center the reading column in the frame as a deliberate measure, or give the right side a job such as a sidebar or a map. Once item 2 is fixed, the timetable could go there.

**What's working.**
- The type setting under the alignment is sound. Body text looks around 18 px, with a line length of roughly 60 to 68 characters and generous leading. Keep all of that when you change the alignment.
- The scale is clear and small. There are two sizes, and the heading differs in both size and weight, so there's no question where the page starts.
- The palette is near-black on white with no decoration. That restraint suits a plain public notice.
