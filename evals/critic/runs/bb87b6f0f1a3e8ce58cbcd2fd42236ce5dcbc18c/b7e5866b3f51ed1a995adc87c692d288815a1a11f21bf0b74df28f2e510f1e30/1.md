**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline, "Notes for ferry passengers", and reads it cleanly. Then it drops into the body and stops: the lines are packed so tightly that descenders run into the ascenders of the next line ("quay every / forty", "timetables are kept in / the shelter"). Each paragraph looks like one dark smear rather than lines of text. It reads as a plain info page for ferry riders, and it feels broken, not designed.

**What it's for.** Assumed: a web page that tells ferry passengers the schedule, the bicycle rules, and the winter changes, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** It fails at its one job, because the body text can't be read comfortably. The line spacing is less than the type size, so the lines collide. Everything else here is plain and sound, so this single fix decides the piece.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body lines overlap, so they're unreadable (defect).** All three paragraphs have the problem. The type looks about 19 px, but each line sits only about 15 px below the one before it. That is tighter than solid. Descenders of one line touch or cross the ascenders and accents of the next, as in "forty minutes", "lock them to the rail", and "Printed timetables". The reader has to separate each line by hand, loses their place when returning to the left margin, and is slowed down on the numbers they came for: every 40 minutes, about 12 minutes per crossing, hourly in winter, last boat before dusk. The lines run about 75 characters, so they need more space between them, not less (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.2.1: "Longer measures need more lead than short ones"; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 47). Fix: set the line height to roughly 1.4–1.5 times the type size. Check the CSS for a `line-height` below 1 or a fixed pixel value that is too small; this looks like a bug, not a choice.
2. **The paragraphs don't separate (defect, follows from 1).** The gap between paragraphs is only a few pixels larger than the crushed line step. Because the lines inside a paragraph are fused, the three topics (schedule, bicycles, winter) barely read as separate blocks (Arnheim on proximity: the space inside a group must be clearly smaller than the space between groups). Once the leading is fixed, keep the paragraph gap clearly larger than the new line spacing, about one blank line, so a passenger scanning for "bicycles" or "winter" finds it at once.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing separately. The headline could name what's inside, such as the schedule and the bike rules, but it is accurate as it stands.

**What's working.**
- The structure is right for the job. One clear bold heading, a single flush-left column at a sensible width, and black text on white with strong contrast. Keep all of this and only fix the spacing.
- The copy is plain and specific: real landings, real intervals, and real rules ("lock them to the rail along the side of the deck", "noticeboard there lists any changes for the coming week"). A passenger can act on every sentence once they can read it.
- There's only one type family, in two sizes and two weights. The heading stands out by both size and weight, so the hierarchy needs no help.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the collision comes from the stylesheet or from a font that failed to load and fell back with different metrics. Check the computed `line-height` on the paragraphs in a live browser. Reflow at phone widths also can't be judged from this image.
