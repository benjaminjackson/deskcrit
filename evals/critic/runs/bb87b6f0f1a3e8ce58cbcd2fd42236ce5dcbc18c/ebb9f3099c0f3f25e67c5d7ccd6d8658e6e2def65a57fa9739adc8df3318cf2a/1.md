**First read.** The eye lands on "Overview" and then runs along the four stat cards, where the big numbers are the loudest thing on the page. From there it drops to the blue line and the one blue Monday bar, the only color on the screen. It reads at once as a support team's monthly dashboard: calm, tidy and trustworthy. The trust is the problem, because two parts of the page quietly contradict the rest.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a support lead or agent at a small shop opens this to see how November went and what needs attention now.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are competent, and as a composition it does its job. What lets it down is the data. The "oldest open" list includes a solved ticket, and the daily chart doesn't add up to the totals above it. A dashboard that the reader catches contradicting itself loses trust in every number on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Oldest open tickets" table isn't what its heading says** (defect). The third row, "Refund for a returned kettle", has the status Solved and still shows "3 h" waiting. A reader using this list as a to-do queue either chases a closed ticket or starts to doubt the filter behind the whole table. "Waiting" also means two different things here. On the Open row it is the team's delay. On the "Waiting on customer" row it is the customer's. Both are shown as one figure the reader is expected to act on. Fix: filter the table to tickets that really are open. Then make "Waiting" mean one thing, either "time since our last reply" or a column that says whose turn it is. This is a data and labeling problem, not a styling one (WCAG 2.4.6: a heading describes what its section holds).
2. **The line chart doesn't agree with the stat cards** (defect). The gray October line runs about 23 to 33 a day. That comes to roughly 850–900 tickets for the month, but the card says 1,192. The blue November line looks like it averages in the high 30s, about 1,100, against 1,284 on the card. The chart is titled "per day", but it plots only about 12 points across 30 days. A reader who does the rough sum, and support leads do, will trust neither number (Tufte: the size of the effect shown must match the data). Fix: plot real daily values, one point per day, or retitle the chart for the interval it actually shows. Then check that the area under each line matches its card. If this is placeholder data, the real build still needs that check.
3. **"Tickets by weekday" has no scale and doesn't say what it measures** (defect). Only Monday carries a number (262). The other six bars can be read only against each other. Nothing says whether 262 is a monthly total or an average per Monday, and nothing says why Monday is blue. The blue is also November's blue from the chart beside it, so it reads as "November" when it means "busiest day" (Tufte and Few: the reader should never guess what a number measures; Arnheim on accidental grouping). Fix: label every bar's value or add a light axis, state the unit in the title (total or average), and highlight Monday with something other than the series blue. A darker gray plus its label would do.

**Smaller things.**
- Legend on the line chart, top right: label the two lines at their right ends ("November", "October") so nobody has to look back and forth (Tufte; Wilke).
- Status column in the table: it is set smaller and lighter than every other cell, so it reads as a footnote when it is the column that decides what to do. Set it at the row's size, or as a quiet status label used the same way everywhere.
- No search or filter anywhere: a support tool's most frequent action is finding a ticket, so give it a search field in the sidebar or header (Nielsen heuristic 7).
- Settings icon in the sidebar: it reads as a sun or brightness symbol, not a gear. Use the conventional gear (taste, leaning on platform icon conventions).
- Stat cards: each gives last month's figure but leaves the reader to work out the direction. First reply fell from 55 to 42 min, which is good, while tickets opened rose from 1,192 to 1,284. Nothing says which way is good, and nothing points out that 1,284 opened against 1,231 solved means the backlog grew. Consider stating the change and its direction (taste).
- "First reply 42 min": this is the number the team will be held to, so say whether it is a median or an average.

**What's working.**
- The stat cards: each pairs one big number with last month's figure in small gray text below it. You get the number and its context in one glance with no chart noise. Keep that pattern.
- Restraint with color: everything is gray except one blue, so the current period stands out without decoration. It just needs its second use (Monday) taken away.
- Plain-language labels such as "Happy with the answer", "Assigned to me" and "Waiting on customer" use the team's own words, not system terms.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the table rows and the date range ("1 to 30 November 2026") are clickable. Neither shows any sign of it, and both probably should be.
- Focus states in the sidebar navigation.
