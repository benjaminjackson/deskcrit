**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big dark block at the top and finds a generic gray avatar silhouette, the kind used when there is no photo. It then drops to the bold headline, which carries the page. It reads as a bookshop event page for a novelist's reading. It feels calm and well set, but unfinished, because the picture of the person the evening is about is missing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: to get local readers to book a free seat at an in-store author evening.

**Verdict.** The type, copy and button do their job well. The one thing holding it back is the largest element on the page: a placeholder silhouette instead of Ines Varga or her book. It pulls the eye first and gives nothing back, so the page looks unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image is an empty placeholder, and it is the first thing seen.** It is the 600×330 dark box at the top of the column, with a brown head-and-shoulders silhouette on near-black.
   - What the viewer gets: the darkest, largest shape on the page wins the eye before the headline does. The payoff is "no photo here." A reader deciding whether to give up an evening wants to see the author or the book. The stock avatar tells them nothing about either, and it makes the shop look careless.
   - Why it counts: an empty or generic picture as the hero visual is a template tell (unslop, Sokolovsky). The picture and the headline should make one idea together, and here only the headline does any work (common sense).
   - Direction of the fix: put in a real photo of the author or the cover of *The Salt Year*. If there is no image, remove the box so the date and headline lead.
   - If this is a missing image rather than the design, it still shipped this way. Either way, check it in the live page.
   - Kind: defect.
2. **The page never says where the event is.** Below the headline there is a date and time, a description and the button, but no venue or address. "Find us" sits in the nav, but the reader has to guess that the event is at the shop and then go look.
   - What the viewer gets: someone booking a seat is committing to be somewhere at 7 pm. The place is half of that commitment, and it is the one fact they can't check without leaving the page (Nielsen, recognition rather than recall).
   - Direction of the fix: add the venue next to the date line, in the same small style. The shop will know whether that is the store name or a full address.
   - Kind: defect.

**Smaller things.**
- "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. 13 November falls on a Thursday in 2025, not 2026. Check the weekday, and if this page stays up as an archive, add the year so a past event can't be mistaken for an upcoming one.
- The nav doesn't mark "Events" as the current section. Give it one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).

**What's working.**
- The text stack is right. The small rust-colored date sits tight above a large, tightly set two-line headline, then a short paragraph, then the button. That gives three or four clear size steps and one accent color, and the eye moves top to bottom with nothing competing. Keep it.
- The copy is concrete. "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor. Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm" tells the reader exactly what the evening is. "Book a free seat" names the action and removes the cost question in four words.
- Restraint. It uses one warm accent, a dark-gray (not black) button, and no badges, gradients or extra buttons, which suits a bookshop.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the silhouette is the intended design or an image that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- The button's focus and hover states.
