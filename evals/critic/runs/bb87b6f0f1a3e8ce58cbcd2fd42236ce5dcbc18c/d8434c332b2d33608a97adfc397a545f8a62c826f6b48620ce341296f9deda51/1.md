**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing", then drops to the one teal-outlined card, "Full gym", then to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads straight away as the billing settings of a gym-management SaaS, for the person who runs the gym. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy: a lot of white space, one accent color and no decoration.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a gym owner or manager comes here to see or change their plan, update billing details, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The layout is solid, but the page is vague exactly where money is involved. You can't tell whether clicking a plan changes your subscription, what "Cancel" cancels, or when you'll next be charged and to which card. On a billing screen, those are the questions the page has to answer.

**What would move it most**

1. **It's unclear what happens to the plan when you click a card. (Defect.)** The plan cards sit above the "Billing details" heading, and the only "Save changes" button sits at the foot of that form. A user who clicks "Gym group" can't tell:
   - whether that click upgrades them on the spot to $159.00 a month,
   - whether it waits for "Save changes" further down, which seems to belong to the email and address fields,
   - or whether changing plan happens somewhere else.

   One reading leads to an upgrade they didn't mean to make. The other leaves them thinking they upgraded when they didn't. Nothing near the cards marks which one is the current plan as opposed to the one selected, and nothing says when a change starts or how the first bill is worked out. The control sits far from the thing it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping), and help is missing where the task carries a cost (Nielsen, heuristic 10).

   Direction: tie the plan choice to its own commit. That could be a plan action inside the Plan section, or a clear confirmation step that states the new price and when it starts. Also label the current plan in words, not only with the teal outline. The maker has to supply how and when a change is billed.

2. **"Cancel" on a billing page can be read two ways. (Defect.)** Next to "Save changes", under a subscription, "Cancel" can mean "throw away my edits" or "cancel my subscription". An owner who wants to undo a typo may hesitate to press it for fear of losing their gym's account. A label that names the outcome removes the doubt. "Discard changes" is an editorial fix of the existing word, not new content. If cancelling the subscription is offered anywhere, it needs its own clearly named control, set apart from the safe actions (Apple HIG, "Buttons"; Material Design, "Dialogs").

3. **The information people come to a billing page for isn't on it. (Common sense; the Tognazzini source isn't on the approved list.)** There is no payment method, no next charge date or amount, and no way to open or download an invoice. "BS-2093" looks like plain text, not a link. Most visits to a billing page are "when am I charged next, on which card, and where's last month's PDF for my accountant". Here the user has to hunt elsewhere or contact support. Direction: give the current-plan summary (next charge date and amount, payment method) a place near the top, and make each invoice row lead to its document. The real dates and card details have to come from the maker.

4. **Everything is a full pill. (Template tell.)** The plan cards, text fields, currency select, both buttons and the sidebar highlight all use fully rounded ends. On cards about 85 px tall, the curve pinches the corners, so the radio button and text look crowded into the left curve. Viewers have seen this "round everything" look on many products, so Basalt looks like any template and less like itself (unslop, "Every element a full pill"). Direction: scale the corner radius to the size of each element, smaller for the cards and fields, and keep the pill shape for buttons at most.

**Smaller things.**
- The borders of the unselected plan cards, the text fields and the empty radio circles look very pale gray on white. Measure them against the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for, and darken them if they fall short.
- The currency select shows the browser's own chevron, jammed against the right curve. It looks like a different component from the pill text fields beside it. Style it to match the fields.
- "Billing email" and "Currency" sit side by side but aren't a related pair. One column would read more cleanly.

**What's working.**
- **One accent, used with discipline.** The same teal marks the selected plan, the checkbox and the primary button, so the eye learns that "teal = chosen or act". Keep it.
- **The invoice table is set properly.** Amounts are right-aligned with tabular figures, the header aligns to match, the rules are thin, and "Paid" is a word rather than a colored dot. It's easy to scan.
- **Sections are clearly grouped.** Rules and heading spacing make Plan, Billing details and Invoices easy to tell apart, with each heading close to what it introduces.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus indicators on the pill controls and the plan cards.
- What actually happens when a plan card is clicked: an immediate change, a dialog, or nothing until saving.
