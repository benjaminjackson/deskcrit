**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline on the left. Then it gets pulled straight to the bright lime-green confetti in the upper right and stays there, because nothing is to be found there. It reads as a plant nursery's homepage, calm and competent in its type. But the picture behind it is a field of random green ovals, not ferns or any plant, so it feels like a placeholder background where the photo should be.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage hero for a local plant nursery. It should make nearby gardeners want to come in or browse ferns and shade plants this weekend.

**Verdict.** The type and the copy do their job. The picture does not, and it is half the screen. A nursery selling ferns shows no fern, and the bright, busy right half takes the eye away from the headline and the button.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image shows nothing the nursery sells.** The whole background is generated ovals in a dozen greens. The viewer can't tell they are ferns, hostas, or anything with a name. A plant shop's strongest argument is the plants, and here a visitor gets no sense of what is on the benches, how healthy it looks, or whether it is the plant they want for their shady corner. The headline promises "ferns and shade plants" and the picture only says "green". It's also a template tell (Sokolovsky, unslop: a generic picture as the hero visual): the visitor has seen this kind of decorative fill on many sites, so the page looks like anyone's. Fix: replace it with an image from the nursery's own world, meaning its own ferns, its greenhouse, or its shade beds, so the picture and the headline make one claim together. *Defect.*
2. **The right half outshouts the headline and the button.** The ovals get brighter, more saturated, and denser toward the upper right, while the text side is darkened. The heaviest, brightest area of the screen is the empty one, so the eye leaves the headline for a spot with no subject and no action, and it never reaches the button (Arnheim on balance; the eye path has a dead end). The bright lime also works against the message: shade plants are a cool, dim, quiet subject, and the picture reads sunny and loud. Fix: whatever image goes in, keep its brightest, busiest part from competing with the text. Calm its value and saturation, or let its subject sit where it leads back to the headline and the button. *Defect for the eye path, taste for the mood.*
3. **The one action is the weakest object on the page.** "Browse the greenhouse" is a good ask: it names a specific place and fits a weekend visit. But it is a thin white outline over the texture, lighter than the headline and much quieter than the leaf field, so it reads as optional. As the only action in the hero, it should be the second thing the eye reaches. Fix: give it a solid fill so it carries weight against the background. *Taste, argued for this piece.*

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line 3: "corner" sits alone on the last line, a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rebalance the break, for example "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner".
- The headline looks set at text spacing. At this size it would hold together better with slightly tighter letter and word spacing (Bringhurst 2.1.1).

**What's working.**
- The type hierarchy is clean: one dominant headline, a clearly smaller subhead, a small button label, and three levels with big steps between them. Keep it.
- The subhead, "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend", is specific copy. It gives a place, a state (ready to plant), and a time, which speaks to someone deciding what to do on Saturday. Keep that concreteness when revising the headline, which is more generic.
- The text side is darkened enough that the headline and subhead read cleanly over the texture.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states. Whether the background is an animation, or a placeholder for a photo that didn't load. Check that on the live page.
