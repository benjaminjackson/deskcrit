I ran the checklist on this Fernwick Studio contact page with no context. The walk is in my notes above; here is the critique.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves right to the white card with the form in it, and stops on the blue "Send message" button. It reads as the contact page of a small design or creative studio. It feels tidy, calm and competent, and also anonymous: with the wordmark removed, it could belong to any agency.

**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is a studio's contact page. Its job is to get prospective clients to send a first message the studio can act on.

**Verdict.** It does its job. A visitor can see what to do and can send a message without friction. Two things hold it back. The one instruction that would make a message useful sits across the page from the field it applies to. And nothing on the page, words or look, tells a visitor that this studio is the right one for their project.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hint about what to include is far from the field it describes (defect).** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them" sits in the left column under the headline. The Message field it refers to is about 100 px lower and across a 70 px gutter, inside a separate white card. Someone who goes straight to the form, which is where the card and button pull the eye, types into an empty box with no prompt. The studio then gets the vague briefs this line was written to prevent. Help belongs next to the step it concerns (Nielsen 10, help and documentation). Direction: move the budget and timeline line into the form as hint text directly under the "Message" label, and leave the left column to say what happens after sending. The two-working-days promise already does that well.

2. **The headline could sit on any studio's page (taste, copy).** "Tell us about your project" is the stock contact headline, and "Send message" is the stock button. Neither tells visitors what kind of project Fernwick takes on or who it is for, so they can't tell whether they're a fit before writing. The only specific line on the page is "We read every message and reply within two working days". It is a real, checkable promise, and it is the strongest copy here. Direction: name the kind of work or client Fernwick takes on, either in the headline or in a line next to it. Only the studio knows what that is, so it is theirs to supply. The look raises the same issue: stock neutral sans, cool gray background, framework blue. That is harmless, but a design studio's own contact page is a fair place to show some of its own hand.

3. **The form card's right edge lines up with nothing (convention).** The page has a clear left edge at x≈56: the logo, headline and footer links all hang from it. The nav ends at x≈1224, but the card stops at about x≈1068. That leaves a roughly 156 px strip on the right that reads as leftover space rather than a chosen open area, and the card looks placed by eye rather than set on the page's columns (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*; Ruder on gathered white space). Direction: either take the card to the right margin the nav uses, or set both columns on a grid whose outer edges match the header's. Then the open space sits in one deliberate place, below the form, where it already works.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks the same as Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the current page. Give it one cue, such as a bolder weight or no underline (Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", NN/g).
- Form: no field is marked required or optional, even though the intro suggests some details are optional. Put an asterisk on the required labels and explain it once, or add "optional" after the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g).
- Field borders: the light gray outlines on white look close to or below 3:1. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11 and darken them if they fall short.
- Headline: "Tell us about your / project" breaks inside the phrase and leaves one word on the second line. Rebreak it as "Tell us about / your project" or change the column width so it breaks better. This is common sense, not a cited rule.

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy.** The type scale has four levels with clear jumps: headline, wordmark, 16 px body and nav, and small bold labels. Labels and fields come in short bold label–field pairs with more space between pairs than within them, so the form reads in a single pass. Keep that spacing.
- **One clear primary action.** "Send message" is the only filled, saturated element on the page, so it can't be missed.
- **A real promise in the copy.** "We read every message and reply within two working days" turns a blind form into a commitment the visitor can count on. Keep it and keep it prominent.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The focus ring on fields and the button, and whether the email field checks its input or shows errors inline. Both matter on a form, so check them in a live browser.
