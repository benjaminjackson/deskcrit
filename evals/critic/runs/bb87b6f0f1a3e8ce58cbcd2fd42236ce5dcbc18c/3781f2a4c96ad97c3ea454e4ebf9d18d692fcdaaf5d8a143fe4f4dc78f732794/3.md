**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project," drops to the short paragraph, then goes across to the white form card and down to the blue "Send message" button. It's clearly a contact page for a small design or creative studio. It feels tidy, calm and competent, but anonymous: once you cover the wordmark, nothing on the page belongs to Fernwick.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the studio's contact page, and its job is to get a prospective client to send a useful first inquiry.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The ask is clear, the form is simple and the reply promise is specific. What holds it back is a layout that sits off the page's own frame, which leaves the right side and the bottom empty, and a headline that could sit on any agency's site. For a studio that sells design, a page this generic undersells the studio.

**What would move it most**
1. **The content doesn't sit on the page's frame, so the page looks lopsided.** The nav runs from the left margin (~56 px) out to "Contact" at ~1224 px. The content stops at the card's right edge, ~1068 px, which lines up with nothing above it. The result is a dead strip about 150 px wide on the right, plus a wide empty band between the card and the footer. The split between the text column and the card is also neither halves nor thirds of the frame. The page reads as weighted to the left and placed by eye rather than built on columns. Fix: run the content on the same outer edges as the header, so the card's right edge meets the nav's right edge, or center the whole content block on the header's width. Then pick a clear split, such as a third for the copy and two-thirds for the form, so the empty space becomes one chosen margin instead of leftover. Defect/convention (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
2. **The headline is copy anyone could use.** Thousands of studio and agency sites say "Tell us about your project," so it tells the visitor nothing about why they'd write to Fernwick and not someone else. The paragraph below shows what specific copy looks like: "reply within two working days" and "include a budget range and a rough timeline" are concrete and useful. The headline should be just as specific. It could name the kinds of projects the studio takes on, or what happens after the inquiry, but only the studio knows those facts. Taste, argued: a contact page is often the last page a prospect reads before deciding, and here it adds no reason to choose this studio.
3. **No required fields are marked.** The three fields ("Your name", "Email address", "Message") have no asterisk and no "optional" label. The visitor can't tell whether they can skip their name, and only finds out on submit. Fix: put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or mark the optional field "(optional)" (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). Convention.

**Smaller things.**
- "Contact" in the top nav looks exactly like "Work", "Services" and "About", so nothing shows that this is the current page. Give it one cue, such as a bolder weight or no underline with a bar (Nielsen heuristic 1; NN/g menu checklist).
- The input borders are a pale gray on white and look close to or below 3:1. Measure them against #FFFFFF, and darken them if they come in under 3:1 so the fields read as fields (WCAG 1.4.11).
- The headline breaks as "Tell us about your / project", which leaves one word alone on the second line. Rebalance the break, for example "Tell us about / your project", or let the column run a little wider (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- The supporting paragraph is the best thing on the page. It makes a promise the visitor can hold the studio to ("within two working days") and tells them exactly what to include, so the first message is more useful. Keep both of those lines as they are.
- The form is done properly: one column, labels above the fields in bold, a message box taller than the single-line fields, and a single solid primary button whose label names the action ("Send message", not "Submit").
- Restraint: one neutral range, one blue used for both links and the action, underlined links, and a clean type scale with three or four clear levels and no near-duplicate sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and the button, and whether the email field uses the email input type.
- What the visitor sees after sending: a confirmation, and whether it repeats the two-day promise.
- How the two columns stack on a phone.
