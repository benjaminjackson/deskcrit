**First read.** The eye lands on the big cream serif headline, drops to the amber "Set up your studio" button, then crosses to the shelf card on the right. That card shows the product working: four shelves, three full and one half free with an amber "Book". Within seconds it reads as a booking tool for shared kilns in pottery studios. It feels calm and made by hand: deep green, warm cream, one amber accent, with nothing loud.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming this is a landing page meant to get pottery studio owners or technicians to set up an account.

**Verdict.** This is a strong page. It's specific and clear, and it sounds like it was written by someone who has loaded a kiln. The main gap is at the bottom. The page builds to a price and then stops, with no way to act on it, and the price line itself can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page ends on a price with no ask.** "One price per studio" is where a viewer decides, and neither plan has a button. After it comes only the footer. A studio owner who has just picked "Teaching studio" has to scroll back up to the hero to find "Set up your studio", and nothing there carries the plan they chose. This is common sense rather than a cited rule: put the next action where the decision happens. Give each plan, or the section, its own action, styled lower than or the same as the hero's primary button. Related point on the ask: "See a sample firing" is good, because it lets the viewer judge for themselves. But nowhere does the page set Kilnroom against how studios book today. Whatever the members of a shared studio use now is the comparison that would let an owner see the value on terms they already accept. That comparison is the maker's to supply. *Defect.*

2. **The pricing lines can be read two ways, on the one place a buyer holds you to.**
   - **Currency:** the prices are in "$", and the footer says the company is "made in Stoke-on-Trent". A UK studio owner may wonder whether that's US dollars, and whether they'll be billed in a foreign currency. Say which currency it is.
   - **Limits:** "Up to four kilns, classes, and members without limit" can be read as "up to four kilns and up to four classes". It can also be read as "up to four kilns, with classes and members unlimited". If it's the second, the first reading makes a teaching studio think it's capped on classes and may push them away. Reorder it so the limit attaches only to kilns, for example "Up to four kilns, with classes and members without limit". That's an edit of the existing words, not new copy.

   *Defect.*

3. **The headline breaks mid-phrase and leaves one word alone.** The headline is set as "Every shelf of the kiln / booked before the / firing". "Before the" is cut off from "firing", so "firing" sits alone on the third line. This is the largest type on the page, and the one-word last line makes the block look unfinished and slows the first read. Bringhurst (section 2.4.2) covers the one-word last line. Breaking at the phrase is common sense, since the entry's source for that part is unverified. Rebreak so "the firing" stays together, by changing the measure or setting the breaks by hand. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- Pricing: "$12 a month" (serif, about 28 px) is almost the same size as the "One price per studio" heading (about 30 px), so the two read as near-duplicate levels (Bringhurst, 3.1.1). Make the price clearly smaller than the heading, or carry the difference with weight.
- The pricing columns start at about 424 px, while the feature row above uses thirds at 80/467/853. Pricing sits on a grid of its own (Müller-Brockmann). Put the plans on the same columns.
- Below the hero, every section sits in the left 55% of the page. The right side of "For studios that teach" and of the pricing section is empty, so the lower page leans left and looks like it's missing a column (Arnheim). Either use the width or narrow the whole page on purpose.
- Spacing after the section dividers varies. It's about 85 px from the first rule to "From the wheel to the shelf", but about 40 px from the later rules to their headings. Use one rhythm (Gerstner).
- Body paragraphs end on short last lines: "comes out." in the hero, "teacher." in the teaching section (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Rebreak or adjust the measure.

**What's working.**
- **The shelf card is the product, not a decoration.** It names a real firing ("Glaze firing, cone 6", "Loads Friday 16:00"), real loads ("Tomasz, 2 platters"), and the one open slot marked in the accent color. Together with the headline, it makes one idea. Keep it real and specific.
- **The amber accent is spent only on actions:** the primary button and the "Book" link. That's why the eye knows where to go next.
- **The copy is concrete and in potters' words:** "bisque or glaze firing", "a number to write on the foot", "When the kiln is cool". It says who does what, and when, without the viewer filling gaps.
