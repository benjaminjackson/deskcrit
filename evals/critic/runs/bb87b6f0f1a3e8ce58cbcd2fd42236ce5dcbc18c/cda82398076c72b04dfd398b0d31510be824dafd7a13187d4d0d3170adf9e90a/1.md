**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big black headline, reads down through the gray line to the black "Shop the pack" button, and then stops. Everything after that is white space. It reads as the hero of a store page for a backpack. It looks clean and competent, but empty: you're being sold a bag you never see.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a product or launch page for the Wayfarer pack, meant to get travelers to click through and buy.

**Verdict.** The type and the copy are in good shape, but as a sales hero it doesn't work, because there's no product in it. More than half the frame is blank, so you're asked to shop for a bag whose shape, color, and size you have to imagine.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no picture of the pack, and the right half of the frame is empty.** The right ~40% and the bottom third hold nothing. This is a physical product whose selling points are visual: it fits under a seat, opens flat like a suitcase, and weighs under a kilo. You have to take each claim on trust, and none is shown. The empty area also leaves all the weight in the upper left, so the frame looks lopsided and unfinished (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). It isn't shaped space set against the text (Ruder); it's what's left over. If an image was supposed to load there, this is a capture problem, so check it live. As designed in this image, it's a **defect**, and an empty hero visual is also a template tell (unslop): any store could look like this. The fix: put the pack in that space, ideally showing one of the claims the copy makes, such as the bag opened flat or under a seat. Size it to balance the headline.
2. **The ask doesn't give you what you need to act.** "Shop the pack" is a generic button, and nothing nearby says what it costs or what you're committing to. Price is the first thing a shopper checks before clicking "shop". The supporting line handles comparison well, though: "fits under an airline seat" and "opens flat like a suitcase" measure the bag against things you already know, so keep that. The fix: put the price (or "from" a price) near the button. Only the maker has that figure. A button label that names the action would also help. **Defect**, because the decision turns on information that isn't there.
3. **The headline could sit on any bag's page.** "Travel lighter with the…" is a stock promise every pack brand makes. The specific, persuasive material is all in the gray line underneath: 22 litres, under-seat, opens flat, under a kilo. "New for spring" tells you nothing about the bag. The direction is to move one of those concrete claims into the headline, for example by bringing "weighs less than a kilo" up so the headline proves "lighter" instead of asserting it. That's an editorial reshuffle, not new content. **Taste**, but it's what makes this pack different from the next one.

**Smaller things**
- The headline breaks as "with the / Wayfarer pack", leaving "the" stranded from the name it belongs to. Rebreak it so "the Wayfarer pack" stays together, or drop "the".
- Without the picture, the palette is gray, near-black, and one small blue label. That carries no spring or travel mood. A product image would likely fix it. If it doesn't, take one color from the product. (Itten, *The Art of Color*)
- No logo or site navigation is visible. That's fine if this is a cropped section, but a standalone page needs the brand and nav where shoppers expect them (Nielsen, Jakob's law).
- The headline looks extra-bold (800-ish). At this size it reads as stock. A step down in weight would still dominate, because size already does the work (unslop).

**What's working**
- **The type scale.** I count four sizes (eyebrow, headline, supporting line, button and link) with big steps between them, all in one family and on one sharp left edge at 120 px. The order of reading is never in doubt. Keep it when you add the image.
- **The supporting line.** It's concrete, has units, runs about 70 characters per line, and measures the bag against things the viewer already knows (an airline seat, a suitcase). It's the best copy on the page.
- **The primary/secondary split.** You get a solid dark button and an underlined text link, so there's one clear action. "Compare sizes" is a good second option for someone who isn't ready to buy yet.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether a hero image failed to load in the right-hand space. Check in a live browser.
- Focus states on the button and link.
- How the layout behaves at narrower widths.
