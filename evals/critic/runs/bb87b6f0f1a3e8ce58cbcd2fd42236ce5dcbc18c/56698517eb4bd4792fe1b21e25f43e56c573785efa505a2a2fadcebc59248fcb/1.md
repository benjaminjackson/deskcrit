**First read.** The eye goes first to the mustard Glasshouse tile, then to the red Rose arch, and then moves around six colored blocks of equal size. Each block has the same pale circle sitting on its bottom edge. It reads as a tidy three-by-two card grid for a garden show, but the pictures are colored blocks, so it looks like a wireframe rather than a gallery. It feels calm, orderly, and empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page where people visiting a garden show (or thinking of going) browse the displays and pick which ones to see.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound: a clean grid, clear type, and short captions that name real things. But the page is called a gallery and has nothing to look at. Every picture is a flat placeholder, so the one thing a gallery promises isn't there.

**What would move it most**
1. **Every picture is a placeholder, so the gallery shows nothing.** *Defect, and a template tell.* All six tiles are a flat color field with a lighter circle centered in it and cut off by the tile's bottom edge. Rose arch is a red block, not roses over a gate. Herb bed is a green block, not thyme and sage. The captions name specific, visual things (climbing roses, water lilies, runner beans on canes), and the tiles show none of them. So picture and caption don't make one idea; the caption does all the work alone. The circle has no job: take it out and nothing is lost. Its edge also lands exactly on the bottom edge of the tile, which makes the frame feel accidental rather than chosen. An empty box or generic picture in place of a real one is a stock template move (Sokolovsky, unslop). Viewers have seen it on countless unfinished pages, so this reads as "coming soon" rather than "come see this." If these are photos that didn't load in this capture, the design is fine and the fix is in the build. If the blocks are the design, the direction is to put in a photo of each actual display, cropped the same way for all six. This one change decides whether the page works.

**Smaller things.**
- The Rose arch caption doesn't line up with its row. Its title sits about 2 px higher than Herb bed and Pond edge, and its description about 4 px higher, with a tighter gap under the title. The three lines of text don't form straight rows across the columns, and the miss is too small to look deliberate (Arnheim on near-misses; Müller-Brockmann on lines aligning across columns; Gerstner on repeated elements following one program). Give all six cards the same caption spacing.
- "Tomatoes in late July." can be read two ways: the glasshouse is worth seeing in late July, or the tomatoes only show up then. A visitor planning a trip will act on that date. Make clear which it means; restoring the verb would do it.

**What's working.**
- The grid is strict and even. Equal columns, one gutter, and a clear larger gap between rows let the six displays read as peers. That's the right call for a browse page where no one display matters more than the others. Keep it when the photos go in.
- The type levels are clear with only three sizes: page title, bold card title, and smaller gray description. The step from title to description comes from both size and weight, and the gray text still looks easy to read.
- The captions are specific and in the order a viewer needs: a place name, then what's there, in a few words.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blocks are the intended art or images that failed to load. Check the live page.
- Whether the cards are links. Nothing in the image says they are clickable. If they are, check the hover and focus states in a browser.
