Then it goes left to the headline, "Share the kiln without double-booked shelves", which is clear and specific: this is booking software for shared pottery studios. **What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing page for a SaaS tool for community pottery studios, meant to get a studio owner or kiln technician to add their kiln or look at a sample.

**Verdict.** The words and type do their job well. The hero picture doesn't. The page's biggest and loudest element is a generic 3D blob that shows nothing of shelves, firings, or bookings. So the eye starts in the wrong place, and the product never actually appears.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero visual is a stock glossy blob instead of the product.** *Defect and template tell.*
   - **Where:** the right half of the hero, about 420 px wide, with a soft drop shadow.
   - **What the viewer gets:** the headline promises something concrete: shelves, firings, who has work where, what each member owes. The picture shows none of it. Glossy orbs are a stock hero move that viewers have seen on many other products, so this page looks like anyone's at the exact spot where it could have shown its one idea (Sokolovsky, unslop: the hero visual should be "the product itself or one concrete metaphor for what it does").
   - **It also breaks the eye path.** The blob has the most color and the most mass on the page, so it pulls the eye right, away from the brown button and "See a sample firing". After the headline the viewer has to come back across the page to find the action.
   - **Fix:** replace the blob with the thing the page already describes. The "sample firing" itself would be the obvious choice: a shelf-by-shelf loading view with members' pieces and their shares of the cost, using real-looking studio data that you supply. Then the picture and the headline make one argument, and the picture also previews the secondary link. Keep it in the warm clay palette so it doesn't fight the button.

**Smaller things.**
- The blob's drop shadow fades into the divider at about y 578. Once the visual is replaced this goes away, but any new hero picture should sit clearly above that line.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific and does its own work.** The headline names a real pain (double-booked shelves). The paragraph says who does what: members book before they glaze, and the technician loads from one list. The three feature lines each end on a consequence ("so they never mix", "not a flat fee"). Keep that voice through any revision.
- **"See a sample firing" is a strong secondary ask.** It lets a skeptical studio owner check the product against how their own firings run, instead of taking the claim on trust. It's correctly styled lower than the primary button.
- **The type and grid are disciplined.** There's one sans family and a clear scale: a large headline, about 18 px body, medium-weight feature headings, and smaller feature text. The hero text, the rule, and the first feature column share one left edge at 80 px, and the three columns sit evenly spaced. The earthy brown primary button suits a ceramics audience.
