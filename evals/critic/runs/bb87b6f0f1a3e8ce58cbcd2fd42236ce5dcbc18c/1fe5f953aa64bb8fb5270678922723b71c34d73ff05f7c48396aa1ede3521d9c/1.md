**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a section of a public pool's website. It should help a visitor pick the session that suits them and then act on it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job yet. The copy is good, but each card stops halfway down. Nearly half of every card is empty, there's no next step, and the three unrelated colors shout without telling the viewer anything.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards are about twice as tall as their content (defect).** In all three cards the text ends around y≈330, and the colored box runs on to y≈630. That leaves about 300 px of solid orange, green and purple with nothing in it. A viewer reads that as missing content: an image that didn't load or details that were never added. It also means each card's empty bottom half is the largest saturated area on the screen, so the strongest color sits where there's nothing to read. Ruder's point is that empty space should be a deliberate area set against dense ones (*Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Form and counter-form"). This empty space is trapped inside the boxes and stated in full color, so it does no work. There are two ways to fix it: size the cards to their content, or fill the lower half with what the viewer needs to act (see 2). Either way the colored box should end where the information ends.
2. **There's no next step and no context, so the eye path stops at nothing (defect).** Each card gives the viewer a reason to come but no way to act. Nothing on the cards says how to book a class, what anything costs, or what to tap. Nothing above them says whose pool this is or what the section is. Family hour gives "Sunday afternoons" with no hours, and "Learn to swim" gives "Tuesday evenings" with no time. A parent or a beginner can't plan from that. The eye path should end on an action (Arnheim, *The Power of the Center*). Here it ends in the empty color. The fix is a short section heading that names the place, and one specific action per card that names its own case, such as booking the Tuesday class. Add the times and prices you have. Only you know those facts, so I won't suggest any.
3. **Three unrelated saturated colors carry no meaning or mood (taste, plus a template tell).** Burnt orange, green and violet have nothing to do with each other or with water. Each one floods a whole card, so all three are highlighted equally and none stands out. A viewer has seen this set of equal colored cards on many sites, and it makes the pool look like any other. Itten (*The Art of Color*) asks that color support the mood of the subject. A pool calls for something calmer or more watery than this, or at least a color tied to your brand. Refactoring UI asks for a small defined palette. Pick one color family that belongs to this pool, and let the headings tell the programs apart. If you want color coding, keep it to an accent per card instead of a full flood.

**Smaller things.**
- Two headings break in the middle of a phrase: "Join the / lanes" and "Learn to / swim". Widen the text area or adjust the size so each heading breaks as "Join / the lanes" or fits on fewer lines.
- The row of cards sits 56 px from the left edge but about 96 px from the right. That reads as a missing column or a near-miss, not a choice. Center the row or give the two sides equal margins.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific and speaks to the reader.** "With a coach in the water beside you" and "three speeds to choose from" answer the exact worry each audience has (fear of deep water, being stuck behind slower swimmers). Keep that voice when you add times and the action.
- **The type is clean, with two clear levels.** Tight, bold headings sit over plain body text that is set flush left and easy to read. Every card uses the same inner padding and the same type, so the three read as one set.
- **White text on all three colors looks comfortably readable**, by eye at about 5:1 or better.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether the cards are clickable. If they are links, a still shows nothing that signals it: no arrow, no button, no hover state. Check this in a browser.
