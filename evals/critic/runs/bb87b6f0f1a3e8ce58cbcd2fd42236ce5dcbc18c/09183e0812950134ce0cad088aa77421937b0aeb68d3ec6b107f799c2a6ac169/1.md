**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing", then to the teal-bordered Salon card, then down the form to the teal Save button. It reads as the settings page of a booking tool for pet groomers, where the salon owner manages their subscription. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. Nothing jumps out, and on a billing page that is the right feel.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is where a grooming business owner checks what they pay, switches plans, and keeps billing details and invoices in order.

**Verdict.** This is a clean, well-built settings page. The weak spot is the money part. It doesn't say which plan you're on now, when you'll be charged next, or what "Save changes" and "Cancel" do to your plan. A plan switch can't be undone in the owner's head, so that's where the page lets them down.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan cards don't show which plan is current or what happens when you change it.** Salon is selected with a filled radio and a teal border. But a selected radio in a form normally means "your pending choice", not "what you pay today". Nothing says *Current plan* on it, and the page shows no renewal or next-charge date. Now say an owner clicks Salon group. They can't tell whether that already changed something, whether "Save changes" (which sits under *Billing details*, two sections down) also saves the plan, or whether the $95.00 starts today, gets prorated, or starts next cycle. This is a defect: Nielsen's first heuristic, visibility of system status, asks the screen to show the current state. Fix direction: label the current plan as current on its card. Put the plan change next to the cards, as its own action that names its effect, or say plainly that Save covers it. Add the next-charge date and amount near the cards. Only the maker knows those dates and the proration rule.
2. **A bare "Cancel" on a billing page can be read two ways.** In the button pair under the form, "Cancel" could mean "throw away my edits" or "cancel my subscription". Both readings are reasonable on this page, and this is a button that commits the viewer to something. A nervous owner will avoid it, and a hasty one may expect it to end their plan. This is a defect. Fix direction: name the outcome on the button. For example, "Discard changes", if that's what it does. Keep any real subscription cancel somewhere separate and clearly labeled (Apple HIG and Material: label destructive actions with their specific outcome).
3. **Emoji stand in for icons.** The sidebar uses 🐾 👥 💳 🔔 🔌 and every invoice row has ✅. Emoji render differently on each operating system, come in mixed colors and visual weights, and are a stock move people have seen on countless generated dashboards. They make a carefully set page look like a template, and the green check boxes are the loudest color in the invoice table. This is a template tell (Sokolovsky, unslop: one line-icon set or nothing). Fix direction: use one monochrome line-icon set or no icons in the sidebar. For status, the word "Paid" already does the job, so at most add a small neutral mark.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and invoice address carry no required or optional marks. Add asterisks with a one-line legend, or "(optional)" after the optional labels (Budiu, NN/g).
- The plan cards compare different things. Mobile van and Salon give groomer and pet limits, but Salon group gives salons and "one pet list" with no groomer count. An owner weighing an upgrade can't tell whether they'd lose groomer seats. "One pet list" is also internal shorthand, so say what it means for them. Use the same attributes on every card. This is copy: restore the missing fact, which only the maker has.
- The invoice rows show no way to open or download an invoice, and an invoice is usually what someone comes to this table to get. Show the action, or make the invoice number a visibly marked link. This is common sense; no approved source states it.
- In the top nav, "Settings" is marked as current only by a slightly heavier weight, which is easy to miss next to its siblings. Use one stronger cue, such as an underline bar (Laubheimer, NN/g).
- The input borders and the borders of the unselected plan cards look very pale gray, likely under 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border color, and darken it if it fails.

**What's working.**
- The layout is quiet and orderly. One left edge at x≈300 carries the title, every section heading, the fields and the table. Thin rules separate the three sections, and there's one size step between page title, section headings and body. Keep that spine when you add the plan-change messaging.
- The invoice table is set well. Amounts are right-aligned in matching figures, there are no heavy gridlines, and the "Mobile van → Salon" history tells the owner their story at a glance.
- The color is restrained. Teal appears only on the selected plan, the checkbox and the one primary button, so the one action stands out.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the fields, radios and buttons. Whether choosing a plan card asks for confirmation or applies at once.
