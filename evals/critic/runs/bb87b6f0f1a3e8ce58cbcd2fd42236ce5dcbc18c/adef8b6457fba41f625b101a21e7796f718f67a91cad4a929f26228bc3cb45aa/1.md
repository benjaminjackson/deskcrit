The critique is finished; here it is in full.

**First read.** The eye goes to the "Listens" title and the four big numbers across the top, then to the one purple bar, Monday, on the right. It reads as a podcast analytics dashboard for a host checking last month's numbers. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. Nothing shouts, and every number has a comparison under it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the host of "The Long Table podcast" opens this to see at a glance how November went compared with October, and what to do about it, such as which day to publish and which episodes worked.

**Verdict.** This is a solid, well-organized dashboard that mostly does its job. The two charts are what let it down. The weekday chart's headline, "Monday is your day", is partly an artifact of the calendar. The "daily" chart doesn't show days. A host could act on either one and be wrong.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday chart rewards days that happened more often (defect).** In "Downloads by weekday" (right card, middle row), the Monday bar is highlighted in purple and labeled 9,860. But 1–30 November 2026 runs Sunday to Monday, so it has five Sundays and five Mondays and only four of every other weekday. If these bars are monthly totals, which they look like, then Monday and Sunday get a 25% head start. Their lead over Tuesday to Saturday is mostly that extra day, not listener behavior. That breaks Tufte's graphical integrity: the size of the effect shown doesn't match the effect in the data. A host would reasonably move publishing to Monday because of it. The chart also has no value axis and only one value label, so the other six bars can't be read at all, only compared by eye (Tufte; Few: every chart says what its marks measure). Fix: plot the average per weekday, or say plainly that these are totals and how many of each day the month had. Then either add a light value axis or label every bar.
2. **"Daily downloads" isn't daily (defect).** The line chart (middle row, left) plots about 12 points across a 30-day month, roughly one every two or three days. Its last point sits past the "30 Nov" tick. A viewer trying to find a spike on the day an episode came out can't, and the title promises something the chart doesn't deliver. This is common sense, not a cited rule. Fix: plot one point per day, or retitle the chart to say what each point is. Either way, end the line at 30 Nov.
3. **Nearly all the text is a tint of indigo (template tell).** The title, nav, KPI labels, axis labels, legend, table headers, table rows, date range and footer are all set in four or more near-identical purples and lavenders. It's a stock move (unslop: "many near-identical tints of one hue for text"), so the page looks like many other dashboards. It also costs this page something specific. The text loses its roles: in "Top episodes" the column headers and the rows are almost the same purple, and episode names and guest names in link-purple look clickable (see Smaller things). Fix: take the text off the accent hue. Use about three text roles from one neutral ramp (primary, secondary, muted), and keep purple for what it already means in the charts, the current period and the highlighted value.

**Smaller things.**
- Chart titles only name the topic ("Daily downloads", "Downloads by weekday"). The finding is the useful part for a host, for example that November ran above October every day. Taste: let the title or a short caption say it, once the charts above are corrected.
- The November/October legend sits in the top right of the line chart. Label the two lines at their right ends instead, so nobody has to look back and forth (Tufte; Wilke).
- Purple means "November" in the line chart and "top day" in the bar chart next to it. Two neighboring charts teach two meanings for one color. Taste: use a different emphasis for the bar highlight, or make the bar chart a November-vs-October comparison as well.
- The episode titles in "Top episodes" are purple with no other cue. If they open the episode, mark them as links by more than color. If they don't, set them in the plain text color (NN/g; WCAG 1.4.1).
- "1 to 30 November 2026" (top right) gives no sign that the period can be changed. If it's a picker, give it a control shape. Not being able to switch to other months is a real gap for a monthly report (Nielsen 7).

**What's working.**
- Every headline number has a comparison under it in plain words, such as "44,310 in October" and "29 min in October". The host sees the direction without a single arrow or percentage pill. Keep this pattern.
- The page is quiet and well structured. It has one row of KPIs, one row of charts and one table, with even gutters and margins, and everything fits on one screen. Gray for context and one accent for the thing that matters is the right instinct; it just needs to be applied correctly (finding 1).
- The table is set well: numbers right-aligned in tabular figures, light rules only, and no zebra striping or boxes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the episode rows and the date range are interactive, and what hovering over a chart shows. If the line chart's hover shows true daily values, finding 2 matters less, but the plotted line still has to match its title.
