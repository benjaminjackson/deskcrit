**First read.** The eye lands on "Output" and the row of four large numbers, then jumps to the blue bar F, the only strong color on the right. It reads as a calm monthly operations dashboard for a small solar co-op, and it feels clean and trustworthy. That is the trouble: after the first glance I still don't know whether this was a good month or a bad one.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a monthly view for the co-op's operators or board. It should show how much the arrays produced and paid out, whether that is better or worse than before, and which arrays need someone to go out to them.

**Verdict.** It's well made, but it doesn't do its job yet. The main chart seems to contradict the numbers above it, and the page hides its biggest news: output and payouts dropped about a quarter from October.

**What would move it most**
1. **The daily chart disagrees with the totals.** *Defect.* In "Daily output (kWh)" the blue November line sits above the gray October line on every day. But the cards above say November made 38.4 MWh against 51.2 MWh in October, and sold 29.1 against 40.6. Reading the lines by eye, October averages under 900 kWh a day, which gives about 26 MWh for the month, not 51.2. So the legend is swapped, the series are wrong, or the totals are wrong. Whichever it is, an operator who notices stops trusting every number on the page, and one who doesn't comes away believing November was the better month. This is what Tufte means by graphical integrity: what the picture shows has to match what the data says. Fix the data or the legend first; no other change matters until that's done. Also check the x-axis: the line has about 11 points, not 30, so "Daily" isn't what's plotted either.
2. **The page never says the month went badly.** *Taste, argued here; no approved source covers it.* Energy is down about 25%, payouts about 28% ($4,860 against $6,720), and three arrays are faulty. Yet each comparison is a small gray caption ("51.2 MWh in October") that the reader has to subtract in their head. No title or caption says what happened. "Output", "Daily output (kWh)" and "Output by array" name topics, not findings. A member who sees $4,860 in large type and moves on has missed the one thing they'd want to know. The fix direction: show each card's change from October with its direction, in words or signed numbers, not color alone. Then let one line near the top say what the month comes to, using only figures already on the page.
3. **The bar chart highlights the wrong array and can't be read.** *Defect.* "Output by array" has no value axis and no units. Only F is labeled (2.3 MWh), so the other six bars can only be compared by height, and you can't tell whether they start at zero (Tufte, Few: label the axes and units; bars start at zero). The blue goes to F, the best performer, while the table below asks for visits to D, G and B. The chart pulls the eye to the one array that needs nothing and leaves the trouble unmarked. That blue is also the "November" blue from the line chart, so one color means two different things side by side. Fix direction: give every bar its value or an axis in MWh. Then either drop the highlight or move it to the arrays in the visit table, marked by more than color.

**Smaller things.**
- The "November / October" legend in the line chart's top right should become labels at the end of each line (Tufte, Wilke).
- "Arrays needing a visit" lists faults but no owner, date or next step, so the reader has to go elsewhere to act. If rows open a detail view or assign a visit, show that on the row. *(Common sense.)*
- "1 to 30 November 2026" is plain text. If the period can be changed, it should look like a control. If it can't, a dashboard like this needs a way to change it (Nielsen 7).

**What's working.**
- **Restraint.** One neutral ground, one blue, gray for context, plain icons, no template effects. Keep that when you fix the color roles.
- **The visit table.** It's sorted by lost output, the numbers are right-aligned, and the arrays are named by place ("north roof", "barn"), which is how someone heading out to them thinks.
- **The cards.** Each one gives a unit, a period and an earlier value. The parts are there; what's missing is the direction of change (finding 2).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states in the sidebar and the table rows, and whether the table rows can be clicked.
