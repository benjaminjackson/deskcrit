**First read.** The eye goes to the row of three big numbers, starting with 184,220, then down to the three rising lines. It reads right away as a weekly marketing-funnel dashboard for a small SaaS team, and it feels calm and tidy. It also feels very familiar: Inter, a gray page, white rounded cards. Everything looks positive at a glance, with green deltas and lines going up.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: an overview screen where a growth or marketing lead checks, in a few seconds, whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy over the last 12 weeks.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job cleanly, but the numbers don't. The conversion card says the opposite of what the two cards beside it imply, and the chart doesn't add up to the total it sits under. A dashboard that a careful reader can catch contradicting itself loses their trust in every figure on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The conversion delta contradicts the other two cards and is colored as good news.** In the top-right card, if visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups rose 2.4%, conversion must have fallen. Working back from the cards' own figures, the previous rate was about 2.20%, so 2.12% is down about 0.08 pts, not "+0.1 pts". The green color then tells the viewer it's good news. That reverses the usual meaning of green (convention: Apple HIG / Material semantic color). The real story here, traffic up but conversion slipping, is exactly what this screen exists to show, and it's hidden. *Defect.* Fix: check how the delta is calculated, and color each delta by whether the change is good or bad, not by default. If the mock data is placeholder, replace it with numbers that agree with each other before anyone reviews it.
2. **The chart doesn't say what a point is, and its channels don't account for the total.** On the "Visits by channel" y axis (0–8k), nothing says whether a point is visits per week, per day, or cumulative. The viewer has to guess from the dates two weeks apart. Added up by eye, the three lines come to roughly 138k across the 12 weeks, against 184,220 in the Visits card. About a quarter of the traffic is missing, with no "Other" series and no note saying these are only the top channels. The viewer is left wondering which number is wrong (Tufte; Few: label axes and units). *Defect.* Fix: state the unit and period in the title or axis, such as "per week". Either show the remaining traffic as a gray "Other" line or say in the title that the chart covers only these channels.

**Smaller things.**
- Legend at the top-left of the chart: the three lines are well apart and end cleanly at the right, so labeling each one at its end would remove the look-back to small swatches (Tufte; Wilke).
- The type sizes look like about seven or eight close steps: 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 24, 28 px. The 13/14/15 group (deltas, card labels, "Last 12 weeks") and the brand vs. chart title at roughly 17/18 don't read as different levels. Fold them into about four sizes and use weight or gray for the rest (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI). *Taste, judged by eye: measure to confirm.*

**What's working.**
- The KPI row is the right structure: three equal cards, a label above, the number large, and a comparison under every figure. Keep the "vs previous 12 weeks" line on each card. It's what makes the numbers mean something.
- The chart is restrained: a zero baseline, faint gridlines, no markers or fills, and three hues that stay clearly apart by position. Keep it this quiet.
- Margins and gutters are consistent (the 48 px side margin, equal gaps between cards), so the page reads as one grid without visible effort.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- "Last 12 weeks" at top right: if it's a period picker, it currently looks like a plain label with no shape or arrow. Check it in the live screen.
- The chart card stops about 17 px above the bottom of the image. That's probably the viewport cut, but check whether the page continues.
