**First read.** The eye lands straight on the big serif quote at the top left and reads it to the end, then drops to the bold name, then to the gray line under it. It reads as a pull-quote page from a small local newsletter, and it feels calm, unhurried, and editorial, which suits the words. The one thing that catches on the first pass is the top left corner: the opening quote mark pushes "The" in from the edge that every other line follows.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a feature or opener page for an issue of a local newsletter. It should get a reader to stop on one voice from the harbour and want to read on.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the type pairing is sound, and it has nothing extra. The only thing that keeps the craft from being finished is the opening quote mark, which sits inside the left edge instead of hanging outside it.

**What would move it most**
1. **Hang the opening quotation mark.** At this display size, the "“" on the first line of the quote sits on the 100 px left edge. That pushes "The" about 35 to 40 px to the right of "just", "leaves", and "the" below it. The quote is the biggest and most-looked-at thing on the page, so the eye reads the first line as indented and the block's strong left edge breaks at its most visible point. The kicker, name, and caption all hold that edge cleanly, which makes the break stand out more. Move the quote mark into the left margin (for example `hanging-punctuation: first` in Safari, or a negative indent on the first line about the width of the mark) so the "T" lines up with the letters below. Hanging punctuation in the margin is a long-standing typesetting practice: Bringhurst recommends hanging list numerals in the margin, and this applies the same idea to the quote mark. The catalog's source for the quote-mark case itself is unconfirmed, so treat this as craft judgment, and the entry notes the defect grows with type size. At roughly 80 px it is plainly visible. Defect in craft, low cost to fix.

**What's working.**
- **The scale contrast is clear.** The quote is several times the size of everything else, so there is no doubt where to start. Below it, the kicker, name, and caption are told apart by weight and color (bold dark name, regular gray caption) rather than by sizes that nearly match. Keep that approach.
- **The type pairing works.** A text serif for the voice and a plain sans for the facts makes two clearly different faces with two clear jobs, and the "fi" ligature in "first" is set properly at display size.
- **The caption earns its place.** "The dawn crossing departs at 5:40 and carries bicycles, bread deliveries, and a few early walkers" turns the quote's mood into a concrete scene and a time, without repeating it. Its line length is about 70 characters, inside the comfortable range.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the quote rewraps at narrower widths. If it reflows, check that the hung quote mark still has margin to sit in on a phone, where the left margin will be much smaller than 100 px.
