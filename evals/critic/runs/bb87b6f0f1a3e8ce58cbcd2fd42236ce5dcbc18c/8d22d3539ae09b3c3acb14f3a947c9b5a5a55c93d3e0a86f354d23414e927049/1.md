**Verdict.** The typesetting is clean and disciplined, but the page doesn't do its job on the first screen. It is labeled a timetable and gives no time a rider could act on. The headline takes up the top half of the screen to restate the label, and the one line with facts leaves out the facts a rider needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times on the first screen (defect).** "Spring timetable" promises times. What the screen gives is about 40% of its height of display type, then "every forty minutes" with nothing to count from. A rider arriving at the north landing can't tell when the next boat leaves. "By daylight" doesn't say when the first or last crossing is, and "spring through early summer" doesn't say which dates. These are the facts riders plan around and hold the operator to. As written, "every forty minutes" can mean a fixed schedule or a rough frequency, and a rider who guesses wrong waits up to 40 minutes or misses the last boat.
   - **Fix:** Bring the facts above the fold: the first and last departures from each landing, and the dates the season starts and ends. Do it by cutting the headline's size or its length, or both.
   - **Copy:** The headline already carries the season and the daylight idea, so it can lose words without losing meaning. The paragraph needs the missing facts restored, such as "every forty minutes from [first time] to [last time]". Only you have those numbers.
2. **The headline is oversized for what it says (taste).** At about 80 px over four lines, it's the only thing on the page with any weight. It also says less than the 20 px paragraph under it. Size should go to the element the rider came for, which here is a time.
   - **Fix:** Keep the headline dominant, but make it two lines, not four. That frees the space finding 1 needs.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** "early / summer" splits one phrase, and "summer" ends up alone on the last line. Break after "daylight," or shorten the headline so the phrase stays whole (Bringhurst §2.4; Hochuli).
- **Paragraph runt:** "weather." sits alone on the paragraph's last line. Widen the measure slightly or cut a word so the last line holds more than one word (Bringhurst §2.4.2).
- **Headline weight:** The headline looks like extra-bold, around 800. That's a stock template move (Sokolovsky, unslop). At this size, the bold weight (700) or lower would still dominate and look less like every other landing page.

**What's working.**
- **Discipline:** There's one left edge at x=120 for every element, and three type sizes with big steps between them (label about 15 px, headline about 80 px, body about 20 px). Each size has an obvious job. Keep this as you add the times.
- **Headline setting:** The leading is tight, so the four lines hold together as one block.
- **Body measure:** The paragraph runs about 68 characters per line with comfortable leading, which is right for easy reading.
- **Copy voice:** The writing is plain and specific ("north landing," "market quay," "about twelve minutes in calm weather"). It sounds like a local operator, not a template.
