**First read.** My eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers" and then drops into a single column of dark gray text on the left. Before I read a word, the column looks like a stack of separate lines more than three paragraphs, because every line floats in its own band of white. It reads as a plain, calm information page for people taking a local ferry. It feels clean but a bit airy and unfinished, with the right 40% of the frame empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming a short web page or notice that tells ferry passengers when the boat runs, how bikes work, and how winter changes the schedule, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The basics are sound: one clear heading, a readable face, a good line length and solid contrast. But the line spacing is so loose that the three paragraphs dissolve into eight evenly spaced lines. The timetable copy is also too vague to plan a trip by. Those two things decide whether a passenger finds the answer they came for.

**What would move it most**
1. **Line spacing is so loose that the paragraphs disappear (defect).** In all three paragraphs, the lines sit about 45 px apart for what looks like 18–19 px type. That's roughly 2.4 times the type size. The gap between paragraphs is only about 64 px, so the step from "one line" to "next paragraph" is small. The eye can't group the lines into three topics (schedule, bikes, winter) and has to read every line to find the edges. Moving from the end of one line to the start of the next also takes a moment longer, because the lines drift apart. Leading should match the line length (Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* 2.2.1; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 47), and this measure of about 65–70 characters needs nowhere near this much. Groups only show when the space inside a group is clearly smaller than the space between groups (Arnheim, on Wertheimer's proximity rule). Fix: tighten the leading to a normal text setting for this measure, and keep a clearly larger gap between paragraphs. Each of the three paragraphs should then read as one block at a glance. Measure the line-height and paragraph margin in the live page to confirm.
2. **The times a passenger acts on can't be pinned down (defect, copy).** "Between early morning and late evening", "every forty minutes", "in winter" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" are exactly what a reader will plan around and hold the operator to. Each one can be read more than one way:
   - "Early morning" could mean 6:00 or 8:00, and "late evening" could mean 21:00 or 23:00.
   - "Every forty minutes" doesn't say what time the first boat leaves, so the passenger can't work out any departure.
   - "Before dusk" moves by an hour or more across the winter, so someone arriving at 16:30 can't tell whether they've missed the last boat.
   - "In winter" doesn't say which months.

   The cost of a wrong reading is a passenger stranded at the quay. Fix: put the real first and last departure times, the winter dates, and the winter last-boat time where the vague phrases are now. Only the operator has those facts. Once they're in, a small timetable may serve better than prose.

**Smaller things.**
- The bicycle paragraph is clear but ends on the rule rather than the reader. "Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded" works, though saying it to the reader ("wait until foot passengers have boarded, then wheel your bike on") would make the instruction harder to miss. Taste.

**What's working.**
- **Line length is right.** The column stops at about 65–70 characters per line, inside Bringhurst's 45–75 range, so once the leading is fixed the text will be comfortable to read. Keep the column width.
- **Clear heading.** The heading is set apart by both size and weight, with no competing element, so the page has one obvious starting point. It also says plainly who the page is for.
- **Restraint.** Near-black on white, one family, no decoration. For a practical notice that's the right register.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the column reflows on a phone, and whether the loose leading is a fixed value or scales with the type.
