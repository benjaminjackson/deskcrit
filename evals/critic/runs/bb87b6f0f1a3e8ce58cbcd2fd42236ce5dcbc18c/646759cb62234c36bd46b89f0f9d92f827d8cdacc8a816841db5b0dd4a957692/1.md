**First read.** The eye lands on the big headline "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the gray subhead, and stops on the green "Get started". Then it drifts right into a wide, empty beige field and finds nothing there. It reads as a calm, tidy hero for a trip-planning app. It feels capable but unfinished, as if the main image never loaded.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a landing page for a group-trip planner, meant to get people who plan trips with friends or family to understand the product and start one.

**Verdict.** The type and the buttons are well made, but the hero doesn't do its job yet. Almost half the screen is blank, and the most specific thing the page says, that a group can agree on a trip without chat threads, is in small gray text under a headline any travel app could use.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half and the lower third are empty, so the page tips left and seems to be missing something.** Everything with any weight sits in a column on the left, about 600 px wide: the headline, the subhead and the two buttons. The right ~45% of the screen and everything below the buttons is blank beige. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") says a heavy side needs a counterweight, and nothing here plays that role. Visitors don't read the gap as calm space, because the left-aligned column plainly leaves room for something. They read it as a picture that failed to load. It also costs the page its best tool: a product page with no product on it asks visitors to take "one place" on trust when they could have seen it. **Defect.** If a visual exists and simply failed to load in the capture, this point becomes a check: confirm it loads. If there isn't one, the right side needs a preview of the product doing what the subhead promises, such as one trip with its flights, stays and day plans together, or a group agreeing on something. A stock travel photo would just be the empty-hero template move filled in.
2. **The headline is the generic line; what makes this product different is in the subhead.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" could top almost any travel app's page, so visitors can't tell at a glance how this one differs. The point that does set it apart is in the gray subhead: groups, agreeing, "without a dozen chat threads". That line also does what the best asks do. It compares the product with something visitors already have and know is painful, their group chat. Yet it is set at roughly a third of the headline's size, in a lighter gray. **Defect (copy).** Lead with the group-agreement idea. Move it into the headline or bring the headline closer to it, using words already on the page. Don't add new claims.
3. **"Get started" doesn't say what happens next.** It's the only filled button and the strongest thing on the page after the headline, but visitors can't tell whether it signs them up, starts a trip, or invites friends. **Defect (copy).** The label should name the first thing the visitor does, in terms of their own trip or group. The maker knows what that first step is, so the maker should write it.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place", so "in" hangs at the end of line 1, apart from its phrase. Break it before "in" ("Plan the whole trip / in one place") by adjusting the column width or using a non-breaking space. This follows Bringhurst/Hochuli on phrase breaks, but the entry marks it as unconfirmed for headings, so treat it as common sense.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean. There's one dominant headline, a clearly smaller subhead in regular weight and gray, then the buttons. That's about four sizes with big steps between them and no two that collide, so keep that scale when you add the visual.
- The primary and secondary buttons are a correct pair. "Get started" is solid green and "See pricing" is an outline in the same green and size, so the order of importance is obvious without a third style.
- The palette is restrained and has its own character: warm off-white, near-black green type and one deep green accent. It avoids the stock gradient or dark-template look, and it's quiet and trustworthy for a planning tool.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image or animation is supposed to fill the right half and didn't load in this capture. Check in a live browser.
- The click areas of the top navigation links. They look like bare text about 20 px tall, so measure that they reach 24×24 CSS px (WCAG 2.5.8).
- Focus indicators on the buttons and links.
