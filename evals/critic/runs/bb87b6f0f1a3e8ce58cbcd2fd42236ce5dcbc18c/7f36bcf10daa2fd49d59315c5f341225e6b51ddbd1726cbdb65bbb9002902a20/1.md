I've finished the walk and the deep read, and I'm writing up the crit now.

**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline at the top left, then moves right to the field map card. The dark green M4 pitch pulls it in, and the eye ends on "$20 deposit paid." Within a few seconds it reads as booking software for small campsite owners. It feels calm, rural and competent. A soft green haze behind the headline and a yellow glow behind the card make it feel softer and more generic than everything else on the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero of a marketing landing page, built to get small campsite and farm-field owners to add their site or open the sample map.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the copy is specific, and the product picture shows real value. The biggest thing holding it back is the blurred green and yellow color washes. They're the one stock move on a page that otherwise feels like it belongs to this product.

**What would move it most**
1. **Blurred color washes behind the hero (template tell).** There's a large green blob behind the headline and a yellow glow behind the map card. Both run off the top and left edges of the page. Visitors have seen this exact haze on countless startup pages, so it makes Pitchbook look like anyone's product. It's the most generic thing here on a page whose strength is being specific (Hollow Farm, "Track to the wash block," the gate after dark). It also makes the brand's green show up in two different ways: as haze, and as the solid button and M4 tile. Fix: use a plain background and let the field map be the one visual (Sokolovsky, unslop: "plain background or one real product visual"). If you want a color field, choose one that belongs to this subject rather than a glow.
2. **The ask doesn't compare against how owners work today (copy).** "Add your campsite" is a clear, exact action, and "See a sample field map" lets the visitor judge the product, which is good. But nothing on the page puts Pitchbook next to how an owner takes bookings now. The page only lists what the product does, so the visitor has to take the value on trust. Fix: in the body line or the feature row, name the comparison the visitor already knows: the way they handle arrivals and deposits today. That fact is the maker's to supply.

**Smaller things.**
- The heading "Deposits that come back" (feature row, middle column) can be read two ways. Campers getting their deposits back is the intended meaning. To an owner it can also sound like money leaving the business, or payments bouncing. The heading should name what the section holds, which is refunding a deposit when a camper cancels, so lead with the verb the body already uses ("refund").

**What's working.**
- **The field map is a real product visual, not a placeholder.** The named field, pitch IDs, the "Track to the wash block" strip, one selected pitch and its booking detail ("Pitch M4, hardstanding / Arriving Friday, two nights / $20 deposit paid") show the whole loop in one glance. Keep it as the hero visual, and keep the selected pitch tied to the detail line.
- **Clear, quiet order of importance.** One big headline, a readable body text at about 50 characters per line, one solid primary button and an underlined secondary link. Nothing else competes.
- **Copy in the owner's own words.** "whoever is on the gate after dark" and "what is left to pay" sound like the owner's world, not software marketing. The feature row's three columns sit on clean thirds and share baselines.
