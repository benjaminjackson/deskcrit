**First read.** The eye goes straight to the red and orange blotch in the middle of the grid, then to the title, then across to the three numbers on the right. It's clearly a support manager's report on when tickets come in. It looks tidy and calm, apart from the chart's colors, which are louder than anything else on the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a support lead uses this report page in the help-desk app to plan staffing by hour and day.

**Verdict.** The layout, copy and stats are solid, but the page contradicts itself on the one number a staffing decision rests on. The rainbow color scale also makes the shape of the week harder to read than it needs to be. Fix the contradiction first.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Busiest hour, weekdays: 10:00" stat disagrees with the chart.** Read the columns against their labels: the 09:00 label sits over the yellow column, so 10:00 is the orange column, and the 11:00 column is red on Monday and Friday and orange the rest of the week. On the chart, the peak is 11:00, not 10:00. A manager who trusts the stat staffs up an hour early. One who reads the chart starts to doubt every number on the page. Either the stat is wrong, the axis labels are one column off, or the stat counts hours differently (for example, the hour ending at 10:00). Whichever it is, make the stat and the chart agree, and if they count hours differently, say which convention is used. **Defect.**
2. **The rainbow scale works against the data.** The seven bins run violet, blue, teal, green, yellow-green, yellow, orange, red. That's seven hues for one count that goes from low to high. Ordered values should use one hue that goes from light to dark, so the order of the colors follows the order of the values (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). This rainbow causes three problems:
   - **Quiet hours shout.** The 0–4 hours, which are most of the grid, are a strong saturated violet. The emptiest part of the week carries the most color on the page.
   - **The order breaks in grayscale.** Yellow (20–24) is lighter than teal and blue (5–14), so the order of light to dark doesn't follow the counts. In grayscale, or for anyone who reads by lightness, the midday peak doesn't stand out (Albers; Refactoring UI, "Don't rely on color alone").
   - **Neighbors blur for color-blind viewers.** Green (15–19) and orange (25–29) sit next to each other all afternoon, so a red-green color-blind viewer can't tell busy from very busy, and the cells have no numbers to fall back on (WCAG 1.4.1).

   Red and green also bring their usual meanings of "bad" and "good" to what is just a count. The fix: one ramp from a pale near-white for 0–4 to a dark color for 30+, so empty hours fade back and the peak is the darkest thing on the page. **Defect.** The full-spectrum default is also a stock chart look. **Template tell.**
3. **The title names the topic, not the finding.** "When new tickets arrive" tells the viewer what the chart is, then leaves them to find the pattern. The finding is visible in the chart: weekday late mornings are the crunch, and weekends stay low. Once item 1 is settled, a title or caption that states it would save every reader that work. Leave the wording to the maker. **Taste** (common sense: it's a report people check to make a decision).

**Smaller things.**
- **"New tickets, September 3,412 / Up 4% on August."** The chart covers 1 to 28 September. It's unclear whether 3,412 covers those same 28 days or the whole month, and whether the comparison is against all 31 days of August. If the periods differ, the 4% is partly an artifact of month length. Name the period next to the number.

**What's working.**
- **The subtitle and source line.** "Average week, 1 to 28 September", "times in the support team's local time" and "when a ticket was opened, not when it was answered" answer exactly the questions a careful reader would ask. Keep them.
- **The stats carry their own context.** Each big number has a comparison to August under it ("Up 4%", "Down 6 min") instead of standing alone, and the numbers' muted color means you don't have to guess whether "down" is good.
- **The layout is restrained.** The page has one chart, one column of stats, a legend lined up with the grid, plain type, and no boxes or effects. The chart is the page, as it should be.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether hovering or focusing a cell shows its exact count. That matters here because the bins are wide (5 tickets each).
