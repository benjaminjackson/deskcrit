**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, which is large, dark, and alone on the left. It then jumps to the dark teal E4 tile in the floor plan and comes back down to the teal "Add your first floor" button. It reads as a booking tool for people who run coworking spaces. It feels calm, competent and unhurried, and it is clearly about desks rather than "workspace management" in general.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the hero of a marketing homepage that persuades coworking-space operators to set up their first floor in Deskwell.

**Verdict.** This is a good hero that does its job. The headline names a concrete outcome, the product mock shows that outcome, and the ask is one specific step. The biggest problem is in the mock: green marks the booked desks and the free desks are pale and faint. A viewer reads the floor plan the wrong way round, and the plan is the one picture meant to prove the product.

**What would move it most**
1. **The floor plan's colors read backwards.** In the card on the right, 14 desks are filled sage green and the 3 free desks (D3, E6, F5) are white with a faint dashed border. Almost everyone reads green as available, so a first-time viewer takes the green tiles as open desks. Then "18 desks, 3 free" in the corner doesn't match what they see, and they have to work out the key from that count. The free desks are also the hardest thing in the card to see: white on a cream card, edged by a dashed line that looks well below 3:1. This is a **defect** in the mock and breaks a **convention**. Apple HIG and Material reserve green for success or availability and say not to reverse status colors, and WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 on edges that identify a component. Fix: show booked desks in a neutral (gray or a muted sage that doesn't read as "go"), and give the free desks a solid edge or fill that clearly stands out. The three desks a member could book should be the ones the eye finds.
2. **The logo is a stock gradient tile with a sparkle.** The mark at top left is a teal-to-blue gradient square with a four-point sparkle, and the wordmark sits in a brighter blue than the teal used everywhere else. Viewers have seen this mark on many AI-era products. It makes Deskwell look like anyone's product at the one spot meant to be its own, and the sparkle says nothing about desks or floors. **Template tell** (Sokolovsky, unslop). Fix: use a simple flat mark that belongs to this product, and set the wordmark in the page's own teal or ink.

**Smaller things.**
- "For hosts" in the nav: the hero already speaks to the people who run the space, so a separate "For hosts" link leaves the viewer unsure who "Product" is for, or who "hosts" are. Rename one of them so the two audiences are distinct (Norman, conceptual model).
- Headline breaks: "book a / desk" splits the article from its noun, and "home" sits alone on line 3. Rebreak so the lines end on phrases, for example "Let members book a desk / before they leave home". This is common sense; the source for heading breaks is unconfirmed.
- "Desk E4, by the window": nothing in the plan shows a window. Either mark the window side of the plan or drop the detail, so the mock doesn't claim something it doesn't show.

**What's working.**
- **The copy makes the case in the viewer's terms.** "Let members book a desk before they leave home" names a result, and the body ends on "the front desk stops keeping a paper list". That sets the product next to something the operator already has and dislikes. Keep that comparison.
- **The ask is one exact step.** "Add your first floor" is a clear primary button. "Walk through a sample floor" is an underlined text link, clearly lower in rank, and it gives a hesitant viewer a no-commitment path. One primary, one secondary, nothing else.
- **The mock is specific, not decoration.** A named floor ("Mill Yard, second floor"), desk IDs, the kitchen and phone booth band, and a real booking with a price make the product concrete. The feature row below avoids icon cards and states each feature as cause and effect ("Move a desk on the plan, and the bookings move with it").
