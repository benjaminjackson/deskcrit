**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big near-black box at top left, which holds a gray-brown person silhouette. It then drops to the bold headline, picks up the brown date above it, and ends on the black button. The right half of the screen and the band below the button are empty. It reads as a bookshop's page for an author reading: tidy and quiet, but it looks like a page someone hasn't finished building.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the event page on an independent bookshop's website. It's for local readers who should decide to come to Ines Varga's reading and book a seat.

**Verdict.** The text and button do their job well. The page fails visually, though. The largest, first-seen element is a placeholder silhouette, and the whole layout sits in the left half of a wide screen. Together they make the page look unfinished, and nothing on it shows the author or the book.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero picture is a placeholder** (top left, 600×330 px, the largest thing on the page). The first thing a visitor sees is a generic avatar on a dark gradient. It tells them nothing about Ines Varga or *The Salt Year*, and it looks like an image that failed to load. This is a page about a person and a book, and the one picture shows neither. Defect, and a template tell (unsloth, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"): visitors have seen this gray avatar on countless unfinished sites, so the page looks like anyone's. If the real photo just hadn't loaded when this was captured, check it live. Fix: use a real author photo or the book cover, which would also give the headline something to work with.
2. **The layout uses half the screen and leaves the rest as leftover space** (the right half, x ≈ 650–1280, and everything below y ≈ 680). All the weight sits in a ~600 px column on the left, so the page looks lopsided, as if a second column never arrived (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). This empty space doesn't look planned (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"); it looks like the column failed to stretch. Convention. Fix: either put the picture and the text side by side so the two halves balance, or center a narrower column on purpose so the emptiness reads as framing. The right half is also the natural place for the practical details (see Smaller things).
3. **The date can be read two ways.** "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. Thursday matches 13 November 2025, but 13 November in the current year (2026) is a Friday. A reader will either think the weekday is a typo or wonder whether this is an old event still on the site. The date is what people plan around and hold the shop to, so this is a defect, not a style note. Fix: make the date and weekday agree and, if the page can outlive the event, add the year. Only the shop knows which date is correct.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav (top right) doesn't mark "Events" as the current page. Give it one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Nielsen Norman Group, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Nothing near the button says where the event takes place. Readers will assume the shop, but they'd have to go to "Find us" to check. A one-line venue or address next to the date or button would answer it where they decide (common sense).

**What's working.**
- The copy. "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor" tells a stranger exactly what the evening is. "Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm" answers the next question before they ask it.
- "Book a free seat" is an exact ask: it says what you get, that it costs nothing, and that seats are limited, all in four words. It's the only button, so there's nothing to compete with it.
- The type is clean: four distinct levels (headline, body, date, nav) with big steps between them. The brown date is set apart by color and weight, not by another size. The two-line headline is tightly set and holds together.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the silhouette is the real design or a photo that failed to load in this capture. Check it in a live browser.
- How the half-width column reflows on tablet and phone, where it may well look intended.
