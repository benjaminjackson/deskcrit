**First read.** The eye goes first to the blue Monday bar on the right, the only saturated block on the page, and then to the four big numbers across the top. It reads as a calm, well-made support dashboard for a small retail brand (kettles, Harrow & Pine). It feels tidy and trustworthy until you read the bottom table, where an "oldest open" list includes a ticket marked Solved.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home screen for a support lead at a small shop: it shows how this month compares with last, and what needs attention now.

**Verdict.** The layout, type and color are strong, and the KPI row does its job. What's in the way is that the data contradicts itself in two places. A support lead who notices will stop trusting every number on the screen, and the one part meant to drive action, the table, doesn't hold what its title promises.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "Oldest open tickets" table lists a solved ticket and doesn't feel like a backlog.** In the bottom panel, the third row, "Refund for a returned kettle", has a blue **Solved** badge under a heading that says *open*. Its oldest item has waited 6 h. Either the heading is wrong or the filter is. Whichever it is, the viewer can no longer trust that this list is their to-do list. This is also the only place on the page that says what to do next, and with three rows and a 6 h maximum, a lead can't tell whether the queue is healthy or whether the 24 inbox items behind the nav badge are the real problem. *Defect.* Fix direction: make the table hold only what the heading says (drop solved rows, or rename it to say what it really shows). If "oldest" is the point, give the lead a way to tell 6 h from a breach, such as the response target, if the maker has one.

2. **The two charts can't both be true.** "Tickets by weekday" gives Monday 262. November 2026 has five Mondays, so that's about 52 tickets a Monday. Yet the "Tickets opened per day" line never goes above about 47. The bars also say weekends are a third of weekdays, but the daily line shows no weekend dips. It's a smooth climb of about 12 points across 30 days, which doesn't look daily at all. Anyone who checks one against the other will think the data is wrong (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2, on graphical integrity). *Defect.* If this is mock data, fix it before this goes in front of anyone. If it's real, one of the charts is plotting something other than its title says (weekly points, or another date range), and the title or the data has to change.

3. **The bar chart has a single number and no scale.** Only Monday carries a value (262). Tuesday to Sunday have no axis and no labels, so the viewer can see the shape but can't read a single other value. The line chart's y-axis also has no unit, although the title covers it (Tufte, ch. 2; Few on labeling). *Convention, and it costs something here,* because "how many on Saturday" is a normal question for planning staff. Fix direction: put the value on every bar (there's room above each one), or add a light axis to match the line chart.

**Smaller things**
- The count pills in the sidebar (24, 7, 3) and the status badges in the table (Open, Waiting on customer, Solved) have colored glows. That's a stock template move the viewer has seen on many products, and it's the one spot where this careful page looks like anyone's (unslop, "Colored glows around elements", "Glowing pills for counts and status"). Drop the glow and keep the flat tint and text.
- The line chart uses a legend (November / October) at top right. Put each label at the right end of its line, next to the 30 Nov values, so nobody has to look back and forth (Tufte; Wilke).
- The chart titles only name the topic. Taste: on a lead's dashboard, a title that says the finding ("Mondays are busiest", if that's the point) saves the reading. Leave the claim to the maker.

**What's working**
- **The KPI row.** Each number comes with its October comparison in small gray text right underneath it. The plain-language label "Happy with the answer" says what the metric means better than "CSAT" would. Keep this pattern.
- **Color restraint.** One blue for "now", gray for context, in both charts. That's why the Monday bar and the November line read at once without a legend.
- **The type and grid.** One family, three or four clearly separate sizes, panel edges that line up with the KPI row, and the Waiting column right-aligned. The page is calm because nothing fights for position.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether table rows and KPI cards are clickable. Nothing on screen says they are, and on a support home screen a row should open its ticket.
- The pale gray secondary text ("1,192 in October", axis labels) and the light gray October line look close to the contrast minimums. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.3 and 1.4.11.
