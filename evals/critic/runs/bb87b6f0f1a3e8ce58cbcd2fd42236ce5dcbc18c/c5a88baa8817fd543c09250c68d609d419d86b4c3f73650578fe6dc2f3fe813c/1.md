**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild", drops into the opening paragraph, and then goes to the blue Sign up button on the right. It reads as a news item from a small ferry service with a timetable-alert signup next to it. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, like a civic site.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a ferry operator's update page that tells passengers about the rebuilt landings and gets them to sign up for timetable-change notes.

**Verdict.** The page is tidy and easy to read, but the one thing on it that asks the reader to act can't work. The signup promises "one short note" and never asks where to send it. The picture also looks like a stand-in for the photo the caption describes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The signup form has no way to reach the person.** In the right-hand card, "Your name" and "Home landing" are the only fields. The note below the button says "We send one short note when the timetable changes", but there's no email or phone field. A careful reader stops and wonders how the note arrives. A less careful one signs up, gets nothing, and misses the change the form was supposed to warn them about. This is a **defect**, and common sense, not a cited rule. Fix: add the field for whatever channel you actually send on, and name that channel in the note under the button ("We email one short note…"). Then the promise and the form agree.
2. **"Home landing" is a free-text box for what is a fixed list.** The service has a known set of landings. A text box invites spelling variants and names that don't match, so the alert can silently fail to match the person's route. This is a **convention** (Nielsen, heuristic 5: error prevention, which calls for input constraints such as pickers). Fix: a select or radio list of the actual landings, with no default if you don't know theirs.
3. **The figure looks like a placeholder, not the landing.** The image under the opening paragraph is five flat blocks: gray, dark gray, a blue rounded bar, and a pale blue band. The caption promises "The new north landing, seen from the water at low tide". The article's whole case is visible: wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, ramps that meet the deck at every tide. The picture shows none of it, so it adds nothing to what the headline already says. This is a **defect** unless the block drawing is a deliberate diagram, and in that case it still needs labels for the ramp and the cover. Fix: use the real photo the caption describes, or label the diagram so it shows the step-free ramp at low tide.

**Smaller things.**
- Neither field is marked required or optional. Add an asterisk with a one-line legend, or mark the optional field (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- The left column has three right edges. The first paragraph runs to about x=686, the figure ends at about x=656, and the second paragraph wraps near x=590. The column looks set by eye (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Give text and figure one measure.
- The blue rounded bar in the figure is the same blue and shape as the Sign up button, so it reads faintly like a second button. Change its color or shape so only the real control carries the action blue (Arnheim, on similarity).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: heading, card heading, body, then a small gray size for captions and helper text. Every size has an obvious job, and weight does the work between the two headings.
- The opening paragraph is concrete and written for a passenger: wider ramps, a covered area, tea and bread rolls while the boat loads. The second paragraph ends on what it means for the reader ("prams and wheelchairs roll straight on"). Keep that voice.
- The form basics are right: one column, labels above the fields, field borders that look strong enough, and a single primary button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the fields and button show a visible focus ring for keyboard users. Check it in a live browser, since this form is the page's only task.
