**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's lines drift apart.** The three lines of "The farmers who are letting their fields flood…" look about 72 px apart for type around 50 px tall. That's spacing suited to body text, used on display type. The wide white bands between the lines break the headline up, so it reads as three separate lines and loses some of its weight. A long headline needs to hold together most of all. Fix: tighten the headline's line spacing until it's close to the type size. Stop before descenders like the p in "purpose" and the g in "letting" reach the line below. Kind: common sense, not convention. The checked sources (Bringhurst, p. 37; Hochuli, p. 28) only back the opposite limit, that heading lines must not collide. The idea that tighter spacing holds a heading together isn't confirmed in an approved source, so treat this as my judgment. It's still the most visible fix on the page.

**Smaller things.**
- **The header and the story aren't separated by space.** The gaps from label to headline, headline to byline, and byline to first paragraph all look about the same, roughly 35–45 px. So the byline floats halfway between the headline and the text, and the header doesn't read as one unit sitting above the story. Fix: tighten the space between the label, headline and byline, and leave the bigger gap before the first paragraph (Arnheim, on grouping by proximity). Defect, mild.
- **"hand's" in the first paragraph uses a straight typewriter apostrophe.** Fix: use the curly ’. This is at body size, so it's a craft detail, not something readers will stumble on.

**What's working.**

- **The order of importance.** There's one bold headline, a single green label as the only color, and a small gray sans-serif byline. Each level differs from the next in size, weight, typeface or color, so nothing competes. The small, sparing use of green for "Environment" ties the label to the subject without turning it into decoration.
