**What would move it most**
1. **The two columns sit on different baselines.** Convention. Body lines in the left column fall at about y=167, 193, 218 and so on. The right column starts at about y=127, and that 40 px offset isn't a whole line. Neither column's lines line up with the other's, and the "Going home" heading (y≈251) and its paragraph fall between the left column's lines. When you look across the gutter, the lines don't line up and the page looks slightly off. Müller-Brockmann (*Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid") says lines in neighboring columns should sit on the same baselines, and headings should keep to the same line intervals. To fix it, put both columns on one baseline grid of the body leading. Start the right column on a body baseline. Size the space above and below each heading so that heading plus space adds up to a whole number of body lines, so the text after every heading lands back on the grid.

Keep this exactly as it is. It's what makes the text read as continuous prose and not as a stack of blocks. My only reservation is taste: the indent looks close to 2 em, and a little shallower would be quieter.

- **A clear type scale with only three levels.** The title, the bold headings, and the body each have an obvious job. The headings also differ from the body in weight, not just in size, and each one sits clearly closer to the text it introduces than to the text above it.
