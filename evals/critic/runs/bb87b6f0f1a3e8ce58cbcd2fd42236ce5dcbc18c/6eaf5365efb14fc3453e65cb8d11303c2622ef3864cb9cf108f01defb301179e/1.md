**First read.** The eye goes first to "Plan and billing", then to the teal-bordered Garden card, then down the form to the teal Save button. It reads as the settings page of a small SaaS tool for running a community garden, used by a committee member or treasurer. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. It's competent work, with no visual drama, which is right for this page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a committee member or treasurer of a community garden checks or changes the group's plan, keeps the billing details right, and finds past invoices.

**Verdict.** It looks clean and is well built, but it fails at the money moments. The page doesn't make clear what changing a plan does, what the three plans actually differ on, or how to get an invoice. Those three questions are why anyone opens this page.

**What would move it most**
1. **It's unclear what a plan change does or when it happens.** The three plan cards sit above "Billing details", and one "Save changes" button sits below the billing fields. A viewer who clicks the Network card can't tell whether that switches the plan at once, waits for Save, or whether Save covers only the email and address. The page also doesn't say what the switch commits them to: when the new price starts, whether this month is prorated, or what a downgrade takes away. The sidebar shows 12 people on the waiting list, and only the Garden card mentions a waiting list, so moving to Allotment may quietly drop them. A control should sit next to the thing it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping), and help belongs right beside the step where the task gets hard (Nielsen, heuristic 10). Fix direction: give the plan its own commit action, separate from the billing form. Next to it, say when the change takes effect and what it costs, and on a downgrade, what the group loses. Mark the selected card as the current plan in words, not only with a teal border. *Defect.*
2. **The plan cards can't be compared.** Each card describes a different dimension. Allotment gives sites and plots ("One site, up to 40 plots"). Garden gives plots and a waiting list ("Up to 150 plots and a waiting list"), with no site count. Network gives sites and a member list ("Up to 10 sites, one member list"), with no plot limit and no waiting list. To choose between $8, $22 and $60 a month, the viewer has to guess whether Garden is one site and whether Network keeps the waiting list and caps plots. They may guess wrong, and a price is the line they'll later hold you to. Fix direction: give every card the same facts in the same order (sites, plots, waiting list, member list), so the differences line up across the row. Only you know the missing values. *Defect.*
3. **Invoices: says 12, shows 3, and none can be opened.** The heading badge says 12, but the table shows three rows and has no "show all" or paging. Invoice numbers like WF-0212 are set as plain text in the same color as the rest of the row, so there's no visible way to view or download an invoice. A treasurer who opens this page to get the September invoice for the books can't get it. The page shouldn't make the user hunt for what they came for, or rely on something they can't see (Nielsen, heuristic 6). Fix direction: make each invoice openable or downloadable with a visible link or action, and add a way to reach all 12. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The checkbox "Send the treasurer a copy of each invoice" doesn't show the treasurer's address, so the viewer can't check where the copies go. Show the address, or a link to where it's set.
- Billing email sits beside Currency, two fields that aren't a related pair. Nothing says whether changing currency changes the dollar prices on the cards above. My view (taste): put the fields in one column, and say what a currency change does to the price.
- No field is marked required or optional. Mark the required ones, for example with an asterisk and a one-line legend (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- The gray borders of the unselected plan cards and the inputs look pale, maybe under 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure them, and darken them if they fall short.

**What's working.**
- Teal is used with discipline. It appears only on the selected plan, the checked box and the primary button, so color means "chosen" or "act" and nothing else. Keep it that way when you add the new plan action.
- The invoice table is set properly: Amount is right-aligned, figures line up, the rules are light, and status is written as a word ("Paid"), not shown only as a color.
- There's one strong left edge at the content column and the sections come in a clear order (plan, billing details, invoices), so the page scans top to bottom without effort.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the plan cards and form controls, and how the three-card row and the two-column field row reflow on a narrow screen.
