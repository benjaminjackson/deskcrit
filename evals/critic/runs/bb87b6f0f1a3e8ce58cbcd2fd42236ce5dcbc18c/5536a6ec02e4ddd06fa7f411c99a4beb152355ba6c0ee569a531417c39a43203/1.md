**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web page or flyer section. It tells local families what happens at the festival and when, and recruits volunteers.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. Every fact a visitor came for (which day, what time, where, how to help) is buried mid-sentence in three look-alike blocks of prose. On top of that, the grid has visible errors: a dropped middle column and unequal gutters.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts are buried in prose, with no headings to scan by** (defect). The three columns are three separate events: the overview, Saturday's lantern walk, and Sunday's pie contest. None has a heading, so the eye has no way in except reading from the top left. The details people act on are mid-sentence: "first weekend of September," "ten each morning," "at dusk," "by one o'clock," "at four." Nobody skimming on a phone will find "when do I need to bring my pie" without reading everything. Nielsen ("How Users Read on the Web") and Krug both say text people skim has to be broken into headed chunks. Right now the page has only two levels, title and body, with nothing in between (Müller-Brockmann on distinct levels). Fix direction: give each column a heading that names its event and day. Then pull the when and where of each event out of the prose onto a line of its own, so the eye finds them before the description.

2. **The asks lead nowhere, and the key details are vague** (defect). "Volunteers are still needed" gives no way to volunteer: no contact, no place, no time. The paragraph does the recruiting and then stops. "First weekend of September" names no dates, so readers have to work them out. "At dusk" is fine as atmosphere, but families with prams will want a clock time. "Entries must be baked at home and brought to the hall by one o'clock" works well because it says who does what, where, and by when. Hold the other asks to that standard. Fix direction: after the volunteer sentence, add the one concrete way to sign up. Put actual dates next to "first weekend of September." These are facts only you have, so I'm not supplying them.

3. **The middle column is dropped half a line, and the gutters are uneven** (defect). Column 2's first line starts about 14 px below the first lines of columns 1 and 3. Its baselines fall between theirs across the whole block, which reads as an error, not a choice (Müller-Brockmann on baselines across columns; Arnheim on near-miss placements). The gutters are uneven too. There's about 20 px of white before each vertical rule and only about 11 px after it, so each column's text pushes up against its left rule. Column 3 also runs wider than the other two. Fix direction: start all three columns on one baseline and use one gutter width with the rule centered in it. Better still, drop the rules (see below).

4. **The page is top-heavy and the bottom is dead** (taste, with Arnheim on balance). All the content sits in the top 58% of the frame. Below it there's a large empty band that frames nothing and reads as "this page didn't load," not as a choice. Fixing finding 1 will use some of that space. Fix direction: make the empty space look deliberate, either by placing the content to own the frame or by giving the lower area to the asks.

5. **Nothing about it feels like a festival** (taste). The page is dark gray serif on white with hairline rules, which suits a council notice. It's for lanterns on the river, paper boats, and a pie that makes the judges laugh, and the copy has real warmth ("guard the cake tent against hungry seagulls"). The design gives that warmth nothing to stand on: no color, no image, no accent (Itten, color mood). Fix direction: bring in one color or one image that belongs to this festival, and use it on the headings or the dates, not as decoration.

**Smaller things.**
- The vertical rules between columns do work that the gutter could do alone, and they make the uneven spacing more obvious. Remove them, or keep them only if the gutters become equal (Lupton).
- "seagulls." sits alone as a one-word last line in column 1. Reflow it so at least two words carry over (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- There's no clear eye path after the title. All three columns are equal in weight, so the reader has to pick one. Headings (finding 1) fix this.
