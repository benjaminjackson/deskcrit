**First read.** The eye lands on the big serif headline, "Know when the water is slack before you leave the car," and moves right to the tide card. It reads at once as a tide and current app for sea swimmers on a particular stretch of coast. It feels calm, local and grown-up. Then the eye looks for the "safe window" the copy promises and can't find it in the card.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing landing page that gets open-water swimmers to add their beach, on the free plan or the paid one.

**Verdict.** This is a strong, restrained page that mostly works. The biggest problem is the hero card. It is the product's proof, and it doesn't show the one thing the page sells: when it's safe to swim next.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero card doesn't show the safe window.** The headline and lead promise that Slackwater "marks the safe window," and the card is where a swimmer checks that promise.
   - The bar chart has no times, no height scale and no unit, and nothing marks now or the window. You can't match the 06:10 row to a bar, so the chart is just a pretty wave.
   - In the table, "Swim" and "Avoid" are set in the same muted gray at the same weight. The safety call looks like a footnote.
   - The 12:25 row is the one a swimmer acts on, and its verdict has been replaced by an amber "Next." So the row that matters most is the only one with no Swim or Avoid answer.

   This is a defect. Charts need labeled times and units (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few). The rest is common sense for a safety product.

   Fix: mark the window on the chart itself and give the bars a time axis. Show "next" as well as the verdict, not in place of it. Then give the verdict column enough weight or contrast that "Avoid" stands out at a glance.

2. **The prices are in dollars on a page that is plainly British.** The page says "made in Penzance," "car park" and "Sea 13 °C," but the plans read "$0" and "$4 a month." A UK swimmer either wonders what they'll actually be charged or reads it as an overseas template. A price is what viewers hold you to, so this is a defect, not a style note.

   Fix: if you bill in pounds, show pounds. If you really bill in US dollars, say so on the plan.

3. **The pricing section has no action.** The page ends on two plans with nothing to choose. A viewer convinced by "$4 a month" has to scroll back to the hero to find "Add your beach." This is a defect: the strongest moment to act has no way to act.

   Fix: give each plan its own ask, or repeat the primary button under the plans. Keep the amber for the one you most want people to pick.

**Smaller things.**
- The second column doesn't line up between sections. The Year-rounder plan starts about 6 px left of "See the safe window," so the two sections use slightly different column spacing. Put both on one grid (Müller-Brockmann).
- Two short texts end on a single word: "reading." in "See the safe window" and "days." in the Dipper description. Rebreak them or adjust the copy (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The page never compares itself to something swimmers already trust, like the tide table or tide app they check now. "Slackwater reads the tide tables and the buoys" comes close. If you have a fact that shows the difference (for example what the tide table alone doesn't tell them), put it near "Add your beach." That fact is yours to supply.

**What's working.**
- **The headline speaks to the swimmer's real moment.** "Before you leave the car" names a time and place from their day, not a category. "Add your beach" and "See a sample morning" are specific asks. Keep that voice.
- **The palette is disciplined.** A deep sea-green ground, cream type, and amber used only three times (logo, primary button, "Next"), so the amber always means "look here." It has its own character and doesn't look like a generic dark template.
- **The hero visual is real product data.** A named beach, a real sea temperature and real times beat any stock photo. That's why fixing finding 1 is worth it: the frame is right, it just needs to show the answer.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the nav, the button and the underlined link.
