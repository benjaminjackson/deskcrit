**Verdict.** It does its job. The page is clear, well ordered and honest, and the locker map is real proof rather than decoration. The two things in its way are both in that map: the colors say the wrong lockers are available, and the caption contradicts the picture. Since the map is what sells the product, both are worth fixing before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **Green marks the lockers that are taken.** In the map, the 15 sage-green cells are the occupied lockers. The free ones (A3, B6, C5) are white with a faint dashed edge, so they almost sink into the beige panel. Most viewers read green as "available" or "OK", and the free lockers carry the least visual weight. Families choosing a locker and staff scanning for gaps both have to undo that reading before the map tells them anything. This is a **convention** break (semantic color, Apple HIG and Material: green means success or go), and here it costs the viewer something. Fix: move the taken lockers to a neutral or muted fill, and make the free ones the cells that stand out, because free lockers are what both audiences are looking for. **Defect.**
2. **"Locker B4, top row" contradicts the map.** B4 is in the middle row; row A is on top. The caption may mean the top tier of a stacked locker unit, but the map shows rows of a corridor, so the viewer either doubts the map or doubts the label. The detail card below the map is the one place the product shows "who has which key", and a demo that contradicts itself undercuts that promise. Fix: make the label match the picture, or name the tier in words the map doesn't use for rows. **Defect.**
3. **Stock sparkle icons on the main button and the panel title.** The ✦ in "Start with one corridor" and next to "Science corridor" explains nothing. It's the stock "AI feature" mark that viewers have seen on many other products. Here it suggests an AI feature the page never mentions, and it's the only generic touch on an otherwise specific page. Fix: remove both, or replace them with an icon that actually means "corridor" or "locker" if you want one. **Template tell** (unslop, "Sparkle icon as decoration").

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks so that "bell" sits alone on the third line, which ends the hero on a weak runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Adjust the measure or the break so the last line carries at least "the first bell".
- The dashed borders on the free lockers look faint against the white fill and beige panel. Measure them against 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11), since that edge is half of what marks a locker as free.
- "Deposits back in July" has no verb and gives a fixed month, while its body says "at the end of the year". A school whose year ends in another month gets two readings of a promise about money. Make the heading and body say the same thing, with the month only if it's true for every customer.

**What's working.**
- The locker map is a specific, real picture of the product: rows, a stair landing ("Stairs to the labs"), a "18 lockers, 3 free" count, and a selected locker with its year group, key number and deposit. It shows the claim instead of decorating it. Keep this kind of picture.
- The copy is concrete and speaks the audience's language: "Start with one corridor" is an exact, small first step, and the three feature lines each end on what the office gains ("a lost key is a quick lookup").
- The hierarchy and grid are clean. There's one dominant headline, one navy primary button with an underlined secondary link, shared outer edges, and the feature row in even thirds. A single navy accent ties the button to the selected locker.
