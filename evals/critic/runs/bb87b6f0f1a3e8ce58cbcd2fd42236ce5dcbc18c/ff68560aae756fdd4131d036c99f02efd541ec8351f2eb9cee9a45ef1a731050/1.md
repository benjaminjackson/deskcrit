**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing," then goes to the teal-outlined Co-op card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads at once as the billing settings page of a small SaaS tool for honey co-ops: calm, orderly, and with nothing in the way. The pause comes when you try to act. The plan choice and the billing form feel like one form, and you have to guess what "Save changes" will do to your bill.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the billing settings screen where a co-op treasurer checks or changes the plan, keeps invoice details up to date, and gets past invoices.

**Verdict.** It's a competent, clean settings screen that mostly does its job. The one thing that matters is that the only money decision on the page has no clear commit and no stated result. Nothing says whether "Save changes" switches the plan, what it charges, or when.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan change has no clear commit and no stated result.** The plan radio cards sit above a divider, and the one "Save changes" button sits below the Billing details fields. A treasurer who clicks Packer can't tell whether the click changed the plan already, whether Save will change it, or whether Save only covers the email and address. The screen also never says what happens after the switch: when it takes effect, whether the current month is prorated, or what the next invoice will be. This is the click that commits the co-op to $84 a month, so the doubt lands on the riskiest action on the page. The treasurer will either hesitate or switch and get a surprise on the bill. **Defect** (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping: a control sits next to the thing it changes; Nielsen heuristic 10, help placed at the step it concerns). Direction: give the plan change its own commit, next to the cards and named for what it does. Say near it when the change applies and what it will charge. Those facts are yours to supply. Leave "Save changes" covering only the billing details.

2. **The plan cards describe each tier along a different dimension, so you can't compare them.** Backyard is "Up to 10 hives, one seller," Co-op is "Up to 25 members, shared stock," and Packer is "Unlimited members and batch labels." Hives, sellers, members, stock, and labels each appear on only one or two cards. A viewer can't tell whether Co-op caps hives or whether Packer keeps shared stock, so the one question an upgrade asks ("what do I get for $48 more?") can't be answered from the cards. **Defect** (a copy problem; the checklist has no line for it). Direction: state the same few limits on every card, in the same order, so the differences line up across the row. You decide which limits those are.

3. **The page leaves out the two things people most often come to billing for.** There's no next charge date or amount anywhere. And the Invoices table shows invoice numbers as plain gray text, with no link or download, so the screen lists invoices but never lets you get one. A treasurer who needs a PDF for the books hits a dead end. **Common sense** (the anticipation principle; its source isn't on the approved list). Direction: make each invoice row open or download its invoice. Put the next charge, meaning the date and amount, near the plan, where it would also answer finding 1.

**Smaller things.**
- The checkbox says "Add the co-op tax number to every invoice," but the number appears nowhere on this screen, so the user has to remember it or go look it up in Co-op details. Show the number in the label, or link to where it's set (Nielsen heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall).
- The input, select, Cancel, and unselected plan card borders look like pale gray, likely under 3:1 against white. Measure them, and darken any that fall short (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is disciplined. There's one page title, plain section heads ("Plan," "Billing details," "Invoices"), small semibold field labels, and regular body text. Every level is clear, and the finer differences come from weight rather than extra sizes. Keep this.
- Color is held back. Teal appears only on the selected plan, the checkbox, and the primary button, so it marks your current state and the main action and nothing else. The neutral table and plain "Paid" status stay quiet because of it.
- The invoice table is set well: amounts are right-aligned with figures that line up, the rules are light, and there's no chrome. The Sep → Oct invoice rows also quietly show your plan history.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether clicking a plan card switches the plan immediately or waits for Save. That's the behavior finding 1 depends on, so check it in the live app.
- Focus indicators on the cards, fields, and buttons.
