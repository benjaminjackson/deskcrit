**First read.** The eye goes first to "$4.82M" in the left card, moves across the other two figures, then drops to the blue bars climbing to the right. It reads as the revenue home screen of a SaaS billing product, for a founder or finance lead. It feels calm, sober and trustworthy. Nothing shouts and nothing is decoration.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen a SaaS company's leadership or finance team opens to check how revenue is doing this year.

**Verdict.** It's a well-made, honest dashboard and mostly does its job. The one thing in the way is that two figures on the screen don't reconcile, which is what a finance viewer will notice first and trust it less for.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Revenue by plan, 2025" doesn't match the chart beside it.** This is a defect. The table's Total is $4.82M, the same figure as the ARR card. But the twelve monthly bars, read by eye, add up to about $4.49M of 2025 revenue. The two panels sit side by side with near-identical titles ("Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025"), so a viewer reasonably expects them to agree. The table can be read two ways:
   - **2025 revenue split by plan.** Then the chart, or the table, is wrong, and the viewer stops trusting every number on the page.
   - **Year-end ARR split by plan.** Then the heading names the wrong measure.

   Either way, someone may quote $4.82M as 2025 revenue in a board deck. That's the costly misreading. The fix: make the heading say what the table measures. If it's ARR by plan at December 2025, say so in the heading. If it really is 2025 revenue, the figures need to match the chart. Only you know which it is. (WCAG 2.4.6: headings describe their content.)

2. **The "Jan to Dec 2025" chip at top right doesn't say whether you can use it.** This is a defect if it's a control. It's a pale gray rounded box with no chevron, calendar icon or border, so it reads as a static tag. If it's the date-range picker, users won't find it, and they may not realize the whole screen can be filtered. If it's a picker, give it a control's cues: a visible edge and a chevron or calendar icon. If it's only a label, drop the box and set it as plain text next to the title (common sense; Norman on signifiers).

**Smaller things.**
- **The lower row is off the card grid.** The chart and the plan table split at roughly x=960/1012. The cards split at 575/593 and 907/926. So the table's left edge lines up with nothing above it. Start the table on the third card's left edge (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- **The two panel headings almost line up but don't.** "Revenue by plan, 2025" sits about 4 px lower than "Monthly revenue, 2025". Put them on one baseline, since a near-miss reads as a mistake (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*).
- **The plan table is the only set of figures with no comparison.** The KPI cards each carry a "vs 2024" line, but Starter, Team and Business don't. Add last year's figure or the change, in the same green "+x% vs 2024" form the cards use (Few).
- **The chart title only names the topic.** "Monthly revenue, 2025" doesn't say what the bars show: steady growth with small dips in April and July. This is taste. A title that states the trend saves the viewer the reading. Write it from your own figures.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards are complete.** Each one gives the measure, the period, the number and a comparison ("+18% vs Dec 2024"). That's exactly the context a bare big number lacks. Keep this pattern and extend it to the plan table.
- **The chart is honest and quiet.** Bars start at zero, there's one color, the gridlines are faint, the units are stated in the subtitle, and a source line names exactly what's counted ("closed invoices only"). Almost all the ink is data.
- **The hierarchy is restrained.** There are about four type sizes, with weight doing the fine work. The only color besides the blue bars is the green on the deltas, and each delta also carries a "+" sign.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date chip opens a picker and what changes when it does. The focus states on the sidebar navigation.
