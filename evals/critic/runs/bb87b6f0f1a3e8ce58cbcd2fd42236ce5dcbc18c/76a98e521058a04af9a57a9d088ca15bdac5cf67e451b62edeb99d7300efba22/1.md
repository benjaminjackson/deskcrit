**What would move it most**
1. **The form labels, caption, form note and footer are far too small to read.** This is a defect. The labels above the two fields ("Your name…"?), the line under the figure, the sentence under "Sign up" and the footer line at the bottom left all look about 3 to 4 px tall, thin and mid-gray. In the full-resolution crop of the form they are still smudges. So a reader sees two empty boxes and can't tell what goes in either one. The note under the button probably says how often you'll get messages and how to stop, which is exactly what people check before handing over their details, and it's lost too. These lines miss any reasonable minimum by a wide margin (Apple HIG gives 11 pt as the smallest text on iOS). At this size they can't reach readable contrast either (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3). The type scale also breaks: about 32 / 22 / 17 px, then a drop straight to about 4 px, with no caption size in between. Fix: add one small size for labels, captions and notes, a step below the body text (around 13 to 14 px on the web). Set it in the regular weight and a gray dark enough for 4.5:1. Re-measure the labels once they're rendered.
2. **The signup's wording doesn't tell the reader what they're agreeing to.** This is a defect in the copy. "Get timetable changes" doesn't say for which route or landing, or by what channel. "Sign up" names a category, not what happens next. Since the labels and note can't be read, nothing on the page says whether this means email, text messages or something else. Fix: give the heading or button the missing pieces, what arrives and where (by email? for this landing?). Make the note under the button readable so it answers how often messages come. Only you have those facts; I'm not supplying them.
3. **The figure is a placeholder-style diagram, not a picture of the rebuild.** This counts as both a template tell and a defect. The 560 px panel under the first paragraph holds a gray block, a paler block and a blue pill on a blue band. It's the biggest thing on the page after the headline. It shows none of what the text says is new: wider ramps, the covered waiting area, benches facing the water, the kiosk. Readers have seen this kind of generic box many times, so it tells them nothing and reads as an unloaded image. The blue pill also looks like a button, because it's the same blue and the same rounded shape as "Sign up." Fix: show the actual rebuilt stage, a photo or a drawing where you can see the ramp and the shelter. Otherwise cut the panel and let the second paragraph follow the first.
