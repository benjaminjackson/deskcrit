**First read.** The eye goes first to the row of four large figures across the top (386, $21,480, 88%, 57), then jumps to the one saturated blue bar, Saturday's, at the right. Next it follows the two lines in the left chart, then drops to the table. It reads right away as a booking dashboard for a small pottery studio. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, close to a finished product.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the studio owner or manager checks this monthly view to see how bookings, revenue and class demand compare with last month, and which days and classes are busiest.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. It's a clean, honest dashboard, and its numbers always come with a comparison. The biggest problem is that the two charts use the same two colors to mean different things, so a reader who has just learned "blue = November, gray = October" misreads the weekday chart. Second, the weekday chart shows only one value.

**What would move it most**
1. **Blue and gray mean one thing in the left chart and another in the right.** In "Daily bookings" the legend sets up blue = November and gray = October. A few inches to the right, in "Seats booked by weekday", gray means "the other days" and blue means "Saturday, the busiest day". The viewer has just learned a key, and it's natural to read the gray bars as October, or to wonder why only Saturday has November data. That costs a second look and can cost a wrong reading. *Defect (common sense; the Material and Apple color-role guidance is that one role keeps one color).* Direction: keep the November/October key only in the chart that compares months. Mark the busiest weekday some other way, for example a darker gray, or a label on the bar alone, and keep the line chart's blue out of it.
2. **The weekday chart shows one number and hides the other six.** It has no value axis and no labels except "92" on Saturday. The owner can see Saturday is busiest but can't tell whether Sunday is close behind or far off, and that's the question that decides whether to add a Sunday class. The line chart has the opposite gap: its y-axis (0–30) has no unit, and "Daily bookings" doesn't say whether it counts seats or orders. The cards above count *seats*, so the reader can't tell whether the two figures are the same measure. By eye, the line's daily values also look like they add up to more than the 386 seats in the first card. Check that the two agree, or name the unit so it's clear they measure different things. *Convention (Tufte, Few: label axes and units so the reader never has to guess what a number measures).* Direction: put each bar's value on it, as Saturday's already is. Name the unit in the line chart's title or axis.

**Smaller things.**
- Line chart x-axis ticks are 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov, with gaps of 9, 9 and 11 days. Use even intervals, or mark weeks (Tufte: even ticks).
- The line chart's legend sits at the top right, away from the lines. Label "November" and "October" at the right-hand end of each line and drop the legend (Tufte, Wilke: label directly).
- Neither chart title states its finding ("Seats booked by weekday" names the topic, not that Saturday leads). That's taste for a dashboard people check every month, but a short takeaway would save the owner the reading.
- The split between the two chart cards (around x 891/908) lines up with none of the dividers in the stat row (511/756/1002). The middle band looks like it was placed by eye instead of on the same columns (Müller-Brockmann). Make the chart split share a column line with the row above.

**What's working.**
- Every headline number has a comparison under it ("352 in October", "$19,940 in October"). That's exactly the context a monthly view needs. Keep it. Adding the direction or size of the change (+34) would only make it faster.
- Restraint: one neutral range plus one blue, no gradients, no badges, no sparkline on every card. It looks made for this studio's data, not taken from a template.
- The "Fullest classes" table is properly set: numbers right-aligned in tabular figures, light row rules, a plain header. Exact values belong in a table, and that's where they are.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The date range "1 to 30 November 2026" at the top right looks like plain text. If it's the control for changing the period, it doesn't look clickable. Check that in the live page.
- Hover and focus states on the side navigation and the table rows.
