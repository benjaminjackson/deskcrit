**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the sections are easy to scan and the type is clean. The one thing holding it back is that the paragraphs run too wide for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body lines are too long.** The full-width lines run from x≈160 to about x≈945. "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" is about 90 characters before it wraps. With so little text this is a mild cost, but each return trip to the left edge is a bit harder to land, and that will grow if the sections get longer, as how-to pages usually do. Bringhurst gives 45 to 75 characters as the comfortable range for one column, with 66 as the ideal (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). **Convention**, with a small reading cost. Fix: narrow the text column to about 65 characters, roughly 600 px at this size. The right side of the page already has plenty of empty space, so nothing else needs to move.

**Smaller things.**
- "last year's" (Preparing the soil paragraph) uses a straight apostrophe. Use a curly one. This is common sense, not a sourced rule, and at body size few readers will notice.
- Copy: "a spot that gets six hours of sun" has no time frame. A beginner might wonder whether that means in total or per day. Adding "a day" closes the gap. This is an editorial edit, not new content.

**What's working.**
