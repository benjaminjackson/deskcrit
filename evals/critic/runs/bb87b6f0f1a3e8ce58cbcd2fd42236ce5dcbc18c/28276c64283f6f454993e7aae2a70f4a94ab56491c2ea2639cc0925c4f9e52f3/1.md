**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web page telling ferry passengers when and how to use the ferry so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and easy to read, but the page doesn't give passengers what they came for. It talks about the timetable without showing one. Every time on the page is approximate ("early morning", "late evening", "before dusk"), and the last line sends readers to a printed sheet at the landing. That gap, not anything visual, is what stops the page working.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page has no times anyone can plan around.** Paragraphs 1 and 3 give the frequency ("every forty minutes", "one boat each hour") but no first or last departure. Winter has no start or end, "before dusk" changes through the season, and "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" tells someone reading online to travel to the place they're trying to plan a trip to. Departure times are what a passenger acts on and holds the operator to, so leaving them vague is a defect: someone guessing "late evening" can miss the last boat. To fix it, give the actual first and last departures for summer and winter, the dates the winter timetable starts and ends, and the timetable itself or a link to it on this page. Only you have those facts. *Defect.*
2. **The first paragraph hides its useful fact in a sentence that can be read two ways.** "Each crossing takes about twelve minutes in calm weather, and a little longer when the tide turns against the boat … around the sandbank by the old harbour wall on most mornings at low tide" runs to about 50 words. A stranger can't tell whether the delay happens "on most mornings", "at low tide", or only when both are true, and "a little longer" has no number. Someone with a connection to make needs to know which crossings run late and by roughly how much. To fix it, split the sentence after "calm weather". Then state the condition once, plainly, and give the extra time in minutes if you know it. *Defect.*
3. **The heading names a category, not what's on the page.** "Notes for ferry passengers" could head any ferry page anywhere. It doesn't name this ferry or its route (north landing to market quay), which is the first thing a searcher needs to know they're in the right place. Moving the route or the ferry's name into the heading would fix it without adding anything new. *Taste.*
