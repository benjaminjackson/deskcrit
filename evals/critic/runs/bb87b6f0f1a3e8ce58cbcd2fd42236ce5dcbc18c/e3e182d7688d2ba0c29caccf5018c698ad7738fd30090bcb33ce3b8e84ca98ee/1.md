**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Booking links that fill your calendar", then drops to the blue "Create your link" button. Next it notices how empty the right half of the hero is. Then it moves to the row of four bordered cards, and last to a gray line of names at the bottom that looks like footer links. It reads as a clean, competent scheduling-tool landing page that is calm and tidy. Apart from the name, it could belong to almost any scheduling product.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the desktop home page of a booking-link SaaS, aimed at small service businesses (clinics, tutors, coaches), meant to get them to create a link or try the live example.

**Verdict.** The page works at a basic level: the hierarchy is clear and there is one obvious action. But it never shows the product. The right half of the hero is blank, so the visitor has to picture a booking page from words alone, and nothing sets Slotwise apart from the tools they already know.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero shows no product, and the space where it would go is empty.** The top right, roughly x 680–1216 and y 100–400, is blank white. The headline and buttons sit in the left half, so the hero tips left and the empty area looks like something failed to load, not a chosen rest area. Visitors come here to see what a booking link looks like to their own clients, and they get nothing to look at. The page already has "See a live example", which is a strong ask because it lets visitors judge for themselves, but the hero hides that proof behind a click. *Defect* (balance, Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"; the empty hero visual is a template tell from Damir Sokolovsky, unslop). **Fix:** fill that space with the product itself, such as the booking page a guest sees or the live example placed inline, so the empty area becomes the counterweight and the proof. If it really is a picture that didn't load, check it live, because the finding is the same until it shows.

2. **The headline and subhead could sit on any scheduling product's page.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "keep every meeting in the right place" describe the whole category, not this product. A visitor already using another booking tool learns nothing that would make them switch. "Fill your calendar" can also be read two ways: more bookings (good) or no free time left (not what they want). The subhead ends on "the right place" without saying what that place is. It also never says what it means for the visitor, for example that they stop trading emails to set a time. *Defect* (copy). **Fix:** say the one thing Slotwise does that the visitor's current tool doesn't. The customer list suggests appointment-based businesses, and team routing and intake forms point that way too. Only you can supply that fact. Then end the subhead on what the visitor no longer has to do.

3. **The card row isn't consistent, and the first card shows it most.** In "Shared calendars" the title wraps to two lines, so its body text and "Learn more" sit about 30 px lower than in the other three cards. That breaks the row the eye expects to read straight across (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*, "Construction of the grid"). Its "New" badge is filled blue, the only badge with color, and it is pushed to the card's right edge, away from the end of the title. Its calendar icon is an outline, while the clock, envelope, and layout icons are filled. Every card has a badge. "Auto" and "Email" aren't statuses, so the badges stop meaning anything, and the single real "New" gets lost among them. *Convention + template tell* (unslop: badges everywhere; icons in mixed styles). **Fix:** keep the titles to one line, or reserve two title lines in every card. Use one icon style. Keep at most one quiet badge, the one that actually signals something.

**Smaller things.**
- The customer row (y ~770) has no label and is set like footer links, so it doesn't read as proof. Give it a heading that says these are customers, or show it in a way that reads as endorsement.
- The four "Learn more" links are identical. Off screen they all say the same thing (WCAG 2.4.4). Name what each one leads to, or tie it to its card title.
- "Start free" in the nav and "Create your link" in the hero look like the same action under two labels and two button styles of equal weight. Use one label for one action.
- The headline breaks so that "calendar" stands alone on line two. A slightly wider measure or a different break would hold it as one phrase.

**What's working.**
- The type plan is disciplined. There are about four clear sizes: headline, subhead, card title, and body/nav. The headline gets its contrast from weight and size together, and nothing is set in near-duplicate sizes. Keep this scale.
- One filled primary button, with "See a live example" as an underlined text link, is the right ranking of the two asks. The live-example ask is the best idea on the page because it lets visitors judge the product for themselves.
- The card body copy is concrete and lands on the reader ("so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows"). The hero copy should speak the same way.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the buttons and links, and how the four-card row reflows at narrower widths.

What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 9 yes, 4 no (exact alignment; lines align across columns; empty space gathered; balance)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 10 yes, 2 no (one rendering style; consistent line quality)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 30 yes, 2 no (one primary action; link purpose), 1 unsure (focus indicator)
