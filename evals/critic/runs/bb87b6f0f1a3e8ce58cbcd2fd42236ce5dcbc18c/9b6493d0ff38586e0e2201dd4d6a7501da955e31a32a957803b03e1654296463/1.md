**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: it's clear, readable and well spaced. But the sidebar is set louder than the main content it supports, so the hierarchy is upside down below the headline. The schedule copy also gives no times a passenger can act on.

**What would move it most**

1. **The sidebar outranks the main column.** The card's "Good to know" heading looks larger than "When the boats run" and "Bicycles and luggage." Its body text also looks a step larger than the main paragraphs (about 20 px against about 17–18 px). So after the headline, the eye goes to the aside: a note that printed timetables sit in a shelter. Then it has to come back to the actual guide. I count about seven text sizes on one short page: headline, card heading, section headings, subtitle, card body, main body, nav/button. Several are only a few pixels apart, and they don't form a scale. Each level should be obvious at a glance (Müller-Brockmann; Bringhurst §3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). **Defect.** The fix: set the card heading at or below the section-heading size and the card body at or below the main body. Then pull the sizes onto three or four levels with clear steps between them.

2. **The times that matter are vague.** "Between early morning and late evening" and "before dusk" are what a passenger plans around, and they can be read many ways. One reader arrives at 6:30, another at 8:00, and one of them misses the boat or waits an hour. "Thins to one boat each hour" also doesn't say when winter starts. **Defect** (copy, on the lines a viewer acts on). The fix: put in the actual first and last departure times and the dates of the winter timetable. Only you have those facts, or the line can say plainly that the timetable has them.
