**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation is buried in the history.** The paragraph runs three topics together in one block: what the trust looks after and how it began (lines 1–4), its size (lines 4–6), and the work days (lines 6–11). The only line that asks anything of the reader, "anyone is welcome to join for the morning," starts mid-line on line 7 and has the same weight as the 1987 founding date. Someone skimming, which is how most people read a web page (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web"), takes in the first line or two and never learns they could come along. *Defect.* Fix: split the text at its seams, so the trust and its history are one paragraph and the work days are another. Then give the work days enough weight and space to be found by someone who skips the history.

2. **"Every second Saturday" can be read two ways.** It could mean every other Saturday, or the second Saturday of each month. A reader who picks the wrong one turns up on the wrong day, or not at all. This is a date people will act on, so it's a *defect*, not a style note. Fix: say which one it is. The same sentence also stops before the details someone would need to come: when the morning starts, where people meet, and whether they should tell anyone first.
