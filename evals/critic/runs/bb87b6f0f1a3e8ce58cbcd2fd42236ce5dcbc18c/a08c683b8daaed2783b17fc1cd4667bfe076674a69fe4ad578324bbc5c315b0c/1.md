**First read.** The eye goes to the serif headline first, then across the four illustrated cards, and only then to the prices. The dark "Book a visit" button in the top-right corner registers last, if at all. This reads as a local home-services company selling to homeowners. It feels calm, tidy and friendly, and it is pleasant to look at, but it ends without telling you what to do.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page of a home-services business. Its job is to get a homeowner to pick a service and book it.

**Verdict.** It looks good but does only half its job. The subhead promises "pick a service, choose a day", yet nothing on the page lets you pick or book anything, and every price is missing the unit it is charged in.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards don't lead anywhere.** The four service cards are where the viewer's decision happens. Each ends on a price and then nothing: no button, no link, no sign that the card itself can be clicked. The only way to act is the "Book a visit" button in the header, which sits far from the cards and doesn't know which service you just chose. The subhead sets up a two-step flow ("Pick a service, choose a day") that the page never delivers, so a viewer who has decided on window cleaning has to guess what happens next. *Defect (common sense, no approved source).* Fix: give each card its own action for that service, carrying the choice into booking. Or make the whole card visibly clickable. Either way the path from the card to "choose a day" should be obvious. The header button can stay as the general entry point.
2. **The prices have no unit.** "From $89", "From $189", "From $24" and "From $65" don't say what they are per. Is it per visit, per room, per window, per load, per bag, per week? Laundry at $24 with a Monday-to-Wednesday cycle could be per load or per weekly pickup. The two readings differ a lot in what the customer pays, and the price is the line they will hold the company to. *Defect.* Fix: add the unit to each price ("From $89 per …"). Only the business knows what it is.
3. **The headline could sit on anyone's page.** "Help around the house, on your schedule" names the category, not the company's reason to exist. Every competitor could say it, so it gives the viewer no reason to choose Fernway. "A vetted crew" is the one claim that sets the company apart, but it's left unsupported: vetted by whom, and how? *Taste.* Fix: lead with what actually makes this service different. If vetting is that thing, say what the vetting is, using a fact only the business has. Keep the subhead's clear three-step promise.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "on your / schedule", which splits the phrase and leaves one word on the second line. Break it after "house," or widen the text block so it runs "Help around the house, / on your schedule" (Bringhurst, on line breaks).
- Sparkle shapes in the window-cleaning and deep-cleaning illustrations are a stock decoration (unslop, "Sparkle icon as decoration"). They also make two of the four cards busier than the others. Take them out, or replace them with something that belongs to this business.
- Inside each card, the tinted illustration panel starts about 6 px left of the title and text below it (x≈70 vs. x≈76). That reads as a near-miss rather than a choice (Arnheim). Align them, or make the inset clearly different.

**What's working.**
- The card copy is concrete. "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday" tells the viewer exactly what happens and when, and the other three are nearly as specific. Keep that voice and bring the headline up to it.
- The four illustrations share one flat style, one level of detail and one palette. They pick up the teal of the prices and the slate of the button, so the page reads as one piece.
- The type is clean and simple: a warm serif for the brand and the headline, a plain sans for everything else, and only a few sizes with clear steps between them. The headline dominates without shouting.
