**First read.** The eye lands on the large serif quote straight away and reads it to the end, since nothing else competes. Then it drops to the bold name, the gray caption, and only last to the small "Harbour Notes, issue 14" at the top. It reads as a pull-quote or cover card from a small local newsletter. It feels calm, slow and early-morning, which is what the quote is about.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a feature or share card for one issue of a newsletter, where the quote is the hook and the line under it sets the scene.

**Verdict.** It does its job, and well. There is one focal point, a clear reading order, and type that suits the subject. Nothing on it gets in the way of the reader. What's left is small type detail.

**What would move it most**

Nothing would noticeably change how well this piece works. The quote dominates, it is set well, and the supporting lines fall into place under it. Fixing the points below is polish.

**Smaller things.**
- **Small sizes too close together (convention).** The kicker (~15 px semibold gray), the caption (~16 px regular gray) and the attribution (~18 px semibold near-black) look like three sizes only a pixel or two apart. Weight and color keep them apart, but the 15/16 pair looks like a slip rather than a choice (Bringhurst, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Set the kicker and the caption at one size and let weight and color do the separating, as they mostly do already.
- **Word spacing in the quote looks loose (convention).** At this display size, the word spaces in "is quietest" and "and that is when" look wide, so each line reads a little gappy. Large type wants tighter word spacing than text (Bringhurst 2.1.1; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 34). Tighten the word spacing slightly. Leave the letterspacing alone, because it looks right.

**What's working.**
- **The hung opening quotation mark.** The mark sits outside the 100 px left edge, so the quote, kicker, attribution and caption all share one clean left edge. Keep it.
- **The type pairing and scale jump.** A warm text serif at about five times the size of a plain sans for the supporting lines gives one unmistakable level for the voice and one for the facts. The "fi" ligature in "first" shows the large type was set with care.
- **Restraint.** The piece uses one neutral palette with no image, no rule and no ornament, and the empty space is gathered at the right and bottom. The quiet fits a quote about quiet.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** None.
