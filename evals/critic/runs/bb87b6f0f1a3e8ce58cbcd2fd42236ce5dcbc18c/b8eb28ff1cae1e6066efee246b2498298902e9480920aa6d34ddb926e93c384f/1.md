**First read.** The eye goes to the bold serif title, "Winter timetable at the harbour", then drops to two even columns of calm text. It looks like a quiet, well-set information page for ferry passengers. Then it lets you down: I came for a timetable and found paragraphs. I scan for a departure time and there isn't one. The page then trails off into two thin gray rules with nothing under them.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page that tells winter ferry passengers when boats leave and how to travel. It could be a web page or a printed notice.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is competent, but the page doesn't do the job its title promises. It's called a timetable and gives no times, and the one subhead names content that isn't there. Fix what the page says and where each fact sits before anything visual.

**What would move it most**
1. **The title promises a timetable and the page has none. This is a defect.** The only timing facts are buried in body text: "every ninety minutes", the office "opens at seven", and "before the first boat is due". A passenger who wants the next boat can't find a first sailing, a last sailing, or a departure time. They have to read three paragraphs and still work it out themselves, if they can at all. When does the first 90-minute boat leave? The page never says. The heading doesn't describe its content (WCAG 2.4.6). Fix: either the times appear as the most prominent thing under the title (a table is the usual form for lookup of exact values), or the title stops promising them. The maker has to supply the actual departure times.
2. **"Fares and cards" labels a section with no fares in it. This is a defect.** Under it are crossing times, wind, bicycles, prams, wheelchairs, bags and dogs. Meanwhile the one fact about cards ("Passengers who hold a season card…") sits in the left column. A reader looking for prices goes to the right heading, finds none, and assumes the page has no fare information. Fix: sort the facts first, then name the groups. Either retitle the section to what it holds, or move the season-card line under it and add the fares if they exist. The left column also needs a heading of its own. Right now it is the only unlabeled column in a two-column layout, so it reads as if its heading went missing (Gerstner's point about repeated elements following one program).
3. **The two gray rules at the bottom do no job. This is taste plus common sense.** They sit roughly 90 px and 130 px below the uneven ends of the two columns. They divide the text from empty space, so they suggest a footer or a next section that never comes. It looks like a page caught half-built. Fix: remove them, or put under them whatever they are meant to introduce.

**Smaller things.**
- The right column's body lines fall about half a line off the left column's lines, so the rows don't line up across the gutter (Müller-Brockmann). Put the heading and its space on the body's line interval.
- In the right column, "thirty-five." and "bags." each sit alone on a last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Adjust the measure or the wording to pull them back.

**What's working.**
- The type is a sound, quiet setting. One serif family is used at three clearly separate levels: title, bold subhead, and body. The bold subhead differs from body text in weight as well as size. Keep this scale when you add the times.
- The body measure looks like about 60 characters per line, with generous leading and space between paragraphs (no indents). It reads comfortably.
- The grid is plain: equal 120 px margins, two equal columns, and a clear gutter. It will easily hold a timetable block.
