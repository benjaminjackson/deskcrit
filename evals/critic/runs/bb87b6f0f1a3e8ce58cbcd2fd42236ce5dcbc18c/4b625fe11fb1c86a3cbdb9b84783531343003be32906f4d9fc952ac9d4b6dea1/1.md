**First read.** My eye goes to "Low-light houseplants" first, then moves across four matching cards, from the pictures to the teal "Add to cart" buttons. It reads as one category page of a small online plant shop. It's calm, tidy and trustworthy, but nothing on it is memorable. The four pictures are so alike that the row reads as one icon repeated four times, not four different plants.

**What it's for.** This is my assumption: a shop category page meant to help someone with a dim spot choose one of four plants and add it to the cart.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and easy to use, but the page doesn't help anyone choose. The pictures don't show the plants, and the cards say nothing about the low-light promise the page is built on, so the buyer ends up choosing on price.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show the plants being sold.** All four images are the same terracotta pot holding two or three green leaf shapes. They don't look like the species named under them. The Pothos card says "trails to 6 ft", but its picture shows two upright leaves. Snake plant, ZZ plant and cast iron plant look very different in real life, and those differences are what a buyer judges. As drawn, the shopper can't tell the products apart by sight and has to go elsewhere to find out what each one looks like. If these are placeholders, the page isn't finished. If they're final, they hold back exactly what a plant shop sells. Fix: show each actual plant, photo or illustration, drawn true enough to its shape and habit that someone who knows it would recognize it. Defect (common sense: no catalog entry covers it).
2. **The cards drop the page's own promise.** The subtitle makes a sharp, specific claim: "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window". But each card lists only pot size and mature height. There's nothing to tell the four apart on the question the visitor came with: how little light each one tolerates, or what each one needs. So the four look interchangeable, and the only real difference left is price. Fix: give each card one line of the fact that sets that plant apart in a dim spot. The maker has to supply those facts; I'm not suggesting any. Defect (common sense: putting in front of people the information they came for).

**Smaller things.**
- Product name (~18 px semibold) and price (~20 px bold) look almost the same size and weight, so neither clearly leads inside the card. Make one clearly dominant, or set them at the same size and tell them apart by weight or color (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1). On the page overall I count about five text sizes, with two near-collisions: name and price, and specs (~14) with subtitle (~15).
- Nothing in the top navigation marks "Houseplants" as the current section. Mark it with one clear cue: a bolder weight, an underline, or a background (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- "6 in pot" can first be read as the word "in". A buyer holds the shop to pot size, so write the unit out or attach it ("6-inch pot").

**What's working.**
- The subtitle's "more than six feet from a window" turns "low light" into something a person can measure in their own home. Keep that level of concreteness and carry it into the cards.
- A strict four-column grid with equal cards and identical buttons is right for comparing four similar products. Spec lines and prices sit on shared baselines, so you can scan across the row.
- The palette is restrained: one teal for actions, green and terracotta in the pictures, everything else neutral. Color goes to the buy action and the products, and nowhere else.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover, pressed and focus states on "Add to cart", and whether pressing it updates "Cart (0)" visibly.
- How the four-up grid reflows on a phone.
