The dashboard does its job, but the weekday bar chart only gives a value for Saturday. Below is the full critique.

**First read.** The eye goes first to the one strong blue block, the Saturday bar. Then it jumps back to the four big numbers across the top, then to the line chart. It reads as a calm, tidy month-end overview for a small ferry operator. The look is quiet and capable, and a little generic.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a monthly overview for the harbor office's operations and commercial staff, showing at a glance how November went against October and where demand is.

**Verdict.** It mostly does the job: it's easy to tell what this is, the numbers are easy to find, and the hierarchy is sound. Three things get in the way. The weekday chart gives a value for Saturday only. The passenger chart's title promises a level of detail its line doesn't have. And the glow around every card makes the page look like a template.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday bar chart has no scale, so six of its seven bars can't be read** (top-right card). Saturday says 19. Friday and Sunday are plainly high, but you can't tell by how much, or how Monday compares to Tuesday. Someone planning extra sailings needs exactly those numbers, and here they can only guess from bar heights. Every chart should let the reader see what each mark measures, through an axis or direct labels (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few). Fix: put the value above every bar in the same small label Saturday uses. That's seven numbers and needs no axis. Keep the blue highlight on Saturday. *Defect.*
2. **"Daily passengers" doesn't plot daily points** (line chart, middle left). Each line has about 11 points, roughly one every three days across 1 to 30 November, and the labels sit at 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov. A reader will take each point as one day. That hides the weekday swing the next card shows, since Saturdays sell out, and it can make a spike or dip look like a single day when it isn't. Fix: plot all 30 days, or rename the chart to say what each point is (for example, a 3-day average). A heading should name what its section actually holds (WCAG 2.4.6). The October line also needs to be lined up by day of month or by weekday, and the chart should say which. *Defect.*
3. **A blue-gray glow surrounds every card and the active "Overview" menu item.** The haze is clearly visible around the KPI row, both chart cards and the table. It's a stock move from interface templates (unslop, "Colored glows around elements"). Viewers have seen it on many other dashboards, so this one looks like anyone's. On a warm gray background it also makes the edges look soft and slightly dirty. Fix: get depth from the step between the white cards and the gray page, plus a hairline border or a tight neutral shadow. Drop the colored halo, including on the menu item. *Template tell.*
4. **The KPI comparisons make the reader do the math** (top row). "8,870 in October" next to 9,412 is a fact, not a conclusion. To learn whether November was better, and by how much, the viewer has to subtract in their head four times. That's the whole point of this row, so it should be stated. Fix: say the change directly in the sub-line, as a percent or absolute difference with a direction, and keep the October figure if you want it. One related check: October has 31 days and November 30, so if any figure is a total, say whether the comparison allows for that. *Taste, about the copy.*

**Smaller things.**
- The line chart uses a legend at top right for its two series. Put "November" and "October" at the right-hand ends of the lines instead (Tufte; Wilke).
- Both chart titles name a topic, not a finding. Saying what to notice, e.g. that weekends sell out, would make a glance enough. This is taste: no approved source states it.
- The chart row splits at about x=900, which doesn't line up with any of the KPI dividers above it, so the two rows look as if they were placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Put the split on one of the KPI column lines (757 or 1002), or make it deliberately different.

**What's working.**
- Color is held back: everything is gray except one blue. The Saturday highlight and the November line both get that blue, so the eye goes to "this month" and "the peak" without being told. Keep it to one accent.
- The type scale is clean, with about four sizes that each have a clear job: page title and KPI figures, card titles, labels and table text, and small sub-lines and axis labels. The numbers in the table are right-aligned under right-aligned headers.
- The line chart is honest: its axis starts at zero, gridlines are light, October is gray as context, and the same chart compares October with November.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "1 to 30 November 2026" can be changed. If it's static text, staff can't switch to another period from the overview, and that's the obvious shortcut on a page like this.
- Whether chart values appear on hover. Even if they do, the bar values in point 1 should still be visible without hovering.
