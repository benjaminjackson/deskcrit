**First read.** My eye goes to the purple "Sign in" button first. The red border around the Email field pulls it straight back up. It reads right away as the sign-in screen for a product called Quillpost: calm, tidy, and familiar. Then I'm stuck. The email field is clearly marked wrong, but nothing tells me why, so I look around the card for a message that isn't there.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the sign-in screen of a web app, shown here after a failed submit with a bad email address ("dana.ortiz@exmaple", which has a typo and no domain ending), for returning users who want to get in quickly.

**Verdict.** The calm, well-built layout does its job until something goes wrong, and this screen shows something going wrong. The email field is marked as an error only by a red border, with no words. The user can't tell what is wrong or how to fix it, and a user who can't see red gets no signal at all.

**What would move it most**
1. **The error has no words (defect).** The Email field has a red border and nothing else: no message under it, no icon, no line at the top of the card. Users can see something is wrong but have to guess what. Is the account unknown, the address badly formed, or the password wrong? A user who can't tell red from gray, or who uses a screen reader, gets no error at all. They will likely hit "Sign in" again and fail the same way. This breaks WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.1 (Error Identification) and SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color). It also misses NN/g's error-message guidelines: the error should sit next to its cause, in plain words, with a fix. **Fix:** add a short written message right under the Email field, between it and the "Password" label, that says what is wrong. Keep the red border as the second cue.
2. **The error doesn't say how to fix it (defect).** This builds on the first finding. Once a message exists, it has to do more than say "Invalid email." The input lacks a domain ending and has a likely typo. A message that names the expected shape of an address, or points to the part that's missing, lets the user fix it in one pass (WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.3, Error Suggestion). The exact wording is the maker's call. What matters is that it tells the user what to change.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link.** At the bottom of the card it's set in the same gray as "New here?", with no underline. It reads as one plain sentence, while "Forgot your password?" above it is purple. Users who need an account may not see where to click. Fix: give both secondary links the same link style, and add an underline or another cue beyond color (NN/g, Moran; WCAG technique G183). This is the same fault as the next item: two links on one card are styled two ways.
- **The links don't match.** "Forgot your password?" is purple and "Create an account" is gray, so links don't follow one rule on this card (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Pick one link style.
- **The Password field border looks faint.** Its pale gray border on white looks below the 3:1 that WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 asks for on input edges. Measure it, and darken it if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- **The order of the card is right.** Logo, then a plain "Sign in" heading, then labeled fields, then one strong filled button, then the quieter secondary links. There's exactly one primary action, and nothing competes with it.
- **The labels sit above the fields** in bold, each clearly closer to its own field than to the next one. Keep that spacing. Put the new error message inside the email group so it doesn't push the group apart.
- **The palette is restrained.** A cool gray background, a white card, and one purple used for the brand and actions, which leaves red free to mean "error" alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- I can't tell whether the red border is an error state, a focus ring, or both. If it's also the focus style, the error and focus states need to look different.
- Whether the message is announced to screen readers when it appears. Check in a live browser.
