**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tilted blue SALE sticker in the top-right corner, where red letters shimmer against the blue. It then drifts back to the headline, "Linens and cookware for the weekend", and down to four product cards whose pictures are blank tinted boxes. It reads as a home goods store's homepage during a weekend sale. The page itself is calm and tidy, but the sticker looks pasted in from a different, louder site.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the homepage of a home goods store, meant to get shoppers browsing and buying during a weekend sale.

**Verdict.** This doesn't work yet. The page has no product pictures, no sale prices and no action to take, so it shows a sale without letting anyone shop it. The one element built to sell the sale, the sticker, is the loudest thing on the page and says the least.

**What would move it most**
1. **The product cards show nothing to buy.** All four images in "Popular this week" are pale rounded rectangles on tints. On a store page the picture is what sells: a shopper can't tell the stoneware from the duvet without reading, and nothing pulls them in. If this is a capture before the images loaded, it's fine. If not, it's the biggest gap on the page. Fix: put real product photos in, all shot and cropped the same way. *Defect (common sense).*
2. **The sale isn't connected to the products.** The subhead promises "Up to 40% off through Sunday", but every card shows a single price ($34, $89, $52, $68) with no original price and no discount. A shopper can't tell which items are on sale or what they save, so the offer has to be taken on trust. "Up to" also leaves them guessing whether anything here is actually 40% off. Fix: on discounted items, show the sale price next to the price it replaces. Seeing the old and new price side by side lets shoppers judge the deal for themselves. If these four aren't on sale, the section under a sale hero should show items that are. *Defect.*
3. **The SALE sticker takes the focus and adds nothing.** It's the first thing seen, ahead of the larger headline, because it's the only saturated color on the page. But it only repeats "sale", which the subhead says better ("40% off through Sunday"). It also pulls the eye to the far-right corner, where there's nothing to click, and away from the products. Its red-on-blue lettering has two problems. The colors are vivid and close in lightness, so the edge vibrates (Albers, *Interaction of Color*). The red letters also look far below the 3:1 contrast that large text needs (WCAG 1.4.3). The sticker brings in a second heavy, wide typeface and the page's only rotation, so it breaks the muted linen mood the rest of the page sets up. Fix: remove it and let the headline and the 40% lead. If a sale marker has to stay, draw it from the page's own type and palette, and make one of the two colors clearly darker than the other. *Defect (vibration and contrast); the mood clash is taste.*
4. **There's nothing to do next.** The hero has a headline and a subhead but no button. Nothing tells a shopper where the sale lives (the "Sale" nav link is small and at the far right). Fix: give the hero one clear primary action that names what it opens, such as the sale collection, and make it the strongest thing in the hero (Material Design and Apple HIG on primary actions). *Defect.*
5. **The header is missing what every store header has.** It has no cart, no search and no account. Shoppers expect a cart at top right and a search box (Nielsen, Jakob's law). Without them the page reads as a brochure, not a shop. Fix: add the standard store controls in their usual places. *Convention, and breaking it costs something here.*

**Smaller things.**
- The hero headline and subhead start about 8 px to the right of the logo, the "Popular this week" heading and the card column. That's a near-miss that looks like a mistake (Arnheim). Align them to the same left edge.
- Nothing in the nav (Kitchen, Bedroom, Garden, Sale) marks the current page. If this is the homepage that's acceptable, but on category pages mark the current item with one cue such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is disciplined. There are roughly four levels (headline, section heading, logo and product name, body and price), and the steps between them are clear. Bold product names over regular prices group cleanly. Keep this.
- The spacing groups things correctly. Each card holds one product, the gutters are even, and "Popular this week" sits closer to its cards than to the hero.
- The muted beige hero band and soft card tints set a calm domestic mood that suits linens and cookware. That's the voice to build on, and why the sticker is wrong for the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank product boxes are placeholders or images that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the cards and nav links, and whether the whole card can be clicked.
