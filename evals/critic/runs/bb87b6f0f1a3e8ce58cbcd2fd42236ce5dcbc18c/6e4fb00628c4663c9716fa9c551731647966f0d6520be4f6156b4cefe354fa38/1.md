**First read.** My eye lands on the rainbow bar chart on the right. It is the only strong color on a gray and white page, so it beats the page title and the four headline numbers. Next come the KPI figures across the top, then the blue line chart, then the extras table. This reads as a monthly operations dashboard for a farm-box subscription business. It feels calm, competent and neutral, except for that one loud panel.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an owner or ops dashboard for checking how November went against October at a glance: volume, revenue, which days are busy and which extras sell.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The top half is clear and well built. The one real failure is the "Deliveries by weekday" chart: it takes the most attention on the page and is the hardest to read. Its seven colors mean nothing, it has no scale, and its empty Sunday is ambiguous.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday chart shouts and says little** (defect, plus a template tell). In the right panel, each weekday gets its own saturated hue: red, orange, yellow, green, cyan and blue, in pill-shaped bars.
   - **What it costs:** The colors carry no information, because the day is already given by position and label. But the viewer still tries to decode them, and red Monday and green Thursday read as bad and good days (convention: Apple HIG / Material semantic color).
   - **Wrong focal point:** This panel becomes the first thing seen on a page whose headline figures are the KPIs. It is also the one element out of mood with the sober gray UI.
   - **Template tell:** Rainbow bars with fully rounded ends are a stock dashboard move, so this panel looks like anyone's chart (unslop).
   - **Wrong palette type:** It is one series across categories, so a palette of distinct hues doesn't fit at all (Wilke).
   - **Fix:** Draw all bars in one color, preferably the November blue from the line chart. If one day matters (the peak, today), pick it out and gray the rest. Square the bar ends so their tops can be compared.
2. **The weekday chart has no scale, and Sunday is unreadable** (defect). Only Tuesday carries a value (1,140). There is no value axis and no unit, so the other six days can only be judged against Tuesday by eye. The title doesn't say whether these are November totals or an average per weekday. Sunday shows no bar and no "0", so the viewer can't tell "no Sunday deliveries" from "data missing" (Tufte, ch. 2; Few: label axes and units).
   - **Fix:** Label every bar with its value, or add a zero-based axis like the line chart's. State in the title or a caption what the number counts. Mark Sunday explicitly: a "0" or "no deliveries", whichever is true.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line chart legend:** "November / October" sits in a separate legend at the top right. Labeling the two lines at their right ends would save the look back and forth (Tufte, Wilke).
- **Chart titles name only the topic:** "Boxes packed per day" and "Deliveries by weekday" don't say what the viewer should notice, such as whether November ran ahead of October every day. A one-line takeaway would help (taste).
- **Mixed vocabulary:** The page is "Deliveries" and the KPI is "Boxes delivered", but the chart counts "Boxes packed". If packed and delivered are the same thing, use one word. If they differ, the chart needs to say why it is on this page (common sense).
- **Footer:** "Greenacre Farm, Somerset" in the sidebar footer reads like the signed-in account, yet Greenacre Farm also appears as a grower in the extras table. That makes it unclear whose data this is. Label it as the account, or move it to an account area (common sense).
- **Sidebar icons:** A bar-chart icon stands for Deliveries, a document for Growers and a sun-like glyph for Settings. They are consistent in style but don't match their labels (taste).

**What's working.**
- **The KPI strip:** Every figure carries its October comparison right under it, in a quieter gray. The month-over-month read takes one glance and needs no extra badges (Few).
- **The line chart:** November in blue against October in gray is exactly the "one highlight, gray for context" treatment the weekday chart lacks. It has a zero-based axis, light gridlines and even ticks, so keep it as the model.
- **The extras table:** Number columns are right-aligned with tabular figures, the rules are light, and the grid and gutters line up across the cards. The page has a clean structure.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Date range:** I can't tell whether "1 to 30 November 2026" is a control. On a monthly dashboard, a visible way to change the period matters, so check that it is clickable and looks it.
- **Contrast:** The gray comparison lines and axis labels look adequate, but measure them against 4.5:1 to be sure.
