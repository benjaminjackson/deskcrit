**First read.** The eye goes straight to the black headline, "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY", then up to the small gray kicker, then down to the two-line paragraph. Then it stops, because there is nothing else on the page. It reads as an invitation to free ferry rides at a harbor open day. It feels confident and calm, but also blank and generic, more like a software landing page than a morning on the water.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero of a web page or a poster for a public open day, meant to get local people to come and ride the ferry.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy is clear and the type is set cleanly, but the piece doesn't do its job: it never tells the viewer when the open day is or how to go. The headline styling and the colorless palette are stock choices that make it look like any other startup hero.

**What would move it most**
1. **The viewer can't act on it.** The paragraph says "every half hour from the north landing" but gives no date, no first or last ferry, and no next step. Someone who wants to go finishes reading with the same question they started with: when? It's a **defect**, and the costliest one, because the date and times are what people plan around and later hold the organizer to. "City Harbour Open Day" names the event but not the day. "Every half hour" with no start or end time leaves people unsure whether to come at 7 or at 11. Fix: add the date and the hours of service, placed near the headline at a size readers can't miss. Only the organizer has those facts. If it's a web page, add one concrete ask, such as directions to the north landing, rather than a generic "Learn more".
2. **The headline is set wide and heavy, like a stock hero.** The capitals are very bold and tracked far apart. At this size the letter gaps come close to the word gaps, so "ALL ABOARD THE" reads more as a row of letters than as three words. Large type needs tighter spacing than text, not looser (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80; Bringhurst, §2.1.1). That's the **convention**. The other half is a **template tell** (Sokolovsky, unslop): an extra-bold heading under a small gray uppercase tracked line is the stock hero people have seen on many products, so this event looks like everyone else's. Fix: tighten the headline's tracking until the words hold together and drop the weight a step. Then either set the kicker in sentence case at normal spacing or cut it and work "City Harbour Open Day" into the information you add in point 1.
3. **The colors carry no harbor and no morning.** It's near-black and slate gray on white, with nothing warm, nothing of water or daylight. The headline promises an outing, but the page feels like a product announcement. This is **taste**, based on Itten (*The Art of Color*): color should support the feeling of the message, and here it is neutral where the message is about a fun day out. Fix: give the page one color from the event's own world and use it on the information that matters, such as the date. Don't add decoration.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks after "THE", so "the morning ferry" is split across two lines. Break after "ABOARD" so each line is one phrase. This is my judgment; no approved source covers line breaks in headings.

**What's working.**
- One element clearly leads. The headline is several times larger than everything else, so the eye knows where to start. Keep that contrast in scale when you add the date and times.
- Everything shares one strong left edge with a generous margin, so the page looks orderly with no visible grid.
- The paragraph is easy to read: a plain sans at a comfortable size, about 70 characters per line, with open line spacing. "Bring a hat and a thermos" is specific, friendly copy that sounds like a person. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The lower 40% of the frame is empty. If this is the top of a longer page, check in a live browser whether the date, times, and directions follow just below the fold. If this is the whole poster, that empty space should hold them.
