**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web page that tells ferry passengers when the boat runs, how to board and how to buy tickets, read on a laptop.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job well: there's one clear place to start, three distinct type levels, comfortable line lengths and headings that sit with their text. What holds it back is the copy. Where a passenger needs a fact they will plan around (a time, a price, how long a pass lasts), the page gives a loose description instead.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts a passenger acts on are vague or missing (defect).**
   - In the opening paragraph, "between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing. Someone planning the trip home can't tell whether "late evening" means 9 or 11, and guessing wrong strands them on the wrong bank.
   - Under "Tickets", the season pass "covers any number of crossings for a month". That reads two ways: a calendar month, or a month from the day you buy it. Someone who buys on the 20th will hold the crew to whichever reading they took.
   - The Tickets section never says what a single, a return or a pass costs.

   These are the lines readers remember and plan around, so each one should be stated exactly. Put the actual first and last departure times in place of "early morning" and "late evening". Say when the pass period starts and ends. Add the fares, or say where to find them. Only you have those facts, so I haven't supplied any. The "Winter service" paragraph is cut off at the bottom of the screenshot, so check that it states the winter hourly times and the last boat just as exactly.

**Smaller things.**
- "kiosk." sits alone on the last line of the first paragraph under "Tickets". A one-word last line looks like an accident (Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.4.2). Fix it with a non-breaking space between "landing" and "kiosk", or a small edit so the line breaks earlier.

**What's working.**
- **A clean type scale.** I count three sizes: the title at about 40 px, section headings at about 22 px and body at about 18 px. The steps between them are big, and the headings are bold as well as larger, so each level is obvious at a glance. Keep it at three.
- **A comfortable reading column.** Body lines run about 60 to 65 characters with generous line spacing, which sits inside Bringhurst's 45 to 75 range. The page reads without effort.
- **Headings sit with their own text.** Each heading ("Boarding", "Tickets") is close to the paragraph below it and well clear of the one above, so the sections separate cleanly. The short bulleted list under Boarding breaks up the instructions at exactly the point where a rider needs steps.
