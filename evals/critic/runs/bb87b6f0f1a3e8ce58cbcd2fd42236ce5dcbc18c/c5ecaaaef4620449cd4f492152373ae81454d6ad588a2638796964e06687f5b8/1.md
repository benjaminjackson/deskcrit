**First read.** The eye lands straight on "Harbour Lights Festival", set very large in a cream italic serif. It jumps up to the amber date, then drops to the two lines of body text. Within two seconds it reads as the top of a web page for a small seaside town festival. Navy, cream and amber feel like dusk on the water: calm, warm, a little literary.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page for a free local festival. Its job is to get people to put the day in their diary and turn up.

**Verdict.** The design is good: one clear focal point, a tight type scale, a palette that suits the subject, and nothing on it without a job. What stops it doing its job is the copy. It never says where the festival is, and its one time ("from noon") clashes with the dusk events the text promises.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page never says where the festival is.** This is a defect. The copy names "the quay" and "the old net shed", which only mean something to people who already know the town. A stranger, or a visitor sharing the link, learns what and when but not where, and that's the third thing they need before they can come. The date line is already the logistics line, so the place belongs there next to the date. The exact wording is for the maker to supply.
2. **The time can be read two ways.** This is a defect, on the fact people will plan around. "From noon" says to arrive at midday, but the first image the body text offers is "lantern boats on the water at dusk", and the headline is about *lights*. A reader can take it as "it starts at noon" or "the lanterns, the part I want to see, are at noon", and miss them or wait around for hours. Keep "from noon" and add when the lantern boats go out. That's an edit to existing copy using a time only the maker has.

**Smaller things.**
- **The date is set as a stock eyebrow.** It's a small, uppercase, widely letterspaced amber label above the headline, a move seen on countless landing pages, so this part of the page looks like anyone's. It's a template tell (Sokolovsky, unslop). The color is right and should stay, because it makes the date the second thing you read. Set it in sentence case at normal spacing, and let the color and its place above the headline do the work.

**What's working.**
- **The headline owns the page.** The italic serif is several times the size of everything else, so the eye has one clear place to start. The other two levels (the date and the body) are each plainly different in size, color or face, with no near-miss sizes.
- **The palette carries the subject on its own.** Deep navy for the night water, cream for lantern light and amber for flame, with no picture needed. The amber is used once, on the most useful fact. Keep that restraint.
- **The body copy is specific and well set.** "Lantern boats", "music on the quay" and "a fish supper at the old net shed" could only belong to this event, unlike generic festival copy. It's set flush left at about 60 characters per line with comfortable line spacing, and "Free entry for everyone" answers the cost question before anyone asks.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** There's no call to action or link here: no map, program or directions. That's fine if the page continues below with them, but if this screen is all there is, a visitor has nowhere to go next. Check the full page.
