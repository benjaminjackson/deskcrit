**First read.** The eye goes first to the big white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces", set over a dark wall of leaves. Then it drops below the band to the lede and moves across the three cards: hours, workshops, delivery. After that it stops, because nothing invites it further. It reads as a calm, tidy local garden center home page, pleasant and a bit anonymous: the leaf pattern could belong to any plant shop.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page of an independent garden center. Its job is to tell local people with shady or small gardens and balconies that this place has plants for them, and to get them to come in or buy this weekend.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and its facts are good, but it doesn't do its job yet. The lede says "ready to pot this weekend" and then the page gives you nothing to act on. The hero, half the screen, shows wallpaper instead of the plants it promises.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no next step.** The lede ends on "ready to pot this weekend," which is an invitation to act. Then the page ends at three information cards with no button, link, or ask anywhere in the content. The only ways onward are the four small gray nav links in the top right. A viewer who is convinced has to work out alone whether to browse, come in, or order delivery. The eye path dead-ends at the cards. **Defect.** The fix is one clearly primary action placed with the headline and lede, styled above everything else (Material Design and Apple HIG: one dominant action per view). Name one exact thing to do: for example, see the shade plants in stock, or plan a visit. Ideally it points to a case from the viewer's own life, such as the kind of spot they're trying to fill. Which action, and which case, is the maker's call.
2. **The hero picture is generic, and it doesn't say what the headline says.** The 400 px band is a repeating pattern of flat, illustrated leaves and dots. It shows no fern, no hosta, no shade, no small space, and nothing of the nursery the lede says the plants are grown at. Headline and picture don't make one idea. The picture is just a green background, so the page's most specific claim (shade plants, small spaces, grown here) has no visual proof. Viewers have seen this kind of stock texture-as-hero on many shops, so the page looks like anyone's. **Template tell** (unslop: the hero visual should be an image from the product's world). The fix is to replace the pattern with a picture from their own world: their plants, their nursery, or a planted shady corner or balcony. Then the image carries "shade and small spaces" and the headline doesn't have to do it alone.
3. **The hero splits the headline from its own lede, and the headline is off the grid.** The headline sits inside the dark band. The sentence that explains it sits below the band's edge, about 200 px away, closer to the cards than to the headline it finishes. The box edge breaks one message into two groups (Palmer, common region; NN/g). Also, the headline's left edge sits at about x=72, while the logo, lede, and all three cards share a left edge at about x=56. That 16 px near-miss reads as a mistake rather than a choice (Arnheim on ambiguous placement). **Defect** (grouping) and **convention** (grid). The fix is to keep headline, lede, and the new action together in one group, inside the band or all below it, on the same 56 px left edge as everything else.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: "spaces" sits alone on the second line, a one-word widow (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rebalance the break, for example "Plants for shade / and small spaces".
- Opening hours card: "until 8 / pm" breaks a time from its unit (Bringhurst 2.4.6). Use a non-breaking space in "8 pm".
- Saturday workshops card: "ten places in each class" implies booking, but the card gives no time and no way to book, so the viewer must go hunt for it (Nielsen 6). Put the time and a booking link on the card, or link the card to the workshops page.

**What's working.**
- The card copy is concrete: "8 am to 6 pm, until 8 pm on Thursdays", "free within ten miles on orders over $60", "ten places in each class". These are real facts a local customer acts on. Keep this voice and this precision.
- The headline names a real audience and a real problem (shade, small spaces) instead of a slogan. It's the right thing to lead with.
- The type plan is restrained and clear: a dark green serif logotype set against one plain sans for everything else. There are four or so sizes with distinct jobs, and card titles are set apart by weight rather than another size.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the nav links and cards.
