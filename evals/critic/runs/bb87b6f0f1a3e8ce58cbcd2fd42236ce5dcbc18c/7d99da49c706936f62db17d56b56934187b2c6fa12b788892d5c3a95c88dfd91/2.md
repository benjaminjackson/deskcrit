**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. Every line is the same dark gray, the same weight, and about the same size, so the page reads as one even block of text at the top left. You find the four topics only by reading each line. From the body text it seems to be a bakery's help page for bread orders. Nothing on the page says so. It feels calm and plain, but more unfinished than restrained.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help or FAQ section where a bakery customer who already has an order scans for their one question (change, collect, allergies, cancel) and gets an answer they can act on.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job yet. The page exists so people can scan for their question, but the questions look the same as the answers. The headings are even a little smaller than the body text. A reader has to read every line to find the one they need. The cancellation line, the one people will hold the bakery to, can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings don't look like headings.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two are the same face, weight, and color as the answers below them. Up close they look about a pixel *smaller* than the body. On a scanning page this is the whole problem. The four questions and four answers read as eight paragraphs in a row, so someone looking for "cancel" has to read down the page instead of jumping to it. Defect. Fix: make the questions a clear level of their own. Following Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8–9), pair a modest step up in size with a change of weight, usually semibold, so a reader can find their question at a glance. Never make a heading smaller than its text.
2. **The cancellation terms can be read two ways.** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" doesn't say ahead of what (the pickup time? the bake?), and "a day" could mean 24 hours or "by the day before." A customer who reads it as "the day before, any time" and cancels at 9 p.m. for a 10 a.m. pickup could find they're too late. This is the line people will quote back at the bakery. Defect. Fix: restore the missing reference point and an exact cutoff, such as "up to 24 hours before your pickup time" *if that's the rule*. Only the bakery knows the real cutoff, so it has to supply it. "After that the bread has already been baked for you" is good. It gives a reason, and the reason lands on the customer.
3. **Nothing says what this page is.** There's no title above the first question, so a first-time viewer has to work out from the answers that this is bakery order help (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: a page should be self-evident). Defect, though a small one if this crop sits under a page header I can't see. Fix: give the section a plain title that names its subject, set clearly above the question level so the page has three levels: title, question, answer.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the body runs nearly to 980 px, and lines like "Give the person your order number. They will need to show it at the counter along with a name." are about 95 characters, well past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column to roughly 65 characters.
- One-word last line: "anything." sits alone under the allergies answer. A narrower measure will likely fix it. If not, rebalance the break.
- Copy, "along with a name": whose name, the collector's or the person who ordered? Someone sent to collect may bring the wrong one. Say whose name.
- Copy, "Open your order": open it where (the email, an account page)? Name the place if there's more than one.
- Copy, "holds orders for two hours after the chosen time": it doesn't say what happens after that. If the order is released or not refunded, people need to know, and that's a fact only the bakery has.

**What's working.**
- The spacing groups things correctly. Each question sits about 8 px above its answer and about 30 px below the previous answer, so once the headings get real weight, the pairs will read as clean units. Keep those gaps.
- The answers are short, direct, and spoken to the reader ("Give the person your order number", "Ask the baker if you are unsure"). Most of them tell you what to do, not just how the system behaves.
- One typeface, one text color, no decoration. For a help page that's the right amount of design. Keep that restraint when you add the hierarchy.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether this block is the whole page or a crop below a header. If a page title and site navigation sit above it, finding 3 goes away. Check it in the live page.
