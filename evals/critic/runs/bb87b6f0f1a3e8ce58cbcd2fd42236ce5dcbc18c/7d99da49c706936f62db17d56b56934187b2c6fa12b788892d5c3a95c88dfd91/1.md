**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming this is a help or FAQ block on a bakery's order-pickup page. Customers scan it for their own question and read only that answer.

**Verdict.** The copy is short and clear, and the spacing groups each question with its answer. But the questions don't stand out from the answers, and that defeats the one thing an FAQ is for: letting a reader scan for their question. That's the single biggest problem.

**What would move it most**

1. **The questions look like body text, or slightly smaller.** Look at "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients" and "Cancelling an order". Each heading is in the same regular weight and the same dark gray as the answer under it. Up close (around x 160, y 60) it even looks about a pixel *smaller* than the answer. A customer looking for "can I cancel?" has to read the page from the top instead of jumping down a column of questions. The only thing that tells them apart is a slightly bigger gap above each pair, so the page reads as one even list with no place to start.
   - This is a **defect** for this job. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8) says a difference this small reads as an accident, not a choice. Bringhurst (3.1.1) says sizes this close together don't read as separate levels.
   - Fix: make every question obviously different from its answer at a glance. A heavier weight alone would do it on a page this simple, or a darker color for the question with the answer set back to a lighter gray. Don't make the question smaller than the answer.

2. **The answers run too wide.** The one-line answers in "Collecting for someone else" and "Cancelling an order" are about 90–95 characters long, well past Bringhurst's 45–75 (66 ideal, 2.1.2). The text column is about 820 px wide while most of the frame sits empty, so nothing forces that width. Answers this short survive it, but a longer answer added later will be tiring to read. This is a **convention**, and it costs something here. Fix: narrow the column to about 60–70 characters. The two-line answers will then wrap with a better rag too.

3. **"Up to a day ahead" can be read two ways, and cancelling is a commitment.** It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot. It could also mean any time on the day before. A customer who reads it the generous way and tries to cancel at 9pm the night before may find the order locked and still owe for the bread. The same goes for "along with a name": the person collecting may not know whether they need *their own* name or *yours*. This is a **defect**. Fix: state the exact cutoff, and say whose name the counter needs. Only the bakery knows both facts, so supply them rather than rewording around them.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer. A narrower column (finding 2) or a small rewrap fixes it (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- The spacing does the grouping: each question sits close to its answer (about 10 px) and well apart from the next pair (about 25 px). Even with no difference in type, you can tell which answer belongs to which question. Keep these gaps when you change the weights.
- The copy is plain and has a human voice. "After that the bread has already been baked for you" explains the rule instead of just stating it, and it lands on the customer. Keep that tone.
- It has no decoration at all, no accordions, icons or cards, and on a page this small it needs none.
