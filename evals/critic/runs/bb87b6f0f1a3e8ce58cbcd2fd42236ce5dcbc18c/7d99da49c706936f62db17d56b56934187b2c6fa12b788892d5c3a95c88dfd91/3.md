**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It starts top left at "Changing your pickup time" and reads straight down, eight lines of what looks like one size, one weight, one color. Only the spacing tells you it is four questions with answers, and you only see that on the second pass. It reads as the help section of a small bakery's pre-order service: calm and plain, but it looks unstyled, like a draft.

**What it's for.** Assumed: an FAQ block on a bakery's ordering page, so a customer can scan for their question and get the answer fast.

**Verdict.** The copy is mostly good and the spacing groups it correctly. But the headings can't be told apart from the answers, which defeats the point of an FAQ: the reader can't scan for their question. Fix that first. Two answers that commit the bakery to something can also be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The questions look the same as the answers.** The four headings ("Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients", "Cancelling an order") look like the same face, weight and near-black as the answers, and are within a pixel or two of their size. If anything they look slightly smaller. The only cue is the gap above each group. A customer looking for "can I cancel?" has to read from the top instead of jumping to the fourth heading. It would also still be flat in grayscale, because nothing separates the levels by lightness either. **Defect.** A difference this small reads as an accident, not a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8; Arnheim, ch. I). Fix: make the headings differ from the answers in a way you see at a glance. The usual way is a clearly heavier weight, with or without a small step up in size. Or drop the answers to a softer gray and keep the questions full black. Pair a smaller step in size with a change of weight or color, not size alone (Dair, ch. 8a–8g and 9).

2. **The cancellation deadline and the pickup-for-someone-else rule can each be read two ways.** These are the lines customers will hold the bakery to.
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" doesn't say ahead of what, or how long a day is. It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time on the day before. Someone who cancels the evening before an 8 am pickup will think they're in time under one reading and find they're not under the other. **Defect.** Fix: state the cutoff against the pickup time, in a unit that can't be misread. Only you know which rule is true.
   - "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name": whose name? The collector's own, or the name on the order? If the collector gives their own name and the staff expect yours, they leave without the bread. **Defect.** Fix: name whose name it is. Restoring that one word is the whole edit.

3. **The answers run too wide.** The text block is about 820 px wide, so a one-line answer like "Give the person your order number… along with a name." runs about 85–90 characters. That's above the 45–75 range (Bringhurst, 2.1.2). With answers this short it costs little now, but it gets worse the moment an answer runs to three lines. **Convention.** Fix: narrow the column to roughly 60–70 characters. The page has plenty of empty space on the right.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the second line of the allergies answer. Re-break the line or adjust the width so the last line holds more than one word (Bringhurst, 2.4.2).
- There's no page title or bakery name above the four questions. As a stand-alone page, a reader landing here gets no "Help with your order" or similar to confirm they're in the right place. That's taste, and it doesn't matter if this sits inside a page that already says so.

**What's working.**
- **The spacing groups things correctly.** The gap from question to answer (about 10 px) is clearly smaller than the gap between groups (about 28 px), so each answer belongs to its question. Keep this when you add weight to the headings.
- **The copy is plain and written to the reader**, and two answers end on what the rule means for them. "The bakery holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" tells you how much slack you have. "After that the bread has already been baked for you" explains the cutoff in the customer's terms, so it reads as fair rather than arbitrary. Keep that voice.
- **One typeface in one dark color on white, flush left, with comfortable spacing between lines.** It's calm and readable, and has none of the stock template effects. It only needs one clear difference between question and answer.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether these are static text or expandable questions. If they open and close, the headings need to look clickable and the open/closed state needs checking in a live browser.
