**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, drops through two paragraphs of plain gray-black text, and stops at the pale gray panel. It reads as a calm, plain notice from a ferry service, a help or info page. It feels tidy and trustworthy. The one thing that looks off right away is the panel: its text sits near the top and leaves an empty band below it, so the box looks like it is missing a line.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a public info page for ferry passengers. Its job is to tell them when the winter ferry runs, what can delay it, and what vehicles pay or where they go.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The hierarchy is clear, the copy is concrete, and nothing is decorative for its own sake. Two craft problems hold it back: the lopsided padding in the gray panel and body lines that run far too long.

**What would move it most**
1. **The gray panel's padding is lopsided** (defect). In the panel at the bottom of the column, the text starts about 24 px below the top edge but ends about 50 px above the bottom edge. The left inset is also about 24 px. Readers will see the empty strip under "the cargo deck at the stern." and think a line failed to load or was cut off. The panel then looks broken rather than deliberate, and on a page about fares that makes readers wonder whether part of the vehicle rules is missing. Ruder's point applies: the empty space here isn't doing any job, it's left over. Make the padding equal on all four sides, or check whether a line of content really is missing. If one is, that's a bigger problem than the spacing.
2. **The lines are too long** (convention, and it costs something here). The body paragraphs and the panel run across the full ~880 px column at about 100 to 105 characters per line, against Bringhurst's 45 to 75 (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). Readers lose their place when jumping back to the start of the next line. That matters most where the content is times and rules people need to read exactly ("every forty minutes", "seven in the morning"). Narrow the text column to about 65 characters. The heading can stay wider if you want.

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- **The heading.** It's in heavy weight at roughly twice the body size, so there's one clear place to start. It says exactly what the page holds, with no slogan.
- **The copy.** It's concrete and actionable: real months, times and intervals, and one clear instruction ("Check the board at the ticket hut before you queue"). Keep that plain, specific voice.
- **The restraint.** There's one typeface, one neutral palette, and a pale tint rather than a border to set off the vehicle rules. Nothing competes with the information.

- Whether the empty band in the panel comes from content that failed to render. Check this in a live browser.
