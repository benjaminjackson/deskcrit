**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Winter timetable at the harbour," then drops into two even columns of calm serif prose. It reads as a well-set information page for ferry passengers, and it feels quiet and trustworthy. Then it breaks down. I look for the timetable and there are no times. Below that, "Fares and cards" sits over a rule with nothing under it, and a matching rule on the right has no heading at all. The page stops about three quarters of the way down.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that tells winter ferry passengers when boats leave and what to expect, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is good, but the page doesn't do its job. It promises a timetable and gives no departure times, and its second section is an empty heading. Missing content is the problem here, not styling.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline promises a timetable the page doesn't have.** "Winter timetable" sits over six paragraphs that never give a departure time. The closest the page gets is "every ninety minutes," "the first crossing" and "the last boat," all with no clock times. A passenger who came to find out when the first boat leaves can't, and has to read every sentence to learn that. That's a defect: the heading doesn't name what the section holds (WCAG 2.4.6), and the one fact the viewer came for isn't self-evident (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). The fix has two parts. Put the actual departure times on the page, as a table people can scan, with the first and last boat clearly marked. Only the maker has those times. If the times live somewhere else, retitle the page to say what it actually covers.
2. **"Fares and cards" is an empty section, and the right column has a rule with no heading.** At about y=560–585, the left heading and its rule have nothing below them. The right column has the same rule with no heading over it. The page then ends in blank space. To a viewer this looks broken: either the fares didn't load or the page was published unfinished. Either way, the information people pay by is missing. This is a defect. If fares do load in the live page, check that in a browser (see below). If they don't, the section needs its content. The right-hand rule needs a heading of its own, or it should be removed so the two section starts follow one pattern (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*).
3. **The practical facts are buried in running prose under one heading.** Opening hours, sailing frequency, weather cancellations, the waiting room, crossing times, bicycles, prams and dogs are six or seven topics set as one block. Someone checking "can I bring my bike?" has to read everything to find it. That's a defect for a page people skim (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web"). Break it into short sections with headings that name their topic, such as hours, sailings, crossing time and on board, so each answer can be found by its heading. Keep the wording. It's already plain and friendly.

**Smaller things.**
- "bags." and "thirty-five." each sit alone on the last line of their paragraphs in the right column. Rebreak those lines so no single word is left on its own (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- Copy: "the first crossing is usually the quietest" and "notices are pinned to the board beside the gate" speak to someone standing at the harbour. If they're meant for someone planning online, say where a cancellation is posted for them, if the maker has such a place.

**What's working.**
- The type is a sound choice. A serif made for reading, at a comfortable size, with lines of roughly 55–65 characters and generous leading, makes the prose easy to read. Keep it when the content is restructured.
- The headline clearly dominates through size and bold weight, and the sizes step down cleanly from headline to section heading to body. Three distinct levels with no near-collisions, so the hierarchy is ready to carry more sections.
- The two columns share one grid and their baselines line up across the gutter, so the page looks orderly and calm.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty "Fares and cards" section and the blank lower quarter could be content that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check the live page. If fares appear there, finding 2 shrinks to the unlabeled right-hand rule.
- How the two columns reflow on a phone, and whether the reading order (all of the left column, then the right) holds up.
