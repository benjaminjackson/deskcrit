**First read.** The eye lands on the "Plan and billing" title, then on the Co-op card with the teal border, then on the teal "Save changes" button halfway down the page. It reads as the billing settings page of a small SaaS tool for honey co-ops, aimed at whoever handles the money. It feels calm, plain, and well kept.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a co-op treasurer uses this page to check or change the plan, keep the billing contact and address current, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The page is well built and mostly does its job. The one real problem is that changing the plan, which commits the co-op to a new monthly charge, has no clear action, no stated cost or timing, and no "current" marker. The only button in reach, "Save changes", sits under a different section.

**What would move it most**

1. **Nobody can tell how to change the plan or what it will cost.** *Defect.* The three plan cards (top band) are radio buttons with no action of their own. The only primary button, "Save changes", sits about 260 px below them, directly under the Billing details fields, inside the area marked off by the rule above "Billing details". A treasurer who clicks "Packer" is left with questions:
   - Does that already switch the plan?
   - Does "Save changes" switch it, or does it only save the email and address?
   - Is the charge from today, prorated, or from the next invoice on the 3rd?

   This is money the user will later hold the maker to, so the gap is costly. One reading means the co-op is upgraded without noticing. The other means it thinks it upgraded and didn't. The button breaks natural mapping: a control belongs next to what it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*). The when-and-how-much question is exactly where short help belongs, at the step it concerns (Nielsen, heuristic 10). On top of that, the current plan is marked only by a selected radio and a teal border. That looks the same as a choice the user just made, so after one click they can no longer see what they're on now (common sense).

   Fix direction: give the plan its own action next to the cards, or make plain that one save covers both sections. Label the current plan as current. Next to the action, say when a change takes effect and how it is billed. Only the maker knows that policy, so I'm not writing the line.

2. **The Packer card breaks the pattern of the three cards.** *Defect, minor but visible.* "Unlimited members and batch labels" wraps and leaves "labels" alone on the second line. That pushes "$84.00 a month" down, so it no longer sits level with the $9.00 and $36.00 prices. The gap between the description and the price also shrinks to almost nothing, while the other two cards keep a clear gap. The prices are the one row a viewer compares across the cards, and here they don't line up (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*; Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*, on elements of one kind sharing one spacing). Fix direction: pin the price to one baseline in every card and keep the same gap above it. The card's wording can be tightened if the maker wants it on one line.

**Smaller things.**
- Tax-number checkbox: "Add the co-op tax number to every invoice" doesn't show which number. The user has to remember it or go to Co-op details to check. Show the number, or link to where it's set (Nielsen, heuristic 6).
- Required fields: Billing email and Invoice address have no required or optional markers. Mark them, for example with an asterisk after each required label and one line explaining it (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- Input, select, and plan-card borders look pale gray on white, perhaps under 3:1. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11, and darken them if they fall short.

**What's working.**
- The structure is calm: one left edge at x≈300 for the title, section headings, fields, and table, inside one content column. Thin, pale rules separate the sections without turning them into boxes. Keep that restraint.
- The invoice table is right for lookup: Amount is right-aligned under a right-aligned header, and Status is a word, "Paid", not a colored dot. It's easy to scan, and nothing depends on color alone.
- The plan cards say what each plan includes in the user's own terms ("Up to 10 hives, one seller", "shared stock") and give the price in full ("$36.00 a month"). That's exactly what a treasurer needs to compare plans.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What "Save changes" actually does to the plan selection. Check this live, since finding 1 depends on it.
- Focus indicators on the radios, fields, and buttons.
- Whether the invoice list goes on below the fold, and how it is paged.
