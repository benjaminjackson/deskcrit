**First read.** The eye lands on the "Plan and billing" title, moves to the Marina card with the teal outline, and then drops to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads as the billing settings page of a SaaS tool for running a marina. It feels calm, orderly and competent, but plain: nothing on the page is wrong at first glance, and nothing stands out either.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is for a marina office manager who comes here to check or change the plan, keep the billing details correct, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the craft is solid. The page falls short where money is involved: it doesn't say what "Save changes" commits the viewer to, or when and how they'll be charged. On a billing page those are exactly the things the user will later hold you to.

**What would move it most**
1. **It's unclear what "Save changes" does to the plan.** A rule separates the plan cards from Billing details, and the only Save and Cancel buttons sit at the bottom of Billing details. A manager who clicks Harbor group can't tell whether that switches the plan right away, waits for Save, or does nothing until they save. They also can't tell whether $149.00 is charged now, prorated, or charged on the first. The rule and the separate heading tell the eye that the plan is its own group, yet one button seems to control both groups. Because the control doesn't sit next to the thing it changes, the user has to guess, and here a wrong guess costs money (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping; Nielsen heuristic 10, help where the task is complex). **Defect.** Fix direction: give the plan change its own explicit commit step, or make the shared button's scope visible. Either way, say next to the cards when a change takes effect and how it's charged. Only you know those billing facts.

2. **The autopay line can be read two ways, and the card it names isn't shown.** "Charge the card on file before the first of each month" doesn't say which card, or how long before the first. One reader will take "before" to mean a day before; another will take it to mean any time in the prior month. The line commits money, so that ambiguity is a defect, not a style note. Nothing on this screen shows the card on file either, so the user has to remember which card it is or go find it under "Payment terminal" (Nielsen heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall). Setting "before" in bold draws attention to the vaguest word in the line. **Defect.** Fix direction: name the timing exactly, for example the number of days, and show the card (brand and last four digits) with a way to change it, right beside this checkbox.

3. **The plan cards compare on different terms.** Harbor gives "Up to 30 slips, **one** dock", Marina gives "Up to 150 slips, **no** booking fees", and Harbor group gives "Up to 5 marinas, one boater list". A manager deciding whether to upgrade can't line the plans up against each other. Does Harbor charge booking fees? How many docks does Marina allow? The bold words also differ from card to card: one card bolds "one", the next bolds "no", and the third bolds nothing. So the emphasis looks random rather than pointing at the difference between plans (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: elements of one kind follow one program). **Defect.** Fix direction: describe every card on the same few attributes in the same order, and use bold for the same attribute on each card or not at all. You'll need to supply the missing facts for each plan.

**Smaller things.**
- Invoices: the invoice numbers and rows offer no way to open or download an invoice, and getting the PDF is usually why someone scrolls down here. Make each row or invoice number a link to the PDF (common sense).
- Billing email and invoice address carry no required or optional marking. Mark them (Nielsen Norman Group, Budiu, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").

**What's working.**
- The page skeleton is disciplined. The sidebar, the content column from x≈300 to x≈1120, and the rules all share one set of edges. Keep it.
- The selected plan shows in two ways, by a filled radio and by the teal border, so the choice doesn't depend on color alone.
- In the invoice table, the amounts are right-aligned and the figures line up, the dates are unambiguous ("1 Nov 2026"), and there's one primary button with Cancel clearly styled lower.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't see focus states on the plan cards and fields, or whether picking a card changes anything before Save. Check both in a browser, since finding 1 depends on the second.
