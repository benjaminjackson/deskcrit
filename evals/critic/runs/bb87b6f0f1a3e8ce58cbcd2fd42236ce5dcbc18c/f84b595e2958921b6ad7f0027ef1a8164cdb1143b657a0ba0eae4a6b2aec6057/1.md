**First read.** Your eye goes straight to the dark middle card, "Woodland loop". It is the only dark block on a white page, so it pulls ahead of the page title and the other two cards. After that the eye moves to the two white cards, which match each other. This looks like a list of walking routes for local walkers. It feels tidy, but the middle card looks like it came from another website.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a page where someone compares a few walking routes and opens the one that suits them.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page gives one route star billing for no reason it shows, using a style that matches nothing else on the page. Because the cards mostly hold prose and the lower half of each is empty, comparing the routes takes reading when it should take a glance.

**What would move it most**
1. **The middle card breaks the system and gives no reason (defect).** Every attribute of "Woodland loop" differs from its neighbors:
   - a dark ground
   - a serif heading about as large as the page title "Walking routes"
   - a yellow outlined pill button where the others have filled blue rounded rectangles
   - square corners
   - roughly half the inner padding (text about 15 px from the edge, against about 33 px)
   - a heading, text, and button that sit higher than in the other cards, so nothing lines up across the row

   The viewer sees that it is different but not why. Is it recommended, sponsored, or a bug? Because its heading rivals the page title, the page has two competing starting points. The outlined button is also the weaker button style, sitting in the most emphasized card, which is backwards. Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: elements of one kind share one style, and a variant appears only where the rules call for it. WCAG 1.4.1: if the dark treatment means "featured", that meaning needs a text label too, not color alone.

   Fix: pick one.
   - If no route is featured, set Woodland loop in the same card style as the others.
   - If one is featured, keep the same padding, corner radius, type, and button so it still lines up with the row, and mark it with one change plus a word that says why. Keep its heading below the page title.
2. **The cards are about half empty, and the useful facts are buried in sentences (taste; common sense).** The content ends about 395 px down, but the cards run to about 600 px. That leaves a band of bordered empty space under every button. It doesn't read as breathing room; it looks like content failed to load. Ruder, *Typography*: empty space works when it is gathered on purpose, not trapped inside boxes. Meanwhile the facts a walker compares (distance, steepness, surface, shade, places to stop) are inside sentences, so comparing the routes means reading all three. Fix:
   - Make the cards as tall as their content, or use the space for things that belong to a route, such as a map or a photo, if you have them.
   - Bring the comparison forward using the words already there: lead each card with its distance. "Six kilometres", "Four kilometres", and "Ten kilometres" are the first words now, but they sit in body gray inside a sentence. Then let the rest of the line describe the walk.

**Smaller things.**
- "View route" appears three times and never names the route. Screen-reader users and anyone scanning links get three identical links (WCAG 2.4.4). Put the route name in the link text or its accessible label.
- Single words sit alone on the last line: "end." in Cliff path and "metres." in Woodland loop (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Adjust the width or the wording so the last line holds more than one word.

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete and written for walkers: "two steep climbs and a cafe at the far end", "benches every few hundred metres", "good for bicycles". Each line tells you who the route suits. Keep that voice when you pull the distances forward.
- The three-column grid is sound: equal columns, equal gutters, and a generous page margin. Card headings sit clearly closer to their own text than to the next card. The two white cards show the system working, so build the rest from them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the "View route" buttons, especially whether the yellow outline button shows a visible focus ring on the dark ground.
