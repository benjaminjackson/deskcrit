**First read.** The eye goes first to the big black "Garden show gallery," then drops to the large red Rose arch tile, then moves across the row of colored tiles. Every tile has the same pale half-circle rising from its bottom edge, so it looks like a wireframe or a set of flag swatches. It doesn't look like a garden. The page feels tidy and confident, but it shows nothing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page where visitors browse photos of the spots at a garden show, with one spot featured.

**Verdict.** The layout is good and the gallery doesn't work yet. A gallery's whole job is its pictures, and there are none: six flat colored blocks with an identical circle stand where the gardens should be. Until real images go in, a visitor can't see a single rose, herb, or pond.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders.** All six tiles are flat color with the same centered half-circle. Every caption promises something specific, like "Climbing roses over the east gate," "Reeds and water lilies," or "Tomatoes in late July," and the image above it shows nothing. A visitor reads six labels and sees no gardens, so there is nothing to browse and no reason to stay. On the Rose arch tile, the featured visual is an empty box, which is the stock template move (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"). That makes the page look like any unfinished template. *Defect.* Fix: put a real photo of each spot in each tile. This is fine only if this is a wireframe or the images failed to load in the capture; check that in a live browser.
2. **The colors carry the page and point the wrong way.** Six saturated hues (brick red, green, steel blue, ochre, mustard, violet) cover about half the page. Their mood is a corporate category chart, not plants and soil (Itten, *The Art of Color*: color mood should match the subject). They also mean nothing: blue for the pond is apt, but violet for an orchard and red for roses look arbitrary. Once real photos go in, this problem goes away, as long as the colored fills don't survive as frames or loading states. If they must stay as loading placeholders, use one muted neutral so the photos supply all the color. *Taste*, argued from the subject.

**Smaller things.**
- **Caption near-misses.** The Rose arch caption sits beside the Pond edge caption, and their baselines miss each other by a few pixels ("Rose arch" against "Pond edge," and the two description lines). The Rose arch image also ends about 37 px above the Pond edge image, so the bottom edges don't line up either. Either align these lines with the right column or move them clearly apart (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).
- **Headline weight.** "Garden show gallery" looks extra-bold, which is a stock template move (unslop, "Headings at weight 800 or 900"). At this size, a bold or regular weight would still lead the page and would feel less like a default.

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** A three-column grid with even gutters of about 14 px. The featured tile spans two columns, and everything else snaps cleanly to the column edges. Keep this structure when the photos go in.
- **Clear type levels.** Headline, feature title, feature description, tile title, tile description: each step is clear. The small titles are set apart from their descriptions by weight and color, not by another near-identical size (*Refactoring UI*: bolder, not bigger).
- **Specific captions.** "Runner beans on canes" and "Plum and pear trees" name real things a visitor could go and find. Keep that specificity.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether the tiles are links, or what happens when you open one. I also can't tell whether the flat fills are an image-loading state; check in a live browser.
