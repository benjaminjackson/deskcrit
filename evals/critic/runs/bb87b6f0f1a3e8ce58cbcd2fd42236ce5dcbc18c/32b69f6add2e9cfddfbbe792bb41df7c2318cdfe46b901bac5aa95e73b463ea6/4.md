**First read.** The eye goes to the three big KPI numbers first, mostly "184,220", then drops to the chart: three smooth colored lines all trending up. This reads as a calm, tidy marketing analytics overview, and the first glance is reassuring. The second glance is where it falls apart. I go looking for which line is which channel, what the numbers are, and which weeks these are, and the chart doesn't say.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: an overview screen where a marketer or founder checks traffic and sign-ups over the last 12 weeks, and sees which channels drive them.

**Verdict.** The KPI row works. The chart is the largest thing on the screen and it can't be read. It has no channel names, no values and no dates, so "Visits by channel" shows three anonymous lines. Until it's labeled, the main panel on this dashboard says only that "something went up."

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't say what any line is (defect).** In the "Visits by channel" panel the blue, orange and green lines have no names, no legend and no direct labels. The viewer can see that one channel leads and one is choppy, but can't tell which channels they are. That is the one question the title promises to answer. Label each line at its right end, next to its last point, with the channel name. Tufte and Wilke both favor labels on the data over a legend, so the eye doesn't have to go back and forth matching colors. Labeling the lines directly also fixes the color-only problem: right now the only difference between series is hue, which fails WCAG 1.4.1. A viewer with a color-vision deficiency can't separate them, and nobody can separate them in a grayscale print.
2. **The chart has no values or time axis (defect).** There are faint gridlines but no y-axis numbers and no week labels on the x-axis. The empty gutter to the left of the plot and the empty band below it are exactly where they should go. As a result:
   - The viewer can't tell whether the blue channel brings 3× the orange channel's visits or 10% more.
   - They can't tell whether the y-axis starts at zero. That decides whether the gaps between the lines mean anything.
   - They can't match the week-9 dip in the blue line to a date.

   This breaks Tufte's and Few's basic rule that a reader should never have to guess what a number measures. Add y values with the unit ("visits per week", or whatever it actually is) and week labels along the bottom.
3. **The title names a topic, not a finding (taste).** "Visits by channel" tells the viewer what kind of chart this is, then leaves them to work out the point. That's hard on a chart with no labels, but even once it's labeled, a dashboard serves people better when the panel says what changed. Two kinds of fact are missing, and only you have them: which channel grew the most, and whether that growth explains the +6.1%. A title or caption that says it would save every viewer the same reading job. This is my view as a designer, not a cited rule.

**Smaller things.**
- Green carries two meanings. It's the "up vs previous period" color in the KPI cards and also a chart series. The viewer may read the green line as "the good one." Take a series color that isn't also a status color. (Apple HIG and Material both keep green for success.)
- The "Visits by channel" title looks indented a couple of pixels further than the KPI card labels ("Visits", "Sign-ups") above it. Check the inner padding, and either match it exactly or make the difference clearly deliberate.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards.** Each has a quiet label, one dominant number and a delta with its comparison period spelled out ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). "+0.1 pts" on the conversion rate is the right unit, because it avoids the percent-of-a-percent confusion. Keep this pattern exactly.
- **Restraint.** The canvas is neutral, borders are hairlines and the chart has no chrome beyond faint gridlines, so nearly all the ink is data. Keep that when you add labels: they need to be small and gray, not heavy.
- **The type scale.** There's a clear title, numbers, labels and captions, with weight doing the work that would otherwise need extra sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" in the header is a range picker or a static label. If it's a picker, it needs to look like a control.
- Whether the chart shows values on hover. That would help, but it wouldn't replace the labels a glance needs.
