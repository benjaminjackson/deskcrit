**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers first (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%), then down to the three colored lines, and then looks for the key that says which line is which. There isn't one. It reads as a tidy, calm marketing-analytics overview for a small team. The header and KPI row feel finished and trustworthy, but the chart, which takes up two thirds of the screen, says almost nothing.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's an internal web dashboard where a marketing or growth person checks traffic, sign-ups, and conversion over the last 12 weeks, and sees which channels are bringing visits.

**Verdict.** The KPI row does its job well. The chart doesn't do its job at all. It has no series labels, no axis values and no dates, so "Visits by channel" can't tell you anything about any channel. Until that's fixed, the biggest element on the page is decoration.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't say which line is which channel, or how many visits any point stands for.** In the "Visits by channel" card, the blue, orange and green lines have no legend and no direct labels. The y-axis has five gridlines with no values, and the x-axis has no week labels. A viewer can see that "blue is highest and rising" but can't say what blue is, how many visits it is, or which week the dip around the middle was. That leaves the question the title asks unanswered. The card also holds empty space where labels would normally sit: about 50 px left of the plot and about 75 px under the baseline. That looks like labels that are meant to be there and didn't render. If so, this is a bug, not a design choice, so check it in a live browser. Either way, this capture shows a chart with no readings. The fix is to name each line at its right-hand end, in its own color (Tufte, Wilke: label directly instead of using a legend), and to add y values with a unit and week labels along the bottom (Tufte, ch. 2; Few). Without y values the viewer also can't tell whether the baseline is zero, so the gaps between the lines can't be read as sizes either. **Defect.**
2. **Hue is the only thing that tells the series apart, and two of the hues are close.** Orange and green look similar in lightness, so for a viewer with red-green color blindness, the two lower lines become two same-looking lines that differ only by position. Right now position can't help, because nothing names them (WCAG 1.4.1). Direct labels from finding 1 fix most of this. Also check the palette in a color-blindness simulator, or move to a color-blind-safe set. **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- The chart title names a topic, not a finding. Once the lines are labeled, a title that says what the chart shows, in words, would save the viewer from working it out. Only the maker can supply that finding. (Taste. The usual source here isn't on my approved list.)
- "Last 12 weeks" at top right is plain gray text. If it's the date-range control, nothing about it looks clickable. Give it a button or dropdown shape. If it's just a label, it's fine. (Defect only if it's a control.)

**What's working.**
- Each KPI card puts its number with a comparison and spells out the period ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). Conversion uses "+0.1 pts" rather than a percent, which is the correct and careful unit for a rate. Keep both.
- The type scale is tight: four sizes (page title, card title, big figure, small label and delta), each with a clear job. Size and weight do the work, and there's no decoration. The calm gray-and-white ground means the data colors can stand out.
- The grid is clean. The KPI cards and chart card share the 48 px margins and the same gutters, so the page reads as one ordered thing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether axis labels or a legend show up after loading, on hover, or in a tooltip. The empty bands inside the chart card suggest they were meant to be there, so check in a live browser.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a picker.
