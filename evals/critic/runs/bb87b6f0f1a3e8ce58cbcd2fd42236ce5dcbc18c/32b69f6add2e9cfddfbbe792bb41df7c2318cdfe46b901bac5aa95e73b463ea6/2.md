**First read.** The eye goes first to the three big numbers across the top, 184,220, 3,912 and 2.12%, then drops to the three colored lines, which take up most of the screen. It reads as a clean, calm weekly marketing dashboard for a small team. Within about two seconds the chart stops making sense: three lines are rising, but nothing says which channel is which or how big any of them are.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an overview screen where a marketing or growth person checks how traffic and sign-ups did over the last 12 weeks and which channel drove it.

**Verdict.** The layout is tidy, but the piece does not do its job. The main chart can't be read, and one of the three headline figures seems to contradict the other two. Fix the chart labels and check that number before anything else.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "Visits by channel" chart has no channel names, no values and no dates.** The blue, orange and green lines have no legend and no labels. There are no y-axis numbers and no week labels along the bottom. The viewer can see that "something went up" but can't say which channel, by how much, or when. They also can't tell whether the gaps between the lines are big or small, or whether the axis starts at zero. There is an empty strip under the baseline and an empty gutter on the left, which look like space kept for axis labels that never got drawn. **Defect.** A chart should say what its marks measure, with axis values and series names (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few). Put each channel's name at the right-hand end of its line instead of adding a legend (Tufte; Wilke). That also fixes the fact that the lines are told apart only by color, which fails anyone who can't separate blue, orange and green (WCAG 1.4.1). Add a few y-axis values and week labels. If labels exist in the live build and didn't render, this finding is about the capture, but as shown it is broken.

2. **The conversion-rate change looks wrong, and it's shown in "good" green.** Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups only 2.4%. When sign-ups grow more slowly than visits, the conversion rate has to fall. Working back from these figures, the previous rate was about 2.20%, so 2.12% is down about 0.08 points, not "+0.1 pts". This is the one figure a growth team acts on, so it can be read two ways: "conversion is improving" (what the card says) or "we're buying traffic that doesn't convert" (what the other two cards imply). The wrong reading hides a real problem. **Defect.** Check how the change is calculated and how it's rounded, and use green only if the rate actually went up.

3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** "Visits by channel" tells the viewer what the chart shows but not what happened. Even once it's labeled, they still have to work out which channel grew and whether anything changed. **Taste** (common sense; no approved source states this): give the title or a caption the finding the maker sees in the data, such as which channel drove the growth. Leave the wording and facts to the maker.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is plain gray text. If it is the date-range control, it doesn't look like one: give it a control's shape and an affordance such as a chevron. If it's only a label, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- Each stat card shows a comparison with the previous period. That's the right habit for headline numbers on a dashboard (Few), so keep it, as long as the deltas are correct.
- The type scale is tight and clear: page title, card numbers, chart title, labels and deltas each have their own step. Smaller details use weight and gray rather than extra sizes (Refactoring UI).
- It's restrained: one neutral ground, white cards with thin borders, and color kept for data and status. Once the labels are in, nothing will compete with the chart.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart has hover tooltips that show values. Even if it does, the static chart needs labels, because a glance shouldn't depend on hovering.
