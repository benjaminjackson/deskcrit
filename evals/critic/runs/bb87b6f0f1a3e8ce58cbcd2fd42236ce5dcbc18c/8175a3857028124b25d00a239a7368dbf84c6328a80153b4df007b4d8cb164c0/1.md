**First read.** The eye goes to the "Plan and billing" title, then right away to the one teal-outlined card, Marina, and the teal Save button below it. It reads as the billing settings of a SaaS product for marina operators. It feels calm, orderly, and familiar, almost too quiet: nothing on the page tells you what you're currently paying or when.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's the account owner's settings screen, where they check their current plan and charges, change plan or billing details, and look up past invoices.

**Verdict.** This is a clean, competent settings page that mostly does its job. The biggest gap is about money: it never states the next charge or its date, and it leaves you to work out when the card is charged and that "Save changes" also commits a plan change.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page never says what you will be charged and when, and the line that tries to is vague.** The Marina card shows "$69.00 a month" and the table shows past charges. Nowhere does it say what the next charge is and on what date, though that's usually the first thing someone opens a billing page to find. The checkbox line "Charge the card on file *before* the first of each month" can be read two ways. It could mean "on some day before the 1st" (which day?) or "in advance for the month". The italic only stresses the word that's unclear. This is a line people hold you to, so a wrong reading means a charge they didn't expect. Direction: add the next-charge amount and date near the top of the Plan section, using facts only you have, and name the actual charge day in the checkbox line. *Defect* (common sense; the "anticipation" principle behind it isn't on my approved source list).
2. **"Save changes" quietly commits a plan change.** The Save and Cancel buttons sit under Billing details, and a full-width rule cuts them off from the plan cards above. Someone who clicks the Harbor group card may think it took effect, or may not realize that Save will now bill $149 instead of updating an email. Controls should sit next to what they change (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). Direction: give the plan change its own action and confirmation that states the new price and when it starts, or at least make the shared Save say what it will change. *Defect.*
3. **You can't compare the plan cards side by side.** Each card names a different thing: "one dock", then "no booking fees", then "one boater list". So the reader has to guess whether Harbor has booking fees (and how much), or whether Marina is limited to one dock. Price, the line people decide on, is the same size, weight, and gray as the feature line above it. Direction: describe each tier on the same points in the same order, and make the price the line that stands out (by weight, not size). *Defect* for the comparison, *taste* for the price emphasis.
4. **Italic emphasis scattered through the interface copy:** *one* dock, *no* booking fees, *every* invoice, *before*. It's a stock move a viewer has seen on many generated products (Sokolovsky, unslop: emphasis in interface text comes from weight or color, not italic). Here it adds tone without meaning. Most of the stressed words don't need stress, and "as shown on *every* invoice" is filler in a field label ("Invoice address" says it). Direction: remove the italics. Where a word really carries the difference, such as on the plan cards, let the parallel wording from finding 3 do that work. *Template tell.*

**Smaller things.**
- Section headings "Plan", "Billing details", and "Invoices" look only about 2 px bigger than the plan names under them, so "Plan" and "Marina" read as nearly the same level. Make the section headings clearly larger or heavier (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8).
- Text-field and unselected-card borders look very pale, likely below 3:1 against white. Measure them and darken to 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- There's one primary action: the filled teal Save next to an outlined Cancel, and the same teal marks the selected plan. Color means "this is yours / do this" and nothing else.
- The invoice table is done right. Amounts are right-aligned with matching decimals, rules are light, status is a word rather than a colored pill, and the Plan column shows the Harbor→Marina change across months.
- You're never lost. The current page shows in the top nav (Settings), in the tinted sidebar item, and in the page title, and the content sits on one clear column from 300 to 1120 px.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the plan cards, fields, and buttons. Whether choosing a plan card asks for confirmation or shows a price preview. Whether the invoice numbers link to downloadable invoices.
