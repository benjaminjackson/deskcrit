**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing" and drops straight to the three bold prices. The teal outline on "Small fleet" and the teal "Save changes" button come next. It reads as the billing settings of a small SaaS tool for food-truck operators. It's calm, tidy and trustworthy, a little generic, and it looks finished.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: an operator comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details, and get invoices for their books.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and mostly well made. But the one thing on it that costs money is the hardest to understand. Changing plan is a money decision, and the page doesn't say what you're on now, what a switch costs or when it happens, or which button commits it.

**What would move it most**
1. **Changing plan has no terms and no clear commit button (defect).** The plan cards sit above a rule, and "Save changes" sits below it, under "Billing details". So a viewer who clicks "Commissary" can't tell whether that switch is already live, waits for Save, or needs something else. Nothing on the page says:
   - that "Small fleet" is your *current* plan rather than just the selected radio button;
   - when a change takes effect, or whether it's prorated;
   - when the next charge is;
   - which card will be charged. No payment method appears anywhere on a billing page.

   These are what the viewer will later hold you to. Read one way, the button saves an email address. Read the other way, it raises a bill from $59 to $129. A user who reads it the first way gets a surprise charge. The divider breaks up a group the controls need: the rule splits the plan from the action that commits it (Palmer and NN/g on common region; Norman on natural mapping). The direction:
   - Give the plan change its own clearly named commit button, beside the cards.
   - Label the current plan.
   - Put the timing and cost of a change, the next charge date and the payment method within reach of that button.

   The facts themselves are yours to supply.
2. **The plan cards can't be compared (defect, copy).** "One menu, 300 orders a day" gives an order count for one plan only. "Up to 5 trucks, shared menu" and "Up to 20 trucks and one kitchen" say nothing about orders. A stranger can read that two ways: the bigger plans have no order cap, or the cap just wasn't written down. Someone choosing a plan on order volume can't tell from this. "300 orders a day" also doesn't say whether it is a hard limit or what happens at order 301. The fix is to state the same facts, in the same order, on every card, so each line can be compared straight across. Restore the missing verb, too: "Takes up to 300 orders a day" is clearer than the bare number. The real limits are yours to supply.
3. **The invoices are a dead end (common sense; the source I would cite is off the approved list).** Getting an invoice or receipt for your accountant is probably the most common reason to visit this page. The table lists each invoice, but nothing looks clickable: no link style on "TR-4471" and no download or view action. If the invoice numbers are links, they need to look like links. If not, each row needs a clear way to open or download the invoice.
4. **The type scale is muddled (convention).** By eye I count about seven sizes:
   - page title, about 26 px;
   - prices, about 18 px bold;
   - plan names, about 16 px;
   - section headings ("Plan", "Billing details", "Invoices"), about 15 px semibold;
   - inputs and body text, about 14 px;
   - card descriptions and table headers, about 12 px;
   - field labels, about 11 px bold.

   I also see three weights. The 14, 15 and 16 px sizes don't read as separate levels (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). The prices outrank the section headings, so the cards look like the page's headings and "Billing details" looks like a footnote. A tighter scale would keep four sizes: title, section heading, body, small. Use weight to set the prices apart, not yet another size.

**Smaller things.**
- Section headings ("Plan", "Billing details", "Invoices") sit about midway between the rule above and the content below. Move each one closer to its own content.
- No field is marked as required or optional. Mark them (Budiu, NN/g).
- "Billing email" and "Currency" share a row but aren't a related pair. Taste: stack them, or put currency with the plan or payment.
- The currency dropdown uses the browser's native style while the other fields are custom-styled, so it looks borrowed from another design. Bring it in line with the text fields.
- Plan switching has no short help on proration or timing beside it. That help belongs next to the cards; it is part of finding 1.

**What's working.**
- **Restrained color.** Teal appears only on the selected plan and the primary button. Everything else is neutral, so the selected plan and the primary button stand out without effort. Keep it that rare.
- **The invoice table.** Amounts are right-aligned with figures that line up. The rules are hairline, and there is no zebra striping or boxes. It's easy to scan.
- **You always know where you are.** "Settings" is bold in the top nav and "Plan and billing" has a tint in the sidebar. The rest of the page stays quiet.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether choosing a plan card applies the change at once or waits for Save. Check this live, because finding 1 depends on it.
- Focus states on the cards, fields and buttons.
