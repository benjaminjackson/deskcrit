**First read.** The eye lands on the "Overview" title, then slides across the four KPI figures. They're all the same size, so it doesn't stop on any of them. Next it jumps to the solid blue Saturday bar, the only saturated spot on the page. It reads at once as a clean monthly sales dashboard for a small wine business. It feels calm and competent, but also anonymous.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a monthly overview for the shop owner: how November went, and where to look next.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, and the layout, palette and charts are mostly well judged. What gets in the way is trust and emphasis. The numbers don't share one format, and one of them looks like a bug. The page also shows a lot of figures without telling the owner which one matters or how the month went.

**What would move it most**
1. **The numbers don't look like they came from one system.** This is a defect.
   - In the table, Barolo's bottle count is "09" next to 84 and 61.
   - Revenue is "$1,512" next to "$1,159.50" and "$1,128.00".
   - In the KPI row, Revenue has cents ($24,815.60) but Average basket doesn't ($38), while its own comparison does ($36.25).
   - On a dashboard, the numbers are the product. A padded "09" reads as a broken query or a placeholder, and once one figure looks wrong, the owner starts doubting the rest.
   - Fix: one format per kind of number. Pick cents or no cents for money and use it everywhere, and never pad counts.
2. **Nothing tells the owner what matters or how the month went.** This is partly a convention (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through size) and partly taste.
   - The four KPIs share one size, weight and color, so none stands out first.
   - Each one gives October's raw value ("$21,970 in October") but no change or direction. To find out that revenue rose about 13%, the owner has to do the subtraction in their head four times.
   - The chart titles name topics ("Web shop visitors", "Bottles by weekday") rather than what they show. The Saturday highlight is the one place the page points at a finding, and it doesn't say why Saturday matters.
   - Fix: make the figure this owner checks first clearly dominant, probably Revenue. Show each comparison as a change with a direction, said in words and not just with color. Let titles state the finding where there is one.
3. **The visitors chart doesn't say what it counts.** This is a convention (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*: label units; keep scales honest).
   - The y-axis runs to 900, but you can't tell whether that's daily or per some other interval. The line has about 14 points across 30 days.
   - The x-axis ticks fall at 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov, which are uneven gaps of 9, 9 and 11 days.
   - Someone reading "~700" can't tell whether that's a good day or a good two days.
   - Fix: name the unit and interval in the title or on the axis, and use evenly spaced ticks.

**Smaller things.**
- Line chart legend (top right): label "November" and "October" at the ends of the lines instead, so the eye doesn't have to go back and forth (Tufte).
- Bars by weekday: only Saturday has a value (148) and there's no axis, so the other days can only be compared by eye. Label every bar lightly, or add a minimal scale.
- Date range "1 to 30 November 2026" (top right): it's plain text with no sign it can be changed. If the period can be switched, it should look like a control.

**What's working.**
- **Restrained color.** Everything is gray except the blue for the current month and the Saturday bar, so color always means "this is the one to look at". Keep that discipline when you add trend indicators.
- **Charts suit their data.** It's a line chart for change over time with last month grayed behind as context, bars from zero in natural weekday order, and a right-aligned table for exact values.
- **Calm, consistent layout.** Gutters are even, card padding is consistent, the type scale has clear steps, and the current page is highlighted in the nav. Everything fits on one screen.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the date range or the table headers can be clicked.
- Focus states in the sidebar nav.
