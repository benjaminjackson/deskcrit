**First read.** My eye goes first to the "Plan and billing" heading, then to the teal-bordered Salon card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. Both teal items are the same color, so they read as one choice and one action. It's clearly the billing settings for a small SaaS tool used by grooming salons. It feels calm, orderly, and trustworthy, with nothing shouting.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a salon owner or front-desk manager comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details, or find a past invoice.

**Verdict.** This is a competent, restrained settings page that mostly works. The one thing in the way is the money decision. The plan picker has no "current plan" marker, doesn't say what a switch will cost or when it starts, and the only commit button sits in a different section. So a user can't tell whether "Save changes" will change their bill.

**What would move it most**
1. **It's unclear whether "Save changes" changes the plan (defect).** The plan cards sit above a rule. "Save changes" sits under "Billing details", below that rule and about 250 px from the cards. Two readings are possible:
   - (a) Clicking a card and then Save moves you to a $95 plan.
   - (b) Save only stores the email and address, and the plan change needs some other step, or happens the moment you click the card.

   If the user believes (b) and it's really (a), they get an unexpected charge, which is exactly what they'll hold you to. If the reverse, they think they upgraded and didn't. A control should sit next to what it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping). Fix direction: give the plan change its own commit, next to the cards, with a label that names the action (for example "Switch to Salon group"). Keep "Save changes" for the billing fields only.
2. **There's no "current plan" marker and no consequence shown for switching (defect).** The Salon card's teal border and filled radio could mean "you're on this" or "you've selected this". Only the invoice table below (Salon, $45.00) confirms it. Nothing near the cards says when a change takes effect or how the next invoice changes, whether prorated, next cycle, or immediately. The design should show current state (Nielsen, heuristic 1: visibility of system status). Fix direction: mark the current plan with a word on the card itself, not just the selection style. Put a short line by the cards about when a switch takes effect and how it's billed. That fact is yours to supply.

**Smaller things.**
- Sections are crowded against the dividers. "Billing details" and "Invoices" sit about 15–20 px below their rules, and the plan cards and the Save button about 14 px above the next rule. That's barely more than the gaps inside a group (label to field, field to next label), so the rules do all the separating. Open up the space between sections so the groups show without the lines (Arnheim, on proximity).
- No field is marked required or optional (Billing email, Invoice address). Mark the required ones with an asterisk and a one-line legend, or mark the optional ones "optional" (Nielsen Norman Group, Budiu, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- The invoice rows give no visible way to open or download an invoice. "CL-3307" etc. aren't styled as links and there's no download control. Getting an invoice PDF is a main reason people visit billing pages, so put it in reach on each row. If the rows are clickable, make that visible. This is common sense, not a cited rule.
- Billing email and Currency share a row but aren't related. Pair fields only when they're short and belong together, or stack them. This is taste.
- Some copy makes a stranger fill gaps (Nielsen, heuristic 2: match the real world):
  - "one pet list" on Salon group: presumably shared pet and owner records across salons, so say what it means for the owner.
  - Salon group doesn't state a groomer limit, though the other two cards do.
  - Dates are "5 Nov 2026" next to a US address and USD; a US reader expects "Nov 5, 2026".

**What's working.**
- **Color restraint.** Teal appears only on the primary button, the checkbox, and the selected plan, so the accent always means "chosen" or "act". Keep it that rare when you add the plan-commit control.
- **The plan cards are well built.** Each has the same three lines (name, limit, price), so they compare at a glance, and the prices line up across the row.
- **The invoice table is set correctly for lookup.** Amounts are right-aligned with equal-width digits, rules are light, there's no zebra striping or boxes, and status is a word rather than a colored chip.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether clicking a plan card applies the change right away. This matters for finding 1.
- Whether the invoice rows are clickable.
- Focus rings on the cards, fields, and buttons.
