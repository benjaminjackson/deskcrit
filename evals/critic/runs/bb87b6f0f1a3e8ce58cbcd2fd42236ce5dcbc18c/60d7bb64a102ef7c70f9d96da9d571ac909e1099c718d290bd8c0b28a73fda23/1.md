**First read.** My eye goes to "Library opening hours" and then drops into a single column of eight lines with exactly the same gap between each. Halfway down I lose track of which branch a line belongs to, because "Saturday, ten to four" sits as close to "Harbour branch" as it does to "Main branch". It looks quiet, clean and municipal. It's easy to trust, but it is slow to scan.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page section where people check, quickly, when a given branch (or the mobile library) is open.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The type is clean, but the even spacing removes the three groups. The hours are also written out in words, without am/pm, and the mobile library has no times at all. So the one thing people come for, "when can I go", takes reading where it should take a glance.

**What would move it most**
1. **The spacing doesn't show the three groups.** All nine lines below the title are about 45 px apart. "Harbour branch" is no nearer its own hours than it is to the Main branch's Saturday line above it. Someone looking for the Harbour hours has to read every line from the top to find where one branch ends and the next begins. This is a defect. Equal gaps erase the groups, and the rule is that space inside a group is clearly smaller than space between groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on proximity). A heading should also sit closer to the text it introduces than to the text before it (Bringhurst 2.2.2: more space above a subhead than below). Fix: pull each branch's lines tight under their heading and open up a clearly larger gap before the next heading. Stacking the three branches as columns, or giving each branch a day/time row layout, would make this even clearer.
2. **The hours are written as words, with no am/pm.** "Monday to Friday, nine to eight" and "Saturday, ten to two" have to be read and converted in the head. Numbers like "9–8" can be taken in at a glance. "Nine to eight" also never says morning or evening. That's obvious for a library, but these are times people act on. This is a defect, and the reasoning is common sense rather than a published rule: opening hours are lookup data, and words slow down lookup. Fix: keep the same facts and switch to numerals with am/pm, e.g. "Monday to Friday, 9 am – 8 pm". Line up the days and times so the eye can run down a column.
3. **The mobile library entries give a place but no time.** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" sit under a heading that promises *hours*. A reader can't tell whether to turn up at 9 or at 3, and someone who guesses wrong misses the van for a week. This is a defect, unless the times are given somewhere off this page. Fix: add the time window for each stop. Only the maker has those facts.
4. **It doesn't say when the branches are closed.** Harbour opens "Tuesday to Friday", and nothing says Monday or Sunday are closed. Most people will work it out, but someone scanning for "Monday" finds nothing, not "Closed". This is taste with a practical cost. Consider stating the closed days, or showing all seven days, so an absence can't be misread.

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- The branch headings differ from the hours by weight, not size (bold versus regular at nearly the same size). That's a clean, quiet distinction, and it shows restraint. Keep it when you fix the spacing.
- One sans family, dark gray on white, with a clear left edge and generous margins. It's calm, readable, and free of decoration, which suits a public-service page.
- The title is the only large element, so there's no doubt where to start.
