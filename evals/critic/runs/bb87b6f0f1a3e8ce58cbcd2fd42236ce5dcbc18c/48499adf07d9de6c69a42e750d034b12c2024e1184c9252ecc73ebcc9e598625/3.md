**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline at top left, drops to the gray subhead, and then gets pulled straight down into the row of four blue-outlined cards, which are louder than the "Create your link" button. The right half of the hero is empty white, so the top of the page feels lopsided and unfinished. It reads as a scheduling SaaS landing page for small service businesses: clean and competent, but generic.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing homepage. Its job is to get a small-business owner (dentist, tutor, physio) to click "Create your link", or to check the live example first.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: the offer is clear in one read and the ask is good. Two things hold it back. The hero has nothing on the right showing the product, and the same blue is on nearly everything, so the primary button doesn't stand out. Right now it looks like every other booking-tool template, not like Slotwise.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half of the hero is empty, and the page tips left.** All the weight sits in the left column, and the space from x≈660 to the right edge does nothing. It reads as leftover space, not as a choice. A viewer who is deciding whether to sign up sees no booking link, no calendar and no booking page. They have to take "fill your calendar" on trust, and the only proof is a link they have to click. This is a **defect** for balance (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). It is also a **template tell**: an empty hero visual (unslop). If an image failed to load in the capture, check that first. If not, fix it by putting a real product preview there, such as the booking page a guest sees, so the hero shows the promise the headline makes. That also gives the "See a live example" link something to back it up.
2. **The same blue is on nearly everything, so the main call to action doesn't stand out.** The headline, the four nav links, the "Start free" button, the "Create your link" button, all four card titles, all four card borders, four badges, four "Learn more" links and the rule above the customer names are all the same saturated blue. The 2 px blue card borders take up more blue area than the primary button does, so they compete with it. This is a **template tell** with a real cost (unslop, "Every element highlighted" and "Neon or bright colored borders"). Keep the blue for things you can click, and mainly for the two buttons. Set the headline and card titles in a dark neutral. Turn the card borders into pale hairlines, or drop them and let spacing group the cards. Then "Create your link" becomes the most colorful thing on the screen.
3. **The feature row is stock template.** It's four identical outlined cards, each with an icon, a bold title, a pill badge, two lines of text and "Learn more". Viewers have seen this row on many other product sites, so Slotwise's features look like anyone's. The badges cost the most. "New" and "Beta" are real status labels. "Auto" and "Email" aren't statuses at all, just a second word for the title. With a badge on every card, none of them carries information (unslop, "PRO, New, and Beta badges everywhere": at most one quiet badge per screen). This is a **template tell**. Remove the badges that aren't status, and keep at most one quiet one. The row would also stop looking stock if the cards weren't all the same, for example if the feature that matters most to these customers got more room.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1: the "New" badge squeezes "Shared calendars" onto two lines. That pushes the card's body text and its "Learn more" about 30 px below the other three cards, so the rows don't line up across the four columns (Müller-Brockmann). Removing the badge fixes this.
- The four "Learn more" links all have the same text. Out of context, such as in a screen reader's list of links, you can't tell which goes where (WCAG 2.4.4). Name the feature in each link, or label each one for screen readers.
- The row of customer names (Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets) has no label. It looks like stray gray text under a heavy blue rule, not like proof that these businesses use the product. Give the row a short lead-in that says what these names are, and make the rule a neutral hairline.
- The headline copy is serviceable but could sit on any booking tool's page (taste). The strength of this page is the customer list: dental, tutoring, physio, vets. Letting the subhead speak to that kind of business would make the page sound like it's for them.

**What's working.**
- The ask: "Create your link" names one exact thing to do, and "See a live example" lets viewers judge the product for themselves before they commit. Keep both, and keep the secondary styled as a link.
- The card copy lands on what the reader gets: "so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows", "so the first minutes are useful". Each feature ends on the benefit, not just the mechanism. Keep that.
- The type scale is clean: a large bold headline, a clearly smaller subhead, card titles set apart by weight, and comfortable body text with short lines.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the buttons and links. Whether the empty right half of the hero is a slow-loading image (check in a live browser). How the four-card row reflows on narrower screens.
