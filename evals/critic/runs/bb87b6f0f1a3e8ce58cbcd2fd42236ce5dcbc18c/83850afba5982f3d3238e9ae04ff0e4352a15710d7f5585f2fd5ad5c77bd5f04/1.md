**First read.** The eye lands on the "Plan and billing" title, drops to the one card with a teal border (Full gym), then to the teal "Save changes" button, then down into the invoice table. It reads as the settings page of a gym-management SaaS (Basalt) where the person who runs a climbing gym manages their subscription. It feels calm and orderly, and a little generic.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this lets a gym owner or manager see what they pay, change their plan or billing details, and get past invoices.

**Verdict.** It looks clean and is well built, but it doesn't make clear the one action that costs money. Nothing marks which plan you have now, and nothing says what happens to your bill when you pick another one. The page also leaves out the two facts people usually open a billing page to check.

**What would move it most**

1. **A plan change has no clear commit, no stated outcome, and no "current" marker.** The three plan cards sit in their own band above a rule. "Save changes" and "Cancel" sit in the next band, under the billing fields. A viewer who clicks "Gym group" can't tell whether that switched the plan right away, whether "Save changes" commits it, or whether Save only covers the email and address. Clicking another card moves the teal border and the radio dot. After that, nothing on the page says which plan you're actually paying for. Only the invoice table hints at it. This is the one choice here that changes what the gym is charged ($79 to $159, or a downgrade to a 200-member cap). Yet it uses the same Save button as a typo fix in the address and says nothing about proration, when the change takes effect, or what happens to members over a limit.
   *Defect.* It breaks visibility of system status (Nielsen 1), natural mapping (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*), and common region, because Save is boxed in with the wrong group (Nielsen Norman Group, "Common Region").
   **Fix direction:** label the plan you're on now, for example "Current plan" on the Full gym card. Give the plan change its own action, next to the cards, that names what it does and shows the new price and when it takes effect before the user commits. Keep "Save changes" for billing details only. Only you can supply the proration and effective-date facts.

2. **The page leaves out the payment method and the next charge.** It shows the plan price and past invoices. It doesn't show which card or account is charged, the next charge date, or how much that charge will be. Those are usually why someone opens a billing page. As it stands, they have to work them out from the invoice dates or look somewhere else.
   *Defect* (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). **Fix direction:** add a short summary near the top (payment method, next charge date and amount) with a way to update the payment method.

3. **The invoices are a dead end.** "BS-2093" and the other invoice numbers are plain gray text, like every other cell. Nothing shows that you can open, download, or print an invoice, which is mostly why people come to this list (for an accountant, a reimbursement, or tax time).
   *Defect* if they really aren't links. *Common sense* if they are, because nothing shows it. **Fix direction:** make each invoice openable and downloadable with a visible link or button on its row.

4. **The plan cards describe different things, so they can't be compared.** "One wall, 200 members", then "Unlimited members", then "Up to 5 gyms". Each card switches to a new measure. A viewer can't tell whether Full gym covers more than one wall, or whether Gym group has a member cap per gym. That's exactly the comparison someone thinking about upgrading or downgrading needs to make.
   *Defect* (copy). **Fix direction:** describe each card in the same terms (walls or gyms, and members) so the cards line up. Use the real limits, which only you have.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and Currency sit side by side but aren't a related pair. One column would read top to bottom more cleanly (common sense; the multi-column form source isn't on the approved list).
- In the top nav, "Settings" shows it's the current page only by being slightly darker than the other items. Give it one clear cue, such as an underline or bolder weight (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The borders of the unselected plan cards and of the text inputs look pale. Measure them against white for the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of component edges.

**What's working.**
- The left edge at x≈300 and the right edge at x≈1120 hold for every block: title, cards, fields, buttons, rules, and table. That's why the page feels calm. Keep it.
- The type scale is tight: one title size, one section-heading size, and body and labels, with field labels told apart by weight rather than another size. Every level has a clear job.
- The Amount column is right-aligned with matching figures, so the amounts scan as numbers. Status uses the word "Paid", not color alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What clicking a different plan card actually does (switches immediately, or waits for Save). Finding 1 rests on this, so check it in the live product.
- Focus indicators on the cards, fields, and buttons.
- Whether changing Currency also changes the plan prices shown in dollars.
