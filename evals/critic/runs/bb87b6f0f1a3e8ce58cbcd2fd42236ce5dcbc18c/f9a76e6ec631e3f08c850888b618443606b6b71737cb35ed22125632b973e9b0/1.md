**First read.** The eye goes to the page title, then straight to the teal "Save changes" button, the only saturated spot on the page, and then up to the outlined Studio card. It reads as the billing settings page of a booking tool for tattoo studios: calm, tidy, well built. Then the first question comes: if I click Studio or Multi-studio, does "Save changes" switch my plan and charge me?

**What it's for.** My assumption: a studio owner uses this page to check and change their plan, keep billing details current, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid, but the page doesn't do its main job well. It never says which plan you're on now, what changing it does, or which button makes the change. Before anyone acts on a price, they need a clear answer.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan has no action and no status of its own, so the one Save button seems to commit everything.** "Save changes" sits under the Billing details fields, two sections below the plan cards. With no action of its own, the plan section seems to be committed by the same button. Clicking Multi-studio and saving could raise the bill from $39 to $89 a month, and nothing near the cards says so. Nothing says when the change takes effect, whether you're charged now or at the next renewal, or what happens to the current month. Nothing labels Studio as the current plan either. The teal outline and the filled radio could mean "your plan" or "what you just picked". The invoices suggest it's the current plan, but you have to work that out. This is a defect. A control should sit next to what it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping), and the page should show its current state (Nielsen, heuristic 1). Fix: mark the current plan in words. Give a plan change its own action at the plan cards, one that states the new price and when it starts. Leave "Save changes" to commit only the billing fields.
2. **The billing page leaves out what billing pages are for.** No payment method is shown and there's no way to change it. There's no next charge date or amount. The invoice rows can't be opened or downloaded. The owner who comes here to check which card is charged, see what's due next, or get last month's invoice for their accountant will find none of it. This is a defect: what the user needs should be on the screen, not remembered or hunted for elsewhere (Nielsen, heuristic 6). Fix: add the payment method and the next charge where the user will look for them. Make each invoice row open or download its invoice. I'm assuming these exist somewhere in the product; the maker knows the real details.
3. **The plan cards can't be compared.** Guest spot lists artists and bookings. Studio lists artists and online deposits. Multi-studio lists studios and a shared client list. Each card names different limits, so the owner can't tell whether Studio caps bookings or how many artists Multi-studio allows. Choosing between plans is the whole point of these cards, and the viewer ends up guessing. This is a defect, and it's in the copy. Fix: give every card the same attributes in the same order, using only facts the maker has. For example, every card states artists, bookings, and deposits.

**Smaller things.**
- Four badges on one screen (New on two sidebar items, Beta in the top nav, PRO on Multi-studio). PRO on one paid tier among three paid tiers tells the viewer nothing. Keep at most one quiet badge, and drop PRO. This is a template tell (Sokolovsky, unslop).
- Required fields aren't marked in Billing details. Put an asterisk on the required ones, or "optional" on the others (Nielsen Norman Group, Budiu).
- Billing email and Currency sit side by side but aren't a related pair. Currency also affects how prices read, so it probably belongs nearer the plan. This is taste: a single column reads in one order.

**What's working.**
- The restraint. One neutral range plus one teal used only for the primary button, the selected card, and the checkbox, so the action is easy to find. Keep the accent this scarce.
- The invoices table is set properly: right-aligned amounts in matching-width figures, light row rules, and plain column labels. It reads at a glance.
- Clear location: the sidebar's tinted "Plan and billing" row, the darker "Settings" in the top nav, and a matching page title. You always know where you are.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus indicators on the plan cards, fields, and buttons. Check them with a keyboard in a live browser.
- What "Save changes" actually does when a different plan is selected. Check before shipping, because finding 1 depends on it.
