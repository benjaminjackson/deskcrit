**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing", then moves straight to the one card with a teal outline, Small fleet, then down to the teal Save changes button. It reads right away as the billing settings for a food-truck ordering product. It's calm, plain, and orderly, with nothing loud. The page looks finished and trustworthy. Where it falls short is what it tells the owner, not how it looks.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a food-truck owner or manager comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are sound, but as a billing page it leaves out the facts someone needs before spending money. It doesn't show which plan they're on, when they'll next be charged or how, or what happens if they switch. The biggest problem is that you can't tell what picking a different plan card actually does.

**What would move it most**
1. **Picking a plan doesn't show when it takes effect.** The three plan cards sit in their own section, with a rule below them. Save changes sits under Billing details, after the receipt checkbox. A user who clicks Commissary can't tell whether that upgrades them on the spot, waits for Save, or what Save covers. This is a choice that changes what they pay, so reading it the wrong way is costly. One user thinks they've upgraded and never saved. Another saves an address change and finds they've also moved to $129 a month. Defect: the control should sit next to what it changes (Norman, natural mapping), and the rule splits the plan choice from the button that commits it. Fix direction: give the plan change its own commit right at the cards, such as a button that names the plan and its price, or make it plain that Save covers both sections.
2. **The page leaves out the billing facts people come here for.** No card is marked as the current plan. The teal one is only "selected", and you have to check the invoice table to learn it's the one you pay for now. The page also doesn't show the next charge date, the payment method on file, or how a mid-month switch is billed. Owners will leave this page to find those answers, or contact support. Defect (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall; Nielsen 10, help close to a complex step). Fix direction: label the current plan on its card. Put the next charge and the payment method near the top of the page. Add one short line at the plan cards saying how a switch is billed. The policy has to come from you.
3. **The plan descriptions don't compare like with like.** One truck says "One menu, 300 orders a day". Small fleet says "Up to 5 trucks, shared menu". Commissary says "Up to 20 trucks and one kitchen". Each line measures something different, so a viewer can't tell whether Small fleet also limits orders or whether Commissary allows more than one menu. This is the exact moment they're deciding whether to pay more. Defect (copy). Fix direction: give each card the same few facts in the same order, such as trucks, menus, and orders a day. You'll need to supply the limits the cards don't state.
4. **Invoices can't be opened.** TR-4471 and the other invoice numbers are set as plain table text. There's no link styling and no download control, so nothing on the page suggests you can get the invoice itself. For bookkeeping, that file is the reason to visit this section. Defect: anything clickable should look clickable (NN/g, Moran). If the numbers already link, style them as links. If they don't, add a way to view or download each invoice.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and Currency sit side by side, but they aren't a related pair. Currency could go on its own row, or next to something it relates to. This is taste: a top-to-bottom form is easier to scan.
- The current item in the top nav ("Settings") is shown only by slightly darker text. That's weaker than the gray tint marking the sidebar item. Match the two treatments.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight: a page title, section headings, then labels and body, all in one family, with weight doing the finer work. Keep it.
- The invoice table is clean. Amounts are right-aligned with figures that line up, the rules are light, and there's no extra chrome. That's the right way to show a short list of exact values.
- There's one teal primary button with an outlined Cancel next to it. The accent appears only on the selected card, the checkbox, and Save, so it always means "this is chosen or this commits".

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What clicking a plan card actually does: whether it changes the plan at once or waits for Save. Check this in the live product, since finding 1 depends on it.
- Focus states on the cards, fields, and buttons.
