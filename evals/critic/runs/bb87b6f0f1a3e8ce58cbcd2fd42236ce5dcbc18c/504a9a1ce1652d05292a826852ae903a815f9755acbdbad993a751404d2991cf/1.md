**First read.** The eye lands on "Walking routes," drops to three identical white boxes, and then goes to the three blue "View route" buttons, which are the loudest things on the page. It reads as a tidy but anonymous list of three local walks, closer to an admin screen than an invitation to go outside. Then you notice that each card's content fills only the top half of the card, and the bottom 200 px of every card is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page where someone picks one of three walks and opens its details, on a desktop site.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and works, but it doesn't help anyone choose. The facts that decide a walk are buried in a sentence of prose and differ from card to card, so the viewer has to read all three to compare them. The empty bottoms of the cards make the page look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **You can't compare the walks at a glance.** *(Defect. This is common sense, not a cited rule.)* Each card gives its facts in a different sentence: the Cliff path has length, climbs and a cafe; the Woodland loop has length, flat, shade and benches; the Canal towpath has length, paved and bikes. Lengths are spelled out ("Six kilometres") inside the sentence, so the eye can't jump from number to number. The terrain facts don't line up either. Is the towpath flat? Is the woodland loop paved? Can you cycle the cliff path? Someone choosing a walk has two or three questions (how far, how hard, what's underfoot) and has to read every word to answer them, and for some routes the answer isn't there. Fix direction: pull the deciding facts out of the prose into the same short line in the same spot on every card, with distances as numerals (for example, "6 km" pulled from the existing copy). Answer the same questions for every route. If time or difficulty matters to these walkers, only you have those facts, so add them consistently. Keep the sentence for character (the cafe, the beech trees), not for data.

2. **The cards are about twice as tall as what's in them.** *(Defect.)* Each card ends around y=600, but its content stops at the button around y=394. That leaves a 200 px empty band at the bottom of all three cards, and each card is top-heavy (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). This isn't white space doing work, which Ruder describes as open areas set in tension against dense ones (*Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). It's leftover space inside a frame. It reads as something that didn't load, and it pushes the page's weight upward. If a map or photo is meant to fill that band, check it in a live browser and judge again with it in place. If not, size the cards to their content.

3. **Nothing on the page is about walking.** *(Taste.)* Slate-gray borders, cool gray body text and one saturated corporate blue would fit an invoicing tool just as well. Itten's point (*The Art of Color*) is that color should carry the mood of the subject, and here it carries none. Three bordered boxes, each with a bold title, a gray paragraph and a filled button, is a layout viewers have seen on many products, so these walks look like anyone's. The fix isn't decoration. Give each route something of its own: a picture or route shape if you have one, or a palette that belongs outdoors. Let one thing on the page come from the walks themselves.

**Smaller things.**
- In the Cliff path card, "end." sits alone on the last line (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Rebreak the line or edit the copy so the last line holds more than one short word.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean. Three sizes (page title, card title, body) are clearly apart, and the bold weight does the rest. Keep it.
- The grid is strict: equal columns and gutters, a shared left edge for the title and cards, and consistent padding inside each card. It will hold up when you add facts or pictures.
- The descriptions are concrete and specific ("benches every few hundred metres," "a cafe at the far end"). That's the right raw material. It's just being used to carry data it's bad at carrying.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the "View route" buttons, and whether the whole card is clickable or only the button.
- Whether the empty band at the bottom of each card is waiting for an image or map that didn't load.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 6 yes, 2 no (every element does a job; color carries the mood)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (empty space gathered; visual weight balances)
Text: 30 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (no widows or runts)
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: skipped, no pictures
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 31 yes, 1 no (puts what the user needs in front of them), 1 unsure (focus indicator)
Template tells: 10 lines, 10 yes
