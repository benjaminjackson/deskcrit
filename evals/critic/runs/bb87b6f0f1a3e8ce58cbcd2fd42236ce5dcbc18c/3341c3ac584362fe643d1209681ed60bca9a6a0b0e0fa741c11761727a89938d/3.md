**First read.** The eye goes straight to the black headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then down through the paragraph to the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a local ferry operator's homepage, for day-trippers and commuters. It feels calm and trustworthy, but also bare, almost like a wireframe. All the content sits in a narrow block in the upper left, and the right side and bottom of the frame are empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero of a ferry company's website. It should tell a visitor what the crossing is like and send them to the timetable.

**Verdict.** The message and the path to the one action are clear, and the type is set with care. Two things hold it back. The copy holds back the one fact a ferry passenger needs, which is when the boats go. And the layout leaves almost half the screen empty, so the piece looks unfinished rather than calm.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy describes the schedule but doesn't give it.** "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" in the paragraph tells the visitor how often boats run, not when. They can't plan a trip from it, so everyone has to click "See the timetable" to answer the most basic question. A passenger holds the operator to a departure time, so a vague one here is a real cost. The direction: put the real first and last sailings in place of "early morning" and "late evening", or show the next departure from the north landing right on the page. Then the button is for detail, not for the basics. Only the maker has those times. (Common sense, not a published rule. The source that states it isn't on my reading list.) Defect.
2. **The page tips to the upper left.** The text column ends at about x=790 on a 1280-wide frame, and the button ends about 230 px above the bottom edge. Nothing on the right or along the bottom balances the weight of the headline and paragraph. The empty space doesn't read as a choice. It reads as a missing picture or a layout that wasn't finished (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The copy invites the viewer to "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck", yet nothing on the page shows the bay or the boat. Give the right side something with weight that belongs to this piece, such as a picture of this crossing or the next-departure information from finding 1. Or, if the page is meant to be text only, scale and place the column so the empty space looks chosen. If a hero image was supposed to load here, this finding falls away; see below. Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "twelve / calm minutes", which splits the number from its unit (Bringhurst, 2.4.6). Rebreak it so "twelve calm minutes" stays on one line, for example "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes".
- The three lines from "Bicycles travel free" to "Hot drinks on board" look a size or two smaller than the paragraph above them, close enough to look like a mistake rather than a separate level (Bringhurst, 3.1.1). Set them at the paragraph size, or mark them as a list by weight or bullets instead of a near-identical size.

**What's working.**
- There is one obvious place to start. The headline is clearly the largest and darkest thing on the page, and "twelve calm minutes" is a concrete promise in the passenger's own terms, not generic copy.
- The palette is restrained. A single blue is used only on the company name and the one button, so the action stands out without any decoration.
- The paragraph is set well. Its lines run about 68 characters, with comfortable line spacing, a clean flush-left edge, and one shared left margin for every element.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is the design or a hero image that didn't load. Check it in a live browser.
- The button's hover and focus states.
