**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", and then drops past the orange button to the purple box below it. The purple box is bigger, darker, and has more text, so it wins. After that the eye goes to the teal panel on the right, which holds two outline chat bubbles and nothing else. It reads as a landing page for a language-learning app: friendly and clean, but loud in three colors and generic in its one picture.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the top of a marketing page meant to turn a first-time visitor who wants to speak a new language into someone who starts the free week.

**Verdict.** The headline and the three-step strip are strong, but the hero doesn't send people to its one action. The purple streak box outweighs the "Start your free week" button, and the teal panel uses the best spot on the page to show nothing about the product.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple "Keep your streak going" box takes attention from the button it sits under.** It is a solid saturated block, larger than the button and set right below it, with white bold text, so it looks like a second, bigger call to action. The eye goes from the button to the box and then has nowhere to act, because the box asks for nothing. Its message is also for people who already use the app, about keeping a streak, on a page aimed at people who haven't started. A first-time visitor gets a promise ("ten weeks") followed by a demand ("practice five days in a row") before they've clicked anything. Defect: this breaks the one-primary-action convention (Material Design 3, "Buttons"; Apple HIG) and the eye path from headline to action. Fix: make "Start your free week" the only filled, saturated element in the left column. Either cut the streak box from the hero or move it down the page as plain text with no fill, near the steps it relates to. If the completion stat is meant to persuade, it can sit as a quiet line under the button.

2. **The hero picture is an empty stand-in.** The teal panel holds two blank speech bubbles. Anyone could have drawn this for any chat, messaging, or support product, so it tells the viewer nothing the word "conversation" hadn't already told them. This is a template tell (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"). The viewer has seen it on many other products, so the page looks like anyone's. It also wastes the page's best evidence: the product's claim is a tutor that listens and gives feedback on each word, and the viewer can't see that anywhere. Defect, because the right half of the hero carries no information. Fix: put in a picture of the product itself, such as a lesson screen or the per-word feedback the "2. Speak" step promises, so the picture shows what the headline claims instead of repeating it.

3. **Three strong colors pull against each other.** Orange (button), purple (streak box), and teal (panel) are each fully saturated and fill large areas. The orange no longer means "act here", because it's just one loud block among three. Convention: a limited palette with one accent kept for the main action (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*; Apple HIG, "Color"). Fixing finding 1 removes the purple. Then calm the teal, for example by letting the product picture sit on a light ground, so orange is the only strong color on the page.

**Smaller things.**
- The teal panel ends at about x=1076, but the nav and the right step column run to about 1224. Its left edge (about 676) also lines up with none of the step columns below (461, 867), so the right side looks unfinished and the hero sits off the page's grid. Make the panel span to the right margin, or size it to the step columns.
- Balance: once the streak box is there, the left column runs down to about y=500 while the right column stops at 410 with empty space beside it, so the hero leans left. Sizing the picture to the column (previous line) fixes this.
- The white bold label on the orange button looks like roughly 4:1 contrast. At about 18 px bold, it falls just short of WCAG 1.4.3's large-text size, so it needs 4.5:1. Measure it, and darken the orange slightly if it falls short.
- Copy: "practice with a tutor that listens" doesn't say whether the tutor is a person or software. The viewer will decide it for themselves, and that changes what they expect from the free week. Name which it is, in the maker's own terms.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a concrete, checkable promise in plain words with a time frame ("ten weeks"). It's set large and tight, and it clearly comes first.
- The three-step strip (Listen, Speak, Use it) explains the lesson in a few seconds. It uses one consistent pattern: a rule, a bold numbered label, and a single line of plain text. Keep that restraint.
- The subhead names the four languages, so a visitor knows right away whether theirs is offered.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button and nav links; how the two-column hero reflows on a phone, where the streak box could end up sitting above the fold in place of the button.
