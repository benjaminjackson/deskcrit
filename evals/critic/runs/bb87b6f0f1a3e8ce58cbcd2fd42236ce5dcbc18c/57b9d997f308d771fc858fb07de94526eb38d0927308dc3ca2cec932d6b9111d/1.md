The space on the right can take it, since nothing sits there. *Convention, and a mild defect for longer pages.*

**Smaller things.**
- The section headings ("Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant", "Watering") differ from the body text only in size. They use the same regular weight and the same near-black. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8–9) argues for pairing a smaller size step with a change of weight or color. A medium weight would let the headings come down a size and still stand out. This matters less than usual because your three sizes are already well spaced.
- "last year's heap" uses a straight apostrophe. Use the curly one (’). At body size it's barely visible. This is common sense, not a sourced rule.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing.** Each heading sits about 8 px above its own paragraph and about 40 px below the paragraph before it. Each heading clearly belongs to the text it introduces, so the page reads as four separate steps. Keep that ratio.
- **A tight type scale.** The page uses three sizes with large steps between them (title, section heading, body), and each size has an obvious job. Don't add a fourth.
- **The copy is specific and leaves the reader no gaps.** "Six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "twice a week, rather than a little every day", and named crops ("radishes, lettuce, and bush beans") each tell the reader exactly what to do. Every line has a verb and a measurable action.
