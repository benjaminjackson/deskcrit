**Verdict.** The page reads well, but the signup can't do its job: it asks for a name and a landing but never asks where to send the note. Second, the illustration is a few generic blocks that show none of what the text describes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The signup collects no way to reach the person.** In the card at right, the fields are "Your name" and "Home landing." Underneath, the card promises "We send one short note when the timetable changes." Nothing on the form says how the note arrives. A rider who fills it in either stops and wonders where the note goes, or signs up and never gets anything. The form doesn't let them build a correct picture of what happens next (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, conceptual model). **Defect.** Fix: add the contact field the service actually uses, such as email or a phone number for text messages. Then make the helper line name that channel, so the promise and the field match.
2. **The picture shows nothing the text talks about.** In the figure under the first paragraph, a gray slab, a pale box and a blue bar sit on a band of light blue. The text and caption describe wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, a kiosk, and "the new north landing, seen from the water at low tide." The reader can see none of that, so the caption reads as describing a picture that isn't there. The page's one image becomes the stock "generic box" move (Sokolovsky, unslop) when it should be the evidence for the article. **Defect.** Fix: use a real photo of the north landing, or at least a drawing that shows the ramp meeting the deck and the covered area. The image should prove the article's claim that prams and wheelchairs roll straight on.
3. **The boat looks like a second button.** In the illustration, the blue rounded rectangle uses the same blue and corner radius as "Sign up." Things that look alike get read as related (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). Here that creates a false link between a drawn boat and the page's one action, and it weakens the button as the only blue thing to press. **Defect.** Fix: once the picture is replaced this goes away. Until then, keep the brand blue for the action only.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Home landing" field is free text, so riders will type names, abbreviations and misspellings the system can't match. Make it a picker of the landings (Nielsen, heuristic 5, error prevention).
- Neither field says whether it's required. On a short form this costs little, but mark the required fields, or put "optional" after the label of any field that is (Budiu, NN/g).

**What's working.**
- The headline and the closing line work together. "The ramps now meet the deck at every tide, so prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" lands the rebuild on the rider: it says what they can now do. Keep that line and its "so."
