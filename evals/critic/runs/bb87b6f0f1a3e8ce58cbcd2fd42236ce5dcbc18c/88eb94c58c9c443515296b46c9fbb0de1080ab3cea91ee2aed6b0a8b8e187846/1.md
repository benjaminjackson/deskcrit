**First read.** The eye lands on the bold sans heading at top left, then drops into four short serif paragraphs. It reads as a plain information page for people taking a river ferry: calm, quiet, a bit like a notice pinned in a shelter. The second thing you notice is the deep indent at the start of every paragraph, the first one included.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that tells ferry passengers how the service runs, so they can plan a crossing and know the rules on board.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is mostly sound and the page is easy to read, but it doesn't give a passenger what they need to plan a trip. The times are all vague phrases, and the information people act on is spread through prose. A smaller typesetting fault, paragraphs marked twice, is the most visible flaw.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule doesn't say when the boat runs.** The facts a passenger acts on are "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening," "one boat each hour" in winter, and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk." None of these gives a clock time. A reader can't tell when the first or last boat leaves, which minute past the hour the forty-minute cycle starts from, or which months count as winter. "The last crossing leaves the quay" can be read two ways: the last boat from the market quay, or the last boat of the day in either direction. Read the wrong way, someone ends up stranded on the north landing at dusk. The page then sends you to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing," so the reader has to be standing at the dock to get what this page should tell them. **Defect.** Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing as times, name the winter dates, and say which direction the last crossing runs. These are facts only you have. Once they're times, pull them out of the paragraph so they can be scanned. A short list or a small table would do it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Paragraphs are marked twice.** Every paragraph has a first-line indent of about 3 em and a blank-line gap above it, and the first paragraph under the heading is indented too. Pick one: keep the space and drop the indents, or keep a 1 em indent and close the gaps. Either way, set the first paragraph after the heading flush left. Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.3.1) states the flush-left opening. "Not both" is standard practice but not a rule I can cite: it's common sense, because the second mark adds nothing. At this size the indent also cuts a visible notch into a narrow column. **Convention.**

**What's working.**
- **The type pairing is clear.** A bold sans heading over a serif text face is a plain contrast of form, not two faces that almost match. Each one has an obvious job.
- **The reading column is comfortable.** Lines run about 70 characters with generous leading, the right edge is ragged without a staircase pattern, and the text is near-black on white. It reads without effort.
- **The page is restrained.** There are no boxes, rules, or decoration, and the empty space sits in one block on the right instead of scattered gaps. Keep that if you add a timetable.
