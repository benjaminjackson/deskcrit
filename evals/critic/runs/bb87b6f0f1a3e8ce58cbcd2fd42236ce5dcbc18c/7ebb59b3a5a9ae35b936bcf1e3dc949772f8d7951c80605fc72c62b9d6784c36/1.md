**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, "Ferns and shade plants for every corner", and then gets pulled away by the wall of lime and green ovals behind it. The ovals cover the whole screen at full strength. After that the eye wanders the pattern and finds the subhead and the outlined "Browse the greenhouse" button last. It reads as the homepage of a small local plant nursery. It feels lush and cheerful, but loud: more like green confetti than ferns or shade.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: the homepage hero of a local nursery. It should tell nearby gardeners what Fernhill sells and get them to start browsing plants that are ready now.

**Verdict.** The headline and copy do their job. The background works against it. A loud, generic pattern of bright ovals fills every pixel, fights the text, and shows nothing the nursery sells. Replacing it is the single change that would help most.

**What would move it most**

1. **The background is a busy pattern with no subject, and every line of text sits right on it.** The ovals fill the whole hero at even, full intensity, so the page has no calm area anywhere. The white headline survives because it is huge and bold. The regular-weight subhead does not: "hill" and "weekend" cross pale lime leaves and break up. The button's thin white outline and label sit on bright leaves and look washed out in places. In grayscale the whole hero turns into a mid-gray mottle that only the headline escapes. Text over a picture has to hold up at the worst point behind it, not on average (WCAG technique F83), and text needs a clear ground (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Figure and ground"). Value should carry the structure (Albers). Large areas of full-strength color tire the eye and weaken emphasis (Itten; Refactoring UI). The fix: give the text a calm area. Use a dark scrim or solid panel behind the text column, or a picture with a quiet region where the text sits. Also turn the pattern way down, darker and less saturated, so the bright green is saved for something that matters. Defect for the subhead and button; convention for the saturation.

2. **The picture fights the headline instead of completing it.** The headline promises ferns and plants for shade. The picture is sunny, saturated, chartreuse ovals: no fern, no shade, no plant you could buy. A nursery's strongest asset is its actual stock. A random leaf pattern could sit on any "green" brand, so the page looks like anyone's and "Grown on the hill" has nothing backing it up. The mood is wrong too: shade plants call for cool, deep, quiet greens, and this is bright and jittery (Itten, *The Art of Color*, on color mood). The fix: show the product, meaning ferns or shade plants from their own greenhouse, in the lower-light palette the headline describes. This is a template tell (unslop: a generic picture as the hero visual) and taste on the mood.

3. **The ask is hard to find and vague.** Because of the pattern, the button is the last thing the eye reaches. A white outline with no fill is the weakest button style there is, and here it sits on the loudest ground on the page. "Browse the greenhouse" also says less than the subhead promises. The subhead's best hook is "ready to plant this weekend", but the button doesn't pick it up or point to a specific case from the shopper's world, such as a kind of shady spot they have. The fix: once the ground is calm, make the button solid so it is clearly the next stop after the headline. Consider tying its label to the "ready this weekend" promise, or to the kind of shady spot the viewer is shopping for. The maker should supply the specific case. Taste on the copy, defect on visibility.

**Smaller things**
- Headline break: "corner" sits alone on the third line. Re-break it or adjust the width so the last line carries more than one word.
- Headline spacing: the gap between "and" and "shade" looks wider than the other word spaces. Check for a double space. At this size the heading could also take slightly tighter word spacing than body text (Bringhurst 2.1.1).

**What's working**
- The headline copy is concrete and local: "Ferns and shade plants for every corner" says what they sell and for whom. "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" adds where the plants come from and when you can have them. Keep both.
- The hierarchy is clear: a big bold headline, a regular subhead clearly smaller, then the button. That's three distinct levels with no near-miss sizes.
- The header is restrained: a dark green wordmark on white, four plain nav labels, and the same green family as the hero. It's calm, readable, and conventional in the right way.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Hover and focus states on the outlined button, which matter more because its outline is so faint on this ground.
- How the text column and pattern hold up on a phone, where the subhead would wrap over more of the texture.
