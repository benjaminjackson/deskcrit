**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, then falls onto the row of four big prices. They all have the same weight, so it moves along them like reading a table row, and it stops nowhere. It reads as a clean, calm SaaS pricing section. On a closer look, Free offers 3 editors and Solo offers 1, which stops you and makes you wonder whether it's a typo.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the pricing section of a collaborative SaaS product. Its job is to get a visitor to choose a plan and start.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and readable, but it doesn't help anyone choose. All four plans look equally right, and the feature ladder contradicts itself on editors, which is the main thing that separates the plans. Both problems slow down the one decision this page exists for.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing tells the viewer which plan is for them.** The four cards, the four prices and the four solid blue buttons ("Start free", "Choose Solo", "Choose Team", "Talk to us") are identical in size, weight and color. The viewer has to compare all four line by line with no cue for where to start. A pricing page that can't point to its main plan leaves people stuck at exactly the moment they should act. Tschichold asks that the order of importance be shown by differences of size. Material Design and Apple's HIG both call for one filled primary button per view, with the other actions set back. *Defect.* Fix: make one plan clearly the first thing seen, probably the one most visitors should buy (Team, if the business is built around studios). Mark it with weight, a stronger card edge or a short label. Keep the solid blue button on that card only and turn the other three into outline or lower-emphasis buttons.

2. **The editor limits break the ladder.** In the "editors" row, Free gives 3, Solo gives 1, Team 15 and Company 100. Every other row only goes up from left to right, so this one looks like a mistake. It also gives a careful reader a reason not to pay: why pay $12 for fewer editors than the free plan? Norman's point about the conceptual model applies here. The viewer reads the tiers as a ladder, and this row breaks it. *Defect* (the copy also looks unfinished). Fix: if Free really allows 3, the card needs to say why (for example, if Solo is single-user by design, its card should say so in words). If it's a typo, correct it. Only you know which is true.

3. **The words that decide the plan aren't explained.** The plans differ mainly by "editors", and the subtitle promises "unlimited viewers". The page never says what an editor can do that a viewer can't. A visitor sizing a team can't count how many editors they need, so they can't pick a tier. Nielsen's tenth heuristic asks for short, in-context help where the task needs it. *Defect.* Fix: put a one-line definition of editor and viewer next to the subtitle or the first "editors" row. Use the product's real terms; don't invent them.

**Smaller things.**
- The empty band of about 70 px under every button inside the cards does nothing, and the cards look taller than their content. Trim the bottom padding to match the top, or use the space for the plan definitions in finding 3. (Common sense.)
- "Full history" on Company and "Named contact" break the pattern of the other rows, which give a number. "Full history" reads well enough. "Named contact" is a category, not something the viewer can picture. Say what it means, such as who it puts the viewer in touch with, if you can do that in a few words.
- "Talk to us" is the vaguest of the four asks. Name what happens when you click it.

**What's working.**
- The scale is disciplined. There are four clear levels: the 32-ish px headline, the very large bold prices, the bold plan names, and the regular feature text. The gray "/ month" and the gray audience lines sit back by color, not by another size. Keep that.
- The feature rows line up across all four cards on the same baselines, with hairline dividers. That makes row-by-row comparison easy, and it's the right structure for this job.
- The one-line audience tags ("For freelancers", "For small studios") are the best copy on the page, because they let a viewer find themselves quickly. Lean on them when you mark the main plan.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Button hover and focus states. How the four columns reflow on a phone (whichever card comes first there becomes the default by accident). The exact contrast of the gray audience lines and "/ month", which look close to the 4.5:1 limit, so measure them.
