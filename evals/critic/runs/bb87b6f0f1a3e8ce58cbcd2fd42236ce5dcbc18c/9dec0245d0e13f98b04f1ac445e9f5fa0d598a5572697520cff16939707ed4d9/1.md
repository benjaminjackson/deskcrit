**First read.** The eye lands on "Fill every market pitch before Saturday", mostly because of the orange-red "before Saturday". It then jumps to the rust F4 tile in the map on the right, and from there to the rust "Plan your next market" button. That rust carries the eye neatly from map to button. Within seconds this reads as booking software for whoever runs a street market. It feels calm, plain and competent, and it has more character than most SaaS heroes because the map is specific: pitch numbers, a footway to the car park, a named bakery, a fee.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page for a tool that market managers use to lay out and fill pitches, and its job is to get a manager to start planning a market.

**Verdict.** This mostly works. The page is clear, well set and specific, and a manager would know what it is in a few seconds. The biggest problem is that the headline promises filled pitches, while nothing on the page (the map, the paragraph or the three features) says how Stallkeep fills an empty pitch.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's promise has nothing behind it.** "Fill every market pitch before Saturday" is a promise about results. The map next to it shows "18 pitches, 3 free", with E3, F6 and G5 empty. The paragraph covers seeing the layout, seeing who paid, and booking by phone. The three features cover regulars, fees and the council. None of it explains how an empty pitch gets a trader. A manager with empty pitches will read the headline, look for the "how", and not find it. The visual and the headline don't make one idea: the picture shows the problem and the page never shows the fix. This is a defect: it slows the exact viewer the headline speaks to. It's also my judgment, since the convention's source isn't on the approved list. Fix direction: if Stallkeep does something to fill free pitches, say so in the paragraph and let the map hint at it, for example in how the free pitches are treated. If it doesn't, change the headline to match what the product actually does: show the layout and take bookings and payment.
2. **The gradient on "before Saturday" is a template move, and it fights the page's own color.** The phrase fades from orange to a pinkish crimson. Neither hue matches the rust of the button and the F4 tile, so the page has two competing accents. Gradient text in a headline is a move viewers have seen on countless AI and SaaS pages (Sokolovsky, unslop). It is the one place this page looks like anyone's, and it sits on the line the page most needs to look like its own. This is a template tell. Fix direction: set the phrase in one solid color. Use the rust if it should stay highlighted, so the headline, the selected pitch and the button share one accent. Or set it in the headline color and let the words do the work.
3. **The paragraph stops talking to the manager partway through.** It opens with "shows *your* market layout", then switches to "the market manager stops taking bookings by text". The reader *is* the market manager, so the one benefit aimed at them is put in the third person and loses its pull. This is a defect in the copy. An editorial fix: "…and you stop taking bookings by text." The ask is also soft. "Plan your next market" doesn't say what happens on click (an account? a layout editor? a trial?). Point it at one concrete thing the manager does first, which only you can name. The secondary link, "Look at a sample layout", offers what the hero already shows. Either say what more the sample gives, such as a full market, or drop the link.

**Smaller things.**
- The map uses "pitch", "footway", "car park" and "council" (British usage) next to "$32 paid". If the audience is UK, the dollar sign will look wrong to them. Check that the currency matches the market you're selling to.

**What's working.**
- **The map as the hero visual.** It's specific (F4, 3 m with power, Rye and Rind Bakery, $32 paid), so it shows the product's real job instead of a generic mockup. Free pitches are marked by being empty and dashed, not by color alone. Keep this.
- **The feature row's copy grounds each benefit in a real chore.** "Nobody walks the rows with a cash bag" and "the layout and trader list it asks for each quarter" compare the product to what the manager does today, which is the strongest kind of claim.
- **Restrained type and hierarchy.** The headline is clearly dominant at a medium weight. The body runs at a comfortable line length (about 60 characters). Below that there are only a few sizes, each with a clear job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the map tiles are interactive (hover, select) and whether the page holds up on a phone. The map's six-across rows are the part most likely to break at narrow widths.
