**First read.** I see the bold title first, then the tall blue Organic search bar. Then my eye moves sideways along a row of six bright colors before it settles on the numbers. It reads as a clean internal growth dashboard: quarterly sign-ups by channel, for a marketing or leadership team. It feels tidy and competent, with the color doing more shouting than the data needs.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal Growth page that tells the team where Q3 sign-ups came from and what changes because of it.

**Verdict.** This is a solid, honest chart that does most of its job. The story lives in a side note the chart doesn't support. The note is about two things: organic search rising since Q2, and podcast ads plus events being cut. The chart shows Q3 alone in six colors that carry no meaning, so nothing on it points at either claim.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't show the change the page is about.** The side note says organic search went "up from a quarter in Q2", and that's the most important line on the page. But the chart has only Q3 bars, so you take the rise on trust. You also can't see whether the other channels fell to make room. Put the comparison next to the number: the Q2 value or the change beside each bar, or a paired bar for Q2 (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). *Defect*: the page's main claim has no evidence.
2. **Six colors encode nothing and bury the point.** The bars are blue, orange, red, teal, green and purple. They're one series, and each bar is already labeled on the axis. The colors make six channels look equally important. They also bring in meanings you don't want: red on Paid social reads as "bad", green on Podcast ads reads as "good", and the note says the opposite about podcasts. Draw all bars in one muted color. Save a single accent for what the note talks about, the Organic search bar, and perhaps a quieter mark for the two channels being cut (Few; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 19.1). *Defect.* This is also a familiar template move, the rainbow bar chart (unslop). It makes the page look like any generated dashboard.
3. **The title names the topic, not the finding.** "New sign-ups by channel" plus the subtitle tell you what the axes are. The finding sits in a 15 px side column that the eye reaches last. Say the takeaway in the title or the subtitle. The words are already on the page: organic search brought a third of new accounts, and podcasts and events are being cut. Or tag those bars with a short note on the chart itself. *Taste*: the source on this point isn't on the approved list, but here the whole point of the page is in the note, so the chart should lead you to it.

**What's working.**
- The bars are sorted by value, start at zero, and carry direct value labels over a faint grid. The chart is honest and easy to read precisely, so keep all of that.
- The source line gives the record, the exact date range, and the total. The figures add up: 5,400 total, 1,840 is a third, and podcasts plus events come to 670, under 700. That earns trust.
- The side-note copy is concrete and lands on a decision ("The events budget moves to referrals in Q4"). That's exactly what a reader of this page needs, and it only needs the chart to back it up.
