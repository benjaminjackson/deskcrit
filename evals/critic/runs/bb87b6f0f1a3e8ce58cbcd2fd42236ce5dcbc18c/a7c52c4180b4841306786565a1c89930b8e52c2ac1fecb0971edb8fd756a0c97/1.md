**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours," then drops down the three bold branch names and the plain lines under each. It reads as a simple information block on a library or council website, for residents checking when they can go. It feels calm, orderly and a little bare. The whole thing sits in the top-left third of the frame, and the rest is white.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or section that lets a local resident check quickly when and where they can visit each library service.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job. It's clear, well grouped and easy to scan. What lets it down is the copy. The mobile library gives no hours at all, and every time is spelled out in words, so the one thing a visitor came for is either missing or slower to read than it should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The mobile library has no times.** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" tell you the day and the place but not when the van arrives or how long it stays. Under a heading that promises opening hours, this is the one entry where you can't plan a visit. You'd turn up at the market square without knowing whether you're hours early or have already missed it. A mobile stop usually lasts only a few hours, so getting this wrong costs a wasted trip. Fix: give each stop its time window, in the same form as the branch lines. Only the maker has those times. *Defect.*
2. **Times are written as words.** "nine to eight," "ten to four," "ten to six," "ten to two." Someone asking "is Harbour open at 5:30?" has to turn each phrase into a number before comparing. "Ten to four" can also be read as the clock time 3:50, especially read aloud or scanned quickly. That's two readings on the line people act on. Numbers are faster to scan and compare down a column, and they remove the "ten to" problem. Fix: write the times as numbers, with a.m./p.m. or the 24-hour clock, e.g. "Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 8 p.m.," and use the same format on every line. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Closed days are only implied by leaving them out: Sunday at both branches, Monday at Harbour. A visitor can't tell whether a missing day means closed or just not listed. If they're closed, say so in a short line under each branch. This is common sense, not a published rule.

**What's working.**
- The grouping is right. The space between branches is about twice the line spacing inside each one, so each bold name clearly belongs to the two lines under it and the three services read as three separate units at a glance. Keep that ratio.
- The type scale is tight and clear: one title size, one body size, and bold for the branch names rather than a third size. That's three levels with no near-duplicates (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- Dark text on white is high contrast, and the body size is easy to read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the column behaves on a phone, and whether the large empty area to the right holds other page content at full width. At desktop, the block hugging the top left is normal for a content page and isn't a defect here.
