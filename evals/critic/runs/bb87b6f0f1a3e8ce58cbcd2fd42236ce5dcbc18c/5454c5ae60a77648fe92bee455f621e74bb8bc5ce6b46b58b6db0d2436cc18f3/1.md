**First read.** The eye lands on the leaf pattern, not the words. The hero is a full-width wall of bright greens, yellows, and red dots, and the white headline is buried in it. I had to work to read "Plants for shade and small spaces." After that the eye drops to the calm white lower half: a clear lead sentence and three tidy info cards. It reads as a friendly local garden center's home page, and it feels cheerful and honest. But the loudest part of the page says nothing, and the page never asks me to do anything.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of an independent garden center, meant to get local people to come in (or order for delivery) for shade and small-space plants this weekend.

**Verdict.** The copy and the lower half are good. The hero is working against them: the headline is hard to read on the pattern, the pattern shows none of the plants the page is selling, and nothing on the page turns interest into a visit or an order.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is hard to read on the pattern.** This is a defect. The white headline at the left of the hero sits right on pale yellow and white leaves and white dots. Up close, the end of "small" runs into a pale leaf, and the period after "spaces" merges with a white dot. The page's one statement takes a second read, and on a phone or in sunlight it will be worse. Text over an image has to hold its contrast at the worst point behind it, not on average (W3C, Techniques for WCAG 2.2, F83). The fix: put a dark scrim or a solid panel behind the headline, or calm the pattern where the text sits, so every letter stands clear.
2. **The pattern outshouts the message and shows the wrong thing.** This is a defect, plus a template tell. The hero takes about half the screen at full saturation, so it wins the first look over the headline (Tschichold and Hofmann: one element has to lead, and here it's the decoration). It's also generic confetti of sunny, mixed leaves. It shows no fern, no hosta, no shade, no small space. So the picture and the headline don't make one idea together (taste, but the cost is plain). A decorative pattern as the hero is a stock move (Sokolovsky, unslop: the hero should be an image from the product's world). The viewer has seen this kind of hero on many sites, so the page looks like anyone's, and this nursery's own stock (the thing that sets it apart) is invisible. The fix: replace the pattern with the actual plants, ideally in a shady corner or a small space, or at least tone the pattern way down so the headline leads.
3. **There's no ask.** This is a defect. The page ends on three information cards. There's no button and no link in the content: nothing to see the plants, book a workshop, or get directions. "Ten places in each class" is good copy that creates urgency, but the viewer can't act on it here. The lead line, "ready to pot this weekend", sets up a visit, and the page then leaves the viewer to find the right nav link on their own. The fix: add one primary action that follows from the headline (Material Design and Apple HIG: one primary action per view, everything else lower). For example, browse the shade plants, or plan a visit. Give the workshop card a lower-emphasis link of its own. Name one exact thing, not "Learn more."

**Smaller things.**
- The headline starts at about x=72, while the logo, the lead sentence, and the cards all start at x=56. That's a 16 px near-miss, so it reads as an error, not a choice (Arnheim). Align it to the 56 px edge.
- The headline breaks "small / spaces.", which splits the phrase and leaves one word alone on line two. Break it as "Plants for shade / and small spaces", or widen the measure so it fits on one line.
- In the Opening hours card, "until 8 / pm" splits the number from its unit (Bringhurst 2.4.6). Use a non-breaking space.
- "Delivery" and "Opening hours" give the terms but not where. The nav says "Visit", but no address or town appears on the screen. If the job is a visit this weekend, the address belongs near the hours. You have to supply it.

**What's working.**
- The lead sentence, "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, grown at our nursery and ready to pot this weekend", is specific, local, and timely. It names real plants, says where they come from, and says when. Keep it as it is.
- The three cards each answer one practical question in plain, complete sentences with real numbers ("8 am to 6 pm", "ten places", "over $60", "ten miles"), plus one nice extra (the plant care card). The headings name exactly what each card holds.
- The type plan is restrained and clear: a serif logo in a deep green against a plain sans for everything else, few sizes, and generous margins. The lower half feels calm and trustworthy, so keep that while you fix the hero.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the nav marks the current page or shows a visible focus ring. The hero headline's contrast also needs checking on narrow screens, where the pattern will sit behind more of the text.
