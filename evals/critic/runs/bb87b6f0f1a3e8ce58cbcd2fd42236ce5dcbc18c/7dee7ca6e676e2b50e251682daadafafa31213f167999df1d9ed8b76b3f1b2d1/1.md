**First read.** My eye goes first to the row of four big numbers, then to the blue dot on the map and the blue Friday bar, which carry the only color on the page. This looks like an ops dashboard for a city bike-share. Calm, tidy and easy to trust. But it takes a moment to see that one station is empty right now and a van is on its way, which is the most urgent thing on the screen. The two map callouts float without pointing at anything, so you have to work out where Canal Basin is and where the van is.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a daily ops view for the fleet manager or dispatcher. They come to it to see what needs action now (empty stations, bikes in repair) and how today compares with normal.

**Verdict.** It's clean and well made, but it shows reporting first and action second. The four equal stat tiles lead, and the one live problem is spread across a map whose callouts aren't attached to anything and a table you can't act on. On top of that, every comparison is against "last Monday" on a Tuesday, so the context numbers can't be trusted.

**What would move it most**
1. **The urgent item doesn't lead, and its story is split across two cards.** Four stat tiles of the same size and weight open the page, and no tile stands out (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size). The thing that needs action is "Canal Basin, 0 bikes, empty 40 min." It appears as a callout inside the map card and again as a row in "Stations to restock" at the bottom. Neither copy says what's being done about it, except the van callout, which is a third place. A dispatcher has to connect three spots to answer "is this handled?" Related items split across boxes read as separate things (NN/g, common region). Direction: let restocking lead. Merge the map and the restock list into one unit, near the top. Each station row should show how long it has been empty and which van is assigned. The KPI row can move down and get quieter. *Defect.* The row of four identical tiles with no lead is also the stock dashboard opener (unslop, "Equal cards side by side"), so the page reads like any admin template until you reach the map.
2. **The map callouts don't point at anything.** In the "Stations now" card, the "Canal Basin" box sits at top left with no leader line or tail. The only blue (empty) dot is in the middle, about 150 px away. "Van route 2, 12 bikes on board" sits on a road at bottom right, and no van marker appears anywhere. The viewer has to guess that the blue dot is Canal Basin and can't see where the van is or how far away it is. On a map, that's the whole point. Direction: anchor each callout to its spot with a tail or leader, and give the van its own marker. *Defect.*
3. **The comparisons are against the wrong day, and "Rides" gives no time window.** The date reads "Today, 24 November 2026," a Tuesday. All three comparison lines say "last Monday" ("2,960 last Monday," "16 min last Monday," "41 last Monday"). Either the label is wrong or the baseline is, and both readings hurt. If the numbers really are from Monday, the manager is comparing a weekday against a different weekday and will misjudge whether today is busy. "Rides 3,184" also doesn't say whether it's today so far or a full day. Few ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design") asks for the unit, the period and a comparison next to every headline number. Direction: name the period on each number ("today so far," for example) and compare like with like ("last Tuesday," or the usual Tuesday). Only the maker knows the right baseline. *Defect.*
4. **"Rides by weekday" doesn't say which week, has no scale, and highlights Friday for no stated reason.** Only Friday has a value (3,410). The other six bars have no numbers and no axis, so you can't read Monday's figure and compare it with the 2,960 above (Tufte, ch. 2: the reader should never have to guess what a number measures). The title doesn't say whether this is last week, this week (where Saturday and Sunday haven't happened yet), or an average. Friday is in the accent blue, but nothing says why: busiest day? It isn't today. Direction: name the period in the title, add a light axis or a value on every bar, and either say what the highlight means or highlight today instead. Putting the finding in the title would help too ("Fridays are busiest"); that's my judgment, not a cited rule. *Defect.*
5. **Blue means two different things.** On the map, blue is "Empty," a problem state. In the chart, the same blue marks a highlighted bar. In this palette blue is the only signal color, so a viewer scanning for problems sees Friday light up like an alert. "Empty" is also shown by color alone, with no icon or label on the dot (WCAG 1.4.1). Direction: give the problem state its own warning color and a second cue such as a label or a ring, and keep the brand blue for emphasis. *Convention* (Apple HIG and Material color roles; WCAG 1.4.1).

**Smaller things.**
- The restock table has no action: no assign, dispatch or "view on map" control. The screen shows the problem but nothing to do about it. Give each row (or the table) one clear next step.
- The table leaves out what a dispatcher needs to decide, like time empty or van assigned, so they have to read it off the map callout. Bring those columns into the table.
- In the sidebar, the "Rides" icon is a shopping cart and "Repairs" is a document. Both suggest something else; use icons that match bikes and repairs.
- With no search or filter on the stations table or the map, finding a station gets slow once the list is longer than three rows.
- "Stations now" also shows a van, so the title undersells the card. Something like "Stations and vans now" would fix that.

**What's working.**
- The restraint. A neutral gray page, one accent color and no stock effects, so color can carry meaning once you fix finding 5.
- Every KPI tile has a comparison line under it, in one steady style (label, big number, gray context line). Keep that pattern and fix the baseline.
- The table is set well: numbers right-aligned under right-aligned headers, light dividers, enough row height. It reads at a glance.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether table rows, map dots and the KPI tiles can be clicked. Nothing in the image shows they can, and for a dispatcher that matters.
- Hover and focus states on the sidebar and map.
