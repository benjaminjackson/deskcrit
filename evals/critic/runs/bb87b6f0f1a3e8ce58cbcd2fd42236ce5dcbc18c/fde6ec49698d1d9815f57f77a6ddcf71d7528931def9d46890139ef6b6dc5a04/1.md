**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, "See where every dollar goes this month", then drops to the subhead and the two buttons. Then it drifts right into a field of empty blue, finds nothing, and falls to the three feature columns. It reads as a consumer budgeting app's landing page: confident, clean, and a bit bare. The empty right half makes it feel unfinished rather than calm.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing page for a personal-budgeting app, meant to get people to start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The copy and the type mostly do their job. The page fails at showing anything. It promises to let you "see where every dollar goes" but shows nothing, and the empty right half of the hero is the biggest problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero's right half is an empty blue field where the product should be.** About 450 px of saturated blue to the right of the headline holds nothing. All the weight sits on the left, so the band looks lopsided (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The empty space reads as left over, not chosen. The bigger cost is the promise. The headline says you will *see* your money, and the viewer has to take that on trust because no screen, chart or category view backs it up. With a finance app, people want to see what they're handing their accounts to. If an image belongs there and failed to load, this is a capture problem, so check it live. If the space is empty by design, it's a defect. It's also a familiar template move: an empty hero visual looks like anyone's page (unslop, "Empty box or generic picture as the hero visual"). Fix: put a real preview of the product in that space, ideally the monthly view the headline describes: categories, limits, and the warning before you go over. Then the headline and the picture make one point together. *Defect.*
2. **"Try it free" doesn't say what the viewer is committing to.** It's the main ask, and it doesn't say for how long, or whether it needs a card or needs you to connect a bank first. Those are exactly what someone hesitating over a money app holds the maker to. "Free" could mean a free plan or a trial that converts to paid, and someone who reads it the wrong way will feel misled later. Fix: name the terms on or right beside the button. Only the maker knows them. The page's strongest trust line, "Read-only access … can never move money", is buried in the third feature column. It answers the real worry behind clicking, so move it near the buttons. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The headline is set extra-bold, around 800. That's a stock template move, and the 56 px size already carries the rank, so a lighter weight would keep the hierarchy and look less like every other SaaS hero (unslop, "Headings at weight 800 or 900"). *Taste/convention.*

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and in the viewer's terms, "every dollar … this month", and the subhead uses three verbs to say who does what: connect, set a limit, get a heads-up. Keep that plain wording.
- The feature copy says what happens, not a category name: "you can fix any of them in one tap", "can never move money". Each line ends on what it means for the viewer.
- The layout is disciplined. There's one left edge at 64 px from the logo down through the feature columns, three equal columns, and a clear primary/secondary button pair: a filled white button and an outlined one.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The bottom ~200 px is blank white. This might be the end of the capture or a section that hadn't loaded. Check it in a live browser.
- Whether the right half of the hero is meant to hold an image or animation that hadn't loaded.
