**What it's for.** You gave no context, so I'm assuming this is a hero or poster announcing a free ferry ride at a city harbor open day, meant to get the public to turn up.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job yet. The headline is so over-tightened that letters collide and the words lose their shapes. The copy also never says when the open day is, so a reader who's sold on it can't act.

**What would move it most**
1. **Headline letters collide (defect).** Across both headline lines, neighboring capitals touch and merge: LL in ALL, D and T across "ABOARD THE", NN and IN in MORNING, RR in FERRY. The word spaces have nearly closed too. Reading in all caps already depends on clear letter shapes. Here the reader sees dark slabs and has to decode MORNING letter by letter. At poster distance it would blur into a black bar. Large type does want tighter spacing than text (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80; Bringhurst 2.1.1), but this goes past tight into collision. Hochuli is blunt about it: "individual letters should not touch" (*Detail in Typography*, p. 30). Capitals in fact want some letterspacing, not less (Bringhurst 2.1.6). **Fix:** open the tracking until no pair touches and every word space is clearly wider than any letter gap. Then kern the worst pairs (LL, RR, D–T) by eye.
2. **No date, time, or place for the open day (defect, copy).** "Free rides every half hour from the north landing" tells the reader how often and where to board, but not which day or between what hours. "City Harbour Open Day" names an event without a date. The one thing a reader needs before they can come is missing, and it's also the line people remember and hold the organizers to. Whoever reads this can't act on it. **Fix:** add the date and the hours of the ferry service. Only you have those facts, so put them where the eye goes right after the headline, beside or just under the body line.
3. **Heaviest weight plus crushed tracking is a stock look (template tell).** A black-weight headline with negative tracking, over a small uppercase label that's also squeezed, is a move people have seen on many product sites (Sokolovsky, unslop). It makes the piece look like anyone's launch page instead of a harbor event, and it works against the gentle tone of the body copy. **Fix:** let the headline get its rank from size, in a less extreme weight and normal or slightly open spacing. Or give it something that belongs to the harbor rather than to a template.

**Smaller things.**
- The eyebrow "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" (top left) is small capitals set tight. "OPEN DAY" nearly runs together and the label reads as a gray smudge. Letterspace it a little (Bringhurst 2.1.6 suggests 5 to 10% for capitals) so it reads as a clear label.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear order: label, headline, body. All three are flush left on one strong edge at about x=90, so the eye always knows where to start and where to go next.
- The body line is calm and specific ("every half hour", "the north landing", "a hat and a thermos"). It sounds like a person talking and sets the scene. Keep that voice.
- The empty right side and lower half read as deliberate breathing room, not leftovers. Once the type is fixed, that space will let the headline carry the page.
