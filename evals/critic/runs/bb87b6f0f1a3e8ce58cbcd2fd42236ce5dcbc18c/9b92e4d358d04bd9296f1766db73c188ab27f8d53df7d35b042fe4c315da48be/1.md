**First read.** The eye lands on "Project notes," then goes straight down a stack of five identical boxed cards. On the way down it catches on the third card, "Sketches," which sits about 14 px further right than the others and is narrower. That makes you wonder whether Sketches belongs under Research or whether something slipped. Otherwise it feels calm, plain and orderly, a bit heavy because of the thick dark outlines.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a simple page or doc that lists the stages of a design project in order, for the team working on it.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: the title is clear, the type is clean and each card says one plain thing. The one real problem is the misaligned Sketches card, which raises a question the content doesn't answer. After that, the heavy borders make simple notes read like a grid of boxed compartments.

**What would move it most**
1. **Sketches card is out of line (defect).** The third card's left edge sits about 14 px to the right of the other four, and its right edge stays put, so it is also narrower. That's too far to miss by accident and too little to read as a clear choice. So the viewer can't tell which it is: is Sketches a sub-step of Research, or did the layout slip? Either way they stop to work out the structure of a list that should take two seconds to read. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") describes this: a placement that is neither clearly here nor clearly there makes the eye waver and makes the whole look unfinished. It also breaks the pattern the other four cards set, and Gerstner (*Designing Programmes*) says elements of one kind should share one style and spacing. Fix: if Sketches is a peer stage, put it back on the shared left edge at the shared width. If it really is a sub-step of Research, make the nesting obvious: a clearly larger indent, plus a visible tie to its parent (a lighter or smaller treatment, or tucking it inside the Research card).

**Smaller things.**
- Every card is outlined in what looks like a 3 px near-black border, so the page reads as five heavy compartments, and the outlines compete with the words inside them. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*) suggests removing the borders or replacing them with space or a pale tone. The 20 px gaps already separate the stages.
- The stages run in a clear order (Kickoff to Handover), but nothing on the page says it's a sequence. That's taste: step numbers or a "stages" framing in the title would say so. The title "Project notes" is accurate, so this isn't a defect.

**What's working.**
- **Clear type hierarchy.** I count three sizes with clear steps between them: title about 32 px bold, card headings about 20 px semibold, body about 16 px regular. One family is used throughout. Every level is obvious at a glance; keep this scale.
- **Plain copy.** Each body line starts with a verb and says who does what ("Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need"), so every card reads as a concrete action, not a label.
- **Restrained palette.** Near-black on white, with no decoration, suits working notes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable or expandable. If they are, nothing in the image signals it.
