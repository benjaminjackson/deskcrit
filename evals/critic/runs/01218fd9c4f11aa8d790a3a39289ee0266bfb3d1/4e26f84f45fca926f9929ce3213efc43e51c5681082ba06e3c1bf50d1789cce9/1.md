**First read.** My eye goes to the "Create your account" heading, then down a column of six empty boxes to the solid blue "Create account" button. It's clearly a sign-up screen for a note-taking product called Quillnest. It looks calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: change the logo and this could be the sign-up page for any SaaS product. What the eye takes in first is how long the form is, not what you get for filling it in.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the web sign-up page for a notes app, and its job is to turn a curious visitor into an account with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The craft is sound, but the page asks too much and gives too little back. Six fields, one of them a marketing survey, sit between the visitor and a product the page hardly describes. The form length is the biggest obstacle, followed closely by required fields and password rules being invisible.

**What would move it most**

1. **The form asks for more than sign-up needs (defect).** Down the card you're asked for first name, last name, email, password, workspace name and "How did you hear about us?" Only email and password are needed to create an account. "How did you hear about us?" helps your marketing team, not the visitor, and putting it on the critical path tells them their time comes second. "Workspace name" asks a newcomer to name something they haven't seen yet, so they stop to think about a decision that doesn't matter at this point. Each extra field is a place to drop off (Nielsen's heuristic 8, aesthetic and minimalist design: every element should support the user's main task). Fix: cut the form to email and password, with name as one optional field at most. Give the workspace a default name they can change later, and ask the attribution question after sign-up or not at all.

2. **Nothing says what's required or what a valid password looks like (defect).** None of the fields is marked required or optional. The password field has no hint about length or character rules, so the visitor finds out the rules only when the form rejects them. The survey dropdown is the worst case: a visitor can't tell whether they're allowed to skip it. This breaks Nielsen's heuristic 5 (error prevention) and the forms guidance to mark required and optional fields. Fix: show the password rule as helper text under the field before they type, and once you've cut the form as in point 1, mark any optional field that remains as "(optional)".

3. **The "Sign in" link doesn't look like a link (defect).** In "Already have an account? Sign in" below the button, "Sign in" is the same small grey as the rest of the sentence, with no underline, colour or weight. Returning users who land here by mistake are looking for exactly that way out, and nothing marks it as clickable. It breaks WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color) and the rule that links must differ from surrounding text by more than colour. Here they don't even differ by colour. Fix: set "Sign in" in the brand blue and underline it or make it bolder.

4. **The subtitle is generic and "free" can be read two ways (defect).** "Start a free workspace for your notes" could be on any notes app's page, so it gives the visitor no reason to choose Quillnest over the tool they already use. "Free" is also a pricing claim visitors will hold you to. One reader takes it as free forever, another as a free trial, and whoever guessed wrong feels misled when a paywall appears. Fix: say exactly what "free" means ("Free forever for personal notes", or "Free for 14 days, no card needed"). Then add a line about what the visitor gets that their current notes app doesn't give them.

**Smaller things.**
- **Input borders:** The field borders look pale grey-blue on white and may be under the 3:1 non-text contrast minimum (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border colour, and darken it if it falls short.
- **Empty dropdown:** The dropdown is completely blank, with a small unstyled native chevron, so it looks broken rather than waiting for a choice. Add placeholder text ("Select one") and a chevron that matches the field styling (moot if you cut the field).
- **Logo:** The logo sits alone in the top-left corner, far from the card, in a lot of empty pale blue. Either tie it to the card by placing it above the card on the same axis, or use the space for a short line about the product.
- **First/last name pair:** The side-by-side pair is acceptable as a tightly related pair, but it's the only two-column row. If you cut to one name field, the form becomes a clean single column.

**What's working.**
- Labels sit above their fields, stay visible, and are closer to their own field than to the one above. Keep this layout after you cut fields.
- There's one clear primary action: the full-width filled button is the only heavy element in the card, with nothing competing with it.
- The type scale is disciplined. I count four sizes (heading, subtitle, bold labels, small footer line), each with a clear job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings, error messages and inline validation. These matter a lot on a form, so check them in the live page.
