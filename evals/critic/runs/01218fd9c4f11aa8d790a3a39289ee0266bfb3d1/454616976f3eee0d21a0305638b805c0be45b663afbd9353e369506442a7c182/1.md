**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers across the top, $4.82M first, then drops to the blue bars climbing left to right. Within two seconds it reads as a SaaS finance dashboard showing a healthy year. It feels calm, competent and trustworthy, which is the right mood for revenue reporting.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal revenue overview for founders, finance or leadership, used to check how 2025 went at a glance and to quote figures from.

**Verdict.** The layout and craft are solid and it mostly does its job. The one real problem is the numbers: two different figures are both labelled as 2025 revenue, and on a screen people quote from, that does more damage than any layout issue.

**What would move it most**

1. **"Revenue by plan, 2025" totals $4.82M, but the chart beside it adds up to about $4.49M.** (Defect.) Adding the twelve bars by eye gives roughly 312 + 328 + … + 447 ≈ $4.49M of recognised revenue for 2025. The table on the right gives the same title, "2025", and totals $4.82M, which is exactly the ARR figure in the first card. So the table is almost certainly ARR split by plan, labelled as revenue for the year. A viewer will see one of two things. They may notice that the two "2025 revenue" figures disagree and stop trusting the whole page. Or they may not notice, and repeat "$4.82M revenue in 2025" in a board deck, a figure the page itself contradicts. Either way they lose. Fix: name the table for what it measures, e.g. "ARR by plan, Dec 2025", so it clearly ties to the first card. Or, if it really is meant to be revenue for the year, make its numbers sum to the chart. Then check every label on the page names its metric and whether it's a point in time or a period, the way the KPI cards already do.

2. **The chart says what it measures but not what happened.** (Taste. This is a widely followed dataviz practice, not one of the approved sources.) "Monthly revenue, 2025" makes the viewer work out the story from the bar heights. The story is roughly a 43% rise from January to December, with small dips in April and July. This is an overview screen, so the takeaway should be readable in one glance, e.g. "Monthly revenue grew from $312K to $447K". The dips may deserve a short note if anyone will ask about them.

**Smaller things.**
- **Units switch between panels:** the chart is in thousands of dollars and the plan table in millions. Use one unit, or put "$" and "K" on the chart axis ticks, so nobody has to convert between the two.
- **The page doesn't say clearly where you are:** "Overview" is highlighted in the sidebar but the title says "Revenue overview", and "Revenue" is a separate nav item. Make the title and the active nav item match.
- **The "Jan to Dec 2025" pill looks like a filter but has no chevron or calendar icon** to show it can be clicked (Norman: signifiers). Add one if it's a control. If it isn't, style it as a plain label.
- **The plan table only lists amounts.** Since Business makes up nearly half the total, a share column or a small inline bar would show the mix faster than three dollar figures.
- **Empty space under the plan table:** the right column stops about halfway down, leaving a dead block beside the chart. A useful secondary item could go there, or the column could be narrowed.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards show their context:** each names its metric and time frame ("Net revenue retention, 2025") and gives a comparison with last year. The +3 pts on NRR is correctly given in points, not percent. Keep this pattern and copy it to the table label.
- **The chart is honest:** it has a zero baseline, a single colour, light gridlines, a stated unit and a source line limited to closed invoices only (Tufte, Wilke, Few). Nothing in it misleads except the missing takeaway.
- **The type scale is tight:** I count about five sizes (page title, KPI figure, section heading, body, small labels) with clear jumps between them, and weight does the remaining work in the table totals.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- December revenue × 12 comes to about $5.36M against ARR of $4.82M. That may be fine, for example if annual prepayments or one-off invoices are recognised in December. It's worth confirming the two definitions agree, because viewers who do that same multiplication will ask.
