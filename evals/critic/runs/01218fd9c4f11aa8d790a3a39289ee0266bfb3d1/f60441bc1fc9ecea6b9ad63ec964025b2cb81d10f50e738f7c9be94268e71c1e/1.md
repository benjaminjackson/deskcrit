**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers first: 3.8%, €64.20 and 12.4k. Then it drops to the orange bars and finds the Saturday peak and the Sunday dip. Within two seconds it reads as the admin home of a small online bakery, built for the owner. It looks calm, tidy and trustworthy.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the owner's daily overview: a quick check on how the shop is doing and when the orders arrive, so they can plan baking and delivery.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job, and the craft is good. The biggest problem is that the chart is wrong in a way nobody would notice. It totals a month in which some weekdays occur more often than others, so its bars can't be compared, and the gentle Monday-to-Thursday rise is partly made up.

**What would move it most**

1. **The weekday bars aren't comparable (defect).** The source line says "orders delivered in September 2026". That September begins on a Tuesday and has 30 days, so it has five Tuesdays and five Wednesdays but only four of every other day. Those two bars get an extra day's orders. Per delivery day, Monday comes to about 160 orders, Tuesday about 142 and Wednesday about 152. The chart shows Monday as the lowest weekday, but per day it beats both Tuesday and Wednesday. An owner who sets staffing or bake quantities from this chart will plan Tuesday and Wednesday wrong. This is the "Keep scales honest and consistent" line in the checklist (Few and Wilke cover charts that are honest about what is being compared). **Fix:** plot average orders per delivery day, and change the title and axis label to say so. Or, if you keep the totals, show how many of each day the month had under each label.

2. **The chart shows the topic but not the point (taste, leaning toward convention).** "Orders by delivery day" names what is plotted. The two things that matter for planning, the Friday–Saturday peak and the Sunday drop to about a third of Saturday, are left for the viewer to work out. All seven bars are the same orange, so nothing tells the eye where to look. **Fix:** once item 1 is fixed, state the finding in the title or a one-line caption, for example "Saturdays bring the most orders; Sundays the fewest". Keep the bars that matter in orange and turn the others grey (the dataviz entry on colour used with restraint and emphasis: grey for context, one accent for the point). If Sunday is low because you deliver to fewer areas that day, say so on the chart. Otherwise it reads as a problem to fix.

3. **The three cards compare against different periods (defect, minor).** Two cards compare "Last 30 days vs previous 30 days", the subscriber card compares "As of 30 September vs 31 August", and the chart covers calendar September. A viewer can't tell whether "Last 30 days" means September or the 30 days before today. That matters when they set the €64.20 next to the chart. **Fix:** put the whole page on one stated period, such as "September 2026 vs August 2026", ideally in a single line under "Shop overview". Then each card only needs the change.

**Smaller things.**
- The axis tick labels and the source line look about 12 px and mid-grey. Check they reach 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.3). They look close to the limit.
- The value axis has no unit. The title implies "orders", but once item 1 changes the measure to orders per day, label the axis.
- The gridlines are already faint, which is right. Removing the top 1,200 line wouldn't lose anything, because the Saturday bar never reaches it.

**What's working.**
- **The key figures.** Each card has one large number, with the unit (%, €, k) set smaller and lighter, then a label, then a change with both an arrow and a sign. The number gets read first and the unit never competes with it. The change doesn't rely on colour alone. Keep this pattern.
- **A tight type scale.** I count about five sizes (big figures, page title, card and chart titles, labels, axis and source text), each with a clear job and clear steps between them.
- **The source line and the change copy.** "+0.4 pts" rather than "+0.4%" for the conversion rate is exactly right and avoids a common misreading. The source line under the chart is what let me catch item 1, so keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether hovering a bar shows its exact value, and whether the cards link through to Orders and Subscribers.
