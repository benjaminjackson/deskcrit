**First read.** The eye lands on "Get the weekly show list", drops to the purple Subscribe button, and stops there. It reads as a newsletter signup for a local radio station: calm, plain and clear. Both versions are identical except for one detail. In version 2 the Subscribe and "Read last week's list" buttons have nearly square corners (about 2 px). In version 1 they have the same soft rounding (about 6 px) as the email field and the card.

**What it's for.** My assumption: getting listeners of Riverside FM to subscribe to a weekly email of the schedule.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job, and version 1 does it slightly better. Its corner rounding is consistent. In version 2 the two buttons have sharp corners while the email field right above them and the card around them are rounded, so the form looks put together from two different kits. That's a craft flaw, not a usability one. It won't stop anyone subscribing, but it makes the page look a little less finished and a little less trustworthy, and nothing is gained in return.

**What would move it most**
1. **Corner rounding (the only difference between the versions).** In version 2 the field has rounded corners, the buttons have square ones, and the card is rounded again, all within about 150 px. The viewer won't be able to say what's wrong, but the form looks less carefully made. Version 1 uses one rounding throughout, so the field and buttons read as one unit. Keep version 1's consistent rounding. This is a matter of consistency (common sense; Refactoring UI makes the same case for applying a system consistently), and it doesn't affect usability.

Apart from that, the piece has nothing else that would noticeably change how well it works.

**Smaller things.**
- The placeholder "you@example.com" looks fairly light grey. Check that it meets 4.5:1 contrast, or don't rely on it, since the "Email address" label already does that job.
- The card sits above the vertical centre with a lot of empty grey below it. That's fine on a page that only does this one thing, but on a real page the space could hold something useful, such as a sample show line-up.

**What's working.**
- **The copy says exactly what you get and when:** "Every Friday we send the next week's programmes, guest names, and any changes to the schedule." The viewer knows what they're signing up for before they type anything.
- **"Read last week's list" is a strong second option.** It lets the viewer look at a real issue and decide for themselves whether it's worth it, instead of trusting the pitch. It's also clearly secondary (outlined, lighter text), so it doesn't compete with Subscribe.
- **The hierarchy is clean.** The station name is small and purple, the headline is clearly the biggest thing, the body text is readable, and there's one filled button. That's three or four type sizes, each with a clear job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The focus and error states of the email field, and what happens after you press Subscribe.
