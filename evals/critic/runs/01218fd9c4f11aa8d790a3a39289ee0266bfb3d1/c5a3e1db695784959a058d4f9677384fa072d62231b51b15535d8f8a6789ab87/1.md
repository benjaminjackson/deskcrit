**First read.** The eye goes straight to the large serif quote and reads it top to bottom without effort. Then it drops to the bold name, then the grey caption, and only after that goes back up to the small "Harbour Notes, issue 14" label. It reads as a pull-quote card from a small local publication. It feels calm, literate and a little wry, and the gulls line pays off.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a standalone quote card or feature opener for a local newsletter, meant to draw readers into the issue.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear and the typesetting is careful. Two things keep it from being finished: the block sits high and to the left with margins that don't relate to each other, and the line breaks cut the quote's phrases in the wrong places.

**What would move it most**
1. **The block sits too high, and the margins look leftover rather than chosen.** The kicker is about 65 px from the top edge, but the left margin is about 100 px, the bottom is about 215 px empty and the right is about 250 px empty. The space is all in one L-shaped area at the bottom right, which is good (Ruder: gather white space). But the top margin is the smallest of the four, so the piece reads like the top of a web page that stopped, not a composed card. Fix: make the top margin at least as large as the left one and lower the block, so the empty area at the bottom right reads as intended. Or, if this is a card, scale the quote up until it fills the width. *Taste, argued on composition. If this is just one section of a longer page, ignore it.*
2. **The line breaks cut the sentence's phrases.** "…the first ferry / leaves, and that is when / the gulls…" splits "ferry leaves" (subject from verb) and "when the gulls" across lines. Display text is read one line at a time, so each line should be a phrase you could say aloud. Better breaks: "The harbour is quietest / just before the first / ferry leaves, and that / is when the gulls get bold." Or put the breaks at "quietest / … leaves, / … when / …". *Convention (line breaks at natural phrase boundaries). In a piece made of one sentence, this is a large share of the craft.*

**Smaller things.**
- Small type sizes: I count four sizes in total, and three of them are close together. The kicker looks about 15 px, the caption about 16 px and the attribution about 17 px, so they read as near-misses rather than levels. Set all three at one size and separate them by weight and colour, which they already mostly do (Refactoring UI; Bringhurst).
- The display quote looks set at the default letter and word spacing. At this size the word spaces ("is quietest", "that is when") look loose. Tighten tracking slightly (about −1% to −2%).
- The gap from kicker to quote (about 45 px) and from quote to attribution (about 45 px) look the same. The kicker belongs to the whole card, so give it a clearly different gap so it doesn't read as a stray caption.
- The caption is a timetable fact ("departs at 5:40…") that doesn't connect to the gulls. If the card is meant to pull readers into the issue, end it on why the reader would care, e.g. "…early walkers, and a fair few gulls." That's a copy choice for the editor.

**What's working.**
- The opening quote mark hangs outside the text edge, so the left edge of the quote, name and caption is clean at x≈100. Keep this.
- The contrast in size between the quote and everything else is large and certain: one element dominates, and the rest clearly sit under it.
- Serif display type with a sans-serif for the small text is a clear contrast, not a clash. The "fi" ligature in "first" is in place, and the grey caption looks comfortably readable.
