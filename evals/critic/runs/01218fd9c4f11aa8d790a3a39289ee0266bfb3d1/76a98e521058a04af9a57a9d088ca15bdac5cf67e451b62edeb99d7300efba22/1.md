**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild", drops into a short, clear paragraph, then jumps right to the blue "Sign up" button. Then it stalls. The form has two empty boxes with no labels you can read, so you can't tell what you're signing up with. It reads as a ferry operator's news page with a timetable-alerts signup. It feels calm and tidy, but parts of it look unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a ferry service page telling passengers about the rebuilt landing stage and getting them to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** The article half works. The signup half doesn't, and the signup is the only thing on the page that asks the reader to do anything. Every bit of small print on the page (field labels, the note under the button, the figure caption, the footer) is set so small it can't be read. That leaves the form unusable and the small print pointless.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form labels can't be read, so nobody can fill in the form.** The labels above the two fields in the right-hand card are about 3–4 px tall. Even at full resolution I can't make out the letters; the second one might say "Home landing", but that's a guess. A passenger sees two blank boxes and has to guess what goes in each, and whether one of them wants an email or a phone number. Many will just leave. *Defect* (convention: Apple HIG gives 11 pt as the smallest text size; WCAG asks for a visible label on every input). Fix: set the labels at body size or one step down, never below 11–12 pt, close above their fields, and name exactly what to enter ("Email address", "Your usual landing").
2. **All the other small print has the same problem, and some of it matters.** The line under "Sign up", the "Figure 1" caption under the illustration, and the footer line at the bottom left are all about the same unreadable size. The line under the button looks like it says how often you'll be emailed and that you can unsubscribe. That's the reassurance that gets someone to commit, and hiding it costs signups. *Defect.* Fix: one small size for all detail text, large enough to read (roughly 13–14 px on screen), in dark grey rather than faint grey.
3. **The illustration says nothing, and its blue bar looks like a button.** The picture is flat placeholder shapes: grey blocks on a blue band. It doesn't show the things the text says are new (ramps, benches, cover, kiosk). The rounded blue rectangle at the right is exactly the colour and shape of "Sign up", so it reads as something to click. *Defect* (a false clickable cue, against Norman on signifiers), plus *taste* on the content. Fix: use a real photo, or an illustration that shows the ramp meeting the boat, and don't use the button blue anywhere that isn't clickable.

**Smaller things.**
- "Sign up" is a generic label. "Email me timetable changes" says exactly what happens and to whom.
- The card heading "Get timetable changes" doesn't say which route or how you'll get them (email? text?). Add it, e.g. "Get timetable changes for this crossing by email".
- The lower half of the page and the space under the card are empty. That reads as unfinished rather than deliberate. Either end the page sooner or let the article carry on.
- The last paragraph is the strongest sentence on the page (step-free boarding at every tide) but it sits after the picture like an afterthought. Move it up or make it the lead.

**What's working.**
- The body copy is concrete and speaks to the passenger: "prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" lands the claim on the reader. Keep that voice.
- The main type sizes (headline, card heading, body) are few and clearly apart. The body line length looks to be about 65–70 characters, which reads comfortably.
- The two-column split, article left and action right in a quiet bordered card, is easy to follow. Fixing the small print is all it needs.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the fields have placeholder text that only appears on focus, and what error and confirmation states look like after you press "Sign up".
