**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The text is readable, the order is clear, and the hierarchy works. What holds it back is the grid. The two columns don't share baselines and don't end together, so the layout looks accidental rather than set.

**What would move it most**

1. **The columns don't end together, and the bottom of the page is left over.** The left column stops at "flowers." about two-thirds of the way down. The right column stops about four lines higher, at "the last boat." Below both is a wide blank band. Because the left column doesn't reach the bottom, the break in the middle of "The market quay" (from "flowers." to "Stallholders arrive…") has no visible reason. The reader goes to the top of the right column without knowing whether a heading is missing. The leftover space doesn't read as a choice, because it's scattered across two uneven column ends instead of gathered in one place (Ruder: white space must be gathered and stand in tension with the print).
   - Fix: balance the columns so both end on the same line. Or, if the page has a fixed height, fill the left column to the foot of the page before breaking. Either way, the break should look set by the page, not by the text running out.
   - *Defect* (the break makes the reader stop and wonder whether something is missing), plus *convention*.

2. **Lines in the two columns don't sit on shared rows.** The right column's first line ("Stallholders arrive…") sits level with the "Early boats" heading. It doesn't line up with any line of text on the left. Read across the gutter and every line on the right sits between two lines on the left, all the way down. "Going home" and the paragraph under it miss the left-hand rows too. On a two-column page you see that ragged step across the gutter even if you can't name it. It's a big part of why the page feels slightly off (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid").
   - Fix: put both columns on one baseline grid. Also make the space above and below each subhead a whole number of body lines, so the text after every heading lands back on the grid.
   - *Convention*. Breaking it costs polish here, not comprehension.

**Smaller things.**
- **Indents after headings.** The first paragraph under each subhead ("The first ferry…", "The market quay is…", "In the evening…") is indented. Bringhurst 2.3.1 says an opening paragraph after a heading should start flush left, because the heading already marks the start. Keep the indent only for the paragraphs that follow.
- **The indent looks deep.** It's roughly 2 em. About 1 em would mark each paragraph just as clearly and leave a less ragged left edge. This one is taste.
- **Short last lines.** "flowers." sits alone at the foot of the left column, and "the engine." and "the last boat." are also very short last lines (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rewrapping or a small copy edit would remove them, and balancing the columns may fix "flowers." on its own.
