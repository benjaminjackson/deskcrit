**First read.** The eye lands on the bold H1, "Update the card that pays for your subscription," then drops into a column of grey-black prose with blue links scattered through it. It reads as a calm, well-mannered help-centre article for a SaaS billing product. It feels trustworthy and quiet. Then you notice it never tells you how to update the card.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help article for an account owner or billing admin who wants to swap the card on their subscription, usually because a payment just failed or a card expired. They want to get it done in under a minute.

**Verdict.** Visually the page is clean and competent, but it doesn't do its job. The title promises a task, and the body describes how billing behaves without ever giving the steps. The second biggest problem is the decline section: the user acts on it, and it can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **There are no steps. The task the title promises is missing.** In the first paragraph, "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page" is the only line about the action itself. "The billing page" isn't a link, and nothing says where it is, what to click, or what the user will see. Everything else on the page covers timing, permissions and edge cases. Someone who arrives with an expired card reads all three paragraphs and still has to go hunting in the app. That is a **defect**, because it blocks the job the title names. Nielsen's "match between system and real world" and help-and-documentation heuristics both expect help to list concrete steps, and Krug's *Don't Make Me Think* argues for the same. Fix: right under the H1, add a short numbered list of three to five steps ("Go to Settings → Billing", "Click Change card", …) and make "billing page" a link straight to that screen. Then move the current first two paragraphs below the steps as "Good to know". The page is also all prose, so the one sequence people would scan for has no list to scan.

2. **The decline schedule can be read two ways, and the reader's subscription depends on it.** "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days … If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan."
   - "After seven days" could mean seven days after the first decline or seven days after the three-day retry, so day 7 or day 10.
   - "The second attempt" could be the three-day retry, since that is the second charge overall, or the seven-day retry, the second *retry*. Under the first reading the user is downgraded around day 3. Under the second, around day 7 or day 10.

   This is a deadline that gets someone's workspace downgraded, so it's a **defect**, not a style note. Readers will plan around one reading and some will be downgraded earlier than they expected. Fix: state it as a timeline counted from one fixed point, for example "If your card is declined, we retry it 3 days and 7 days after the first failed payment. If the day-7 retry also fails, your subscription moves to the free plan." Also say what the free plan means for the viewer: what they lose access to, and whether any data is at risk. "Your workspace stays open" promises less than the reader needs to know.

3. **"The new card is charged from the next invoice, and any invoice that is already open keeps the old card until it is paid" leaves the urgent case unanswered.** Most people reading this have an *open, failing* invoice on a dead card. Read as a stranger would read it, the sentence says the new card won't pay that invoice, so how does it get paid? This is a **defect** for the most likely reader. Fix: answer that case directly, either "To pay an open invoice with the new card, open it in billing history and click Pay now" or whatever the product actually does.

**Smaller things**
- Left nav: "Update a payment card" isn't marked as the current page (no weight, colour or bar), so the sidebar doesn't show where you are (Nielsen 1, visibility of system status). Bold it or add an indicator.
- The sidebar item says "Update a payment card" and the H1 says "Update the card that pays for your subscription." Use one wording so people searching can tell they've found the right page.
- Body measure looks to be about 80–85 characters per line ("You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page. The new card is" is about 84). That's above Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column a little or bump the size. The leading is generous, which helps.
- The H1 wraps with "subscription" alone on the second line. A shorter title would sit on one line and match the nav label better ("Update your payment card").
- Breadcrumb and "In this section" are small and grey. They look close to the 4.5:1 limit, so check the actual contrast (WCAG 1.4.3).
- There's no next step at the bottom of the page: no "Still declined? Contact support" and no "Was this helpful". The article just stops above a lot of empty space.

**What's working**
- The link text is good. "check your billing history", "roles and permissions" and "paying by invoice" each say where they go and are set in the sentence that explains why you'd click (WCAG link purpose). Keep this.
- The type scale is tight and clear: H1, one H2, body, and one small size for nav and meta, with big steps between them and no near-duplicate sizes. Keep it when you add the steps list.
- Permissions are covered up front ("Only an account owner or a member with the billing role…") with a link to fix it. That heads off the most common "why can't I do this" ticket. Keep it, but place it just before the steps rather than in their place.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Link hover and focus states, and how the two-column layout reflows on a phone. Phones matter here, since a declined-card email is often opened on one.
