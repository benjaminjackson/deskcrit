**First read.** The eye lands on "Winter timetable at the harbour", then drops into two even columns of serif text that read like a pleasant local notice. Then it stops at "Fares and cards" and a pair of grey rules with nothing under them. It feels calm and trustworthy, but nothing on the page shows a time you could catch a boat by.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a web or print notice telling ferry passengers when boats run this winter, how to get on, and what it costs.

**Verdict.** It fails its main job. The page is called a timetable but has no timetable, so nobody can find out when the next boat leaves. The fares section, which is the other thing a passenger needs, appears to be empty.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable is missing (defect).** The headline promises departure times. The body only gives "every ninety minutes", "the first crossing" and "until the last boat has left". The first boat time is never stated: the only clock time on the page is "seven in the morning", which is when the office opens, not when a boat leaves. A passenger reading this will reasonably guess the first boat is at 7:00, and may miss it or wait for nothing. They also can't find the last boat home, which is the time that matters most. Fix: put a real departure table high on the page, near the headline, with every sailing listed. Times like these are for looking up, so they belong in a table (Few; Krug: make it scannable). Keep the prose for the softer details.
2. **"Fares and cards" has no content (defect, unless it's a capture problem).** The heading and its rule sit at about y=560 with blank page below. A second rule in the right column has no heading at all. As shown, the viewer is told fares exist and then gets nothing, and the season card mentioned in paragraph one is never explained. The unlabelled right-hand rule looks like a leftover from the template. Fix: fill the section with prices per journey, return and season card, and either run the heading and rule across both columns or remove the orphan rule.
3. **Information is grouped by paragraph, not by what the passenger needs (defect).** Facts about timing are scattered. Office hours and frequency are on the left, crossing length is at the top right, and the weather-cancellation rule is mid-left. Between them sit the waiting-room stove and dogs. A passenger planning a trip has to read all seven paragraphs to piece together "when do I leave and when do I arrive". Fix: group under short headings that answer passenger questions, such as "When boats leave", "If the weather is bad", "Bikes, prams and dogs" and "Fares" (Krug; checklist: text is broken into chunks).
4. **The weather rule doesn't say what the passenger should do (defect, copy).** "Notices are pinned to the board beside the gate" only helps someone already standing at the gate. A traveller deciding at home whether to set out has no way to check. Fix: say where they can check before leaving home, such as a phone number or a web page, and say it to them: "Check … before you leave home; the captain may cancel a sailing in rough weather."

**Smaller things.**
- Crossing times are spelled out ("twenty minutes", "thirty-five"). Use figures so they can be scanned.
- The columns end unevenly: the right one stops at "Dogs…", leaving a large hole above the rules. Grouping the content by topic will fix this too.
- "Bags." sits alone at the end of the bicycles paragraph. Reflow the paragraph or adjust the wording so it doesn't end on a one-word line.
- The headline doesn't say which harbour or which island. Name them so the page makes sense when shared or found by search.

**What's working.**
- The type is clean and has a clear scale: three sizes (headline, subhead, text), with the headings in a heavier weight of the same serif. Keep that restraint.
- The text is comfortable to read: lines look about 55 to 65 characters, the leading is generous, the text is a dark grey on white, and paragraphs are spaced, not indented.
- The tone is warm and plain ("the crew will always help with bags", "a shelf of paperbacks"). That voice suits a local service, so keep it in the prose once the hard facts move into a table.
