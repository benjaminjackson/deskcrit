**First read.** In both versions the eye goes from "Review your order" to the bold $1,385.00 total, then down to the dark green "Continue to payment" button. It reads as a calm, plain checkout review for an expensive furniture order. The yellow notice is the only colour on the left, so it gets a look on the way through. In version 2 that look turns into a slog: the notice is a solid block of capitals.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the last review step before payment, where the buyer checks the items and the total and takes in the delivery terms before committing about $1,400.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change is the delivery notice, which version 2 sets in all capitals, and that makes the most important paragraph on the page the hardest one to read.

**What changed and why it matters**
1. **The notice in all capitals (version 2), in the yellow box on the left.** This is a defect. Four lines of running text in capitals have no word shapes to read by. The block looks like shouting or legal small print, and people skim past both. That is the wrong result for this paragraph. It's the only text on the page that asks the buyer to do something before paying ("measure doorways and stairs first"), and it warns of a costly failure (the drivers can't take doors off their hinges). It also sets terms the buyer will hold the shop to: weekday, 8 am to 6 pm, a two-hour window texted the evening before. If the buyer skims it, they find out on delivery day that the table doesn't fit through the door. The capitals also run the box to four lines instead of three, so the card gets taller. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps"): don't capitalise whole paragraphs; keep capitals for headings shorter than one line, labels and captions. Version 1 already follows this, so keep its upper and lower case. If the warning needs more pull, make the one instruction bold ("Please measure doorways and stairs first") rather than raising the whole paragraph's volume.

**Smaller things (version 1)**
- Delivery appears as a line item in "Review your order" and again in Summary, where "Items" ($1,320) leaves it out. The sums are right, but they're split two different ways. Either drop delivery from the item list or label it there as a delivery charge.
- The notice doesn't tell the buyer what to do if something won't fit. Add one clause or a link (for example, contact us before you pay) so the warning ends on a next step.
- The Summary card is stretched to match the left card's height, which leaves an empty band under the button. Let it sit at its own height, or make it sticky.

**What's working**
- The type scale is tight and clear. There are about four levels: the 28-ish px heading, the bold card titles, body text and the bold total. The total is clearly the biggest number, so the price is impossible to miss.
- There's one strong button, in the only dark colour on the page, placed right under the total. The ask is specific ("Continue to payment"), so the buyer knows exactly what clicking it does.
- The item names are concrete ("Ash dining table, 180 cm", "Two-person delivery to room of choice"), so the buyer can check the order without opening anything else.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the items can be edited or removed from this screen, and how the two cards stack on a narrow screen.
