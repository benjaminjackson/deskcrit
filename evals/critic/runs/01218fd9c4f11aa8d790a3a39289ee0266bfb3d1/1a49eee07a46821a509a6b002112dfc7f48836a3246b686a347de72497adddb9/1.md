**First read.** The eye lands first on the big dark image block, finds only a grey default-avatar silhouette, and drops to the bold headline: "An evening with Ines Varga on her new novel, The Salt Year." From there it goes to the dark "Book a free seat" button. It reads as an event page on an independent bookshop's site. It feels calm and tidy, but the largest thing on the page is empty, so it also feels unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an event page meant to get local readers to book a free seat at an author reading in the shop.

**Verdict.** The copy and the single clear ask do the job. The page is let down by its biggest element: a placeholder silhouette that takes up 40% of the view and says nothing. It also leaves out where the event is, and its date line may not match the calendar.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero image is a default avatar** (top, 600×330 or so). This is the first thing anyone sees, and it tells them "no photo here." A generic person shape on a dark gradient looks like a broken profile picture. It makes the event seem less real and makes the shop look careless, at the exact moment you want the reader to care about a specific author. *Defect.* Fix: use a real photo of Ines Varga or the cover of *The Salt Year*, whichever a local reader is more likely to recognise. If you have neither, cut the image and let the headline lead. An empty frame is worse than no frame.

2. **The date line may be wrong, and the event never says where it is** (the brown line above the headline). A reader will hold you to "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm," but it has no year, and 13 November 2026 falls on a Friday, not a Thursday. So either the weekday is wrong or this is last year's event still on the site, and either way someone turns up on the wrong night. Nothing on the page gives a venue either: not "at the shop," not an address or branch. "Find us" in the nav doesn't count, because the reader shouldn't have to leave to learn where to go. *Defect.* Fix: check the weekday against the year, and add the place right next to the time, e.g. "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm · Margin Books, [street]." If seats are limited, say so here too: it's the honest reason to book now.

3. **The pitch assumes the reader already knows who Ines Varga is.** The headline and standfirst tell you what happens (a reading, a talk about the island, questions, wine) but never why this author or book is worth an evening. Anyone who hasn't heard of her gets no reason to click. *Taste, but it decides the conversion.* Fix: add one line, in terms the reader already trusts, on who she is or what the book is: her last book, a prize, a comparison to a writer they know, or one sentence about the island. It can go between the standfirst and the button or just under the button.

**Smaller things.**
- "7 pm" and "Wine… from 6.30 pm" leave arrival time a little vague. Label them "Doors 6.30 pm, reading 7 pm."
- "Events" in the nav has no current-page state, so you can't tell where you are on the site. Underline it or make it darker.
- Below the button the page just stops, with a large blank area. Add how long it runs, accessibility details, and a short note on the book, or end the page there on purpose with the footer.

**What's working.**
- The copy is plain and specific. "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor" tells you exactly what the evening is, and "Book a free seat" names the action and removes the cost worry in four words. Keep both.
- The hierarchy is clean: a small coloured date label, then one big headline, then body text, then one button. There's one action and one dominant line, and four or five type sizes with clear jumps between them.
- The narrow single column suits one event and stays easy to read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** What happens after "Book a free seat" (whether it asks for a name and email, sends a confirmation, or shows seats left), and how the page reflows on a phone.
