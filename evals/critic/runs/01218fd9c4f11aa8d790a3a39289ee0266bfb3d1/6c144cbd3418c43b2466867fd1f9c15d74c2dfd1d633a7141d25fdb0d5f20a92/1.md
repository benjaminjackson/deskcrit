**First read.** My eye goes straight to the brush script "Crossing the river". It then jumps right to the second script heading, "Good to know", before coming back to the serif subheads on the left. It reads as a small, friendly guide for a local ferry. Up close it feels more like a café menu than a transport notice, and the main column has no single place where you find out when you can travel.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page for passengers on one ferry route, who mostly want to know when the boat goes, what to bring, and where to find the full timetable.

**Verdict.** The page is calm and easy to scan, but it doesn't do its main job. It never gives a passenger an actual time to act on. The type also pulls in three directions, so the side box competes with the guide instead of supporting it.

**What would move it most**

1. **The schedule copy gives no time a passenger can use (defect).** In the "When the boats run" paragraph, "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" and "the last crossing leaves before dusk" all leave the reader to guess. Is the first boat at 6:00 or 8:00? When does "winter" start? Dusk can fall anywhere between about 4 and 9 pm depending on the date. Someone reading this to catch the last boat can't plan from it, and a vague line about the last departure is the kind that strands people. Give the first and last sailing times, the dates of the winter timetable, and the exact last crossing, even if it changes with the season ("last boat 16:20, Nov–Feb"). If the times change too often to print, say that plainly and put the timetable link right in this paragraph.

2. **Three unrelated typefaces, and they mix inside the same roles (convention, Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 7–8).** The headings use a brush script, the subheads and the deck use a serif, and the main body uses a sans. The side box body is in the serif, though, while the main body is in the sans. So two passages that do the same job look different, and none of the faces clearly relates to the others. The viewer gets three voices and can't tell what is primary. Pick one text family for all body copy. If you keep the script, limit it to the single page title. "Good to know" should then use the same serif as "When the boats run", so it reads as part of the same guide.

3. **The side box outweighs the main column (defect).** "Good to know" is set in the big display script, and its body text looks larger than the main body, around 20 px against 18. The aside is louder than the content it sits beside, which is why the eye leaves the guide for it on first read. It also says less: the "Good to know" text only tells you where printed timetables are. Bring the aside heading down to subhead level and its body to the main body size or smaller. That puts the left column back in charge.

4. **The type scale is muddled (convention, Bringhurst, *Elements*, §3.1.1).** I count about seven sizes: the title (~64), the aside heading (~40), the subheads (~28), the deck (~24), the aside body (~20), the main body (~18) and the nav (~16). The deck, aside body and main body are close enough to look accidental rather than like separate levels. Four levels would carry this page: title, subhead, body, and small for the nav.

5. **The headline tone is wrong for a transport notice (taste).** The script makes the page feel like a gift shop or a wedding site. People reading ferry information want to feel that the times are reliable. The script's warmth undercuts that, and its bouncing baseline also makes the title slower to read. I'd use the serif in a bold weight for the title and let the friendliness come from the plain-spoken copy, which already has it.

**Smaller things.**
- The deck "A short guide for passengers on the north landing to market quay route" is grey on white and looks light. Check that it reaches at least 3:1 at that size (WCAG 1.4.3). "North landing to market quay" also reads better with the places capitalised as names: North Landing, Market Quay.
- "See the timetable" repeats the "Timetable" nav link, and the main column has no link of its own. Put the link right after the schedule paragraph, where the reader actually wants it.
- Fares, the other thing every passenger needs, appear only in the nav. Even one line such as "Adult single £X, pay on board" would save them a click.
- Below the bicycles section the lower third of the screen is empty while the aside floats at the top. The page ends abruptly. That's fine if this really is the whole guide, but it suggests content is missing (fares, accessibility, what happens in bad weather).

**What's working.**
- The structure is right for scanning. The two subheads, "When the boats run" and "Bicycles and luggage", are plain questions turned into labels, which is what a passenger skims for. Keep that pattern.
- The main body measure looks to be about 70 characters with comfortable leading, so the paragraphs read easily.
- The bicycles paragraph is the best copy on the page: who does what, in what order, and where ("wheel them on after foot passengers… lock them to the rail"). The schedule paragraph should be written to the same standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the side box reflows on a phone: whether it lands above or below the guide matters, because on mobile it would push the actual times further down.
