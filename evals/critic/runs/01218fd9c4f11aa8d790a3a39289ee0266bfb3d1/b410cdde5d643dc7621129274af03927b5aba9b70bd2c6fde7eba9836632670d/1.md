**First read.** In both, the eye lands on the large headline "Plan a week of dinners in ten minutes", reads down through two short paragraphs and three feature lines, then stops at the green "Plan my first week" button. It's clearly a landing page for a meal-planning service aimed at busy home cooks. It feels calm and plain. The only thing that changed between the two versions is alignment: version 1 is set flush left, and version 2 centres everything under the header.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: the hero section of a landing page that should get a busy home cook to understand the offer and click "Plan my first week".

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. Its left edge gives the reader a fixed place to start every line. Version 2 centres a five-line description and a three-item list, so both edges of the text are ragged and the content is slower to read. Neither version is weak: the copy is unusually specific and the hierarchy is clear. The centring is the one change, and it makes version 2 worse.

**What would move it most**
1. **Centred running text and list (version 2):** *defect.* The two paragraphs in version 2 are set centred, about 70 characters a line. Each line starts at a different point ("Tell Weekplate…", "on weeknights…", "goes to waste…"), so after every line break the eye has to search for where the next one begins. The three feature lines lose their shared left edge, so they stop reading as a list and look like three captions. Centring works for a line or two of display text. Running text reads best against a fixed left margin (Bringhurst; Butterick, *Practical Typography*). Version 1 also lines the text up with the "Weekplate" logo at x≈100, so the page has one strong vertical axis. Version 2 breaks that axis: the logo stays on the left and the content floats in the middle. **Fix direction:** keep version 1's flush-left setting. If you want version 2's centred, symmetrical feel, centre only the headline and the button, and keep the paragraphs and list flush left in a centred column.

**Smaller things** (both versions)
- **Feature lines (y≈438–505):** they have no bullets, ticks, or other markers, and they're the same size and colour as the body text. They read as a loose third paragraph. Give them a marker or a weight change so they register as a list.
- **Headline:** "minutes" sits alone on the second line. Tighten the measure or the size slightly so the line breaks more evenly, or break it after "dinners".
- **Space from headline to body:** the gap looks tighter than the gaps between paragraphs, so the headline crowds the copy under it. Add a little space above the first paragraph.
- **Version 1, right half of the screen:** it's empty. That isn't a defect, but it's an obvious place for a sample week or a shopping list that would show the product instead of describing it. *Taste.*

**What's working**
- **The copy is specific and lands on the reader.** "Who you cook for, what you will not eat, and how long you have on weeknights", "less food goes to waste by Friday", and "sorted by the aisles of your usual shop" each tie the product to something the reader already does. Keep that.
- **The button names one exact action.** "Plan my first week" tells the reader what happens when they click, which is far stronger than "Get started".
- **The type scale is clean.** There are about three sizes (headline, body, nav and button) with big steps between them, and a single restrained green for the brand, the button, and nothing else.
