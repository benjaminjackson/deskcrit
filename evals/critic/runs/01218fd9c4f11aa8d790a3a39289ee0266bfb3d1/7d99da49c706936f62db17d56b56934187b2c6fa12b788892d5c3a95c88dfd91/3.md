**First read.** The eye lands on nothing in particular. It drops into the top-left corner and reads straight down a uniform grey-black column. It took me a second pass to see that there are headings at all: "Changing your pickup time" looks like the first line of a paragraph, not a label above one. It reads as an FAQ or help panel for a bakery's order-ahead service. It feels calm and plain, but flat.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help or FAQ section on a bakery's online ordering page. A customer who already has an order lands here with one question and needs to find the answer quickly and trust it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The headings are what a scanning customer needs to find their question, and they're set smaller than the answers with no other difference. The page reads as one undivided block. Second, the two lines people will hold the bakery to, the cancellation deadline and the collection rule, can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings are weaker than the text they label** (defect). "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two headings look a pixel or two *smaller* than the answers. They're also the same weight and the same colour. Someone who came for "can I cancel?" can't run their eye down the headings. They have to read each block to find out which one is theirs. Dair's point applies directly: weak contrast is worse than none, because a difference that small looks like a mistake rather than a level. Fix: make the question clearly the stronger element. Bold or semibold at the same size or one clear step up is enough (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). You could also soften the answer colour a little. Then the four questions become a scannable index.

2. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways** (defect, copy). "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" doesn't say a day ahead of what, or when exactly the cutoff falls:
   - **24 hours before the pickup time.** Under this reading, a customer collecting at 10:00 Saturday can cancel until 10:00 Friday.
   - **Any time on the day before.** Under this reading, they think they have until Friday night, try to cancel at 18:00, and are refused.

   That second reader becomes an angry customer with a loaf they didn't want. Fix: give the exact cutoff in the customer's terms, e.g. "You can cancel until 24 hours before your pickup time. After that we've already baked your bread." That last clause also does a good job of landing the reason on the viewer. Keep it.

3. **"Along with a name" is ambiguous at the counter** (defect, copy). Whose name: yours (the person who ordered) or the collector's? If staff check one and the collector gives the other, someone gets turned away. Fix: say which, e.g. "…along with the name the order was placed under."

4. **The lines are far too long** (convention). The answers run the full ~820 px width. "Orders can be cancelled… baked for you." is roughly 100+ characters, well past Bringhurst's 45–75. On short answers like these the cost is modest, but the long single lines are part of why the column reads as an undivided slab. There's also a mass of unused space to the right and below. Fix: cap the text column at roughly 60–70 characters. If this sits on a page with other content, use the freed width for that.

5. **The allergy answer sends an online customer to the shop** (defect, content). The person reading this is ordering online, before they ever see "a card by the till". So the answer tells them they can't find out until they're standing in the bakery with an order already placed. Fix: link to the ingredient list from the product page, or at minimum give a way to ask before ordering (phone, email).

**Smaller things.**
- There's no page or section title, so nothing says "Questions about your order" or similar. Add one as the single dominant element.
- "The bakery holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" stops at the fact. Add what it means for the customer: what happens after two hours? Do they lose the order, the money, or both?
- "Choose a new slot from the list": say whether there's a limit on how late you can change it, since that's the same deadline question as cancelling.
- "Ask the baker if you are unsure about anything" ends on a short orphaned "anything." at this width. That disappears once the measure is fixed.

**What's working.**
- The spacing does the grouping correctly. Each heading sits tight to its answer, with a clearly larger gap between pairs, so once the headings are strengthened the structure will read instantly. Keep those gaps.
- The copy is plain, specific and human. "Give the person your order number" and "the bread has already been baked for you" are written for a customer, not by a system. Keep that voice while you tighten the ambiguous lines.
- One clean sans-serif face, dark text on white, flush left. Contrast looks comfortably above WCAG 4.5:1, so there's nothing to fix there.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether these headings are meant to be accordion toggles. If they are, they currently carry no signifier (chevron, plus sign) that they open or close.
