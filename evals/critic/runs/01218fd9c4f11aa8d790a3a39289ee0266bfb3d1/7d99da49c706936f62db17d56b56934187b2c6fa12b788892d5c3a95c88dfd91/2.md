**First read.** The eye lands on the top-left corner and then reads straight down, line by line, as if this were one block of grey text. Nothing stands out. The topic lines ("Changing your pickup time" and the rest) look a little smaller than the answers under them and the same weight, so on a first scan they read as lead-in sentences, not headings. The tone is calm and plain, like a small bakery's help page, but the page has no title and no structure, so it reads as unfinished.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a help or FAQ panel on a bakery's online ordering site. Customers who already have an order skim it to find the one rule they need: changing, handing over, checking allergens, or cancelling.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well. A skimming customer can't pick out the topics, because the headings are weaker than the text they introduce. Several answers also leave out the detail the customer will act on: the deadline, whose name, and what happens next.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings look smaller than the answers and no heavier, so the page can't be scanned.** All four topic lines look about one size step below the answer text, in the same weight and colour. This page only works if someone looking for "cancel" can jump to it. As it stands, they have to read every line. This is a weak contrast, which Dair treats as worse than none: when two levels are meant to differ, the difference has to be obvious at a glance. Bringhurst says the same about a type scale, where sizes only a little apart look like mistakes. **Defect.** Fix: make the headings clearly stronger than the answers. A semibold weight at the same size or one clear step up usually does it (Refactoring UI: use weight, not only size). You could also set the answers in a softer grey so the headings carry the page.

2. **The answers leave out the facts the customer will act on.** This is a policy page, and people will hold the bakery to what it says. Each gap below can be read two ways. **Defect.**
   - "Cancelled up to a day ahead": a day ahead of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot or any time on the day before. A customer who picks the wrong reading loses their money or argues at the counter. The answer also never says *how* to cancel, even though the first answer does say how to change a slot. Fix: give the exact cutoff and the action, for example "Cancel from your order up to 24 hours before your pickup time."
   - "Along with a name": whose name? The person who ordered or the person collecting? Whoever is sent to the counter can't tell. Fix: say "your name" or "their own name".
   - "Holds orders for two hours after the chosen time": the line stops at the fact and never says what it means for the customer. Does the order go to someone else, and do they get their money back? End the line on what happens to them.
   - Allergies: "a card by the till" helps only once someone is standing in the shop. An online customer needs to know before ordering. If ingredients are listed online too, say where. If they aren't, say that, and give a way to ask before ordering.

3. **The lines are too long for comfortable reading.** The column looks about 820 px wide, so the one-line answers run to about 95 characters ("Orders can be cancelled… baked for you."). That is well past Bringhurst's 45–75. The eye has trouble finding the start of the next line, and on a page this short there is no reason for it. **Convention.** Fix: narrow the column to about 60–70 characters, around 560–620 px at this size. Plenty of room is left over.

**Smaller things.**
- No page title: nothing says this is help for bakery orders. Add a short heading above the first topic, such as "Your order: questions".
- Single-word last lines: "anything." sits alone in the allergies answer, and "after the chosen time." trails in the first answer. Narrowing the column will reflow these; check the breaks again afterwards.
- The bottom 40% of the frame is empty, so the content looks dropped in rather than placed. That matters less once there's a title and a narrower column, but check it in the real page container.

**What's working.**
- The spacing groups the content correctly. Each heading sits closer to its own answer (about 12 px) than to the answer above (about 25 px), so the four pairs already read as four units. Keep those proportions when you strengthen the headings.
- The voice is plain and human ("the bread has already been baked for you"). That line gives a reason, not just a rule. Keep the tone and add the missing facts.
- The text contrast looks strong: near-black on white, comfortably above 4.5:1.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether these are meant to be expandable items (accordion rows). If they are, nothing on the page shows they can be opened, so check it in a browser.
