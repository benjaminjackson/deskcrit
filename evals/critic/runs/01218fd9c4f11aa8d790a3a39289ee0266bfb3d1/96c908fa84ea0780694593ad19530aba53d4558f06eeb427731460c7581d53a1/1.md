**First read.** In both versions the eye goes to the inbox illustration, then the headline "No open conversations", then the button. It reads as the empty state of a customer-support inbox: calm, tidy and clear. Apart from the illustration's colour, nothing changed between the versions. Version 1 fills the tray with mint green and version 2 with pale blue. Layout, type, copy, button and sidebar are the same pixel for pixel.

**What it's for.** My assumption: to tell a support agent the inbox is empty, that this is expected, and to give them one useful next step (start a conversation from the customer list).

**Verdict.** The two versions do the job equally well. The fill colour is the only change, and it doesn't change what the viewer understands or does. The illustration is decoration and neither tint carries meaning. You could argue green hints at "all clear" and blue reads as a neutral brand accent, but that's a matter of taste and too small to change the result. The design underneath works, with a few small issues listed below.

**What would move it most**
1. **The colour choice should come from the product palette, not from this screen alone (taste).** Neither tint is wrong. Pick whichever one the rest of the product uses for neutral illustrations. Keep green only if the product consistently uses it to mean "done, nothing pending". Otherwise an empty inbox might read as a success state in one place and a neutral one in another.

**Smaller things.**
- Sidebar: the selected item "Inbox" is shown only by bold weight. Add a background tint or an indicator bar so it's clear where you are without comparing weights.
- Button "Open customer list": the label names a place rather than the task. "Start a conversation" would tie it to the body copy's second sentence. Otherwise the agent has to work out that the list is the way to do that.
- The group of illustration and text sits slightly above centre and leaves a lot of empty space below it. That's fine for an empty state, but check how it looks on taller viewports.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear three-level hierarchy (illustration, headline, body plus button) with large steps between sizes, so the scan takes a second.
- The body copy explains what will happen ("New messages from customers will appear here") and gives a next action. An empty state should do both.
- The secondary-style button is the right weight here. Nothing is urgent, so it shouldn't shout.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states, and whether the sidebar items show counts once conversations exist.
