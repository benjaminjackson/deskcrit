**First read.** The eye goes to the large dark image block, finds a grey silhouette where a face should be, and drops to the bold headline: "An evening with Ines Varga on her new novel, The Salt Year." It reads as a bookshop event page. It's calm and well set, but it feels unfinished: a generic avatar on the left and an empty right half.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since nothing was given: an event page for a local independent bookshop, meant to get readers to book a free seat at an author reading.

**Verdict.** The type and the call to action are good. The page doesn't do its job yet, though, because the one picture that should sell the evening is a placeholder silhouette, and it never says where the event happens.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image is a generic avatar (defect).** It's the largest and darkest thing on the page, so it's where the eye lands first, and what it finds is the "no photo" silhouette. To a stranger that reads as broken or unfinished, and it gives the shop's taste nothing to show. For an author evening, the picture has to pull its weight: either the author's face, which turns this into meeting a person, or the cover of *The Salt Year*, which tells readers whether the book is for them. Use a real photo or the cover. If neither exists, drop the image and let the headline lead, rather than giving the most prominent spot to an empty frame. If this is a stand-in that will be swapped later, the rest of this crit still holds.
2. **The page never says where (defect: copy).** It gives the date, start time and drinks time, then asks the reader to commit with "Book a free seat". It doesn't give the venue, the address, how long the evening runs, or whether seats are limited. Someone booking for an evening event needs the place before the date is any use to them. "Find us" in the menu sends them off to look it up somewhere else (Krug: don't make me think). Put the venue and address on the same line as the date, or right under it. Add an end time or rough length. If seats really are limited, say so near the button, because that's the real reason to book now.
3. **The right half of the page is empty (taste).** All the content sits in a column about 600 px wide on the left, and the right half of a 1280 px screen is blank. The empty space isn't doing anything. It doesn't frame the content, it's just left over, so it looks like a sidebar that failed to load. Either commit to the layout (centre the column, or let the image run wider), or use the right side for what readers want next: venue and map, the other upcoming events, or the book itself.

**Smaller things.**
- The book title, "The Salt Year", is set in roman inside the headline. Titles of books are set in italic by convention (Bringhurst; Butterick). Italicising it also separates it from "her new novel".
- The gap between the date and the headline looks about the same as the gap between the headline and the paragraph. Tighten date to headline a little so the date, headline and description read as one unit.
- The menu links are small and mid-grey. They look close to the limit, so measure them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- The logo and menu baselines look a couple of pixels apart in the header. Align them.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: about four sizes (logo, headline, body, and small date/menu text), each with a clear job, and emphasis that comes from weight and colour rather than more sizes. Keep it.
- "Book a free seat" is a good ask. It names exactly what the reader gets and removes the cost worry in three words. It's also the only filled button on the page, so nothing competes with it.
- The description is concrete and sounds like a person talking: "a reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor. Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm." Readers can picture the evening. Don't trim it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What happens after "Book a free seat" (a form, a confirmation, how many seats).
- Whether the empty right side or the silhouette is a loading or capture problem. Check both in a live browser.
