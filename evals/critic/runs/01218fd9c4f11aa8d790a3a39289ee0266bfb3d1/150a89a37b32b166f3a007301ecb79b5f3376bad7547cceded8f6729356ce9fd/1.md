**First read.** In both versions the eye goes to the serif headline, "Hold a real conversation in Italian by summer", then down to the subhead, then to the buttons. In version 1 it stops at the buttons: three orange blocks of the same size and colour, so you have to read all three before you know which one to press. In version 2 it goes straight to "Start your first lesson", and the two underlined links read as side options. It feels warm, calm and credible: an Italian course with a real teacher behind it, not an app full of game mechanics.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero of a landing page for an Italian course, and its job is to get adult beginners to start a first lesson.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. It's the only change between the two, and it's the one that matters: there is now a single clear next step. Version 2 is a solid hero. The biggest remaining gap is that the chat panel never shows the thing the page promises, a teacher correcting you as you speak.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Version 1 has three equal calls to action** (defect). "Start your first lesson", "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" have the same fill, colour, size and weight. Nothing tells the viewer which one the page wants. A first-time visitor has to compare three options before acting, and "Gift a course" is a niche path for a buyer, but it gets the same pull as the main conversion. Refactoring UI makes this point: secondary and tertiary actions should drop in weight so the primary one stands out.
- **Version 2 fixes this.** It keeps one filled button and turns the other two into text links. The eye lands on "Start your first lesson" without effort, and the plan and gift options are still easy to find for the people who want them.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The chat panel shows a conversation but not the product** (defect). The subhead promises a teacher "who corrects what you say as you say it". But the learner's reply in the panel, "Sono andato al cinema con mia sorella", is already correct, so the panel never shows a correction. It also has no teacher face or name, and nothing marks it as a video call. The viewer sees a generic chat any app could show, and the one thing that sets this course apart goes unseen. A fix: have the learner make a typical beginner's mistake and show the teacher's inline correction, maybe with a small face and name ("Giulia, Bologna"). That also fills the empty bottom third of the panel.
2. **"By summer" and "Start your first lesson" leave the viewer guessing** (defect, copy). "By summer" is a promise with a deadline, but the page doesn't say from what level or starting when. A visitor in May reads it very differently from one in October. The button also doesn't say whether the first lesson is free, or whether clicking it starts a payment, and that question is what stops people clicking. A fix: anchor the promise ("from zero, in about 4 months") and add a short line under the button ("Free, about 15 minutes, no card").

**Smaller things**
- The two text links sit inside button-sized padding, so they float away from the primary button with uneven gaps. Pull them closer, or space them as ordinary inline links.
- The whole bottom third of the viewport is empty below the hero. Nothing hints at more content, so on a short screen the page can look finished. Let the next section's top edge show.
- The nav uses middle dots as separators at small size, so it reads as one sentence. That's fine for four items, but spaced links would be easier to tap.

**What's working**
- The headline names a result the viewer wants ("hold a real conversation"), not a feature. The heavy serif makes it the clear first stop.
- The subhead is specific, which most competitors aren't: fifteen minutes a day, a weekly call, a teacher in Bologna.
- The palette is tight, with one orange used for action and warm neutrals everywhere else. Version 2 now uses that orange with the restraint it needs.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the chat panel animates or plays a sequence. If it does, the correction from finding 1 could happen there.

Better: version 2
