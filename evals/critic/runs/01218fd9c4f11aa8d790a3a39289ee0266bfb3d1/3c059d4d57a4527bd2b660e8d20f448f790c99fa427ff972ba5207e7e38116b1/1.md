**First read.** The eye goes to the bold headline "Plan the week with your whole team" first, then over to the white card and its blue "Create account" button. Within a second it reads as a standard SaaS sign-up page: clean, calm, competent, and anonymous. Nothing on it says what Tallyboard actually does beyond "planning."

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: the sign-up page for a team planning tool, aimed at a team lead who has already clicked "Sign up" somewhere and needs to finish quickly and trust what they are agreeing to.

**Verdict.** The layout works. The form does not hold up once someone starts typing. Every field is labelled only by hint text that disappears on the first keystroke. The one commercial promise, "free … for up to five people," can be read two ways, and those two readings lead to very different bills.

**What would move it most**

1. **The fields have no labels, only placeholders (defect).** All five inputs on the right card ("Enter first name," "name@company.com," "At least 10 characters," "Company name") use the grey hint text as their only label. When the user types, the hint is gone. Anyone who tabs back to check a field, or gets an error on submit, sees a filled box with nothing saying what it is for. The password field is the worst case. Its hint is a rule, not a name, so nothing on screen ever says "Password," and the 10-character requirement disappears exactly when the user needs it. Fix: put a visible label above or beside each field, and keep the hint text inside the field for an example only, because the hint disappears as soon as the person starts to type (Nielsen Norman Group, "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful"). Move "At least 10 characters" out of the box as helper text under a "Password" label, so it stays visible while the user types.

2. **The price line can be read two ways (defect, copy).** "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." Reading A: free forever for five seats, and you pay once you add a sixth. Reading B: a free trial, with "later" meaning after you start paying. The second sentence makes it worse. "Invite the rest of the team later" sounds like a step, not a price change, so a team of eight may sign up thinking it is all free. This is the line people will hold you to. Fix: state the terms in words that allow only one reading, such as what stays free, for how long, and what happens at person six ("Free for up to 5 people, no time limit. Adding a 6th moves you to a paid plan.").

3. **The left half doesn't make the case (taste, copy).** "Plan the week with your whole team" could sit on any of a dozen planning tools. Below it, the left column is about 400 px of empty grey. Someone who arrived unsure has nothing here to tell them why this tool and not their current spreadsheet or Asana board. You don't need marketing clutter on a sign-up page. One concrete line or a small product glimpse that shows what "planning the week" looks like in Tallyboard, compared with what the visitor uses now, would use the space you already have.

**Smaller things.**
- The input borders look like a pale blue-grey on white and may fall below 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure them. If they fail, darken them so the fields read as fields.
- The placeholder grey looks close to the 4.5:1 line (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure it, especially since it is currently carrying the labels.
- "Already have an account? Sign in" is small and in the same grey as the question, with no colour or underline on "Sign in." Returning users have to hunt for the link. Make "Sign in" look like a link, for example in the brand blue.
- No field is marked as required or optional. "Company name" in particular invites the question "do I have to?" Mark the optional ones, or drop the field from sign-up.
- The left column's top (the logo at about y=140) doesn't line up with anything in the card (card edge at about 96, title at about 148). Align the logo or the headline with the card title so the two halves read as one composition.

**What's working.**
- **Type and hierarchy.** I count about five sizes with clear steps: headline, card title, body, input text, and a small footnote. Nothing collides, and the headline clearly leads.
- **One primary action.** The single full-width blue "Create account" button is the only saturated block on the page, so there is no question what to do. Keep it the only thing in that blue apart from the logo.
- **A short form.** Pairing first and last name is the right exception to single-column layout, and the whole form fits without scrolling.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Error and validation states, especially what the password field shows when a password is too short, and focus states on the inputs. Check both live, because once labels exist, error messages will need room next to them.
