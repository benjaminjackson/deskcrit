**First read.** The eye lands on the "Bin collection days" heading, then drops to the grey "Next collection: Thursday" box, which answers the main question straight away. After that comes a short block of body text with five blue links. It's a calm, plain council page, and it reads as trustworthy. The two versions differ only in how the links are underlined.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a mobile council page where residents check which bins go out on which day, then find the calendar, report a missed bin, or replace a bin.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job slightly better. Its links use a real text underline that sits just under the letters and breaks around descenders. Version 1 uses a rule that hangs well below the text, so it looks like a line between lines rather than a mark on the words. The page is otherwise identical in both, and the "Next collection: Thursday" line, which matters more than the underline, is equally vague in both.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Version 1 (links in the three body paragraphs):** the underline sits about 4–5 px below the baseline, close to the tops of the letters on the next line. When a link wraps ("full / calendar for the year", "report it within two / working days", "replaced / for a fee of $25"), each half gets its own floating bar. These read as dividers between lines, which makes the text block look striped and the links harder to see as words you can tap. Defect, though a small one.
- **Version 2:** the underline sits tight to the baseline and breaks around the descenders in "replaced", "yearly" and "days" (Butterick, *Practical Typography*, on underlines: keep them thin and clear of descenders). Each link reads as one underlined phrase, even across a line break. Keep this.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **"Next collection: Thursday" has no date.** This is the one fact people act on. If it's Wednesday night, does "Thursday" mean tomorrow or next week? Someone who reads it the wrong way puts the bins out on the wrong day or misses the collection. Defect. Fix: give the full date ("Thursday 26 November"), and add "tomorrow" when that's true.
2. **"$25" on a page that asks for a postcode.** The page reads as a UK council, so a dollar fee looks like a mistake and makes readers doubt the figure they would pay. Defect. Fix: use the local currency and say what the fee covers ("£25 per bin").

**Smaller things**
- "Green bin for garden waste, and the blue box for glass and cans": nothing tells me whether the other bins also go out this week. Add "only" or list what's not collected.
- Below the last paragraph the page is empty. Taste: the postcode lookup is the most useful action on the page, but it's only a link buried mid-paragraph. Bring it up as a field or button straight after the summary box.

**What's working.**
- The summary box puts the answer first, ahead of the explanation, so most visitors are done in two seconds.
- The type scale is tight: heading, box title and body text, three clear levels.
- The link text says what happens ("report it within two working days"). It doesn't say "click here".

Better: version 2
