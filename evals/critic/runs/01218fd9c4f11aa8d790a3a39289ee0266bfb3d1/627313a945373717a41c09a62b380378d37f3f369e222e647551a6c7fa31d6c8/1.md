**First read.** The eye goes to the heading "Create your account", then down a tidy column of seven inputs to the dark blue "Create account" button. It reads as a standard SaaS sign-up card for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels clean and calm but anonymous: apart from the logo, it could be anyone's sign-up page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a first-time visitor who has already decided to try Quillnest needs to create an account with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The layout is orderly, the labels are clear and the button is unmistakable. What holds it back is that it asks for more than it needs to: two fields that do nothing for the person signing up, including an empty dropdown, make the form look like more work than it is. A second problem is that the way back for people who already have an account barely reads as a link.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for more than it needs.** The last two fields, "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?", don't have the red asterisk, so they're optional. But nothing says so next to them, and they look exactly like the required fields. A newcomer sees seven boxes and assumes all seven have to be filled. "How did you hear about us?" only helps your marketing, and its dropdown is completely empty with no prompt, so it reads as broken, or as a choice you're forced to make. The checklist rule is that required and optional fields are both marked and optional fields are kept few. *Defect* (it slows sign-up and adds friction at the worst moment). Fix: drop the attribution question, or ask it after sign-up. Fill in the workspace name for them (for example "Ben's notes"), or ask for it during onboarding. If either field stays, label it "(optional)" and give the dropdown a placeholder like "Choose one".
2. **"Sign in" doesn't look like a link.** In "Already have an account? Sign in" at the bottom, "Sign in" looks the same grey and weight as the question in front of it, with no underline. Returning users who landed here by mistake are exactly the people looking for it, and they have to guess. This breaks the rule that links must look clickable and must differ from body text by more than colour (Norman on signifiers; WCAG 1.4.1). *Defect.* Fix: set "Sign in" in the brand blue and underline it. Better still, also put a "Sign in" link at top right, next to where the logo sits.
3. **The password field gives no rules up front.** "Password" is a blank box with no hint about length or characters, so the first time people learn the rules is when they hit an error after pressing Create account. *Defect* (common sense, and the help-near-the-field heuristic, Nielsen 10). Fix: put a one-line hint under the field, such as "At least 8 characters", and show a show/hide toggle in the field.

**Smaller things.**
- Subtitle "Start a free workspace for your notes." is copy that could sit on anyone's page. Give one concrete reason to pick Quillnest, or what happens next ("free, no card needed").
- The "* Required field" note sits on its own line between the subtitle and the form, which adds a step. Once the optional fields are labelled or cut, you can delete it.
- Splitting the name into First name and Last name is a convention that fails some names. One "Name" field is simpler and asks for one fewer thing (taste, leaning convention).
- "Already have an account? Sign in" looks around 13 px and pale. Check that it reaches 4.5:1 contrast on white.

**What's working.**
- Every field has a label that stays visible above it, and each label sits closer to its own field than to the field above. Keep this when you cut fields, because the grouping reads without any lines.
- Size steps are few and clear: heading, then bold labels, then small helper text. The single filled, full-width button is the only strong colour on the card, so there's no doubt where the form ends.
- Required fields are marked with an asterisk, not colour alone, so colour-blind users don't lose the information.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Error and validation states (whether errors appear inline when you leave a field), focus rings, and how the card behaves on a narrow phone screen. The two name fields side by side need to stack into one column.
