**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the bold title, then moves down through three bold subheads: deposit, photos, notice. You can see it is a short checklist for renters before you read a paragraph. In version 2 the title and subheads are regular weight and the same slate grey as the body. The page reads as one even grey block, and the subheads are only a little bigger than the text under them.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a plain-language guide page for renters, which they skim to find the three things to do before and after signing.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The two versions differ in one way only, the weight and colour of the headings, and that change decides whether the page can be skimmed. Version 2 takes away the structure that makes it work as a checklist.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's headings stopped working as headings (defect).** The only change from version 1 is that the title and the three subheads lost their bold weight and their near-black colour, and now match the body text's grey. Size alone now separates them from the body: about 24 px against about 18 px for the subheads. That step is too small to show levels on its own. A renter scanning for "what about my deposit?" has to read lines instead of spotting a heading, and the page's promise of three clear actions only shows up once you read it. Refactoring UI's advice is to show hierarchy through weight and colour, not only size, and version 1 does exactly that. Fix: keep version 1's bold, darker headings.

**Smaller things** (in version 1)
- Body lines run to about 80–85 characters ("Your deposit must go into a protection scheme within thirty days, and the landlord"), a bit past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrowing the text column by about 10% would help.
- The gap between each subhead and the paragraph above it (about 30 px) is only about three times the gap below it. Each pair still groups correctly, but a little more space above the subheads would make the three sections clearer.
- "two from the landlord" leaves out "months". This is a notice period, which the reader will act on and hold the landlord to, so write "two months from the landlord".

**What's working**
- Version 1 has a clean three-level scale: title, subhead, body, with weight and colour doing the work. Keep it.
- The subheads are verbs telling the reader what to do ("Check the deposit", "Take photos on day one"), and they work as a checklist even if read alone.
- The copy is concrete and tells the reader why each step matters: "Email the photos to the agent so they carry a date" gives both the action and the reason.

Better: version 1
