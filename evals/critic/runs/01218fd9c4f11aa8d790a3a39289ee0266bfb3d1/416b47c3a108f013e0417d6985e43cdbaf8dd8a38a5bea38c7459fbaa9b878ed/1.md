**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Harbour Lights Festival", a big cream italic serif on deep navy, then goes up to the amber date, then down to the two-line description. Within a second it reads as the hero of an event page for a small coastal town festival. It feels calm, warm and a bit literary, like a harbour at dusk. Then the eye runs out of things to do: the lower quarter is empty navy and nothing tells you where to go.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the top of a web page (1280×800) that should get local people and visitors to turn up at a free town festival.

**Verdict.** The typography is well judged and the hierarchy is clear. But as an invitation it fails one basic test: it never says where the festival is, so a stranger who wants to come can't. That gap, plus the lack of any next step, is the main problem.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's no place.** *(Defect.)* You get the date, the start time and the price ("free"), but no town, no harbour name and no address. "The quay" and "the old net shed" only make sense to someone who already knows the place. For everyone else they leave a gap, and an event page exists to fill exactly that gap. Anyone who wasn't told by a friend reads it and still can't come. Fix: add the location to the amber line, e.g. "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour". That line already holds the facts people act on, so the place belongs there in the same style. Don't hide it in the paragraph.

2. **No next step.** *(Defect for a web hero; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should make its next action obvious.)* Below the paragraph the page is just empty navy, about the bottom quarter. Entry is free, so the ask isn't "buy a ticket". It's "plan your visit": a map or directions link, the programme (when do the lantern boats go out?), or "add to calendar". Pick one and give it a clear button in the empty space under the paragraph, which is the natural end of the eye's path. Don't add three equal links.

3. **"From noon" has no end, and the best moment has no time.** *(Defect, in the copy people act on.)* The draw is the lantern boats "at dusk", but the page gives only a start time. Someone deciding when to come can't tell if noon is the event or just the warm-up. Fix: give a range ("noon till late") or put a time on the main moment ("lanterns on the water from 9.30pm"). For mid-June in the UK, dusk is late, so this matters.

**Smaller things.**
- Date line: there's no year. The weekday narrows it down, but anyone who finds this page later can't tell if it's this year's festival or last year's. Add the year, or make sure the page is updated after the event.
- Headline line spacing: "Harbour Lights" and "Festival" look a little loose for display type. Close the gap slightly so the two lines read as one block (Bringhurst: display lines are set tighter than text).
- Copy: "Free entry for everyone" ends the paragraph with a separate sentence. It's important to the decision to come, so it could go up in the amber line next to the date and place.

**What's working.**
- The type contrast. A large italic serif headline against a small, plain, tracked-caps label and a sans paragraph is a clear contrast of structure, size and colour (Dair). There are three distinct levels with big steps between them, which is the right scale for this job.
- The palette. Navy ground, warm cream text and a single amber accent read as lights on dark water at night. It carries the "Harbour Lights" idea without any picture. Keep the amber for one job only: the key facts and, later, the button.
- The paragraph's specifics. "Lantern boats on the water at dusk, music on the quay, and a fish supper at the old net shed" names real things you can picture, not "a day of fun for all the family". It sounds like a real event in a real place, which makes item 1 more frustrating: the place is the one thing it leaves out.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether more content (map, programme, practical info) sits below the fold. If it does, the empty bottom band should hint at it so people scroll.
- How the large headline wraps on a phone, where "Harbour Lights" may break badly at this size.
