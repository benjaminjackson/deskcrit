**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on "Good morning, Dana", drops to the paragraph, then to the two class rows and the dark "Book a class" button. It reads as a yoga studio's mobile home screen for a member with a class pass. It feels calm and warm. In version 1 that calm breaks at the paragraph, which is set in all capitals and reads like a terms-and-conditions block.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a member opens the app, checks what's left on their pass and the rules, and books one of today's classes.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the body paragraph: version 1 sets it in all capitals, version 2 in normal sentence case. That change matters because this paragraph carries the facts the member acts on: sessions left, the expiry date, how far ahead to book, and the cancellation cutoff. In version 2 a few problems remain: the booking button doesn't say which class it books, and the cancellation line can be read two ways.

**What changed, and why it decides it**
- **Paragraph under the greeting: all caps (v1) vs sentence case (v2).** *Defect in v1.* Four and a half lines of capitals take away the word shapes readers rely on, so the paragraph reads slowly and looks like legal fine print. That's the worst look for the one block holding the dates and deadlines. Capitals also make "30 NOVEMBER" and "8 PM" no more visible than the words around them, and the block clashes with the gentle serif headline. Bringhurst and Butterick both limit capitals to short runs such as labels, not running text. Version 2 reads in one pass and fits the tone of the page.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **"Book a class" doesn't say which class it books.** *Defect.* The rows above show two classes, but the rows have no chevron, button, or selected state. The member can't tell whether to tap a row or the button, or what the button does: open a list, or book Slow flow? Fix: put the action on each row (a "Book" button per class), or make the bottom button name the class, such as "Book Slow flow, 7:00 am".
2. **"cancel by 8 pm the night before to keep the session" can be read two ways.** *Defect.* This is a rule with a penalty, so the wording has to be exact. "Keep the session" could mean you keep your place in the class, or that the session isn't taken off your pass. It's actually the second, and the line has the logic backwards from how a member thinks about it. Fix: say what happens to them, for example "Cancel by 8 pm the night before and you won't lose a session from your pass."
3. **"4 spaces" has no context.** *Defect, minor.* It doesn't say whether these are spaces left or the class size, and neither number is flagged as filling up. Fix: "4 spaces left", with a stronger colour or weight when spaces are low.

**Smaller things.**
- The three facts the member needs (six sessions left, expires 30 November, 8 pm cutoff) sit inside one paragraph. Pulling the session count and expiry date out as a short line would make them readable at a glance.
- There's a large empty area below the button. Today's other classes could go there, or the button could sit higher.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clear: one serif headline, then the body text, with bold row titles and regular details. Every level has an obvious job.
- The warm off-white background, the dark ink colour and one dark pill-shaped button give the page a calm, consistent look that suits a yoga studio.
- The class rows are easy to scan: name on top, time and room underneath, spaces on the right.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The image block at the top is a plain gradient. It could be a placeholder or a photo that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken, so check it on a live device.

Better: version 2
