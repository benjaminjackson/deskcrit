**First read.** Both versions are the same account overview: a "Your account" heading, a left nav, and four equal cards in a 2×2 grid. In version 1 the eye goes to the dark, heavy card outlines before the content. You see five black boxes first and the details inside them second. In version 2 the outlines drop to a pale grey hairline, so the eye goes straight to the heading and then to the card titles. It feels calm and orderly.

**What it's for.** My assumption is that this is the account home page of a small online shop. A returning customer uses it to check their details at a glance and jump to one area to change something.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the border weight, and it is the right one. In version 1 the dark outlines are the loudest thing on the page and compete with the information they hold. Version 2 lets the content lead. Its remaining weakness is the large empty space at the bottom of each card.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **Card and nav borders: dark 2px outlines (v1) became light grey hairlines (v2).** This is a defect in v1. On a page with no images or colour, the dark outlines are the strongest marks present. They carry more visual weight than the "Your account" heading and the bold card titles, so the structure out-shouts the content. Five equal black frames also make the page look like a wireframe rather than a finished product. The cards only need to group content, and v2's pale edge does that with less ink (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*: use fewer and lighter borders, and let spacing and subtle edges separate things). Now the hierarchy runs as it should: page title, then card titles, then details, then blue links. Text, sizes and positions are otherwise identical. A 1px shift in the inner padding is not something a viewer would notice.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The cards are much taller than their content.** This is a defect. Each card's text and link fill about the top half, leaving a blank band of roughly 110–140px below. The cards look half-loaded, and the page is pushed taller than it needs to be. On a laptop screen the payment card's link sits close to the fold for no reason. The fix is to size cards to their content, or to set the row height by the fuller card and nothing more.

**Smaller things.**
- Left nav: the current page ("Account") is shown only by bold weight. Add a clearer active state, such as a tint or bar, so it reads as "you are here" and not just as emphasis.
- Left nav: "Sign out" sits in the same list as the destinations, with the same weight and spacing. Separate it with a gap or rule so nobody mistakes it for a section.
- Left nav: the box is also taller than its five items, the same padding problem as the cards.
- "Recent order" card: "View all orders" sits under a single order. A link to *this* order ("View order 48213") is the action this card actually sets up.

**What's working.**
- Version 2's light borders. Keep the hairline weight so the content carries the page.
- The copy is specific and complete: "Card ending 4417 / Expires 09/28" and "Order 48213, placed 12 October … Delivered 15 October" answer the customer's question without a click.
- Each card ends in one plainly worded blue link ("Edit details", "Change card"), so the next action in every area is obvious. Only the recent-order card would benefit from a sharper target, as noted above.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the links and nav items, and how the 2×2 grid reflows on narrow screens.

Better: version 2
