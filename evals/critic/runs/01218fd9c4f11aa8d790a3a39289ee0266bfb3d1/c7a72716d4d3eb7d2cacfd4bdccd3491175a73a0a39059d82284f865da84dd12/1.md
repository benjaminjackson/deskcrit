**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", then down to the orange button. That path is clean and quick. Next it hits the grey "Keep your streak going" box directly under the button and slows down, because that box is talking to someone who already uses the product. The teal speech-bubble panel on the right gets a glance and gives nothing back. It reads as a calm, competent, fairly templated landing page for a language-learning app.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing homepage aimed at adults who haven't signed up yet, and its job is to get them to start the free week.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The promise is specific, there is one clear action, and the order of reading is right. Two things hold it back: a retention message sits in the spot that should back up the sign-up, and the illustration takes up half the hero without showing what makes this product different.

**What would move it most**

1. **The streak box is aimed at the wrong person and sits in the wrong place.** (Defect.) It's directly under "Start your free week", where a hesitant visitor looks for a reason to click. What it says ("Keep your streak going") only makes sense to someone who already has a streak. A newcomer reads an instruction they can't act on, and the grey panel looks like a notice or tooltip rather than part of the pitch. It also makes the left column run about 90 px below the illustration, so the hero ends unevenly and crowds the steps row. The statistic doesn't land either: "finish the course twice as often" leaves the viewer to work out "than whom?" and "why does that matter to me?" **Fix:** take it out of the hero. If you want proof under the button, use something a newcomer can judge for themselves, such as what a learner can say after week one, or the stat rewritten to speak to them ("Five days in a row and you're twice as likely to finish").

2. **The illustration is generic and sits off the grid.** (Taste on the image, convention on the grid.) Two empty speech bubbles could belong to any chat, support or messaging product. Your actual difference, which your own step 2 names as "get feedback on each word", is nowhere in the picture, so half the hero adds nothing to the case. The panel also stops about 150 px short of the right margin that the nav and the steps row both use, so the top half and the bottom half of the page don't share a right edge (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). **Fix:** show the product doing its one thing: a phrase with per-word feedback marked on it, or a short exchange with the tutor. Size the panel so it ends on the same right edge as "Log in" and the third step.

3. **The subhead leaves out the two facts people decide on.** (Defect, because it's about what the viewer is committing to.) "A tutor that listens" can be read two ways: a human tutor, or software. That difference changes what people expect on price, scheduling and quality, and someone who guesses wrong will feel misled after signing up. "Short daily lessons" plus "ten weeks" is a promise with no amount attached, so the viewer can't tell whether it means 5 minutes a day or 30. **Fix:** say who or what listens ("an AI tutor that listens and corrects you", or the human version) and give the daily time ("15 minutes a day").

**Smaller things**
- **Headline line break:** it currently splits as "…conversation in / ten weeks". Break it as "Hold a real conversation / in ten weeks" so the time promise stays together.
- **Heading sizes:** the streak box heading and the "1. Listen / 2. Speak / 3. Use it" headings look like almost the same bold size, 1 px or so apart. Once the box is gone this sorts itself out. If it stays, make the two clearly different or exactly the same.
- **Steps row:** the three steps have no heading, so it's unclear what they describe. A short lead-in like "Every lesson, three steps" would tell the viewer this is the lesson format.
- **Spacing above the steps:** the gap between the hero and the steps (about 50 px) is barely bigger than the gaps inside the hero, so the two sections don't separate clearly. Open it up.

**What's working**
- **The headline:** a concrete result plus a time frame, set big and bold. It's the right thing to see first. Keep the specificity, and keep its size well above everything else.
- **One loud action:** "Start your free week" is the only saturated warm colour on the page, against teal and greys, so there's no doubt what to do. The label says exactly what you get.
- **The steps copy:** each step uses one verb and one concrete line ("Say it back and get feedback on each word"). It's the clearest writing on the page, and the illustration should borrow from it.
