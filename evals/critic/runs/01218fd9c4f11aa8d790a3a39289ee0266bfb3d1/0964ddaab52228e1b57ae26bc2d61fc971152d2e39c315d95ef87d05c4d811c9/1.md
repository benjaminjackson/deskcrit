**First read.** The eye goes straight to "River ferry timetable", a large bold heading in the upper left, and then finds nothing. Under it sits a small grey smudge of three short paragraphs, and the rest of the frame, about 90% of it, is empty white. It looks like a page that failed to load, or a heading someone forgot to finish.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public web page where passengers look up when the river ferry leaves and how long the crossing takes.

**Verdict.** It doesn't work. The only element anyone can read is the heading. The body text is too small to read even at full resolution, and the page promises a timetable it doesn't show.

**What would move it most**

1. **The body text can't be read.** *Defect.* The three paragraphs under the heading are set at roughly 3–4 px cap height, on lines about 100 px wide. I cropped them at native resolution and still couldn't reliably make out the words. I could only piece together a rough gist: the route from a north landing to a market quay, a sailing roughly every forty minutes, crossing time, bikes on the lower deck, the winter service, and where printed timetables are posted. A passenger gets none of that. This looks like a scaling or CSS bug, for example a transform or a root font size gone wrong, more than a design choice, but on screen the result is the same. Fix: set the body at normal reading size (about 16 px or more on the web) and let the lines run to 45–75 characters (Bringhurst). At that size these paragraphs will fill the column the heading sets up.

2. **The "timetable" has no times in it.** *Defect.* The heading promises departure times. A passenger arriving here wants one answer: when is the next boat? From what I could make out, the page answers with prose, roughly "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", and even that is buried in the first paragraph. "Early morning" and "late evening" aren't times, and "before dark" in winter changes every day. These are exactly the lines people act on and later hold the operator to. Fix: put an actual timetable at the top: first and last sailing from each landing, the interval, and the winter pattern with clock times, set as a table or a short list of times. Move the prose about bikes, tides and noticeboards below it as supporting notes.

3. **There's no hierarchy between heading and content.** *Defect, and it mostly follows from 1.* The page has two type sizes: a very large heading and microscopic text. Nothing sits in between, so the heading carries the whole page and the empty canvas makes it look broken rather than spare. Once the body is at reading size, you need a clear scale of about three levels (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI): the heading, a mid-size level for the times or for subheads such as "From North Landing" and "From Market Quay", and body text. Then the page reads as complete.

**Smaller things.**
- The body text looks mid-grey, and at that size it would fail contrast even if it could be read. Check it against WCAG 1.4.3 (4.5:1) once it's resized.
- Nothing appears to mark a change of service or today's notice. If the noticeboard changes matter, give them a visible spot near the times.

**What's working.**
- The heading is plain, says exactly what the page is, and has clear left alignment and a sensible margin. Keep it, along with the left edge it sets for the content.
- The underlying content looks well chosen for passengers: interval, crossing time, bikes, winter service, where to find updates. It just needs to be readable, and to lead with the times.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiny text is the intended design or a rendering or capture fault: check in a live browser at 100% zoom. The empty lower page could also be content that hadn't loaded.
