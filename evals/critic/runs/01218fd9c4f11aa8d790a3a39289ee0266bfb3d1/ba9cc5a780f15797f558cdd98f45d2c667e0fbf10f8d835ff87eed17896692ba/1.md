**First read.** The eye goes to "Project notes" and then straight down a stack of five heavy dark-outlined boxes. The outlines are the darkest, thickest marks on the page, so you see five compartments before you read any words. Once you do read, it turns out to be a project plan in five stages, from Kickoff to Handover. It feels tidy and clear but heavy, a bit like a wireframe.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal page that shows a team the stages of a design project in order, so they can see what each stage involves and what comes next.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The copy is unusually good and the type is clean. What holds it back is the boxes: their heavy borders are the loudest thing on the page, and they break a single ordered sequence into five unrelated tiles, which hides the most important fact about the content, that these stages happen in order.

**What would move it most**

1. **The borders outshout the content** (defect, plus convention). Each stage sits inside a near-black outline about 3 px thick, darker and heavier than any text stroke on the page. Your eye keeps landing on the frames, and the page reads as a grid of compartments instead of a list of stages. Lupton warns against exactly this: rules and boxes should "illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell" (*Thinking with Type*, p. 157). Tufte makes the same point about ink that carries no information. Fix: remove the borders, or replace them with space or a pale tone, so the lines stop competing with the content (Lupton). If the cards need an edge, a 1 px light-grey rule or a faint tint is enough.

2. **The order is invisible** (defect). These are clearly stages in sequence: you agree the scope, then visit sites, then sketch, review and hand over. But nothing on the page says so. There are no numbers, no connecting line and no "step 1 of 5". Each one is boxed the same way with the same gap, so they read as five unrelated items you could take in any order. The title makes it worse: "Project notes" tells the viewer these are loose notes, when they're a process. Fix: make the order visible, either by numbering each stage or by joining them with one continuous line or rail instead of separate boxes. Retitle the page to say what it is, for example "How a project runs: five stages".

**Smaller things.**
- The gap between cards (about 20 px) is close to the padding inside them (about 25 px), so the space doesn't separate one stage from the next any better than the borders do. Once the borders soften, either tighten the inside padding or widen the gaps so the groups read from space alone.
- The boxed, bordered cards look like they might be clickable. If they're static, losing the heavy outline also removes that false signal. If they do open, they need a clear cue such as a chevron.
- The column sits left with roughly 360 px of empty space on the right. That's fine for a document, but the column (about 760 px) is wider than the one-line descriptions need, so each card is mostly empty on the right. A narrower column would tighten it.

**What's working.**
- **The copy.** Every description starts with a verb and says who does what: "Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need." "Pick one direction and list the open questions." Nothing is left for the reader to fill in. Keep it exactly like this.
- **The type scale.** There are three clear levels: the page title, the stage name in bold, and the body text in regular weight. The difference between stage name and description comes mostly from weight, not size, which is the right tool for it (Refactoring UI). Don't add more sizes.
- **Contrast and alignment.** All text is dark on white and easily readable, and the stage names and descriptions share one clean left edge.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell whether the cards are interactive (hover, focus, expand). If they are, check that they have visible focus states and a clear sign that they open.
