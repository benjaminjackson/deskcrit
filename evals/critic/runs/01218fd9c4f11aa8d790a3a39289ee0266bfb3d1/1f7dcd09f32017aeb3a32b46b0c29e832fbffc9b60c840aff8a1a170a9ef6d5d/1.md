**First read.** The eye goes to the serif headline, "Savings deposits rose every month", then down the six blue bars to the tall June bar at the right edge, then to "$4.82M" in the first card. In about three seconds it reads as a calm, trustworthy half-year update from a credit union to its members. It feels clean and well made. The thing that sticks is June's jump, and the page doesn't explain it where you're looking.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public page in the member report that tells members their savings deposits grew in the first half of 2026, backed by a chart and three key figures.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The chart is honest and clearly labelled, and the figures agree with each other. The biggest problem is that the largest feature on the page, June's jump to $1150k, is a one-off bonus payroll. That is explained only in the footnote, so the headline's story of steady growth rests partly on a spike the viewer is never told about where it shows.

**What would move it most**

1. **June's spike goes unexplained at the spot where it shows (defect).** June rises by $282k, more than four times the step between any two earlier months, and it's the tallest, darkest thing on the page. The only explanation, "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll", is in small grey type below the chart panel, which most viewers won't read. So a member takes away "deposits are speeding up", which the data doesn't support. The same issue affects two of the three cards: "Average deposit, Jun 2026" and "Accounts that made a deposit, Jun 2026" both measure the bonus month. "Up $11 on Jun 2025" only compares like with like if June 2025 also had a bonus payroll, and the page doesn't say whether it did.
   - Put the note on the chart itself, as a short label next to the June bar ("includes mid-year bonus payroll").
   - Say in the card captions whether the June 2025 comparison also had a bonus.
   - A chart's annotations should state its key points on the chart, not leave them for a footnote (Tufte, *Visual Display*, on integrity; the checklist's "titles and annotations state the takeaway").

2. **The headline states a fact but doesn't say what it means for the member (defect, in the copy).** "Savings deposits rose every month" describes a pattern. Monthly growth from January to June can be normal for the season, and the chart has no last-year line to show whether this is good. The real evidence that it's good is in the first card, "Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025", set in small green text. A member reading this wants to know what it means for them: is the credit union healthy, and are their savings safe and growing? Two fixes:
   - Lead with the year-on-year comparison, since that's the one the viewer can actually judge.
   - End the subhead on what it means for the member, rather than repeating the chart title. Right now "New money paid into member savings accounts, by month…" and "Deposits into savings accounts, US dollars per month" say nearly the same thing.

**Smaller things**
- **Number formats don't match.** The chart says "$1150k" and "$1200k", while the cards say "$4.82M". Use $1.15M and $1.2M on the chart, or at least add thousands separators.
- **"Up $11 on Jun 2025" and "Up 402 on Jun 2025".** "On" can read as "on that date". "Than in June 2025" or "vs June 2025" has only one reading, which matters because these are the numbers people remember.
- **The bottoms don't line up.** The card stack ends around y≈618 and the chart panel at y≈693, leaving a ragged gap in the lower right. Either align the bottoms or let the cards stack without trying to match the chart.
- **The chart repeats its values.** Every bar has a value label, so the four gridlines and the y-axis add nothing. Drop or lighten them to keep more of the ink on the data (Tufte, data-ink).
- **The source line looks small and light grey on a light grey background.** Check it reaches 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.3), especially since it carries the bonus-payroll caveat.

**What's working**
- **The chart is honest.** The bars start at $0, months are in time order, each bar has its value printed on it, there is one colour, units are in the title, and there's a source with a cut-off date. Keep all of it.
- **The figures check out.** The six months add up to $4.82M, and $214 × 5,374 ≈ $1.15M, which matches June's bar. That consistency builds trust, so keep it.
- **The type is clear and restrained.** There's one serif display size for the headline, a big sans size for the card figures, and a few small sizes with clear jobs. Each card has a label, a number and a comparison, so none of them is a bare figure.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the green "Up…" lines meet contrast at their real size. They look fine, but measure them.
