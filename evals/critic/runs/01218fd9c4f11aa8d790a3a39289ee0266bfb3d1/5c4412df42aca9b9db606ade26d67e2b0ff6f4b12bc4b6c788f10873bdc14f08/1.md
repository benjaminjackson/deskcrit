**First read.** The eye goes to "Pricing", then moves across three identical cards, reading $9, $29, $79 from left to right. Then it stops, because there is nothing to do there. The only button on the page is "Start free" in the top-right corner, outside the path the eye just took. It feels clean, calm and generic, like a default SaaS template.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the pricing page for a scheduling or booking product. Its job is to help a small team or solo user pick a plan and sign up.

**Verdict.** It looks tidy, but it doesn't do the job of a pricing page. The cards give no way to choose a plan and no help deciding. The prices can also be read two ways, so a buyer can't tell what they'd actually pay.

**What would move it most**

1. **The cards have no action** (defect). Each of the three cards ends after its third feature, with no button. A viewer who has decided "Team" has nothing to click. They have to find "Start free" in the header, which doesn't say which plan it starts or what "free" means: a trial, a free tier, or how long it lasts. The page asks the viewer to choose but gives them nowhere to act on the choice. Fix: put a button at the bottom of each card that names the outcome, such as "Start 14-day Team trial" if that's the offer. Make one of them the filled primary, and the other two outlined.

2. **Nothing tells the viewer which plan is for them** (defect). The cards share the same border, weight and layout, so the page treats all three plans as equally likely. Most viewers want to be told "this is the one most teams like you pick." With nothing to land on, they have to compare the cards one line at a time. Fix: make one plan dominant, probably Team given the "for teams" subtitle. Use a filled or accent border, a short "Most popular" label, or slightly more height. Then add one line under each plan name saying who it's for, in the viewer's terms ("For one person taking bookings", "For a team sharing one booking page").

3. **The prices and features can be read two ways** (defect, in the place where it matters most). "$29 / month" could be per user or a flat price per account. A team of eight could pay $29 or $232, and they'll hold you to whichever one they assumed. The feature lists don't build on each other either. Business lists SSO and priority support but not "Booking page" or "Reports", so a reader could fairly conclude it lacks them. And "1 calendar" doesn't explain itself: is a calendar one person, or one bookable resource? Fix: write "per account" or "per user" next to every price. Start the Team and Business lists with "Everything in Solo, plus" and "Everything in Team, plus". Say what a calendar is ("1 person's calendar").

4. **The copy could sit on anyone's page** (taste, argued). "Pricing" just labels the page, and "Simple plans for teams of every size" is the default line of a thousand SaaS sites. It gives no reason to pick Tidewell. The second sentence, "Change or cancel whenever you like", is the one that actually reassures people. Fix: use the headline to make a promise in the viewer's terms. Lead with no-risk terms such as the trial, no card required and cancel anytime, if those are true.

**Smaller things**
- The subtitle wraps so that "you like." sits alone on the second line, a runt. Rebreak it or widen the measure so it fits on one line.
- The feature list text looks smaller and greyer than the nav links. Set it at least at nav size, at body contrast.
- Each card lists only three features, so the reader has too little to compare. A short "Compare all features" link or a table below the cards would help.
- The bottom quarter of the frame is empty. Use it for annual pricing, an FAQ or customer logos, rather than leaving it unused. That's my judgment, and it depends on what lies below the fold.

**What's working**
- The prices are set large and bold, with "/ month" lighter and smaller. That's the right way to rank figure over unit; keep it.
- The three cards follow one layout (name, price, list) with equal gaps between them, so the eye reads across them easily. Keep that structure when you add the button and the highlight.
- The blue underline under "Pricing" in the nav clearly shows where you are, and the single blue accent is held back for interactive things.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether more content (FAQ, plan comparison, footer) sits below the fold. The blank lower area could just be where the capture stopped. Scroll the live page to check.
