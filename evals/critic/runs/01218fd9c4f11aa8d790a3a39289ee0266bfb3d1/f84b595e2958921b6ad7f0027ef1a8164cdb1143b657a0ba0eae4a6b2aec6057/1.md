**First read.** The eye goes straight to the dark middle card. It's the only dark mass on a white page, and the eye lands there before reading the page heading. Then it moves to the two blue buttons on either side, which outweigh the middle card's own outlined button. It reads as "three walking routes, one of them featured". But nothing says why that one is featured, and on a second look the middle card seems to come from a different design system. It feels tidy but unsettled, almost like a bug.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page listing walking routes on a local or tourism site, where someone compares routes and picks one to open.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The middle card breaks the card pattern in five ways at once and gives no reason, so the viewer can't tell whether it's being recommended or just rendered wrong. Behind that, the cards hide the facts people compare routes on (distance, difficulty, surface) inside sentences.

**What would move it most**
1. **The middle card breaks the pattern in every way at once.** Compared with its neighbours, "Woodland loop" has:
   - a dark fill
   - a serif heading
   - much tighter padding (the text sits about half as far from the edge)
   - square corners instead of rounded ones
   - a yellow outlined pill button instead of a filled blue rectangle

   When one of three equal items looks this different, viewers read it as special. There's no "Recommended" or "Easiest" label to say why, so the emphasis promises something the page never explains. Several of the changes also look like accidents rather than choices. The cramped padding and square corners make it look like a component from another stylesheet. This breaks "repeated elements follow one program" (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Müller-Brockmann on breaking a grid decisively or not at all). **Defect**, because it misleads the viewer about the choice. Fix: decide whether this route is meant to be featured.
   - **If it isn't:** make it match the other two cards.
   - **If it is:** keep the same padding, corners, heading face and button shape, change one thing (the fill or a border), and add a label that says why.

2. **The actions are weighted the wrong way round.** The middle card is the loudest thing on the page, but its button is the weakest of the three: an outline, where the others are solid blue. So the eye goes to the featured route, and the strongest call to action pulls it away to the routes beside it. This is a **convention** (one high-emphasis action, set by one rule; Refactoring UI, Material). Fix: give all three cards the same button. If one route is featured, its button should be at least as strong as the others, never weaker.

3. **The facts for choosing a route are buried in prose.** Distance, how steep it is, the surface, and who it suits (bikes, benches, a cafe) are all inside sentences, written differently on each card ("Six kilometres", "Four kilometres", "Ten kilometres"). To compare three routes, the viewer has to read all three paragraphs and keep the numbers in their head. This goes against Krug's point that pages should be self-evident and scannable. **Defect** for a page whose whole purpose is comparing routes. Fix: add a short line of facts in the same place on every card, such as "6 km · 2 steep climbs · cafe", with the distance as a numeral. Keep the sentence as colour under it.

4. **Each card's bottom half is empty.** All three cards stretch to about 440 px tall, but their content ends around halfway down. The result is a band of dead, boxed-in white about 200 px tall, so the cards look unfinished, as if an image or details never loaded. **Taste/defect**: it reads as missing content. Fix: either size the cards to their content, or use the space for something that helps the choice, such as a route map, a photo, or the facts line from point 3. If an image was meant to go there, see the last section.

**Smaller things.**
- The heading baselines don't line up across the three columns: "Woodland loop" sits higher because of its padding and typeface. Matching the padding fixes this.
- The page uses two heading typefaces (a sans-serif for "Cliff path" and "Canal towpath", a serif for "Woodland loop"), and the difference reads as a clash rather than a deliberate contrast. Use one.
- All three buttons say "View route". Something more specific helps people choosing without a mouse and people using screen readers, e.g. "See the cliff path map" (WCAG 2.4.4, link purpose).
- "Walking routes" is a generic heading that tells the viewer nothing about where these routes are. Name the place.
- The first and third cards have a pale grey border that looks faint, and the middle card has a hairline border on a dark fill. Check that both are at least 3:1 against the background if the borders are meant to show where each card ends (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- The page heading, card titles and body text have clear, well-separated sizes. There are three levels and none of them collide, so keep that scale.
- The descriptions are concrete and written for walkers ("benches every few hundred metres", "good for bicycles"). That copy is worth keeping as the second line under the facts.
- The three-column grid, with even gaps and a generous outer margin, is calm and easy to scan.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether an image or map was meant to fill the empty lower half of each card and failed to load. Check this in a live browser.
- What the buttons look like on hover and keyboard focus, especially the yellow outline on the dark card.
