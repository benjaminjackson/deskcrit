**First read.** The eye goes straight to the four-line black headline, then drops to the paragraph. It reads as the top of a ferry timetable page: clean, calm, competent. Then the reader looks for the timetable, and there isn't one. The page tells you the ferry exists but never when to be at the landing.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page for river ferry passengers who want to know when the ferry leaves and how long the trip takes.

**Verdict.** The typography is sound. The page fails because it has no times on it. The last line says the heading is "cramped, overlapping", but that's wrong: at full resolution no descender touches the line below. The real problem is the missing information.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no actual timetable** (defect). The label says "Spring timetable" and the paragraph says "every forty minutes", but nothing says when the first boat leaves, when the last one leaves, or which days it runs. "By daylight" and "spring through early summer" are both vague, and daylight changes by more than an hour over that period. A passenger can't plan a trip from this, and someone who guesses arrives to find the ferry not running. Add the hard facts under the paragraph: first and last departure from each landing, the days it runs, and the start and end dates of the season. The usual form is a short table of departure times, or "First boat 7:00, last 19:40, daily, 1 April – 30 June".
2. **The closing sentence is not page content** (defect). "The cramped, overlapping heading makes this page hard to read" looks like a review note left in the build. It's set in the same style as the real paragraph, so a passenger reads it as something the ferry operator is saying about its own page. It's also false: the heading's line spacing is tight, which is right for a heading this big, but the lines don't collide. Delete it and put the timetable in that spot.
3. **The headline is a sentence where a name should be** (taste, with a cost). Its 14 words run to four lines at display size, and "summer" ends up alone on the last line. Those words are doing the subhead's job, so the page's biggest element is the slowest thing to read. Make the headline short ("Quayside ferry"), and move "Spring–early summer, daylight crossings" into a smaller line or the dates in the timetable. That leaves a one- or two-line headline with no stranded word.

**Smaller things.**
- Body paragraph: "weather." sits alone on the last line. Adjust the text width or wording so the last line has a few words.
- "Each crossing takes about twelve minutes in calm weather" leaves the reader to wonder what happens when it isn't calm. Say what the reader should expect, e.g. "allow 20 minutes when it's windy".
- The paragraph doesn't say whether the 40-minute service runs both ways or only north to south. Say it once, as part of the timetable.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three sizes (small grey bold label, large heavy headline, body text) with big steps between them, so each size has an obvious job. Keep it.
- The line spacing in the heading suits a heading this size: the lines read as one block without touching. Leave it alone whatever the stray note says.
- Body text looks to be about 70–75 characters per line, at the upper end of Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Everything is set flush left on one margin, with a generous empty field to the right. That structure will hold a timetable without needing a redesign.
