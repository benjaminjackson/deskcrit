**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Planning a vegetable bed", then drops down the left edge through three subheads that are easy to pick out. It reads as a short how-to article or help page for a beginner gardener. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, a bit like a default document, and nothing about it fights the reader.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short instructional web page that a first-time gardener reads through once or scans for one step.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the type scale is tight and the copy is unusually good. The one real fault is that the paragraph lines are too long for comfortable reading. It's a small page that's close to finished, not a weak one.

**What would move it most**
1. **The lines are too long.** In the body paragraphs, the "Preparing the soil" line runs to about 90 characters ("Dig to the depth … Rake the surface level"), and the column looks about 790 px wide. Bringhurst gives 45 to 75 characters as comfortable and about 66 as ideal (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). At this width the eye has a long trip back to the start of each next line, and the short paragraphs break as one long line plus a stub ("and leave it to settle for a week."). That makes each paragraph look ragged rather than like a block. The fix is to narrow the text column to roughly 600–640 px at this size, so lines land around 65 characters. The empty right side of the page already has room for this, and it will make that space look chosen rather than left over. *Convention, and it costs something here.*

**Smaller things.**
- **Headings change size only.** The subheads ("Preparing the soil", "Watering") use the body's weight and colour and differ from it only in size. The size step is big enough that the hierarchy still works, so this isn't a defect. A medium or semibold subhead at a slightly smaller size would look more deliberate and less like a default (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9). *Taste.*
- **Subhead to body gap is tight.** Each subhead's descenders sit very close to the first line of its paragraph. The grouping is right, with much more space above than below, but a few more pixels under each subhead would stop it looking cramped.
- **Apostrophe.** In "last year's heap" the apostrophe looks straight rather than curly. Check it at full size and use a typographic apostrophe.
- **Order of steps.** The sections are sequential (site, soil, plants, water), but nothing marks them as steps. If readers are meant to follow them in order, numbering the subheads would say so.

**What's working.**
- **Type scale.** There are three clear sizes: title, subhead and body, with big steps between them and nothing in between. Each size has one job. Keep this when you change the weights.
- **Copy.** Every line tells the reader exactly what to do, with a quantity or a specific case: "six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans", "twice a week, rather than a little every day". A beginner can act on it without guessing. Don't trim it.
- **Spacing and colour.** Subheads sit with the text they introduce, and the near-black text on white has plenty of contrast. The page reads in one pass.
