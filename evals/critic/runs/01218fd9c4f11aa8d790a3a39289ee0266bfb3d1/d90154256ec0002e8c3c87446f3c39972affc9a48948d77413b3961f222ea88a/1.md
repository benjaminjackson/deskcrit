**First read.** Three saturated blocks of equal size (orange, green, purple) hit at once, and the eye moves left to right across the three white headlines with nowhere to stop. It reads as a swimming pool's programme page: adult lessons, lane swimming, family sessions. It feels bright and friendly. It also feels unfinished, because the bottom half of every card is empty colour with nothing in it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a section of a local pool's website, meant to get adults and families to pick a session and book it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do that job yet. The cards say what exists, but nothing tells the viewer when exactly, for how much, or how to sign up, and no offer leads. The line under the cards says "one offer clearly leads the page", which is false. All three cards carry exactly the same weight, so I've treated that line as copy, not evidence.

**What would move it most**

1. **There is no way to act. Defect.** None of the three cards has a button, link, price, or booking route. A parent who wants Family hour gets "Sunday afternoons" and then a dead end. The page describes the sessions and leaves the viewer to find out how to join. Fix: give each card one exact ask that names the session, like "Book a Tuesday class" or "See lane times". Put it in the same spot on every card so it can be scanned across all three. Don't use "Learn more", which could sit on anyone's page.

2. **No card leads, so the viewer has to choose with no guidance. Defect, because hierarchy is the stated intent.** Same size, same type, same layout, and three equally loud colours: nothing gets seen first (Refactoring UI; Müller-Brockmann on a clear order of importance). If the pool has a priority, such as filling the adult classes, make that card dominant. Make it larger or wider, or keep the strong colour for it and calm the other two. If the three really are equal, drop the claim that one leads and let the order and the asks do the work.

3. **The empty lower half of each card is wasted space. Taste, though it costs something real.** The copy fills roughly the top 45% of each card, and the remaining 300 px or so of flat colour holds nothing. It reads as a placeholder or a missing image, not as space left on purpose (Ruder: empty space has to be held in tension with the content, not left over). Either fill that area with what the viewer needs (exact times, price, level, the ask) or shorten the cards so the colour frames the content.

4. **The copy leaves out the facts people act on. Defect.** "Tuesday evenings", "Sunday afternoons" and "every morning from six" have no clock time, length, price, or age and level guidance. These are the details a viewer plans around and later holds the pool to. "Three speeds to choose from" makes sense to a regular swimmer, but a newcomer can't tell whether they're fast enough. Say it in their terms: "slow, medium and fast lanes, so you swim at your own pace". Add the exact times (for example "Tuesdays 7–8pm"), plus price or "free with membership".

**Smaller things**
- Colour does no job here. Orange, green and purple are equally saturated and don't encode anything, so they compete instead of sorting the cards. Tie colour to a meaning (for example, audience) or keep one strong hue for the lead card.
- The right margin looks wider than the left (about 96 px against 56 px). Check whether the row is meant to fill the width or sit centred.
- The line under the cards is set like body text with no visible role. If it's a real caption it needs a purpose, and if it's a review note it shouldn't ship.

**What's working**
- The type scale is clean: two sizes with a big step between them, bold display headlines set tight in two lines, and body copy at a comfortable length. Keep it.
- The headlines are verb-led and plain ("Learn to swim", "Join the lanes"), so each one names what you'd do, not a category. Keep that voice when you write the asks.
- White text looks like it has enough contrast on all three backgrounds. Still check the orange with a contrast tool against the 4.5:1 minimum for body text.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cards are clickable as a whole. Nothing on them suggests they are, which is part of finding 1.
