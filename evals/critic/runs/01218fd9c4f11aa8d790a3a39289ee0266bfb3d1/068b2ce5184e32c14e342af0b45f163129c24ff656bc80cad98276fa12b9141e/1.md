**First read.** The eye goes to the big title, then drops straight into the large red tile, then to "Rose arch". From there it moves round a tidy mosaic of coloured blocks. This reads as an online gallery for a garden show: a featured garden, then six smaller ones. It feels clean, orderly and a little bare. The "images" are flat colour with a pale circle, so this looks like a layout with placeholder art, not photographs.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a web gallery page where visitors browse a show's gardens and pick one to look at more closely.

**Verdict.** The layout works. There is one clear lead, a real grid, and the order is easy to follow. What holds it back is that everything past the hero is too small and too pale to read comfortably. The captions are the content here, and they're in the weakest type on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The small-tile captions are too small and too grey.** This covers "Herb bed / Thyme, sage and chives." and the five like it. The descriptions look like about 12–13 px grey text on white. On a gallery page that text is the only thing telling a visitor what each garden is, and right now they have to lean in to read it. They're also lighter than the stray note at the bottom of the page. *Defect* (WCAG 1.4.3 needs 4.5:1 for normal text; check the actual grey). The fix: set the descriptions at body size, around 15–16 px, and darken the grey until it clearly passes. Keep the bold title to show which line is the label.
2. **The type scale has too many sizes, and they sit too close together.** I count six: the page title (~64), the hero title (~36), the hero line (~18), the bold tile labels (~14), the tile descriptions (~13) and the footer note (~15). The 13, 14 and 15 sizes don't read as separate levels; they read as sizes picked one at a time (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). *Convention.* The fix: use four levels. Keep the page title and the hero title, then one body size for all descriptions and the hero line. Mark the tile labels with weight, not size.
3. **The copy doesn't say whose show this is.** "Garden show gallery" could head any show's page. There's no name, place or year, so a visitor who lands from a search or a shared link can't confirm they're in the right place. *Defect* for a page meant to be found and shared. The fix: name the show and the date in the title, or in a line directly under it.

**Smaller things.**
- The footer line "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size" looks like leftover review or test text. It's also untrue, since the hero is clearly larger. Remove it before this ships.
- All seven tiles are fully saturated and equally loud, so only size marks the hero. This is fine with placeholders. With real photos, check that the hero still leads.
- Every tile, including the hero, uses a very wide crop, about 2.5:1. Gardens photographed upright or square will lose most of the plants. *Taste*: consider a less extreme ratio for the small tiles.

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** The hero's width equals the two bottom tiles plus their gutter, so the left edges line up and the right column keeps an even rhythm. The page reads as one system, not as a pile of cards. Keep this when the type changes.
- **One clear lead.** Size alone makes "Rose arch" the featured garden, and its larger caption confirms it, so the order of the page is obvious at a glance.
- **Even gutters and a generous left margin.** These keep a busy colour field calm.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The tiles hold placeholder art, so I couldn't judge how real photographs sit against the captions, or whether the hero still leads once real photos are in.
- I couldn't judge whether the tiles are links, or how they respond to hover and focus.
