**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline, then goes straight to the four cards. The bright illustrations pull harder than anything else below the headline. It reads as a local home-services company: warm, tidy, trustworthy enough. Then the eye stops. It reaches the bottom of the cards and finds nothing to do. The only button, "Book a visit", is small and up in the top-right corner, and the eye has already passed it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage hero for a consumer home-services business. Its job is to get a homeowner to pick a service and book it.

**Verdict.** It looks competent and calm, but it doesn't close. The page tells you to "pick a service, choose a day", yet nothing in the main view lets you do either. The prices you'd decide on don't say what they're a price for.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's no action where the decision gets made. (Defect.)** The subhead gives a three-step process: pick, choose a day, crew arrives. The cards are where you'd pick. But they show no sign they can be clicked: no button, no arrow, no "Book" link. The only action is the small "Book a visit" button in the header, which the eye passes on the way in. So a visitor who has just chosen "Deep cleaning" has to go back up to the corner and hope it remembers their choice. That's a signifier failure (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*), and it makes the user think (Krug). Put a specific action on each card, such as "Book window cleaning", or make the whole card read as a button. Also put one primary action under the subhead that starts step one. The empty band of about 150 px below the cards shows there's room for it.

2. **The prices can be read more than one way. (Defect.)** "From $89", "From $189", "From $24" and "From $65" have no unit. Is $89 per visit, per window, or per hour? Is laundry $24 per bag, per load, or per week? Is garden care $65 per mow or per month? These are the numbers a customer remembers and later holds you to. If someone reads $24 as "a week of laundry" and gets billed per bag, that becomes a complaint. Add the unit and what it covers, e.g. "From $89 per visit, up to 10 windows". The prices are also the smallest, lowest level on each card: about body size, in green. For a sales page they should rank just under the service name.

3. **The headline could be anyone's, and one card contradicts it. (Defect, copy.)** "Help around the house, on your schedule" would fit every cleaning, handyman or concierge service. It gives a visitor no reason to pick Fernway over the one they already use. Then the laundry card says "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday". Those are fixed days, which goes against "on your schedule". Either make the headline say something only you can claim, like the vetted crew that brings its own supplies (currently buried in the subhead), or a guarantee, or the area you serve. Then make the laundry card fit it, or say why the days are fixed.

4. **Nothing backs up "vetted". (Defect for this job.)** You're asking someone to let strangers into their home. Apart from that one word in the subhead, the view has no reviews, guarantee, insurance note, crew photo or service area. The illustrations are friendly but generic clip-art style, so they add warmth without adding trust. Put one concrete trust signal near the main action: a rating with a count, a re-clean guarantee, or "Serving [neighbourhoods]".

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line break: "on your / schedule" leaves one word alone on the second line. Widen the headline box or rebreak it as "Help around the house, / on your schedule".
- The accent colour has two jobs: green for the prices and illustrations, dark navy for the button. Pick one colour for "this is actionable" and keep the other decorative.
- The subhead and card titles look only about 1–2 px apart in size, at different weights. They read as near-equal levels. Set them clearly apart in size or colour.
- The nav includes "Pricing" while the cards already show prices. Make sure the two say the same thing, or the unit problem gets worse.

**What's working.**
- The type pairing. The serif in the wordmark and headline against a plain sans for everything else is a clear contrast in structure, not a conflict (Dair). It gives the brand a warm, domestic voice without being cute. Keep it.
- The card program. The four cards use the same grid, gutter, illustration panel, title → description → price order and baseline for the price row. They scan in one pass. Build the fixes into this system, not around it.
- The card descriptions are concrete: "screens and sills wiped" and "back folded on Wednesday" tell you what actually happens. Write the headline and the prices in that same voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable and show a hover state. If they are, the problem in item 1 is smaller but not gone, because nothing on screen says so.
