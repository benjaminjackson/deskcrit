**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web gallery that shows visitors the areas of a garden show so they can see what's there and choose what to visit.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. In a gallery the pictures are the content, and here every picture is a flat colour block with a pale circle, so a visitor learns nothing about any of the six areas. Behind that, the page title is no bigger than the card titles, and the first card's caption sits out of line with the other five.

**What would move it most**

1. **The images say nothing about the gardens (defect).** All six tiles use the same drawing: a solid colour with a pale circle cut off at the bottom edge. Only the hue changes. A visitor cannot see roses, herbs, a pond or anything else, so the gallery reduces to six one-line captions. The colours also mean nothing. Purple has no link to an orchard path and yellow has no link to a glasshouse, so the hues ask for attention without adding meaning. Real photos should replace the blocks, shot or cropped with the same light, crop style and subject distance so the six still read as one set. If these are placeholders on purpose, the page can't be judged as a gallery until real images are in. Its whole effect depends on them.

2. **The page title doesn't lead (defect, type scale).** "Garden show gallery" looks like about 20 px bold. Each card title ("Rose arch", "Herb bed"…) looks like about 22 px bold in the same face. The heading for the whole page is the same size as, or smaller than, the items under it, so nothing tells the visitor what they're looking at before they hit the tiles. I count three text sizes (page title, card title, caption), and the top two collide. Make the page title clearly the top level: a big step up in size, and a line saying which show this is and when (Bringhurst on sizes from a scale; Refactoring UI on using weight and colour for the smaller steps).

3. **The first caption sits out of line (defect, craft).** Under Rose arch, the title sits about 2 px higher than the other cards in that row, and the description sits about 4 px higher. The gap between title and description is also tighter (about 22 px against 24 px elsewhere). In a grid of six identical cards, a slip in the top-left card, where the eye starts, makes the whole page look unfinished. Every card should use one caption component with the same spacing, so baselines line up across the row (Bringhurst on lines aligning across columns; Gerstner on following one program). Measure in the browser to find which spacing value is different.

**Smaller things.**
- Gaps: the tiles are about 14 px apart across a row, but the rows are about 60 px apart (caption to next image). The tight side-by-side gaps make each row of tiles read as one striped band. Wider column gaps would let each tile stand alone.
- The page title sits about 18 px above the grid, almost as close as a caption sits to its image. Give it more space above the tiles so it reads as the heading for the whole page.
- The copy is generic. "Garden show gallery" could be any show, and nothing names this one, its date or its place. Captions like "Tomatoes in late July" leave the visitor unsure whether that is when to come or just a description.
- There is no next step. Nothing says whether a tile opens a larger view or a map location, so a visitor can't tell what clicking does.

**What's working.**
- The captions are short, concrete and specific to each area ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Reeds and water lilies"). That is the right voice. Keep it, and add when or where where it helps.
- The structure is right for the job: an image, then a bold title, then a grey description, with each caption sitting close to its own image and well clear of the next row. Keep it once real photos go in.
