**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline, then down to the dark green "Get started" button. That path is clean. Then it drifts into the empty right half and the empty bottom third and finds nothing. It reads as a tidy but generic SaaS landing page for a group trip planner. It feels calm and trustworthy, and also unfinished, as if an image never loaded.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing page for a group trip-planning product, meant to get people who organise trips to sign up.

**Verdict.** The type and hierarchy are competent, but the screen doesn't do its job. It never shows the product or makes the case for it. The viewer gets a claim ("one place"), a vague button, and more than half the frame empty.

**What would move it most**

1. **Nothing shows the product, and the right half is a dead hole.** Everything sits in the left half above y≈500. The right half and the bottom 300 px are blank cream. Group trip planning is a visual, concrete promise, so the viewer should *see* flights, stays and day plans together in one view. Here they have to take it on trust. The empty space also isn't working as space: it isn't framing anything, so the composition looks lopsided, as if something failed to load, rather than deliberately open (Hofmann on balance and tension; checklist "White space is gathered, not scattered"). *Defect.* Fix: put a real product view on the right, such as a shared itinerary with a flight, a stay and a day plan, and a couple of group members' votes or avatars. Make it large enough to balance the headline block. If this capture missed a hero image, see the last section.

2. **The copy could sit on anyone's page, and the ask is vague.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" is the stock all-in-one claim every planning tool makes, so nothing tells the viewer what's different here. The real difference is buried in the subhead: "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads." That line names a pain the viewer already has and recognises, the group chat. It should lead. "Get started" names no action and no outcome. The viewer can't tell whether it means signing up, a form, or a payment. *Defect.* Fix: make the headline the group-agreement promise, compared against the group chat they use now (e.g. "Get your group to agree on a trip, not a 400-message chat"). Make the button one exact first step, such as "Start a trip and invite your group," so they know what happens on click.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline break: "in" is stranded at the end of line 1. Break it as "Plan the whole trip / in one place" so each line is a phrase.
- Subhead text (grey on cream) looks slightly light. Check it meets 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). It probably passes, but it's the faintest text doing real work.
- Nav links look small and light next to the bold wordmark. Fine for nav, but check the contrast too.
- No sign-in link in the nav. Returning users have nowhere to go except "Get started."

**What's working.**
- A clean type scale with about four sizes (wordmark/nav, subhead, button, headline) and big steps between them. The headline clearly dominates (Refactoring UI; Bringhurst). Keep this when you add imagery.
- The primary and secondary buttons are styled correctly: one solid, one outlined, so the main action is clear (checklist "Secondary actions are not styled as strongly as the primary one").
- The restrained palette of deep green, near-black and warm cream feels calm and travel-appropriate. The single accent colour is used only for the brand and the actions.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The empty right side could be a hero image or animation that hadn't loaded when this was captured. Check it in a live browser. If an image is meant to be there, finding 1 is about whether it shows the actual product, not stock travel scenery.
