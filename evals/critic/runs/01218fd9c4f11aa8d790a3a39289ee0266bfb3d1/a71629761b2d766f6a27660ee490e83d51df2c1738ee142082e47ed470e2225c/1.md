**First read.** My eye lands on the big white headline on the left, then gets pulled straight to the bright lime leaves on the right, which are the loudest part of the screen and sit where nothing is. "Fernhill Nursery" and the headline tell me this is a plant shop within a second. The mood is fresh and green, but the leaves are flat, randomly scattered ovals, so it feels like a pattern fill more than a greenhouse.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage hero for a small independent nursery, meant to get local gardeners to browse ferns and shade plants and then buy or visit.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, but it sells nothing you can see. The hero shows no actual plant, and its only action is the weakest button style there is. The biggest problem is the background: it takes up the space where the product should be and draws the eye away from the message.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image shows no product, and its loudest part is empty texture.** The whole right half (about x 680–1280) is scattered, saturated green ovals. They're brightest and highest-contrast at the far right, so after the headline the eye goes there and finds nothing to read or want. A nursery's best argument is the plants: a real fern frond, a shady bed, the greenhouse itself. Abstract leaves could be on any eco brand's page, so the visitor can't tell what Fernhill grows or why it's better than a garden centre. *Defect* (the hero spends its space on something that doesn't help the viewer decide), plus *taste* on the look. Fix: use a real photograph of your own ferns, with a calm, darker area behind the text and the plant as the focal point on the right. If you keep an illustration, it should depict a fern, and it should be quieter than the headline.

2. **The only call to action is an outline button with nothing to hold it.** "Browse the greenhouse" is a thin white outline on a busy background. It's the one action on the page, yet it has the least visual weight of anything in the hero, less than the subhead above it. *Convention*: a view needs one high-emphasis action (Refactoring UI; Material Design's filled vs. outlined buttons). Fix: make it a solid filled button in a colour that stands out against green (white fill with dark green text works).

3. **The copy is generic, and the ask doesn't connect to the visitor's own garden.** "Ferns and shade plants for every corner" would fit any shade-plant seller, and "every corner" is a filler phrase. "Ready to plant this weekend" makes a real promise but leaves out how: can I order online and get delivery by Saturday, or do I come to the nursery? With "Visit" in the nav, both readings are possible, and a visitor who expects delivery and finds it's pickup-only will feel misled. *Defect* (the time promise can be read two ways). Fix: make the line say what happens ("Pick up this weekend at the nursery" or "Order by Thursday, planted by Sunday"). Make the ask point at the visitor's own problem, e.g. "Find a fern for your shadiest spot" or shopping by how much light they have, so they're matching plants to a corner they already know rather than browsing at random.

**Smaller things.**
- There looks to be a vertical seam in the background at about x 680–720, where the dark overlay ends and the bright leaves start. Feather the overlay so it doesn't read as a hard band.
- The headline breaks with "corner" alone on the last line. Rebreak it ("Ferns and shade plants / for every corner") or rewrite it so the lines are closer in length.
- The nav links are small and mid-grey on white. Check they reach 4.5:1 contrast, and consider a slightly darker colour.
- The left edge of the logo (x≈64) doesn't line up with the hero text (x≈96), which looks like two grids. Align them.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is disciplined: a big bold headline, one subhead size, small button and nav text, with clear steps between them. Keep it.
- The darkened left side gives the white headline solid contrast, so the main message reads instantly. Keep that dark area behind the text when you change the image.
- The brand name is set plainly in dark green with a simple four-item nav, which fits a small, trustworthy local business.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether anything below the hero (product cards, opening hours) shows above the fold on common laptop heights, and whether the button has visible hover and focus states.
