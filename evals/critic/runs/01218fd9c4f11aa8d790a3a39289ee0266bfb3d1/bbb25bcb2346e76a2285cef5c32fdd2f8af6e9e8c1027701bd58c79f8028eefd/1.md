In version 2 the eye jumps cleanly from the title to "What you will see" to "Opening times". It reads as a quiet, bookish museum exhibition page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an exhibition page for a museum visitor deciding whether and when to go. The key facts are what's on show, the opening hours, the closing date and the price.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the headings, from regular serif to bold sans, and that turns a column that reads as one block of text into one the visitor can scan for the facts they came for. Both versions share the same remaining weaknesses: the hero image is a blank tan block, and there is no clear next step.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Heading contrast (defect in v1, fixed in v2).** In version 1, "What you will see" and "Opening times" (about 26 px) differ from the body text (about 19 px) only slightly in size. They share its typeface, weight and colour. Size alone with a small step doesn't make a separate level, so a visitor looking for opening hours has to read rather than scan. Version 2 makes them bold and sans, which adds weight and a second typeface on top of the size step, so each section is obvious at a glance (Refactoring UI: use weight, not just size). The sans headings also match the "Calder Museum" wordmark and the nav, which ties the page together. The title gains too: it now clearly outranks everything else.
- What version 1 loses is some warmth. The light serif title suits a page about 17th-century charts better than a heavy grotesque does (taste). But that atmosphere costs the visitor their ability to scan, and on a page whose job is to inform, scanning wins.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The image is a blank gradient (defect, if it's real).** The right-hand box, about 420×560 px, takes up half the page and shows nothing. For an exhibition about hand-coloured charts, the chart is the thing that makes people come. Put a real map in it, cropped tight enough that the detail shows. (It might be an image that failed to load; see below.)
2. **There's no next step (defect).** The page tells visitors what's on and when, then stops, leaving a large empty area below. The only link to act on is the small "Tickets" in the nav, and entry is free anyway. Add one specific next step under "Opening times", such as directions to the ground-floor gallery or "Plan your visit" with the address.
3. **The closing date has no year (defect, in a date visitors will act on).** "until 2 March" doesn't say which year. Write "until 2 March 2027" (or whichever year it is), and consider giving the closing date its own line, since it's what makes someone go before it closes.

**Smaller things.**
- The subheads sit very close to the text below them, nearly as close as the lines within a paragraph. A little more space above each heading than below it would group each section more clearly.
- The nav ends about 36 px short of the image's right edge. Align the two to the same right margin.

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete and specific: "Forty charts drawn between 1620 and 1790", "a brass sounding lead found in the harbour mud in 1958". It could only belong to this exhibition. Keep it.
- The serif body text is comfortable to read, with sensible line lengths and generous line spacing.
- The type scale in version 2 is tight: the title, the subheads, the body, and one small size for the nav.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the blank tan block is the intended design or an image that didn't load. Check it in a live browser.
