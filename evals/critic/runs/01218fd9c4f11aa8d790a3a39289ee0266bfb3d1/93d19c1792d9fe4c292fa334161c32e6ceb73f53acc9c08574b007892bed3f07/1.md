**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question "Save changes to the shared calendar?", drops to the line about choir rehearsal times, and then stops at a row of four identical blue buttons. No button stands out, so I had to read all four labels left to right to find the answer to the question. That last button is "Save changes", the one furthest from where reading starts. The dialog feels calm and tidy until the moment you have to choose.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a confirmation dialog in a calendar app. Someone who has just edited a shared calendar should be able to publish the new rehearsal times with confidence, or back out safely.

**Verdict.** The question and the explanation are good. The buttons let it down. Four equal filled buttons give no answer to the question asked, and "Discard changes" looks exactly like "Save changes". So the main action is slow to find, and the one choice that throws work away is one easy mis-click from the one that keeps it.

**What would move it most**

1. **All four actions have the same weight.** (Convention, and here a defect.) Every button in the row has the same blue fill, size and white bold label. The viewer has to read every label to find the answer, and nothing tells them which choice the dialog expects. Material Design 3 ("Buttons") says to give a view one high-emphasis action and to style the others with lower-emphasis styles, such as outlined or text buttons. Keep "Save changes" as the only filled button. Make "Keep editing" a text or outlined button as the cancel. Move "Export as file" out of this row; see point 3.

2. **"Discard changes" is styled like a safe action and sits in the middle of the row.** (Defect.) It is the only action here that destroys anything: the user's edits to the rehearsal times. Yet it is the same blue as Save and sits two buttons away from it, between the other choices. If the user is moving quickly, or the keyboard focus lands on it, the edits are gone. Apple HIG ("Alerts") and Material ("Dialogs") both style destructive actions as destructive and keep them apart from the safe ones. Give it a destructive style (red text or a red outline, not a fill), move it to the far left away from Save, and make sure it is never the default focus. If the product allows it, offer undo after a discard.

3. **"Export as file" doesn't answer the question.** (Taste, with a cost.) The dialog asks a yes/no question about saving. Export is a separate task. As a fourth equal option it makes the choice harder and dilutes the decision. Take it out of this dialog, or turn it into a quiet text link set apart from the decision buttons.

**Smaller things.**
- The button order runs Export, Discard, Keep editing, Save. That puts the primary action last and the destructive one second, with no grouping. Group them as: destructive on the left; cancel, then primary, on the right.
- The body line says the times "will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar". That's good, but it stops at the fact. It could finish on what it means for the user, such as "everyone who follows this calendar will see the new times straight away". That would make the stakes of Save plain.
- The dialog has both a border and a large soft shadow, and there is no dimmed backdrop behind it. That may simply be how it was captured. Normally a modal dims the page behind it so it reads as blocking.

**What's working.**
- The title is a real question that names both the action and where it applies ("the shared calendar"). Keep it.
- The body line names the specific change (choir rehearsal times) and who will be affected. That is exactly what someone needs before publishing to other people.
- The type scale is clean: one bold title size, one body size and one button label size, with clear steps between them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button gets keyboard focus by default. It must be Save or Keep editing, never Discard.
- Whether Esc or a click outside the dialog closes it, and if so whether that counts as Keep editing or Discard.
