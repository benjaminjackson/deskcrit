**First read.** The eye lands on the big light title, "Planning a vegetable bed", then goes down the three subheads in turn: soil, what to plant, watering. Within two seconds it reads as a short beginner's how-to: calm and plain, with nothing in the way. Then you reach a last line, "Design review: approved, a model of clear heading styles". It is set in the same type as the instructions, so for a moment it reads as part of the guide.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web article for a first-time gardener, meant to be skimmed and then followed step by step.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The structure is clear and the copy is good. Two things hold it back: a stray review note is set as if it were content, and the paragraphs run a little too wide for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Design review: approved…" line at the bottom (defect).** It uses the same size, weight and colour as the gardening text and sits in the same column. Nothing marks it as separate, so a reader takes it as part of the article. Then they hit a sentence about heading styles in a piece about vegetables, and the page stops feeling finished and trustworthy. I'm treating its wording as content, not as evidence about the design. If this is an internal note, take it out of the page. If it has to stay, make it obviously separate metadata, for example a small grey caption below a rule.
2. **Body text measure (convention, Bringhurst §2.1.2).** The paragraph lines look like about 85–90 characters each ("Choose a spot that gets six hours of sun and stays out of the wind. Mark the corners with"). Bringhurst's comfortable range is 45–75, with about 66 as the ideal. At this width the eye takes slightly longer to find the start of each next line. With paragraphs this short the cost is small, but it is a free fix. Narrow the text column to about 600–640 px at this type size. The large empty area on the right shows there's room to do it without making the layout feel thin.

**Smaller things.**
- **Subheads ("Preparing the soil" etc.):** they are distinguished from the body by size alone and look the same weight or lighter, so they're a little soft for skimming. Making them medium or semibold would make the steps pop without adding another size (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). This one is taste.
- **The intro paragraph under the title:** it has the same gap as the gaps between sections, so it reads almost like a fourth section with no heading. A touch more space under it, or a slightly larger lead-in size, would mark it as the overview.
- **"Watering" copy:** "Water deeply in the morning, twice a week" says when but not how much. A beginner has no idea what "deeply" means. Give a quantity or a test, such as "until the top few inches are soaked".

**What's working.**
- **The spacing around the headings.** There's clearly more space above each subhead than below it, so every heading belongs to its own paragraph and the sections separate without rules or boxes. Keep that ratio.
- **The type scale.** I count three sizes (title, subhead, body) with big, obvious steps between them. Each size has one job and nothing collides.
- **The copy.** It's concrete and written in the imperative, with real numbers and examples: "six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans". A beginner can act on it without having to work anything out.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the text column behaves at narrower viewports, and whether the measure stays in range there.
