**First read.** My eye goes to "Changing your payment card", then down the three bold subheads, which work like a table of contents. It reads straight away as a SaaS billing help article: calm, plain and trustworthy. Nothing competes with the content, and nothing on the page gives you a next step either.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article for a customer who needs to swap, remove or rescue a payment card. They'll read it once, probably while a little anxious, and then go and do it.

**Verdict.** The layout and type do their job. The words don't always: two of the three tasks leave out the one fact the reader needs to finish them. The failed-payment section is the most serious case, because it's about money and access, which people act on and later hold you to.

**What would move it most**

1. **"If a payment fails" is ambiguous and gives no action (defect).** "After a third failed try" can be read two ways:
   - The original charge plus two retries, so read-only arrives about day 6.
   - Three retries after the original, so read-only arrives about day 9.

   A customer planning around that deadline may get the wrong one and lose access. The section also stops at what *we* do. It never tells the reader what *they* should do: update the card, and how fast. It also says "email the account owner", so anyone who isn't the owner learns they won't be warned, but not how to find out. **Fix:**
   - Give the timeline as dates relative to the first failure, e.g. "We try again on day 3 and day 6. If the day 6 try fails, your account becomes read-only."
   - Say what read-only means for them ("you can view but not edit…").
   - End on the action: "Add a new card to restore access straight away."

2. **"Remove an old card" depends on a step the page never explains (defect).** "Once another card is set as the default" is the condition for removal, but nothing says how to set a default. The reader who came to remove a card hits a wall and has to guess or contact support. This is the "read it as a stranger" gap: the writer knows where the default toggle is, and the reader doesn't. **Fix:** put the missing step in order: "Select the new card and choose Set as default. Then select the old card, choose Remove, and confirm."

3. **Steps are buried in prose, and the interface names don't stand out (convention: Krug, scannability; Nielsen 6, recognition over recall).** "Open Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card" is a sequence of clicks written as a sentence. "Add card" is set like ordinary words, so a reader moving between this page and the app has to re-read to pick out what to look for on screen. **Fix:**
   - Bold the interface labels (**Settings**, **Billing**, **Add card**, **Remove**).
   - Consider a short numbered list for each task.
   - If the help centre allows it, make "Settings → Billing" a direct link, which would also give the page the next step it currently lacks.

**Smaller things**
- Intro, "nothing already paid is moved": "moved" makes the reader stop and decode it. Something like "Invoices you've already paid stay on your old card" says the same thing plainly.
- "A hold of one pound": write "£1". Numbers that matter scan faster as figures. "Release it within three working days" is fine.
- Measure: the body lines look like they run about 80–85 characters, a little over Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrowing the column by about 10% would fix it, and the empty right side means it costs nothing.
- The "hold" paragraph ends with "days." alone on its own line, a single-word last line. It disappears with the narrower column or a small copy edit.
- The grey breadcrumb text looks like it passes contrast, but it's the lightest text on the page. Measure it against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working**
- **A clean three-level type scale.** Title, subhead and body are clearly different sizes, and the breadcrumb is set apart by colour rather than by yet another size. Keep it that way when you add bolded interface labels.
- **Spacing groups the content correctly.** Subheads sit tight to their own text with a much bigger gap above. Each section reads as one unit, and you can skim the subheads alone.
- **Task-shaped subheads.** "Add a new card", "Remove an old card" and "If a payment fails" match what a reader arrives wanting to do.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the breadcrumb and any interface names are actually links, and whether they have visible focus states.
- How the column reflows on a phone, which is where many people will read this mid-task.
