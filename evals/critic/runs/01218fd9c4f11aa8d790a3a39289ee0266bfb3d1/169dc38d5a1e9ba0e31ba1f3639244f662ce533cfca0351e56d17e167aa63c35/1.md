**First read.** The eye goes to the bold "Notes for ferry passengers" at top left, then down into three even grey-black paragraphs that look exactly alike. It reads as a calm, tidy help page or notice, set in a clean sans. After the heading there is nothing else to catch the eye. To find out when the boat leaves, you have to read every word.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: a notice, on the web or posted at a landing, that tells ferry passengers when the boat runs, how long the trip takes, what to do with bicycles, and how winter changes things. People will scan it to plan a trip or to settle one question.

**Verdict.** It is set well, but as a passenger notice it does its job poorly. The facts people come for are buried in three paragraphs of prose with no way to scan them, and the timings people will act on are too vague to plan by.

**What would move it most**

1. **The facts are locked in prose, so nobody can scan them.** People come here with one question: when's the next boat, can I bring my bike, is it different in winter? They have to read the whole page to answer it. The three paragraphs hold about six separate facts: route, how often, how long, bike rules, winter timetable, where to find changes. They all get the same weight, and no heading or label points to any of them. A passenger checking the winter last crossing has to get through the sandbank and the bike rail first. This is a **defect** for this job. Text that people skim should be broken up with headings, short blocks and lists (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; Nielsen Norman Group on scannable text). Fix: put the facts people act on up top as short labelled lines, such as *Route*, *Every 40 min*, *Crossing ~12 min*, *Bikes free, lower deck*, *Winter: hourly*. Then add a small heading over each prose section, or replace the prose entirely.

2. **The times people plan around can't be pinned down.** "Between early morning and late evening", "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" and "a little longer" are all things passengers will act on. You can't plan a trip from any of them. "Before dusk" moves by more than an hour through the winter, and a passenger who guesses wrong misses the last boat. "Every forty minutes" doesn't say from when. This is a **defect**, because times are what passengers remember and later hold the operator to. Fix: give first and last departure times for summer and winter (or say plainly that the noticeboard times are the only ones to go by). Give the longer crossing as a number too, for example "up to 20 minutes against the tide."

3. **The heading tells you nothing about what's below.** "Notes for ferry passengers" could sit over anything. It names a category instead of telling the reader what they'll get. This is **taste**, but it costs something: the heading is the one place the eye lands, and that moment is spent on nothing. Fix: use it for the main fact, for example "River ferry: North Landing ↔ Market Quay, every 40 minutes". The calm tone can stay in the body.

**Smaller things.**
- Third paragraph: the last line, "high tide.", is a two-word runt. Rewrite the line or let it re-wrap.
- Bikes paragraph: "The crew asks that bags stay on the bicycle" reads as a polite wish. If it's a rule, say it as one, to the rider: "Keep your bags on your bike so the deck stays clear."
- "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" doesn't help someone reading this on a phone at home. Say where they can see the current timetable online, if there is one.

**What's working.**
- The text is easy to read. Lines look to run about 70 characters, which sits in Bringhurst's 45–75 range, and the leading looks generous for that line length. Keep both when you add structure.
- The type scale is tight and has clear jobs: one bold heading size and one body size, with a big step between them. Any labels you add should come from weight, not a third or fourth size (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
- The text is near-black on white, set flush left, with space between paragraphs and no indents. Contrast and paragraph marks are clean.
