**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline, then drops to the tallest bar (Q3 2026, 705), then moves along the row of chunky blue 3D columns. It reads as a quarterly sales slide in a board deck: tidy, corporate, and quick to scan. The headline makes a claim about third quarters, but the chart gives the Q3 bars no special treatment, so you have to hunt for them yourself to check the claim.

**What it's for.** Assumption: one slide in a 12-page board deck, there to show the board the seasonal pattern in shipments and where Q4 is heading.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and the headline states a finding. The chart doesn't prove that finding, though, and it presents a forecast as if it were already in the books. The biggest problem is that Q4 2026 looks exactly like the seven actual quarters, and that's the kind of thing a board remembers and holds you to later.

**What would move it most**

1. **The forecast looks like an actual.** The Q4 2026 bar, far right, has the same colour, fill and label style as the real quarters. The only sign it's a projection is a note in brackets in the subtitle and the source line. A board member skimming the slide will read 560 as shipped, or will remember it as a figure the company committed to. *Defect.* Projections should look different from observed data (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). Draw Q4 2026 as an outline or a lighter tint, label it "forecast" right at the bar, and show a range if the September forecast has one.

2. **The chart doesn't show the headline's claim.** "Shipments peak each third quarter" is about two bars out of eight, and those two look the same as the other six. The viewer has to find Q3 on the axis twice and compare the bars by hand. A good finding-style headline is wasted when the chart doesn't point at what it says. *Taste, argued:* put the one accent colour on the two Q3 bars and grey the rest (Few's "neutral for context, one accent for the point"). Also, two cycles are thin evidence for "each". The stronger news for a board is that Q3 rose from 610 to 705 year on year (about +16%). Consider saying that in the headline, in terms the board cares about: growth, not just seasonality.

3. **3D bars.** Every bar has an angled top face and a side face. The value labels sit above the top face, so it's unclear whether a bar's height is read at the front edge or the back. The extrusion also adds a second, darker blue that carries no data. *Convention:* Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 26.1 ("Avoid gratuitous 3D"), and Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, both class this as chartjunk. Approved fix: draw the chart flat, with no depth, sides or tilt, so each value is read from one length.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle says Q4 2026 twice ("eight quarters to Q4 2026 (Q4 2026 is the forecast)"). Rewrite it as "…eight quarters, Q3 2025 to Q4 2026; Q4 2026 is forecast", or drop the date range, since the axis already shows it.
- Every bar has a value label, so the gridlines and y-axis numbers are doing the same job twice. Keep one or the other. With labels on the bars, drop the y-axis numbers and keep a zero baseline.
- There's an empty band of about 120 px below the footer, so the content sits high on the slide. Either let the chart take that height or centre the block vertically.
- "Units shipped by quarter / Thousands of units" mostly repeats the subtitle. "Thousand units shipped" placed as the axis unit would do it in one line.

**What's working.**
- The headline states a finding rather than a topic. Keep that habit; just make the chart point at it.
- The bars start at zero and the axis is honest, so the differences between quarters are shown at their true size.
- The frame is restrained: the masthead, the context line ("prepared for the October board meeting"), the source line and the page number are small, quiet and consistent. There are about four clear type sizes, and none of them clash.
