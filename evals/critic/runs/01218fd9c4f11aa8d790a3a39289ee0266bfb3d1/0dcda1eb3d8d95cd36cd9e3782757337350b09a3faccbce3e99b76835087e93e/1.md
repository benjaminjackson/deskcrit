**First read.** The eye goes to the bold title, then down the left column, where it reads "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday…" before it reaches any times. It reads as a clean, calm, generic web table, probably a ferry operator's page or a printed notice. It feels orderly and trustworthy, but every row looks the same, so nothing stands out.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: this lets a rider quickly find which boats they can catch from North landing on the day they plan to travel.

**Verdict.** It is easy to read but not easy to use. The fact that decides whether a sailing exists on your day sits in the far-right column at the same weight as "Daily". The best position on the page, the first column, goes to period labels that only repeat what the times already say. It works as a list, but a rider can't plan with it at a glance.

**What would move it most**

1. **The day restrictions are buried, so riders can pick a boat that isn't running.** (Defect.) "Weekdays" (12:40, 17:10) and "Weekends" (19:30) sit in the "Runs" column at the far right, about 600 px from the departure time. They are set in the same regular black as the five "Daily" rows. A Saturday rider reading down the "Leaves north" column sees 17:10 and plans on it, because nothing in that column warns them. Three of the seven sailings are exceptions, and the exceptions are the information that matters. The fix should put the day next to the time it limits, or split the table by day. Two short lists ("Monday–Friday" and "Saturday–Sunday"), or two time columns, let each reader see only their own boats. If you keep one table, mark the exceptions so they look different from "Daily" and move the column next to the departure time.

2. **The first column carries no information and pushes the times aside.** (Defect, mild.) "Period" takes the left column, where the eye starts every row. Its labels repeat in pairs ("Morning / Morning", "Midday / Midday") and only restate the clock. "Afternoon" for 17:10 is also debatable. As a result the reader's first glance down the table lands on words rather than times. Make departure time the first column. If the morning, midday and evening grouping matters, show it with extra space between the groups or a small group label (layout: "Space shows the groups"). Don't repeat a cell on every row.

3. **The subtitle and headers leave out facts a rider will act on.** (Defect.) "Spring season" has no dates. A rider can't tell whether this timetable applies on the day they are travelling, and they will hold you to it. "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are shortened from the place names in the subtitle. A stranger has to put "landing" and "market" back in themselves. Give the season's dates and the year, and use full stop names in the headers ("Leaves North landing", "Arrives Market quay"). Also say whether "Weekdays" includes public holidays. That line can be read two ways, and the wrong reading strands someone.

**Smaller things.**
- Times are left-aligned and look like proportional figures. In 11:00 and 11:12 the colons seem to drift against 06:40. Turn on tabular figures (Inter's `tnum`) so the times line up as a column.
- The table spans the full 1080 px for four short columns, so the gaps between columns (roughly 240–300 px) are larger than the content. Tighten the columns so each row reads as one line of information rather than four separate words.
- Every crossing takes 12 minutes. Saying "Crossing takes 12 minutes" once in the subtitle would tell riders more than the "Arrives quay" column does.
- Only one direction is shown. If the return times aren't nearby, the page answers half of a rider's question.

**What's working.**
- The restraint is right for the job. There is one typeface, a clear three-level hierarchy (bold title, grey subtitle, bold column headers over regular body text) and pale row rules that help the eye track across without getting in the way. Keep all of that through the revision.
- The 24-hour times are consistent and unambiguous, so there's no confusion between a.m. and p.m.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the live page highlights the next departure or the current day. That would partly ease finding 1.
- Whether the table holds together on a phone, where most riders will check it. Four wide columns are likely to wrap or scroll horizontally at narrow widths.
