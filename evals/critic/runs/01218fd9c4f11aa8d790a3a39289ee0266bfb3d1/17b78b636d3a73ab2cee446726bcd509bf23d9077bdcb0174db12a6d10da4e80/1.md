**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "Book a workshop seat" headline at top left, then runs down a stack of four identical outlined boxes and stops in empty white space. Nothing at the bottom tells you how to finish. It reads as a plain, unfinished booking form for someone signing up for a class. It feels like a wireframe: tidy, but cold and not quite put together.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web form where someone books one or more seats at a workshop, filled in and sent in one go.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job. There is no way to send the form, and every label sits closer to the field above it than to its own field, so the viewer has to guess which box is which. Fix those two before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no submit button.** Below "Notes for the host" there is about 250 px of empty white and no action. The person filling this in has nowhere to go when they finish, so the booking cannot happen. The form has no primary action (Nielsen and Norman Group; Material and Apple HIG: one filled, dominant action per view). Add one filled button straight under the last field, worded as the result: "Book my seat" or "Reserve 2 seats", not "Submit". **Defect.**
2. **Labels are grouped with the wrong field.** Each label sits about 4 px under the box above it ("Preferred date" almost touches the "Workshop name" box) and about 40 px above its own box. Proximity tells the eye that "Preferred date" belongs to the first box, "Number of seats" to the second, and so on. Someone scanning will type into the wrong field, or stop and work it out. The empty top box makes it worse: from "Workshop name" to its field is a large gap, so the first pairing looks broken too. The rule is that a label sits nearer its own field than the one before it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on grouping by proximity). Put the label about 6–8 px above its own field, and leave about 24–32 px between one label-and-field pair and the next. **Defect.**
3. **Every field is the same blank text box, whatever it asks for.** "Workshop name" is free text, but the viewer cannot know the exact name, and a typo means a booking that cannot be matched. "Preferred date" gives no format and no calendar. "Number of seats" is a full-width box for a one- or two-digit number. "Notes for the host" is one line high, which tells people to write very little. Matching the control to the answer prevents errors (Nielsen heuristic 5; Norman on constraints). Use a list of the real workshops, a date picker or a list of the dates actually on offer, a narrow number field with a default of 1, and a multi-line box for notes. If people choose the workshop before they reach this page, show its name as fixed text instead of asking for it. **Defect.**
4. **The page gives the viewer nothing to decide with.** There is no price, no time, no place, no seats left, and nothing on what happens after booking (a confirmation email? payment?). "Preferred date" also suggests a request that might be turned down, not a booking. Since the headline says "Book", the viewer cannot tell whether they are committing to something. The commitment points of a form are where wording has to be exact. Add one line under the headline that says what they get and what happens next, for example "You'll get a confirmation email within a day; pay at the door." If dates are fixed, drop "Preferred". **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- No field shows whether it is required or optional. Mark the optional one ("Notes for the host (optional)") and leave the rest unmarked.
- The field borders look like a mid grey, probably above 3:1 on white, but measure them against WCAG 1.4.11.
- The form uses the left 40% of the frame and leaves a large dead area to the right and below. A narrow single column is right for a form, but on a wide screen the right side could hold the workshop details (date, place, price) that finding 4 asks for.
- Type scale is fine: two sizes (headline and bold labels) with a clear step. No change needed.

**What's working.**
- A single column in a fixed top-to-bottom order. That is the right structure for a short form. Keep it.
- Persistent labels above the fields, not placeholder-only labels. They stay visible while typing. Keep them, just move them next to their own fields.
- The headline is plain and says what the page does: "Book a workshop seat" is better than a generic "Registration".

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus, validation and error states, and what the date and seat fields do when clicked. Check that errors appear next to the field when the user leaves it.
- Whether the missing button and blank space are the actual design or a cut-off or half-loaded capture. Confirm in a live browser. If a button exists below this, findings 2–4 still stand.
