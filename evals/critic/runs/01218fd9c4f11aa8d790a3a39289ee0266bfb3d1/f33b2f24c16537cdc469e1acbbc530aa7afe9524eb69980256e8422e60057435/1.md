**First read.** The eye lands on the black headline "Garden show gallery" first, then drops straight into the big red Rose arch tile, then moves across to the bright yellow Glasshouse before going round the rest. It reads as a photo gallery from a local garden show. It feels tidy, calm and friendly, but a little anonymous.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page where visitors browse the highlights of a garden show, with one featured exhibit and the rest as secondary tiles.

**Verdict.** It does its job. There is one clear feature, the grid is clean and the captions are specific. The main problem is the headline, which is the loudest thing on the page and says the least. A gallery should open with its pictures, and this one opens with a generic label.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is heavier than the pictures and could be anyone's.** "Garden show gallery" is set at about 56 px in heavy near-black. It's the darkest, densest shape on the page, so the eye stops there before reaching Rose arch. The words don't name which show, where, or when, so a visitor arriving from search or a shared link learns nothing they didn't already know.
   - Defect (the copy) plus taste (the weight).
   - The fix: make the headline say what this is, e.g. "<Show name> 2026: highlights from the east garden". Then step its weight or size down so Rose arch is what the page leads with. The Refactoring UI advice fits here: mark rank with weight and colour, not only size.
2. **The tiles fight each other on colour.** This only matters if the flat colour blocks are the real artwork rather than placeholders. Six fully saturated hues at similar strength each pull at once. The warm, light yellow of Glasshouse wins second place over Herb bed and Pond edge, which come before it in reading order, so the path jumps from top-left to bottom-centre.
   - Convention: saturated colour should mark what matters, with the rest held back.
   - The fix: keep the feature at full strength and mute the other tiles, or let real photos carry the colour.

**Smaller things.**
- **Gap above the grid:** there's only about 25 px between the headline's descenders and the top of the grid, so the heading crowds the pictures. Give it the same clear space as the page margin.
- **Vertical gaps between stacked tiles:** the gap from a caption to the next image (about 15 px) is barely larger than the gutter between images (about 14 px), so the right column reads as one continuous stack. Open the gap between rows so each tile and its caption read as a unit.
- **Feature image doesn't line up with the right column:** the bottom of the Rose arch image (around y 437) falls partway down the Pond edge image (which ends around y 474), so they share no horizontal edge. Either align the image bottoms, or keep things as they are, since the caption bottoms already line up.
- **Small captions:** the small-tile captions look like 13–14 px. They're readable on desktop, but check they hold at least 16 px body size on mobile, where they'll be the main text.
- **Type scale:** I count five sizes (about 56, 36, 18, 14 and 13). The 14/13 pair is told apart by weight and colour, which works, so this one is fine.

**What's working.**
- **The grid:** a three-column grid with the feature spanning two columns is clear and easy to repeat. Every tile takes its edges from the same column lines, and the gutters are even. Keep it.
- **One clear feature:** Rose arch is about four times the area of any other tile and has its own larger caption (about 36 px title, 18 px line below). There's no doubt about what's first.
- **Specific captions:** "Climbing roses over the east gate." and "Tomatoes in late July." tell the visitor what they're looking at and where or when. They're the best copy on the page; bring the headline up to their standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Placeholder art:** every tile shows a flat colour field with a pale disc cropped at the bottom edge. I've treated these as placeholders. Judge colour, crop and consistency again once the real photos are in.
- **Interaction:** whether the tiles are clickable, and how they look on hover and focus. If they open a larger view, they need a visible cue that they do.
- **Small screens:** how the feature and the side column collapse on a phone.
