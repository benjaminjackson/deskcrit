**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands straight on **$184,320**. It reads as "October revenue is up 12%", and only then moves to the smaller numbers. In version 2 the eye has nowhere to land. Seven cards of the same weight read left to right like a table, and revenue is just the first cell. Both feel calm and clean, and both look like a template.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal sales dashboard for a small retailer's owner or manager, who wants to know "how is this month going?" at a glance.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. It answers the main question before you start reading, while version 2 makes you read every card to find the number that matters. The two share one real weakness: the chart under the cards has no axis, no labels, and an unexplained number of bars. It can't be read, so it adds nothing.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Revenue is the clear focus in version 1.** It gets a double-width card and a much larger figure, about twice the size of the others. Its card also sits on the top row with Orders and Average order, the two numbers that make up revenue. So the top row reads as one story: revenue, and why. Version 2 gives every metric the same size and position, so the eye skims and gets no priority. Arnheim and Müller-Brockmann both make the point that a composition needs one dominant element for the eye to settle on. Dashboards depend on this more than most pages do.
- **Version 2 has a hole in its grid.** Seven cards in a four-column grid leave an empty slot at the bottom right. It looks like a card failed to load or is missing. Version 1's 2-1-1 / 1-1-1-1 layout fills both rows exactly.

**What would move version 1 most**
1. **The "Revenue by week" chart can't be read** (defect). There's no y-axis, no values, and no week labels. The bars fill only the left half of the card, and the right half is empty. There are eight bars, but the page title says "October so far", which covers four or five weeks. Viewers can't tell which week is which, how big any week was, or whether the empty space means weeks still to come or a broken render. Label each bar with its week, add values or a light axis that starts at zero (Few, Wilke), and either fill the width or say what the empty space is. If the eight bars span two months, make that visible, for example by greying September's bars.
2. **Most of the change figures don't say what they compare against** (defect, in a place where people act on the numbers). Only Revenue says "+12% on September". "+5%", "+9%", "+2 points" and "+0.2" leave you to guess: last month, or the same point last month? Comparing a part-month to a full month would make every number look bad, so the baseline changes what these figures mean. State the baseline once, for example in the subhead: "October so far, compared with the same days in September".

**Smaller things**
- "+0.2" on Items per order has no unit; write "+0.2 items".
- All the change figures are green, including the Returns drop of −0.4 points. Green there is correct, since fewer returns is good. Make sure the colour follows good or bad rather than the sign of the number, and that the green text passes 4.5:1 contrast on white (WCAG 1.4.3). It looks borderline at this size.
- The page bottom cuts the chart off at the 800 px fold, so the bar baseline isn't visible in either version.

**What's working**
- In version 1, the size jump between the revenue figure and the secondary figures is large. That makes it read as a real hierarchy rather than a mistake. Keep it.
- The type scale is tight: header, section title, labels, figures and changes, about five levels, each with a clear job.
- The labels are plain words ("Repeat customers", "Average order"), not internal codes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't see what the chart shows beyond the fold, or whether the empty right half of the chart is the design or a capture that hadn't finished loading. Check it in a live browser.

Better: version 1
