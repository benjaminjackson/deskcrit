**First read.** The eye goes to the three big white headlines and reads across them left to right: "Learn to swim", "Join the lanes", "Family hour". In about two seconds it's clear this is a swimming pool or leisure centre offering three ways to swim. Then the eye drops through the short descriptions into a large block of flat colour with nothing in it, and stops there. It feels friendly and confident, but it doesn't finish.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a section of a public pool's website that sends three kinds of visitor (adult beginners, lap swimmers, families) to the session that fits them, and gets them to book or turn up.

**Verdict.** It does the sorting well and then stops. Each visitor can find their card, but nothing on the card tells them what to do next or gives them the facts they'd need to do it. On top of that, the bottom 40% of every card is empty colour that looks like something is missing.

**What would move it most**

1. **There is no next step (defect).** None of the three cards has a button, a link, or a visible sign that it can be clicked. A beginner who has decided "that's me" has nowhere to go: book a class, see times, or find out more. The headlines are written as invitations ("Learn to swim", "Join the lanes"), but nothing lets you accept them. Krug: the viewer shouldn't have to guess whether the whole card is a link. Give each card one specific action that matches its session, such as "Book a Tuesday class", "See lane times" or "Plan a family visit". Put it on the card, styled the same way on all three, so it's obvious each card leads somewhere.

2. **The bottom 40% of each card is dead space (defect).** The text ends at about y≈330 and the cards run to y≈630. That space isn't shaped and has no job, so it reads as a missing image or a page still loading, not as breathing room. It also pushes the cards' weight downward, away from the content. Either use the space for what's missing (the action from point 1, plus the key facts from point 3), or make the cards shorter so they end a comfortable margin below the text. Fixing point 1 will probably fix most of this.

3. **The copy leaves out the facts people act on (defect).** "Tuesday evenings" gives no time. "From six" has no am or pm, though most people will guess morning. "Sunday afternoons" sits under the heading "Family hour", so is it an hour or the whole afternoon? None of the cards gives a price or says whether you need to book. Times and prices are what people plan around and later hold the pool to, so a line that can be read two ways (an hour, or all afternoon) means some families arrive at the wrong time. Add one short line of hard facts to each card, e.g. "Tue 7–8pm · £X · book ahead", set apart from the description.

**Smaller things**
- The row of cards isn't centred. The left margin is about 56 px and the right is about 96 px, so the group looks like it's slipping left. Centre the row or make the three columns fill the width.
- "Family hour" names a category, while the other two headlines are things you do. "Swim as a family" or "Bring the kids" would match the other two and read as an invitation.
- The three colours are equally loud and look picked one at a time. That's fine for three equal options, but the colours don't mean anything (orange isn't tied to beginners in any way). If they're meant to code the session types across the site, use them that way everywhere. If not, consider one hue at different strengths. This is taste.

**What's working**
- The type scale is strong: two sizes, bold display against regular text, a big jump between them, and tight leading on the two-line headlines so each reads as one unit. Keep it.
- The descriptions are specific and written to a person: "a coach in the water beside you", "three speeds to choose from", "floats and toys for the children". Each one answers the fear or question that particular visitor has, which is exactly what this kind of section needs. Keep that voice when you add the facts.
- White text on all three colours looks well above 4.5:1. Check the orange to be sure, since it's the closest call.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the empty lower half of each card is meant to hold an image or button that didn't load in this capture. Check in a live browser. If something does go there, points 1 and 2 may already be partly solved.
- Whether the cards are clickable, and what hover and focus states they have.
