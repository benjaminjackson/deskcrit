**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation is buried and has no next step.** *(Defect.)* The volunteer pitch starts at "Work days run every second Saturday…" in line 7 of 11. It's the most useful part of the section, and it looks exactly like the founding history around it. People skim web text and don't read a long, unbroken block (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web"), so most visitors will never reach "anyone is welcome." Even someone who does reach it gets no date, meeting place or way to sign up. "Every second Saturday" also reads two ways: every other Saturday, or the second Saturday of the month. That's a defect in a line people will act on, because someone who reads it the wrong way turns up on the wrong day. **Fix:** split the text into two parts. First, what the trust is and how it began. Second, the work days, under their own subhead, with the next date, where to meet and the start time, plus one specific link or button such as "Join the next work day on Saturday 6 December."
2. **The facts are strung together in one long sentence instead of laid out.** *(Taste, but it matters here.)* "Eleven miles of footpath, two small woods, the old quarry" and "four staff, around ninety volunteers, just over two thousand members" are the trust's proof that it's real and worth joining. Inside running prose they go by without registering. **Fix:** give these figures their own short paragraph, or a small row of figures, so the reader sees the scale of the trust at a glance.
3. **The copy stops at facts and never says what they mean to the reader.** *(Defect, copy.)* "Most of whom live within a few miles of the moor" is the hook ("people like you, near you"), but it's left as statistics. The line about tools, gloves and a hot drink is the warmest in the piece, and it sits in the middle of a run-on sentence. **Fix:** speak to the reader with "you" ("You don't need any experience. We bring the tools, gloves and a hot drink"), and finish on what they get out of it, such as the paths they walk staying open.

**Smaller things.**
- Composition: the text column sits left with nothing on the right. That's fine if a photo or sidebar goes there. If not, it looks unfinished; either fill the space with an image of the moor or a work day, or let the column be the page's single, deliberate measure.
