I compared the two versions. Only one thing differs: in version 2 the plan descriptions and feature lists are a darker grey. Layout, copy, prices, buttons and spacing are the same in both.

**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, then to the three prices, then to the blue "Start free trial" button on the middle card. This is a pricing page for small-business accounting or invoicing software. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. In version 1 the cards look washed out below the prices. In version 2 they read as solid text.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this: a pricing page that helps small-business owners pick a plan, steers most of them to "Small team", and gets them to start a trial.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The feature lists are the part people actually compare when choosing a plan, and version 1 makes them hard to read. With that fixed, the page is good. The main problem left is that it's unclear whether the higher plans include the features of the lower ones.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Feature and description text (defect in v1, fixed in v2).** In version 1, the descriptions ("For one person who sends a few invoices a month") and all twelve feature lines are a pale mid-grey. Against white, that looks below the 4.5:1 contrast minimum for normal text (WCAG 1.4.3). These lines are where the decision gets made. A visitor comparing "One bank feed" with "Three bank feeds" has to squint, and anyone with low vision or a glare-washed laptop may not be able to read them at all. Version 2's darker grey looks like it passes, and the prices and plan names still stay in charge. To confirm, measure the hex value against #FFFFFF.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **Unclear what each higher plan includes (defect).** The right card's feature list starts with "Everything in Small team". The middle card doesn't say "Everything in Sole trader". So a viewer can't tell whether Small team includes "Receipt capture by photo" and "Year-end tax summary". Those are features a sole trader growing into a team would worry about losing, and the doubt lands on the plan you're trying to sell. Fix: open the middle list with "Everything in Sole trader", so every tier visibly builds on the one below.
2. **The recommended plan is only signalled by button colour (taste).** The blue button does steer attention to "Small team". But nothing tells the viewer why it's the default choice. A card in the middle with a filled button is also the most familiar template on the web, so it doesn't stand out. A small label such as "Most businesses pick this", or a slightly stronger border on that card, would give the nudge a reason.

**Smaller things.**
- "Talk to us" sits under a published price of $58. It's unclear whether this plan has a free trial or needs a sales call. If it has a trial, offer it. If not, say what the call is for.
- Each card has roughly 60 px of empty space below its button, more than the padding at the top. Trim it so the buttons sit closer to the bottom edge.
- "Up to 20 invoices a month" is a figure people will hold you to. Say what happens at invoice 21 (blocked, or moved to the next plan) somewhere nearby.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight: headline, price, plan name, body and button. Each size has an obvious job, and the bold prices with a light "a month" read at a glance.
- The plan names and descriptions use the buyer's own words ("For one person who sends a few invoices a month"), so people can find themselves in a plan without decoding features.
- The subhead ("Pay monthly, cancel when you like. Prices exclude sales tax.") answers the two main worries about commitment and hidden costs before anyone asks.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the buttons, and how the three cards stack on mobile.

Better: version 2
