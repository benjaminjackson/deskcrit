**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then down the paragraph to the blue button. It reads as the top section of a local ferry operator's website, and it feels calm, tidy and plain. After that, nothing on the page tells me where the bay is, where the boat goes, or when it leaves, and the right half of the screen is blank.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a ferry company's homepage for local travellers and day-trippers, and its job is to get them to the timetable and onto a boat.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the hierarchy works. It still falls short because the words are vague exactly where a traveller makes a decision: no destination, no departure times, no price. On top of that, the page shows nothing of the trip it's selling.

**What would move it most**

1. **The facts someone acts on are missing or vague (defect).** "Boats leave the north landing every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" is the line a traveller plans around, and they can't plan from it.
   - "Early morning" could mean 6:00 or 8:00, and "late evening" could mean 21:00 or 23:30. Someone who reads it the hopeful way can miss the last boat home.
   - "The north landing" only means something to people who already know the route.
   - The headline says "cross the bay" but never names either side. Someone landing from search can't tell if this is their ferry.
   - Fix: name both ends and give real times, e.g. "North Landing to Harbour Town, every 40 minutes, 06:20 to 22:40." Add the fare if there is one.

2. **"Twelve minutes" has nothing to compare it to (defect, copy).** Twelve minutes sounds pleasant, but the viewer can't tell if it's the fast option. The headline asks them to take its claim on trust. Compare it with something they already know, such as the drive or bus round the bay ("12 minutes across, 45 by road"), and they can see the value for themselves. That one comparison is what makes this ferry a choice instead of a curiosity.

3. **The feature list repeats the paragraph and blurs the type scale (defect, with a convention behind it).**
   - "Bicycles travel free" and "Seats on the open deck" repeat "Bring a bicycle" and "the open deck" from the paragraph just above.
   - The list has no bullets or other markers, and its text looks about 18 px against roughly 20 px for the paragraph. Those two sizes are too close to read as different levels (Bringhurst), so the list looks like stray leftover lines rather than a group.
   - I count about five sizes: the small brand line, the headline, the paragraph, the list and the button. The paragraph and the list collide.
   - Fix: either cut the list and fold "bicycles travel free" into the paragraph, or make it a real list with its own clear treatment. Use the list for new information (price, accessibility, journey time), not repeats. Set it at the paragraph size and mark it out with markers or weight, not a second near-identical size (Refactoring UI).

4. **The page looks like any template (taste).** Inter, one blue, a left-aligned headline and a rounded button: it could be anyone's product. This page sells a calm crossing with a harbour view, and the viewer is told about that but shown none of it, so nothing here is memorable. Fix: give the boat, the deck or the harbour real presence, whether with a photo, a route map or the next-departure time set large.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "twelve / calm minutes", splitting the key phrase. "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" keeps it together.
- "Coastal Ferry Co." is small blue bold text sitting in the eyebrow-label position above the headline. As the only brand mark it's easy to miss. Give the name proper presence, or confirm a logo in the header does that job.
- "See the timetable" is clear, but the strongest version of this button does the work for the viewer: "Next boat 10:40, see all times."

**What's working.**
- There is one clear focal point and a simple top-to-bottom path. The headline dominates, then the copy, then a single high-emphasis action. Keep that order.
- The headline is spaced tightly, the paragraph runs about 70 characters per line with comfortable line spacing, and contrast is strong throughout. The craft is sound.
- The headline sounds like a person talking ("twelve calm minutes"), and the button names a specific next step. That tone fits the product.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The blank right half.** Roughly 40% of the width and the lower quarter of the screen are empty. That could be a hero image that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser. If it really is empty, that becomes finding 4: the space is wasted, not deliberate.
- Hover and focus states on the button, and how the layout reflows on mobile, where most people checking a ferry will actually be.
