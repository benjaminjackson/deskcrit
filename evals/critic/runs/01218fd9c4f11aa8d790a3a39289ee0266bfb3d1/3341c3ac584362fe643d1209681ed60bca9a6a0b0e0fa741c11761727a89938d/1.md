**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes". From there it drops through the paragraph and three loose lines to the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a website for a small local ferry, aimed at day-trippers and locals. It feels calm, clean and very plain. The right half of the screen is empty, so the page looks half-finished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a ferry operator's website, meant to get a casual visitor to open the timetable and plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The basics work: one clear headline, readable text and one obvious action. But the page leaves out the facts a rider actually plans around, and it promises a scenic crossing without showing anything. It's competent but thin, and it could belong to any ferry company.

**What would move it most**

1. **The copy skips the facts people act on.** *Defect.*
   - "From early morning until late evening" doesn't tell a rider whether they can catch a 7:00 boat or get home at 22:00, and those are the things they plan around.
   - "The north landing" doesn't say where that is, and nothing on the page says where the boat goes. "Cross the bay" names no destination.
   - There's no price.
   - A stranger has to click through just to learn the three things that decide whether they go.
   - **Fix:** put the real numbers in the paragraph, for example "Every 40 minutes from 6:20 to 22:40, from the north landing at [place] to [place]", and add the fare. Then "See the timetable" becomes a way to look up details, not the only way to find out if the ferry suits them.

2. **The three-line list repeats the paragraph and looks like leftover text.** *Defect, mostly in the copy.*
   - "Bicycles travel free" repeats "Bring a bicycle", and "Seats on the open deck" repeats "from the open deck". The viewer reads the same thing twice and learns nothing new.
   - The lines have no bullets or other markers. They're set only a little smaller than the paragraph (about 18 px against about 20 px) with about the same line spacing, so they read as a ragged continuation of the paragraph rather than a list of features.
   - **Fix:** use these three lines for the hard facts from point 1 (crossing time, how often boats leave, fare, bikes free) and cut the repeats from the paragraph. Then give the list a clear look of its own: icons or bullets, or a slightly heavier weight. Change it in weight, not by another small step in size. *Refactoring UI*: bolder, not bigger.

3. **The page is selling a view but shows nothing.** *Taste.*
   - "Watch the harbour slide past from the open deck" is the emotional pitch. The roughly 480 px of blank space to the right of the text is the obvious place for the boat or the bay, and right now it's just leftover space.
   - The text block alone isn't big enough to make that empty space look intentional, so the layout looks lopsided rather than calm.
   - **Fix:** put one photograph of the crossing in the right-hand space (the deck, the water, the far shore). Alternatively, make the headline much bigger so the type fills the screen with confidence. Be careful which way you go: a stock photo of a generic ferry would make the "could be anyone" problem worse.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** "twelve / calm minutes" splits the number from its unit. Break it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes".
- **Text sizes:** I count five (about 15, 18, 20 and 56 px, plus the button label). The 18 and 20 px sizes collide, so drop one of them.
- **Blue brand label:** "Coastal Ferry Co." uses the same blue as the button, so it may look like a link. Either set it in the dark text colour or make it the logo.
- **Spacing between groups:** the gaps between paragraph and list, and between list and button (both about 40 px), are barely bigger than the line spacing inside the list, so the groups don't separate clearly. Open up the gaps between groups.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** it's specific and promises the viewer something: a number, a place and a feeling ("calm"). Keep it.
- **The single action:** "See the timetable" names exactly what you get, and there's no second button competing with it.
- **The paragraph:** its line length looks to be about 65 characters, it's comfortably line-spaced, and the dark text on white has strong contrast. It's easy to read.
