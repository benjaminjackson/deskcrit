**First read.** In both versions the eye goes to the heading, then down the bold times on the left. It reads straight away as a bus departure board for one stand. The two red statuses ("4 min late", "Cancelled") are the only things that stand out. Version 1 feels like a boxed spreadsheet. Version 2 feels calmer and easier to read across each row.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a departure screen or web page for passengers at Stand C. It needs to answer "when is my bus, and is it running?" at a glance.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The two share the same content, type and colour. The only change is that version 2 swaps the full black cell grid for faint horizontal rules, which takes away the visual noise that was competing with the data. What still holds version 2 back is that a cancelled bus looks almost the same as a running one.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Version 1** boxes every cell in heavy black lines: an outer frame, vertical rules between all five columns, and dark rules under every row. Those lines are as dark as the text, so the grid competes with the times and destinations. The verticals also cut each row into five boxes, when a passenger needs to read across one row (time → route → destination → status). This is a convention issue (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*: ink that doesn't carry data; Butterick, *Practical Typography*: keep table borders light). Here it has a real cost: the red statuses have to fight through the grid to stand out.
- **Version 2** drops the verticals and the frame, keeps faint row rules, and uses the spacing between columns to keep them apart. The columns still line up clearly, rows are easier to follow across, and the red exceptions now stand out at once. Nothing was lost.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **A cancelled bus looks almost like a running one** (14:40, route 19, Northgate). Only the word "Cancelled" in red, at the same size and weight as "On time", tells it apart. The time, route and destination look exactly like every other row. Someone scanning for "19" or "Northgate" lands on a bold 14:40 and may not read across to the far-right column. That's a defect: someone could wait for a bus that isn't coming. Fix: make the whole row say it. Dim or strike through the time, and set "Cancelled" in bold. A cancellation should look stronger than a delay, not the same.

**Smaller things**
- **Route numbers** (12, 7A, 31) are plain weight. Many passengers look for their route number before the time. Making them bold too, or putting them in a small badge, would help scanning. That's taste.
- **Wide gap between Via and Status:** the Via column runs to about x≈985, which puts Status a long way from the row it belongs to. Narrowing Via would bring the status closer to the time and destination.
- **"4 min late"** gives no new expected time. Passengers would have to work out 14:15 themselves. Adding "Exp. 14:15" would answer the question they actually have.

**What's working (keep in version 2)**
- **Bold times in the first column** make an obvious scanning column, in departure order.
- **Red is used only for problems**, so it means something every time it appears. Because the words also carry the meaning, the board doesn't rely on colour alone.
- **The heading and the "Stand C · Updated 14:02" line** set where you are and how fresh the data is, without getting in the way.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- How often it updates live.
- Whether it's readable on the real screen, at real viewing distance and brightness. The faint row rules in version 2 may disappear on a bright outdoor display.

Better: version 2
