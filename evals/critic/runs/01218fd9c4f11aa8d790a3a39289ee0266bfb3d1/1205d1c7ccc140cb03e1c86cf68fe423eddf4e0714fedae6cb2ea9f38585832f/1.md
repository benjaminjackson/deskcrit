**What would move it most**
1. **Hang the opening quotation mark** (convention). At about 80 px, the "“" at the start of line one sits inside the text edge. That pushes "The" about 35 px to the right of "just", "leaves" and "the" below it. At this size the step is easy to see: the quote's left edge, the strongest vertical on the card, starts with a notch, and the first word looks indented by mistake. The usual fix is to move the quote mark out into the margin so the "T" lines up with the left edge of the lines below. That edge then runs clean from the kicker to the caption. The idea that the left edge should stay crisp and opening quotes should hang is standard practice, but my reference has no confirmed source for the quote-mark point. I'm arguing it on its own terms: the step is visible, and it is the one flaw in an otherwise exact layout.

**Smaller things.**
- **Bottom of the frame:** the content sits in the top three-quarters and leaves about 200 px of empty space below the caption that has no job. Either move the block down a little so the empty space reads as chosen, or crop the card tighter (taste).
- **Tracking:** at display size the quote looks set at its default spacing. A small amount of negative tracking would make it read more as a single shape (Bringhurst §2.1.1; Hochuli). This is a subtle point; check the letter spacing in the source file.

**What's working.**
- **Contrast between the two typefaces.** A warm, sturdy serif for the voice and a neutral sans for the facts. They contrast clearly and never compete. The real "fi" ligature in "first" and the curly quotes show the setting was done with care.
- **Hierarchy through weight and colour, not just size.** The name and the caption are almost the same size. They are separated by bold dark versus regular grey, which is the right tool for that job (Refactoring UI). Keep the scale at these four levels.
- **The copy.** The quote is specific and has a turn at the end ("that is when the gulls get bold"). The caption gives a time, 5:40, and three concrete things the ferry carries, so the scene feels real rather than templated. Don't trim it.
