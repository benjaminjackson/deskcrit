**First read.** In version 1 the eye skips the headline and lands on the three card titles. "Perennials / Fruit trees / Workshops" are set larger and heavier than "Hardy plants grown on our hillside", so the page reads as a category menu with a caption above it. In version 2 the eye lands on the headline first, then the intro sentence, then the cards. It reads as a nursery with something to say about itself, and then what it sells. Both feel calm and plain, and a little empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of a small plant nursery's online shop. It should tell a gardener why these plants are different (they're grown outdoors, so they're hardy) and send them into a category.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the headline size, and it fixes the one thing that was broken in version 1: the type order ran backwards. Version 2 still has no clear next step and grey boxes where the photos should be.

**What changed and why it matters**
1. **The headline now outranks the card titles (defect in v1, fixed in v2).** In v1 the headline looks about 26 px and the card titles about 28 px, both bold. The page's main message was smaller than its own sub-labels, so a visitor took in "three categories" before learning why this nursery is worth buying from. In v2 the headline looks about 60 px, more than twice the card titles. That makes the steps clear: headline, card title, body, nav. That's four levels with real gaps between them, which is how a type scale should work (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI). The "hardy, grown outdoors" pitch now gets read first, as it should.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **There's no ask (defect).** Nothing on the page says what to do next. The cards don't look clickable: no link text, no arrow, no button. The only actions are small nav links in the top right. Since "ready for a cold garden" is the pitch, give one specific next step that ties to it, e.g. "Shop plants hardy to your area" or "Browse 300 perennials". Make the cards look clickable too.
2. **The image slots are flat colour blocks (defect, if this is the final art).** A nursery sells something you look at. Three green rectangles tell the visitor nothing and make the page feel unfinished and generic. Put real photos of the plants on the hillside here. They're the proof of the headline's claim.
3. **The card copy describes but doesn't sell (taste).** "Over 300 kinds, sold in one and two litre pots" and "Apples, pears, and plums on dwarf roots" are clear facts, but they never say what that means for the buyer. "Dwarf roots" is the place to say "fits a small garden; fruits in two to three years" (only if true). "Each spring" for workshops needs a date or "book now" so it can be acted on.

**Smaller things.**
- In v2, "hillside" sits alone on line two. Widen the headline box slightly, or break the line on purpose ("Hardy plants / grown on our hillside").
- The bottom third of the screen is empty. The page has nothing below the cards, such as a visit/location block or the cold-hardiness proof.
- The nav dots ("Plants · Workshops · Visit · Basket (0)") run together as one line of text. Give them more space so each reads as a separate target.
- "one and two litre pots" should read "one- and two-litre pots".

**What's working.**
- The intro sentence links the product to the benefit in the visitor's terms: "so they are ready for a cold garden on the day they arrive." Keep that clause.
- The colours are restrained: dark green text on an off-white background with sage tints. They suit a nursery and keep the contrast high.
- The three-column grid is clean, with the edges lined up to the logo and the nav.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are links and how they respond on hover. How the 60 px headline wraps on a phone.

Better: version 2
