**First read.** My eye goes straight to the tilted blue SALE badge on the right. It's the only saturated colour on the page and the only thing set at an angle. Next it jumps back left to "Linens and cookware for the weekend", then down to four calm product cards. It reads as a small, tasteful homewares shop running a weekend promotion. It feels quiet and competent but generic, and the loud blue badge doesn't belong with the warm, linen-toned palette around it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a desktop homepage for a home-goods retailer, and its job is to get shoppers into a time-limited sale of up to 40% off and get them buying.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do that job yet. The page announces a sale, then gives the shopper no way into it and no sign of it on the products shown. The biggest problem is that the most eye-catching element, the SALE badge, is a dead end.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero has no ask, and the badge sits where one should be.** *Defect.* The banner has a headline, a sub-line and a badge, but no "Shop the sale" button or link. The blue badge is a filled, rounded rectangle in the only accent colour, so it looks like a button (Norman: affordances and signifiers). It's tilted like a sticker, though, and it says the same thing as the sub-line. Shoppers will either click it and maybe get nothing, or ignore it and have nowhere to go except the small grey "Sale" link at the far right of the nav, which looks just like Kitchen, Bedroom and Garden. **Fix:** decide what the badge is. Either make it a real primary button with an exact label ("Shop 40% off linens & cookware"), or keep it as decoration and add one clear primary action under the sub-line. Give the blue only to clickable things (one accent per primary action; Material Design, Refactoring UI).

2. **The products don't show the sale.** *Defect.* The page promises "Up to 40% off", but the four cards right under it show single prices ($34, $89, $52, $68) with no old price, discount or sale tag. Shoppers can't tell whether these items are discounted, which ones are, or how much they'd save. That makes the promise feel empty at the moment they're judging value. The fair comparison is the item's own regular price, and that's exactly what's missing. **Fix:** if these items are on sale, show the regular price struck through next to the sale price, plus a small "–30%" marker. If they aren't, make this row sale items, or label it plainly as full-price items so nobody feels misled.

3. **The copy could be on any shop's homepage, and the deadline is vague.** *Defect (copy).* "Linens and cookware for the weekend" names two categories but doesn't say why to act now or what's special here. "Through Sunday" is the line shoppers will hold you to, and it has two readings: this Sunday or the next, and ending at the start or the end of the day. Someone who comes back Sunday night and finds the sale over will feel cheated. **Fix:** put the deadline in exact terms, like "Ends Sunday, Nov 29 at midnight". Put the reason to act in the headline itself, like "Up to 40% off linens and cookware, this weekend only", and let the sub-line add detail.

**Smaller things.**
- The hero text starts about 8 px to the right of the logo, "Popular this week" and the card edges, all of which share one left edge. It's too small to look deliberate, so it looks like a mistake. Line it up with the rest of the page (Müller-Brockmann).
- There are too many sizes too close together. I count about seven: headline ~40, section heading ~22, logo ~20, sub-line ~18, card title 16 bold, price 16, nav ~15. The section heading and logo are nearly the same size, and so are the sub-line and card titles. Cut it to headline, section heading, body and small, and separate the rest by weight (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- The cool, saturated blue of the badge works against the warm beige, stone and linen palette. If it has to stay, a deeper colour from the warm side of the palette would still stand out without looking like it came from another site. *Taste.*
- Each card has a thin border around a coloured image block around a rounded inner placeholder: three frames for one product. Dropping the card border and letting space and the image carry the group would look calmer (Refactoring UI).
- Prices look a little light and small next to the bold titles. Once sale prices go in, the price should be the stronger of the two.

**What's working.**
- The restrained warm palette and single sans-serif family suit linens and cookware, and they look trustworthy. Keep that and bring the accent into line with it.
- The product row is clean and regular: four equal cards, even gaps, and the same name-then-price order in each, so they're easy to scan. Keep that structure when you add sale pricing.
- The headline's tight two-line block is set well and holds together as one unit.
