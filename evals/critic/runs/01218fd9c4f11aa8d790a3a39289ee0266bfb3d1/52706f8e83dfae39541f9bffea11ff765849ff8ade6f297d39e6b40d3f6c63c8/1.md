**First read.** My eye goes to the colourful stacked-area chart at the bottom first, then up to the bold heading "Fossil fuels accounted for about 58%…". The sidebar headings "Learn more" and "Also on Energy Explained" pull at the eye too, because they are bigger than that heading. The figure that should carry the section, the outlet-shaped share graphic near the top, is a pale, blurry patch I can't read. It reads as a government explainer page: careful and credible, but it feels like a reference document rather than something that explains.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an EIA "Energy Explained" page for a general audience such as students, journalists and curious citizens. Its job is to show where U.S. electricity came from in 2025 and how that mix has changed.

**Verdict.** The text does its job. The two charts that should carry the story don't. The 2025 share graphic is unreadable at page size and doesn't show the headline number. The two charts give the same sources different colours. And neither chart tells you what it shows.

**What would move it most**

1. **The 2025 share graphic is a picture of text you can't read.** This is the outlet graphic below the intro.
   - Its labels ("nuclear 17.7%", "coal 16.6%"), the renewables breakdown table (wind 10.5%, hydro 5.6%…) and the source note look about 5–7 px tall. They are soft from scaling and sit on top of a ghosted outlet drawing. At page size the viewer gets four pastel bands and has to click "Click to enlarge" to learn anything. The link admits the figure doesn't work where it sits.
   - It is also an image of text, so the numbers can't be read by screen readers, found with page search, or reflowed (WCAG 1.4.5).
   - The outlet metaphor adds nothing to the data. It is the main reason the labels are cramped.
   - **Fix:** rebuild it as a live chart (a sorted horizontal bar, or one stacked bar) at the full column width. Set the labels at body size and drop the outlet.
   - *Defect* (convention: WCAG 1.4.5; Tufte on chartjunk and data-ink).

2. **The headline makes a claim the chart doesn't show, and the numbers disagree with each other.**
   - The heading says "Fossil fuels… about 58%". No chart shows 58%. The reader has to add 40.8 + 16.6 + 0.7 themselves.
   - The figures also clash. The text says petroleum alone was "about 0.7%" and "other gases and other sources" another 0.2%. The graphic labels "petroleum *and other* 0.7%". A careful reader now has two different values for the same slice and has to wonder which to trust. On a statistics agency's page, that is the one thing that must not happen.
   - Smaller wobbles: the two charts cite different months of *Electric Power Monthly* (February 2026 vs January 2026). The natural-gas paragraph says "utility-scale" generation while the coal paragraph just says "U.S. electricity generation".
   - **Fix:** group the three fossil sources visibly in the share chart and label the group 58%. Make the petroleum figure match between text and chart.
   - *Defect.*

3. **The two charts give the same sources different colours.**

   | Source | Share graphic | Area chart |
   |---|---|---|
   | Natural gas | grey-white | blue |
   | Coal | pink | brown |
   | Nuclear | light blue | red |
   | Renewables | yellow-green | green |

   Natural gas, the largest source, is light blue in the first chart, but light blue means nuclear here. In the second chart blue means natural gas. Someone moving from the 2025 snapshot to the history brings the wrong colour key with them.
   - **Fix:** give each source one colour across the whole page, or the whole site.
   - *Defect* (consistency: Nielsen 4; Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*).

4. **The area chart has a topic, not a takeaway, and its most important story is buried.**
   - The title "U.S. electricity generation by major energy source, 1950–2025" names the subject. The real story is that coal peaked around 2007, natural gas overtook it around 2016, and renewables have grown fast since. Stacking hides this. Only coal, sitting on the zero baseline, can be read as a trend. Natural gas, nuclear and renewables ride on top of the layers below them, so you can't compare their sizes over time.
   - The colours are hard to tell apart. Coal brown and petroleum tan are near neighbours. Nuclear red and renewables green sit next to each other, which is the classic red-green pair that fails for colour-blind readers.
   - The legend makes you look back and forth between the chart and the key.
   - **Fix:** use a line chart, one line per source, labelled directly at the right end. Give it a title that states the finding ("Natural gas overtook coal in 2016"), and annotate the crossover.
   - *Defect for the stacking and the colours; convention for the title and labels* (Wilke; Few; Tufte).

5. **The sidebar outranks the content.** "Learn more", "Also on Energy Explained" and "Also on EIA.gov" are set larger than the main heading, "Fossil fuels accounted for about 58%…", and sit under heavy purple rules. The navigation shouts while the finding speaks at normal volume.
   - **Fix:** make the section heading in the main column the largest type on the page, and step the sidebar headings down to the size of a small label.
   - *Defect* (hierarchy).

**Smaller things.**
- **Line length:** body lines run about 90–95 characters. Narrow the text column, not the charts, towards about 70 characters (Bringhurst).
- **Link density:** the petroleum paragraph has seven blue, non-underlined links in four lines, so it reads as broken up. Link each term once, or move the related terms into a list after the paragraph.
- **Current-page marker:** in the sidebar, "▸Electricity in the United States" is pale grey and looks disabled rather than selected. Give it dark text in bold.
- **Second "Click to enlarge":** it floats about 50 px below the area chart's source note and has lost its connection to the chart. Tuck it up under the chart.
- **Empty space beside the share graphic:** the graphic fills only about half the column under a full-width rule, leaving a large empty block to its right. Size it to the column.
- **Too many type sizes:** I count about seven across body, headings, sidebar heads, chart titles and the micro-type, in two families (the geometric body face and the charts' Arial-like face). Tighten to four sizes, and set chart text in the page face.
- **Rules:** the heavy grey rules above each chart and the purple sidebar rules are heavier than anything they separate. Lighten them or use space instead.

**What's working.**
- The section heading states a finding in a full sentence, and each paragraph opens on the source name as a link. That makes the text easy to skim. Keep both patterns.
- The intro defines "utility-scale" vs "small-scale" and gives the 4.43 trillion kWh total before any chart. The reader has the context first.
- The area chart's legend order matches the stack order, its y-axis starts at zero with units, and its source and scope note are clearly set. Keep that rigour when you rebuild it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the area chart is interactive (it has a menu icon) and whether hover tooltips make up for the legend.
- How the two-column layout reflows on a phone, especially the image-based share graphic.
