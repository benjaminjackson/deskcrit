**First read.** My eye goes to the tallest bar (Growth, 4.0%), then to the bold title, then across to the table. Within a couple of seconds it reads as an analytics dashboard page: a calm, competent conversion-by-plan report for people inside a SaaS company. It feels tidy, trustworthy and a bit quiet. Nothing tells me what to make of it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal analytics view. A growth, product or pricing person uses it to see which plan converts trials best in Q3 2025 and whether that should change what they do.

**Verdict.** It's clean, accurate (I checked: the bars match the table, e.g. 286 / 9,210 = 3.1%) and honest. But it shows four bars that look equally certain, with nothing to compare them against. So the reader can see that Growth is highest but can't tell whether that matters or whether it's even real. The design is sound. What it doesn't do is help anyone reach a conclusion.

**What would move it most**

1. **The bars hide how uncertain the numbers are.** *(Defect.)* Enterprise's 3.4% rests on 38 conversions. At that size the true rate could plausibly be anywhere from about 2.3% to 4.5%, which covers every other plan on the chart. Growth (118 conversions) carries roughly ±0.7 points. Starter vs Growth is a real gap; Team, Enterprise and Growth may not differ at all. Yet all four bars are drawn as equally solid, labelled to one decimal place, and the sample sizes sit in a separate table on the right. A pricing person will read "Growth converts best, Enterprise underperforms" and may act on a gap that is just noise. Wilke (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16) says to show uncertainty when you plot estimates from samples. Fix: add interval whiskers to each bar, or at least put "n = 1,120 trials · 38 paid" under each plan label, so the confidence is visible on the chart itself.

2. **The page gives no point of reference, so no number can be read as good or bad.** *(Convention.)* 4.0% next to 3.1% tells me the ranking but not whether Q3 was better or worse than Q2, or how it compares to a target or the all-plan average. Few ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design") flags metrics shown without a comparison. A dashboard reader's first question is "is this up or down?", and this page can't answer it. Fix: show the previous quarter as a ghost bar or tick beside each bar, or a single overall-average line across the chart. Even "All plans: 3.4%" in the subtitle would help.

3. **The title names the topic, not the finding.** *(Taste, and I'd argue it.)* "Trial to paid conversion by plan" is a label, so every reader has to work out the point for themselves, and they may get it wrong (see 1). Once the uncertainty is shown, a title like "Growth converts best; Starter trails by about a point" would tell the reader what's there and what isn't. On a live dashboard the title may need to stay generic. If so, add a one-line takeaway under the subtitle.

**Smaller things**
- Table on the right: add a "Conversion" column (3.1%, 3.6%…). Right now the reader has to match the table to the chart by eye to get the rate next to its sample size.
- The data labels repeat what the gridlines and y-axis show. Pick one: drop the axis and most gridlines (Tufte, data-ink), or drop the labels. Labels are the better choice at four bars.
- The definition says trials open on "30 October" count as not converted. That only works if the latest trial starts 30 Sep with a 30-day window. Say so ("…30 days after the last Q3 trial start") so nobody reads it as a typo.
- The right column ends at about mid-height, leaving a large blank area below the definition. That's fine on its own, but it's the obvious place for the comparison or takeaway from findings 2 and 3.
- The source line and axis labels look about 12 px and mid-grey. They're probably fine, but check they reach 4.5:1 contrast.

**What's working**
- The y-axis starts at zero and the bars aren't truncated. That keeps the gaps looking as small as they really are. Don't "zoom in" to make them dramatic.
- Plans are in the same order (Starter → Enterprise, the natural tier order) in both the chart and the table. That's the right ordering and it makes cross-reading easy.
- The subtitle and the definition state exactly what "converted" means, including the 30-day window and the paid-invoice rule. That kind of precision is rare and is why the page feels trustworthy. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether hovering a bar shows counts or intervals. If it does, findings 1 and 2 matter less in the app, but still matter for screenshots shared in decks.
