**First read.** My eye goes first to the four purple bars, then the 4.0% label on Growth, then the headline. Within a few seconds it reads as a clean internal analytics page: Growth converts best and the other plans are a bit lower. It feels calm and trustworthy. It's well made, and nothing on it shouts.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal analytics report for product and growth people, who will use it to decide whether conversion differs by plan and whether to act on that.

**Verdict.** As a chart it's good: honest, readable and well labelled. As analysis it falls short in one way. It shows a ranking of differences under one point, with no uncertainty and no comparison, so the reader can't tell whether Growth really converts better or whether any of these numbers is good. Fix that, and say the finding in the title.

**What would move it most**

1. **The bars rank differences the data may not support (defect).** The gaps between 3.1%, 3.4%, 3.6% and 4.0% look decisive because each bar has a bold label at the same visual certainty. The table on the right shows how thin some of this is: Enterprise is 38 conversions out of 1,120. Very roughly, a 95% interval for Enterprise runs from about 2.3% to 4.4%, which overlaps every other plan. Starter (286 of 9,210) and Growth (118 of 2,950) are much firmer, so that gap is probably real. A reader who sees only the chart will treat "Enterprise is below Team" as a fact and may act on it. Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization* (ch. 16), says samples should carry ranges or notes about sample size. Add error bars or interval whiskers. At least put n under each plan name ("Enterprise · 1,120 trials"), so the weak bar shows its weakness where people look.

2. **Nothing tells the reader whether 3.6% is good (defect).** There's no previous quarter, target or overall average. The reader can compare plans with each other but can't tell whether conversion is up, down, or on plan. For an analytics page that's the question most people arrive with. Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design": put a comparison next to each number, such as the change from Q2 or the target. A faint Q2 marker on each bar, or a "vs Q2" column in the table, would do it. Also add an all-plans rate (about 3.4%, from 627 of 18,420) as a reference line, so each bar reads as above or below average.

3. **The title names the topic, not the finding (taste, argued).** "Trial to paid conversion by plan" makes every reader work out the point for themselves, and some will settle on the wrong bar. If the finding is "Growth converts best; Enterprise is too small to call", say that, and keep the current wording as the subtitle. No source on my list states this rule. I'm arguing it because this page exists to support a decision, and the title is the only line everyone reads.

4. **The 30-day window and the cutoff date can be read two ways (defect, wording).** The subtitle says trials count as converted "within 30 days". The definition says trials "still open on 30 October are counted as not converted". Reading one: each trial gets its own 30 days. Reading two: everything paid before 30 October counts, so a July trial gets about 90 days. Under reading two, plans with earlier-starting trials look better. The source line ("trial events, 1 July to 30 September") also doesn't mention that invoices up to 30 October were included. Make it one sentence with one rule, for example: "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice is paid within 30 days of the trial starting."

**Smaller things.**
- The table has Trials and Paid but no rate column, so the bars can't be checked against it without arithmetic. Add "Rate", and a total row.
- The y-axis repeats information the direct labels already give. Drop it, or lighten its labels and gridlines further, and end the scale at about 4.5% so the 4–5% band isn't empty (Tufte, data-ink).
- The right column stops at about 40% of the page height, leaving a large dead block beside the chart. Move the source line under the definition, or put the comparison data there.
- Bars are in plan order, not value order. That's the right call here because plan order is a natural order, so keep it.
- The active nav item (Conversion) is marked only by bold and a slightly darker colour. Next to the other items it reads as only a little different. Consider an underline or indicator.

**What's working.**
- The value axis starts at zero, so bar heights are honest: 3.1 vs 4.0 looks like the modest difference it is. Keep that even if someone asks to "make the differences pop".
- The bars are labelled directly, with one restrained colour and no legend. The chart reads in a glance.
- The type is tight: about four sizes with clear jobs (page title, section head, body/table, small axis and source text). The table uses right-aligned tabular figures.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the bars or table rows have hover detail (n, intervals) that already covers point 1. If so, show it on the page anyway, because a screenshot of this page will travel without it.
