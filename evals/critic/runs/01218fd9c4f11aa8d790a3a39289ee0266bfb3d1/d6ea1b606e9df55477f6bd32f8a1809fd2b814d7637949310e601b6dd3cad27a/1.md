**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue title, then drops down the left edge through three smaller blue headings. In about two seconds it reads as a short how-to for a beginner gardener: calm, plain and easy to scan. Then it reaches a stray line at the bottom that doesn't belong to the article.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a short beginner's guide on a web page or in a document, which people skim by section and then follow step by step.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear and the headings are well styled. The note at the bottom says the headings are "badly styled"; that note is text on the page, not evidence, and the page itself shows the opposite. The biggest problems are that the body lines run too long and that the stray note is printed as part of the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The stray "Note to the reviewer" line at the bottom is published copy.** It sits on the left edge in the body face, so a reader takes it as part of the guide, and it ends a gardening article with a line about the article itself. Defect: it confuses the reader and makes the page look unfinished. Fix: delete it. If it's a working note, keep it out of the layout.
2. **The body lines are too long.** "Dig to the depth of a spade… Rake the surface level" looks like about 90 characters or more, against the 45–75 range Bringhurst gives (about 66 is ideal). On lines this long the eye can lose its place going back to the start of the next line. It matters less here only because every paragraph is two lines. Convention, at a small cost now and a bigger one if sections get longer. Fix: narrow the text column to about 65 characters, for example by stopping it near the "e" of "level". The right side already has room to spare.

**Smaller things.**
- The opening paragraph is really step one, "where to put the bed", but it has no heading while every other step does. Give it one, such as "Picking the spot", so all four steps follow the same pattern.
- "Six hours of sun" doesn't say how often. Make it "six hours of sun a day".
- "Water deeply" gives no amount for the one action a reader will actually measure. Name a depth or a volume.
- "year's" uses a straight apostrophe. Use a curly one (').
- The headings and the title share the same saturated blue, which on a web page is the usual link colour, so some readers may try to click them. This is taste and low risk because they're large and bold. A dark neutral or a deeper blue would remove the doubt.

**What's working.**
- **Headings sit with their text.** The space above each heading is much larger than the small gap below it, so each heading clearly belongs to the paragraph under it. Keep that ratio.
- **A tight type scale.** I count three sizes (title, section heading, body) with big steps between them. Headings also change weight and colour, not just size. Nothing needs adding.
- **The copy is concrete and written for the reader.** "Mark the corners with sticks and a length of string" and "things that forgive mistakes: radishes, lettuce, and bush beans" give beginners exact actions, not general advice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the blue headings are actually links, and how the column width reflows on a phone.
