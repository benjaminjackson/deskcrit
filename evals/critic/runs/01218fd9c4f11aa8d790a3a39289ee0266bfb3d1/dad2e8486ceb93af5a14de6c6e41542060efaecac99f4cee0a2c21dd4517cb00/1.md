**First read.** My eye lands first on the bold question, then drops straight to the solid blue "Save changes" button at bottom right. That path is right. Then it slides left across a row of four actions. "Export as file" and "Discard changes" look like twins: same blue, same weight, no outline. I have to read each label to learn that one of them throws away my work. It reads as a tidy, calm confirmation dialog in a calendar app.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web-app confirmation dialog. Someone who has edited a shared calendar uses it to decide whether to publish the edits to followers, keep working, or throw the edits away.

**Verdict.** The dialog gets the main path right. It falls down on safety: the one irreversible choice, "Discard changes", is styled exactly like a harmless extra, and it sits one slot away from the safe "Keep editing". A fourth action that has nothing to do with the decision crowds the row.

**What would move it most**

1. **"Discard changes" looks harmless.** *(Defect, plus a convention: Apple HIG "Alerts"; Material Design "Dialogs".)* In the button row, "Discard changes" uses the same blue text-link style as "Export as file". The viewer gets no visual hint that this button deletes their rehearsal edits. A quick scan along the row, or a misclick aimed at "Keep editing" next to it, loses their work. Both guidelines say a destructive action should be set apart from the safe ones and styled as destructive (usually red text).
   - **Fix:** colour "Discard changes" as destructive and move it away from "Keep editing" and "Save changes". The usual layout puts it alone at the far left, with the safe pair grouped on the right. If you can, also offer undo after a discard rather than relying on this dialog being the last chance.

2. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** *(Defect: Nielsen 8, aesthetic and minimalist design. Norman: constraints.)* The question is "save or not?". Exporting answers a different question, so it adds a fourth option to a choice that should have two or three. It also takes the far-left position, which is the natural place for Discard. And because it's styled like Discard, the two read as a pair. That pairing hides the danger in finding 1.
   - **Fix:** remove it from this dialog and offer export in the calendar's own menu. If it really must stay, separate it clearly from the decision buttons.

3. **The main button undersells what it does.** *(Taste, argued from the copy.)* The body text is good: it ends on what happens to other people ("shown to everyone who follows this calendar"). The button then drops back to the generic "Save changes", and the title says "Save" too. What actually happens is that the change goes live for the whole choir. Someone clicking "Save" may think of it as a private step.
   - **Fix:** name the outcome on the button and in the title, for example "Publish new times" or "Update for followers", so whoever clicks knows they are notifying people.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Keep editing" outline is a mid-grey. It looks close to the 3:1 minimum against white (WCAG 1.4.11), so check it.
- The three tiers make sense (filled, outlined, text), but there are two text-style buttons. Once Export goes, the row reads as Discard (destructive text), Keep editing (outline), Save (filled), which is the standard pattern.
- The card has a border, a shadow and rounded corners all at once. Either the shadow or the border is enough on a white page.

**What's working.**
- There is one clearly dominant action. "Save changes" is the only filled control, so the main path is never in doubt. Keep that.
- The type scale is tight: a bold title, one body size and one button size, all clearly separate levels. Nothing competes with the question.
- The body copy is specific (choir rehearsal times) and states what happens to followers instead of a generic "Are you sure?". Keep that specificity.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button gets keyboard focus when the dialog opens. It shouldn't be "Discard changes", and ideally it isn't "Save" either, since saving publishes to everyone.
- Whether Escape or a click outside the dialog maps to "Keep editing".
