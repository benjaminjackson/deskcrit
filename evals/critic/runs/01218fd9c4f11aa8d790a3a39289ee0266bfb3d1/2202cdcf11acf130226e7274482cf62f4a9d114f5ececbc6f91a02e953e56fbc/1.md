**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline, "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY", which is heavy, black, and fills most of the width. Next it drops to the two-line paragraph, and only after that goes back up to the small grey "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" label. It reads as the hero section of an event page or a simple poster for a free public ferry ride. It feels calm, confident and friendly. It also feels unfinished: everything sits in the top half, and the bottom 40% and the right third of the frame are empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an event announcement or landing-page hero. Its job is to get members of the public to turn up at the north landing for a free ferry ride on the open day.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy and type are clean and work. As an announcement it fails, because it never says when. There is no date and no time, so nobody can act on it.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's no date or time anywhere.** This is a defect. "Open Day" names an event but not the day. "Every half hour" has no start or end, and "morning" in the headline is the only hint of timing. Someone who wants to go can't plan it, so they'll either look it up somewhere else or give up. They might also guess wrong: "morning ferry" could mean the rides only run in the morning, while "every half hour" could mean all day. Because these are the details people act on and hold the organiser to, that ambiguity is a real failure and not a matter of style. To fix it, give the date and the hours as their own line with real weight, either right under the headline or joined to the label ("CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY · SAT 14 JUNE"). Spell out the window in full: "Free rides every half hour, 8–11 am, from the north landing."

2. **Nothing tells the viewer what to do next, and the empty lower half makes it look cut off.** This is partly a defect and partly taste. The paragraph says where the rides leave from, but there's no action: no booking or no-booking note, no map or directions link, no address for "north landing". The blank space below the text adds to this. It doesn't feel like deliberate breathing room. It feels as if a block is missing, and that is exactly where the eye looks for the practical details. To fix it, use that space for one specific action, such as "Get directions to the north landing" or "No booking — just turn up". Alternatively, cut the frame down so the white space is clearly intended.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline capitals are tracked at the loose end. Letterspacing capitals is right (Bringhurst 2.1.6 suggests 5–10% of the type size), but here the word spaces in "ALL ABOARD THE" are only about twice the letter gaps, so the words start to fall apart. Tighten the tracking slightly, or widen the word spaces.
- The headline breaks after "THE". "ALL ABOARD / THE MORNING FERRY" would keep the phrase together.
- The paragraph breaks after "Bring a", which leaves "hat and a thermos." as an orphaned half-sentence. Break after "landing." so each sentence gets its own line.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clear: a small grey caps label, a very large headline, and body text at reading size. These three levels have big steps between them and each has an obvious job. Keep it at three.
- The copy has a voice. "Bring a hat and a thermos" sounds like a person talking and tells you what the morning will feel like. Keep that tone when you add the practical details.
- The single strong left edge lines up the label, headline and paragraph, which holds a sparse layout together.
