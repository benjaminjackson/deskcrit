**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue "Planning a vegetable bed" and then steps down the three blue subheads: soil, what to plant, watering. It reads straight away as a calm, plain beginner's how-to on a website or blog. It feels friendly and tidy, a little bare, and the right third and bottom of the page are empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web article that teaches a first-time gardener to set up one vegetable bed. They read it before they start and may check it again while working.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The hierarchy is clear and the type is well behaved. Two things hold it back. The lines of text are too long to read comfortably. And the page is laid out like a short essay when it's really a list of steps, so a reader who skims the headings misses the first step and the actual numbers.

**What would move it most**

1. **The text lines are too long. (Convention, with a real cost.)** The body paragraphs run from x≈160 to about x≈950. "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" is about 90 characters before it wraps. Bringhurst gives 45 to 75 characters as comfortable and 66 as ideal (*Elements of Typographic Style* 2.1.2). Each paragraph is only two lines, so it doesn't hurt much yet. Still, the eye has a long way to travel back to the start of the next line, and every paragraph ends with a short leftover line ("sticks and a length of string.", "so each kind is easy to find."), which makes the column look looser than it is. **Fix:** narrow the text column to about 600–640 px at this size, or about 65 characters. Wider lines would also need more line spacing, so the current spacing could stay. The headline can keep its full width.

2. **The steps don't look like steps, and the first one is hidden. (Defect.)** The reader is following an order: pick a spot, prepare the soil, choose plants, water. But the first step, choosing a spot, has no heading. It sits as an intro paragraph under the title, so anyone scanning the headings starts at "Preparing the soil" and skips where to put the bed. The numbers the reader will actually act on (six hours of sun, a spade's depth, a week to settle, twice a week) are buried mid-sentence with nothing to make them stand out. **Fix:** give the site step its own heading ("Choosing a spot"). Consider numbering the four headings so the order is explicit. Let the key figures carry a little more weight, either in bold or by starting each paragraph with them, so a reader with muddy hands can find "twice a week" in a glance.

3. **The copy leaves out things a beginner can't guess. (Defect, in the words.)** Read as a stranger would, a few lines make you fill gaps:
   - "Six hours of sun": the writer means per day. Say so.
   - "Mark the corners": the page is about planning a bed but never says how big the bed should be. A first-timer needs a number, such as roughly 1.2 m wide so you can reach the middle.
   - "Water deeply": there's no measure of what deep means, and no mention of when to sow.
   - "Watering" is one line, while the other sections have two. It reads as unfinished rather than as a deliberate short ending.

   These gaps are where a beginner gets it wrong and blames the guide. **Fix:** add the missing quantity or timing to each of these lines, said to the reader.

**Smaller things.**
- Headings: every heading is the same saturated blue, the colour people expect links to be. They may look clickable, and the accent isn't left for anything that actually is a link. Set the headings in a near-black, and keep blue for links or one highlight (Apple HIG / Material: accent restraint).
- Apostrophe: "last year's heap" looks like it uses a straight apostrophe. Use the curly ’ (Butterick).
- Gaps: the gap between sections (about 30 px above each heading) is only about three times the gap between a heading and its text. That's fine, but a little more space above each heading would separate the steps more clearly once there are more of them.

**What's working.**
- **Type scale:** it's clean, with three sizes (title about 48 px, headings about 30 px, body about 17 px) and big steps between them. Each size has an obvious job. Keep exactly these three.
- **Headings sit with their text:** each one is much closer to its own paragraph than to the one above it, so the four sections group themselves without any rules or boxes. Keep that closeness when you add numbers or more text.
- **Restraint:** one typeface, one accent, no decoration. That suits a calm, practical beginner's guide. Don't add ornament to fill the empty right side.
