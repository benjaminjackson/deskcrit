**First read.** The eye goes to the bold headline, "Bring your bike in for a service", then across to the solid button on the right. Within a few seconds it reads as a local bike shop's booking page: calm and plain, and it's clear what to do. The only difference between the two versions is the button colour: teal in version 1, a saturated blue in version 2. The type, layout, copy and spacing are the same in both.

**What it's for.** My assumption: getting a local cyclist to pick a day and book a service drop-off.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job equally well, and the colour change doesn't change that. White text reads clearly on both buttons; both look well above 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). Both are the only saturated colour on the page, so each is equally clearly the one thing to press. Teal versus blue is a brand choice. Neither helps or hurts the booking. What holds both versions back is the booking card itself.

**What would move it most** (applies to both versions)
1. **You can't tell whether a date is selected (defect).** The three date rows in the right card all look the same: white fill and thin grey border, with no radio button, check mark or highlight. A visitor can't tell whether these are options to pick, whether one is already picked, or what "Book drop-off" will book. Show the rows as a single-choice list, with a visible control on each row and a strong selected state.
2. **The service isn't part of the booking (defect).** The prices on the left ($25 / $70 / $160) aren't linked to the form on the right. The visitor books a day without saying which service they want, so they don't know what they're committing to, and neither does the shop. Either make the three services selectable as the first step of the form, or say on the card that the service is agreed at drop-off.
3. **Thursday 30 October is missing with no explanation (defect).** The list jumps from Wednesday 29 to Friday 31. Visitors will wonder whether it's full, the shop is closed, or the page is broken. Show it as greyed out with "Full" or "Closed".

**Smaller things.**
- The page ends about halfway down at 1280 × 800, leaving a large empty band below. Consider adding opening hours or the address ("Find us") so the page answers the next question too.
- The prices have no line saying what each service includes. Add one short line under each.

**What's working.**
- The sub-line, "Drop it off before 10 am and collect it the same evening. We call you before doing any work that costs more than the quote", is specific. It gives the deadline and the guarantee in the customer's own terms. Keep it.
- The type scale is tight: headline, card heading, body and table rows, four clear levels.
- There's one accent colour, used only on the button, so the next step is obvious in both versions.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** What the date rows look like on hover, focus and selection; that is the first thing to check in a live browser.
