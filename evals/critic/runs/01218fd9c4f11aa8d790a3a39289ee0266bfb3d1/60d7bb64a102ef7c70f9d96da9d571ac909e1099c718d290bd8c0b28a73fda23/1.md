**First read.** The eye lands on the "Library opening hours" heading, then falls straight down a single column of evenly spaced lines. The three bold branch names barely register as section breaks. Every line sits the same distance from the next, so it reads as one list of eight things rather than three groups. It feels calm and plain, and it's slow to scan.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or notice where library users quickly check when a particular branch is open, usually for today or a specific day.

**Verdict.** It's legible but doesn't do its job well. Someone checking "is Harbour open on Monday at 5?" has to read prose, convert words to numbers, and work out the closed days on their own. The biggest problem is that the times are written as words in sentences when they should be scannable data.

**What would move it most**

1. **Times are spelled out and have no am/pm** (every hours line). This is a defect. "Nine to eight" and "ten to four" have to be converted to numbers in the reader's head, which slows every lookup. "Ten to four" can also be misread as 3:50, the clock-time sense. Hours are what people act on: a wrong reading means turning up to a locked door. Use numerals with a clear time format: "9 am – 8 pm" or "09:00–20:00", with an en dash.

2. **Closed days and mobile-library times are left unsaid** (Harbour branch, Mobile library, Sunday everywhere). This is a defect. The reader has to work out that Harbour is closed on Monday and that nothing opens on Sunday. The mobile library gives a place and a day but no hours, so nobody can plan a visit. Write "Closed" against every closed day and give the mobile library's start and end times. A page of hours has to answer every day, not only the open ones.

3. **Equal spacing breaks up the groups** (whole list, y≈143–503). This is a convention: proximity (Wertheimer; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II). The gap between "Saturday, ten to four" and "Harbour branch" is the same as the gap between two lines inside a branch. Bold weight is the only thing marking a new section, so the three branches run together. Fix: tighten the lines inside each branch and put clearly more space above each branch name, roughly double.

4. **Prose lines where a table would let people compare** (whole body). This is taste, backed by the guidance to use tables for looking up exact values. Each branch writes its days in a different shape, so there's no common column to run your eye down. Use a small table with days down the side and branches across the top, or at least day and time in two aligned columns under each branch. Then "which branch is open on Tuesday?" can be answered in one glance.

**Smaller things.**
- The content fills the left third of the frame and leaves a large empty area on the right and bottom. On a standalone notice, set it on a deliberate grid or size it up. On a web page this is probably fine.
- There's no address for either branch and no note of holiday closures. Add both if this is the only place people check hours.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight: one heading size, bold branch labels, regular lines. Three levels, each with an obvious job. Keep that.
- Contrast is high and the copy is plain and friendly with no clutter. Keep that restraint when you add structure.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How it reflows on a phone, which is likely where people check hours.
