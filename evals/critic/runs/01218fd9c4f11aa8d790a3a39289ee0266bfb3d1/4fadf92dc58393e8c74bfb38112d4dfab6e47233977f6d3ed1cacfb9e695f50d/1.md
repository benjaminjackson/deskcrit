**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a product detail page in an online shop, meant to tell a home cook what the scale does and get them to add it to their basket.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. In version 1 the description, which is the part that makes the case for buying, is right-aligned. That makes it slow to read and splits the left column into two edges. Version 2 fixes this. What's left in version 2 is small. The one real gap is the image panel, which shows no product.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **Body alignment (version 1 is broken here; version 2 fixes it). This is a defect.** In version 1 both paragraphs (lines starting "A flat glass scale…" and "It runs for about a year…") end on a straight right edge and start on a ragged left edge. At the end of each line the reader has to search for where the next one starts, because every line begins at a different point. Over eight lines of the most persuasive copy on the page, that adds up. It also breaks the column. The "Kitchen" label, the title, the price and the button all line up on a left edge at about x=68, and the body text alone leaves that edge and starts somewhere else. So the page shows two edges where it should show one. Ragged-left text suits a short caption or label, not paragraphs (Bringhurst; Butterick, *Practical Typography*, on alignment). Version 2 puts the text flush left with a ragged right edge. Every block now hangs off one shared edge, and the description reads at normal speed.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The image panel shows no product. This is probably a defect, unless it's a loading or placeholder state.** Half the page is a beige square with an empty circle in it. On a product page the photo is how a buyer checks size, finish and whether the item looks good enough to buy. With no photo, they have to rely on the text alone. Put a real product photo here, ideally one showing the scale with a bowl on it, since the copy talks about the tare button and the bowl.

**Smaller things**
- The left column ends at the button, about 500 px down. That leaves a tall empty block beside the image. Shipping or returns details, or the battery and capacity specs as a short list, could fill it with something useful, but this is a matter of taste.
- The "Kitchen" category label is tiny and brownish-gold. It looks as if it passes contrast, but it's close. Measure it against 4.5:1.

**What's working**
- The copy is specific and talks to the buyer: "up to five kilograms in steps of one gram", "without doing any sums", "stays put on a wet counter". Each line says what the scale does for the person cooking. Keep it.
- The type scale is clear: a big title, body text, a medium-bold price and a bold button label. Each size has a clear job.
- The single dark button sits right under the price, so the next step is obvious.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the empty circle in the image panel is a placeholder or a photo that hadn't loaded. Check this in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on "Add to basket".
