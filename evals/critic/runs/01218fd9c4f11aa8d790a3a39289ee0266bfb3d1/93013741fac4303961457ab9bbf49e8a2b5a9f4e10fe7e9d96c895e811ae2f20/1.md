**First read.** Both versions are identical apart from alignment. The eye lands on the serif headline "Throwing on the wheel for beginners", moves down through two short paragraphs, and stops at the beige card with $320 and "Book a place". It reads as a quiet, trustworthy course page for a small pottery studio. Version 1 reads down a single left edge. In version 2 everything is centred, so it reads more like an invitation and less like a page you study.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a booking page that should get an adult beginner to understand the course, see the price and dates, and book a place.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change is that version 2 centres the body text and the booking card, and that makes the most important reading on the page slower and loosens its structure without adding anything.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **The centred paragraphs are harder to read (defect).** In version 2 the two body paragraphs are about 75 characters per line and four lines deep. Centring gives them a ragged left edge, so after each line the eye has to hunt for where the next one starts. This text is the case for buying: what you'll learn, how sessions run, what's included. Readers of version 2 will skim it rather than read it. Bringhurst and Ruder both keep centred setting for short, symmetrical units, not running prose. Version 1's flush-left text gives a fixed return point and reads easily.
2. **Version 2 drops the single edge that held the page together (convention).** In version 1 the logo, eyebrow, headline, paragraphs, card contents and button all share one left edge at about x=88. That one strong axis (Müller-Brockmann) makes the page feel ordered with almost no effort. Version 2 leaves the logo on the left and moves everything else to the centre, so the header and content no longer relate. Inside the card, three centred lines of differing length and a centred button make a jagged stack that reads less like a list of facts.
3. **Version 2's gains are small.** Centring does fix version 1's empty right half, and the composition looks more balanced at this width. But that empty space costs the reader nothing. The weight of the headline, price and button is the same in both, so neither version finds the booking card any faster.

**Remaining findings for version 1**
1. **"Eight places" can be read two ways (defect).** It sits right above the button, where people decide. It could mean the class holds eight people, or that eight places are left. If someone reads it as "plenty left" and the class is nearly full, or reads it as "already full", they'll act on the wrong idea. Say which one: "Eight places in total" or "3 of 8 places left".
2. **"Starts 8 January" has no year (defect, minor).** The date is 23 November, so it almost certainly means next January. But this is a date the buyer will plan around and hold the studio to, so add the year.

**Smaller things.**
- Version 1's right half is empty at this width. If that bothers you, add a photo of the studio or of finished mugs and bowls. Don't centre the text to fill it. A photo would also do more to sell the course than anything else on the page.
- The headline wraps "beginners" onto a line by itself. A slightly wider measure or a manual break would balance the two lines.
- The type scale is clean: eyebrow, headline, body, card detail, plus the price. Keep it.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and speaks to the reader ("you will learn to centre clay, open it, and pull up the walls"; "You take home everything you make"). A beginner can picture the evenings, and this is what will sell the course.
- The booking card puts price, schedule, start date, capacity and the button in one place, so everything needed to decide sits next to the action.
- "Book a place" names exactly what happens next.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How either layout reflows on a phone. On a narrow screen the two versions may barely differ.

Better: version 1
