**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the big white headline, drops to the yellow "Get tickets" button, then goes back up to the yellow date line. It reads straight away as a small, considered conference for software and design people, and it feels calm and confident. In version 1 the first glance catches on the headline itself: its two lines are packed so tightly they almost touch.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a conference landing page, and its job is to tell a practitioner what Fieldwork is, when and where it happens, and get them to buy a ticket.

**Verdict.** Version 2 is better. The only real change is the headline's line spacing, plus the space below it that moves down to match. In version 1 the headline is set so tight that the descenders of line one ("y" in "days", "g" in "building") nearly hit the tops of the letters in line two ("t" in "quieter", "l" and "f" in "slower software"). The headline reads as one dense block, and its feel works against its message about "quieter, slower" software. Version 2 opens the leading to roughly 1.1 times the type size. Each line now reads cleanly and the headline looks calm, which suits what it says. Everything else is the same, and version 2 still works well.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **The CTA doesn't tell a buyer what they'd commit to.** This is a defect for the reader about to act. "Get tickets" is the ask, but there's no price, ticket type or availability anywhere on this first screen. Someone thinking about three days in Leith has to click before they learn whether this is £90 or £900. Put one line of terms next to or under the button (for example "From £X · early bird until DATE"). Then the click is a decision the visitor has already made, not a step to find out.

**Smaller things.**
- The date line: "14 to 16 April" has no year. "2027" only appears in the small wordmark at top left. Put the year in the date line so it can be read on its own.
- The bottom third of the screen and everything right of the headline are empty navy. That's fine as restraint, but the screen gives no sign that there's more below (speakers, programme). A glimpse of the next section above the fold would pull people down the page. This one is taste.
- The navigation (Programme · Speakers · Venue · Tickets) is small, pale text separated by dots. It looks like it's close to the 4.5:1 contrast minimum, and the gap between links is tight for tapping. Check the contrast and the click targets.

**What's working.**
- The headline copy. "Three days on building quieter, slower software" says how long the event is, what it covers and its point of view in one line. It couldn't sit on just any conference's page. Keep it.
- The type scale is clear: a dominant headline, a medium subhead, and small bold yellow for the date line and button. There are no sizes that are only slightly different from each other.
- Yellow is used sparingly. It appears only on the date line and the button, so it ties "when and where" to "act" and the button has nothing competing with it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button and nav, and how the headline wraps on narrow screens. The tight leading in version 1 would look worse when the headline wraps to three or four lines on mobile.

Better: version 2
