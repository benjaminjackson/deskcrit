**First read.** My eye goes to the big blue headline, then gets pulled down into the four blue-outlined cards. They outweigh everything else on the page, so I never settle on the "Create your link" button. It reads as a scheduling tool for small businesses. It feels clean, tidy and anonymous: put a different name in the top left and it could be any of a dozen booking products.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the landing page for a booking-link product. It should get small appointment businesses (dentists, tutors, physios, vets, judging by the names at the bottom) to create a link or look at a live example.

**Verdict.** It is competently built and does not fail outright, but it doesn't make anyone want to act. One blue does every job on the page, so the main button doesn't stand out. The right half of the opening screen is empty where the product should be shown. The headline could belong to any competitor.

**What would move it most**

1. **One blue does every job, so the main button has nothing to stand out against.** The same saturated blue is used for the headline, the nav links, the card titles, the icons, the badges, the 2px card borders, the "Learn more" links, the divider line and both buttons. The viewer can't tell what is clickable from what is decoration. The blue card titles look like links. The four heavy blue boxes together outweigh the "Create your link" button, so the eye goes to the cards instead of the ask. Fix: keep the blue for things you can click, with the filled button as the strongest of them. Set the headline and card titles in a dark neutral. Make the card borders light grey, or drop them and let spacing hold the grid. Make the icons quieter. *Convention: accent colour kept for the main action; one strong action per view (Refactoring UI; Material Design). Defect: the cost is a weaker conversion path.*

2. **The opening screen describes the product but doesn't show it.** Everything from about x=680 to the right edge, above the cards, is empty. The space looks left over rather than placed on purpose. For a tool whose whole value is "people pick a time that works", the viewer never sees the booking page their customers would use. "See a live example" is the best ask on the page because it lets the viewer judge for themselves, but it sits there as a small text link. Fix: put a real booking page in that space: a clinic's name, a week of open times, one slot selected. Then the viewer compares it with how they book appointments today (phone calls, email back-and-forth) instead of taking the headline on trust. *Defect: nothing on the page backs up the claim.*

3. **The headline could be anyone's, and the copy speaks to the wrong reader.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "keep every meeting in the right place" could sit on any competitor's page, so the viewer can't tell what is different here. The words are also aimed at office workers ("meeting", "team", "the call"), while the customer names point to clinics and tutors, who think in appointments and patients. "Fill your calendar" is the one claim with a promise in it, but it is left vague: fill it with what? Fix: say who it's for and land the result on them. For example, put the no-show reduction ("cut down on no-shows"), currently hidden in the third card, in the headline, in that business's own words. *Taste on the wording; the audience mismatch is a defect.*

4. **The customer names do nothing.** Along the bottom, five business names are set in small grey type with no label, no logos and no context. They read as unexplained grey text rather than proof. Fix: label the strip ("Booking for 2,000 clinics and tutors" or similar, with a real figure), or replace it with one specific customer result. *Defect: credibility you could have had is lost.*

**Smaller things.**
- Badges: "New" and "Beta" give a feature's status, while "Auto" and "Email" describe how it works. They look identical but mean different things. Keep badges for status only and drop "Auto" and "Email".
- Card 1: the "New" badge forces "Shared calendars" onto two lines. That pushes its text and "Learn more" about 30px below the other three cards and breaks the shared lines across the row. Put the badge on its own line or shorten the title.
- "Fill your / calendar" breaks so that "calendar" sits alone on the second line. Widen the headline box or rebreak it as "Booking links that / fill your calendar".
- Four identical "Learn more" links look like a template and say nothing on their own. Make the whole card the link, or name the destination ("How reminders work").
- The nav button says "Start free" and the main button says "Create your link". If they do the same thing, use one label; "Create your link" is the better one.
- The blue rule above the customer names is a line where space would do. Use a light grey rule or none.
- The grey customer names look small and light. Check them for 4.5:1 contrast.

**What's working.**
- The card body copy is the best writing on the page. "So nobody has to do the math" and "so the first minutes are useful" end on what the reader gets. Keep that voice and use it in the headline.
- "Create your link" names one concrete first step, and "See a live example" invites the viewer to judge for themselves. That pairing of asks is right; it only needs better placement and weighting.
- The type has a clear, small scale (headline, subhead, card title, body), and the four cards share one even gap on a clean grid.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Where "See a live example" goes, and whether it opens a real, working booking page. That decides whether point 2 is a layout fix or a bigger gap in the product story.
