**First read.** The eye goes straight to the huge black headline, which is right. Then it stalls inside it: the letters are packed so tight that "ALL ABOARD" and "MORNING" turn into dark slabs, and you read them a second time to be sure of the words. After that the eye drops to the two-line sentence and stops on a lot of white. It feels confident and a bit Swiss. It also feels unfinished, because it tells you nothing about when this happens.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a poster or the top of a landing page for a public harbour open day, and its job is to get people to turn up for a free ferry ride.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline's letters run into each other and hurt its legibility, and the piece never says when the open day is. Without a date, nobody can act on it.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's no date, time or place a stranger could act on.** It says "Open Day" and "every half hour from the north landing" but never gives the day, the hours, or which harbour's north landing. Anyone who isn't already local and already informed can't turn up, and "every half hour" has no start or end. Since people will plan around these facts, leaving them out is a **defect**, not a style note. Fix: add a clear line for date and hours, for example "Saturday 14 June, 9am–1pm", and make it the second-strongest thing on the page after the headline. There's plenty of room in the empty lower half. Also say where the north landing is, either with an address or with something people already know nearby.

2. **The headline's letters collide.** The two Ls in "ALL" touch. R and D in "ABOARD" merge. In "MORNING" the N–I–N stems fuse into a picket fence that reads closer to "MORNIIING". E–R–R–Y in "FERRY" butt together. The viewer gets dark blotches where letters should be, and they have to decode words that should land at once. Large capitals should be spaced open, not closed up. Bringhurst §2.1.6 says to letterspace strings of capitals, and Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 30) says "individual letters should not touch… a small hole between two letters is less bad than letters that collide." This is a **convention** broken at a cost, so it's a **defect** here. Fix: loosen the tracking until every letter pair has a visible gap. Zero tracking or slightly negative is probably about right at this size. Keep the weight and size, because they're doing the work.

**Smaller things.**
- The "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" label at top left has the same problem at small size: tight grey capitals that read as a smudge. Letterspace it, about 5–10% of its size (Bringhurst §2.1.6).
- The body sentence states facts and stops. Ending on the viewer would make it land: "Free rides every half hour, so just turn up. Bring a hat and a thermos, it's cold on the water." This one is **taste**.
- There's no ask or next step. If this is a web page, a single specific action such as "See the timetable" or "Add to calendar" belongs under the body text.

**What's working.**
- Hierarchy is clear: three sizes with big steps between them (label, headline, body), so each size has an obvious job. Keep that scale.
- "All aboard the morning ferry" is warm, concrete and specific to this event. It's not generic copy.
- The body text measure is comfortable, around 60 characters per line, and it hangs on the same left edge as everything above it. That strong left alignment is why the page feels calm and not empty.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether this is a full poster or the top of a longer page. If the date and location appear further down, item 1 becomes "move them up", but they still belong in the first screen.
