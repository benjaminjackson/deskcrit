After that it drops back to "When the boats run". The page reads as a calm, tidy passenger guide for a small local ferry. It feels trustworthy and plain, but it is a little sparse.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an info page for passengers on one ferry route. Its main job is to tell people when they can cross and what they can bring, so they turn up at the right time.

**Verdict.** It is clean and readable, but it fails at its main job: a passenger can't plan a trip from it, because the page never gives a time. The second problem is that the sidebar card outranks the main content, so what matters least looks most important.

**What would move it most**

1. **"When the boats run" gives no times (defect).** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "in winter… one boat each hour" and "the last crossing leaves before dusk" are the lines a passenger acts on. All three are vague.
   - Forty minutes past what? What time is "early morning"? When does winter start? Which clock time is "before dusk" this week?
   - A reader trying to catch the last boat has to guess, and a wrong guess leaves them stranded on the wrong bank.
   - **Fix:** state the first and last departure from each landing, the summer and winter dates, and the minutes past the hour. For example: "From north landing at 6:20, 7:00, 7:40… last boat 21:40. 1 Nov–28 Feb: hourly on the hour, last boat 16:00." If times change too often to print here, say so, and make the timetable link the main thing in this section, not something parked in the sidebar.

2. **The sidebar outranks the content (defect).** In the "Good to know" card, the heading looks about 36px against about 28px for "When the boats run" and "Bicycles and luggage". The card's body text looks about 20px against about 18px in the main column.
   - So a note about where printed timetables are kept pulls the eye ahead of the actual schedule, and the reader reads the page in the wrong order.
   - **Fix:** make the card heading no bigger than the section headings and its text no bigger than the body text, or smaller. Let the border and position mark it as an aside, not its size. Refactoring UI makes the same point: secondary content gets less emphasis, not more.

3. **The type scale is muddled (convention, Bringhurst).** I count about seven sizes: roughly 64, 36, 28, 24, 20, 18 and 16. The pairs 18/20 and 24/28 are too close to read as different levels, so they look like the sizes were picked one at a time.
   - **Fix:** a tighter scale would keep four: headline, one heading size (shared by the sections and the card), lead text, and body. Nav and button text can sit at body size or just under it, and differ by weight.

4. **The card's words don't connect to its button (defect, minor but on the action).** The text talks about *printed* timetables in the landing shelters, then the button says "See the timetable". It's unclear whether that button opens the printed one, an online one, or the noticeboard's changes.
   - "Good to know" is also a label that could sit on any site.
   - **Fix:** give the card one job and a heading that says it, e.g. "Timetable changes this week". Point the button at exactly that, e.g. "See this week's changes".

**Smaller things.**
- **Place names:** "north landing" and "market quay" are in lower case, so they read as descriptions rather than places. Capitalise them if those are their names.
- **Card edges:** the card's right edge (about 1150px) stops short of the nav's right edge (about 1184px), and its top doesn't line up with the headline's cap height. Snap both to the same lines as the nav and headline.
- **Nav state:** none of Timetable, Fares or Landings shows a current page. If this guide belongs under one of them, mark it.
- **Duplicate timetable links:** the card button repeats the nav's "Timetable" link. That's fine once the card's job is clearer (finding 4), but keep the two labels consistent.

**What's working.**
- The left column reads comfortably. Lines run about 65–70 characters and the line spacing is generous, so the body text is easy to follow.
- The bicycles paragraph is good copy. It says who does what and in what order ("Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"), so a cyclist knows exactly what to do. Write the timetable section to the same standard.
- The restraint suits a public-service page: one accent blue used only for the action, plenty of white space, and nothing decorative.
