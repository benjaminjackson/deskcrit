**First read.** The eye lands on the huge four-line headline and stays there a long time. It's set at display size but reads like a full sentence. Next comes the paragraph, which holds the only facts on the page, and then nothing. It reads as a ferry timetable page, maybe for locals or visitors. It feels calm and confident, but it's slow to get to the point.

**What it's for.** My assumption is that this is the top of a web page for a seasonal river ferry, and a rider comes here to find out when they can catch a boat.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The label says "Spring timetable", but there's no timetable. Most of the space goes to a headline that names a topic, while the facts a rider acts on are either in small body text or missing.

**What would move it most**

1. **The page promises times and gives none (defect).** The eyebrow says "Spring timetable" and the headline says "by daylight, spring through early summer". There's no first or last sailing, no start or end date, and no list of departures. A rider can't tell whether there's a boat at 7:10 tomorrow, or whether the service is even running yet. "By daylight" and "early summer" make them guess, and their guess may differ from yours. They may plan around a 9 pm sailing in June that doesn't exist. Put the actual numbers on this screen: the date range ("22 March – 30 June"), the first and last departure, and the departure times or "every 40 min from 07:00". If the table sits lower on the page, show at least the first and last times here so the top of the page answers the question it raises.

2. **The biggest type carries the least useful words (defect, plus taste on the fix).** The headline takes about four lines at what looks like 80px and fills the top half of the frame. It names the subject at length ("Quayside ferry crossings by daylight, spring through early summer"). "Every forty minutes" and "twelve minutes" are the two numbers riders will remember, and they're in body text below. The size order runs opposite to what matters. Cut the headline to a name plus the key fact, such as "Quayside ferry — every 40 minutes". That brings it down to one or two lines. Then let the dates and hours take the next level instead of being buried in a sentence. This follows Refactoring UI's point that hierarchy should follow importance, not word count.

3. **The paragraph leaves riders to fill gaps (defect).** "Runs from the north landing to the market quay every forty minutes" doesn't say whether it also runs back, or whether forty minutes means departures from each side or a round trip. "About twelve minutes in calm weather" raises the question of what happens when the weather isn't calm, and doesn't answer it. These are departure facts riders will hold you to, so each one that can be read two ways is a missed boat. Name both directions, say what the interval applies to, and either say what changes in bad weather or point to where they can check.

**Smaller things**
- The headline breaks leave "summer" alone on line four. Rebreak so the lines are more even, or just shorten it as in point 2.
- "weather." sits alone on the last line of the paragraph. Adjust the measure slightly or rewrite so the last line carries more than one word.
- The eyebrow "Spring timetable" looks about 14px in grey, and it's the closest thing on the page to the real label. It looks legible enough, but check its contrast hits 4.5:1.

**What's working**
- The type scale is tight: three sizes (eyebrow, display, body) with big steps between them, one family, and contrast from both weight and size. Keep that discipline when you add times and dates.
- The headline is spaced well for its size: tight leading and tracking, so the lines hold together as one block.
- The paragraph's line length looks right, about 60–65 characters with comfortable leading, and everything hangs off one clean left edge.
