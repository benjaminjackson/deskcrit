**Verdict.** The page is tidy and easy to read, but its one action is broken. The form promises to "send one short note" and never asks where to send it. After that, the article buries its most useful news and the figure doesn't show what the text claims.

**What would move it most**

1. **The form has no email or phone field** (right-hand card). It asks only for "Your name" and "Home landing", then says "We send one short note when the timetable changes." A stranger reading it will stop and ask: send it where? Some won't sign up because the form doesn't make sense. Others will sign up and get nothing. This is a **defect**: it's the promise the viewer will hold you to, and the form can't keep it. Add the contact field the note goes to (email, or phone for a text) and put it first. "Your name" probably isn't needed at all, so drop it unless you use it.

2. **The figure's blue bar looks like a second Sign up button** (figure, right of centre). It's the same saturated blue and about the same rounded rectangle as the button. Nothing else in the drawing has that colour or treatment, so it reads as clickable, or as a highlight that means something. It's the one strong colour on the page, and it ends up split between the action and a picture. This is a **convention** (keep the accent colour for interactive things; Refactoring UI and Material both say so), and breaking it here costs attention. If the bar is the boat, draw it in the illustration's muted blue-greys, or give it a hull shape so it reads as a boat and not a control.

3. **The best news is at the bottom, and the figure doesn't show it** (last paragraph, below the figure). "The ramps now meet the deck at every tide, so prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" is the line that changes someone's journey. It's set below the caption, after benches and a tea kiosk, as an afterthought. The caption points to low tide, but the drawing shows no ramp meeting a deck, so the picture and the claim don't support each other. This is **taste**, with a real cost: the readers who most need step-free access may stop reading before they reach it. Lead the intro with the step-free boarding. Then either draw the ramp level with the deck at low tide, or caption the figure with exactly that.

4. **"Home landing" is a free-text box** (second field). A newcomer won't know if it means where they board, where they live, or a named stop. People will type spellings the system can't match. This is a **defect** (Nielsen 5, error prevention; Nielsen 2, the user's own words). Use a select list of the landings, labelled in passenger terms, for example "Landing you usually leave from". It also tells the reader why the field is there: so they only get changes for their route.

**What's working**

- Labels sit above their fields, close to them, and stay visible. The note under the button ("one short note… stop at any time") is honest and settles a worry before it comes up. Keep that tone.
- The body copy is concrete (benches facing the water, tea and bread rolls while the boat loads), so it sounds like a place, not a template.
