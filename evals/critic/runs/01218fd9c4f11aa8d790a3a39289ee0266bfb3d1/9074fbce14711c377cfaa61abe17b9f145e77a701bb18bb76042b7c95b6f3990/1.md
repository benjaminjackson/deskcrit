The only thing that catches is the headline, which looks like three separate lines stacked with gaps between them, not one block.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the opening screen of a long-read environment feature on the web. Its job is to make a general reader commit to nine minutes.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the type is restrained and readable, and the opening copy pulls you in. The one thing worth fixing is the headline's line spacing, which is loose enough that the headline comes apart.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's line spacing is too open, so it reads as three lines, not one statement.** The gaps between "their / fields" and "the / birds" look about as tall as the letters themselves. By eye the spacing is roughly 1.4 to 1.5 times the type size, which is body-text spacing used on display type. Each line arrives on its own, and the break after "and the" leaves the reader waiting a beat for the payoff. Bringhurst shows display lines can be set much tighter, at or even below their type size, as long as ascenders and descenders stay clear (*Elements of Typographic Style*, p. 37). The specific claim that a heading needs tighter spacing than text is common sense rather than a sourced rule: big type has big gaps between its lines, and the eye reads that gap as a pause. Fix: tighten the headline to roughly 1.1 to 1.15 of its size, and check that the descenders of "p" and "y" in "purpose," don't touch the "b" and "h" below. *Taste, argued.*

**Smaller things.**
- Headline copy: "and the birds that came back" is the hook, but it gets the weakest spot, the tail of the last line after a comma. That's acceptable for a feature headline, but a line break that ends a line on "the" is avoidable. If the column width can't be changed, a manual break before "and the birds" would keep the payoff together.
- The gap between the two body paragraphs looks like about half a line. It's fine, but it's a touch tight next to the generous space everywhere else. Check that it matches the spacing used further down the article.
- The byline and read time are small grey sans. They look legible, but measure the grey against white to confirm it clears 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clean:** four levels (kicker, headline, meta, body) with big steps between them and no near-duplicate sizes. Keep it.
- **The body column is the right width**, at roughly 65 to 70 characters a line, in a serif at a comfortable reading size, all hanging from the same left edge as the headline. That edge is what makes the page feel ordered.
- **The opening copy does the work a picture would.** "A hand's depth of water" and "the air is loud with lapwings" put the reader in the place. "Five years ago these fields grew wheat… the farm is paid to keep them wet" lands the story's turn in two sentences. Don't let an editor trim the concrete details.
