**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web or print notice that tells locals what the festival is, when and where each event happens, and that volunteers are needed.

**Verdict.** The type is pleasant, but the piece doesn't do its job. Every fact someone would act on (the weekend, the times, the places, the volunteer request) is buried mid-sentence in three paragraphs that look the same. The half-line drop in the middle column makes an otherwise tidy page look like an accident.

**What would move it most**

1. **Nothing tells you when or where to go without reading every word** *(defect)*. The dates ("first weekend of September", with no year or day numbers) are halfway through the first paragraph. "Saturday night… at dusk" and "Sunday afternoon… by one o'clock… at four" are scattered through columns 2 and 3. The three columns have no headings, so someone scanning for "when's the lantern walk?" has to read all three. Below the title the page has only one level, which is body text. Text that people skim needs headings and short chunks. Fix: give each column a short heading that names the event ("Lantern walk", "Pie contest"). Pull the key facts out as a line of their own above or below the paragraph, set in bold or a different colour rather than a new size: day, time, place. Put the festival dates with real day numbers right under the title.

2. **The middle column sits half a line lower than its neighbours** *(convention: Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid")*. Columns 1 and 3 start on the same line. Column 2 starts about 14 px lower, which looks like exactly half the 28 px line spacing. That puts every line in it level with the gaps between lines next door. Being nearly aligned is worse than being clearly offset: it reads as a bug, and it makes the three blocks look unfinished. Fix: start all three columns on the same first line, so the lines form straight rows across the gaps.

3. **The volunteer request goes nowhere** *(defect)*. "Volunteers are still needed…" is the only thing the page asks of the reader, and it's tucked in at the end of the first paragraph with no name, link, phone number or place to sign up. As written, a willing reader can't act on it. Fix: make it a separate short block at the end with one exact action, such as "Sign up at the town hall desk by 25 August" or a link. The seagull line is charming, so keep it.

**Smaller things.**
- Column dividers: the rules aren't centred in their gaps. The text on the left of each rule sits about 20–27 px from it, and the text on the right about 11 px, so columns 2 and 3 look crammed against their rules. Centre the rules, or drop them and widen the gaps, which would do the job on their own (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*).
- The lantern walk "leaves the town hall at dusk": a clock time would help, because dusk in early September varies and families plan around times.
- The title: "festival" in lower case is fine if it's house style, but "Harbour Lights Festival" reads more as a proper name.

**What's working.**
- The serif face, size and line spacing make the body text genuinely comfortable to read. Column measures look like about 40–45 characters, which suits three columns. Keep this.
- There's real weight contrast between the bold title and the regular body, so the title owns the page without being oversized.
- The copy has a voice ("the pie that makes the judges laugh", "hungry seagulls") that sounds like a real town and not a template. Keep that tone when you add the hard facts.
