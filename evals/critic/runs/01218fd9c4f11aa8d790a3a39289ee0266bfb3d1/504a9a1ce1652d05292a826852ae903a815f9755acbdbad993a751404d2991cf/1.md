**First read.** The eye goes to "Walking routes" at the top left, then across the three bold card titles, then down to the three blue buttons. Then it drops into the empty bottom half of each card and finds nothing there. It reads as a tidy, plain route picker for a local walking site. It feels calm but unfinished, as if something was meant to sit under each button and never loaded.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page where a casual walker chooses one of three local routes and opens its details.

**Verdict.** The type and spacing are clean and the cards are easy to scan, but the page doesn't yet help anyone choose. The facts that decide a walk are hidden in sentences. And each card's empty lower half makes the page look broken.

**What would move it most**

1. **Empty lower half of every card (defect).** Each card's content ends at the "View route" button, about 40% of the way down. Below that, roughly 200 px of white sits inside the border. Space inside a box doesn't read as breathing room. It reads as a slot waiting for content, so viewers assume a photo or map failed to load, and the page looks unfinished. Empty space should be shaped on purpose, and large unexplained empty areas are a fault (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Ruder makes the related point that space works when it is gathered into areas set against dense ones (*Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). Fix: either fit the card height to its content and let the empty space gather outside the cards, or fill the space with what the card is missing, such as a map thumbnail or photo of each route. A photo would also give each card something that tells it apart at a glance.

2. **The deciding facts are buried in prose and listed in a different order on each card (defect).** A walker picking a route wants to compare distance, how hard it is, and the surface. Here distance is spelled out ("Six kilometres", "Four", "Ten") inside a sentence, so nothing on the page shows the numbers next to each other. The other facts don't match up either. Cliff path gives climbs and a cafe, Woodland loop gives flatness and benches, and Canal towpath gives surface and bicycles. Is the cliff path paved? Is the towpath flat? You can't tell, so you can't compare the three, and you have to read every card in full and still guess. Repeated elements should follow one program (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*), and pages people skim should be broken into scannable pieces (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Fix: give every card the same short line of facts above the description, in the same order, such as "6 km · 2 steep climbs · unpaved". Write the distance as a figure. Then keep the sentence for the character of the walk: the coast, the beech trees, the water.

**Smaller things**
- "Walking routes" could sit on any site. Add where these walks are (town or area) so visitors know they're in the right place.
- "View route" appears three times with the same label. That's fine on screen, but for screen readers each link should name its route ("View Cliff path route"). Better still, make the whole card clickable so the target is bigger.
- The descriptions break at awkward points: "far / end." leaves one word alone on the last line. Loosen the card width or reword so the last line carries more than one word.

**What's working**
- The type scale is tight and clear: page heading, card title, body, and button label are four sizes with obvious steps between them, plus weight for emphasis. Keep it.
- The descriptions are concrete and speak to a walker ("a cafe at the far end", "benches every few hundred metres"). That is the right voice. Keep it and add the facts beside it instead of rewriting it.
- One accent colour, used only for the action. The blue buttons are the only colour on the page, so it's obvious what to click.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the empty card space is a fixed card height or an image or map that didn't load in the capture. Check in a live browser before fixing. If it is a missing image, finding 1 becomes a loading bug rather than a layout problem.
- How the three columns reflow on a phone, and hover and focus states on the cards and buttons.
