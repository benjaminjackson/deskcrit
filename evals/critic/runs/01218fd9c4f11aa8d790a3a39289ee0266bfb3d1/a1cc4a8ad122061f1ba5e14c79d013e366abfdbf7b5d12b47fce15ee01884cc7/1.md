**First read.** The eye goes straight to "How can we help?", the white serif headline on the navy band, then drops to the search field, then down into the 4×2 grid of topic cards. It reads as a standard help-centre home for a SaaS product. It feels calm, tidy, trustworthy and quite anonymous.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a support landing page for existing customers who have a specific problem and want to reach the right answer in one or two moves, by searching or by picking a topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the grid is disciplined and nothing gets in the way. What holds it back is that both ways in ask the user to do the work. The search field barely looks like search, and the eight topics overlap, so a user with a real problem has to guess which box it lives in.

**What would move it most**

1. **The search field doesn't look like the main action.** The white bar in the hero has no magnifier icon, no submit button and no visible label. Its only cue is the grey placeholder "Search for articles", which disappears as soon as someone types. On a help centre, search is the main thing people do. Here it reads as a plain white rectangle, nearly the same weight as the subhead above it. Users look for the magnifier and a button by habit (Jakob's law, Nielsen/NN/g), and a field with only a placeholder breaks Nielsen's rule on placeholder-only labels. *Convention, with a real cost.* Direction: add a magnifier icon inside the field, and a button or clear submit affordance. Make the field a little taller so it clearly outranks everything except the headline. Swap the generic placeholder for a real query in the user's words, such as "e.g. reset two-step sign-in", which also shows what kind of questions get answered here.

2. **The topics overlap, so picking one is a guess.** "Fix sync problems" under Troubleshooting overlaps with "Connect calendars, chat tools, and storage" under Integrations. "Two-step sign-in" under Account overlaps with Privacy and security. "Troubleshooting" is a catch-all that could cover half the grid. Someone whose calendar has stopped syncing has two equally good cards to choose from, and if they pick wrong they come back and try again. This is the conceptual-model problem Norman describes: the categories follow the docs team's folders, not the user's problems. *Defect.* Direction: make the cards not overlap, so each problem obviously has one home. Then add a short "Common questions" or "Top articles" list near the grid so the most frequent problems are one click away and nobody has to sort them into a category first.

**Smaller things.**
- Orders and shipping ("Track a delivery or start a return") sits next to workspaces, teams and integrations. A stranger can't tell whether Harbor is software, a store or both. If it really is both, a single line in the hero saying what Harbor is would settle it.
- The subhead "Browse a topic or search the guides" lists the two options in the opposite order to the page, which puts search first. Swap them, or cut the line, since the page already shows both options.
- Most card descriptions end on a single word alone on the last line ("your team.", "recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where."). Tighten the wording or widen the text box so they fit on one line or break more evenly.
- Type scale: the logo (~20px), "Browse by topic" (~18px) and the card titles (~16px) are all bold and only a couple of pixels apart. The section heading hardly outranks the cards it introduces. Make it clearly bigger, or set it smaller in a lighter colour as a label.
- "How we protect and store your data" is the only description written about the company rather than a task the user does. Rewrite it as a task, e.g. "Export or delete your data, manage who has access."
- The top nav doesn't show which section is current. That matters once people move on to Guides or Community.

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** The logo, "Browse by topic" and the card column all share the same left margin. The search field's edges line up almost exactly with the inner card columns, so the centred hero still belongs to the grid below it. Keep this when you change the search field.
- **The type pairing.** The serif headline against the sans everywhere else is a clear contrast, not a clash, and it gives an otherwise templated page some warmth.
- **The icons.** They're all simple line drawings with the same stroke weight. The cards read as one set, and each icon actually matches its topic.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the search box suggests results as you type, which matters more than anything else on this page. Hover and focus states on the cards and the field. What sits below the fold, such as popular articles or a contact path. Check these in a live browser.
