**First read.** The eye lands on "How can we help?" and drops straight to the white search bar, which is the brightest shape on the navy band. Then it moves down to a grid of eight identical cards and starts scanning them one by one, with nothing to stop on. It reads as a standard SaaS help center: calm, tidy and trustworthy, but it could belong to any company.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the landing page of a product's help center. Someone arrives with one specific problem and needs to reach the right article fast, either by searching or by recognising their problem in a list.

**Verdict.** It's clean, well built and won't confuse anyone. But it only offers two routes, a bare search box and eight equal categories that overlap. A visitor with a specific problem has to guess which bucket it's in, and the page never shows them their problem in words they would use themselves.

**What would move it most**

1. **Every route goes through a category guess (defect).** The eight cards in the lower two-thirds are all the same size and weight, and several of them overlap:
   - "two-step sign-in" is under Account, but someone worried about security will open Privacy and security.
   - "sync problems" is under Troubleshooting, but a calendar that won't sync is just as plausibly an Integrations problem.
   - Troubleshooting is by definition a catch-all for every other card.

   So the visitor reads all eight descriptions, picks one, and may pick wrong. Nothing on the page names an actual task like "Reset your password", "Download an invoice" or "Start a return". That breaks recognition over recall (Nielsen 6) and makes people think about where things live (Krug). The fix is to put the five or six most-asked articles as plain links directly under the search bar or above the grid, phrased as the visitor would say them. Or replace each card's description with two or three real article titles, so a card shows what's inside it instead of naming a category. Base the choice on your top search terms and tickets, not on how the team is organised.

2. **The search bar doesn't look like search or show what to type (convention).** The white field in the hero has no magnifier icon and no button, so it reads as a white bar more than an input. "Search for articles" describes the system's content, not the visitor's problem. Search is the main thing this page offers, so it should signal that at once (Jakob's law: a magnifier is the expected cue). Add the icon, and use the placeholder to show the kind of query that works, such as *Try "change my plan" or "refund"*. A placeholder is not a label, and placeholder text still needs 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.3). Check the grey against white.

**Smaller things**
- **Type scale:** I count about seven sizes (~34 headline, ~20 logo, ~18 "Browse by topic", ~16 card titles and subtitle, ~15 nav, ~14 descriptions). "Browse by topic" at ~18 barely stands above the ~16 bold card titles. Either make it clearly bigger or drop it to a small label and let space do the job (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- **Card descriptions are smaller than the nav links** (~14 vs ~15) and set in mid-grey. Bring them up to at least the nav size, and measure contrast against white for 4.5:1.
- **One-word last lines in six of the eight descriptions:** "your team.", "recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages.". Rewrite or rebalance so each wraps cleanly or fits on one line.
- **Repeated copy:** the hero subtitle "Browse a topic or search the guides" just restates the two elements below it and repeats "Browse by topic". Cut it, or use it to say what Harbor is or how to reach a person.
- **What Harbor is stays unclear:** "Set up your workspace and invite your team" suggests team software, while "Track a delivery or start a return" suggests a shop. A stranger can't be sure this is the help center for their product. If both are real, say so in one line.
- **Icons float:** in each card the gap between the icon and the title (~35–40 px) is much bigger than the gap from title to description, so the icon sits apart from its label. Tighten it so the icon, title and description read as one unit.
- **"Contact us" is the last of four equal-weight nav links.** If cutting ticket volume is the goal, that's fine. If not, frustrated visitors will look for it lower on the page, so check what's below the fold.

**What's working**
- **Hero:** the serif "How can we help?" on navy is the one moment of character, and it gives the page a clear starting point. Keep that contrast between the one serif line and everything else in sans.
- **Icon set:** one stroke weight, one level of detail, one colour. The cards hold together as a family; keep it that way when you add or rewrite cards.
- **Grid:** a strict 4×2 grid with even gutters, and the card edges line up with the logo and nav margins. The page looks orderly, and none of these fixes should break that.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cards show they're clickable on hover and focus. At rest they rely on the white card on a grey ground and nothing else.
- What's below the fold: the capture ends on an empty grey band.
- How the 4-column grid reflows at narrow widths.
