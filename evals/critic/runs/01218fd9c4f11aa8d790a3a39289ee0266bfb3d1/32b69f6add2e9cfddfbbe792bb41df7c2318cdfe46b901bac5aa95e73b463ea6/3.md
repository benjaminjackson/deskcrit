**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers in the top row and reads them left to right. Then it drops to the chart, which takes up most of the screen. That's where it stalls: three coloured lines rise gently, but there's no legend, no axis values and no dates, so there is nothing to read off them. It looks calm, clean and competent, like a stock analytics template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal marketing or growth dashboard, where someone checks traffic and sign-up performance for the last 12 weeks and decides which channel needs attention.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The chart takes up about 60% of the screen and tells the viewer nothing except "three things went up a bit". The conversion-rate tile also looks like it reports a rise that the other two tiles say is a fall.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart can't be read.** In "Visits by channel" the three lines (blue, orange, green) have no legend or direct labels. There are no y-axis values and no x-axis dates. The viewer can't tell which channel is which, how many visits a line stands for, or which week a dip happened in, such as the blue dip about two-thirds along. A chart called "by channel" that doesn't name its channels fails at the one question it was built to answer. **Defect** (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2, on clear labelling; Few). Fix:
   - Put the channel name at the right end of each line (direct labels, so the viewer doesn't have to match colours to a legend).
   - Add a few y-axis values with a unit, and week or date labels along the bottom.
   - There's an empty band of about 70 px under the baseline where the dates should go.

2. **The conversion-rate delta looks wrong, and it's shown as good news.** The tiles say visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups rose 2.4%. If sign-ups grew more slowly than visits, conversion must have *fallen*. Working back from the tiles: about 3,820 sign-ups from about 173,600 visits in the previous period gives roughly 2.20%, against 2.12% now. That's about −0.08 pts, but the tile shows "+0.1 pts" in green. This is the number a growth team acts on, and right now it tells them all is well when the funnel is getting leakier. **Defect.** Check the calculation behind the tile. Show the delta with its true sign, and to two decimals so it matches the 2.12% it qualifies. Only use green for changes that are actually good.

3. **Nothing says what matters this period.** The title "Visits by channel" and the heading "Overview" only name topics. Even with labels, the viewer still has to work out the story: which channel is growing, and whether the conversion drop is real. **Taste** (common sense for a dashboard someone checks weekly). Fix: let the chart title or a one-line caption state the finding, e.g. "Organic drove most of the growth; paid flat since week 6".

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: "Overview" (~24 px) and the KPI figures (~28 px) are too close to read as different levels. Either make the figures clearly bigger or the page title smaller. Chart title and brand name share a size, which is fine.
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is plain grey text. If it's the date-range control, it needs a button or dropdown look (chevron, border). If it isn't, it's a missed chance to show the actual dates (e.g. "1 Sep – 23 Nov").
- The small green delta lines (~13 px) look close to the 4.5:1 contrast limit (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure them.
- The lines are told apart by hue alone. Blue and green should survive most colour-blindness, but direct labels (finding 1) fix this anyway.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with a period and a comparison ("vs previous 12 weeks"), so no number stands alone. Keep that pattern (Few).
- The chart's gridlines are faint and there's no chart decoration, so the data lines are the darkest marks in the panel. Keep that restraint when you add labels.
- The grid is clean: the three tiles and the chart share the same outer edges and gutters, so the screen reads as one ordered unit.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The axis labels and legend might be missing because they hadn't finished loading when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser whether they ever render. I also couldn't see whether negative deltas turn red, or what hovering on the chart shows.
