**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers along the top: 184,220, 3,912, 2.12%. They read cleanly, and the green deltas say "things are up." Then it drops to the large chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen. There it finds three coloured lines with no names, no values and no dates, and stops. It feels calm, tidy and competent, until you try to learn anything from the chart.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an analytics overview for a marketing or growth team, answering "how is traffic doing and where is it coming from" over the last 12 weeks.

**Verdict.** The top half works and the bottom half doesn't. "Visits by channel" is the biggest thing on the page and can't be read: you can't tell which line is which channel, how many visits any of them is, or when anything happened. The page's main answer to "where is traffic coming from" is missing.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart doesn't say which line is which channel** (defect). There's no legend and no labels on the blue, orange and green lines. "Visits by channel" promises a breakdown, but the viewer can only see that three unnamed things went up. Colour is the only thing telling the series apart, and even that leads nowhere (WCAG 1.4.1, use of colour; Wilke and Few on labelling series directly). Fix: put the channel name, and ideally its latest value, at the right-hand end of each line, where there's already room before the card edge. Then no lookup is needed and the colour stops being the only cue.

2. **No axis values, no units, no dates** (defect). The y-axis has five gridlines but no numbers. The x-axis has 12 points but no week labels, and there's an empty band of about 70 px under the baseline where the labels clearly should be. So the viewer can't tell whether blue is twice orange or 10% more, whether the dip in week 8 was a big drop or a rounding wobble, or which week anything happened in. Every chart needs labelled axes with units, or direct values, so the reader never guesses what a number measures (Tufte; Few; Wilke). This also hides a scale question: if the y-axis doesn't start at zero, the gaps between lines exaggerate the real differences, and with no labels nobody can check. Fix: add a few y-axis values on the left (e.g. 0, 5k, 10k…), plus week or date labels along the bottom, using the existing empty band. Better still, also add a hover readout, though that can't be judged from a still.

3. **The chart takes the most space but carries the least information** (taste, but it matters here). Once the chart is unreadable, the page is really three numbers. The KPI cards give the totals, and the chart should explain them: which channel drove the +6.1%. Once labelled, also check that the three channels add up to the 184,220 shown above, or say what's left out ("Other"). Otherwise the viewer will wonder why the parts don't match the total.

**Smaller things**
- "vs previous 12 weeks" is repeated word for word under all three cards. Say it once near the "Last 12 weeks" range, and the deltas can shrink to "+6.1%", "+2.4%", "+0.1 pts".
- The green delta text is small, light-weight type. Check it reaches 4.5:1 contrast on white. It looks borderline at that size.
- The deltas use colour alone to show "good." Add an up/down arrow so the direction doesn't depend on seeing green (WCAG 1.4.1).
- "Last 12 weeks" at top right looks like plain text. If it's the date-range control, it needs a visible control cue (a chevron or a border, as Norman calls a signifier). If it's not a control, the page has no way to change the range.
- There's a lot of empty space at the bottom of the chart card, under the baseline and in the padding. Use it for the x-axis labels, or tighten it.

**What's working**
- The KPI cards: one label, one large number and one comparison each, in a tight two-size scale with weight doing the rest. The top row can be read in a couple of seconds. Keep this structure exactly.
- "+0.1 pts" for the conversion rate is correctly stated in percentage points, not percent. That's a small detail most dashboards get wrong.
- The restrained palette and very light gridlines keep the data as the loudest thing on the page. Once the chart is labelled, that restraint will pay off.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the chart has hover tooltips that reveal values and channel names. Even if it does, the static view still needs labels, because a summary screen shouldn't need hovering to be read.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive.
