**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers along the top, left to right, then drops into the chart. It reads as a clean, calm marketing analytics overview for someone checking traffic and sign-ups. It feels tidy and trustworthy for about three seconds. Then I try to read the chart and find it tells me nothing.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard where a marketer or founder checks whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy over the last 12 weeks, and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The chart takes up two-thirds of the screen and can't be read: there's no channel name, no value scale and no dates. On top of that, the conversion card contradicts the other two cards, so a careful reader will stop trusting the numbers.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart has no labels at all.** This is a defect. "Visits by channel" shows three coloured lines, but nothing says which channel is blue, orange or green. The y-axis has gridlines but no numbers, and the x-axis has no weeks or dates. The viewer can see that "blue is biggest and rising", but can't answer the question the title asks: which channel, how many visits, when. Tufte (*Visual Display*, ch. 2) and Few (*Show Me the Numbers*) both say every chart needs labelled axes with units, so readers never have to guess what a number measures. Label each line where it ends on the right, with the channel name and maybe its latest value, in the line's colour (Tufte, Wilke: label at the data rather than in a legend). Add a few y-axis values and week or date ticks along the bottom. There's plenty of empty space on the right and at the bottom of the card for this.

2. **The conversion card disagrees with the other two.** This is a defect, because it's the one card people will act on. Visits went up 6.1% and sign-ups went up 2.4%. Since sign-ups grew more slowly than visits, conversion must have fallen: about 2.20% before, 2.12% now, so roughly −0.08 points. The card shows "+0.1 pts" in green. A reader who does that sum will doubt every number on the page. One who doesn't will think conversion improved when it got worse. If this is placeholder data, fix it before anyone reviews the design with it. If it's live, check the calculation. In either case, the colour of a change should follow whether it's good or bad, and a drop needs to look like a drop.

3. **The cards and the chart don't connect.** This is taste. The headline cards show visits, sign-ups and conversion, but the only chart breaks down visits alone. The cards raise the question "where did sign-ups come from, and why did conversion move?", and the page doesn't answer it. Sign-ups by channel, or a way to switch the chart to another metric, would make the main chart explain the numbers above it instead of repeating the least important one.

**Smaller things**
- Green change lines under each number (e.g. "+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"): the green text is small and looks borderline on white. Check it reaches 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- "vs previous 12 weeks" appears three times. Say it once near the date range and shorten each card to "+6.1%".
- "Last 12 weeks" at top right looks like plain text. If it's the date-range control, make it look clickable. If it isn't, it's in the spot people expect one.
- "Overview" as the page title adds nothing. The product name and date range already say where you are.
- The empty band below the chart's baseline, about 70 px, looks like unused space. Put the date labels there.

**What's working**
- The number cards: label, big figure, then the change. That's a clear three-step order with real jumps in size and weight between steps. Keep it.
- The restraint: one typeface, a calm grey background, white cards on a consistent margin and gutter. It looks reliable and is easy to scan. Labelling the chart won't make it busy.
- The three line colours are distinct hues that look like they'd stay apart for colour-blind viewers too. Double-check orange against green.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether hovering the chart shows values or a tooltip. That would ease item 1, but it doesn't replace labels for anyone glancing at the screen or a screenshot.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is a working control.
