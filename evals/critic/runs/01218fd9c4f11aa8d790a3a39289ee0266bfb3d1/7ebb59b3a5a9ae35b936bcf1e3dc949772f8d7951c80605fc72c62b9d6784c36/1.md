I've finished checking the image and the checklist. Here is the critique:

**First read.** The eye goes to the big white headline, then gets pulled out to the busy green confetti around it, and has to be dragged back to find the subline and the button. It reads as a garden centre's home page: friendly and fresh, but loud. The background shouts louder than anything set on top of it.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the home page hero for a local plant nursery. Its job is to get nearby gardeners to look through the stock and come in, or order, this weekend.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The type and the message are sound, but they sit straight on top of a loud, high-contrast leaf pattern. The subline and the only button are hard to read, and the page has no calm place for the eye to rest.

**What would move it most**
1. **Text sits directly on a busy pattern. (Defect.)** Behind the subline "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend." and the "Browse the greenhouse" label there are pale lime leaves that come close to white. Where they cross the letters, contrast looks well under 3:1, which is roughly 1.5–2:1 at the worst spots. The subline is thin and fairly small, so it breaks up first, and that line carries the real reason to visit ("this weekend"). WCAG judges contrast at the worst point behind the text, not the average (W3C, Techniques for WCAG 2.2, F83). Fix: put a dark scrim or a solid panel behind the text block, or calm the pattern down (darker, lower contrast, sparser) across the left half where the text sits. Then check contrast against the lightest leaf behind each line.
2. **The only action is the weakest thing in the hero. (Defect.)** "Browse the greenhouse" is a thin white outline button with no fill, sitting on the same busy pattern. That style is normally used for a secondary action, but here it is the primary one, and it is the hardest thing on the screen to see as a button. Fix: give it a solid fill, such as the dark logo green or white with green text, so it is the single high-emphasis action on the screen.
3. **The background argues with the message. (Taste, but it costs something.)** The headline sells *ferns and shade plants*. The image is a flat scatter of bright, sunny lime-green ellipses that show neither ferns nor shade. It is decoration that could sit on any "eco" site, so the viewer gets no sense of what the nursery actually grows. A darker, cooler, deeper-green image would solve the contrast problem in item 1 and tell the story at the same time: real fronds in low light, or a stylised fern pattern in a narrow range of dark greens.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline's left edge sits at about x=96, while the logo sits at about x=64. Line them up on one edge so the hero belongs to the header.
- The headline breaks with "corner" alone on the last line. Widen the text box slightly or rebreak it so the lines are more even ("Ferns and shade plants / for every corner").
- "For every corner" is a line any plant shop could use. Something about *shade* specifically ("for the corners nothing else will grow") would say why this nursery is the one to visit.
- The nav links are mid-grey and fairly light at a small size. Check them against 4.5:1.

**What's working.**
- The copy has a real angle: a niche (ferns and shade plants) and an immediate reason to act ("ready to plant this weekend"). Keep both and make them readable.
- The type scale is clean. There are about four sizes (headline, subline, button and nav, logo) with clear steps between them, and the bold headline has the weight to lead.
- The header is calm and well balanced: a dark green wordmark and four plain nav labels that say what's behind them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the outline button. These matter here because its edge is already faint.
- Whether the pattern moves or shifts on scroll, which would make the readability problem worse.
- How the text holds up over the pattern at narrower, mobile widths.
