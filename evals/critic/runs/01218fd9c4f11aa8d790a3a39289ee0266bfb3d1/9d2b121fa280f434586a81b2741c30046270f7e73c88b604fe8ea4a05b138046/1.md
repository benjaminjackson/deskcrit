**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces", on the dark leaf pattern. It drops to the grey intro line under the banner, then moves across three equal outlined cards, and stops there because there's nothing to do next. It reads as a local garden centre's home page. It feels calm, tidy and a bit anonymous.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of a local garden centre, meant to get nearby people to visit, book a workshop or order plants for delivery.

**Verdict.** It looks clean and the facts on it are useful, but it doesn't work as a home page, because it never asks the visitor to do anything. There's no button, no link and no next step, so a visitor who's interested has nowhere to go but the small nav links.

**What would move it most**

1. **No call to action anywhere** (defect). The hero, the intro and the three cards are all statements. The visitor learns that shade plants are "ready to pot this weekend", that workshops exist and that delivery is free over $60, then hits empty white space. Each of those is a reason to act, and the page drops it. Krug and Nielsen both make the point: the next step should be visible, not something the visitor has to work out from the nav. The fix is one dominant action in or right under the hero, pointing at the offer the headline makes. "See the shade plants in stock this weekend" is better than a vague "Shop now". Then add a quiet text link to each card: "Book a Saturday workshop", "Get directions", "Order for delivery". Only one of these should be a filled button.

2. **The headline and its supporting line are separated, and the headline is off the page's left edge** (defect plus convention). The headline sits inside the banner, starting at about x=72. The logo, the intro line and the cards all start at about x=56. That 16 px gap is too small to look deliberate and too big to miss, so the headline looks nudged rather than placed (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one grid governs the page). Worse, the intro line ("Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs… ready to pot this weekend") is the actual offer, and it sits below the banner's bottom edge. It reads as the start of the body, not the second half of the headline. Put the headline on the same 56 px line as everything else, and keep headline and intro together: both inside the banner, or both under it.

3. **The banner doesn't show what's on sale** (taste, with a cost). The banner is a flat, repeating pattern of generic leaves with random coloured dots. It says "green" and nothing else. You can't see a fern or a hosta, the plants don't look like they're in shade, and nothing looks small enough for a balcony. This shop's best argument is "grown at our nursery", and the image gives up the one thing a stock pattern can't fake. Use a real photo of the shade stock: a corner of the nursery, or a pot of ferns on a small balcony. That makes the headline's promise visible, and the photo itself becomes the reason to click.

4. **The workshop card describes the class but leaves out what you need to sign up** (defect). "Repotting, pruning and balcony herb beds, ten places in each class" has no time, no price and no way to book. "Ten places in each class" is a fact that stops before it reaches the reader. What it means for them is "these fill up, so book early", and the card should say that and link to booking. Delivery has a similar gap: "Free within ten miles" leaves the reader to guess ten miles from where. Name the town or give a postcode check.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line break: "spaces" sits alone on line two. Either widen the text box so the headline fits on one line, or break after "shade" so each line is a phrase.
- The headline is two lines of white text sitting directly on a busy pattern. It reads now because the pattern is dark, but check contrast at the lightest leaves and the grey dots, and darken the image behind the text if any spot falls below 3:1.
- The space under the cards (about 120 px of blank white in this view) looks like the page just ran out. Either end on something such as an address and map strip, or tighten the space.
- The opening-hours line "until 8 pm on Thursdays" breaks badly, with "8" at the end of one line and "pm" on the next. Stop a number from splitting from its unit.
- The card borders do the same job the even spacing already does. Taste: lose the borders, or make the cards clickable so the box means something.
- The nav has no "Visit" or "Book" emphasis, and there's no address or phone number on the page at all. For a local shop those are the top task.

**What's working.**
- The headline names a specific audience and need, shade and small spaces, rather than a generic "Welcome to our garden centre". Keep that specificity.
- The card copy is concrete and plain: real hours, a real delivery threshold, real class topics. It only needs the next step added.
- The type is restrained: a serif wordmark against a sans everywhere else, with about four clear sizes. It reads as one coherent piece.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the cards and nav behave on hover and focus, and whether the banner image scales or crops cleanly on mobile.
