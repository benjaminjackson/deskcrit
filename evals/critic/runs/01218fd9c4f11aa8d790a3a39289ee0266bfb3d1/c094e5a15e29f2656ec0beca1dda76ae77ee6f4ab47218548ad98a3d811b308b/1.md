**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a section that tells people who need help, donors and possible volunteers how the food bank works each week, so they can see where they fit.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. Its left-aligned body text lines up with the headline and the stats, so the section reads as one block and the two paragraphs are easier to read. Both versions still share one real problem: the page explains the process but never tells a reader in need what to do next.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **The body text went from centred to left-aligned (version 2 fixes a defect in version 1).** In version 1 the two paragraphs are centred under a left-aligned headline and left-aligned stats. That gives the section three edges: headline at the left margin, a ragged centred block, then the stats back at the left margin. Every line of body text starts somewhere different, so after each line break the eye has to find the next start. Over four-line paragraphs of practical detail (days, places, how referral works) that slows reading and makes it easier to lose your place. Centring suits a few short lines, not running paragraphs (Bringhurst; Butterick, *Practical Typography*, on centred text). Version 2 puts everything on one left edge, so the headline, paragraphs and stats now read as one column with a clear top-to-bottom order.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **There's no next step for the person who needs food (defect).** The second paragraph tells a reader in need the key facts: you need a referral from your doctor, a school or the housing office, and you can collect or have a parcel delivered. Then the section stops. The only way forward is a small "Get help" link in the header. A reader who has just learned they need a referral is left asking "how do I get one, and where are the four centres?" Add one specific ask under the paragraph, such as "Find your nearest collection point" or "How to get a referral". It should name exactly what happens when they click.
2. **The lines are too long (convention).** The body lines look like roughly 90–95 characters, above the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Version 2 has the same measure as version 1, but now that the text is ranged left the long lines are the main thing slowing reading. Narrow the column, or raise the body size a step, to get nearer 65–75 characters.

**Smaller things.**
- The right third of the page and the band below the stats are empty. It looks unused rather than intentional. A map of the four centres or the ask above would earn that space.
- "412 parcels last month" sits next to "96 volunteers" and "4 collection points", but only the first has a time frame. That's fine for counts that don't change, but "96 volunteers" could mean active this month or ever. Say which if it matters to donors.

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete: named days, Carver Road, three days of meals, the home-delivery exception. It reads like a real organisation, not a template. Keep it.
- The type scale is clean, with three clear levels (headline, stat numbers, body/labels). The serif body and sans headings are well separated.
- The stats row is a good proof strip: big green numbers with short plain labels, easy to take in at a glance.
