I prefer version 1, but only by a little. The background colour is the only change I could find between the two versions. Both pages put the reader the same way: title first, then ingredients, then method.

**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif title "Leek and potato soup with brown butter". Then it drops past a wide empty band to the two columns: ingredients on the left, numbered method on the right. It reads as a calm, editorial recipe page. On version 1's cream background it feels warm, like a cookbook. On version 2's pure white it feels plainer and more like a website template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a recipe page that a home cook reads to decide whether to make the dish, then cooks from.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job. The layout, type and copy are identical, and the content is clear. Version 1 is a little better for two reasons. The cream ground suits the warm food-writing tone. And the thin rules between ingredients look like they were drawn for that cream. On version 2's white, some of those rules read faintly yellow and others grey, which looks like a mistake. The biggest weakness is shared by both: the spacing at the top breaks the title away from its own details.

**What would move it most**
1. **The serving size and time are cut off from the title** (both versions). There are about 100 px of empty space between the title and "Serves 4 · 45 minutes", and almost as much again before the columns. That gap makes the line look like a separate, minor note, when it is one of the first things a cook checks. The line is also small and pale. Pull it up close under the title so the two read as one unit, and close the gap before the columns. Spacing should show what belongs together. *Defect, mild: it slows the decision "can I make this tonight?"*
2. **The ingredient rules on white** (version 2 only). Looked at closely, the lines under "3 large leeks", "1 litre vegetable stock" and "A small bunch of chives" have a yellow cast. The others are neutral grey. On cream that difference disappears. On white it looks like the colours didn't get updated. Either keep the cream, or set every rule to the same neutral grey. *Defect of craft: a small one, but it is the actual difference between the two versions.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Serves 4 · 45 minutes" looks light grey at a small size. Check that it reaches 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.3).
- The method doesn't say how much salt and pepper to use, or when to add the chives' half of the garnish. The quantities match the ingredients list in every other case, so this is minor.

**What's working.**
- The two-column split, with ingredients at a scannable width and method lines at about 60–70 characters. It suits the way people cook: glance at the list, then follow the steps.
- The serif steps against the sans-serif ingredients clearly separate "what you need" from "what you do".
- The method copy is concrete and sensory: "until the potatoes break when pressed", "until it smells of hazelnuts". It tells the cook exactly when each step is done.

Better: version 1
