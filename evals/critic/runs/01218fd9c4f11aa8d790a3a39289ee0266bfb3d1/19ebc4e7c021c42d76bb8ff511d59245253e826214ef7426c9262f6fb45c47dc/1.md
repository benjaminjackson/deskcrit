**First read.** My eye goes to the red "Rose arch" tile at top left. That's only because it's first in reading order and the warmest colour. After that it moves left to right in even steps across six tiles of the same size. It reads as a gallery template with placeholder art: each tile is a flat colour field with a pale circle. It feels tidy, calm and generic.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web gallery that shows visitors the areas of a garden show, so they can browse them and pick one to visit or read about.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. No tile shows anything from the garden, so the viewer can't see a rose arch or a herb bed, which is the only reason to open a gallery. The line at the bottom says there is a "large feature tile" and a "clear focal point". There is neither: all six tiles are the same size and weight.

**What would move it most**

1. **The pictures show nothing (defect).** All six tiles use the same image, a centred pale circle on a flat colour, changing only in hue. A gallery makes its case through its pictures. Here the viewer has to read every caption to learn what each tile is, and nothing makes one area more appealing than another. The colours are arbitrary: purple for an orchard path and ochre for a glasshouse tell the viewer nothing. If these are placeholders, the layout can't be judged until real photos are in, because photo content will change both the balance and the hierarchy. Fix: put in real photos, all treated the same way (same light, grade and crop distance), before going further.

2. **No focal point, and the footer claims one exists (defect).** The two rows of three are identical in size, value and spacing, so the page has no entry point and no reason to prefer one area to another (checklist: one element dominates, contrast of scale). The sentence under the grid says "The large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point." That is false here. A visitor reading it will look for a feature tile that doesn't exist, and it reads as a design note left in the live copy. Fix: either make one tile clearly dominant (say, the show's headline garden spanning two columns and both rows), or delete the sentence. If you keep the even grid, that's a defensible choice for a neutral index, but the copy must not describe a different layout.

3. **The heading and captions don't tell a visitor anything (defect).** "Garden show gallery" names a category. It doesn't say which show, when, or what the visitor can do. The captions are noun fragments ("Reeds and water lilies.", "Plum and pear trees.") ending with full stops, and only one carries information a visitor could act on: "Tomatoes in late July." That line can be read two ways: the tomatoes are ripe in late July, or the photo was taken then. If it's a timing hint for visitors, say so ("Tomatoes ripen here in late July"). Nothing tells the visitor what happens when they click a tile, or that they can. Fix: give the heading the show's name and dates, and make each caption say what the visitor will see or do there, with a verb.

**Smaller things.**
- The first column's title and caption ("Rose arch / Climbing roses…") sit about 2–4 px higher than the other two columns, so the baselines don't line up across the row. Use one caption offset for every tile.
- The space between titles and the row below (about 60 px) is several times the 14 px horizontal gutter. Columns read tight and rows read loose, so the grid looks like two separate strips instead of one field. Choose one interval or a deliberate ratio.
- The space between "Garden show gallery" and the first tiles is barely more than the gutter, so the page title reads like a tile caption. Give it more room above the grid, or more size or weight contrast.
- The grey caption text looks close to the 4.5:1 limit at that small size (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure it, and darken it if it falls short.
- Colour is the only thing that tells the tiles apart visually. With real photos this goes away; with placeholders, red/green and ochre/brown pairs will blur for colour-blind viewers.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: one bold title size, one caption size and one heading, told apart by weight and colour as well as size. Keep it.
- The left margin and column edges are consistent. Tiles, titles and the heading share the same left lines, which gives you a sound base once the content is real.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are clickable, and what hover or focus state shows that. With no visible signifier, the live page needs one.
