**First read.** The eye goes to the bold title, then gets caught by the heavy black grid, which is the strongest thing on the page. It reads as a plain, competent one-way ferry timetable for local commuters. It feels sober and clear, but more like a spreadsheet than a timetable.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a printed or web timetable that lets a rider find the next ferry they can catch on a given day, quickly.

**Verdict.** It works: every departure can be found and read. But the layout answers "what times exist?" when the rider is asking "which ferry can I catch today?" The day restriction sits in the last column, and the most valuable left-hand column holds a label you don't need.

**What would move it most**

1. **"Runs" is in the wrong place for the question riders ask.** Three of the seven sailings don't run every day: 12:40 and 17:10 are weekdays only, and 19:30 is weekends only. That fact sits in the far-right column, about 600 px from the departure time. A Saturday rider has to read every row to the end to rule sailings out, and anyone who stops at the time column will turn up for a 17:10 boat that isn't running. This is a **defect**, because it is the line people act on. Fix: put the condition right next to the time, or split the table into Weekdays and Weekends so each reader sees only their own boats. If you keep one table, keep "Daily" plain and mark the exceptions so they stand out.

2. **The heavy black grid outweighs the times.** Every cell has a thick dark border on all four sides, so the rules are darker and busier than the numbers they hold. The table reads as a cage of boxes instead of rows of times. This breaks a **convention**: Tufte's data-ink principle, and Wathan and Schoger's *Refactoring UI* advice to use fewer borders and let spacing and light dividers do the work. Here it costs scan speed down the times column. Fix: drop the vertical rules and use thin, light horizontal rules, or zebra shading, with a single firmer rule under the header.

3. **The "Period" column takes the best spot and adds nothing.** "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday…" just restates what the times already say, and it sits in the leftmost column, where the eye starts each row. This is a **defect**, though a mild one: it delays reaching the time on every row. Fix: lead with "Leaves north". If you want the grouping, show it with spacing or a small group label between blocks of rows, not as a repeated column.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle says "spring season" with no dates. A rider can't tell whether this timetable applies today. Add the date range.
- Naming isn't consistent: the subtitle says "North landing" and "market quay", but the headers say "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay". Use the same names in both places.
- The columns are spread evenly across the full width, so 06:40 and 06:52 sit about 300 px apart. Size the columns to their content so each departure and arrival reads as a pair.
- The page only covers one direction. If a return timetable exists, point to it. Otherwise the rider is left wondering how to get back.
- There's a stray line of body text under the table that has nothing to do with the timetable. It shows in the design itself, so take it out of the published piece.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight: one bold title, one muted subtitle, a bold header row and regular body text. Every level has a clear job. Keep it.
- The times appear to be set in tabular figures, so the digits line up down each column and are easy to compare. Keep that when you restyle the table.
- The "Leaves / Arrives" headers are plain and use verbs, so it's clear what each time means.
