**First read.** The eye goes to "Account settings", then down a stack of four identical white cards, each with a bold title, one grey line and an outlined button on the right. It reads as a calm, tidy settings overview for a team SaaS product. Then the left nav pulls the eye back: "Billing" is highlighted, but the page is not a billing page.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the settings landing page of a workspace tool. Someone comes here to find one setting and get into it fast, and to check things like renewal and active sessions at a glance.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and competent. But the navigation and the content contradict each other, so the viewer can't tell where they are or where things live. The one line they would act on, the billing renewal, is too vague to rely on.

**What would move it most**

1. **The nav says you're on Billing, but you're on an overview.** Location: the left sidebar, against the page heading. "Billing" has the selected tint, while the heading says "Account settings" and the page lists Profile, Notifications, Billing and Sessions. The viewer either thinks the click didn't work or wonders whether Billing is only this one card. Either way, they no longer trust the nav to tell them where they are. *Defect* (Nielsen 1, visibility of system status; WCAG 3.2.3, consistent navigation). Fix: the selected item must match the page. Either add an "Overview" item and select it, or make this the real Billing page.

2. **The sidebar and the cards list different things.** The nav has Members, Integrations and Security. The cards have Sessions, which isn't in the nav, and leave out Members and Integrations. The viewer has to guess whether "Sessions" is the same as "Security". There's also no telling whether Members and Integrations were left out on purpose. A second mismatch sits on top of this: the header names a *workspace* ("Harbour Studio"), the heading says *account*, and Billing and Members are usually workspace-level. *Defect* (WCAG 3.2.4, consistent identification; Norman, conceptual model). Fix: use one name for each section in both places, and either give every nav section a card or make it clear the cards are a subset. Then decide whether this is your account or the workspace's, and title the page to match.

3. **The billing line can be read more than one way.** "Your plan renews on 1 March. The invoice goes to the address on file."
   - There's no year, no amount and no plan name. The viewer can't tell what they'll be charged or when.
   - "The address on file" could mean an email address or a postal address, and the line doesn't show which one. Someone who moved, or changed their billing contact, can't tell whether the invoice will reach them.
   
   This is the one line on the page the viewer acts on and holds you to. *Defect.* Fix: state the plan, the amount and the full date ("Team plan, £X, renews 1 March 2027"), and show the actual destination ("Invoice emailed to accounts@…"). Then the viewer can check it against what they already know.

**Smaller things.**
- Card buttons: "Edit", "Manage", "View invoices" and "Review" each start a different kind of action, but they all have the same outlined style. That's fine for an overview, but "Review" on Sessions undersells it: the card's own text says you can sign out of devices. "Review sessions" or "Sign out devices" would say what happens.
- "You are signed in on three now": say "three devices", so the number has its unit right next to it.
- The cards are tall (about 127 px) for two lines, with a lot of empty padding inside each one. Tightening them would bring the whole list into view sooner on smaller screens. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- The type scale has three clear levels: page heading, card title and grey description. Every size has an obvious job. Keep it.
- The description lines are mostly written for the viewer. "Your name and photo appear on comments and in the member list" tells them why the setting matters, not just what it is. Write the billing line to the same standard.
- The left edge of the content lines up with the heading, and the first nav item sits on the same line as the heading. The page feels ordered.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** What clicking a sidebar item does: whether it scrolls to a card or loads a new page. That decides which fix for finding 1 is right. Focus states on the outlined buttons are also not visible in a still.
