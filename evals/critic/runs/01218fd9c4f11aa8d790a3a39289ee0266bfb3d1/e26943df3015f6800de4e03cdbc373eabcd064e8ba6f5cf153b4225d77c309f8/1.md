**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold two-line heading, then down into the body text. It reads at once as a help-centre article for a SaaS billing product, aimed at an account owner or admin. It feels calm, plain, and trustworthy, with nothing competing for attention. The one thing that catches on a second look is the left sidebar: it lists this very article, but nothing shows that you are on it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help article for a customer who has come to change the card on their subscription, often because the old card is expiring or was declined. They need to do that and know what happens to their money and their plan.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are sound and mostly out of the way. What lets it down is the content. It's a how-to page with no "how": no steps, and no link to the page where you actually do it. Its one deadline, when you lose your plan, can also be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The task itself is missing.** The first line of the first paragraph says you can replace the card "from the billing page", and that's all the page says about doing it. "Billing page" isn't a link, though three less important destinations in the same paragraphs are (billing history, roles and permissions, paying by invoice). A reader who came to do one thing gets the edge cases first and the main task never. Nielsen's tenth heuristic asks that help be task-focused and lay out concrete steps. Krug's test is whether the page lets you act without working anything out. **Defect.** Fix: put a numbered list of three or four steps directly under the heading ("Go to Settings → Billing", "Select Replace card", and so on), with the first step linking to the billing page. Move the paragraph about open invoices and the paragraph about roles below the steps as notes.

2. **The decline deadline can be read two ways.** This is in the "When the new card is declined" section. "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days" could mean day 3 and day 7, or day 3 and then seven days later (day 10). "If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan" makes it worse. If the first charge counts as attempt one, the second attempt is the day-3 retry. If only the retries count, it's the last one. So the reader could believe they have about 3, 7, or 10 days before losing their plan. This is the line they'll remember and hold you to. Their reading decides whether they fix the card today or next week, and whether they file a complaint when the plan drops. **Defect.** Fix: give one timeline counted from one event, and name the outcome in the reader's terms. For example: "If your new card is declined, we try it again 3 days later and 7 days later and email you each time. If the 7-day retry also fails, your subscription moves to the free plan." Then add what moving to the free plan does to them (which features or data they lose access to).

3. **The page doesn't say where you are.** In the left sidebar, "Update a payment card" looks the same as its neighbours, and "Billing" in the top navigation isn't marked either. The sidebar label also doesn't match the heading ("Update a payment card" vs "Update the card that pays for your subscription"). Someone arriving from search has to compare the two to confirm they're on the right page. This is Nielsen's first heuristic (visibility of system status). **Convention**, and here breaking it costs orientation. Fix: give the current sidebar item a weight or colour change, or a left bar, and mark Billing as active in the header. Either use the same wording in both places or make the sidebar item an obvious short form of the heading.

**Smaller things.**
- Body measure looks wide. The first line runs to about 84 characters, above the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrow the column by roughly 10% or raise the body size slightly.
- Type scale near the bottom is muddled. I count about seven sizes: the heading, the subheading, body text, the header nav, the sidebar links, the "In this section" label, and the breadcrumb. The header nav, sidebar links, and breadcrumb/label sizes sit only a point or so apart. Use one small size for all of them and separate them by weight and colour (Refactoring UI).
- "The new card is charged from the next invoice" says what the system does, not what the reader gets. Something like "Your next invoice will be charged to the new card" puts it in the reader's terms. The same paragraph should say what happens if the old card can't pay an open invoice, because an expiring card is the usual reason someone is on this page.
- The page ends without a next step after the last paragraph, such as "Still stuck? Contact support" near the content. Support is only in the top-right nav, which the reader has scrolled away from by this point on a longer article.

**What's working.**
- Links are blue and underlined, and their text says where they go ("roles and permissions", "paying by invoice"). They pass WCAG's link-purpose check without help from the surrounding sentence. Keep this.
- The "When the new card is declined" subheading sits clearly closer to its paragraph than to the text above it, and it's set apart by weight, not just size. Use the same pattern for the step list and notes.
- The voice is plain and direct ("You can…", "We try…"), and the page has no decoration. A worried reader can trust it, so keep that tone when you add the steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the sidebar and the wide body column reflow on mobile, and whether links and sidebar items have visible focus states.
