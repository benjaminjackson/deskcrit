**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline on the blue band, drops to the subline, then to the two buttons. After that it has nowhere to go: the right 40% of the blue band is empty, and the three feature columns below are small and grey next to the hero. It reads as a landing page for a personal budgeting app. It feels clean and confident, and also generic, like a standard SaaS template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the top of a marketing landing page for a consumer budgeting app, and its job is to get first-time visitors to start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The copy does its job: the headline and subline are specific and plain. The page around them doesn't. It promises you'll "see where every dollar goes" but never shows you, and the one thing that would stop someone connecting their bank account (can this app touch my money?) is tucked away in small grey text in the third column.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero promises a view it never shows.** *Defect.* The right side of the blue band, about 500 px across, is empty. The headline is about seeing something, yet the viewer has to imagine the product. Since there's no picture, the plain blue band and Inter-style type are the only visual identity, and that look could be any startup's. The cost is that nothing tells the viewer what *this* app's month looks like, or why it beats the bank's own app or their spreadsheet. Fill that space with the product doing what the subline says: one month split into categories, with one category close to its limit and the heads-up showing. That lets viewers compare it with how they track spending now, instead of taking the headline on trust.

2. **The trust answer sits far from the ask that needs it.** *Defect.* The subline's first step is "Connect your accounts", which is the biggest worry for a finance product. The reassurance is the third feature, "Read-only access", in about 15 px grey text, a long way from the buttons. A cautious viewer reaches "Try it free" with the worry still open, and many will leave rather than look for the answer. Move a short version right next to the call to action, and say it to the viewer: "Read-only: Penny Path can see your balances but can never move your money." The current line, "The app can see…", states a fact about "the app". It doesn't tell the viewer they're safe.

3. **"Try it free" can be read two ways.** *Defect.* This is the button that commits the viewer, so the wording matters. "Free" could mean a free plan or a trial that starts charging later. People who read it as a free plan and then get billed will hold it against you. Pricing is in the nav but not near the button. Say exactly what it means on the button or just under it (for example "Free for 30 days, no card needed", or "Free plan, no card"), whichever is true.

**Smaller things**
- Type scale: the middle sizes are too close together. The logo (~19 px), subline (~20), button labels (~17) and feature headings (~18) are about 1–3 px apart, so they look like mistakes rather than separate levels. Pick one size for them and separate them by weight instead.
- The feature row sits tight under the hero (about 40 px gap) and leaves about 200 px of empty white below it. It looks squeezed against the band rather than placed. Give it more space above, or make it a proper section.
- The feature headings name categories ("Automatic categories", "Shared budgets"). The body lines are better because they say what happens. Headings that name the benefit would make the row skimmable.
- The outline "Watch the tour" button is almost as strong as the filled primary: a 2 px white border on saturated blue stands out a lot. A text link with a play icon would leave "Try it free" clearly in charge.

**What's working**
- The headline is specific, concrete and about the viewer ("every dollar", "this month"). Keep it.
- The subline gives the whole product loop in one sentence, using verbs and "you": connect, set a limit, get a heads-up before you go over. That's well written; leave it alone.
- White text on this blue has good contrast (roughly 6–7:1 by eye), and the left edge is crisp: the logo, headline, buttons and first feature column all line up on one margin.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- The empty band at the bottom could be the end of the screenshot rather than the design. Check in a browser what comes next and whether it shows above the fold.
