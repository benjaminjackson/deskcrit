**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page for a free local festival (it's 1280×800). Its job is to tell people what the event is, when and where it is, and get them to plan a visit or find out more.

**Verdict.** The look is good and the order of reading is right. As a message it's incomplete: it never says where the festival is, and it gives the reader nothing to do next. It sets up the visit and then stops.

**What would move it most**
1. **No place (defect).** You get the day, the time and what happens, but not the town or harbour. "The quay" and "the old net shed" only mean something to people who already know the place. A visitor from outside, or anyone seeing this shared on social media, has to search to find out where it is. Put the place in the gold line at the top so it reads as one unit: date, time, place (for example "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour"). That line already has the right colour, size and spacing for it.
2. **No next step, and a bottom third that feels unfinished (defect).** Under the blurb is about 200 px of navy with nothing in it. The eye reaches "Free entry for everyone." and has nowhere to go. You don't need a ticket button for a free event, but you need some specific action. Two good ones:
   - "See the evening programme", because people will want to know when the lantern boats go out.
   - "Plan your visit: parking and trains", for people coming from further away.

   Put one clear link or button at the left edge of the text, under the blurb. That uses the space as the end of the reading path instead of leaving it as leftover.

**Smaller things.**
- Gold date line: "from noon" gives no end time and no year. The highlights (lantern boats at dusk) are hours after noon, so readers can't tell when to arrive for the main event. Add the end time or "lanterns at dusk, around 9pm". Add the year if this page will stay up past the event.
- Taste: the festival is called "Harbour *Lights*", but the only light on the page is the gold date. One warm glow, one lantern image, or a lit detail in the empty lower right would make the name visible instead of only spelled out. This is optional. The type-only version works.
- Headline line spacing: the "g" of "Lights" sits close to the blurb's first line, while the gap above the headline is larger. Check that the space under the headline is at least the space above it, so the blurb clearly belongs to the headline block.

**What's working.**
- **Type scale:** there are three clear sizes, each with one job. The display italic is huge, the body is about 20 px, and the eyebrow is small, letterspaced gold capitals. The eyebrow and body are close in size but differ in colour, case and weight, which is the right way to separate them (Refactoring UI). Keep this.
- **Colour:** cream on navy reads easily and suits an evening on the water. The single gold accent is the one warm note, and it marks the facts people act on.
- **Copy:** "Lantern boats on the water at dusk, music on the quay, and a fish supper at the old net shed" is concrete and specific to this place, with no generic festival wording. "Free entry for everyone" answers the cost question in four words. The blurb's lines are about 60 characters, which is comfortable to read.
