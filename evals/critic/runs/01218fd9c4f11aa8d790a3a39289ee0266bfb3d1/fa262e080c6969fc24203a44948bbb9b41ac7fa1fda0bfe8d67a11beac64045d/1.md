**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the headline "Change the card you pay with" and then on the intro line. In version 1 it next steps down three bold subheads, so the page reads at a glance as a short help article with three parts. In version 2 the eye drops below the intro onto a solid grey column. The subheads are the same weight and almost the same size as the body text, so the three parts blur into six paragraphs of equal weight.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article for a customer who wants to change their payment card or fix a declined card. Most of them will scan for their own case rather than read top to bottom.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The words and layout are the same in both. The one change is that version 2 sets the three subheads ("Add a new card", "Make it the default", "If the card is declined") in regular weight at about body size, where version 1 has them bold and a step larger. That costs version 2 the one thing a help page has to do: let the reader find their section without reading everything.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **Version 2's subheads don't read as headings (defect).** In version 2 the subheads are set at about 17px regular, and the body text is about 18px regular in the same colour. Only a slightly tighter gap tells a heading from the paragraph under it, and a 1–2px difference in size reads as a mistake rather than a level (Bringhurst). Someone whose card was just declined has to read the first two sections to find the third. Version 1 is a clear scale: a large bold headline, a bold mid-size subhead, and regular body text. Each subhead in it is something the eye can stop on. Refactoring UI's advice applies directly here: separate levels with weight ("bolder, not bigger"), which is exactly what version 1 does. Keep version 1's treatment.

**Remaining findings for version 1**
1. **The interface labels aren't marked out in the steps (defect).** Phrases like "choose Use for payments", "select Add card" and "select Save" run straight into the sentence. "choose Use for payments" in particular reads as broken English until you realise "Use for payments" is a menu item. Readers will then look on screen for words they can't be sure of. Set the on-screen labels in bold, the usual help-centre convention, so the reader knows exactly what to look for.

**Smaller things.**
- "Add a new card" is really a sequence (Settings → Billing → Add card → fields → Save). Numbered steps, or the "›" path style already used in the breadcrumb, would make it easier to follow while switching between this page and the app.
- "call the number on the back of the card": "your bank's number on the back of your card" would remove any doubt about who to call.

**What's working.**
- The headline names the reader's task in their own words. The intro answers the worry about being charged before anyone asks it ("nothing is charged when you make the change").
- The measure is comfortable, at about 75–80 characters a line, with generous line spacing. Each subhead sits closer to its own text than to the section above, so the groups are clear.
- The copy is plain and complete. Every step says who does what and where.

Better: version 1
