**First read.** The eye goes to the serif headline, then to the tall June bar, which is the biggest and busiest shape on the page. Next it lands on the three stat cards on the right. It reads as a calm, well-made member update from a credit union. The striped bars make the chart feel busier than the rest of the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public report page that tells members how savings deposits went in the first half of 2026, so they can trust the credit union and feel good about saving there.

**Verdict.** The page mostly works. The structure is clear, the claim is checkable, the totals add up and the axis is honest. The biggest problem is that the most dramatic fact in the chart, June, is mostly the bonus payroll, and that explanation is hidden in the footnote. A reader comes away believing a growth story that is partly a one-off.

**What would move it most**

1. **June's jump is explained only in grey fine print.** June goes from $868k to $1,150k, by far the biggest step in the series. The only explanation is the last clause of the source line under the chart: "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll." Most readers never reach it, so they will read June as the savings trend speeding up. The headline "rose every month" makes this worse, because June is what makes the rise feel steep. In a report a member is meant to trust, a reader who later finds that clause may feel misled. **Defect.** Fix: put a short note beside the June bar ("includes mid-year bonus payroll"). Consider showing the bonus as its own segment of that bar, or giving the figure without it. The title should still be true either way. (Tufte, *Visual Display*, argues for putting explanations on the data itself.)

2. **The diagonal hatching on the bars is chartjunk.** The blue-and-white stripes flicker, especially on the tall May and June bars. They make the bars look grey and textured instead of solid, and they draw the eye away from the numbers. The stripes carry no meaning: there is only one series, so nothing needs telling apart. **Convention** (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 5: moiré hatching is chartjunk). Fix: use solid fills. If you take the advice in point 1, a solid colour with a lighter tint for the bonus part of June gives the colour a real job.

3. **"Average deposit" says something different from what was calculated.** $214 × 5,374 accounts ≈ $1.15M, which is June's total. So $214 is the average per account that made a deposit, not the average size of one deposit. A member will read "average deposit" as the size of a typical deposit, which is a different and smaller number if accounts deposit more than once a month. Readers remember and repeat figures like this. **Defect.** Fix: label it for what it is ("Average deposited per account, Jun 2026"), or change the figure to match the label.

**Smaller things**
- Number formats don't match. The bars show "$1150k" and the axis shows "$1200k", but the card shows "$4.82M". Write millions as $1.15M and $1.2M, and add the thousands comma wherever "k" stays.
- The three "Up … on …" lines are clipped. "Up $11 on Jun 2025" only makes sense once the reader has worked out what "on" means here. Write "up $11 from June 2025".
- "Up 6.1%" is the only percentage among the three changes; the other two are absolute. Either add the base figures or note why the formats differ. As it stands, the 6.1% year-on-year rise sits awkwardly next to a chart that seems to show 88% growth within the half, and that tension is worth one plain sentence.
- The chart has value labels, gridlines and a y-axis all doing the same job. With every bar labelled, the gridlines could go or be made fainter.
- The green "Up" lines look pale and small. Check that they reach 4.5:1 contrast on white.
- The right column ends well above the bottom of the chart card, which leaves an empty patch at the lower right. Either align the column's bottom with the chart, or accept the gap on purpose.

**What's working**
- **The headline states the finding**, "Savings deposits rose every month", not just a topic. The kicker, subtitle and chart title each add something new (period, scope, units). Keep that structure.
- **The chart is honest.** The axis starts at $0, the units are in the chart title, every bar has its value written on it, and there is a dated source line. The stated total ($4.82M) matches the sum of the six bars exactly.
- **The type scale is clean.** I count roughly four levels: headline, big numbers, chart title and labels, small caption. There are big steps between them, the serif is kept for the brand and the headline, and the sans handles the data.
