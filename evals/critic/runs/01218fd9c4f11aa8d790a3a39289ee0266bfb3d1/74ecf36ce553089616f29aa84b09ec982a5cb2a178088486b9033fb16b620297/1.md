**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers across the top, left to right, and then down to the blue line rising across the chart. It reads as a marketing analytics dashboard for a small SaaS team, checked weekly. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and that makes the problem below worse.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a growth or marketing lead opens this to see in a few seconds whether traffic and sign-ups are getting better, and which channel is driving it.

**Verdict.** The layout and type do their job well, but the numbers contradict each other, and the dashboard tells the viewer good news that its own figures don't support. A dashboard that is confidently wrong is worse than one that is ugly. Fix the data and the framing before anything visual.

**What would move it most**

1. **The conversion change has the wrong sign and is coloured as good news (defect).** Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups only 2.4%, so the conversion rate must have fallen. Working back from the cards: the previous period was about 173,600 visits and 3,820 sign-ups, a 2.20% rate. Today's 2.12% is a drop of about 0.08 points. The third card says "+0.1 pts" in green. A viewer will take away "conversion improved" and stop looking. In fact traffic is growing faster than sign-ups, which is the most important finding on the page. Fix the calculation. Then make the delta colour follow the meaning ("worse" is red or neutral, never green), and give it a second cue besides colour, such as an arrow or the word "down" (WCAG 1.4.1).

2. **The chart doesn't add up to the headline number (defect).** If you read the three lines by eye and sum them over 12 weeks, you get roughly 72k Search, 43k Social and 24k Email, so about 140k. The Visits card says 184,220. Either about 45k visits come from a channel the chart leaves out (Direct? Referral?), or one figure is wrong. Either way, the viewer can't reconcile the two parts of the screen and will stop trusting both. Add the missing channel(s), even as an "Other" line, or say in the subtitle which channels are shown. Also check the two figures against the source.

3. **The page states numbers but never what they mean (taste, argued).** "Visits by channel" names a topic. The real story here is that Search drives the growth while Social swings week to week, and that sign-ups aren't keeping pace. That is the part the reader came for, and they have to work it out themselves. A title that states the takeaway would fix this, e.g. "Search is driving growth; sign-ups aren't keeping up". So would one line of annotation on the chart. The source for this rule in my references isn't on the approved list, so treat it as my judgement. I'd still push for it on a screen meant to be read at a glance.

**Smaller things**
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top right looks like a plain label, not a control. If it changes the period, give it a dropdown chevron or a button outline. Also show the actual dates (Jul 7 – Sep 22) so a screenshot of this still makes sense next month.
- The y-axis has no unit: 2k, 4k, 6k of what? Add "Visits per week" to the subtitle or the axis.
- The legend sits above the chart, so the reader looks back and forth to match colours. Put the labels at the right-hand end of each line instead; there's room.
- "vs previous 12 weeks" appears on all three cards. Say it once, next to the date range, and keep only the delta on each card.
- The x-axis labels skip every other week, and the last point (Sep 22) has no label. Label the final week so the line's endpoint is anchored.

**What's working**
- **Type scale:** I count about four sizes (page title, KPI number, card label/chart title, small delta/axis text), with large, clear steps between them. Keep that discipline when you add annotations.
- **Each KPI comes with a comparison,** which is exactly right (Few). The structure is good; it's the content of the conversion comparison that's wrong.
- **The chart is restrained:** light gridlines, no frame inside the card, distinct hues that should stay distinguishable for most colour-blind viewers. Almost all the ink in it is data.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the date control works, and whether chart points show values on hover. Hover values would partly ease the missing-units problem, but only for people who hover.
