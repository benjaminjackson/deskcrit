**What would move it most**

1. **The headline breaks in the wrong places** *(convention)*. Line 2 ends on "and the" and line 3 opens with "birds that came back". That splits the article from its noun, and it also splits the story's two halves across lines. The reader reaches the turn of the story, "and the birds", mid-line and half-broken. At this size each line is read as its own unit, so the breaks are part of the copy (Bringhurst, Hochuli: break display lines by sense). Break by phrase instead: "The farmers who are letting their fields / flood on purpose, / and the birds that came back". Another option is to set the headline measure so that "and the birds that came back" stays whole on the last line. That final line is the payoff, so give it a line to itself. Also check the breaks at every width the page reflows to, because a fix tuned for this width can break again on mobile.

**Smaller things**
- **"hand's" in the first paragraph looks like a straight apostrophe (') rather than a curly one (’).** At reading size in a serif this good, it stands out. Use real typographic quotes throughout (Butterick).
- **The gaps above and below the byline look almost equal**: headline to byline, then byline to body. So the byline floats instead of belonging to the headline. Tighten the gap above it a little, or open the gap below, so it groups with the title block.
- **The byline's grey looks a little light at this small size.** Check that it clears 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- **The headline is 15 words and two clauses long.** It earns its length as a story hook, but it's at the limit. Don't let it grow.
