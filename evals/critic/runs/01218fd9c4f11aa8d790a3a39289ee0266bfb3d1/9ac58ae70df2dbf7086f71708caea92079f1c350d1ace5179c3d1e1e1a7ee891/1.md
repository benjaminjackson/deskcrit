**First read.** The eye lands on the big "Pricing" heading, then drops to the three dollar figures, which carry equal weight. From there it has nowhere to go. No card has a button, so the only action on the page is the small blue "Start free" in the top-right corner, back where the eye started. The page is clean and calm, but it gives no direction. It reads as a template that hasn't been finished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a SaaS pricing page for a scheduling or booking tool. It needs to help a small business or team pick the right plan and start signing up.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. It shows three prices but doesn't help anyone choose between them or take a step toward buying. The biggest thing in the way is that the cards are a dead end: there's no action on any plan and no plan is recommended. The pricing copy is also too thin to choose on.

**What would move it most**

1. **No plan has a button, so choosing a plan leads nowhere** (defect). In all three cards, the last feature line is followed by empty white space. A visitor who decides "Team is for me" has nothing to click. They have to go back to the header's "Start free", which doesn't say which plan it starts or whether it's a trial. Norman's point about signifiers applies: nothing shows where to act. Give each card its own button that names what happens, such as "Start 14-day Team trial". Make one of them the filled primary and the other two outlined. Then the header button and the card buttons don't compete, and the page has one main action.

2. **Three equal cards, so nothing tells the viewer which plan to pick** (convention: one element dominates; Tschichold, *The New Typography*). The cards have the same size, border, weight and colour. Only the numbers differ. People who don't know which plan to choose stall here. Make one plan, probably Team, clearly the first thing seen: give it a tinted or accent border, a "Most teams pick this" label and the filled button. Set the other two back, so the order of importance is plain.

3. **The plans can't be compared, and the price lines can be read two ways** (defect). Each card lists three different features: Solo has "Email reminders" and Team has "Reports". Does Team include a booking page and reminders? Does Business include shared availability? As written, a viewer could fairly assume they don't. Start each higher tier with "Everything in Solo, plus:", or show the shared features as one comparison. "$29 / month" is also ambiguous. It could be a flat price for the team or a price per user. A buyer commits on that number and will later hold you to it, so the wrong reading costs either a sale or a support ticket. Write "per month, whole team" or "per user / month", and add whether it's billed monthly or yearly. "Calendars" needs the same fix: say whose calendars, e.g. "1 person's calendar" or "Up to 10 team members".

4. **The copy could sit on any SaaS page** (taste, but it costs you here). "Simple plans for teams of every size" tells the viewer nothing about what Tidewell does or why it's worth $29. "Solo / Team / Business" is the default naming, and there's no product noun anywhere on the page. Use the subtitle to land a claim on the viewer, e.g. "Let clients book you without the email back-and-forth." Keep the useful part, "Change or cancel whenever you like", and move it under the buttons, where it lowers the risk of clicking.

**Smaller things**
- Nav: "Pricing" looks the same as the other links, so the menu doesn't show where you are. Mark the current page with one cue, such as weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- Subtitle: it's centred over two lines and ends on a short runt, "you like." Rebreak it or set a narrower measure so it sits on one line, or two balanced ones (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- Bottom third: about 230 px of empty space under the cards looks left over, not planned. Use it for an FAQ, billing note or comparison link, or pull the content up.
- Nav: "Product" and "Features" look like they point to overlapping things. Consider merging them.
- Feature lines (grey, about 15 px) look slightly lighter than ideal. Check that their contrast against white is at least 4.5:1.

**What's working**
- The type scale is tight: heading, price, plan name, and body with a small "/ month". Each size has a clear job, and the large bold prices against the light "/ month" are the right kind of contrast. Keep this when you add buttons and badges.
- The cards share one interval and one internal program (name, price, list), so the comparison reads as a set. Keep that structure when you emphasise one plan.
- Restrained colour: blue appears only on the logo and the action. Keep it reserved for actions when you add the card buttons.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- How the three cards stack on a phone, and whether the plan you recommend comes first in that stacked order.
