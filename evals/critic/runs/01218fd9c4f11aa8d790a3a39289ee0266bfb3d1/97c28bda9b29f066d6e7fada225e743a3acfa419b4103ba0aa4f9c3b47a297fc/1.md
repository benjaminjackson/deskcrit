**First read.** My eye goes to the bold serif headline "Harbour Lights festival". From there it drops into three grey-ruled columns of text that look the same, and nothing in them gives it a second place to stop. It reads as a page from a local newsletter or a town website. It feels calm and well set, but it gives you no way in: to find out when or where anything happens, you have to read every sentence.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web or print notice for a small town festival, aimed at local families. Its job is to tell them what is on, when and where, and to recruit volunteers.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is competent, but the page fails as a notice. Every fact a visitor needs, such as days, times, places and deadlines, is buried mid-sentence in three paragraphs that look the same. A skimmer leaves without the schedule, and the request for volunteers goes nowhere.

**What would move it most**

1. **The three columns are three different events, but nothing labels them.** Column 1 is an overview, column 2 is the lantern walk and column 3 is the pie contest. With no subheads, they look like one article poured into three columns, and you only learn what a column is about by reading its first line. People skim notices like this for "what, when, where". Here they get running prose with the answers inside clauses: "at dusk", "by one o'clock", "at four", "Sunday afternoon", "the long hall behind the church". *Defect* (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web": text people skim needs headings and chunking). **Fix:** give each column a short head naming the event, such as "Lantern walk" or "Pie contest". Pull the day, time and place out of the paragraph into a line of their own under the head, set in bold or a different weight rather than a new size. Keep the prose under it for the flavour.

2. **The page has no "when" at the top, and the ask goes nowhere.** The headline names the festival but not the date. "First weekend of September" sits in line 3 of column 1 with no year and no actual dates. The volunteer request ("Volunteers are still needed…") ends the first column with no contact, link, time or place, so a reader who wants to help can't do anything about it. The page also mentions a children's boat race and then never covers it. A family that came for that event learns nothing more. *Defect.* **Fix:** put the dates and the place directly under the headline, e.g. "Sat 6 – Sun 7 September · Old market square". Make the volunteer ask a separate, findable item with one exact action, for example "Email X to take a shift handing out programmes". Either give the boat race its own column with its time and place, or remove it.

3. **The spacing between text and rules is uneven, so the grid looks off.** Column 1's text ends about 20 px before the first rule. Columns 2 and 3 start only about 11 px after their rules, so the text sits tight against the line on its left and loose on its right. The rules also stop at the bottom of column 1, below the shorter columns 2 and 3, and the lower 40% of the page is empty. *Convention* (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: columns are separated by one fixed interval). **Fix:** centre each rule in an equal gutter about one line-height wide, or drop the rules and use the space alone. Once there are subheads the rules aren't needed to separate the columns.

**Smaller things.**
- Column 1 ends on "seagulls." alone on its last line, a near-runt. Rebreak the line or edit the text.
- The baselines look as if they line up across the three columns. Keep that when you add subheads by giving every head the same height.
- The headline uses sentence case ("festival") while the body treats the full name as a proper noun. Choose one: "Harbour Lights Festival" or keep "festival" lowercase everywhere.

**What's working.**
- The body text is a well-chosen reading serif. It is near-black on white, with generous leading and a measure of roughly 45–50 characters (inside Bringhurst's range), so it is easy to read once someone starts. Keep it.
- The type scale is two sizes with a large step between them. That is clean. Add the subheads and key facts through weight, not a third or fourth size.
- The copy has warmth and specific local detail ("the pie that makes the judges laugh", "guard the cake tent against hungry seagulls"). That is what makes it feel like this town and not any town. Keep it in the body text under the new structure.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether this is a web page: if so, check that the three columns stack into one at narrow widths and that the rules don't end up as stray horizontal or vertical lines.
