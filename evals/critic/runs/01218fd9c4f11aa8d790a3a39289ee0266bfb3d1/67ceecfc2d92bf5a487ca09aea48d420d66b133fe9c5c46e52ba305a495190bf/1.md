**First read.** The eye goes to "Help for ferry passengers", then splits between two equally weighted columns and reads left to right. It looks like a plain, sensible information page: calm and clear, a little bare, with a large empty area below the right-hand column. Nothing in it looks like it was designed for the moment someone actually needs help.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web or kiosk help page for local ferry passengers: practical tips for boarding, and how to report a problem.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The type is readable, the structure is simple and the copy is plain. The biggest problem is the right-hand column. Its heading promises help "if something goes wrong", but what follows is a slow complaint process with no way to get help now. The list typography also blurs where each item starts.

**What would move it most**

1. **"If something goes wrong" promises more than the column delivers** (defect). Someone who has missed the last boat, lost a child on the quay or been hurt on the ramp will read this heading as meant for them. They then get "Write down the date and time of your crossing…" and a note-box at the quay office. The heading invites an urgent reading, and the steps only serve a later complaint. Rename the heading to what the steps actually do, for example "To report a problem after your crossing". Then add one line above the steps for the urgent case: who to ask right now (a crew member, the quay office) and an emergency number. The steps also stop at "leave your note". Say what happens next, and by when, so the person knows the note isn't going into a void.

2. **The bullets and numbers don't hang, so the list's left edge is ragged** (convention: Bringhurst, Hochuli on hanging punctuation). In "Before you board", each bullet sits at the margin with a wide gap before the text. The second and third lines then wrap back under the bullet. So the left edge alternates between bullet, indented text and wrapped text, and the start of each item is harder to find when skimming. The numbered list has the same problem with a different gap ("1. Write" with one space), so the two columns also set their lists differently. Hang the markers outside the text edge so every line of text starts on one vertical, and give bullets and numbers the same marker-to-text gap.

3. **The copy sends the reader somewhere else for the one detail they need** (defect). Step 3 says "send it to the address on the noticeboard in the shelter". Someone reading this page at home can't see that noticeboard. Print the address, or the email, on the page. The same applies, more mildly, to "the last boat leaves before dusk" and "once an hour". These are the facts people plan around and hold the operator to, but "dusk" isn't a time. If the timetable allows, give the last sailing time, or link straight to the timetable, rather than describing it.

**Smaller things.**
- "Before you board" mixes two jobs: tips for everyone (timetable, exact change) and rules for some passengers (bicycles, prams). A sub-label or a reordered list would let cyclists and parents find their line without reading all four.
- Bullet 2 is about 3.5 lines long, and its reason clause ("since the machine is slow to give change when the morning queue is long") could be cut to "the machine is slow to give change".
- The left column runs well past the right, which leaves a dead block bottom-right. That's fine on a scrolling web page. On a fixed kiosk or printed sheet, balance the columns or put the short one first.

**What's working.**
- Line length is about 55 to 60 characters, and the leading is generous. Body text reads comfortably (within Bringhurst's 45 to 75 range).
- The type scale is clean: three sizes (title, bold section heads, body) with clear steps between them, and weight does the work at the section-head level. Keep it.
- The copy is written to "you" and leads each item with the action ("Check", "Bring", "Wheel", "Ask"), with the reason attached. That's the right voice for this audience.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the two columns reflow on a phone. If the complaint steps end up below all four boarding tips, the urgent reader scrolls past everything else first, which is another reason to put the "need help now" line near the top.
