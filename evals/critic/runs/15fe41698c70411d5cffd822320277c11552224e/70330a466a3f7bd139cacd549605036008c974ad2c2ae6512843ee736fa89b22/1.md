**Verdict.** It does its job well. The eye has a clear path, and the copy is specific and concrete. The one real weak spot is the floor plan, the piece's main picture. It shows desk status with color alone, and the colors run opposite to what most people expect. So the picture that's meant to make the product obvious takes a second look to decode.

**What would move it most**
1. **The floor plan's colors read backwards and have no key** (defect). In the panel on the right, the sage-green tiles are the taken desks and the white dashed tiles (D3, E6, F5) are the free ones. You only learn that by counting them against "18 desks, 3 free". Green usually means available (Apple HIG and Material: green signals success, the "go" state), so a quick look says "almost everything is open", which is the reverse of the truth. Nothing on the plan says what the dark E4 tile means either. You have to connect it to "Desk E4… Booked for Tuesday" below. WCAG 1.4.1 asks for a second cue whenever color carries meaning. A host judging the product from this picture is decoding a map instead of seeing their own floor. Fix: put a small key or labels on the plan for free, booked and yours. Then either make the free desks the strong, positive state and the booked ones gray or neutral, or keep these colors and let the labels carry the meaning.

**Smaller things.**
- Free-desk tiles: the white fill and pale dashed border look below 3:1 against the cream panel (WCAG 1.4.11), and these are the desks the picture is about. Give them a firmer edge or a stronger fill.
- Headline breaks: "book a / desk" pulls the article away from its noun, and "home" sits alone on line 3. Break the lines at phrase boundaries, for example "Let members book / a desk before they / leave home" (Bringhurst 2.4.2), or widen the headline box.
- Copy, "Quiet rows kept quiet": "calls and meetings book somewhere else" has no person in it, since calls don't book anything. Put the people back, so the line says who does the booking and where, and what the host gets from it.
- Copy, "Desk E4, by the window": the plan doesn't show a window, so the label points at something the viewer can't see. Either mark the window side on the plan or drop the phrase.

**What's working.**
- **The two calls to action.** "Add your first floor" names one exact first step. "Walk through a sample floor" lets a doubtful host judge the product for themselves instead of taking the claim on trust. Keep both, and keep their order and styling.
- **The body copy compares against what the host has now.** "The front desk stops keeping a paper list" measures the product against the tool the viewer already uses, which is the strongest kind of claim. Don't lose this line in edits.
- **One dark teal for "the thing that matters".** The booked E4 tile and the primary button share the same deep teal, and almost everything else on the page is muted. That creates the eye path. Keep that color for those two jobs only.
