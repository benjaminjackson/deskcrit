**First read.** My eye goes to "Plan and billing" first, then to the teal-outlined Garden card, which is the only colored shape above the fold except the Save button. The page reads as the account settings for a community-garden management tool, written for the committee member who handles money. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. It is also a bit anonymous, and I couldn't tell what I'm committing to when I click Save.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this lets a garden's treasurer or committee member see and change their subscription tier, keep billing contacts up to date, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and competent, but the page doesn't do the most consequential part of its job: changing a paid plan safely. You can't tell which plan you're on now, what Save will do to it, or what you'd gain or lose by switching. That is the main problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **Changing plans has no clear commit and no clear "current" state (defect).** The plan cards sit above a divider. Save changes / Cancel sit at the bottom of the Billing details section, under the checkbox, so by position they belong to the email and address fields. A reader can take that two ways:
   - Save commits the plan too, so clicking Network and then Save might switch to $60 a month at once.
   - Save only stores the billing fields, so a newly picked plan silently doesn't take effect.

   Either way the reader guesses wrong some of the time, and here a wrong guess costs money. Nothing marks Garden as the plan you're on now rather than one you just clicked. Nothing says when a new price starts or how the current month is handled. Norman's natural mapping puts a control next to the thing it changes, and Nielsen's first heuristic asks the screen to show the current state.

   Direction: label the current plan in words on its card. Then either give plan changes their own action next to the cards, or move Save so it plainly covers both sections. In both cases, before the change commits, state the new price and when it starts. The maker has to supply the billing timing and proration facts.
2. **The three plan cards can't be compared (defect).** Each card describes its tier on different terms:
   - Allotment: "One site, up to 40 plots"
   - Garden: "Up to 150 plots and a waiting list", which gives no site count
   - Network: "Up to 10 sites, one member list", which gives no plot count

   So a reader can't tell whether Allotment drops the waiting list, or whether Network allows 150 plots per site or in total. That matters for this garden: the sidebar shows 12 people on the Waiting list, and the page never says whether downgrading would remove them. Norman's conceptual model asks the screen to make the structure clear enough to form a correct picture. Direction: give every card the same attributes in the same order (sites, plots, waiting list, member list). Show the limit or "—" where a tier lacks something, so the differences line up down the row.
3. **Invoices promises 12 and shows 3, with no way to reach the rest or open one (defect, common sense).** The "12" badge next to Invoices raises the question and the table never answers it. There's no "show all" control. WF-0212 and the other invoice numbers don't look like links, and there's no download action. For a treasurer, getting an invoice to file or forward is likely the main reason to visit this section. Direction: make each invoice open or download from its row, and add a visible control for the full list. Or drop the count if three rows are meant to be the whole view.

**Smaller things.**
- The borders on the text fields, the currency select and the unselected plan cards look pale gray, possibly under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for component edges. Measure the field border against white, and darken it if it falls short.
- None of the fields are marked required or optional. Every field is filled in here, but someone clearing the address can't tell whether it's allowed. Add an asterisk with a one-line legend, or "(optional)" after the optional labels (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- The emoji in the count badges (👥 🌱 🔔 🧾) are the only playful, multicolor marks on an otherwise restrained page, so they read as pasted in. This is taste: either use monochrome icons drawn in one line style, or plain counts.

**What's working.**
- One teal does one job. It appears only on the selected plan, the checked box and the primary button, so the eye's two stops are the right ones. Keep the palette this tight.
- The invoice table is set properly. Amounts are right-aligned with figures that line up by place value. The rules are hairline-thin. Status is spelled out ("Paid") rather than shown by a colored dot.
- The structure is sound. The sidebar's highlighted "Plan and billing" row matches the page title. Labels sit above their fields. All the content shares one left edge and one right edge.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What Save actually does to the plan, and whether a confirmation follows. This is the crux of finding 1, so check it in the live flow.
- Focus rings on the radios, fields and buttons.
- How the three plan cards and the two-up email/currency row reflow at narrow widths.

- What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes

- Charts and data: skipped, no chart; the table is plain lookup
