**First read.** The eye lands on the four big figures across the top, then jumps to the only saturated block on the page, Monday's blue bar, then drops to the table. It reads right away as a monthly overview for a small shop's support desk. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, if a bit stock.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the support lead's monthly check: how busy the desk was, how fast and how well it answered, and what's stuck now.

**Verdict.** The structure is good and does its job at a glance. What gets in the way is trust: the line chart doesn't match the headline figure it sits under, and the "Oldest open tickets" table lists a solved ticket. A support lead checks exactly these numbers, and when they disagree with each other, the lead stops believing the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The line chart contradicts the 1,284 above it.** The top-left card says 1,284 tickets opened in November, which is about 43 a day. The November line in "Tickets opened per day" climbs from about 27 to 47 and looks like it averages in the high 30s. That works out nearer 1,100 for the month. The gray October line looks about 10 a day below October's stated 1,192 as well. The weekday bars, by contrast, add up to roughly 1,284, so the line chart is the odd one out. There's also a second problem. The chart is titled "per day" but has only about eleven points for thirty days, and its date ticks (1, 10, 19, 30 Nov) are unevenly spaced. Either the points are weekly or every-third-day sums mislabeled as daily, or the data is a placeholder. A viewer who does the sum concludes that one of the two figures is wrong. Defect: scales and labels must be honest and consistent (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*). Fix: plot one point per day from the same source as the card, or retitle the chart to match what it actually plots. Then space the ticks evenly.
2. **A solved ticket sits under "Oldest open tickets."** The third row, "Refund for a returned kettle," carries a blue "Solved" pill. Either the table isn't filtered to open tickets or the heading doesn't describe it. In both cases the lead can't tell whether this list is their real backlog. "Waiting on customer" raises the same question: does a ticket that's waiting on the customer count as open for the desk? Defect. Fix: make the heading and the filter agree. If the list really is only open tickets, the solved row has to go. If it's meant to include other states, the heading should say so.

**Smaller things.**
- **Badge glow:** the blurred colored glow behind the sidebar count badges (24, 7, 3) and the table's status pills adds fake depth that nothing else on this flat, bordered page has. It makes the labels look slightly out of focus. Drop the glow and keep the flat tint. (Taste; it does no job here.)
- **Weekday chart has no scale:** "Tickets by weekday" has no value axis, and only Monday's bar carries a number (262). You can't read Tuesday through Sunday except as "less than Monday." Label every bar directly, or add an axis (Tufte; Few).
- **Legend instead of labels:** the line chart's legend sits at the top right, so you have to look back and forth to decode it. Put "November" and "October" at the right-hand ends of the lines (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*).
- **Chart titles name topics, not findings:** both chart titles say what is measured, not what happened. The page already shows the story, with November running above October all month and Monday the peak day, but leaves you to find it. A title or short caption that states it would save that look. (Taste, but it's how a summary page earns its place.)

**What's working.**
- **"in October" under every figure:** each big number has its comparison right underneath ("42 min / 55 min in October"). You can tell at a glance whether things got better without hunting for a baseline. Keep this pattern.
- **Restrained color in the weekday chart:** everything is gray except the bar that matters, Monday, in the single accent blue. It's the clearest emphasis on the page, and it's done with almost nothing.
- **Plain labels:** "Happy with the answer" instead of "CSAT," and "First reply" in minutes. The labels are in the reader's words, not the help-desk tool's.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the sidebar badges and table rows are clickable and lead to the right filtered lists. Whether the date range at the top right ("1 to 30 November 2026") can be changed.
