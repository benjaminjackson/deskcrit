**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing," then to the teal-bordered "Small fleet" card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It's the billing settings page of an ordering product for food trucks. It feels calm, orderly and competent, and fairly stock: a default SaaS settings layout.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a food-truck owner or manager uses this page to check their plan, change it, update billing details and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and well put together, but it's vague at the one point where it takes the owner's money. The page doesn't mark which plan they're on. It doesn't say whether "Save changes" also changes the plan, or what happens to their bill if it does. The craft is fine. The fixes are about what the page tells the owner before they commit.

**What would move it most**
1. **Changing plan isn't a clear, separate action.** The plan cards sit at the top. The only commit button is under Billing details, beside the email and address fields. If an owner clicks "Commissary" and then "Save changes," they can't tell whether they just upgraded to $129 a month or only saved an email address. The reverse is also possible: they pick a plan, never scroll to the button, and think it took effect. A plan change is a commitment the owner will hold you to, and a step that can be read two ways here is a defect. Norman calls this natural mapping: a control belongs next to the thing it changes, and this button sits with the billing form, not the plans. Fix: give the plan change its own action, in or right under the plan row, labeled with what it does. Leave "Save changes" for billing details only. *Defect.*
2. **Nothing marks the current plan or explains what a switch costs.** The selected radio and teal border could mean "your plan" or "your new choice," and the page says neither. Nothing says when a change takes effect, whether it's prorated, or when the first charge at the new price lands. The owner has to work that out or take it on trust before risking a bill. The invoice table quietly shows the last switch, from One truck to Small fleet. That proves the page knows the history, but it doesn't say so where the decision is made. This breaks Nielsen's first heuristic, visibility of system status, and his tenth, help shown near the step it concerns. Fix: put a plain "Current plan" label on the card the owner pays for. Next to the plan action, add one line on timing and billing. Only you know what that line says (immediate or next cycle, prorated or not). *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Invoices: each row lists an invoice number but offers no way to view or download it, and a copy for their books is usually why an owner opens this section. Make the invoice number, or a "Download PDF" link, the action on each row. (Common sense.)
- Billing details: "Billing email" and "Currency" share a row, but they aren't a related pair, so the form reads in a Z shape. Stack them, or pair currency with something it belongs to. (Taste.)
- Billing details: nothing says which fields are required. With every field prefilled it costs little, but mark the required ones or label the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g).
- Input borders and the unselected plan cards' borders look like pale gray. Measure them against white for WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1, and darken them if they fall short.
- Currency selector: prices show in dollars right above it, and the page doesn't say whether changing currency changes the price or only the invoices. Add a few words under the field.

**What's working.**
- The plan cards each give a name, the deciding limit (trucks, menu, kitchen) and a price, in three short lines that are easy to compare side by side. Keep that structure.
- The type scale is tight: page title, section heading, body, and a gray secondary size, with weight doing the finer work. Nothing collides.
- The invoice table is clean: amounts are right-aligned and line up, the rules are light, there's no chart junk, and the status is a word rather than a color.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether selecting a plan card does anything right away, such as a confirm dialog or a charge preview. If it does, part of finding 2 is already handled.
- Focus states on the cards and fields.
