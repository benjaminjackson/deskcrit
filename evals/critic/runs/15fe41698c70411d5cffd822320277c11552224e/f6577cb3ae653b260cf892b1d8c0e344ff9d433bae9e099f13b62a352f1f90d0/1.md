**First read.** The eye goes to the row of four big numbers, then straight to the one saturated blue thing on the screen, the Saturday bar, then over to the blue November line. It reads as the monthly overview screen for a small wine shop's back office (Corkline, used by Two Rivers Wine). It feels calm, tidy, and trustworthy, close to a well-made stock admin template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a shop owner opens this to see how the month is going against last month and what to act on, such as stock, staffing, or promotions.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The layout, type, and restraint are solid, and the screen is easy to scan. The biggest problem is that the charts and labels leave the owner to guess what is being measured and why something is highlighted. The most eye-catching mark on the page explains itself least.

**What would move it most**
1. **The charts don't say what they measure or what they found.** *(Defect.)*
   - **Bottles by weekday:** there is no value axis, and only Saturday has a number (148). The owner can't read Monday or Sunday without guessing from bar height. They also can't tell whether these are November totals or averages per weekday. That matters because November 2026 has five Sundays and five Mondays but only four of every other day, so totals would flatter those two days.
   - **Web shop visitors:** it doesn't say visitors per what. It plots about 11 points across 30 days, so it isn't plainly daily, and "600" has no unit to anchor it.
   - **Titles:** both titles name only the topic. Saturday is painted the strongest blue on the screen with no words saying why. It could read as "today", "selected", or "best day".

   A chart should never leave the reader guessing what a number measures (Tufte, ch. 2; Few, *Show Me the Numbers*). Fix:
   - Give the bar chart a light value axis or a value on every bar.
   - State the unit: "per day", "total", or "average per Saturday".
   - Let each title or a one-line caption say the finding the highlight is already pointing at (common sense, not a cited rule).
2. **Two labels the owner will act on can be read two ways.** *(Defect.)*
   - **"Best-selling wines":** the list is ranked by revenue. Barolo 2019 sold 9 bottles yet comes third, ahead of whatever sold the 4th-most bottles. An owner reading "best-selling" as most bottles will reorder the wrong wine. Either rename the heading to say it ranks by revenue, or rank by bottles.
   - **"Wine club share 27%":** this could be a share of revenue, of orders, or of customers. Each one leads to a different decision. Name it ("…of revenue" or whichever it is).

   (WCAG 2.4.6: a heading must describe what its section holds.)
3. **The KPI row makes the owner do arithmetic to find the news.** *(Taste.)* Every comparison line ("$21,970 in October") is a raw number in small gray type. The fact that all four figures went up, and revenue by about 13%, is something the reader has to work out. The comparison is there, which is good. Add the change itself next to each big number, as a signed percent or difference in a weight or color one step above the gray line, so you see the month's direction at a glance before reading.

**Smaller things.**
- Line chart legend (top right, "November / October"): put the labels at the right-hand ends of the lines so the eye doesn't travel back and forth (Tufte; Wilke).
- Line chart x-axis ticks at 1, 10, 19, 30 Nov are spaced 9, 9, 11 days apart. Use even intervals, or mark only the start and end.
- Best-selling table shows only three rows inside a full-width card, with a wide empty gap between Wine and Producer. Either show more rows or narrow the table so the columns sit closer to the figures they belong to.

**What's working.**
- **Restraint with color:** everything is gray except one blue for "this period / the point". Keep exactly one accent when you add labels and changes; don't bring in a second hue for up/down unless it truly carries status.
- **The KPI strip:** four figures, one size, each with last month's figure beneath. It's the right content in the right place at the top.
- **The table is set properly:** right-aligned tabular figures, light rules, gray header row. It reads cleanly and should stay as is.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The "1 to 30 November 2026" range at top right looks like plain text. If it's meant to switch periods, it needs to look like a control. Check in the live product.
- Check whether the month is complete or month-to-date. Comparing a partial November with a full October would make every figure look worse than it is.
