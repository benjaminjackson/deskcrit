**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing," drops to the teal-outlined Salon card, then jumps to the teal "Save changes" button, which is the strongest color on the page. It reads as the billing settings for a pet-grooming salon app, set up for an owner or front-desk manager. It feels calm, orderly and competent, and also stock: it could be the billing page of any SaaS product.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a salon owner comes here to check or change their plan, keep billing details correct, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and mostly works, but money is handled loosely. The user can't tell which plan they're on now, and changing plan seems to happen through a generic "Save changes" button that sits under a different section and says nothing about what the change will cost or when it starts. The invoice list shows that invoices exist but gives no way to open one.

**What would move it most**
1. **Plan change is a commitment hidden behind a form save.** The plan cards are radio buttons in the top section. The only action is "Save changes," which sits under Billing details, two sections below the plans. Someone who clicks "Salon group" can't tell whether that click alone changes their plan, whether "Save changes" commits it, or whether Save only covers the email and address fields it sits under. The wrong reading costs real money: a $50-a-month jump they didn't mean to make, or a downgrade they think happened and didn't. Norman's natural mapping says a control belongs next to the thing it changes, and this one doesn't. *Defect.* Fix direction: give the plan its own action next to the cards, named for what it does (an edit like "Switch to Salon group"). Before the user commits, say when the change takes effect and how the next charge is affected. The maker has to supply those facts. Keep "Save changes" for the billing fields only.
2. **The current plan isn't marked as current.** The Salon card is selected, but the selection can mean "your plan" or "what you just clicked," and the page never says which. There's also no next billing date or amount anywhere, though that's the number an owner most often comes to a billing page to check. The invoice history hints at the answer, but making people work it out from the table is recall where recognition should do the job (Nielsen heuristic 6). *Defect.* Fix direction: put a plain "Current plan" label on the active card and show the next charge (date and amount) near the plan section. A billing page normally shows the payment method too, so add it here unless it lives elsewhere.
3. **Invoices can't be opened.** "CL-3307" and the other invoice rows are set in the same plain gray-black as the dates and amounts. Nothing looks clickable, and there's no download or view control. An owner who needs an invoice for their accountant reaches the list and stops. *Defect.* Fix direction: make each invoice number a styled link or add a "Download" action to each row, so it's clearly something to click.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and Currency share a row, but they aren't a related pair, so the form reads in a Z instead of straight down. Put Currency on its own row or move it near the plan prices it affects. This is taste and common sense, not a cited rule.
- None of the three fields marks whether it's required. Mark the required ones with an asterisk and explain it once, or add "(optional)" after the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g).
- "Up to 3 salons, one pet list" is internal shorthand. A stranger has to guess that it means pet records are shared across locations. Say that in plain words.

**What's working.**
- The type is quiet and controlled. It uses one family and about four sizes with clear jobs (page title, section heading, body, gray secondary text), and section headings differ by weight as well as size. Keep this scale.
- The invoice table is set well. Amounts are right-aligned in matching figures, and it uses only thin row rules with no boxes, so the column of prices scans in one look.
- The single teal accent marks the selected plan and the primary button and nothing else, so color reliably means "this one." The selected card also has a radio dot, so the selection doesn't depend on color alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the whole plan card is clickable or only the roughly 12 px radio dot. If only the dot is, it's below the 24 px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8). Check in a browser.
- Focus rings on the cards, fields and buttons.
- What "Cancel" does on a settings page that isn't a dialog.
