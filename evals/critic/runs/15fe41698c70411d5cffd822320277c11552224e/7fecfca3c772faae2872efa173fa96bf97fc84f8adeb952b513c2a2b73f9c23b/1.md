**First read.** The eye goes first to the dark blue Saturday bar, the one solid block of strong color on the screen, and then to the row of big blue numbers along the top. It reads as a monthly bookings overview for a pottery studio: calm, tidy and competent. It also looks like a template. Almost everything is the same blue, so once you've seen the Saturday bar nothing else tells you where to look.

**What it's for.** Since I wasn't told, I'm assuming this is the studio owner's monthly check: how November went compared with October, which days and classes sell, and where to act.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly well built, but it reports the month without telling the owner what to take from it. Worse, the daily chart doesn't seem to add up to the headline seat count, and that undermines every number on the page. Fix the numbers first, then make the page say what changed.

**What would move it most**

1. **The daily chart and the headline figures don't seem to agree (defect).** In the "Daily bookings" chart the November line runs from about 13 to 23 per day, roughly 18 on average. Over 30 days that's about 550, but the first card says 386 seats booked. October's gray line has the same problem: about 14 a day, or about 430 for the month, against "352 in October." Two things could explain it:
   - The chart counts a different unit. The chart says "bookings" while the cards say "seats," and the viewer can't tell whether those are the same.
   - The line isn't daily at all. It has only about 11 points across the month, not 30.

   I'm reading values off the chart by eye, so check them against the source data. If the gap is real, an owner who adds things up will stop trusting the whole screen, and that costs more than anything else here.
   - **Fix:** make the chart and the cards count the same thing and name it the same way: either seats in both, or bookings in both with the unit stated.
   - If the line really is daily, plot all 30 days. If it's grouped, say how.

2. **Blue does every job, so it marks nothing (defect).** The same blue is used for:
   - the page title and the panel titles
   - the side menu, the KPI numbers and the date range
   - the legend text, the table headers and the Revenue column ($5,760, $3,240, $2,430)
   - the November line and the Saturday bar

   This causes three problems:
   - The two places where blue is meant to mean "look here" (the November line and the Saturday bar) have to compete with every heading on the page.
   - The table headers and the Revenue values look alike.
   - On a screen, blue text reads as a link. Revenue figures in blue look clickable, and so does "1 to 30 November 2026" in the top right, which is either a period picker that doesn't look like a control or plain text that looks like one.

   The convention is one color per role (Apple Human Interface Guidelines, "Color"; Material Design color roles).
   - **Fix:** set headings, table headers and figures in the dark text color, and keep blue for the data you want to stand out and for things that can actually be clicked.
   - If the date range can be changed, make it look like a control. If it can't, set it as plain text.

3. **The page shows data but doesn't say what it means (defect, plus taste on the titles).**
   - **Bar chart:** "Seats booked by weekday" has no value axis and only one label (92, on Saturday). The other six bars can only be compared by guessing their heights, so the owner can see Saturday is biggest but can't tell whether Tuesday is a quarter of it or a third. Charts should carry a readable scale or direct values (Tufte, ch. 2; Few).
   - **Chart titles:** both name a topic ("Daily bookings," "Seats booked by weekday") rather than a finding. Stating what each chart found would spare the owner the reading. That's my taste, not a published rule.
   - **KPI cards:** each one gives October's figure ("352 in October"), which meets Few's rule, but none shows the change. The owner has to subtract four times to learn that November was up.
   - **Fixes:**
     - Put the value on every bar, or add a light axis.
     - Show the change next to each October figure.
     - Let each chart title say what it found. Only the studio knows which finding matters.

**Smaller things**
- **"Fullest classes":** "fullest" suggests how full each class is (a percentage), but the table ranks by seat count. Either rename the panel to match what it ranks or add a fill-rate column.
- **Line chart ticks:** 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov are 9, 9 and 11 days apart. Space them evenly (Cairo's even-tick rule; Tufte).
- **Line chart legend:** the legend sits apart from the lines, and its "October" text is blue while the October line is gray. Label each line at its right end instead (Tufte; Wilke).
- **Grid:** the split between the two charts (around x≈900) and the Teacher column (around x≈782) don't line up with any divider in the KPI row, so the lower half looks placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann). Take them from the KPI row's four columns.
- **Period control:** for a page that's all about one month, there's no visible way to change the month. Add one, or make the date range that control (see finding 2).

**What's working**
- **Gray for context:** October in gray against November in blue, and gray bars against the one blue bar. Once blue is freed from headings (finding 2), this will be the strongest thing on the page. Keep it.
- **KPI row:** four short, plainly worded labels, each with last month's figure underneath. The comparison is already there; it only needs the change shown.
- **The table:** numbers are right-aligned under right-aligned headers, rules are light, and the Teacher column is useful. It's easy to scan.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the date range, the table rows or the revenue figures can actually be clicked, and what hover and focus look like on them. That matters here because finding 2 depends on it.
