**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing," then to the teal-bordered "Full gym" card, the only colored thing above the button. Next it drops through the form to the teal "Save changes" button, then to the invoice table. This is the settings page of software for running a climbing gym, viewed by the owner or front-desk manager. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also fairly generic.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a gym owner comes here to check or change the plan, keep billing contact details current, and find past invoices for their books.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, competent screen that mostly does its job. The real problem is the plan picker. Changing a plan commits the viewer to a new price, yet here it's wired into the same "Save changes" as the email field. Once another card is clicked, nothing shows which plan the gym is actually on, or what saving will cost and when.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan change has no commit of its own.** The plan radio cards sit at the top. The only button is "Save changes," below the billing email, currency and address fields, after a divider and a new heading. A viewer who clicks "Gym group" can't tell from the layout whether "Save changes" switches the plan or only saves the address. A viewer who edits the address and saves might switch the plan without meaning to. It's a $79 to $159 difference, the kind of thing people later hold you to. Norman's natural mapping (*The Design of Everyday Things*) says a control belongs next to the thing it changes. Here one button governs two unrelated groups, and they read as one form even though they're two jobs. Direction: give the plan its own action and confirmation, next to the cards, and keep "Save changes" for billing details only. **Defect.**
2. **The current plan isn't marked, and the price change isn't explained.** The selected radio is the only sign of the current plan. Click another card and that information is gone. The viewer has to remember what they're on (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). Nothing near the cards says when a change takes effect or how the current month is charged, and that's exactly what someone asks before upgrading or downgrading (Nielsen 10: short help at the step it concerns). Direction: put a persistent "current plan" label on the card the gym is on. Add one line by the cards saying when a change applies and how it's billed; only you can supply those facts. **Defect.**
3. **The invoices can't be opened, as far as the screen shows.** The invoice table is probably what most visitors to this page come for, at tax time or when the bookkeeper asks. "BS-2093" and the other invoice numbers look like plain text. No row has a download or view control. If the rows are links, nothing signals it. If they aren't, the owner can see an invoice exists but can't get it. Direction: give each invoice a visible way to open or download it, such as an underlined invoice number or a "Download" link at the row's end. **Defect.** (Common sense, backed by Nielsen Norman Group's finding that links styled as static text draw less attention.)

**Smaller things.**
- Space doesn't separate the groups well. "Save changes" and "Cancel" sit about 14 px above the divider before "Invoices," about the same gap as between a label and its field. The plan cards are just as tight against their divider. Leave more space between sections than inside them (Arnheim, proximity).
- The type sizes are too close together. Section headings (~16 px medium), field labels (~14 px semibold) and card sublines (~13 px gray) are a step or two apart, so "Billing details" barely outranks "Billing email." Make section headings a clear step larger, or drop the labels to regular weight. Then keep the 13 and 14 px sizes as one size and separate them by color only.
- Billing email and currency sit side by side but aren't a related pair. Currency reads as part of the email row. Give each its own line, or pair currency with something that belongs to it.
- No fields are marked required or optional. It costs little here because everything is filled in, but on a first setup the viewer won't know what they can skip.
- The plan cards don't describe their tiers in parallel terms. "One wall, 200 members," then "Unlimited members," then "Up to 5 gyms": the third switches from members to gyms, so the viewer can't compare all three on one dimension. State the same things for each tier, in the same order.

**What's working.**
- The plan cards each give the name, one-line limit and price in a fixed order. The selected card carries a teal border and a filled radio, so the choice doesn't depend on color alone. Keep that structure when you add the "current" marker.
- The invoice table is quiet and readable. Amounts are right-aligned, there's no extra ruling, status is spelled out as "Paid," and the Plan column shows the past switch from Bouldering wall to Full gym without any extra element.
- The single teal fill on "Save changes" against the outlined "Cancel" makes the primary action clear. Teal is used sparingly enough that it means "selected or act here" everywhere it appears.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the invoice rows are clickable on hover. Whether focus rings on the radio cards and buttons are visible. Whether picking a plan card asks for confirmation before charging.
