**First read.** The eye goes first to the row of four big numbers at the top, then to the purple Monday bar on the right, which is the only saturated block on the page. It reads at once as an analytics dashboard for one podcast (the show's name, "The Long Table podcast," sits in tiny type at the bottom left). It feels calm, tidy and well made, but nothing on the page tells you whether November was a good month. You have to work that out yourself.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a podcast host checks this monthly to see how the show is doing and which episodes and days work best.

**Verdict.** It's a competent, clean dashboard that shows the numbers but doesn't draw any conclusions from them, and two of its charts aren't what their titles say. The biggest problem is that the main answer, "how did this month go," is left for the reader to calculate.

**What would move it most**

1. **The page never says what changed, so nothing stands out.** The four headline figures (48,920 / 6,214 / 31 min / 62%) are all the same size and weight. Each one sits over a gray "in October" line. To learn that downloads rose about 10%, the reader has to subtract 44,310 from 48,920 in their head, and then do the same for the other three. The chart titles, "Daily downloads" and "Downloads by weekday," only name a topic. Neither says what the chart shows. The October figure is there, so Few's rule about giving metrics a comparison is half met. What's missing is the result of that comparison.
   - **Fix:** show the change itself next to each figure, as a signed difference or percentage, marked up or down. Then make the most important one dominant, or let the page title or a summary line say the month's result in words.
   - **Type:** Defect, because it slows down the one thing the page is for. Putting the takeaway in the chart titles is taste: the source for that rule isn't on the approved list.

2. **"Downloads by weekday" is probably biased by the calendar, and you can't tell what it measures.** There's no axis and no unit, and only Monday has a value (9,860). November 2026 starts on a Sunday and ends on a Monday, so it has five Sundays and five Mondays but only four of every other weekday. If these bars are monthly totals, which 9,860 out of 48,920 suggests, then Monday and Sunday are about 25% taller just because they came up more often. The "Monday is best" highlight could be an artifact of the calendar, and a host might plan releases around it.
   - **Fix:** plot the average per weekday, or say clearly that the bars are totals. Label the values, or add an axis with a unit (Tufte, ch. 2, on clear labeling).
   - **Type:** Defect.

3. **"Daily downloads" isn't daily, and its date axis is uneven.** The November line has 11 points, one about every three days, not 30. The ticks fall at 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov, which are gaps of 9, 9 and 11 days. The "30 Nov" label also looks slightly left of where the line ends. A host looking for the spike after a release day can't find it, and the uneven ticks make the spacing hard to trust.
   - **Fix:** plot one point per day, or rename the chart to match the real interval. Put the ticks at even intervals, such as weekly (Tufte and Cairo on even tick intervals).
   - **Type:** Defect for the mismatched title, convention for the ticks.

**Smaller things**
- The line chart's legend sits at the top right. Put "November" and "October" labels at the right-hand ends of the lines so the reader doesn't have to match colors (Tufte; Wilke).
- The page title "Listens" and the KPI figures look nearly the same size, so the title doesn't read as its own level. Make the title clearly larger or the figures clearly smaller, and use weight for the finer difference.
- The page and nav item are called "Listens," but every number on the page counts downloads, followers or minutes. Use one word, the one hosts actually use (Nielsen, heuristic 2).
- There's no visible way to change the period or compare other months. If the date at the top right is meant to be clickable, it needs to look like a control. If you can't change the period, a monthly check-in turns into a dead end. (Common sense.)
- Taste: purple means "November" in the line chart but "the top day" in the bar chart, right next to it. Give highlights a role that doesn't clash with the series color.

**What's working**
- The color is restrained. The rest of the page is neutral, so the one purple accent and the gray comparison series are easy to tell apart, and the purple-versus-gray difference still shows in grayscale. Keep that discipline when adding up/down markers. Don't bring in red and green across the whole page.
- The table is set well. Numbers are right-aligned in columns that line up, with only hairline row rules. The headers match their columns, and it reads quickly.
- Spacing and grouping are consistent. Card padding, gutters and the left edge all repeat, and every card holds exactly one thing.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the date range and the chart points are interactive (period picker, hover values on the lines). If they are, that partly answers the missing values and period switching, but they still need to look interactive.
