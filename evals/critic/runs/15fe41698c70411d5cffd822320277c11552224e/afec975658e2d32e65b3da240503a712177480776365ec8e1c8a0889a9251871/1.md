**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing", drops to the three plan cards, and stops on the bold prices. The "Small fleet" card with the teal border reads as the current plan. Next comes the teal "Save changes" button, the only spot of saturated color, then the invoice table. This is the settings screen of a point-of-sale or ordering tool for food trucks. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and a little generic.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a food-truck owner uses this screen to see and change their plan, keep billing details current, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid: tidy grid, clear navigation, one primary action. The weak spot is the one place where money is at stake. The screen never says whether picking a plan charges you now or only after Save, or what changing plans will cost. It also leaves out the two things people most often open a billing page for: the next charge and a copy of an invoice.

**What would move it most**

1. **Changing the plan is ambiguous at the moment it commits money.** The plan cards sit at the top, and a divider cuts them off from everything below. The only "Save changes" button sits under Billing details, inside the group that starts below that divider. A user who clicks "Commissary" can't tell what happens. One reading: the plan switches at once and they are now paying $129.00 a month. The other: nothing happens until they press a button that looks like it belongs to the email and address fields. If they guess the first way and the second is true, they leave on the old plan thinking they upgraded. If they guess the second way and the first is true, they get charged for something they were only browsing. Nothing near the cards says when a change takes effect or how the current month is handled (prorated, or charged from the next cycle). **Defect** (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping: a control sits near the thing it changes; Nielsen, heuristic 10, on short help where the task is complex). Direction: make the plan change its own explicit action next to the cards, with a confirmation that states the new price and when it starts. Or, if one Save really covers both, move it so it clearly governs the whole form. Mark the current plan in words as well, such as a "Current plan" tag, not only by the selected radio.

2. **The page leaves out what a billing page is mostly visited for.** Nothing shows the next charge date, the amount, or the card it will go to, and there is no payment method to view or change anywhere on the screen. In the invoice table, the invoice numbers (TR-4471 and so on) are plain text, with no way to view or download a PDF. An owner who needs a receipt for their accountant, or wants to know when $59 leaves their account, has to hunt elsewhere or contact support. **Defect** (Nielsen, heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall). Putting the next need in front of the user is **common sense** here, not a cited rule. Direction: add a short summary at the top (current plan, next charge date and amount, card on file, with a way to change the card), and make each invoice row open or download the invoice. The maker has to supply the real values.

**Smaller things**
- Small type sizes sit too close together. Card helper text looks about 12 px, field labels about 11 px bold, and table headers about 12 px: three sizes doing roughly one job. The plan name (about 16 px) and the price (about 18 px) are also only a step apart. Settle on one small size and separate levels by weight or color (Bringhurst, scale).
- Billing email and Currency share a row but aren't a related pair, so the eye reads across them as if they were. Stack them, or give Currency its own short row. **Taste/common sense.**
- No field is marked required or optional. It costs little on a prefilled settings form, but a user who clears the email field gets no warning in advance. Mark the optional fields, or the required ones with an asterisk explained once (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- "$24.00 a month" and the others: the ".00" adds noise to whole-dollar prices. **Taste.** "300 orders a day" on One truck reads as a hard limit without saying what happens at 301. Say whether it's a cap or a guideline.

**What's working**
- The selected plan is marked twice, by the filled radio and by the teal border, so the selection doesn't depend on color alone. Keep that redundancy when you add a "Current plan" label.
- One strong button (filled teal "Save changes") beside a quiet outlined "Cancel". The primary action is never in doubt, and the teal is used almost nowhere else, which keeps its signal strong.
- Everything in the content area hangs on one left edge and one right edge (cards, fields, divider lines, table). The amounts in the table are right-aligned, so they compare at a glance. Location is clear at both levels: "Settings" is bold in the top bar and "Plan and billing" is tinted in the sidebar.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether selecting a plan card saves instantly or waits for Save; this is the deciding question behind finding 1, so check the live behavior. Also the focus indicators on the radio cards and fields.
