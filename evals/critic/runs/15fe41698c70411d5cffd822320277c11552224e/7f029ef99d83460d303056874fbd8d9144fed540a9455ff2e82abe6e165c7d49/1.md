**What would move it most**
1. **The seat map's colors fight what viewers expect, and nothing explains them.** *Defect.* In the card on the right, 15 seats are sage green, 3 are white with a dashed outline, and B4 is oxblood. On almost every booking map people have used, green means available and red means taken. A first-time viewer will read this hall as mostly open, with B4 blocked. That is the opposite of what it shows: a nearly sold-out night, which is the headline's promise ("Sell every seat in the hall"). Only the small gray "18 seats, 3 free" in the corner corrects that, and it is the last thing the eye reaches. The difference between sold and selected is also carried by hue alone (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1). Your sage green is a brand color, not a status color, but on a seat map it gets read as one (Apple HIG, Color). The fix: make the sold state read as filled or taken without depending on hue. That could mean a muted neutral, or a small key under the map (sold / free / this booking), or both. Keep oxblood for "this booking" only if the caption below clearly ties it to B4.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: "row" sits alone on the third line, so the punchline of the line ends as a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Change the break or the column width so "front row" stays together.
- Grid: the hero splits at about x 652 (roughly halves), and the three feature columns start at 80, 469 and 859 (thirds). The middle column and the image card line up with nothing above or below them (Müller-Brockmann). Put both on one column system, for example a 12-column grid that gives both 6/6 and 4/4/4.
- Copy in the card: "Seat B4, row B" says the row twice. "Seat B4" is enough, or "Row B, seat 4".
- Copy: "Talk to us" is the only ask on the page that names no specific thing. It is a third button competing with "Try a sample plan", which is the stronger secondary ask because it lets a venue judge the product on a hall like their own. Consider making it a text link.
- Spelling and currency: "Centre", "theatres" and "7.30 pm" are British, but the price is in "$". Fine if the market is Australia, New Zealand or Canada. If it's the US, the spelling will look like a typo; if it's the UK, the currency will.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a concrete promise in the audience's own terms ("not just the front row"). The body copy names three real chores the product takes away: drawing the hall, the door list, and phone orders. This copy could only belong to this product.
- Showing a real night ("Fernhill Village Hall, Friday", "The Winter's Tale, 7.30 pm", "$12 paid") in place of an abstract dashboard tells a hall volunteer right away that this is built for them. Keep that level of specificity.
- The palette is restrained (warm off-white, sage, one oxblood accent) and there is one primary button. The feature row below is quiet, and each heading names exactly what it covers.
