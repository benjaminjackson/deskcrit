**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing" first, then right away to the Co-op card in the middle with its teal outline, then down to the solid teal "Save changes" button. It reads as a calm, tidy settings page for a small beekeeping co-op's account. It feels trustworthy and plain, close to a well-made default, and that suits this page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the account settings page where a co-op's treasurer checks or changes the plan, keeps the billing details current, and finds past invoices.

**Verdict.** The page does most of its job and is well built. What's in the way is money. Nothing says whether "Save changes" also switches the plan, when a new plan starts, or how it is charged. The invoice list shows records the treasurer probably can't open.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Save changes" doesn't say what it covers, and the plan cards don't say what switching costs.** The button sits under the billing fields, with a rule between it and the plan cards above. A treasurer who clicks Packer can't tell whether that switch saves at once, waits for "Save changes", or is ignored by it. Nothing on the page says when a new plan starts or how the price difference is billed. The Co-op card is selected, but nothing labels it "current plan", so after a click the screen can't show what is saved versus what is pending. This is a defect. A plan change commits the co-op to a recurring charge, and a control placed away from the thing it changes breaks natural mapping (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*). The status of what's saved isn't visible either (Nielsen, heuristic 1). Fix it in one of two ways:
   - give the plan its own action, right next to the cards, with one short line on when the change takes effect and how it's charged;
   - or clearly scope "Save changes" to the billing details.

   Either way, mark the current plan in words.
2. **The plan descriptions use a different limit for each plan, so they can't be compared.** Backyard is "Up to 10 hives, one seller". Co-op is "Up to 25 members, shared stock". Packer is "Unlimited members and batch labels". The viewer can't tell whether Co-op caps hives, or whether Backyard allows shared stock. These lines set what the co-op is paying for, so this is a defect, not a style note. Fix: state each plan on the same terms (hives, members, sellers, features) so they line up across the three cards. Only the maker knows the real limits.
3. **The invoice list is a dead end.** "PB-0912" and the other invoice numbers look like plain text. There's no download or view control, and a treasurer coming to this table usually wants the PDF for their books. This is common sense rather than a cited rule. Fix: make each invoice open or download from its row, and make that visibly clickable.

**Smaller things.**
- Top navigation: "Settings" is marked as the current page only by slightly darker text. Give it one clear cue, such as weight, an underline or a background (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Text field and plan card borders look like very pale gray. Measure them against the 3:1 non-text contrast minimum (WCAG 1.4.11) and darken them if they fall short.
- "Billing email" and "Currency" share a row but aren't a related pair. A single column would read more cleanly, and the address field already spans the full width. This one is taste.

**What's working.**
- The selected plan is shown by both the filled radio button and the teal border, so the choice doesn't rely on color alone. The teal is used only for the selection, the checkbox and the one primary button, so it always means "this one" or "do this". Keep that restraint.
- There is one primary button and one quieter outlined "Cancel" next to it, which is the right pairing.
- The invoice table is plain and readable: amounts are right-aligned with lined-up figures, rules are light, and there are no extra boxes. The plan history in it (Backyard, then Co-op) helps a treasurer reconcile charges.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether choosing a plan card applies the change, shows a confirmation, or waits for Save. Check this in the live page, since it decides finding 1.
- Focus states on the cards, fields and buttons.
