**First read.** My eye lands on the headline, then moves right to the dark green M4 pitch on the field map, then down to "Pitch M4, hardstanding… $20 deposit paid". In about three seconds it's clear what this is: booking software for people who run small campsites. It's calm, practical and rural, and it sounds like someone who has actually run a field.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of the marketing homepage for a campsite booking tool, aimed at small campsite owners, and its job is to get them to sign up.

**Verdict.** It works. The message is clear, the hierarchy is clean, and the product mock earns its space. What holds it back is the ask: "Add your campsite" doesn't tell the owner what happens when they click or what it costs. Nothing on the page compares Pitchbook to how they take bookings today, so they have no easy way to weigh the switch.

**What would move it most**
1. **The ask stops short of what an owner needs to commit.** Where: the green "Add your campsite" button and the copy above it.
   - **What's missing.** The button names an exact action, which is good. But nothing near it says whether adding a campsite is free, how long it takes, or what the owner has to set up first. Pricing sits in the nav, away from the decision.
   - **No comparison.** The page never puts Pitchbook next to what the owner already uses, such as a phone, a paper diary or a spreadsheet. So they have to take "keeps everything together" on trust.
   - **The hint is there but not used.** The headline's "while you are out on the field" quietly points at the current pain of being stuck by the phone, then leaves it unsaid.
   - **Fix.** Put one line of fact by the button about cost or setup effort, using facts only you have. In the body copy, say what the owner stops doing compared with their current routine, in their words.
   - Defect: an owner who can't judge the cost or the gain is more likely to leave and come back later, or not come back at all.
2. **On the map, green means "taken".** Where: the pitch grid in the hero.
   - **The problem.** Booked pitches are filled sage green. Free pitches (L3, M6, H5) are white with a dashed border. Green normally means available or OK, and most booking maps (seats, pitches, tables) use green for "you can have this". A quick glance can read it backwards.
   - **Why it matters beyond this picture.** The copy says campers choose a pitch online. If the camper-facing map uses the same colors, campers are the ones who will misread it.
   - **What saves it here.** The dashed borders and "3 free" correct the reading, so this is a cost at a glance rather than a dead end.
   - **Fix.** Make booked pitches a neutral fill and give free pitches the positive treatment. Or keep the colors and add a two-item key.
   - Convention: green is the success color (Apple HIG: Color; Material Design color roles). Breaking it here buys nothing.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Deposits that come back" can be read two ways.** An owner may read it as money leaving them. It's really about refunding a camper in one step. Name the action instead, for example with "refund" in the heading (WCAG 2.4.6).
- **"When a camper cancels in good time" is vague.** It's a money promise, so readers will hold you to it. It could mean a cancellation window the owner sets, or one Pitchbook decides. Say whose deadline it is.
- **"Out on the field" may read like a slip.** The usual idiom is "out in the field". If "on" is meant literally (a campsite field), it may still read as a mistake. This is my taste; check it with a couple of owners.

**What's working.**
- **The field map is real, not decoration.** The rows L, M and H, the "Track to the wash block" band, "18 pitches, 3 free" (which matches the grid) and the selected pitch's details make the product concrete. It shows the promise instead of claiming it. Keep it this specific.
- **The type has three clear levels:** headline, body and feature headings, and small detail. Weight and color do the finer work, so nothing competes with the headline.
- **The copy speaks to the owner.** "You see tonight's arrivals on one screen" and "whoever is on the gate after dark" land on daily life, not on features.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- Whether the empty band at the bottom is the page or just where the capture ends.
