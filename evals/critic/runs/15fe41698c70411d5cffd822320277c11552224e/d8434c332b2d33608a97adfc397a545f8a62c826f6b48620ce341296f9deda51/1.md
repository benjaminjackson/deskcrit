**First read.** My eye lands on the "Plan and billing" title, then goes straight to the teal-outlined "Full gym" card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads as the settings page of a gym-management SaaS for a climbing gym. It feels calm, orderly, and trustworthy, and a little generic.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a gym owner or front-desk admin uses this to check or change their subscription, update billing details, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and mostly well built. But on the one action that costs money, changing plan, it doesn't say what will happen. You can't tell which plan you're on now, whether "Save changes" also commits a new plan, or what the plans actually differ by. These are the gaps that lead to a wrong charge and a support ticket.

**What would move it most**
1. **It's unclear what commits a plan change, and what it will cost.** The plan cards sit at the top of the page. "Save changes" and "Cancel" sit under Billing details, after a divider, so they read as belonging to the billing form only. Picture a user who clicks "Gym group": they can't tell whether that alone switched them, whether Save will switch them, or whether Save leaves the plan alone. Nothing says when a change takes effect or how the first bill is worked out. This is the button that commits them to $159 a month, which makes it a **defect**: it breaks Norman's natural mapping, where a control sits next to the thing it changes. Nielsen's help heuristic adds that a complex step should have short help next to it. Fix direction: give the plan change its own commit, next to the cards, that names the plan and price being switched to. Add a line about when the change applies and how billing is handled. The maker supplies those facts.
2. **The current plan isn't marked.** "Full gym" is selected, and only the invoice table tells you it's the plan you're paying for. Once you click another radio, nothing on screen shows what you're on now. This is a **defect** under Nielsen's first heuristic, visibility of system status. Fix: label the current plan in words, such as a "Current plan" tag on its card, and keep that label separate from the radio selection.
3. **The plan descriptions compare different things.** "One wall, 200 members", "Unlimited members", and "Up to 5 gyms" each state a different limit. A stranger can't tell whether Full gym covers more than one gym, or whether Gym group has a member cap. This is the line people pick a price from and later hold the company to, so the ambiguity is a **defect**. Fix: describe all three plans on the same terms, members and locations, in the same order. Fill in the values the plans actually have; don't guess them.
4. **The invoices can't be opened.** Invoice numbers like "BS-2093" are set as plain gray text, and there's no download or view control. The most common reason to visit a billing page is to get an invoice for the books. If these rows are clickable, nothing shows it; if they aren't, the task can't be done here. This is a **defect**, argued on common sense since there's no approved source, plus the general rule that links must look clickable. Fix: make each invoice openable or downloadable, with a visible link or button on its row.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email, Currency, and Invoice address have no required or optional marks. The cost is low with only three prefilled fields, but add marks if any field is optional (Budiu, NN/g).

**What's working.**
- The structure is clear: Plan, Billing details, and Invoices are separated by plain rules and short, literal headings. The left rail marks "Plan and billing" with a gray pill, so you always know where you are.
- There's one primary action. "Save changes" is the only filled teal element besides the selected card, and "Cancel" is clearly a secondary outline button.
- The invoice table is set well: Amount is right-aligned with even figures, status is in words rather than color, and the rules are light hairlines. Keep that restraint.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus states on the plan cards and fields.
- Whether the invoice rows or numbers do anything on hover or click.
- Whether the table runs past the last row shown (1 Sep 2026) with paging.
