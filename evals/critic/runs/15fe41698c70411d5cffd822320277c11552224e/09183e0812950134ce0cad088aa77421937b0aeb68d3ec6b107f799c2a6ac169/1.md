**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing", drops to the teal-outlined Salon card, then to the teal Save changes button. It reads as a tidy, ordinary settings screen for the owner of a small grooming business. It feels calm and trustworthy. Next to the gray UI, the color emoji in the sidebar look a little like a toy.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a salon owner or manager comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The screen is clean and mostly sound, but its most important action doesn't work as the screen suggests. Changing plans is the one thing here that changes what the owner pays. The layout separates that choice from the Save button, and nothing says what saving will charge or when. The invoice list also gives no visible way to get an invoice.

**What would move it most**
1. **Changing plans commits money, but the screen never says how or when.** A divider runs right under the three plan cards, and Save changes sits under Billing details, so the button looks like it saves only the email, currency, and address. An owner who clicks "Salon group" can't tell whether Save changes moves them to $95.00 a month, whether that happens now or at the next invoice, or whether they'll be charged a partial amount. The radio button is also the only sign of the current plan. Once they click another card, nothing on screen shows which plan they're actually on, and there's no next charge date to anchor them. This is a defect: the viewer may change plans by accident, or avoid the action because they don't trust it. Several rules point the same way:
   - The rule above Billing details splits Plan from its own Save button (common region, Nielsen Norman Group).
   - The Save control isn't next to the thing it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping).
   - Current status isn't visible (Nielsen 1), and the user has to remember which plan they started on (Nielsen 6).

   Direction: mark the current plan on its card in words, and show when the next charge happens. Then either give the plan change its own confirm step that states the new price and when it starts, or remove the divider so it's clear Save covers the plan too. The facts about proration and start date are yours to supply.

2. **The invoice table shows invoices but gives no way to get one.** CL-3307, CL-3254, and CL-3198 are set like the plain text around them, and there's no download or view action anywhere in the rows. The most common reason to open a billing page is to get an invoice for the accounts, and here that dead-ends. This is a defect. If the numbers are links, they don't look clickable ("Buttons and links must look clickable"; Nielsen Norman Group on flat UI elements). If they aren't links, the feature is missing. Direction: give each row a clearly styled link or a download action.

**Smaller things.**
- **Emoji icons:** The sidebar icons (🐾 👥 💳 🔔 🔌) and the ✅ in the Status column bring in yellow, blue, purple, and orange that appear nowhere else. They're also rendered in a glossy style that clashes with the flat teal-and-gray UI. Replace them with one simple icon set in the neutral color, or drop them. This is taste, plus palette discipline (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
- **Billing email next to Currency:** These two unrelated fields share a row, which breaks the top-to-bottom reading of the form. Stack them. This is taste.
- **Required fields:** Nothing tells the owner whether Invoice address can be left blank. Mark the required fields, or put "optional" on the ones that aren't (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- **Copy, Salon group card:** "Up to 3 salons, one pet list" can be read as a limit ("only one list") when it probably means a feature ("one shared pet list across your salons"). Restore the missing verb so it reads only one way.

**What's working.**
- **Plan cards:** They're scannable, with name, limits, and price in the same order on each, and the selected card is marked by both a radio button and a teal border. Keep that structure when you add the current-plan marker.
- **Invoice table:** It's quiet and well set. Amounts are right-aligned, "Paid" is spelled out next to its icon, and the only lines are thin row rules.
- **Buttons:** There's one teal primary button with an outlined Cancel next to it, so the main action is never in doubt.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether clicking anywhere on a plan card selects it or only the small radio does.
- What happens after Save changes when the plan has changed (confirm step, success message).
