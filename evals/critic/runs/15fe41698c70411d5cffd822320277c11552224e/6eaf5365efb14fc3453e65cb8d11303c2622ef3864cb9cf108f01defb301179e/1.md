**First read.** My eye goes to "Plan and billing", then to the Garden card with its teal outline, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It's a settings page for a community-garden management tool, written for a volunteer committee. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and also fairly stock. It looks like a common SaaS settings layout with nothing that marks it as Wrenfield's own.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a garden committee member (often the treasurer) uses this page to check or change the plan, keep the billing details right, and get invoices.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly does its job. Its weak spot is the one action that costs money: changing plan. The page doesn't say how a plan change is confirmed, when it takes effect, or whether the garden is close to its limits. Someone who wants to upgrade or downgrade has to guess.

**What would move it most**
1. **You can't tell how a plan change is committed.** *Defect.* The three plan cards sit above a horizontal line, under their own "Plan" heading. The only "Save changes" button is about 260 px lower, tucked under "Billing details" and almost touching the line above "Invoices". The spacing and the line both say the button belongs to the billing form only. A treasurer who clicks "Network" can't tell whether that switch already happened ($22 → $60 a month), happens only on Save, or needs some other step. The money action is the one place where they need certainty. Two principles are broken here. A control should sit next to what it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). A dividing line shouldn't cut one task in two (Palmer; NN/g, "common region"). Fix: give the plan change its own explicit commit. That could be a button inside the Plan section that names the change and the new price. Or put the plan and billing details under one Save, with no line between them. Either way, the button and what it changes need to sit together.
2. **Nothing says when a plan change takes effect or what it costs this month.** *Defect.* The cards show monthly prices, but the page doesn't say whether a change applies now or at the next invoice, or whether part-months are prorated. The invoice history shows this garden already moved from Allotment to Garden in October, so people do change plans. This is where short help next to the choice earns its place (Nielsen, heuristic 10). Fix: put one line next to the plan choice saying when a change starts and how the next invoice works out. That policy is the maker's to supply.
3. **The plan cards can't be compared, and they don't show where the garden stands now.** *Defect, and it's in the copy.* Each card lists a different thing:
   - Allotment: "One site, up to 40 plots"
   - Garden: "Up to 150 plots and a waiting list"
   - Network: "Up to 10 sites, one member list"

   A stranger can't tell whether Allotment includes a waiting list, or how many plots Network allows. The cards also leave out the one thing a committee member has and trusts: their own current numbers. The sidebar shows 38 members and 12 on the waiting list, but the cards never say how many plots or sites this garden uses against each limit. So the viewer has to take "up to 150 plots" on trust instead of seeing that, say, they'd outgrow Allotment. Fix: describe every card by the same measures (plots, sites, waiting list), and show current usage next to the limits. The maker has those figures; this is common sense, not a cited rule.

**Smaller things**
- In the Invoices table, the invoice numbers (WF-0212 etc.) look like plain text. There's no visible way to open or download an invoice, which is the main thing a treasurer comes here for. Make the number a link or add a download action in each row.
- The heading says 12 invoices but the table shows 3, with no "Show all" or "Older invoices" link. Add one under the table.
- "Send the treasurer a copy of each invoice" doesn't say which address gets the copy, so you have to know where the treasurer's email is stored. Show the address in the line, or link to where it's set.
- The text-field, dropdown and Cancel button borders look about #ccc on white, which is probably under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Darken the field edges, and measure them to confirm.
- The same gray number pill means a total on Members, Waiting list and Invoices, but on Notices "3" probably means unread. If that's right, give the unread count a different style so one look doesn't carry two meanings.
- The billing fields have no required or optional markers. If the invoice address is optional, write "(optional)" after its label (Budiu, NN/g).

**What's working**
- The Garden card's selected state is easy to see. The filled radio button and the darker teal border both mark it, so the choice doesn't depend on color alone.
- The invoice table is set well. Amounts are right-aligned with matching decimals, the column headers are quiet, and the rows have only thin rules. Keep that restraint.
- The palette is small: one teal for the selected card, the primary button and the checkbox, with everything else gray. The one thing to act on stands out without effort.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- What clicking a plan card actually does (switches the plan right away, or only selects it). That decides how serious finding 1 is, so check it in the live product.
- Keyboard focus on the plan cards and form fields.
