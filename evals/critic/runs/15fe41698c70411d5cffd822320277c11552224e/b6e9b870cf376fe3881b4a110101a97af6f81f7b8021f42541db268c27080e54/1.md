**First read.** The eye lands on the "Plan and billing" title, then goes to the teal outline around the Studio card and the teal "Save changes" button. These are the only two colored things on the page, so they look connected. The page is clearly the billing settings for a studio-booking product, probably for tattoo studios. It feels calm, orderly, and competent, and it also looks like a lot of other SaaS settings pages.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a studio owner comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and easy to scan, but it's vague exactly where money changes hands. It never says whether "Save changes" switches the plan, what a switch costs or when, or what "Cancel" cancels. And it leaves out the things people usually come to a billing page for: the card being charged, the next charge, and a way to get an invoice.

**What would move it most**

1. **Nobody can tell whether "Save changes" changes the plan, or when the new price starts.** The plan cards sit above a divider. "Save changes" and "Cancel" sit below the next section, Billing details, so the buttons seem to belong only to the email, currency, and address fields. Picture an owner who clicks "Multi-studio". They can't tell whether they've just moved to $89.00 a month, whether they still need to press Save, or whether the button only saves the address. They also can't tell whether the charge is immediate, prorated, or starts at the next cycle. Clicking from $39.00 to $89.00 should never be a guess. That's a defect. Controls should sit next to the thing they change (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping), and on a task that involves money, a short explanation belongs next to the step (Nielsen, heuristic 10). The fix: give the plan change its own action, separate from saving details, and have it state the price change and when it takes effect. Or, if one button really does save both, put it where it clearly covers both sections. The proration and timing terms are facts only you have.

2. **"Cancel" on a billing page can be read two ways.** Next to "Save changes", most people will read it as "discard my edits". But this is the page where people come to end a subscription, so some will read it as "cancel my plan". One group will hesitate to press it when they only want to undo a typo. The other will press it expecting to unsubscribe and leave thinking they've cancelled. Because the button commits the viewer to something, the double reading is a defect. The fix is an edit to the words already there: "Discard changes" removes the second reading.

3. **The page doesn't show the plan's current status.** The Studio card is selected, but nothing says that's the plan being paid for now rather than a choice the user just made. As soon as someone clicks another card, the page can no longer tell them what they're actually on. That's a defect (Nielsen, heuristic 1, visibility of system status). The fix is to mark the current plan in words, separately from the radio selection, and to show when the next charge falls.

4. **Things people come to a billing page for are missing.** There's no payment method: which card is charged and how to change it. The invoice rows have no visible way to view or download an invoice. "IM-5520" looks like plain text, and an owner who needs a receipt for their accountant reaches a dead end. This is common sense, not a cited rule: those are the jobs that bring people to this page. The fix is a payment-method entry under Billing details and a clear download or view action on each invoice row.

**Smaller things.**
- The text-field and unselected plan-card borders look pale gray on white, likely under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the input border color. If it fails, darken it so the fields read as fields.
- No field marks required or optional. If the invoice address is optional, say so after its label.
- Billing email and Currency share a row but aren't a related pair, so the eye zigzags. That's my taste, not a rule: put them in one column, in the order people fill them in.
- The invoice dates read "2 Nov 2026" on an account in US dollars with a Rhode Island address. US readers expect "Nov 2, 2026". Make the format match the account's locale.
- In the plan cards, each tier lists different kinds of limit ("30 bookings a month", "deposits online", "one client list"). A buyer can't tell whether Studio keeps the booking cap or whether Multi-studio includes online deposits. Describe each tier on the same terms. Only you know those facts.

**What's working.**
- Color discipline: teal appears only on the current selection, the primary button, and the checkbox, so color always means "this one" or "do this". Keep it that way when you add the new actions.
- The invoice table is quiet and easy to read. Amounts are right-aligned with matching decimals, there are no heavy lines, and the column headers are a muted gray. Don't add boxes or zebra stripes.
- The left settings list marks the current page with a soft gray background, and the page title repeats its name, so there's no doubt where you are inside Settings.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the plan cards and fields. Whether the whole plan card or only the radio circle is clickable. What happens after a plan is selected: a confirmation step, or an immediate charge.
