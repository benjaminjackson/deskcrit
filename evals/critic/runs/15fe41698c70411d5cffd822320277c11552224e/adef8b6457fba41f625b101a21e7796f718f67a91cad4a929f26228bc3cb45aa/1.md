**First read.** The eye starts at "Listens" and the row of four equal numbers, then jumps straight to the purple Monday bar, the only strong color on the page. This reads as a podcast analytics dashboard for the host of "The Long Table". It feels calm and tidy, and could pass for any SaaS template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the host opens this monthly to see whether the show is growing and to decide things like release day and which episodes to make more of.

**Verdict.** It looks finished, but it isn't trustworthy yet. The two charts the host would act on most likely mislead: the weekday chart adds up raw totals for a month where some weekdays occur more often than others, and the "daily" chart doesn't look daily. The month's real story, growth over October, is buried in small gray text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday chart compares unequal days.** This is in "Downloads by weekday", on the right. November 2026 starts on a Sunday, so it has five Sundays and five Mondays but only four of every other day. Summed totals give Sunday and Monday a 25% head start. By eye, Sunday's bar looks like the second best, but per day it looks no better than Tuesday or Wednesday. Monday's lead is partly real and partly the calendar. A host choosing a release day from this chart could make the wrong call. Only Monday has a value (9,860), and there's no axis, so the viewer can't check the others either. Fix: plot average downloads per weekday, or say plainly that these are totals, and give every bar a value or an axis (Tufte, *Visual Display*, on clear labeling; Few). *Defect.*
2. **"Daily downloads" doesn't look like daily data.** The November line has about 11 points across 30 days, roughly one every three days. If it were daily, the Monday peak that the right-hand chart highlights would show up as a weekly spike. Instead the line zigzags evenly, so the two charts don't back each other up, and a careful viewer stops trusting both. Fix: plot all 30 days, or rename the chart to say what each point is. Check the data behind it. *Defect.*
3. **The growth story is there, but you have to work it out yourself.** It sits in the KPI row along the top. Every card has an October comparison, which is good, but it's set in small, pale text with no change and no direction. To learn that downloads are up about 10% and followers about 6%, the viewer has to subtract in four places. "Is the show growing?" is the main question this page answers, and right now nothing on the page states the answer. Fix: put the change, with an up or down marker, next to each big number, and keep the October value as the reference (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). *Defect.*
4. **The metric labels can each be read two ways, and these are numbers the host will repeat to sponsors.** "Finished an episode 62%" could mean 62% of listeners or 62% of downloads. "Average listen 31 min" could be per download or per listener. The table's "Finished" column (71%, 64%, 59%) seems to measure something per episode, so it's unclear whether it's the same measure as the 62%. If a host quotes "62% of our listeners finish" when the real figure is per download, they've overstated their audience to a sponsor. Fix: write each label as who does what, e.g. "Downloads played to the end", using whichever is true (Nielsen, heuristic 2). *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The page title "Listens" and the four KPI figures look like the same size, so the page has no clear top level. Make the title clearly bigger or smaller than the figures, or set it apart by weight.
- The card edges follow no shared grid. The split between the two charts (around x 900) lines up with none of the KPI dividers, and the table's Guest column lines up with neither row above it. Take the chart split from the KPI columns.
- The line chart uses a legend in its top right corner. With only two lines, put "November" and "October" at the right-hand ends of the lines instead.
- The chart titles name a topic ("Daily downloads"), not a finding. For a monthly summary, one line saying what changed would save the viewer the reading. (Taste.)
- "Top episodes" doesn't say what they're ranked by (downloads, it seems) or for which period (November, or all time). Name both.
- Two nav icons don't match their labels: Sponsors uses a shopping cart, and Settings uses a sun or crosshair shape instead of a gear. Use icons that mean what the label says.

**What's working.**
- The color is restrained. Everything is gray and muted indigo, and the one saturated mark is used for emphasis. Keep that rule when fixing the weekday chart.
- October is shown in gray behind November on the line chart. That's the right way to give context without competition: keep it, just label it directly.
- The table is set well: figures are right-aligned with even digits, the rules are light, and the headings are clear. Nothing needs to change there.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The date range "1 to 30 November 2026" in the top right looks like plain text. If it's meant to be a picker, check in a live build that it looks clickable.
