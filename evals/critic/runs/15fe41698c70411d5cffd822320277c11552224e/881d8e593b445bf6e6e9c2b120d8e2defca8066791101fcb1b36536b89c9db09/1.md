The crit is written. I read the whole screen at full resolution and zoomed in on the line chart.

**First read.** My eye goes straight to the dark blue Saturday bar with "92" above it. It's the only saturated mark on a calm gray-and-white screen. Then it slides left to the row of four big numbers, which all have the same weight, and I read them in order like a sentence. It reads as a monthly bookings report for a pottery studio's classes. It feels tidy and trustworthy, but it's quiet and looks like a template. Nothing on it says "look here, act on this."

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the owner or manager of a small pottery studio checking each month how classes are selling, and deciding what to schedule or push next.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly well made, but the main chart doesn't add up against the numbers above it, and on a report that breaks trust. Past that, it says business is up and stops there: it gives the owner nothing to act on.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "Daily bookings" line disagrees with the totals.** (Defect.) The chart is titled daily, but it plots only 11 points, one about every three days. The November line runs from about 13 to 23, so even read as daily it averages around 18 a day. The card above says 386 seats in 30 days, which is about 13 a day. October has the same gap: 352 over 31 days is about 11 a day, but the gray line sits between 11 and 17. On top of that, "bookings" in the chart title and "Seats booked" in the card read like two measures that may or may not be the same thing. An owner who notices any of this stops trusting every other number on the screen. Fix: plot every day if the title says daily, or retitle the chart to match what it plots (for example "per 3 days"). Make the chart and the card count the same thing under the same name, so the line visibly adds up to the total (Tufte, *Visual Display*, graphical integrity). The x-axis ticks at 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov are also unevenly spaced (9, 9, then 11 days). Put them at regular steps.

2. **Nothing leads, and nothing points to a next step.** (Convention plus taste.) The four KPI cards are equal in size, weight and color. The page title "Bookings" looks about the same size as those numbers. So the strongest thing on the page is a supporting chart, the Saturday bar, rather than the month's main result (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance by differences of size). Then there's the job itself. Every block reports good news: up on October, fullest classes, best day. The owner learns things went well and nothing about what to do. "Fullest classes" shows the three classes that need nothing. The decisions sit elsewhere: classes that aren't filling, or whether Saturday needs more sessions. Fix: decide the one number this screen exists for and let it lead. Give the page at least one block that shows where action is needed. The Saturday highlight already points that way; build on it.

3. **The charts show data but say nothing.** (Taste/common sense. The catalog's source here isn't on the approved list.) "Daily bookings" and "Seats booked by weekday" only name their topics. The Saturday highlight implies a finding, but only Saturday has a value. The other six bars have no axis and no labels, so you can't tell whether Sunday is close behind or far off. Fix: say the finding in the title or a short caption, using numbers already on the chart. Label every bar's value, or add a light value axis, so the comparison the highlight invites can actually be made (Tufte; Few: label axes and units).

**Smaller things.**
- The line chart's legend sits in the top right, away from the lines. Put "November" and "October" at the right-hand end of each line instead (Tufte; Wilke).
- The line chart's y-axis has no unit. Say whether it counts seats or bookings, once item 1 settles which.
- The Settings icon looks like a sun or a crosshair, not the gear people expect, so it's slower to recognize. Use the standard symbol (Apple HIG, Material).
- "Seats filled 88%" doesn't say what it's a share of (seats offered across all classes this month?). A stranger could read it as a near-duplicate of "Seats booked". Add the missing reference so it reads as a fill rate.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI carries its October figure right under it. That gives each number a point of reference without adding clutter. Keep it.
- One saturated blue against neutral grays, used for both "this month" and "the standout day", is a disciplined palette. The emphasis reads instantly and still shows in grayscale.
- The table is set properly: numbers are right-aligned in tabular figures, the dividers are light, and the headings are plain. It scans in one pass.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- I can't tell whether "1 to 30 November 2026" can be clicked to change the period. If it can, it needs to look like a control. If the month can't be changed at all, the owner can't look back.
- Hover tooltips on the charts. If they exist, they'd partly answer the missing values in item 3.
