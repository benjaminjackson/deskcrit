**First read.** The eye goes straight to the one blue bar (Saturday), then up to "Overview" and across the row of four big numbers. It reads as a monthly operations dashboard for a public library's staff. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, close to a well-made template. It takes a second look to see that the page has no story beyond "Saturday is busy."

**What it's for.** My assumption: library managers check how the month went and what to act on (staffing, holds), at a glance, on desktop.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly readable, but the one claim it highlights is drawn in a way that misleads, and its numbers don't say which way things are moving. Fix the weekday chart first.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday chart compares weeks of unequal length, and its callout can be read two ways.** *Defect.* November 2026 has five Mondays and five Sundays but only four Saturdays. Totals per weekday therefore overstate Monday and Sunday against Saturday. Monday's bar looks level with Wednesday's, but per day it is lower. Anyone using this for rostering reads the wrong shape. The callout "2,480 loans on Saturdays, the busiest day" can mean all four Saturdays together or each Saturday. That is roughly a fourfold difference, and it's the number a manager will repeat in a meeting. Only Saturday carries a value, so the other six bars can't be read at all (Tufte and Few on labeling). Direction: chart the average per weekday, or say plainly that these are totals and how many of each day the month had. Restore the word that's missing from the callout ("per Saturday" or "across 4 Saturdays", whichever is true). Give the other bars values too, either light direct labels or a quiet axis.
2. **The figures don't tell the viewer whether things got better or worse, and the two side cards follow a different pattern.** *Defect, plus convention (Gerstner's program, Few's pitfall 2).* The KPI row gives "10,480 in October" under 11,206. That's a reference point, but the manager has to subtract four- and five-digit numbers in their head to see the change, four times over. "Returns this month" and "Holds placed this month" have no comparison at all. They also switch format: the unit word goes into the number ("10,870 returns") and the gray label disappears, so they look like they come from another dashboard. "Most come back on Monday" can mean more than half or simply the most common day. Those are different facts for staffing the returns desk. Direction: one card pattern for every figure (label, number, comparison), and show the change or its direction next to each comparison. Make the Monday line say which reading is true.
3. **The page's only accent goes to a secondary fact, and every figure is the same size.** *Taste.* The page title, the four KPIs, 2,480, 10,870 and 1,620 are all set at about 24 px. Card headings (about 16 px) are only a step above the gray labels (about 14 px). So nothing marks the month's headline. The blue Saturday bar wins by default, and the viewer starts with a weekday pattern instead of the month's result. On an overview, the top row should be what you see first. Direction: give the KPI row clear priority, through size or weight against the side cards' figures. Then let the chart's blue be the second thing seen, not the first.

**Smaller things.**
- 2,480 appears twice in the chart card, once over the bar and once in the callout. Keep it in one place.
- The empty space is scattered rather than gathered. The right third of the chart card is empty below the callout, Returns and Holds are half-empty cards, and the bars sit inset from the card's left edge. Tighten these, or give the space to bar values.
- The right-column cards (left edge at about x 949) don't line up with the KPI dividers (about 757 and 1002). Put the two rows on the same columns.
- The callout wraps to leave "day" alone on its second line. Rebreak it or widen it.
- The nav icons send the wrong signals: Holds uses a shopping cart and Settings a crosshair or sun rather than a gear (platform convention).
- "1 to 30 November 2026" reads as static text, so there's no visible way to look at another month or filter. If it's a picker, make it look like one.

**What's working.**
- Restraint: grays, one blue used only to mark the chart's point, and the takeaway written next to the chart. Keep that one-accent rule when you fix the hierarchy.
- Every KPI has a previous-period line under it, which is more than most dashboards bother with. The change is only in how it's shown.
- The "Most borrowed" table: right-aligned tabular figures, hairline row rules and no boxes. It's easy to scan.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date range is a working control. Focus states in the sidebar navigation.
