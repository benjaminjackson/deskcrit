**First read.** The eye goes to "Overview" and straight along the four big numbers in the top row, then drops to the one blue bar (Saturday, "19"), then the blue line. It reads as an operations dashboard for a small ferry company, for the harbor office checking the month. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and a little mute: nothing on the page says whether November went well.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a monthly overview so an operator can see at a glance how November compares with October and where demand is concentrated.

**Verdict.** It's well built and mostly does its job. The layout, restraint and table are solid. The biggest problem is that it gives you numbers but not changes: you have to do the arithmetic yourself, and one of the cards hides its change completely.

**What would move it most**

1. **The KPI cards make you subtract, and "Average fare" hides a real change.** In the top row, each card shows November's figure over October's raw figure ("9,412" over "8,870 in October"). To learn whether things got better, and by how much, you have to work out the difference four times. That defeats the point of a glanceable row. Worse, "Average fare" shows "$15" over "$15 in October", which reads as flat. But the page's own numbers say otherwise: $142,630 ÷ 9,412 ≈ $15.15 and $131,200 ÷ 8,870 ≈ $14.79, so the fare went up about 2%, and rounding to whole dollars erased it. Someone reporting "fares held steady" from this card would be wrong.
   - **Kind:** defect. Few's dashboard guidance asks for the comparison as a change next to each headline number, not a second number to decode.
   - **Fix:** put the change (up or down, as a percentage or an amount) next to each figure, with the October value as the reference. Show the fare to the cent so a real change can't round to zero.

2. **"Sailings sold out by weekday" shows only one value.** In the right-hand chart, Saturday is labeled "19". The other six bars have no number and there is no axis, so you can't tell whether Sunday was 15 or 16, or Monday 5 or 7. The chart says Saturday is biggest, and nothing else you could act on, like whether it's worth adding a Friday sailing. The title also doesn't say what period the counts cover (November, presumably).
   - **Kind:** defect (Tufte, Few: every mark needs a readable value or scale).
   - **Fix:** put the value above every bar and keep Saturday as the highlight. Then the chart answers "how much more" as well as "which".

3. **"Daily passengers" isn't plotted daily, and its date ticks are uneven.** The line chart's title says daily, but each line has about 11 points across the month, roughly one every three days. Anyone reading it as daily will take each bump as a single day. The x-axis ticks (1, 10, 19, 30 Nov) are 9, 9 and 11 days apart, so the last stretch is quietly compressed.
   - **Kind:** defect (Tufte on graphical integrity: tick intervals must be even).
   - **Fix:** either plot all 30 days, or retitle it to the interval actually plotted. Put the ticks at even intervals.

**Smaller things.**
- The Saturday bar uses the same blue as the "November" line, so it looks connected to that series when it's just a highlight. Use a different emphasis, or make the shared meaning deliberate.
- The line chart uses a legend at top right. Label "November" and "October" at the right-hand ends of the lines so you don't have to look back and forth (Tufte, Wilke).
- The chart titles name topics ("Daily passengers", "Sailings sold out by weekday"), not findings. A title that states what the chart shows would let a skimmer stop there (taste).
- "1 to 30 November 2026" sits as plain text with no visible control for changing the period or comparing other months. That's fine only if it's clickable, and nothing shows that it is.

**What's working.**
- **Disciplined color:** gray for context (October, the other weekdays) and one blue for what matters, so the eye always knows where to look in each chart. Keep it.
- **The "Busiest routes" table:** numbers are right-aligned in tabular figures, rules are light, and there's a clear column structure. It scans in one pass, so don't box it up further.
- **One grid throughout:** the cards share edges and gutters, the type scale has clear steps (page title, KPI figures, card headings, body, captions), and each card holds one group. The calm comes from this, so keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the date range, table rows or chart points are interactive (period switching, drill-down into a route).
- Exact contrast of the gray caption and axis text: it looks adequate, but measure it against 4.5:1.
