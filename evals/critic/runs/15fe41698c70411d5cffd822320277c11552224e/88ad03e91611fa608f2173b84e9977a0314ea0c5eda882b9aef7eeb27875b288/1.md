I've written the critique below and changed no files.

**First read.** I look at the big numbers along the top first, then the blue line chart, then the single blue bar. It reads as a monthly dashboard for a small solar co-op. It is calm, tidy and competent. Then the numbers stop making sense together. The cards say November produced less than October (38.4 vs 51.2 MWh), but the line chart shows November above October on every point. The bright cyan outline on every card makes the whole screen look like it's in a selected state.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a monthly operations view for whoever runs the co-op's arrays. They need to see whether output is on track, why it isn't, and which arrays need someone sent out.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the screen contradicts itself. The headline numbers, the daily chart and the faults table each tell a different story, so the operator can't trust any of them. Behind that, the one actionable panel (arrays needing a visit) sits at the same weight as everything else, at the bottom of the screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The figures disagree with each other.** Defect. There are three conflicts:
   - **Cards vs. line chart:** The "Energy generated" card shows 38.4 MWh against 51.2 MWh in October, a drop of about a quarter. The "Daily output" chart puts the November line above October at every point.
   - **Panels online vs. the table:** "Panels online 1,184" (1,190 in October) means 6 panels down. The table lists 64 + 48 + 22 = 134 panels at faulty arrays, including 64 behind an inverter that has been offline for 2 days.
   - **Bars vs. total:** "Output by array" shows F at 2.3 MWh, with the other six bars lower. Seven bars of about 2 MWh can't add up to a 38.4 MWh month.

   The operator will either distrust the whole screen or act on the wrong number. If this is mock data, fix it before anyone judges the design. A dashboard whose parts disagree can't be judged for clarity. If it's live, one of these queries is wrong. Either way, the chart and the cards must describe the same period and the same measure, and each chart title should name it.
2. **Nothing tells the operator what matters, and the thing to act on is last.** Defect. The four cards, two charts and table all have the same white fill, the same cyan outline and the same heading style, so no panel is clearly first (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance by differences of size). The story this month is a drop in output and payouts, with three arrays down. The cards give October's figure but no change or direction. The reader has to subtract $6,720 − $4,860 in their head to learn payouts fell by over a quarter. "Arrays needing a visit" is the only panel that leads to action, yet it sits at the bottom with plain rows and no way to act from it. Fix:
   - Make the drop and the faults the first things seen.
   - Show the change next to each card's comparison.
   - Give the faults panel weight, or move it up.
   - Let each row lead to its array or to dispatching someone.
3. **The cyan outline on every card is the loudest color on the screen, and it marks nothing.** Convention (Apple HIG and Material: keep the accent for the main action and one highlight; Lupton, *Thinking with Type*: boxes should not trap content). The gaps already separate the cards. The saturated outline competes with the blue data series and reads like a focus or selected state. Drop it, or use a pale neutral border or a tint, and let the blue belong only to the data.
4. **The bar chart can't be read on its own.** Convention (Tufte, Few: label axes, units and period). "Output by array" has no axis, no period and only one value (F, 2.3 MWh), so the other six bars can't be compared to anything. F is picked out in blue with no reason given, and blue elsewhere means "November", so the color is borrowed. The arrays that matter this month are D, G and B, the ones in the fault table, and they aren't marked at all. Label every bar with its value and the period. Highlight the faulty arrays, not the best one, and say why they are highlighted. Highlighting only the faulty ones is my taste, but it ties the chart to the table below it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line chart legend:** It sits top right, away from the data. Label "November" and "October" at the line ends instead (Tufte; Wilke).
- **Line chart title:** "Daily output (kWh)" plots about 12 points over 30 days, so it isn't daily. Either plot every day or rename it to say what each point is.
- **Line chart x-axis:** Ticks fall at 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov, gaps of 9, 9 and 11 days. Use even intervals.
- **Chart titles:** They only name the topic ("Output by array"). Saying the finding in words, such as which array is down and by how much, would tell the operator what to do. This is common sense; the source for it isn't on the approved list.
- **Payouts icon:** The nav shows a shopping cart, which means buying, not paying members. Use an icon people already read as payments (Nielsen, Jakob's law).
- **Fault table:** The rows give no next step: no link to the array, no assignee, no "since" date. Add one action per row. This is common sense.

**What's working.**
- **Grid:** One grid governs the whole page. The card row, the chart row and the table share left and right edges and equal gutters, so the page scans calmly. Keep that when you add weight to the faults.
- **Fault table:** It is well set. Numbers are right-aligned with units, rules are light, and the fault text is plain and specific ("Inverter offline 2 days", "One string open").
- **October as gray context:** Showing October in gray behind November is the right idea for a month-on-month view. Once the data agrees, it will carry the comparison well.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "1 to 30 November 2026" can be changed, and whether the 30 Nov point is a real reading or a projection. If the month isn't over, the late points should be marked as an estimate.
