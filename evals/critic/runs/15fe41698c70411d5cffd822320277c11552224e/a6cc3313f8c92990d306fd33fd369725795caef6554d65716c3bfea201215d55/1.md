**First read.** The eye goes to "Overview," then along the four big figures in the top row, then to the one blue bar (Saturday). It reads as a calm, tidy monthly dashboard for a small farm shop, and it looks like a lot of other admin templates. Nothing on the page looks wrong, so you finish reading it thinking November went fine. The numbers on the page say it didn't.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a monthly overview the shop owner checks to see how the shop is doing and what needs attention.

**Verdict.** The design is clean and well made, but it fails at the main job of a monthly overview, which is to tell the owner what changed. Revenue and orders both fell, and shop visitors appear to have risen, yet every figure is set the same way and the viewer has to do the math to find any of that out.

**What would move it most**

1. **The comparisons show no direction, so the month's story stays hidden.** In the top row, "$31,270" sits over "$34,105 in October" and "2,214" over "2,390 in October". That is a drop of about 8% in revenue and about 7% in orders. Nothing marks either one: there's no change, no arrow, no color, and they're set exactly like Average order and Subscribers, which barely moved. Meanwhile the November line in Shop visitors sits clearly above October the whole month. Put together, the page is saying "more people came and fewer bought," and the owner can only see that by doing subtraction across three boxes. Four equal tiles with nothing standing out is the problem Tschichold describes: the order of importance should show through differences in size or strength (*The New Typography*). **Defect.** Fix: show the change against October as a signed figure in words, not just the raw October number. Then make the biggest move on the page the strongest thing on it. Chart titles that state the finding instead of only the topic would help too (taste, but that's what an overview is for).

2. **"Orders by weekday" doesn't say what it measures.** Only Saturday has a value (148), and there's no y-axis. If the bars were November totals they'd add up to 2,214, but scaling by Saturday's bar they come to roughly 700. If they were daily averages they'd be around 74. So the viewer can't tell what "148" means: total orders on Saturdays, average orders per Saturday, or something else. They also can't read Monday through Sunday except against each other. **Defect** (Tufte: label axes and units so the reader never guesses what a number measures). Fix: say the unit in the title or the label (for example, whether it's a per-day average), and either label every bar or add a light axis.

3. **The headings leave out what they rank or count.** "Best-selling produce" lists Maple syrup first, but it sold the fewest units in the table (164 against 203 and 231). The table is sorted by revenue, and the heading doesn't say so. An owner reading "best-selling" as most units would get the wrong answer. "Farm box subscribers" shows "18%", a share of something unnamed, under a label that names a head count. **Defect** (WCAG 2.4.6, headings describe their content). Fix: name the sort in the heading ("by revenue"). Then either show the subscriber count, or say what the 18% is a share of.

**Smaller things.**
- Sidebar labels start at five different x-positions (Overview ~60 px, Orders ~66, Produce ~62, Customers ~68, Settings ~64) because the icons are different widths. Put the icons in a fixed-width slot so the labels line up on one edge.
- In Shop visitors, the y-axis "0" sits right on top of the "1 Nov" x-label at the bottom left of the chart. Drop the 0 label or move the axis labels apart.
- Shop visitors uses a legend in the top right instead of labeling the two lines at their right-hand ends, which is where the eye already is (Tufte; Wilke).
- Shop visitors' x-ticks (1, 10, 19, 30 Nov) are spaced 9, 9, then 11 days apart. Use even intervals or weekly ticks.
- Blue means "November" in the line chart and "Saturday" in the bar chart right next to it. That's minor because Saturday also has a label, but one meaning per color is cleaner (common sense).

**What's working.**
- Color is held back: everything is gray except one blue, used to make a point (this month, the peak day). Keep that once the change markers go in, rather than adding red and green everywhere.
- Every headline figure comes with a comparison to the previous month. The data is already there; it just needs a direction.
- The type scale is tight and clear: page title, big figures, card titles, body, and small comparison lines, each easy to tell apart. The table's numbers are right-aligned in tabular figures.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "1 to 30 November 2026" can be clicked to change the range. It's set as plain text, so if it's a control, nothing shows that it is.
