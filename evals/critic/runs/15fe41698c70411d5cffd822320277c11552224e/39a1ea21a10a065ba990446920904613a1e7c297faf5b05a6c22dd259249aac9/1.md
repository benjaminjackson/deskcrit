**First read.** The eye goes first to the four big numbers across the top, mostly "3,184". Next it jumps to the blue Friday bar, the only strong color at that size, and only then to the map and the table. This reads as a bike-share operations dashboard for a fleet manager. It feels calm, tidy and well made, but generic. Nothing on the screen says "something needs doing", even though the map and table exist because a station is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a daily operations screen for the person who decides where restock vans go next.

**Verdict.** It's clean and legible, but it puts the job in the wrong order. Four equal rider stats come first. The one urgent fact, that Canal Basin has had no bikes for 40 minutes, is spread across a map card, a blue dot and the first table row, and none of them leads. A secondary chart steals the only strong color on the page.

**What would move it most**

1. **The urgent item doesn't lead (defect).** The top band gives four summary stats equal size and weight, and none of them asks for action. The empty station appears three times: a frosted card at the top left of the map, a blue dot near the river, and row one of "Stations to restock". It never ranks above the rides count. A dispatcher has to piece "Canal Basin is empty, and van 2 is on its way" together from three places. Fix: make the restock situation the dominant element, so the order of importance is plain (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show logical order by differences of size). The four KPIs can step down to a quieter strip.

2. **Blue means two things, and "empty" gets the calmest color (defect).** On the map, blue means "Empty" station. In the chart next to it, blue means "Friday". So the eye links the two, and the brightest thing on the screen is the least useful one: a past weekday peak. An empty station is an alert, yet it gets the same soothing brand blue, and a gray dot with a blue dot is the only cue on the map. Fix: give one role one color (Apple HIG, "Color"). Keep the chart neutral, or use a different emphasis there. Give "empty" a status color plus a second cue, such as a label or a different marker shape at the dot (WCAG 1.4.1).

3. **The map's cards float free of what they describe and cover the map (defect).** The Canal Basin card sits in the top-left corner with no pointer to the blue dot it describes. The "Van route 2" card covers the lower right, sits on top of a station dot, and the van itself has no marker. Both cards are frosted and glow, which adds depth for no reason and blurs the streets under them (taste). The viewer can't tell which dot is Canal Basin or where the van is. Fix: tie each card to its point with a leader line or by placing it next to the point. Show the van as a mark on the map. Drop the blur and glow so the map stays readable.

4. **The KPI comparisons point at the wrong baseline (defect).** Every figure is compared with "last Monday", but the header says 24 November 2026, which is a Tuesday. "3,184 vs 2,960" also sets a day still in progress against a full day. The viewer will read "rides are up 8%", which may simply be wrong. Fix: compare with the same weekday at the same time of day, and say so in the label. Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design", asks for a comparison the reader can trust. A wrong one is worse than none.

5. **"Rides by weekday" doesn't say what it shows (defect).** It has no y-axis and no values except Friday's "3,410". It doesn't say which week, and today isn't in it. A reader can't tell whether this is last week, an average, or this week so far, or how today's 3,184 compares. Fix: name the period in the title, label values or add an axis (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2), and mark today. If it doesn't help someone send a van, make it smaller.

**Smaller things.**
- The map uses a legend ("Docking station", "Empty") for two markers. Label the empty dot directly (Wilke).
- The "Docks" column is ambiguous: total docks or free docks? "Canal Basin 0 / 24" reads either way. Name it plainly, e.g. "Free docks".
- The restock table lists problems but offers no next step, such as which van is assigned or its ETA, even though the map knows van 2 is heading to Canal Basin. Put that in the row (common sense).
- The Rides nav item uses a shopping-cart icon, which signals commerce, not trips. Use a bike or route icon.
- "Stations now" also holds a van. A heading like "Stations and vans now" would cover what's in it.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI already has a comparison line under it. The structure is right; only the baseline is wrong.
- A restrained neutral palette and a clear three-step type scale (big number, label, caption) keep everything legible. That's what lets one alert color stand out once you move it.
- The restock table is plain and right-aligned on its numbers, sorted with the worst first. Keep it that way as it moves up the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the map and the table update live, and whether the map cards respond to hover or click.
