**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing" first, then straight to the Marina card with the teal border. From there it runs down the form to the teal Save button and finishes on the invoice table. This is an account settings screen in a SaaS product for marina operators. It feels calm, orderly, and trustworthy, a bit generic but not in a way that costs anything here.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it lets a marina's office manager see and change their plan, keep billing details current, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, competent settings page with a solid layout. What holds it back is that it handles money vaguely. Changing your plan, which commits real money, uses the same generic Save as fixing a typo in an email address. The page also refers to a card it never shows and to a charge date it never states.

**What would move it most**

1. **Changing plan is a money decision hidden in a generic form.** The three plan cards sit above Billing details, and the only commit is "Save changes" further down, after an email field, a currency menu, and a checkbox. Someone who clicks Harbor group and saves can't tell from this screen when the change starts, whether they pay a partial month now, or that the same button also sends their address edits. Nothing next to the cards gives the new price or the start date. This is a defect: it slows and worries exactly the person who is about to spend more. It breaks natural mapping, since the control sits far from what it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*), and it gives no help at the step where the task is complex (Nielsen, heuristic 10). The fix should make the plan change its own action, with a confirmation next to the cards. Show what changes and from which date, using facts only you have, such as how partial months are billed. Keep "Save changes" for the billing fields only.

2. **"Charge the card on file *before* the first of each month" doesn't say what will happen to the viewer's money.** Which card? None is shown anywhere on the page, and "Payment terminal" in the sidebar may or may not be where it lives. And before the first by how much: a day? A week? A stranger can read this as "we charge some time before the 1st" or "we charge on the 1st, in advance for the month." Unchecking it says nothing about what happens next, whether that's an invoice to pay by hand or a paused account. Because this line commits the viewer's money, an unclear wording here is a defect. It also makes the viewer remember something from another screen (Nielsen, heuristic 6). The fix: show the card on file here (brand, last four digits, a "Change" link), state the charge day exactly, and say in a few words what unchecking means. The italic on "before" shows you knew the timing mattered, but italics are no substitute for an exact date.

3. **You can see the invoices but you can't get them.** HL-1185 and the other invoice numbers look like plain gray text, with no underline, icon, or "Download" link. The first thing an office manager wants from this table is a PDF for the accountant. This is a defect unless the rows are clickable in the live page, and even then nothing on screen says so. Make the invoice number a visible link, or add a download action to each row.

**Smaller things.**
- Four italics ("*one* dock", "*no* booking fees", "*every* invoice", "*before*") add emphasis that doesn't carry meaning, so the one that matters (*before*) gets no more weight than the rest. Drop them all. This is taste.
- "Up to 150 slips, *no* booking fees" hints that Harbor charges booking fees, but the Harbor card never says so or says how much. Put the fee on Harbor's card so the viewer can compare. That's a fact only you have.
- "One boater list" on Harbor group is shorthand. A stranger can't tell that it means a shared list across marinas, if that's what it means. Restore the verb, e.g. "share one boater list."
- Billing email and invoice address aren't marked required or optional. Mark them, e.g. "optional" after the optional label (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- The text field, select, and unselected card borders look like a light gray that may fall under 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the field border color and darken it if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- The plan cards show the current plan by a filled radio and a teal border together, not by color alone. They also put the limit and the price in the same two lines on each card, so the three compare at a glance. Keep that structure.
- The single teal accent goes only on the current plan, the checkbox, and the one primary button, so the eye's path matches the order of the page. Don't add more color.
- The invoice table is right for its job: amounts are right-aligned in figures that line up, rules are quiet, and statuses are written as words. It needs a download action, not a restyle.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the plan cards and checkbox show a visible focus indicator when tabbing, and whether the whole card is the click target or only the small radio.
- Whether the invoice rows are clickable.
