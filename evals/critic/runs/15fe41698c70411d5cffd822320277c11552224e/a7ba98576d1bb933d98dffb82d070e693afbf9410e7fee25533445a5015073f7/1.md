**First read.** The eye starts at "Deliveries," drops to the row of four big numbers, then goes straight to the one blue bar, Tuesday, the only saturated block in the right half. It reads as a calm, well-made monthly operations dashboard for a small produce-box business. It's quiet, orderly and trustworthy, and a little flat: nothing tells me what happened this month.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the farm owner or operator checks it at month's end to see how November went against October and what to act on.

**Verdict.** As a layout this is solid, and it does its basic job. The weak spot is the data story. The line chart's label doesn't match what it plots, and neither chart says what it found. That leaves the viewer to do the arithmetic and reach the conclusions themselves.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "Boxes packed per day" chart doesn't plot days, and its numbers don't add up to the page's total.** *Defect, unless the data explains it.*
   - **What it shows.** There are 13 points between 1 Nov and 30 Nov, about one every two and a half days. The values sit at 100 to 190, which over 13 points comes to about 2,000 boxes. The card above says 4,736 boxes were delivered.
   - **Packed or delivered?** The bar chart beside it gives Tuesday alone 1,140, roughly 285 a Tuesday, which is higher than any point on the line.
   - **What the viewer meets.** Anyone who cross-checks sees three numbers that don't agree and starts doubting the whole page. "Packed" against "delivered" makes it worse, because it's unclear whether they're the same count.
   - **Fix.** Either plot every day (or every packing day) and say which, or rename the chart to what each point is, such as a packing run. Pick one verb, packed or delivered, for the whole page.
   - **Source.** Tufte, *Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few: the reader should never have to guess what a number measures.
   - **Where it would be fine.** If the points really are packing runs, the label is the only thing wrong.

2. **Neither chart says what it found, and the Tuesday highlight goes unexplained.** *Taste, but it decides whether the page is useful.*
   - **The titles.** "Boxes packed per day" and "Deliveries by weekday" are topic labels.
   - **The line chart.** November runs above October all month and the gap widens, which is the best news on the page. Nothing says so.
   - **The bar chart.** Tuesday is painted blue and given a number, but the viewer isn't told why (busiest day? a capacity limit?).
   - **Sunday.** The Sunday slot is empty with no label. "No deliveries on Sunday" and "missing data" look the same.
   - **No value axis.** Only Tuesday has a number, so Thursday, which is almost as tall, can't be read.
   - **Fix.** Let each title or a short caption state the finding in the maker's own words. Label the bar values or add a light axis, and mark Sunday explicitly.

3. **The headline numbers give the comparison but leave the subtraction to the viewer.** *Taste.*
   - Each card says "4,512 in October," and so on, which is good context. But to see that boxes rose about 5% and paused subscriptions fell, the viewer has to do the math four times.
   - Paused subscriptions is the one card where down is good, and nothing tells you that.
   - **Fix.** Add a direction and change to each card, styled so that "better" reads the same way on all four.

**Smaller things.**
- **Legend.** The line chart's legend sits top right, away from the lines. Label "November" and "October" at the right ends of the lines instead (Tufte; Wilke).
- **Blue means two things.** In the line chart, blue is "November." In the bar chart beside it, the same blue is "Tuesday" (highlighted). Side by side, this reads as if Tuesday were November data. Use a different emphasis for the bar, or make it clear the blue means "highlighted." *Common sense.*
- **Sidebar icons.** "Growers" has a document icon, "Boxes" a shopping cart, and "Settings" a sun-like glyph instead of the usual gear. All three take a moment to decode. Use icons that match the nouns. *Common sense (Apple HIG and Material on familiar iconography).*

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** The KPI row, the two-chart row and the table share exact left and right edges and equal gutters, so the page reads as one calm system. Keep it.
- **Restraint.** One blue, grays for context, one typeface with weight doing the hierarchy, and real white space. Nothing competes with the data.
- **The table.** Figures are right-aligned in tabular numerals, there are hairline row rules, and the column headers are quiet. Exact values are easy to scan.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Date range.** I can't tell whether "1 to 30 November 2026" is a control for changing the period. If it is, nothing about it says so.
