**First read.** Your eye lands on the large cream serif headline, "Know when the water is slack before you leave the car". It drops to the amber "Add your beach" button, then crosses to the tide card on the right. In about three seconds it reads as a tide and current planner for sea swimmers in Cornwall. It feels calm, local and made with care, not templated. The card is the proof of the product, but the bar chart at its top doesn't say what it shows.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing page for a paid swim-planning app. Its job is to get open-water swimmers to add a beach and later pay for Year-rounder.

**Verdict.** This is a strong page. The headline and the hero copy are specific, the main action is clear, and the look holds together. The biggest problem is the sample card. It is the only proof of the core promise, "marks the safe window", and it shows times, not windows. Its chart has no labels, and the one row the eye is pulled to has lost its swim/avoid verdict.

**What would move it most**
1. **The sample card shows moments, not the "safe window" the copy promises** (defect). The hero text says Slackwater "marks the safe window for each swim". The card on the right lists four single times (06:10, 09:30, 12:25, 18:40), each with a one-word verdict. A swimmer can't tell from it how long they have: when does the window at 06:10 open and close? The card also never tells them what makes a window safe.
   - The amber "Next" on the 12:25 row replaces that row's verdict. The one row the eye is drawn to is the only one that doesn't say Swim or Avoid.
   - Amber usually means caution, so "Next" on low water can also read as a warning (Apple HIG and Material, on status colors).
   - On a safety product, that is the line a swimmer acts on. Readers will split between "12:25 is the next safe slack" and "12:25 is the next thing to be careful about".
   - Fix: show each row as a span with a start and an end. Keep the Swim/Avoid verdict on every row. Mark "next" by position or a small tag beside the verdict, not instead of it.
2. **The bar chart doesn't say what it measures** (convention: Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few). The 22 bars above the rows have no axis, no units and no hour marks. There's no "now" line, and nothing ties them to the times below.
   - From the shape you can guess tide height with low water mid-day, but it's a guess. They could just as well be current strength, which is the thing that decides a swim.
   - As drawn, the chart is decoration standing in for the product's main claim.
   - Fix: name what the bars measure, put a few hour labels under them, and shade the swim windows on the chart itself. Then the chart and the rows tell one story, and the "safe window" becomes something you can see.
3. **Pricing breaks the grid and ends the page with nothing to do** (convention plus common sense).
   - The steps section uses three columns starting at x≈80, 469 and 859. The plans use two columns starting at x≈80 and 464. "Year-rounder" sits about 5 px left of "See the safe window" above it, which reads as a miss, not a choice (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I).
   - The pricing area leaves the right third empty, which looks like a missing third plan.
   - Neither plan has a button. A viewer who has just decided $4 a month is worth it has to scroll back to the hero.
   - Fix: put the plans on the three-column grid, and give each plan its own action, with the paid plan's ranked below the main "Add your beach".
4. **The plan copy leaves open what the viewer is paying for** (defect, on the line people hold you to).
   - "Two beaches and the tide for the next three days" says "the tide". The product's whole point is current and slack water. Does Dipper show the current and the safe window, or only the tide? Readers will take it either way. If it's only the tide, it is no better than the tide table they already use.
   - The prices are in "$" on a product "made in Penzance" that uses °C and "car park". A UK swimmer will wonder whether they're charged in dollars.
   - Fix: say in Dipper's line whether it includes the safe window. Confirm the currency is the one you'll actually bill in.

**Smaller things**
- The steps section says "reads the tide tables and the buoys" but never sets Slackwater against the tide app the swimmer already uses. A line on what their tide table can't tell them would let them judge the value for themselves. That fact is yours to supply.
- Runts (a short last line, often one word): "days." sits alone under Dipper and "reading." alone under "See the safe window". Rewrap or edit so the last lines aren't one word (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working**
- The headline names a real moment from the swimmer's day ("before you leave the car") and a real thing to know ("when the water is slack"). It could only belong to this product, so keep that voice.
- "Add your beach" is an exact, low-effort ask in the viewer's own terms. The underlined "See a sample morning" is clearly a lower-ranked second action. One primary, one secondary, no doubt between them.
- The palette has one job per color: deep green ground, cream type, amber kept for the logo mark, the main button and one status word. The serif headings with sans text give it a calm, local character without looking like a template.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- How the hero card and the two-column hero stack on a phone, where most swimmers will check this at the beach.
