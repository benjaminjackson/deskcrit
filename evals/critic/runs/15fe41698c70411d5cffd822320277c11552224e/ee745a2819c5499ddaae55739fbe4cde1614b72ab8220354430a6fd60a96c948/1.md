**First read.** My eye lands first on the big glossy orange, red and teal blob on the right. Then it jumps back left to the headline, "Share the kiln without double-booked shelves". The headline tells me what this is right away: booking software for shared pottery studios. Once I've read it, the brown "Add your studio kiln" button is easy to find. The page feels calm, clean and competent. The blob is the one part that feels like it came from a different site.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page for Kilnshare. The audience is people who run community pottery studios, plus their members and technicians. It needs to convince them to add their kiln or look at a sample firing.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The headline, body copy and asks are specific and well set. The biggest problem is the hero image. It's a generic gradient blob, and it takes the first look away from the copy while showing nothing about shelves, firings or bookings.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image says nothing and still gets seen first.** The blob takes up the right half of the hero (about x 715–1135, y 133–514). It's the biggest, most saturated and most heavily shadowed thing on the page, so it outweighs the headline. A visitor looks at it first, learns nothing, and has to go back left to start reading. The headline makes a concrete promise: no double-booked shelves, one list for the technician. A colored orb doesn't back it up or add to it. The image and headline don't add up to one idea. The orb's glossy highlight and drop shadow also belong to generic tech-startup hero art, not to a craft studio. That's what makes the page look like it could belong to any product, while the copy plainly belongs to one. *Defect* (it misdirects the first glance), with a *taste* argument on the mood. **Fix:** use the right half to show the promise. The most direct option is the thing the copy already names: a firing's shelves with members' work on them, or the technician's loading list. At minimum, make the image quieter than the headline so the eye starts at the words. If the warm kiln colors are the reason for the blob, they can survive in a picture that shows something.

2. **The page never compares itself to what studios use now.** The body copy explains the product clearly. But nothing puts it next to how studios handle this today, whatever they currently use to book shelves and split costs. So the visitor has to take "without double-booked shelves" on trust instead of recognizing their own problem. *Taste.* **Fix:** in the subhead or the feature row, briefly name the studio's current way of doing this and what goes wrong with it. That's a fact you have, not me. "See a sample firing" is already a good, concrete second ask. The fix only needs to give people a reason to click it.

**Smaller things.**
- "Bisque and glaze apart" (middle feature heading) has no verb, so the reader has to work out what it means. A small edit fixes it, for example "Keep bisque and glaze apart". Its body text already explains it.
- The three feature blurbs describe what the product does but stop before what it means for the visitor. For example, "Each member pays for the shelf space their work takes, not a flat fee" could end on why that matters to a studio owner. That's your claim to make, not mine.

**What's working.**
- **The headline names a real, specific pain.** "Double-booked shelves" could only come from someone who knows shared kilns. It's set large and dark with tight leading, and it holds together as one unit.
- **The asks are exact.** "Add your studio kiln" tells the visitor exactly what to do. "See a sample firing" is clearly secondary and is still a real link (underlined, not just colored). Using the brown accent only on the primary button keeps it the only strong color in the text column.
- **The type is quiet and well organized.** There are about four clear levels (headline, intro, feature heading, small body), the intro runs about 60 characters per line, everything shares one left edge, and the feature row sits on one even three-column grid.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the nav, the button and the link. What sits below the 800 px cut: the page ends in empty white, so check that the next section starts close enough to show the page continues.
