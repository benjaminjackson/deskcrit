**First read.** My eye goes to the "Plan and billing" title, then straight to the Studio card with its teal outline, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads as the billing settings page of a booking tool for tattoo studios. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also fairly stock: it could be the settings page of almost any SaaS product.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is where a studio owner checks or changes their plan, keeps billing details up to date and finds past invoices.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, well-built page that mostly works. What gets in the way is money: the page never says which plan you're on now, what happens when you click a different card, or when you'll be charged next. Those are the things someone opens this page to find out.

**What would move it most**

1. **It's unclear whether clicking a plan card changes your plan, and when you pay for it.** The plan cards sit above a divider, and the only "Save changes" / "Cancel" pair sits below Billing details, under the address field. That layout says Save belongs to the billing form. So an owner who clicks "Multi-studio" can't tell which of these happens:
   - The plan switches the moment they click, and they're charged $89.00.
   - Nothing happens until they press a button that looks like it belongs to a different section.

   On the wrong reading they either get an upgrade they didn't mean to buy, or leave thinking they upgraded when they didn't. Any line that commits someone to a price has to be readable only one way. This is a **defect** (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: the control should sit next to what it changes, and the screen should show how its parts relate). Fix it by giving plan changes their own action: either a button inside or right under the plan cards, or a confirmation step that states the new price and when it starts. Leave Save for the billing fields alone. If a plan change is prorated or starts at the next cycle, say so in one short line by the cards (Nielsen, heuristic 10: help where the task is complex). Only you know which rule applies, so that line has to come from you.

2. **Nothing says what you have now or what you'll pay next.** The teal outline on Studio could mean "your current plan" or "the one you just picked." Only the top invoice row (2 Nov, Studio, $39.00) hints that it's the current one. Nothing anywhere shows the next charge date or amount, even though that's usually the question that brings someone here. This is a **defect** (Nielsen, heuristic 1: show the current state). Label the current plan in words on its card, so the outline isn't the only cue. Then add a line near the top of the Plan section with the next charge date and amount. Same reasoning, as common sense: invoice numbers are plain text with no download or view link, and getting a PDF for the accountant is the other main reason to open this page. If the invoices can be downloaded, the row should show it.

**Smaller things**
- The "PRO" badge on Multi-studio: all three plans are paid, so it's unclear what "PRO" adds beyond "most expensive." It's also all caps, while the other badges on the page ("New", "Beta") use title case. Drop the badge, or replace it with words that say what it means, in the same case as the other badges.
- Input borders (Billing email, Currency, Invoice address) and plan card borders look very light gray on white. Measure them: field edges need 3:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.11).
- No field is marked required or optional. Since everything is prefilled this costs little, but an owner who clears the address field won't know whether it's needed. Mark the optional fields "optional."

**What's working**
- The type is well controlled. Title, section headings, field labels, body text and gray detail text each have a clear job, and there are no near-duplicate sizes. Keep that scale.
- The invoices table is right for exact lookups. Amounts are right-aligned with matching decimals, the rules are light, and nothing decorative is added. Keep it that plain when you add a download action.
- Teal is used sparingly: the selected plan, the checkbox and the primary button. That's why Save stands out. Don't spend the color anywhere else.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- What clicking a plan card actually does: whether it changes the plan right away, asks for confirmation or waits for Save. That behavior decides how serious finding 1 is.
- Focus rings on the plan cards, fields and buttons.
