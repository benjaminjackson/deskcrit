**First read.** My eye goes first to the one saturated blue bar (Saturday, 148), then up to the KPI row and "$24,815.60". This looks like the monthly overview for a small wine shop's owner. It's calm, tidy, and finished-looking: a clean, modern admin template. For about two seconds it feels trustworthy, and then the numbers start to wobble.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the screen the owner of Two Rivers Wine opens to see how November went against October and what's selling.

**Verdict.** The layout is solid and the main read works: revenue is up, Saturday is the big day, and you can see the top wines. What holds it back is the numbers. A chart that doesn't say what it counts, plus a sloppy "09" and money shown three different ways, make the owner doubt figures they should be able to take at a glance.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Bottles by weekday" doesn't say what it counts, and it doesn't add up to the headline number.** *Defect.* The chart has no value axis, no unit, and only one labeled bar. Scaled from Saturday's 148, the seven bars look like they total roughly 700, but the KPI above says 1,384 bottles sold in November. So the owner can't tell whether this is a total, an average per weekday, or web orders only. They can't trust the 148 either, and "Saturday is our biggest day" is the most actionable thing on the screen. Tufte and Few both require every chart to say what its numbers measure. Fix: put the basis in the title or a subtitle (total, or average per weekday, and which channel), and give every bar its value or show a light axis. (Bar lengths do look proportional from zero, so the scale isn't the problem. The missing meaning is.)
2. **The number formatting reads as data errors.** *Defect.* In the Best-selling wines table, Barolo shows "09" bottles. A leading zero on a count looks like a bug, and once someone spots one bug they question every other figure. Money is formatted three ways: "$24,815.60" and "$1,159.50" with cents, "$1,512" and "$38" without, all next to "$36.25". In the Revenue column the decimals don't line up, so the column reads as ragged (Bringhurst 4.4.3: align columns of figures on the decimal or the right edge). Gerstner's point applies too: things of one kind should follow one rule. Fix: pick one money format, either whole dollars everywhere at this level or cents everywhere, and drop the leading zero.
3. **The KPIs give October's raw number and leave the math to the owner.** *Taste.* "$24,815.60" over "$21,970 in October" is a real comparison, but to see whether things got better and by how much, you have to subtract in your head four times. On a glance screen, the change is the point. Fix: show the change in each card, as a direction and an amount or percent, computed from the figures already there, and keep the October value as the reference.

**Smaller things.**
- Web shop visitors: the y-axis (0 to 900) has no unit or period (visitors per day? per three-day bucket?), and the x-ticks at 1, 10, 19, and 30 Nov are unevenly spaced. Name the unit and space the ticks evenly.
- Web shop visitors: the November/October legend sits top right, away from the lines. Label each line at its right end and drop the legend (Tufte).
- Chart titles name topics ("Web shop visitors", "Bottles by weekday"), not findings. A title that states what happened ("Saturday sells the most") would save the owner a step. *Taste.*
- "Best-selling wines" is ordered by revenue, but the third wine sold only 9 bottles. Say "by revenue" in the heading, or the viewer will read "best-selling" as most bottles.
- The split between the two chart cards (about x=900) lines up with none of the KPI dividers above it, so the middle row looks placed by eye. Put that split on one of the KPI column lines (Müller-Brockmann).
- The Settings icon in the sidebar is a sun- or brightness-style glyph, not the gear people expect. Use the standard icon.

**What's working.**
- Restraint with color. Everything is gray except the current month's line and the one bar that matters, so blue reliably means "this month" or "look here". Keep the palette at one accent.
- Every KPI carries its October comparison in a smaller, lighter line under the value. The hierarchy inside each card is exactly right: label, value, context.
- Everything fits on one screen with generous, consistent card padding and a clear current-page state in the sidebar. The owner sees the whole month without scrolling.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "1 to 30 November 2026" can be clicked to change the period. As shown it reads as static text, and if it's the only way to compare other months, it needs to look clickable. Check this in the live app.
