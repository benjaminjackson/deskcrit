**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline, "Book life-drawing easels without a paper list", then moves right to the studio floor plan. There, the purple B4 is the only strong color and holds the eye. This reads as a calm, well-made landing page for a booking tool for life-drawing studios. It looks quiet and professional, and the floor plan makes the product easy to understand. The free easels are the one thing in the plan you have to search for.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop landing page meant to get studio owners or tutors to add their studio, with a sample studio as the lower-commitment option.

**Verdict.** It does its job: the purpose is clear in seconds, there is one clear ask, and the type and layout are disciplined. The biggest problem is the floor plan, the product's own picture. Taken easels are green, free ones are nearly invisible, and nothing explains the colors, so the screen that should sell the idea takes a second look to decode.

**What would move it most**
1. **The floor plan's color code works against the viewer** (defect, plus convention). Of the 18 easels, the 14 taken ones (excluding B4) are filled with sage green. The 3 free ones (A3, B6, C5) are white with a dashed border that looks too faint to see on the cream panel. Green normally means "available" (Apple HIG and Material status color conventions), so a first read says most easels are open. The "3 free" note in the corner then contradicts that, and the viewer has to work out the code backward. Taken, free, and yours are shown by fill alone, with no legend or label (WCAG 1.4.1). The free easels are what a student is looking for, and their only edge, the dashed line, looks well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of a component boundary. To fix it, make free easels the visible state: give them a clear edge or fill and a second cue such as a label or icon. Move taken easels to a neutral or muted fill that doesn't read as "go", and add a small key ("taken / free / yours") next to "18 easels, 3 free".

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks "a paper / list", leaving "list" alone on line three and splitting the noun phrase the promise rests on (Bringhurst 2.4.2 on lone last-line words; where to break a heading is common sense). Rebalance the lines so "paper list" stays together, for example by narrowing the measure so the break falls after "easels".
- The feature heading "Terms paid up front" covers only half its text, which goes on to say drop-ins pay per evening. A studio owner who takes drop-ins may read the heading as excluding them. Make the heading cover both cases (WCAG 2.4.6).

**What's working.**
- **A clear type scale.** There are about four sizes: headline, intro paragraph, feature headings, and small panel text. The steps are big, and the feature headings stand apart from their text by weight rather than an extra size. Keep this scale when you add sections.
- **The copy is specific and compares against what the viewer already uses.** "Without a paper list" sets the product next to what studios use today. The intro paragraph names concrete things: easels, evenings, who has paid, the model stand. "See a sample studio" lets the viewer judge the product themselves instead of trusting a claim. Keep both the paper-list comparison and the sample link.
- **The palette fits the subject.** Muted sage, warm cream, and one aubergine accent feel like a studio, not a fintech app. With purple used only for the button and the booked easel B4, the eye connects the ask to the result. Keep the accent that scarce.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the easel tiles in the sample studio are clickable, and if so their hover and focus states. If the plan is interactive, check that a selected or focused easel stays distinct from B4's "yours" purple.
