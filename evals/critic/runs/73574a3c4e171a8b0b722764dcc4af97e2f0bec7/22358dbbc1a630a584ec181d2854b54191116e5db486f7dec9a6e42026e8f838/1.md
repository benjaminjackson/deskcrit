**First read.** At a glance these read as one product. The left nav, the burgundy-and-gray palette, the serif type, the page titles and the content's left edge are the same on every screen. The sameness ends in three places. Settings is narrower than every other screen. The Support screen holds a money-transfer form. The Cards screen gives the nav's icons a different meaning.

**Verdict.** The visual system holds, but the set breaks in three places. The biggest is tile 4: the nav label promises one thing and the screen does another. The other two are tile 5's narrower content and tile 3's reuse of the nav icons.

**Breaks**
1. **Screen name matches screen content.** Tiles 1, 2, 3 and 5 keep it; tile 4 breaks it. The nav item and title say "Support", and the subtitle says "Talk to a member of the team". The screen itself is the transfer form: From / To / Amount / Date fields and a "Send money" button. The same "Send money" button sits on Transfers (tile 2). Someone who wants help lands on a payment form. Someone who wants to send money is sent to Transfers, though this is the screen with the form. This is a defect: the label no longer predicts what is behind it (Nielsen, consistency and standards; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). To fix it, put the transfer form under Transfers (as the screen that "Send money" opens) and give Support content that matches its name. The real support content has to come from you.

2. **Content right edge.** Tiles 1–4 run content from x≈272 to x≈1240 on the 1280-wide screens: the stat cards, the table and the form fields all end at 1240. On tile 5 the settings rows and their dividers stop at x≈832, so the toggles sit about 400 px short of where every other screen ends. Moving from Cards or Support to Settings, the page looks half-built or broken, and the toggles fall outside the column where the eye expects the right-hand edge. This is a convention (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one grid across the pages of a piece). Either run the rows to the shared 1240 edge, or make a narrow column part of the system and use it everywhere.

3. **One icon, one meaning.** In the nav on every screen, the house means Accounts, the three lines mean Transfers and the bar chart means Cards. On tile 3 the same three icons, in burgundy, label "Debit card", "Credit card" and "Virtual card". A user who has learned the nav reads the house on the Debit card tile as "Accounts" and the bar chart as charts, so the icons point the wrong way. This is a defect (Nielsen, consistency and standards). Give the card types their own icons, or drop the icons. While you're at it, the nav's bar-chart icon for "Cards" is a poor match on every screen.

**Smaller things.**
- Tile 3 repeats tile 1's three balance cards (Everyday account, Savings, Credit card) above the card list. Two account balances on a screen titled "Your debit and credit cards" blur the line between Accounts and Cards. Keep only what belongs to cards.
- Tiles 1 and 2 show the same "Recent activity" table with the same four rows. If Transfers is meant to show only transfers, filter it; if it really is the same feed, one of the two copies is extra.

**What's working.**
- The shell is exactly the same everywhere: brand block, nav order and icons, the selected-item pill, the title at x=272 with a gray subtitle beneath it. You always know where you are.
- The components match across screens. The burgundy filled primary and outlined secondary buttons are the same size on tiles 2 and 4. The white bordered cards and table share one corner radius and border. The category pills and the burgundy toggles use the same accent.
- The type scale is the same on every screen: page title, section heading, body and small label. Moving between screens doesn't change how important a heading looks.

Breaks: tile 3, tile 4, tile 5
