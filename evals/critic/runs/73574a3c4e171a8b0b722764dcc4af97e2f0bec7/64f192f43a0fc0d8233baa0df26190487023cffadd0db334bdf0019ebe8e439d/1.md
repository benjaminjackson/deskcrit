I've compared all six screens at full size, and I'm writing up the crit now. To save time I skipped opening the reference entries, so the sources below are cited from memory.

**First read.** This reads as one product. The sidebar, page title, gray canvas, teal accent and 264 px content edge are the same on every screen. It stops feeling like one product in three places: on New booking the sidebar highlights the wrong section, on Alerts the content steps in from the edge, and on Tide report the nav icons are reused to mean something else.

**Verdict.** The shell holds together, but tile 4 tells you that you're in the wrong place. That is the biggest break, because the sidebar's highlight is the one thing people use to find out where they are.

**Breaks**
1. **The sidebar highlights the screen you're on:** tiles 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 keep this; tile 4 breaks it. On New booking, "Crew" is highlighted (teal fill at y≈203–243, bold teal label), but the screen is a booking form whose button is "Book trip", the same button shown on Boats. Someone who arrives from Boats sees the highlight jump to Crew and thinks they've left the booking flow, or that the form is about assigning crew. Nielsen calls this match between the system and the real world, plus consistency: the location cue has to be true on every screen. **Defect.** Fix: highlight the section the form belongs to (Trips or Boats, whichever owns bookings), or none if it's a modal flow.
2. **Content starts at the 264 px edge:** tiles 1 to 5 keep this, with title, subtitle, cards, table, form and settings rows all starting at x=264; tile 6 breaks it. The Alerts list has a gray rule at x≈270 and its text starts at x≈290, so it sits about 26 px in from its own title and subtitle. It is also the only list in the set with no white panel or row dividers; Departures and Settings both have them. Moving to Alerts, the list looks like a quote or a nested block rather than the page's main content, and the shared left edge that makes the other screens feel ordered is lost. Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one column edge, kept on every page. **Convention, with a defect cost.** Fix: bring the list text back to x=264 and give alerts the same row treatment as the other lists (a white panel with hairline dividers, or Settings' ruled rows).
3. **One icon means one thing:** the sidebar sets up house = Trips, three lines = Boats, bar chart = Tides; tile 3 breaks it. The Tide report cards reuse these icons with other meanings: the house on "Next low tide", the three lines on "Wind", the bar chart on "Swell". Anyone who has used the sidebar will read the house as "Trips" and the lines as "Boats", so the cards seem to link to those sections. Nielsen's consistency heuristic applies here. **Defect.** Fix: give the tide, wind and swell cards their own icons, or no icons, and keep the nav icons for nav only.

**Smaller things.**
- Tile 3: the Tide report repeats the Trips summary cards ("Trips this week 12", "Boats free today 4", "Crew on shift 7") above the tide data. A page whose subtitle is "Low and high water for the next five days" opens on trip numbers instead, and moving from Trips to Tides looks like nothing changed. Drop them or replace them with tide data; which data is the maker's call.
- Tile 2: Boats ("Your fleet and who has booked it") shows the same Departures table as Trips, row for row. Moving between the two tabs, users see the same list twice and no list of the fleet. Show the boats, which only the maker can supply.
- Tile 5: the Settings rows end at x≈824, while every other screen's content runs to x≈1240. A narrower column suits a list of toggles, so it isn't a break. Just set that width on purpose and reuse it for other single-column screens such as Alerts.
- Tile 2 vs tile 4: the secondary button reads "Hold dates" on Boats and "Cancel" on the form. The style matches; just check that the outline button always means the less committal action.

**What's working.**
- The page header is the same on every screen: a bold title at the same size and position, then a gray subtitle. Each screen announces itself the same way.
- The control styles carry over exactly: the filled teal primary with an outlined teal secondary (tiles 2 and 4), the white cards with light borders (tiles 1 and 3), and the teal status pills. A new screen never brings in a new visual language.
- The palette stays to one teal accent on a cool gray and white base, and it's used the same way for the nav highlight, buttons, pills and toggles.
