**First read.** Five of the six screens read as one running app, Pacehound. Each has a white left sidebar with the same six items, a pale gray content area, a large page title with a gray subtitle under it, a bold section heading, and white cards or rows. Tile 4 (Friends) looks like it comes from a different app. The sidebar is gone, the navigation is a horizontal bar across the top, and all the content sits far to the left of where it is on every other screen.

**Verdict.** The set does not fully hold together, and tile 4 is the only screen that breaks it. Its navigation and content edge both move, so the user loses their place when they open Friends.

**Breaks**
1. **Where the navigation sits and how it shows the current page.** Tiles 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 keep it; tile 4 breaks it.
   - **What the others do:** a 224 px white sidebar on the left lists the items vertically, and the current page gets a pale orange pill behind it (Runs on tile 1).
   - **What tile 4 does:** a 64 px white bar across the top lists the same six items horizontally. The current page is marked only by orange text, with no pill.
   - **What the user experiences:** they click "Friends" in the sidebar and the sidebar disappears. The links they just used are now in a different place and laid out differently, and the "you are here" marker has changed style. To get back to Runs they have to find it again. Nielsen's consistency heuristic covers this: navigation that moves between screens of one product makes users relearn it.
   - **Fix:** put Friends in the same sidebar shell as the other five screens, with the same pill marking the current page.
   - Defect.

2. **The content edge and the content width.** Tiles 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 keep it; tile 4 breaks it.
   - **What the others do:** the title, cards and tables start at x = 264 and run to about x = 1240, filling the full width of the content area.
   - **What tile 4 does:** the "Friends" title and the form start at x = 40, 224 px further left. The two form columns stop at x = 1040, leaving about 240 px of empty space on the right that the other screens fill.
   - **What the user experiences:** the page title jumps sideways when they arrive, which makes it feel like they have left the product rather than moved within it. Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* calls for one grid across every page so content returns to the same edges.
   - **Fix:** this mostly resolves itself once the sidebar is back. Then make sure the form keeps the same left edge, and either span the full content width or narrow on purpose with a visible reason.
   - Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle on tile 4, "See what your running group did", promises a feed of friends' activity, but the screen holds only an invite form. On the other screens the subtitle describes what is on the page (tile 2: "Pick a plan for your next race", and a list of plans follows). Either show the activity, or change the subtitle so it describes the invite form.

**What's working.**
- **The page header pattern:** the title is the same size and position on all five sidebar screens, with a gray subtitle under it and a bold section heading below that (Runs, Plans, Stats, Settings, Alerts). Every screen opens the same way, so users always know where to look first.
- **One accent color with one job each:** orange-red is used only for the current page, primary buttons, the toggles that are on, pill tags and the personal-best icons. Because nothing else uses it, the eye goes to what is active or can be acted on.
- **One look for each kind of element:** the white 1 px-bordered cards and tables, the pill tags (run types on tile 1, plan levels on tile 2), and the filled-plus-outlined button pair (tile 2, and also on tile 4) all match across screens.

Breaks: tile 4
