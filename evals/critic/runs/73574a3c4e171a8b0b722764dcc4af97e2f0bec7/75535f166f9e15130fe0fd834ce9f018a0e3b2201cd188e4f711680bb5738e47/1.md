**First read.** At a glance this is one product. The same Fernhill sidebar, warm gray canvas, serif type and green accent appear on all five screens, and the content always starts at the same left edge (x=264). It stops feeling like one product when you read the screens: on two of them the page heading doesn't match the nav item that's lit, and one of those also has a smaller heading and a narrower column.

**Verdict.** The visual system holds, but the naming doesn't. The biggest break is tile 5: the nav says Settings, the page says "Restock / Order seedlings from your suppliers", and the content is a list of preference toggles. A user can't tell where they are.

**Breaks**
1. **The lit nav item, the page title and the content all name the same place.** Tiles 1, 2 and 3 keep this: Shop shows a shop overview, Orders shows "Orders" with an order table, Plants shows plant stock. Tiles 4 and 5 break it.
   - **Tile 5:** Settings is lit, the heading reads "Restock", the subtitle promises ordering seedlings from suppliers, and the content is four app preferences (stock alerts, recycled boxes, packing slips).
   - **Tile 4:** Customers is lit and the heading says "Customers / Your regulars and new sign-ups", but the content is a supplier purchase form (Supplier, Plant, Delivery, Quantity). It also reuses tile 2's "Place order" label, which on tile 2 is about customer orders.
   - **What the user experiences:** They click Settings and land on a page named for another task. They click Customers and get a form to buy stock. The nav stops telling them where they are, and "Place order" means two different actions in two places.
   - **Basis:** Nielsen's heuristic "Consistency and standards" (one name for one thing in every place) and Krug's point that every page needs a name that matches what you clicked.
   - **Fix direction:** Make each heading match its nav label and put the matching content under it. The supplier form and "Order seedlings" text belong under one name, and the toggles under Settings. Give supplier ordering its own button label so it isn't confused with customer orders.
   - Defect.
2. **One heading size and position on every screen.** Tiles 1 to 4 set the page heading at about 36 px, starting near y=40, with the subtitle at about y=94. Tile 5 breaks it.
   - **Tile 5:** The heading is visibly smaller (about 30 px) and sits higher. The subtitle is at about y=87, so the whole header block is 6 to 7 px higher.
   - **What the user experiences:** Moving between pages, the title jumps and shrinks. It reads like a page from a different build, not a deliberate level in the type scale.
   - **Basis:** Bringhurst on a consistent type scale; Müller-Brockmann on fixed positions within the grid.
   - **Fix direction:** Use the same heading style and top spacing as the other screens.
   - Defect (craft).
3. **Content fills the full content column.** Tiles 1 to 4 run from x=264 to x=1240: cards, table and the two-column form all end at 1240. Tile 5 breaks it.
   - **Tile 5:** The settings rows and toggles stop at about x=824, leaving the right 40% of the column empty.
   - **Basis:** A shorter row for toggles is defensible, because it keeps each label near its switch. But the set gives no other narrow column for it to line up with, so it reads as an accident.
   - **Fix direction:** Either run the rows to 1240 like everything else, or make the narrow width a real column of the grid. For example, it could end where the form's left field ends (x=736).
   - Taste, leaning convention (Müller-Brockmann).

**Smaller things.**
- **Tile 3, icons:** The plant cards reuse the nav icons. Monstera has the Shop house, Fiddle leaf fig has the Orders list, Snake plant has the Plants chart. On the same screen those icons name pages, so they suggest links or meanings they don't have. Use one plant icon, or none.
- **Tile 3, stat cards:** The Shop screen's stat cards (Orders today, Revenue) appear unchanged on Plants. Two of the three aren't about plants, so the two screens blur together. Keep only the stock figures here, or drop the row.
- **Tiles 2 and 4, secondary buttons:** Both have an outlined secondary button with different widths and jobs ("Save draft", "Cancel"). The style matches, which is fine. Just check that "Cancel" and "Save draft" sit in the same position relative to the primary button on every form.

**What's working.**
- The sidebar is pixel-identical on every screen, and the active item is always marked the same way (green text on a pale green pill). Moving between pages, the frame never moves.
- The left content edge (x=264) and the heading-to-content rhythm are consistent on tiles 1 to 4. Cards, tables and form fields share one border, radius and white-on-gray treatment, so different kinds of content still look like one system.
- The status chips and the green primary button are the same everywhere. Green consistently means an action or a state.
