**First read.** Five of the six screens read as one product right away: a white 224 px sidebar with the Pacehound wordmark, a coral pill on the current item, and a bold title with a gray subtitle at x=264. The Friends screen (tile 4) breaks that. The sidebar turns into a top bar, the content jumps to the left edge, and the page holds a settings form. A second look shows a quieter problem: Plans, Stats and Friends each hold content that belongs to a different nav item.

**Verdict.** The visual system mostly holds, but the set does not work as one product. The biggest break is tile 4, which changes the navigation pattern and the content edge on one screen. Next to it, the pages' content does not match their nav labels.

**Breaks**
1. **Navigation and content edge: tiles 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 keep it, tile 4 breaks it.** On every other screen the nav is a 224 px left sidebar, the content edge is at x=264 and the title baseline sits near y=56. On tile 4 the nav is a 64 px horizontal top bar, the content edge is at x=40 and the title drops to about y=120. The current-item marker also changes from a filled coral pill to plain coral text. When users click Friends, the whole frame moves under the cursor. The nav they just used disappears from where it was, and their eye has to find the page again 224 px further left. It looks like a different app or an old build. This breaks Nielsen's "consistency and standards" heuristic and Müller-Brockmann's point that one grid carries every page of a system (*Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). It's a defect. Fix: use the same sidebar and the same 264 px content edge on Friends.

2. **Page content matches the nav label: tiles 1, 5 and 6 keep it; tiles 2, 3 and 4 break it.**
   - **Tile 2, "Training plans / Pick a plan for your next race":** the page shows no plans. It shows the same Recent runs table as tile 1.
   - **Tile 3, "Stats":** the page repeats tile 1's three stat cards exactly, then shows three plan cards (Half marathon plan, First 10K, Base building). These are the plans that tile 2's subtitle promises.
   - **Tile 4, "Friends / See what your running group did":** the page shows a form for distance unit, week start, max heart rate and profile visibility. Those are settings, so settings are now split between tile 4 and tile 5. Tile 5's subtitle, "Units and privacy," shows no units.

   As a result, someone who wants to pick a plan has to guess that plans live under Stats. Someone who wants to change units goes to Settings and doesn't find them. This is a defect against Nielsen's "match between system and the real world" heuristic and Krug's point that the label has to predict what's behind it (*Don't Make Me Think*). Fix: put each block under the nav item that names it. That means plans on Plans, units with the other settings, and activity from friends on Friends. If tiles 2 to 4 are placeholder mockups, they need their real content before the set can be judged.

3. **One label, one action: tile 2 keeps it, tile 4 breaks it.** On tile 2, "Log a run" is a primary button that starts a new run entry. On tile 4 the same coral button with the same label submits a settings form, next to "Cancel." Users who learned the button on Plans will expect it to open a run log, and instead it saves their heart-rate and privacy settings. This is a defect against consistency and standards. Fix: give the form's button a label that says what it does, such as "Save." Keep "Log a run" for the one action it names.

**Smaller things.**
- **Tile 3 icons:** the plan cards use the sidebar's Runs (house), Plans (lines) and Stats (bars) icons as plan icons, in coral. An icon that means a destination in the nav now means something else. Use distinct plan icons, or none.
- **Tile 6 content edge:** the alert list's rule sits at x=270 and its text at x=290, off the 264 px edge that the title and every other screen's content use. Start the rule at 264, or hang it outside so the text aligns.
- **Tiles 1 and 3:** the stat cards are repeated word for word on two pages. If Stats is meant to go deeper than the Runs summary, it needs content of its own. Only the maker knows what that content is.

**What's working.**
- **Sidebar (tiles 1, 2, 3, 5, 6):** the width, item order, icons and coral-pill active state are identical on all five. Users always know where they are.
- **Header block:** the title and subtitle size, weight, gray and position match on all six screens, including tile 4. That gives every page the same opening.
- **Palette and controls:** one coral accent carries the active nav, the primary buttons, the type tags and the toggles. Outline buttons ("Import file," "Cancel") share one style, and cards and table share one border and radius. The set reads as one palette throughout.

Breaks: tile 2, tile 3, tile 4
