**First read.** The set reads as one product. All five screens share the same sidebar, the same "Marrow" wordmark, the same light-gray canvas, one rust accent, and the same page title with a subtitle below it. Content starts at the same left edge (x≈264) and ends at the same right edge (x≈1240) on every screen. The one place it stops feeling like one product is the Pantry screen (tile 3), where every icon suddenly turns solid.

**Verdict.** The set mostly holds together. The single break is tile 3: its icons are drawn in a different style from the rest of the product.

**Breaks**
1. **Icon style.** Tiles 1, 2, 4 and 5 keep it; tile 3 breaks it. On tiles 1, 2, 4 and 5 the sidebar icons are thin outline glyphs (the book, calendar, jar, cart and sliders on Recipes are all stroked). On Pantry, every sidebar icon is filled solid: the book, calendar, jar and cart are dark blocks, and only the Settings sliders stay as lines. The three Shelf cards repeat the solid style with a filled circle, jar and cart. The sidebar is the one element that is meant to stay put as people move between screens. When its icons change weight on one screen, the nav seems to jump or change state on Pantry, and the solid dark glyphs pull the eye toward the inactive items. It also makes this screen look like it came from a different kit. The usual practice in both Material Design and Apple's HIG is one icon family with one style across the product. *Defect* (a small one): it costs a moment of "did something change?" and weakens trust that the sidebar is the same control. Fix: switch tile 3 back to the outline set used everywhere else. If the Shelf cards need icons, take them from the same outline family in the accent color.

**Smaller things.** None found at full size. On every screen the sidebar item positions, the active-item pill, the title and subtitle sizes, the section headings, the white bordered containers, and the edges of the content column all match.

**What's working.**
- **Stable shell.** The sidebar width, item spacing (about 42 px apart at the same y on every screen), and the rust-tinted active pill are identical everywhere, so where you are never moves.
- **One content column.** Stat cards, tables, form fields and the settings list all sit between the same left and right edges. Recipes, Plan, Shopping and Settings feel like pages of one document even though they hold different kinds of content.
- **Consistent controls.** The rust color is used the same way on every screen: filled primary button, outlined secondary button, tinted diet tags, and toggles. You learn it once and it means the same thing everywhere.

Breaks: tile 3
