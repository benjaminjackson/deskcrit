**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild" at top left, jumps to the blue "Sign up" button, then drops to the flat blue-grey drawing. It reads as a ferry operator's news item with a sign-up box for timetable alerts. It feels calm, plain and civic, but the form's labels, the caption, the note under the button and the footer are specks, so parts of the page look unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: tell passengers what changed at the landing stage, and get them to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** It doesn't work yet. The page's only action can't be completed with confidence, because the two form fields have labels about 4 px tall. The article's only picture also shows none of the changes the text describes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form labels and all the small text can't be read.** The labels above both fields (about x 789, y 135 and y 200) are about 4 px tall. Even zoomed in they are smudges that might say "Your name" and "Home landing". So are the note under Sign up (y≈325), the figure caption (y≈437) and the footer (y≈757). A visitor sees two empty boxes and a button and has to guess what goes in each. They also can't read the promise under the button (probably how often you'll be emailed and how to stop) at the moment you ask them to commit. Labels that can't be read act like no labels (NN/g, "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful"; WCAG 3.3.2). The text is about a third of Apple HIG's 11 pt minimum. Fix: add one small step to the type scale, about 14 px, and use it for all of this text, with labels in the dark text colour directly above each field. Once the grey note, caption and footer are that size, check them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). Defect.
2. **The form doesn't seem to ask how the alerts will reach you.** As far as the labels can be made out, the fields are a name and a home landing. Neither is an email address or a phone number, so the visitor can't tell where "timetable changes" would arrive. This is fine only if the hidden labels actually ask for a contact address. Once the labels are readable, make sure one field is the contact (email or phone) and that the label says so. If "Home landing" is a field, make it a list of landings rather than free text, so nobody misspells a stop (Nielsen, error prevention). Defect.
3. **The illustration is a placeholder, and its boat looks like a second button.** The drawing (x 96–656, y 220–420) is a few flat blocks on pale water. The headline and text promise wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water and a kiosk, and none of them is visible. The pale grey block (probably the shelter, x 176–296) nearly disappears into the sky behind it. So the reader looks at the picture to see what changed and learns nothing, and the page looks unfinished. Worse, the boat (x 496–606, y 340–364) is a rounded bar in exactly the "Sign up" blue with the same corner radius. These are the only two saturated shapes on the page, so the boat reads as an unlabelled button in the article column and takes attention from the real one. Fix: use a photo of the rebuilt stage, or redraw it to show the ramp meeting the deck and the covered area, with each part clearly separated in value from what's behind it. Either way, keep the action blue off the picture so it belongs only to the button. Defect (common sense; no approved source).

**Smaller things.**
- Neither field is marked as required or optional. With two fields the cost is small, but mark the optional one "(optional)".
- The illustration's right edge (x≈656) stops about 30 px short of the text column (about x 686), so it's nearly full width but not quite. Make it the full column width, or clearly narrower (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- The card's top border (y≈57) sits about 5 px above the H1's cap height (y≈62). Align the two exactly, or drop the card clearly lower.
- The page is heavy at top left (taste): content ends at y≈510, the right column is bare below the card, and the footer is stranded at y≈757. Bring the footer up to a set distance below the content.

**What's working.**
- The headline is clearly in charge. It is the biggest and darkest type, where reading starts, and both headings are bold against regular body text, so the levels read at once. "Get timetable changes" names what you get, not just the action. Keep it.
- The body text reads well: about 18 px with comfortable spacing between lines, and about 70 characters per line, within Bringhurst's 45–75.
- Colour is held back: blue-grey neutrals plus one saturated blue. Once the boat stops borrowing that blue, "Sign up" will be the only strong colour on the page, as it should be.
