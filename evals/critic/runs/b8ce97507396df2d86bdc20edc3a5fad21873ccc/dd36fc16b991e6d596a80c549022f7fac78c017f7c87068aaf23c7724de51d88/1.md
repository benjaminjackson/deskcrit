**First read.** The eye goes to "$4.82M" in the first card, then down into the blue bars, then to the small plan table on the right. Its total is also $4.82M, which makes a careful reader stop. It reads as a SaaS revenue dashboard for a founder or finance lead. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, though fairly generic.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: it gives a founder or finance lead a quick, trustworthy read of how revenue did in 2025, with numbers they can quote.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: the chart is honest and every figure has a comparison. But the numbers on screen don't add up, and on a finance screen that one problem undoes the rest. The second weakness is that the date range, the control a user is most likely to want to change, doesn't look like a control.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan table's total doesn't match the chart beside it** (defect). "Revenue by plan, 2025" (right column) totals $4.82M. That is exactly the "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" figure in the first card. The twelve monthly bars, labelled "recognised revenue", add up by eye to about $4.49M for 2025. So two different totals are both labelled 2025 revenue. ARR doesn't fit neatly either: December's bar of about $447K times 12 is near $5.4M, not $4.82M.
   - What the reader meets: anyone who checks one figure against another finds a gap, and then stops trusting every number on the page. Anyone who doesn't check may quote the wrong figure to a board.
   - Why: a label has to say exactly what a number measures (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*; Few). On top of that, financial reporting has to reconcile.
   - Fix: name the measure. If the table splits ARR, title it "ARR by plan, Dec 2025". If it's recognised revenue, show plan figures that add up to the chart's total. Then make sure the ARR card and the December bar can be squared, or say in a footnote why they differ.
2. **The "Jan to Dec 2025" pill doesn't look like something you can change** (defect). At the top right, it's a pale grey rounded box with plain text, with no chevron, calendar icon or edge. It reads as a caption. The period is the main thing a user of a revenue dashboard changes, and a user who wants Q4 or 2024 may conclude the page can't show it.
   - Why: the control works, but nothing on it tells you so; Norman calls that missing cue a signifier (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*).
   - Fix: add a chevron or calendar icon and a visible edge. If it really is only a label, drop the pill shape and set it as plain text.

**Smaller things.**
- **Page title vs. KPI figures:** "Revenue overview" and the three KPI figures look only a few pixels apart in size, roughly 26 px and 30 px; measure to confirm. Both are bold near-black, so they read as one level. Shrink or lighten the title so the numbers are clearly the largest type (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8).
- **Equal KPI cards:** the three cards are identical, so nothing marks ARR as the headline; only left-to-right order puts it first. If ARR is the main answer, give it a larger figure or a wider card (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). This is a common dashboard pattern, so it costs little.
- **Lower row off the grid:** the KPI cards split the width into thirds with about 18 px gutters. The chart (right edge around x 960) and the plan table (starts around x 1012, about a 52 px gap) line up with none of the card edges. Put the chart under the first two cards and the table under the third, with the same gutter (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).
- **Headings off by a few pixels:** "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025" sit about 4 px apart vertically, which reads as a slip. Put them on one baseline.
- **Empty lower right:** below the four-row table there's roughly 230×330 px of blank space that looks left over, not planned. Moving the table onto the card grid shrinks it, or the column could hold something that ends level with the chart. This is my taste.
- **Chart title:** "Monthly revenue, 2025" names a topic but not what happened. Something like "Monthly revenue rose 43% in 2025, with dips in April and July" would state it. This is my taste; topic titles are normal on dashboards.
- **Nav vs. page title:** the active nav item is "Overview", the page is titled "Revenue overview", and "Revenue" is a separate item below it, so users can't tell what each covers. Make the names match. Also set "Settings" apart from the data sections with a gap or a divider at the bottom.
- **No shortcuts:** there's no export on the chart or table and no search, which a user preparing a board report will want. Add export at least (Nielsen, heuristic 7).

**What's working.**
- **The chart:** it's honest and quiet. The axis starts at zero, the gridlines are faint and there's one blue series. The subtitle states the unit and basis ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue"), and a source line says "closed invoices only". Keep all of that; it's what makes fixing finding 1 worth doing.
- **Comparisons on every card:** each KPI card names its period and baseline ("+3 pts vs 2024"), so the reader sees whether a number is good, not just how big it is. The green is backed by a "+" sign and words, so the meaning doesn't depend on colour.
- **Colour discipline:** blue appears only on the data and green only on the changes; everything else is neutral. The chart is the one coloured mass, so colour points straight at the data.
