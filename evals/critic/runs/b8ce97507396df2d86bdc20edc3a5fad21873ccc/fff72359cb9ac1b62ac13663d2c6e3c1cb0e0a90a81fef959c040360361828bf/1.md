**First read.** The eye lands on the big bold headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops through a three-line paragraph and three plain lines, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a small local ferry company's website, aimed at day-trippers and cyclists. It feels calm, tidy and quiet: one blue, one typeface, lots of white. It's also thin, with no picture, no header, and an empty bottom third.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of the ferry's website. Its job is to sell the crossing to a would-be passenger and send them to the timetable.

**Verdict.** It does the basic job. The headline makes a clear, specific promise and the one button says exactly what it opens. But it holds back what a passenger came for (when, how much, where) and spends the space below on nothing. The biggest problem is that the passenger still has to click before they can plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The screen withholds the facts a passenger needs to plan, while its bottom third is empty.** All the content sits between y≈70 and y≈548, and the 250 px below it is blank, so the screen looks top-heavy, as if something failed to load. Meanwhile the copy stays vague just where planning starts:
   - "every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" gives no first or last sailing.
   - There's no fare (only that bicycles are free).
   - "the north landing" assumes the reader already knows where that is.

   So someone deciding whether to go has to click away just to find out if the 7 pm boat exists or what it costs. Fix: put the next few departures (or at least the first and last sailing), the adult fare and where you board, in one line or a small strip next to the button. Use the empty lower band for that strip, or for a photo of the boat or the bay. A photo would also back up "calm" and "open deck", which the page currently asks you to imagine. Keep the button for the full timetable. This is a defect for the planning visitor; the balance point is Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance". If the live page continues right below the fold, the balance half falls away, but the missing facts still stand.
2. **There is no site header or navigation.** Apart from the small blue "Coastal Ferry Co." line at the top, there's no logo, no links (Timetable, Fares, Tickets, Getting here) and no way to see what else the site holds. A visitor after tickets or directions has only the timetable button. A transport site is expected to have a standard header (Nielsen Norman Group, on users expecting sites to work like the ones they already know). Fix: add a plain header with a real logo and the three or four tasks a passenger comes for, and leave the hero as it is. Convention. This doesn't apply if the capture is a section cut from below an existing header.
3. **The three selling points blur into the paragraph.** "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" (x≈540–740, y≈368–455) has the same weight, colour and centring as the paragraph above. It's only a hair smaller (about 18 px against about 20 px). Its line spacing (about 33 px) is almost the paragraph's (about 31 px), and the gap above it (about 35 px) is no bigger. Nothing marks it as a list, so it reads as a leftover tail of the paragraph, and "bicycles travel free", a real reason to choose this ferry, gets skimmed past. Bringhurst (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 3.1.1) warns that sizes this close look like mistakes, not separate levels. Arnheim's grouping by proximity says the gaps around the list must be clearly bigger than the spacing inside it. Fix: set the list at body size and separate it by structure: markers or small icons, a block on a shared left edge, or a row of three. Also give it about twice as much space above and below as between its own lines. Convention, and cheap to fix.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Cross the bay in twelve / calm minutes", which splits the number from its unit, so the one promise arrives in two pieces. Break it as "Cross the bay in / twelve calm minutes" (Bringhurst 2.4.6 on keeping numbers with their units; for headings this is common sense).
- Everything is centred out of habit. Centring suits the short headline and the button. The unmarked list is harder to scan with three ragged starts (Tschichold), and the fix in finding 3 takes care of that.
- The eyebrow "Coastal Ferry Co." is bold and in the button's blue, the page's only action colour, so it looks like a link but isn't clearly a logo. Make it a proper logo in the header, or set it in the text colour. Taste.

**What's working.**
- **The headline is specific and believable.** "Twelve calm minutes" gives a number and a feeling, and nobody else's ferry could claim it word for word. It's also by far the biggest, heaviest thing on the page, so the focal point and the message are the same. Keep both.
- **One blue does all the colour work.** The only saturated, filled shape on the page is the button, so the eye ends on the action with nothing competing. The order of headline, copy, button still holds in greyscale.
- **The button label "See the timetable"** says exactly what happens when you click, in the passenger's own words, and the button is a large, easy target.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the page continues just below y=800. That decides how much of the empty bottom band is real.
- Whether the button shows a visible focus ring. Keyboard users need it, since this is the page's only action.
