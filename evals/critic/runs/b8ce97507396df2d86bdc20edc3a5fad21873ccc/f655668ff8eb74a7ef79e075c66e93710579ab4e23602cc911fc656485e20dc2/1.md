It jumps to the blue Sign up button, then drops to the blue rounded shape inside Figure 1, which looks like a second button. It reads as a short news item from a ferry operator with a sign-up box for timetable alerts beside it. It feels calm, plain and civic, but the picture and the form both look unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a ferry operator's web page for passengers: it tells them about the rebuilt landing and asks them to sign up for timetable-change notes.

**Verdict.** The page is well ordered and easy to read, but it fails at its one action: the sign-up form has nowhere to send the note it promises. The illustration is also too abstract to show anything the article claims.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form can't reach anyone.** The card on the right (about x 760–1120) asks only for "Your name" and "Home landing". The note under the button says "We send one short note when the timetable changes" but doesn't say where it goes or how. A passenger can't tell how signing up would ever reach them, and as drawn the form can't deliver anything. Add a labelled contact field (email or phone). If the user is already signed in, say where the notes will go instead. Rewrite the note so it says how and when they hear from you, for example: "We'll email you once whenever your landing's timetable changes." Defect.
2. **Figure 1 shows none of what the article says changed.** The picture (x 96–656, y 220–420) is the biggest thing on the page, but it's just four flat blocks: a pale box, a dark bar, a blue rounded bar and a blue band. The caption has to explain what they are. The text talks about wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, a kiosk, and above all "ramps now meet the deck at every tide". None of these can be seen in the picture, so it reads as a placeholder or an image that didn't load, and the page looks unfinished. Use a photo or drawing that shows the ramp meeting the deck at low water, since that is the article's main point. If it has to stay a diagram, label the parts. If this is a capture where the real image failed to load, check it in a live browser. Defect (common sense).
3. **"Home landing" is a free-text box for a short fixed list.** The second field (about x 789–1091, y 240–280) looks exactly like the name field. Users have to remember and type the operator's exact name for their landing, and "north", "North landing" or a typo may match nothing, so they get no notes or the wrong ones. Make it a picker that lists the landings (Nielsen: error prevention; recognition rather than recall). Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- The boat in Figure 1 (x 496–606, y 340–364) uses the Sign up button's blue, corner radius and size, so it reads as a second control and weakens the one call to action. Keep that blue for controls only and draw the boat in a muted slate or navy (Apple HIG and Material Design, accent and colour roles).
- The left column has two right edges: the intro text runs to about x 685 and the picture stops at x 656, so the gap to the card changes. Make the picture exactly as wide as the text, or the text as wide as the picture (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- The content block sits off-centre: the left margin is 96 px and the right is about 161 px. That is too little to look like a deliberate asymmetric layout and too much to look centred. Either centre the block or commit to a clearly left-weighted layout (taste).
- Neither form field is marked required or optional. Mark the optional one, or drop the name field if notes don't need it.
- The footer at y 749 ("…a sample for a fixture image. All names and places are invented.") is scaffolding text. Replace it with the real footer before this ships.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean: headline, then form heading, then one solid blue button. There are four type sizes, and the headings and small text differ from the body by weight and grey as well as size. Keep this order when revising.
- The body text reads easily. It's about 17 px with lines of about 66 characters, generous line spacing and an even ragged right edge.
- Spacing does the grouping without rules: each label sits tight above its field, the caption sits tight under the figure, and the ask is enclosed in one card that sits level with the headline.
