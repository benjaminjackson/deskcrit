**First read.** The eye lands on "Account settings", the only large type on the screen, then moves down four identical white cards to the blue outline buttons on their right. Next it catches the highlighted "Billing" in the sidebar, and that is where it snags: the page under it isn't Billing. It reads as a calm, tidy, fairly generic settings hub for a SaaS team product.

**What it's for.** Assumed: the account-settings hub of a web app, where a workspace member finds a group of settings and opens it to check or change something.

**Verdict.** The visual craft is clean, but the page fails at its one job as a hub. It doesn't tell people where they are, and it doesn't give them a reliable map of the settings. The sidebar and the cards look like two different site maps that disagree.

**What would move it most**
1. **The sidebar and the page disagree about where you are.** The sidebar marks "Billing" as current with a pale blue fill and bold blue text (top left, third item). But the heading says "Account settings", and the content is an overview that starts with Profile and puts Billing third. Someone who clicked Billing lands on what looks like the wrong page and has to hunt for the billing card. Someone who came to the overview is told they're on Billing. A current-page marker only helps if it's accurate (Nielsen heuristic 1, visibility of system status; heuristic 4, consistency). Fix: make the marker, the heading and the content name the same place. Either add an "Overview" item and mark that one here, or make this the Billing page with "Billing" as its heading and billing content below. If the capture just caught a stale nav state, this goes away once the menu marks the right item. **Defect.**
2. **The names and sections don't match up, so you can't build a map of the settings.** The sidebar lists six areas: Profile, Notifications, Billing, Members, Integrations, Security. The cards show four: Profile, Notifications, Billing, Sessions. "Sessions" has no sidebar item, so you have to guess it lives under "Security". Members, Integrations and Security have no card, so the overview looks complete but isn't. On top of that, the header says "Workspace: Harbour Studio" while the title says "Account settings". You can't tell whether a change here affects only you or the whole team, and that matters most for Billing and Members. Fix: use one name per area in both places, show every area in the overview (or drop the overview), and label or separate personal settings from workspace settings (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, conceptual models). **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- Two indexes side by side: the sidebar and the card stack both list Profile, Notifications and Billing, so the page offers two ways to the same places and makes you wonder whether they go to the same place. Give each one its own job: the sidebar navigates and the main area holds the actual settings, or the cards become the full index and the sidebar goes (Nielsen heuristic 8).
- Cards describe each setting instead of showing its current value. Profile shows no name or photo, Notifications doesn't say what's on, and Billing mentions "the address on file" without showing it. Show the current value on each card, as Sessions already does with "You are signed in on three now". This is my judgement as a designer (taste), not a published rule.
- Billing copy ("Your plan renews on 1 March. The invoice goes to the address on file."): this is a date and a charge people will hold you to. It gives no year and no amount, and "address" could mean an email or a postal address. Name the year, the amount and where the invoice goes. Billing's only button, "View invoices", also doesn't let you change the address the line mentions.

**What's working.**
- **One strict grid.** The left edges sit at x=64 and x=304, the right edge of the cards and the header's workspace label at x=1216, and all four buttons end on one edge. The sidebar's first item and the page title start on the same line. The page reads as ordered at a glance, so keep these edges as rows get added.
- **Colour is kept for things you can act on.** The one blue appears only on the buttons and the selected nav item. Everything else is grey, and the near-black / mid-grey / white / light-grey steps carry the structure. Anything blue reads at once as clickable or selected.
- **Hierarchy by weight, not size.** A 28 px title sits over bold near-black card headings, which sit over regular grey descriptions. Each card scans as a heading plus one plain sentence (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). The descriptions are written in the reader's terms.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether clicking "Billing" really lands on this overview, or the capture caught a stale nav state. Check in the live app, because finding 1 depends on it.
- Visible keyboard focus on the menu items and buttons, and whether the header covers focused controls when you scroll.
