**First read.** The eye goes to "Project notes" at top left, then to the five thick dark outlines, which stand out more than the words inside them. It reads as a plain list of project stages, Kickoff to Handover, each with a one-line task. It feels tidy, but heavy and boxed in. It also looks a bit like a form.

**What it's for.** You didn't say, so I'm assuming this is an internal web page that shows a team the stages of a design project and what happens in each one.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: the order of the page and the copy are clear. The one thing in the way is the heavy outline around each stage. It's the loudest mark on the page and makes static notes look like input fields or buttons.

**What would move it most**
1. **The heavy card outlines are louder than the content and look clickable.** Each of the five boxes (x 160–920, starting at y ≈ 120, 238, 356, 474, 592) has an outline that looks about 3 px thick and near-black. That's darker and heavier than the stage names inside.
   - **What the viewer gets:** they see five frames before they read a single stage. The column reads as a stack of form fields, so a viewer may click a box expecting to edit it or open the stage.
   - **Why it doesn't earn its place:** the ~18 px gaps between the cards already separate them, so the outlines add no information. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*) says boxes should show relationships, "not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell". Tufte's point about non-data ink is the same. Norman (*The Design of Everyday Things*) says something that does nothing on click shouldn't look like it does, and a full heavy outline around white space is the usual look of a web text field.
   - **Fix if the stages are static:** drop the outlines. Let spacing do the separating under the bold stage names, or use a pale background tint or a hairline divider.
   - **Fix if they're clickable:** keep a lighter card and add a clear cue, such as a chevron or a "View" link.
   - **Kind:** defect, plus a convention break.

**Smaller things.**
- **Column placement:** the column sits off-centre, with about 160 px empty on the left and 360 px on the right, so the right side looks unused rather than chosen. Either centre the column or give the right side a job, such as dates or stage status. First check that a sidebar outside the screenshot doesn't explain the offset. (Taste on the proportion, mild defect.)
- **Order of the stages (taste):** the content is a sequence, but nothing marks it as one. Numbering the stages, or a thin line connecting them, would show the order without needing the boxes.
- **Page title (copy):** "Project notes" names a type of document rather than what's on the page. Something like "How the project runs, stage by stage" tells a stranger what they're looking at.

**What's working.**
- **Clear type levels:** there's one sans family at three well-separated sizes: title about 32 px bold, stage names about 20 px bold, descriptions about 16 px regular. Each level is obvious, and you can scan down the stage names alone. Keep it to these three.
- **The copy:** each stage is a one-word name plus one plain sentence that starts with a verb and says who does what ("Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need."). A stranger can follow the plan without filling in gaps.
- **Alignment and spacing:** the title and cards share the x≈160 edge, the text inside each card sits on one indent at x≈187, and the gaps between cards repeat evenly. Each name sits closer to its description than to the next card, so the grouping reads correctly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the boxes respond to hover, focus or click, and whether a focus indicator shows when you tab through. That decides which of the two fixes above applies. Check in a live browser.
