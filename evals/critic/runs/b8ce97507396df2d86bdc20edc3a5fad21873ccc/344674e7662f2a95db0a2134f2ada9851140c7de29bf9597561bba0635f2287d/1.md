**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation is hidden in the history, and it has no ask.** The text is one paragraph of 12 lines, from y≈200 to y≈585. It does two jobs. The first half is who the trust is: the 11 miles of path, 1987, the staff and the members. The second half is an invitation: "Work days run every second Saturday… nobody needs any experience to help." That invitation starts in the middle of a line after "moor." at about y≈406, at the same weight as the founding story. A reader skimming for "can I help?" sees a grey block of background and leaves before reaching the best offer on the page: no experience needed, tools provided, a hot drink at the end. The voice also changes at that same point, from "it" (the trust) to "we", which is another sign these are two pieces of text pushed together. Even a reader who gets there is left asking "how do I join?" There is no time, no meeting place and no contact. That counts as a **defect**, because long text that isn't broken up gets skimmed and missed (Nielsen Norman Group, "How Users Read on the Web"). The fix is to split at "Work days…" into its own short section with its own subhead, such as "Join a work day". Then end it with one exact next step: where to meet and at what time, or a link or email to sign up for the next Saturday.
2. **The right side is empty, so the page tips left and looks broken.** All the content sits in a column at x≈180–787. From x≈790 to 1280, about 38% of the width is empty from top to bottom, and another ~220 px is empty below the text. The space is all in one place, which is good, but it has no job. Nothing on the right balances the column, so the page looks as if a sidebar or photo didn't load rather than a choice to keep the column narrow. This counts as a **convention**: Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, says the lighter side of an off-centre layout needs a counterweight. There are two ways to fix it:
   - Put something on the right that belongs to this section and top-align it with the heading at y≈120. The obvious candidate is a photo of a work day or the volunteer panel from finding 1.
   - Or place the column so the space around it looks chosen. Either way, keep the current column width.
