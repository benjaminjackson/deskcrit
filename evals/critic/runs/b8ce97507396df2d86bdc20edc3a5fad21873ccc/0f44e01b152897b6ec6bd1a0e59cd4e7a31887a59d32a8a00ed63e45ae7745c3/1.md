**What it's for.** You didn't say, so I'm assuming it's the hero of an event page or poster for the general public: it should say what the festival is, when it is and where, and get people to come.

**Verdict.** As a piece of type and colour it's strong and finished. As an announcement it isn't done yet. It never says where the festival is or what the viewer should do next, and those are what a visitor acts on.

**What would move it most**
1. **Say where it is and give one next step.** The date line, at about (110,125)–(505,145), says "Saturday 14 June, from noon". The body, at about (110,545)–(725,605), mentions "the quay" and "the old net shed", but nothing names the town or the harbour. A local fills that in without thinking. Anyone who is sent this, or finds it in a feed, can't tell whether it's near them or how to get there. They can't save it, share it or plan around it, so they move on. There's also no next step: no address, map link or programme. The bottom 190 px (y ≈ 610–800) is empty, so the space for one is already there. This is a defect, because place and time are facts people act on and hold the organisers to. Fix: put the place in the date line or right under it, for example "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour". Below the body, add one specific action such as "Find the net shed", "See the programme" or the address. Keep it small and in the same amber-and-cream system so the title still leads.

**Smaller things.**
- Date line: there's no end time and no year. "From noon" leaves people guessing when the lantern boats go out, and "at dusk" is when visitors will want to arrive. Add "noon till late" or the time of the lantern parade. Add the year if the page will stay up after this year's festival.

**What's working.**
- **The palette says what the festival is before the words do.** There are three colours: deep harbour navy (not black), warm cream type (not white), and one amber used only on the date. Together they read as lanterns on water at dusk. Because amber appears only on the date, the date is the one fact that stands out, and it doesn't compete with the title. Keep the amber for facts the visitor needs, like the place and the next step you'll add.
- **The hierarchy is clear and the type scale is tight.** The italic serif title is about six times the size of anything else, so the eye has one obvious place to start. The date and the body are about the same size. The date is set apart by weight, caps, tracking and colour rather than by a fourth size ("bolder, not bigger", Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). The pairing of a warm italic serif with a plain sans fits a quayside festival with a fish supper.
- **The left edge and the spacing hold it together.** The date, the title (by its foot serifs) and the body all start at x ≈ 110. The date sits close enough to the title to read as its label. The body sits further off, in about 58-character lines that are comfortable to read. The empty space is gathered into one field at the lower right instead of being scattered.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- Whether the title is live text or an image of text. If it's an image, it needs alt text, or screen readers and search engines lose the page's main heading (WCAG 1.4.5).
