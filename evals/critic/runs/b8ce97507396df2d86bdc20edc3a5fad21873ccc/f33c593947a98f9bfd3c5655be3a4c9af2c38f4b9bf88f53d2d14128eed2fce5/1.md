**First read.** The eye lands on the big serif quote and reads it in one pass. Then it drops to the bold byline and the grey caption, and only after that picks up the small "Harbour Notes, issue 14" at the top. It reads as a pull-quote page from a small local newsletter. It feels calm and literary, and the empty space suits the dawn mood of the words.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a feature or pull-quote card for a community newsletter, where the quote should catch and hold a reader and the lines below say who said it and set the scene.

**Verdict.** It works. There is one idea, the quote clearly dominates, and the words are specific and human. What holds it back is one craft flaw right where the eye enters: the opening quote mark isn't hung, so the first line of the quote looks indented.

**What would move it most**
1. **The opening quote mark pushes the first line in.** The "“" sits on the shared left edge at x≈100. That pushes the "T" of "The" in to about x≈137, while "just", "leaves" and "the" below all start at x≈100, as do the label and byline. At this size the mark is a light, small shape. The eye takes the letters as the edge of the block, so the largest line on the page looks set in by mistake, and the clean left edge that the whole layout depends on breaks right where the reader starts. Fix: hang the quote mark in the left margin so that "T" lines up at x≈100 with everything else. Then check the "j" of "just", which already pokes a little past the edge. *Convention:* hanging punctuation at display sizes is standard typesetting practice.

**Smaller things.**
- Quote line breaks: "the first ferry / leaves" separates the subject from its verb, and line 3 ends on "when". At this size each line reads as its own unit, so the reader stops on "the first ferry" and has to pick the sentence up again. Rebreak by sense, for example "…quietest just / before the first ferry leaves, / and that is when the gulls / get bold." Or change the measure until the breaks fall at the commas. *Taste.*
- Three small sizes that nearly match: the label "Harbour Notes, issue 14" (~11 px cap height, bold grey), the byline (~13 px, bold near-black) and the caption (~12 px, regular grey). They differ by a pixel or two, so they look like sizes chosen one at a time. The label in particular reads as a paler copy of the byline. Put the byline and caption on one size, separated by weight and colour as they are now, and make the label clearly smaller or a different form, such as tracked capitals (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, on working to a scale). Check the three sizes in the source file. *Convention.*

**What's working.**
- **The quote clearly comes first.** At about five times the size of anything else, alone in white space, with a switch from serif to sans below it, the reader can't mistake what the page is for. Keep the page this sparse.
- **Lightness alone sets the ranking.** Near-black for the quote and speaker, one cool slate grey for the label and caption. In greyscale the page would rank the same way. Don't add a third grey or an accent colour.
- **Specific copy.** "Departs at 5:40 and carries bicycles, bread deliveries, and a few early walkers" puts the reader in a real place, and the caption runs about 73 characters a line with comfortable spacing. That specificity is why the page doesn't feel like a template.
