**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of an online long-read, and its job is to pull a general reader from the headline into the story.

**Verdict.** It does its job well. There is one clear focal point, the headline sets up a question and pays it off, the opening is a real scene, and the body is set for comfortable reading. Nothing seriously gets in the way. The two things worth fixing are where the headline breaks its lines and the reading column sitting hard against the left with no reason given for it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline breaks against its own sense.** It reads "…letting their / fields flood on purpose, and the / birds that came back" (x≈180–960, y≈100–260). Line 2 ends on "and the", which cuts the article off from its noun right at the turn of the headline. The reader trips on "and the" just before the payoff, "the birds came back", and that payoff is the reason to click. Display lines should break by sense (Bringhurst; Butterick). Fix: break at the comma so that "and the birds that came back" stays together on one line, for example "…letting their fields / flood on purpose, / and the birds that came back". Use a manual break or `text-wrap: balance`, and check it at every width you ship. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- **Column pinned to the left:** kicker, headline, byline and body all start at x≈180. That leaves about 320 px of white to the right of the headline and about 470 px to the right of the body, roughly 2.6 times the left margin. With no side rail or picture, the empty right third looks like something missing rather than space chosen on purpose, and the margins don't relate to the frame or to each other (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). Fix: centre the reading column, or give the right band a job such as a rail, pull quote or picture. *Taste/convention*, and it doesn't matter if a rail fills that space on the live page.
- **Apostrophe in "hand's"** (paragraph 1, line 2, x≈330, y≈373): it looks like a straight typewriter tick in a carefully set serif. Use the curly apostrophe (’) through the CMS's smart-quote setting or in the source text, and check the character in the source.

**What's working.**
- **One focal point and a tight type scale.** The headline is about 2.5 times the body size, heavy and near-black, so the eye has no doubt where to start. The kicker and byline share one small size and are told apart only by colour and weight: green bold for the kicker, grey regular for the byline. That keeps the page to four sizes, each with an obvious job (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). Keep both labels at that one size.
- **Copy and type that pull you in.** The headline is a paradox (fields flooded on purpose) and its payoff (the birds). The lede follows it with a concrete scene (Fenby Farm, a hand's depth of water, lapwings) and a turn (wheat five years ago, now paid to stay wet). The body sets about 67 characters per line with generous leading, close to Bringhurst's ideal, so the read is easy.
- **Spacing does the grouping.** The kicker sits tight on the headline, the byline is a step further down, and the body is a step further again. The paragraph gap is larger than the line spacing but smaller than the gap above the body. Every element hangs from one clean left edge, so no rules or boxes are needed. The single muted green appears only on the kicker, where it marks the section and suits the subject.
