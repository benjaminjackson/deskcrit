**First read.** The eye lands on the "Pricing" heading, drops to the three large prices, then jumps to the blue "Start free" button in the top-right corner. It reads as the pricing page of a small SaaS scheduling tool. It is clean and calm, but templated: three identical white cards, with nothing that says which plan is for you or what to do once you've chosen.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the pricing page of a calendar and booking SaaS, where a small-business or team buyer compares three plans, picks one and starts.

**Verdict.** It's tidy, but it doesn't do a pricing page's job. Visitors can't fully compare the plans, can't be sure what a price means, and have nothing to press at the moment they choose. The biggest gap is that the cards end in a dead end: no action, and no recommended plan.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards end with nothing to do, and nothing points to a plan.** The three cards (x≈162–1117, y≈306–570) are identical in size, border and weight. Each one ends after its third feature line. The only action on the page is "Start free" in the header (x≈1107–1216, y≈14–57), several hundred pixels up and across from where the eye stops. Someone who has just settled on Team has to go back up to a button that doesn't name a plan. It doesn't say whether "free" means a Team trial or a free Solo tier. The moment of decision is where pricing pages lose people, and here it's lost by design. Fix: put an action at the foot of each card that names the plan and what happens ("Start Team free"). The empty band at y≈540–570 already has room for it. Make one plan, probably Team, the clear lead with a tinted card, a label, or the only filled button, using the same blue. Keep the header button as the general way in. **Defect** for the missing per-card action. **Taste** for which plan leads.

2. **The prices can be read two ways, and "calendars" isn't the buyer's word.** "$29 / month" and "$79 / month" sit on plans sold to groups, so they read either as a flat price for the team or as a price per person. "Up to 10 calendars" could mean 10 people or 10 connected calendars. Read wrongly, the bill changes by as much as 10×, and price is the line the buyer will hold the company to. Fix: state the unit next to each price ("per team" or "per user, per month") and count in the unit the buyer pays for ("up to 10 team members"). **Defect** (Nielsen heuristic 2, match with the real world).

3. **The feature lists don't say whether the higher plans include the lower ones.** In the lists (y≈465–525), every card names three different features. Business lists "Unlimited calendars, Single sign-on, Priority support" and never mentions Booking page, Email reminders, Shared availability or Reports. A stranger can't tell whether the $79 plan drops what the $9 plan has. Comparing means holding one card in memory while reading the next, then guessing. Fix: open Team with "Everything in Solo, plus:" and Business with "Everything in Team, plus:". Or use shared rows with ticks. **Defect** (Nielsen heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall).

**Smaller things.**
- The "Pricing" title (y≈140–185) and the prices (y≈385–425) look almost the same size, weight and colour, so the page's two biggest levels blur together. On this page the prices are the content: make them clearly the largest, or make the title smaller and have it say something about the offer.
- The small sizes drift. The feature text (about 15 px), "/ month" and the nav (about 16 px) and the subtitle (about 18 px) are a pixel or two apart. Merge them into one body size and one detail size.
- The header and the content use different edges. The logo sits at x≈64, the nav starts at x≈223 and the cards start at x≈162, so nothing in the header lines up with the column below. Make the header's inner edges land on the card column.
- In the nav, "Pricing" looks the same as the other items. Mark the current page with weight or an underline, not colour alone.
- "Product" and "Features" sit side by side in the nav and probably overlap. Merge them, or rename one so it's clear what each holds (taste).
- The subtitle wraps so its last line is just "you like." (y≈248). Balance the two lines, or cut the copy to one line.
- The subtitle's first sentence, "Simple plans for teams of every size", could sit on any SaaS page, and it clashes with a plan called Solo. Lead with the specific promise instead: the cancellation terms, or the free trial that "Start free" hints at but the pricing area never mentions (taste).

**What's working.**
- **One saturated colour, used only for the brand and the action.** The blue appears only on the logo and "Start free", so the one action reads at once. Take any per-card buttons or a "recommended" marker from that same blue rather than adding a second colour.
- **Parallel cards.** All three have the same width and equal gutters, and the name, price and features sit at the same heights in each. Prices and features line up across the row for comparison, so keep that structure when you add the actions.
- **Weight and grey do the fine work.** Bold dark plan names and prices sit over regular slate-grey details, so each card reads in the order name, price, details without needing more sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the page ends below the cards (y≈570–800 is empty) or an FAQ or comparison table follows. If it really ends there, finding 1 is worse.
- Focus indicators on the nav links and buttons, and whether a sticky header covers focused items when you scroll.
- Whether the subtitle's "you like." wrap happens only at this window width.
