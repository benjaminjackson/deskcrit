**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours" at the top left, then runs down the three bold branch names, each with two short lines under it. It reads as a plain hours notice for a small public library service, and it feels calm and quiet. Then the gaps show: the mobile library has days and places but no times, and the spelled-out times ("nine to eight") are slow to scan. The right two-thirds of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this page tells local residents when and where they can visit each branch, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The layout works: it's clear, well grouped and easy to read. But the words don't fully do the page's one job. Someone looking for the mobile library can't find out when to turn up, and the branch hours can be read more than one way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hours don't tell people when to come.** This is a defect.
   - In the Mobile library block ("Wednesday at the market square", "Thursday at the school gates"), there is a day and a place but no time. A reader knows where to go but not when, so they can arrive and miss the van, which is exactly what an hours page exists to prevent.
   - In the two branch blocks, "nine to eight", "ten to four" and "ten to six" don't say am or pm. "Ten to six" can also be read as the clock time 5:50.
   - Which days each branch is closed is only implied by what's left out. Harbour is closed on Monday and both branches on Sunday, but nothing says so.

   This is common sense rather than a published rule. Times are what people act on and hold the library to, so a line that can be read two ways is a defect here, not a style note. Fix: give every mobile stop a start and end time, written the same way as the branch hours. Write every time so it has only one reading, e.g. "9 am – 8 pm" or "09:00–20:00". Consider adding a "Closed" line for each branch.

**Smaller things.**
- **The page tips up and to the left.** The whole column sits in roughly x 96–400, y 60–500 of a 1280×800 frame, and the rest is blank, so as a full screen it looks unfinished or not fully loaded (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). Give the column a container sized to its content so the empty space reads as margin, or set the three branches side by side. This doesn't apply if the frame is a crop of a wider page.
- **Times are spelled out in words** in all the hours lines. Figures are faster to scan and compare down a list. This is taste: switch to numerals with an en dash.

**What's working.**
- **Grouping by space alone.** Lines within a branch sit about 33 px apart, with clearly larger gaps between branches. Each bold name holds its two lines tight, so the three branches read as three units without rules or boxes. Keep that ratio.
- **Bolder, not bigger.** The branch names are the same size as the hours and differ only in weight (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). Together with the one larger bold title, that gives a three-level hierarchy without a muddled type scale, and a reader can scan straight down the names to find their branch.
- **Plain labels and one left edge.** "Main branch", "Harbour branch" and "Mobile library" are the words a resident scans for. Everything hangs from x≈96, so the column reads as one deliberate stack.
