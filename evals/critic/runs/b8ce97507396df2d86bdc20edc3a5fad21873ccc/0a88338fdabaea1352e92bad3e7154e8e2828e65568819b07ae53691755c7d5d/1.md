**First read.** The eye lands on "Crossing the river", then jumps right to "Good to know", which is nearly as heavy, and then to the blue button. Only after that does it come back to the guide on the left. It reads as a plain, calm notice from a public service. The lower quarter of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this page tells passengers on the north landing to market quay route when the boats run and how bikes and luggage work, and points them to the full timetable.

**Verdict.** It's clean and easy to read, but it doesn't answer the question its main section asks. "When the boats run" gives no times. On top of that, the side card is set larger than the guide, so it pulls the eye away from the guide's own content.

**What would move it most**
1. **"When the boats run" never says when.** The paragraph (left column, y≈365–440) says "between early morning and late evening", "in winter", "one boat each hour" and "before dusk". Passengers plan around these facts, and none of them is a time or a date. Dusk moves through the season, and "winter" has no dates, so a passenger can't tell whether a 6:30 or a 21:00 boat exists and could arrive after the last one has gone. **Defect.** Fix: give the first and last departures, the dates winter service runs, and the last winter crossing (for example "last boat 16:20, Nov–Feb"). If times change too often for that, say so plainly and link to the timetable in this paragraph.
2. **The side card outranks the guide.** "Good to know" looks about 36 px bold. The guide's section headings, "When the boats run" and "Bicycles and luggage", look about 28 px. The card's body (about 20 px, 30 px line spacing) is also larger than the main body (about 18 px, 27 px). So after the H1, the eye goes to the aside first, and the page looks like two designs side by side. The two columns' lines also don't sit on the same baselines. One kind of element should have one style wherever it appears (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Nielsen, consistency and standards), and sizes this close look picked one at a time (Bringhurst, 3.1.1). I count about seven sizes where four would do. **Convention, with a real cost to reading order.** Fix: set the card heading at the section-heading size or one step below, and the card body at the main body size or smaller. Let the border and the button mark the card as separate.
3. **"Bicycles and luggage" promises an answer it doesn't give.** The paragraph (y≈520–595) covers only bicycles. Someone with a suitcase, pram or dog reads the heading, finds nothing, and either rereads or guesses. Headings should describe what their section holds (WCAG 2.4.6). **Defect.** Fix: add a luggage sentence (what's allowed and where it goes) or rename the heading "Bicycles".

**Smaller things.**
- "Good to know" heading: it could go on any page. Name what the card holds, e.g. "Timetables and changes".
- Card measure: at its current size the card body runs about 25–27 characters a line, so it breaks on almost every phrase (Bringhurst, 2.1.2). Fixing its size in item 2 mostly solves this, and widening the card helps too.
- Card right edge (x≈1150) stops about 34 px short of the nav's right edge (x≈1184), so the body sits slightly left of the header. Run the card to the container edge.
- Card top (y≈147) sits about 10 px below the H1's cap height (y≈137). It looks like it's meant to line up and doesn't. Either align it exactly or drop it clearly, for example to "When the boats run" at y≈318.

**What's working.**
- **The H1 and subtitle.** "Crossing the river" is far larger than everything else, and the grey subtitle names the route and the audience exactly. A stranger knows what this page is in one read.
- **One accent, used once.** The blue "See the timetable" is the only saturated thing on a neutral page, and its label says exactly what you'll get. Keep blue for actions only.
- **The main column's text.** Lines run about 65 characters with generous spacing, and short headed chunks let a passenger scan straight to their question. The nav (Timetable, Fares, Landings) names the three things a passenger actually comes to check.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the nav links and the blue button. Check that they're visible: a default blue ring can disappear against the button.
- Whether the header is sticky. If it is, check it doesn't cover a focused element.
