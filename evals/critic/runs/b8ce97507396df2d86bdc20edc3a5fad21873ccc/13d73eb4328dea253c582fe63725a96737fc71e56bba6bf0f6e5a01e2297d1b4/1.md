**First read.** The six blocks of colour all hit at once. The eye goes to the brick red and the mustard first, then moves across the rows without stopping anywhere. "Garden show gallery" is small and easy to miss above the red tile. The pale circles read as placeholder art, not pictures. The page feels tidy, calm and templated, and it shows nothing of a garden.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where visitors to a garden show browse its areas (Rose arch, Herb bed, Pond edge and the rest) and see what each one looks like.

**Verdict.** As shown, it doesn't do its job. A gallery of a garden show has no gardens in it: every picture is a flat colour field with a pale disc. Around that, the page title is no bigger than the card titles, so nothing tells the viewer where to start.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures show nothing.** All six tiles are flat fields of one colour, each with a paler disc at bottom centre. They sit at x 60–1220, in two rows at y 78–278 and y 392–592. The captions promise specific scenes: "Climbing roses over the east gate", "Reeds and water lilies", "Tomatoes in late July". The viewer came to see those and gets six colour swatches. Only the text says what each card is and how one garden differs from another, so the pictures and the words don't make one idea. Defect (common sense: a gallery's pictures are its content). If these are stand-ins for photos that failed to load or haven't been supplied, check the live page and this goes away. If they are meant as the final art, they must be replaced. The fix is a real photograph of each scene, all cropped to the same 2:1 frame with the subject filling it. The captions can then add what the photo can't show, such as "late July" or "east gate".
2. **The page title is no bigger than the card titles, so nothing leads.** "Garden show gallery" (top left, about y 48) looks about 20 px bold. "Rose arch", "Herb bed" and the other card titles look about 22 px bold in the same colour. Sizes a couple of pixels apart read as one level, not two (Bringhurst, on the type scale). Here the order is even reversed, so the page heading looks like a seventh caption that lost its card. Since the six tiles are deliberately equal, the title is the one element that could frame the page, and right now it doesn't. Defect. Make the title clearly the top level, roughly 1.5–2× the card titles, so the page announces itself before the grid begins. Measure both sizes in the browser to confirm. If one garden is featured, giving it a larger tile is a fair second step (taste).

**Smaller things.**
- **First card is off.** Under "Rose arch", the title sits about 2 px higher and the caption about 4 px higher than the other two in row 1. This breaks the shared baselines and tightens the title-to-caption gap in that card only. Give all six cards one spacing rule.
- **Discs touch the frame.** Each disc's bottom edge sits exactly on the tile's bottom edge, at y ≈ 278 and y ≈ 592, so it looks neither inside the frame nor deliberately cut by it. If the motif survives, lift it clear or crop it boldly (taste).
- **Six unrelated hues.** Red, green, blue, olive, mustard and purple share no system and carry no meaning (Albers; Refactoring UI, "Define your shades"). If flat tiles ship, draw them from one muted family. With real photos this goes away.
- **Too much strong colour.** Saturated colour covers roughly 44% of the page at equal strength, so nothing reads as an accent and the grey captions look weak beside it (Itten, contrast of saturation). Mute the fields, or let the photos carry the colour.
- **Warm tiles pull ahead.** Temperature alone makes the brick-red "Rose arch" and the light mustard "Glasshouse" jump forward among six peers (Itten, warm–cool contrast; taste). Even out temperature and value unless one garden is meant to be featured.

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** Three equal columns with one even gutter, both rows and the heading on the same 60 px left edge. Captions sit about 20 px under their own picture, with about 60 px between rows, so every caption clearly belongs to its tile. Keep this when real photos go in.
- **The card text.** A bold, near-black title over a regular grey one-line caption is a clean two-level pair, done with weight and colour rather than size. The copy is concrete: "Herb bed / Thyme, sage and chives." tells a visitor exactly what the spot is.
- **Nothing depends on colour.** Every tile has a text label, so the page works for viewers with colour-vision deficiency.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The pictures.** Whether the flat colour-and-disc tiles are placeholders the live page replaces with photos. Check in a browser once images have loaded. If they are replaced, look again at how much pull each tile has across the grid.
- **Clickable cards.** Whether the cards are links. If they are, nothing marks them as clickable at rest, so check hover and keyboard focus.
