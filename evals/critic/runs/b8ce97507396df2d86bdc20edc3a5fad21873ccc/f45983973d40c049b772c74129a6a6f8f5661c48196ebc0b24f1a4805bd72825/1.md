**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", and then drops to the solid blue "Save changes" button at the lower right. It reads as a calm, tidy confirmation dialog. Then the bottom row stops being simple: there are four choices, and the two blue text links look like twins even though one exports a file and the other throws away your work.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a confirmation dialog in a web calendar app. Someone who has edited a shared calendar decides here whether to publish the edits to its followers, keep editing, or throw them away.

**Verdict.** The question, the explanation and the main button are well done. But the one action that destroys work, "Discard changes", looks exactly like the harmless "Export as file" link and sits right beside "Keep editing", so a slip costs the user their edits. Fix that row and the dialog is in good shape.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" looks harmless and sits among the safe actions.** It's the blue text link in the middle of the action row (about x 487–621). It has the same colour, size and weight as "Export as file", and it is only one gap away from "Keep editing", the option that keeps the edits. Because the two text links match, the viewer reads them as a pair of equally minor options. Someone looking for "the other button", or moving left from "Keep editing", can wipe out the new rehearsal times with one click. Apple HIG (Alerts) and Material Design (Dialogs) both say a destructive action should be set apart from the safe ones and styled as destructive. Desktop save dialogs put "Don't Save" alone at the far left, and here that slot goes to Export. Elements that look alike are read as a group (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), and that is exactly the wrong group here. **Fix:** move "Discard changes" to the far left on its own, give it a destructive style such as red text so it no longer matches Export, and keep "Keep editing" and "Save changes" together on the right. Offering undo after a discard would make a slip recoverable. *Defect.*
2. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** It's the first item in the row (about x 314–419). The dialog asks one question: publish these edits or not. Exporting doesn't answer that question, but it takes the first slot. That turns a quick choice into a scan of four options, and it is what pushes Discard into the middle. Nielsen's aesthetic-and-minimalist-design heuristic and NN/g's progressive disclosure both say: keep the main choice up front and move side actions out of its way. **Fix:** move Export to the editor's own menu or toolbar. If it must stay in this dialog, put it behind a "More" control. The row then holds only Discard, Keep editing and Save. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- The action row starts about 34 px to the right of the heading and body text: the "Export" label starts at about x 314, the text at about x 280. This is probably the link's padding, and it looks like a near-miss rather than a decision. Line the first action's text up with the x 280 edge.
- The gaps along the action row are uneven (about 68, 42 and 16 px), so the row reads as four separate items instead of two clear groups. Once the row is regrouped, use one gap inside each group and a clearly bigger gap between the groups.

**What's working.**
- **The copy.** The title asks the real question and names what it applies to ("the shared calendar"). The body line then tells the user, in their own terms, what happens if they save: everyone who follows the calendar will see the new rehearsal times. They can decide without remembering anything from another screen. Keep this pattern.
- **One clear primary action.** "Save changes" is the only filled, bold button, at the end of the row where the eye finishes. The buttons step down cleanly from filled to outlined to text, and one blue carries every action against white and grey. The answer the dialog expects is obvious at a glance.
- **Spacing and type.** There are three type sizes with clear jobs (bold heading at about 28 px, body and buttons at about 17 px), even padding of about 49 px, and a tight 25 px from heading to body against 54 px from body to actions. The question and its explanation read as one unit, and the buttons read as the answer to it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus:** which button has focus when the dialog opens. It must not be "Discard changes". Also check that the focus ring is clearly visible (at least 2 px at 3:1 contrast).
- **Hover and pressed states:** whether "Discard changes" gets any destructive colour when hovered or pressed.
- **Overlay:** the page behind the dialog is blank with no dimmed overlay. This may just be how it was captured. In the live product, check that the dialog sits over the calendar with a scrim so it clearly blocks the page.
