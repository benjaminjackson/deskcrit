**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "When new tickets arrive", drops to the dark blue block in the middle of the grid at weekday late morning, then moves right to three big numbers. It reads as a reporting screen in a help-desk tool, made for a support lead who plans staffing. It feels calm, sparse and competent. Then a snag: the darkest cells look like 11:00, but the panel beside them says the busiest hour is 10:00.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it shows a support lead when tickets arrive across the week, so they can staff for the peaks.

**Verdict.** This is a well-built, honest-looking report, and the chart does its job at a glance. What gets in the way is trust. The one fact it states outright, the busiest hour, disagrees with its own chart, and the headline total doesn't clearly match the chart's period. On a page that people use for staffing decisions, that is the thing to fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Busiest hour, weekdays: 10:00" contradicts the chart.** It sits in the right-hand panel at about x 1034, y 315–375. In the heatmap, each hour label sits over the left edge of its 38 px column, so the darkest cells (30+ on Mon and Fri, x ≈ 506–542) fall in the **11:00** column. The 10:00 column is 25–29 on every weekday, and 11:00 is that dark or darker each day. A lead who checks the number against the picture finds they disagree, and after that they trust neither, on the very fact a rota would be built around. The cause could be a wrong stat, labels that are off by one column, or an exact-count winner that the 5-ticket bins hide. Make them agree. Correct the stat, or say how it was worked out ("highest weekday average, 10:00–11:00"), and make it clear that each label names a column, not a boundary: label the hours as ranges, or centre the labels over their cells. *Defect.*
2. **The headline total doesn't tie to the chart.** "New tickets, September: 3,412 / Up 4% on August" (x 1034, y 212–278) says "September", but the subtitle says the chart covers "1 to 28 September". August also has 31 days. A reader can't tell whether 3,412 covers 28 days or 30, or whether "up 4%" compares totals over unequal periods, where a total can rise while the daily rate falls. Also, by my reading of the legend bins, an average week in the chart adds up to roughly 1,000 tickets even at the low end of each bin. Four of those come to well over 3,412. That is worth checking against the data, because if it holds, the grid and the total can't both be right. Put the exact period on the stat ("New tickets, 1–28 Sep"), compare equal periods or daily rates, and make sure the cells add up to the total. *Defect*, because this is a number people quote and compare.

**Smaller things.**
- **Title and big numbers are almost the same size.** "When new tickets arrive" (~28 px bold) and "3,412 / 10:00 / 42 min" (~26 px bold) are too close, so after the title the eye gets pulled into the numbers column. Make the title clearly larger, or bring the numbers down a clear step (Bringhurst on sizes a few points apart). *Convention.*
- **The heading names the topic, not the finding.** It could say it outright, e.g. "Most tickets arrive weekday late mornings; weekends are light", with whatever hour finding 1 settles on. *Taste.*
- **No period or queue filter.** The report is locked to "all queues, 1 to 28 September" while its own numbers invite comparison with August. A compact period and queue control between the subtitle and the chart would answer that, and could default to this view (Nielsen, flexibility and efficiency of use). *Convention.*

**What's working.**
- **One blue ramp on neutrals.** Seven evenly stepped, labelled bins, with the night hours fading back, so the weekday block stands out at once and still reads in greyscale. Keep the colour this disciplined.
- **The source line heads off the obvious misreadings.** "Times in the support team's local time" and "when a ticket was opened, not when it was answered" are exactly what a staffing chart needs to say, and it says them right under the chart.
- **The layout is built, not assembled.** The numbers column starts level with the grid's top row and ends at the Sun row, and the legend swatches start on a grid column. Text hangs on a 40 px edge and the chart on an 88 px edge, so the page reads as one block.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether heatmap cells show exact counts on hover or focus. The 5-wide bins are the only way to read values here, and they may be why the stat and the chart disagree.
- Whether the nav links show a visible focus ring.
