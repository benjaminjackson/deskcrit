**First read.** The eye lands on "Learn to swim" in heavy white type on the rust card, then moves right through "Join the lanes" and "Family hour". It reads as the programme section of a local pool's website: three equal offers, loud and cheerful. Then the eye drops into the lower half of each card and finds nothing there, so the piece feels unfinished, like a template still waiting for its content.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a section of a pool or leisure centre's web page. Its job is to let adults and parents see which session suits them and then book it or turn up.

**Verdict.** The headlines and blurbs are clear, but the cards stop halfway. There's no time, no price, no way to act, and more than half of each card is empty colour. The piece makes people want a session and then leaves them with nowhere to go.

**What would move it most**
1. **Each card stops before the decision, and the space for the rest is left empty.** All three cards (about x 56–1184, y 72–632) are about 560 px tall, but the text ends near y 330. That leaves about 300 px of plain orange, green and purple, more than half of each card. Viewers read it as something missing, like a photo that didn't load or a button that never appeared. It doesn't read as space left on purpose.
   - **The copy has gaps.** The headlines are commands ("Learn to swim", "Join the lanes"), but nothing on the card can be clicked or followed. There's no button, link or arrow, so if the whole card is a link, nothing in the still shows it. The details stop short too. "Tuesday evenings" gives no time, "every morning from six" gives no end time, "Sunday afternoons" gives no hours, and no card says what it costs or whether you have to book. Someone who's interested has to go and look all of that up somewhere else.
   - **Why it matters.** Controls need to look like controls (Norman's signifiers; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Empty space should be clearly placed against the content (Ruder, "Form and counter-form"). Here it reads as space left over.
   - **Fix.** Use the lower part of each card for the two or three facts people act on: time, price, and whether to book or just turn up. Under them, put one specific action in the same place on every card, styled the same way, for example "Book a Tuesday class", "See lane times", "Plan a family visit". If there's nothing to add, shrink the cards to fit their text. If a picture was supposed to go there, see below.
   - **Kind.** Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- **No page heading, and no card leads.** The three cards are the same size and colour strength, with no heading above them. The eye starts on the left card only because people read left to right, and nothing on screen says whose pool this is. Add one heading above the row (for example "Ways to swim at [pool]"). Keep the cards equal if the three offers really are equal.
- **Three loud colours.** Rust, green and violet fill about 60% of the frame at full strength, so the colour is the loudest thing on the page. That's fine if each programme keeps its colour across the site. If not, make the fills lighter or quieter and keep the strong colour for each card's action. This is taste.
- **The rust card jumps forward.** It's the only warm colour in the row, so it comes forward (Itten, warm against cool) and "Learn to swim" looks like the main offer, even though the layout treats all three as equal. Either give the warm colour to the programme that should lead, on purpose, or use colours of the same temperature.
- **The row sits off-centre.** The left margin is about 56 px and the right about 96 px. The space above is 72 px and below is about 168 px. These are too close to look chosen and too uneven to look centred. Centre the row, or line it up with the page grid.
- **Headings break in awkward places.** "Learn to / swim" and "Join the / lanes" end their first lines on small words, so those lines read as unfinished. Break them as "Learn / to swim" and "Join / the lanes", or keep the phrases together with non-breaking spaces. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is plain and concrete.** Each headline names the activity, and each blurb says who it's for and what to expect ("a coach in the water beside you", "three speeds to choose from"). A stranger understands each offer in seconds. Keep this voice when you add times and prices.
- **The cards follow one strict system.** They have the same width, the same gutter of about 24 px, the same padding, the same two-line headings, and body text starting on the same line in all three. Only the colour changes, so they read as one set of equal choices. Keep the system when you change the card heights.
- **The text is easy to read.** The heavy headline is about 2.7× the size of the body text. White on these deep fills looks to clear 4.5:1, with orange and green the closest, so measure those two, and keep the fills at least this dark.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The empty lower halves.** They could be slots for photos that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser. If photos are meant to be there, the fix is to make them load, but the cards still need times and an action.
- **Clickability and focus.** I can't tell whether the cards can be clicked, or whether a focus ring would show up against the orange, green and purple fills as well as the white page.
