**First read.** The eye lands on the bold sans heading "Notes for ferry passengers", then runs straight down four even serif paragraphs. It reads as a plain information page for people who use a river ferry. It feels calm, readable and trustworthy, and also bare. The text sits in the left 60% of the frame and the right side is empty from top to bottom.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a web page that gives passengers the practical facts they need to plan and take a crossing.

**Verdict.** The typography is sound and easy to read, but the page doesn't do its main job. A passenger can't plan a trip from it, because every time they would act on is vague, and the timetable in effect today is unclear.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule copy gives no times a passenger can act on.** This is a defect.
   - **Where:** Paragraph 1 says "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". Paragraph 3 says that in winter it's "one boat each hour" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk".
   - **What the passenger meets:**
     - There is no first or last departure time.
     - Paragraph 1 doesn't say it's the summer pattern, so a reader in winter may take "every forty minutes" as true today.
     - "Before dusk" moves by more than an hour across the season.
     - The page sends people to printed timetables "in the shelter at each landing", which they can only read once they're already standing at the quay.
     - The likely result is a passenger who arrives after the last boat has gone.
   - **Why it matters most:** times are what people act on and later hold the operator to. This is common sense rather than a published rule.
   - **Fix:** give the real first and last departures and the frequency for each season, label each season, and link to the current timetable online.

**Smaller things.**
- **Empty right side.** The column runs from x≈120 to 775 with nothing to the right, so the page leans left and looks as if a sidebar didn't load. Either centre the column or use the right side for something that earns it, such as a box with the next sailings. This is taste.
- **Hard to scan.** The four paragraphs carry equal weight with no subheads, so someone looking only for bicycles or winter hours has to read everything. Short subheads (Timetable, Bicycles, Winter, Landings) in the heading face would fix this. This is taste.
- **One-word last line.** Paragraph 2 ends with "crossing." alone on its last line. Rewrite a few words, or use `text-wrap: pretty` or a non-breaking space so at least two words land on that line (Bringhurst). This is a convention.

**What's working.**
- **The heading leads clearly.** It differs from the body in size, weight and form at once (a heavy sans against a regular serif), so you know where to start at a glance. Keep this pairing if you add subheads.
- **The body is comfortable to read.** The text looks about 19 px, lines run about 70 characters, and the leading looks generous. Paragraphs are split by space alone on one shared left edge, so each reads as a unit and the column holds together.
- **Dark neutral type on white with no accent colour.** That gives maximum contrast and nothing that competes with the words, which suits a notice people read quickly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the column re-centres or the right side fills at other window widths. That decides whether the empty right side is a real fault or just this capture width, so check it in a live browser.
