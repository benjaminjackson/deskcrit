**First read.** You land on the serif headline "Savings deposits rose every month", then on the striped bars. The tall June bar pulls hardest, and the three big numbers on the right come next. It reads as a credit union's half-year report to its members: calm, sober, trustworthy. The diagonal hatching on the bars shimmers and is the busiest thing on the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page in the member report that tells ordinary members how savings deposits went in the first half of 2026, in a form they'll trust and remember.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, honest page that nearly works. The two biggest problems are both in the chart. Its most striking mark, June, is a one-off that only the footnote explains. And the striped fill shouts louder than the numbers it carries.

**What would move it most**
1. **The June jump is explained only in the footnote.** June ($1150k) is up about $282k on May. Every earlier month rose by only $43k to $79k, so June is the bar everyone looks at. The headline and subtitle tell one unbroken growth story. The reason for the jump ("June includes the mid-year bonus payroll") sits only in the smallest, palest line on the page, under the chart card. Most members won't read it. They'll take June as growth speeding up. They'll also take the "$4.82M, up 6.1%" card at face value without knowing how much of it is the one-off. On a report people rely on for numbers, the page shouldn't make a claim in the chart that the footnote takes back. Direction: put the note at the mark, with a short label beside the June bar. Better still, split June into its regular part and its bonus part so members can see the underlying trend. Alternatively, have the headline name it ("…rose every month, with a one-off bonus jump in June"). Defect. No approved source states this; it's common sense.
2. **The striped bar fill is chartjunk.** All six bars are filled with dense blue-and-white diagonal stripes. At this spacing they vibrate on screen and pull the eye to texture before bar height or the value labels. The stripes mean nothing, because every bar has the same pattern. Patterned fills compete with the data (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"; Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 5). Direction: use a flat blue for the bars. If you want a mark for June's one-off, a lighter tint or pattern on the bonus part of June alone would give the texture a job. Convention, and it costs a lot here.

**Smaller things.**
- **Chart title repeats the subtitle.** "Deposits into savings accounts, US dollars per month" says again what the subtitle says 65 px above it. Keep one, or use the chart-title slot for the June note.
- **Big numbers rival the headline.** The KPI figures ($4.82M, $214, 5,374) look about the same size as the headline, so the page has two tops. Put them on clearly different steps, with the figures around 24 px or the headline around 40 px. The text sizes from 12 to 16 px also cluster: the subtitle and the KPI labels are both regular grey and close in size. Collapse them to two sizes and use weight and colour for finer distinctions.
- **Column tops don't line up.** The chart title and the first KPI label ("Total deposited…") start level but miss a shared baseline by a few pixels, right where the eye crosses between the columns. Match their padding and line height.
- **Leftover space under the cards.** The KPI stack ends about 75 px above the chart card, leaving an empty patch under the cards that reads like a missing fourth card. End the columns together, or put something real there, such as the June note (taste).
- **"Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025" (and the other two "Up … on …" lines).** These can read as "went up on that date". Name the comparison instead: "vs the same months of 2025", "vs Jun 2025". These are the figures members will repeat.
- **"Average deposit, Jun 2026: $214" doesn't say what is averaged.** The figure works out per account ($1.15M ÷ 5,374), but most readers will assume per transaction. Say "Average deposited per account".
- **"$1150k" uses a different unit from the "$4.82M" card beside it, and has no thousands separator.** Write "$1.15M", or "$1,150k" throughout.

**What's working.**
- **The chart is honest.** Bars start at $0, the ticks are even, and each bar has its exact value above it, so nobody has to read values off the axis.
- **The palette is quiet and disciplined.** There's a navy serif for the brand and headline, a neutral sans for everything else, one blue for the data and green only for the "Up" lines. The green is backed by the word "Up". Keep the blue for the data alone.
- **Every number has a label with its period** ("Total deposited, Jan to Jun 2026") and a year-on-year comparison. The headline states a finding rather than a topic.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Keyboard focus on the four nav links, which are the only controls on the page. Check in a live browser that each one shows a visible focus ring.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 6 yes, 2 no (main image and headline make one idea; every element does a job)
What matters first: 3 lines, 2 yes, 1 no (text levels distinct, no two sizes almost the same)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (lines in neighbouring columns share baselines; empty space gathered, not left over)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes, 0 no, 0 unsure
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes, 0 no, 0 unsure
Pictures: 12 lines, 12 yes, 0 no, 0 unsure
