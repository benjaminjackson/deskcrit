**First read.** The eye lands on the bold sans headline, then drops into the left column and reads across three equal serif columns. It reads as plain information for passengers on a small local river ferry. It feels calm and practical, but unfinished: all the content sits in the top-left of the frame, with an empty strip down the right and an empty band across the bottom 40%.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or panel that tells ferry passengers how the service runs: route, frequency, bikes and winter changes.

**Verdict.** It's readable, but it doesn't yet work as a guide. The headline promises something the page doesn't deliver, and the very narrow columns make passengers read every word to find the one fact they came for. The headline mismatch is the biggest problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline promises the wrong page.** "The river ferry through the seasons" (top left) says this is about how the service changes over the year. Only the third column does that, and it covers winter only. Column 1 is the route and crossing time, column 2 is bicycles, and spring, summer and autumn never appear. A passenger who wants to know how often the boat runs may skip the page, because the title says it's about seasons. A passenger who came for seasonal changes reads two columns of year-round basics to find one paragraph about one season. The only heading on a page decides who reads on, and WCAG 2.4.6 asks that a heading describe what its section holds. Fix: retitle the page to what it holds, a plain guide to using the ferry. Or keep the title and write the seasonal content it promises. *Defect.*
2. **The columns are too narrow, and that leaves the frame lopsided.** Each of the three columns holds only about 20–30 characters per line, about four words. "hold a wider course around" is 26. The eye has to snap back every few words, so the text reads choppily even though there's plenty of room. Bringhurst gives 45–75 characters for a single column and about 40–50 for text set in several columns (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2), and this is below both. The narrow measure also leaves the whole block at roughly x 120–1011, y 65–485. That leaves about 270 px empty on the right against a 120 px left margin, plus a blank band about 315 px tall below the text. Nothing balances the heavy top-left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), so the page looks like a fixed-width container that didn't adapt, or like something on the right failed to load. Fix: widen the columns to about 40–50 characters each, or set the text as one column of about 60–70 characters. Either way, centre the block or let it use the frame, so the empty space looks chosen. *Convention.*
3. **The three topics have no labels.** The columns cover three separate subjects: the schedule and crossing time, bicycles, and the winter timetable. Nothing tells the reader which column holds what. Someone asking "can I bring my bike?" has to read from the top left until they hit column 2. Short subheads, at a level between the title and the body (for example "Route and times", "Bicycles", "Winter service"), would let passengers scan for the answer instead of reading everything. *Taste, argued: on a reference page people scan for one answer, and labels are what make scanning possible.*

**Smaller things.**
- Column 2 ends with "stairs." alone on its last line, which makes that column look unfinished next to the others. Recheck the line breaks after widening the measure, and rewrap or edit so the last line carries at least two words.

**What's working.**
- **The grid is strict.** The columns start at x 120, 436 and 752, an even step, and the headline shares the left edge with column 1. The lines sit on shared baselines across all three columns. Keep this system when you widen the columns.
- **Two clearly different levels.** The bold sans headline over a plain regular serif at about 19 px changes size, weight and typeface together, so there's never any doubt where to start. Only two sizes is right for a piece this short.
- **Restraint.** Near-black text on white with no ornament, icons or colour. Contrast is easily sufficient, and nothing competes with the words, which suits a passenger notice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the layout responds to the screen. The empty right strip may come from a fixed-width container. Resize the window in a live browser to see whether the block centres or reflows.
