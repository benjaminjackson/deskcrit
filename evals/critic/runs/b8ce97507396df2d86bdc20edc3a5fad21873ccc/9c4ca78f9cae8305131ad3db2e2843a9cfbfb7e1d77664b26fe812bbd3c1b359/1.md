**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers" and then works down three even paragraphs. It reads at once as a plain notice for people using a river ferry, and it feels calm, tidy and practical. A passenger's real questions are "when is the next boat?" and "when is the last one?", and the page answers neither in words they can act on. The right half of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that tells ferry passengers when the boat runs, how bicycles board, and how the service changes in winter.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and easy to read, but it does only part of its job. It sounds like a timetable without being one. Every time a passenger would plan around is vague, so they still have to guess or go to the shelter.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times are not times.** Paragraph 1 (y≈140–240) says the ferry runs "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". Paragraph 3 (y≈405–515) says it runs "one boat each hour" in winter and that "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk".
   - A passenger can't tell whether the first boat leaves at 6:00 or 8:00, which minutes past the hour the boats leave, or whether "before dusk" in December means 15:30 or 17:00.
   - These are the lines readers act on and later hold the operator to. Someone who reads "late evening" as 22:00 and arrives at 21:00 to find the last boat gone has been misled by the page.
   - Fix: give the real first and last departures and the minutes past the hour for summer and winter, or a short timetable. If the times shift with the seasons or daylight, say so and say where the current list is posted. The sentence about the shelter timetables then backs up the times instead of standing in for them.
   - Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- **Layout off balance.** All the content sits in one block in the top-left (x≈120–750, y≈70–515), with about 530 px empty on the right and about 285 px empty below. The page looks unfinished, as if a sidebar didn't load. Give the right side a job, such as a short "next crossing / winter hours" panel or a picture of the route, or set the column on the page on purpose. The left-set column itself is fine. Convention (Arnheim on balance).
- **Leftover proportions.** The 120 px margin, the ~630 px column and the ~530 px empty field don't relate to each other, so the layout reads as whatever a max-width setting left over. Choose one measure for the column and margins. The same fix covers the balance point above. Taste.
- **Three topics, no labels.** The paragraphs cover three separate topics (timetable, bicycles, winter) but have no subheadings, so a cyclist looking for the bicycle rule has to read from the top. Short subheadings ("Timetable", "Bicycles", "Winter") would let them jump straight to it. Taste.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** It says exactly what the page is and who it's for, so a passenger knows in a second they're in the right place.
- **The reading setting.** Body text is about 19 px, lines run about 62–72 characters, line spacing is about 1.5 times the text size, and paragraphs are split by space alone. The text reads without effort (Bringhurst). Keep this when adding times or subheadings.
- **Restraint.** There is one typeface in bold and regular, two sizes with a big step between them, near-black on white, and nothing decorative. That suits a practical notice and puts all the weight on the words.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell whether the empty right field comes from a fixed maximum width in a wider browser window. Check the layout at phone and wide desktop widths before deciding how much the balance point matters.
