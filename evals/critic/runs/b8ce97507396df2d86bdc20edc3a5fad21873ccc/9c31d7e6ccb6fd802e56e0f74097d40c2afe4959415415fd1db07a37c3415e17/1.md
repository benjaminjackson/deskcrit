**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif "Harbour Lights festival" at top left, then drops into three equal columns of running text split by thin grey lines. Nothing inside the columns catches the eye, so you have to read all three paragraphs to learn what's on. It reads as a festival notice for local people. It feels calm and bookish, but it looks like a council notice or a document page rather than an invitation. The bottom 40% of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or notice that tells local residents what's on at the festival and when, and asks for volunteers.

**Verdict.** The type is clean and consistent, but the page doesn't do its job well. It has a title and then a wall of prose, and every fact a reader acts on (which day, what time, where, how to help) is buried mid-sentence. The biggest problem is the missing level between the title and the body text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The three events have no headings, and the times are buried in sentences.** Each column (about x 100–450, 478–810, 844–1175) is a single paragraph of 10–12 lines on a different subject: an overview with the call for volunteers, the Saturday lantern walk, and the Sunday pie contest. You only find out which column is which by reading into it. The details people come back to check sit mid-sentence: "first weekend of September", "ten each morning", "at dusk", "by one o'clock", "at four". Someone asking "when is the pie contest?" has to read all three columns. *Defect*: it slows the reader on exactly what they came for. Give each column a short bold heading that names the event and its day and time, such as "Lantern walk · Saturday, dusk" or "Pie contest · Sunday, entries by 1 pm". Better still, pull the times and places out of the prose into a line that can be scanned. That adds the middle level the page lacks.
2. **The two things a reader acts on are missing or have no next step.** The headline gives the name but no date or place. "First weekend of September" and "the old market square" only appear in the first sentence of column 1, and no actual dates are given. "Volunteers are still needed" (column 1, around y 370) doesn't say who to contact, where to sign up, or by when, so a willing reader can't do anything. The children's boat race is mentioned once and then never given a day or time. *Defect*: the ask has no action, and the date has no reference point. Put the dates and place under the headline. Turn the volunteer line into a specific ask with one way to respond, such as "Sign up at the library desk by 25 August" or a link. Give the boat race a time like the other events.
3. **The look works against the content.** The copy offers lanterns at dusk, paper boats on the river, a brass band and "the pie that makes the judges laugh". The page is near-black serif on plain white with grey rules and no colour, picture or warmth anywhere. The name "Harbour Lights" and the lantern walk point to a clear mood, evening blue with warm points of light, and none of it shows. *Taste*: for a page meant to get people to come, the mood is part of the job. Keep the restrained serif, but add one warm accent (on the headline or the date line), a dusk-toned background, or one picture of the lantern walk.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: columns run about 33–42 characters per line, short even for multi-column text (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*). There's plenty of room, so widen the measure toward 45–50 characters, for example by using two columns or by using the empty lower area.
- Column 1 ends on "seagulls." alone (y≈453), one line below columns 2 and 3, so the bottom of the text block is uneven. Rewrap or edit so it ends on two or more words, ideally level with the other columns.
- The column rules at x≈467 and 833 sit off-centre in their gutters: about 11 px from the text on their right and a wider gap on their left. Each rule reads as the left border of the next column rather than a divider. Centre them with equal space on both sides (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).

**What's working.**
- **Disciplined type.** One serif family in two weights and one ink colour. The title is clearly separated by both size and weight, so there's no doubt where to start, and nothing looks borrowed. Keep this when you add colour and headings.
- **Level baselines.** All three columns start at y≈145 with the same line spacing, so the lines sit level across the page and read as one even band of text.
- **Specific, local copy.** "Guard the cake tent against hungry seagulls" and "the pie that makes the judges laugh" give it a voice no template would have. The design should bring that voice forward, not flatten it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- Apostrophes in "children's" and "o'clock" may be straight rather than curly; check them in the source.
