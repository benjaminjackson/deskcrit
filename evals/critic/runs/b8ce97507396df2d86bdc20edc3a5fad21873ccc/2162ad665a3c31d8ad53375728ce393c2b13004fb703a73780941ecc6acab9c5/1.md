**First read.** The eye lands on "Project notes", then runs down five cards with the same heavy outline. It catches on the third card, "Sketches", which starts a little right of the others and so reads as either a slip or a sub-step of Research. The thick dark borders make the page feel boxed-in. This reads as a short, calm, orderly list of project stages in a notes or docs tool.

**What it's for.** Assumed: an internal notes page that lists a design project's stages in order, so the team can see the whole plan at a glance.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: the order is clear, the type is clean and the copy is tight. One misaligned card sends the wrong message about how the stages relate, and that is what's in the way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Sketches" card is off the shared edge.** The third card (about y 356–456) starts at about x 174. The title and the other four cards start at x 160. Its right edge stays at about x 918, so the card isn't shifted; it is narrowed by about 14 px on the left, and its text moves to about x 201 instead of 187. The offset is too big to miss and too small to look intended. The viewer stops to decide whether Sketches sits under Research or whether the page is broken. If they take it as nesting, they get a hierarchy the content doesn't have: Sketches has the same heading, box and gaps as the other stages and is plainly one step in a run of five. On a page this sparse, alignment is most of the design, so this one slip is the first thing a careful reader notices. Müller-Brockmann (*Grid Systems in Graphic Design*) says every block takes its edges from one grid. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I) says a placement that is neither clearly here nor clearly there makes the eye waver. Gerstner (*Designing Programmes*) says repeated elements follow one program. Fix: put the card back on the x 160 edge at full width. If Sketches really is a sub-step, make the indent decisive. Indent by at least the card's inner margin, so the box lines up with the text above at about x 187. Add a second sign too, such as a smaller heading or a lighter border, and use the same rule for any other sub-steps. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The five near-black outlines (about 3 px, x 160–920, y 120–692) are the heaviest marks on the page, heavier than the stage names inside them. The 18 px gaps already separate the cards, so the outlines add weight but no meaning. Remove them, or swap them for a pale fill or a hairline (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*; Tufte on non-data ink). *Convention.*

**What's working.**
- **The copy.** Each card pairs a one-word stage name with a sentence that starts with a verb and says who does what: "Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need." A stranger could follow the plan without filling in gaps.
- **A clean three-level type scale in one family.** The title is about 32 px bold, card headings about 20 px bold, and notes about 16 px regular. Weight does the ranking, so each card reads as one unit and the list scans in a single pass down the page.
- **Even rhythm and gathered space.** The gaps between cards are all about 18 px, and the empty space collects in one block on the right and a band at the bottom. Keep that evenness: it is why the list feels calm, and it will look right again once the stray card is fixed.
