**First read.** The eye lands on the tilted blue SALE sticker at the right of the hero, then jumps back left to "Linens and cookware for the weekend", then drops to four product cards. It reads as a homewares shop's home page running a weekend sale. The page is calm, clean and tidy, but generic. Nothing on it shows a product, and the bottom quarter is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of a homewares shop. It should get shoppers who came for this weekend's sale to start browsing and clicking through to products.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page tells the shopper there's a sale but doesn't show them the sale, what's in it, or how to get to it. The loudest element, the SALE sticker, carries no terms and leads nowhere. Every picture on the page is an empty placeholder.

**What would move it most**
1. **The SALE sticker takes the eye first but tells the shopper nothing, and the hero has no way into the sale.**
   - **Where:** the blue block at the right of the hero (about x 1012–1226, y 111–198).
   - **What the shopper gets:** it's the only saturated colour, the only tilted shape and the only heavy type on the page, so it wins the first look. But it only says "SALE", which repeats the nav link. The terms that would make someone act, "Up to 40% off through Sunday", are the smallest, lightest text in the hero and get read last. The eye starts at the far right edge, gets nothing, and has to double back about 450 px to the headline. The block is also rounded and solid like a button but tilted like a sticker, so the shopper can't tell whether it's clickable. There's no other ask in the hero.
   - **Why:** with two elements pulling equally and the key fact at the bottom, the eye has no single place to start (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). One view needs one obvious primary action (Material Design, "Buttons"; Apple HIG, "Buttons").
   - **It also costs the brand.** Cold electric blue on a warm oatmeal band, in a heavy display face, it looks pasted in from a discount retailer and cheapens a brand that otherwise reads as calm and considered. Itten, *The Art of Color*, on warm-cool contrast; the mood point is taste.
   - **Fix:** make one element dominant and give it the offer.
     - Either put "Up to 40% off, ends Sunday" into the headline or the badge.
     - Then add one real, upright button next to the offer that names the action, such as "Shop the sale".
     - If the badge stays, square it up and take its colour from the warm palette, such as a terracotta or rust, or carry the blue through the rest of the brand.
   - **Kind:** defect.
2. **Nothing on the page shows a product.**
   - **Where:** all four card images (y 358–508) and the hero.
   - **What the shopper gets:** each card image is a flat beige, blue-grey or lilac box with a blank rounded rectangle in it. The hero has no picture of the linens or cookware its headline names. A $34 bowl set or a $68 wool throw is chosen on looks. With nothing to see, the shopper can't judge anything, and "Popular this week" reads as an unfinished template rather than a curated pick. The page has no picture to give it a weekend mood, and it could be any shop's.
   - **Fix:**
     - Put a real photo in each card, with the same background, light and framing so the four read as one set.
     - Give the hero a photograph of the sale goods, such as a made bed or a laid table, that shares one idea with the headline.
   - **Kind:** defect, judged as common sense. If the card images simply hadn't loaded when this was captured, check the live page. The missing hero image stands either way.
3. **The cards don't say whether they're on sale.**
   - **Where:** the "Popular this week" cards, directly under "Up to 40% off".
   - **What the shopper gets:** plain prices ($34, $89, $52, $68) with no was-price, discount or tag. They can be read two ways: these are the reduced prices, or these items aren't in the sale at all. A shopper who assumes the first may feel misled at checkout, and one who came for the sale has to go hunting.
   - **The headline adds a second ambiguity.** "Linens and cookware for the weekend" first reads as goods for use at weekends, not a weekend-only sale.
   - **Fix:**
     - Show the sale price next to a struck-through original on discounted items, or label the row so it doesn't seem to answer the hero.
     - Put "this weekend only" into the headline.
   - **Kind:** defect. Prices are what shoppers hold a shop to.
4. **The header has no search and no cart.**
   - **Where:** the header (y 0–59) holds only the wordmark and four category links.
   - **What the shopper gets:** shoppers expect search and a basket at the top right of every store. Without them, someone after a specific item has no fast route, nothing shows that a basket exists, and the page looks unfinished.
   - **Why:** Nielsen, Jakob's law, and heuristic 7, flexibility and efficiency of use.
   - **Fix:** add search and a cart with an item count at the top right, plus an account link if the store has accounts.
   - **Kind:** convention.

**Smaller things.**
- **Hero text is 8 px off the left edge:** the hero headline and subline start at about x 56, but the logo, "Popular this week" and the cards start at x 48. It reads as a slip on the page's strongest edge. Align them to 48, or indent clearly by a gutter or more.
- **"Popular this week" floats between areas:** it sits about 34 px below the hero and 38 px above the cards, slightly nearer the hero. Open the space above it and tighten the space below it to about 16–20 px.
- **Headline breaks mid-phrase:** "Linens and cookware for / the weekend" leaves "for" hanging. Break after "cookware" (common sense).
- **Wordmark and heading read as one level:** "Marlowe & Finch" and "Popular this week" look almost the same size and weight in the same colour. Give the section heading its own step, or set the wordmark apart by form.

**What's working.**
- **The product cards:** four equal cards with one 24 px gutter span exactly the header's 48–1232 width. Image, bold name and regular price sit in the same order on shared baselines, and name and price are separated by weight, not another size. Real photos will drop straight into this and read as one collection.
- **A restrained palette:** one neutral range of white, oatmeal, pale grey and near-black, with structure carried by value. The hero, heading and grid still separate in greyscale, so when the sale accent comes from the warm side it will have the stage to itself.
- **Plain words in the nav:** Kitchen, Bedroom, Garden and Sale make clear at a glance that this is a homewares shop.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The card images:** whether they are placeholders by design or failed loads. Check the live page.
- **The bottom of the page:** whether it continues below the empty band at y 582–800. If it really ends there, that lower quarter is dead space.
- **The SALE block's states:** its hover and focus states, which would show whether it's clickable. Also whether it is live text or an image; if an image, it needs alt text.
