**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers" and drops straight into one grey-black block of 15 lines. Nothing inside the block catches the eye, and the right half and the bottom of the page are empty. It reads as a public information page for people taking a river ferry. It feels calm and plain, and it looks like a notice nobody has formatted yet.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page a passenger checks to find when the ferry runs, how to board (with or without a bike), and how to buy a ticket.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The sentences are well written and the type is set well, but five separate topics are packed into one paragraph, so a passenger with one question has to read the whole page to find the answer. Even then, the facts they most need to plan around, like the actual times, aren't on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The whole body is one unbroken paragraph covering five topics.** The block at x 120–680, y 125–550 covers crossing times (lines 1–4), boarding order (4–5), bicycle rules (5–9), tickets and the season pass (10–12), and winter service and noticeboards (12–15). Every line gap is the same, about 27 px, so space shows no groups, and there are no subheads. A passenger asking "can I bring my bike?" or "when's the last winter boat?" has nowhere to land and has to read from the top. The winter times ("one boat each hour", "before dusk") sit mid-line around y 458, where a skimming reader will miss them. People scan reference text rather than read it through (Nielsen Norman Group, "How Users Read on the Web"), and equal gaps everywhere erase the groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). The fix is to split the text into short sections under small subheads or bold run-in labels (Times, Boarding, Bicycles, Tickets, Winter), with a gap between sections clearly bigger than the line spacing. The bicycle steps are a sequence, so set them as a short numbered list. Keep the typeface, size, line length and line spacing, because they already work. *Defect.*
2. **The copy says when the ferry runs without giving a single time.** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" don't tell a passenger which boat to aim for or whether they've missed the last one. Instead the page sends them to printed timetables "in the shelter at each landing", which is where they already are by the time they read it. Times are what passengers act on and later hold the operator to, so vague wording here costs them a missed boat. The fix is to give the first and last departures, summer and winter, as actual clock times. Better still, add a small timetable or a link to one, and say what "a little longer" means for a crossing against the tide. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The content fills only the upper-left 560 × 490 px of the 1280 × 800 frame. About 600 px of empty space sits on the right and about 250 px below, and nothing shows the space was chosen, so the page looks unstyled or half-loaded. Either set the column on a deliberate grid and margin, or use the right side for something useful, such as a short at-a-glance panel (frequency, crossing time, last boat, where to buy tickets). That would also help with finding 2.
- The 120 / 560 / 600 split across the width follows no proportion. A soft point, and a matter of taste: it gets fixed along with the one above.

**What's working.**
- The setting of the text is sound. Lines run about 60–66 characters with roughly 1.5 line spacing, the ragged right edge is gentle, and there's no lone word on the last line. Once the block is split, every chunk will read comfortably as it is.
- There are two clear type levels: a heavy, slightly tightened heading over regular body text in one family, near-black on white. The eye knows where to start, the contrast is high, and nothing competes with the text.
- The sentences say who does what and where ("Foot passengers board first, from the ramp on the upstream side"). Keep that plain, direct voice when you add structure.
