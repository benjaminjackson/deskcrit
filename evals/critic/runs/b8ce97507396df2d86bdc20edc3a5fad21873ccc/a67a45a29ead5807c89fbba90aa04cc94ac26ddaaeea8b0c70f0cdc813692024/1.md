**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top, 184,220 first, then drops to the blue Search line at the top of the chart. It reads as a marketing-analytics overview for a small team checking traffic and sign-ups. It feels calm, tidy and competent, and fairly generic.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a marketer or founder opens this weekly to see at a glance whether traffic, sign-ups and conversion are improving over the last 12 weeks, and which channel is driving it.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and easy to read, but the numbers can't be trusted. The conversion card says green "+0.1 pts" when the other two cards show conversion fell, and the chart's channels add up to only about three-quarters of the Visits total. A dashboard whose numbers contradict each other fails at its one job, however tidy it looks.

**What would move it most**
1. **The conversion change has the wrong sign.** It's in the Conversion rate card, top right: "+0.1 pts vs previous 12 weeks", in green. Visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups rose only 2.4%, so conversion must have dropped. Working back, the previous period was about 173,630 visits and 3,820 sign-ups, a rate of about 2.20%. Now it is 2.12%, so the change is about −0.08 pts, not +0.1. A viewer who doesn't do the sum leaves thinking conversion improved, and reports good news that is false. A viewer who does the sum stops trusting every number on the page. Fix: compute the change from the same data as the other two cards, and when it's negative, show it with a minus sign in a colour that isn't green. **Defect.**
2. **The chart doesn't add up to the Visits total.** Read by eye, the 12 weekly points in "Visits by channel" total roughly 71k for Search, 44k for Social and 23k for Email, about 138k in all. The Visits card says 184,220, so about 46k (a quarter of all traffic) has no line. The viewer will assume the three lines are all the traffic, and will get every channel's share wrong. Fix: add the missing channels, or an "Other" line in grey, or say in the title that the chart shows only the top three channels. These are estimates read off a still image, so check them against the data. **Defect.**
3. **The date range doesn't look like a control.** "Last 12 weeks", top right of the header, is plain grey text with no border, chevron or icon, so it reads as a caption. Changing the period is probably the most common thing anyone does on an analytics page. A user who wants last month will look for a control and not find one (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on signifiers). Fix: give it the look of a button or dropdown, with an outline and a chevron, and ideally show the actual dates ("Jul 7 – Sep 28"). If it really is only a label, this finding goes away, but then there is no way to change the period. **Defect if it's a control.**

**Smaller things.**
- The three numbers in the top cards are the same size and weight, so nothing says which one answers "are we on track?" If one is the team's main metric (likely sign-ups), make it lead with a bigger figure or a wider first card. If you treat them as equals, that is a defensible dashboard choice.
- "Overview" (about 24 px bold) is only a little smaller than the card numbers (about 28 px bold), so it reads like a fourth number rather than the page title. Make it clearly bigger or clearly quieter. "Brightpath Analytics" and "Visits by channel" also look like the same style, though one is the product name and the other a section heading. Give the brand its own treatment.
- The chart legend is at the top left, far from where the lines end, and colour is the only link between a line and its channel. Orange and green can look alike with red-green colour blindness. Label each line at its right end in its own colour and drop the legend (Tufte; Wilke; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The chart doesn't say what it measures: no unit, no time step. Write "Weekly visits by channel". The last week (around Sep 22) also has no date under it.
- "Visits by channel" names the topic but not what it shows. A subtitle such as "Search drove most of the growth" would save the reader working it out. This is taste, but it matters on a page people glance at.
- "Overview" suggests there are other views, but there's no navigation to them. Add tabs or a side menu with Overview marked as the current page, or rename the heading if this is the only screen.
- There's no visible way to dig into the numbers: no channel filter, no period comparison, no clickable cards. Show the most common ones, such as legend items and cards you can click to filter (Nielsen, heuristic 7).

**What's working.**
- **One grid, held exactly.** A single left edge at about 48 px runs through the brand, the title, the first card and the chart. The right edges line up too, the three cards are equal thirds, and the gaps are about 20 px throughout. The eye compares the three numbers without any effort. Keep this when you add rows.
- **Each change says what it's compared with.** "vs previous 12 weeks" gives the baseline, and conversion is given in "pts" rather than an ambiguous percent. That framing is right; only the conversion value is wrong.
- **A quiet chart with the right form.** A line chart for 12 weekly points, the y axis starting at zero, light gridlines, no decoration, and colour used only on the data. The trends read straight away.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" and the legend items can be clicked, and what their hover and focus states look like.
- Whether the chart shows exact weekly values on hover. That matters here because the y axis only marks every 2k.
- The bottom of the chart card is only about 17 px from the bottom of the image, against 48 px side margins. Check in a browser whether the page has more padding below the fold.
