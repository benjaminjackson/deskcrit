**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild", drops into the picture where the blue bar catches it, then jumps right to the blue "Sign up" button. The page reads as a ferry operator's news item with a box on the side for timetable alerts. It feels calm, plain and public-service. It is also a little bare: the picture tells you nothing, and there is a large empty band under the article.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a ferry service page that tells passengers about a rebuilt landing and gets them to sign up for timetable-change notices.

**Verdict.** The article reads well, but the page fails at both things it is trying to do. The signup can't deliver what it promises, because it never asks where to send the note. The picture is the one piece of evidence for the rebuild, and it shows none of it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The signup form has no way to reach anyone.** The card on the right (about x 760–1120, y 56–420) asks for "Your name" and "Home landing". The note under the button then says "We send one short note when the timetable changes", but nothing on the card says where that note goes. There is no email or phone field, and the note doesn't name a channel. A visitor can't tell how they will hear anything. They either give up, or they press "Sign up" expecting a next step that may never come. As shown, the form can't do its only job (Nielsen, recognition rather than recall, is the closest rule; mostly this is common sense). Fix: add a labelled "Email" or "Mobile number" field, and name the channel in the note ("We email you one short note…"). If the name isn't used for anything, drop it so the form stays at two fields. *Defect.*
2. **The picture shows nothing the story is about.** Figure 1 (x 96–656, y 220–420) is flat blocks: a grey box, a dark slab, a blue strip and a blue bar. It looks like a wireframe placeholder. The caption promises "the new north landing, seen from the water at low tide". The text talks about wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches, a kiosk, and ramps that meet the deck at every tide. None of that can be seen in the picture, and you can't tell which block is the stage, the shelter or the boat. The picture fills about a quarter of the article column and adds nothing. The one claim a picture could prove, that prams and wheelchairs roll straight on, goes unshown. Fix: use a photo, or a drawing detailed enough to show a ramp meeting the deck, ideally with a pram or wheelchair on it. If this is a stand-in for a missing asset, swap it before shipping. *Defect (common sense).*

**Smaller things.**
- The blue bar in the figure (about x 496–606, y 340–364) has the same blue, corner radius and height as the "Sign up" button. It reads as a second button and pulls the eye away from the real one. Give the boat a muted colour from the figure's own palette and a shape that isn't a pill (Apple HIG, Color; Arnheim on grouping by similarity).
- "Home landing" is a free-text box for what is a short, fixed list of landings. People will type spellings the service can't match. Make it a dropdown or radio buttons (Nielsen, error prevention; Norman, constraints).
- Neither field says whether it is required. Mark the optional one "(optional)", which matters once the contact field is added.
- The small text uses a light weight at about 12–13 px: the field labels, the helper note, the caption and the footer. It looks faint, and the labels look weaker than the input borders under them. Set everything below about 14 px in the regular weight.
- The field labels ("Your name", "Home landing") look almost the same size and weight as the fine print, so the form has no separate label level. Set the labels apart by weight or darker ink, not by another size.
- The figure's right edge (x ≈ 656) stops about 25–30 px short of the body text above it, which runs to about x 683. The article column has no clean right edge. Size the figure to the column width (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).

**What's working.**
- **The article copy.** The heading is plain, and the two short paragraphs end on what the change means for passengers: "prams and wheelchairs roll straight on". Body text is about 17 px with roughly 66 characters to the line and open spacing, so it reads at a comfortable pace.
- **A clear, small type and colour plan.** One sans family, bold headings over regular text, and the headline clearly above the card heading. The palette is neutrals plus one saturated blue, so the button is the only loud thing on the page. Keep that discipline, and keep that blue for the button only.
- **The form's grouping and reassurance.** Each label sits close to its own field, and the fields have solid, readable borders. The honest "You can stop the notes at any time" line sits right where people decide whether to sign up.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The focus states on the fields and the button, and what happens after "Sign up": the confirmation, and the errors for empty or invalid fields.
- Whether the figure is the intended artwork or a missing asset. Check the live page.
- On narrow screens, whether the signup card stacks before or after the article.
