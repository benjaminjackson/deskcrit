**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers" at top left, then runs down three even paragraphs. It reads as a plain information page for people who use the river ferry. It feels calm and clear, but bare: all the content sits in the top-left block, and the right side and the bottom of the frame are empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: the page tells passengers when the ferry runs, how bicycles board, and what changes in winter, so that they catch the boat they mean to catch.

**Verdict.** The page reads well, but it fails on the thing passengers come for. The times they plan around are vague and sit inside running prose, so a reader can't get a departure time from it and may read "late evening" or "before dusk" too generously and miss the last boat.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times you'd act on are vague and hidden in prose** (defect). In the first paragraph (y≈150–245) the boat runs "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". In the third (y≈420–515), "in winter" it runs hourly and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk". Each of these can be read more than one way:
   - "Late evening" could mean 20:00 or 23:00.
   - "Before dusk" could mean 15:30 or 17:00.
   - "In winter" gives no dates.

   A passenger who takes the later reading is stranded on the wrong bank. The page then sends readers to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing", which helps only once they're already standing at the landing. The prose also makes these numbers hard to find: someone scanning for "when's the last boat?" has to read all three paragraphs. **Fix:** give real first and last departures and the winter dates (for example, "First boat 06:40, last 21:20. 1 Nov – 28 Feb: hourly, last boat 16:00"). Pull them out of the paragraphs into a short block of times the eye can find first. If the times change week to week, link to the live timetable rather than pointing to the shelter.

**Smaller things.**
- **Empty right side and bottom.** The column ends at x≈750 and the text ends at y≈520, so about 40% of the width and the bottom third hold nothing, and the page looks pinned to the top left and slightly unfinished (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). A left-hung reading column is common and defensible on the web, so this costs little. The column that comes out of finding 1 is the natural counterweight: put the key times there. If a sidebar was meant to be there and didn't render, check that first.

**What's working.**
- **The heading does its job.** "Notes for ferry passengers" is bold, about twice the body size, and names the subject and the audience in four words. There are only two type levels with a big step between them, so the order is clear at once. Keep that restraint when you add the times block: give it weight or position, not a third and fourth size.
- **The body is comfortable to read.** The text looks about 18 px, lines run about 60–70 characters, and line spacing looks generous, at roughly 1.6. The left edge at x≈120 is clean, and the paragraphs are separated by space alone, one topic each (times, bicycles, winter).
- **It's quiet.** The text is near-black charcoal on white, with no accent colour and no ornament. Nothing competes with the information, which suits a public notice.
