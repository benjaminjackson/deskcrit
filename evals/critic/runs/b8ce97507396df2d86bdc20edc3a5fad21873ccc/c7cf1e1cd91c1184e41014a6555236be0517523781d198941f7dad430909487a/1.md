**First read.** The eye goes first to the centred headline, then runs left to right along four prices of the same weight ($0, $12, $39, $99) and finds nowhere to stop. It reads as a pricing section for a collaboration tool: calm, tidy, neutral, and close to a template. On a closer read, one row makes no sense: the free plan gives 3 editors and the $12 plan gives 1.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a pricing page where a team buyer compares four plans and clicks through on one.

**Verdict.** It's clean and easy to scan, but it doesn't do the work a pricing page exists for. The numbers contradict each other, the price lines can be read two ways, and the layout never says which plan to pick. Fixing the copy comes first, because it costs the buyer's trust.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan numbers contradict themselves, and the prices can be read two ways.**
   - **Editors:** In the editors row, Free says "3 editors" and Solo at $12 says "1 editor". Every other row gets better from left to right; this one gets worse. A buyer reading across sees that paying gets them less than free. They'll assume a typo or a catch and start doubting the rest of the table.
   - **Prices:** "$39 / month" sits right above "15 editors". It can mean $39 for the whole team or $39 per editor, which would be up to $585. Buyers hold you to prices. A wrong guess either scares a buyer off or makes them angry at billing time.
   - **Fix:** If Solo really is one person, say so on the card (for example "Just you" or "For one person"), so the drop reads as intended; otherwise correct the number. Add "per team" or "per editor" to every price line.
   - Defect (it misleads on the numbers the buyer acts on; Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on a correct mental model).
2. **Nothing says which plan to choose.** The four cards are the same size, border and fill. The four buttons ("Start free", "Choose Solo", "Choose Team", "Talk to us") are the same solid blue. The four prices are the same heavy size, and each one is larger than the page headline. The viewer has to compare all 16 feature cells alone, and the page never says "most people pick this".
   - **Why:** When three or more equal elements compete to be seen first, the eye has no starting point (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*). One primary action per view should dominate (Material Design, "Buttons"; Apple HIG, "Buttons").
   - **Fix:** Pick the plan you want to sell, probably Team. Give its card one clear cue, such as a "Most popular" label, an accent border or a slight lift. Keep its button solid and make the other three outlined.
   - Defect (it slows the one decision this page is for), and a broken convention.

**Smaller things.**
- **Empty band in every card:** about 70 px of blank space sits under each button (y≈594–664), roughly twice the top padding. It looks like a missing footnote. Match the bottom padding to the top, or put something useful there, such as "No card needed" under "Start free".
- **"Talk to us" on the $99 card:** the price is shown like the others, as if you could buy it, but the button implies a sales conversation, and it doesn't say what happens next. Either sell it directly ("Choose Company") or name the step ("Book a call"), and write "from $99" if the price isn't fixed.
- **Generic headline and subtitle:** "Plans for every size of team" and the subtitle don't say what the product is. Next to a stock four-card layout, nothing marks this page as yours. Name what the buyer gets (taste).

**What's working.**
- **Rows line up across the cards:** projects, editors, history and help sit on shared baselines with thin dividers. The buyer reads straight across a row to compare plans and never has to remember anything. Keep this when you add a recommended plan.
- **Disciplined type and colour:** one family, sizes with clear jobs, and grey doing the fine distinctions. The only colour is one blue, used only on the actions.
- **A useful subtitle:** "Change or cancel at any time" is the right reassurance. It's set back in grey so it doesn't compete with the headline.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the four buttons show a visible focus indicator, and how the four-column row reflows on narrow screens. Both matter on a page meant to convert.
