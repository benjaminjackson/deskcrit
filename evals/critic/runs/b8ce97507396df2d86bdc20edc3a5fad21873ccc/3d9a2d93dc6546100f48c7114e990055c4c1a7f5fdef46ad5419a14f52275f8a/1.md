**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web page or notice that ferry passengers skim for practical facts: how often boats run, how long a crossing takes, the bicycle rules and winter hours.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job well. The layout is sound, but the body is set in a display script that slows reading of exactly the facts a passenger came for. Fixing the typeface is most of the fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set in a script face.** All three paragraphs (roughly x 120–805, y 140–525) are in a heavy, slanted, joined brush script. Readers have to decode word by word, so the facts they act on ("every forty minutes", "about twelve minutes", "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk") are hard to skim and easy to misread. Readers with low vision or dyslexia pay the most. It also breaks the page in two. The heading "Notes for ferry passengers" is a plain, confident grotesque, and next to it the script reads as party-flyer festive, which suits a timetable badly and looks like an accident rather than a choice. Bringhurst (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 6.2.1) says a text face should not carry excess ornament. That passage does not name scripts, so the rest of the argument is common sense. Direction: set the paragraphs in a text face made for reading, such as a regular weight of the heading's own sans or a plain serif chosen to pair with it. If the page needs a handmade touch, use the script for a few words of display at most, never for running text. **Defect.**
2. **The times a passenger acts on are vague.** "between early morning and late evening" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" give no clock time. Dusk moves by more than an hour across winter, so a reader can't tell whether a 4:30 boat exists, and misjudging it means being stranded on the wrong bank. The only place given for real times is "the shelter at each landing", which helps nobody planning from this page. Direction: state the first and last departures as clock times (or a winter range by month), or link to the timetable from here. **Defect** (copy).

- One left edge at x≈120 carries the heading and every paragraph, and the empty space is in one field to the right and below. The eye drops straight down the column without hunting for the next block.
- The heading names the reader and the topic in plain words, so anyone landing here knows right away whether the page is for them.
