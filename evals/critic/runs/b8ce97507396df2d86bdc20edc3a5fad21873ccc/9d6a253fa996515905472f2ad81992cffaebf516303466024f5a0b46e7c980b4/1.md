**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big bold headline, then drops into the italic intro and the body text. It reads as a quiet, text-only long read from a small magazine: calm, plain and confident. Then the eye reaches the bottom third of the screen, which is blank, and you're left asking where the pictures are, because the byline promised them.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the opening screen of an online magazine feature, meant to pull a general reader into a long story about a community allotment and keep them reading.

**Verdict.** The words do their job and the page reads cleanly. Two things hold it back. A story about a place you could see shows no picture of it, even though the byline credits "Photographs by the residents". And the text column is too wide for comfortable long reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The promised photographs aren't there, and the page ends in a blank band.** The byline (y≈250) says "Photographs by the residents". The story is about things you can see: beans, squash, pallets, bath tubs, the queue at the water tap. Yet the first screen is all text, and below the last paragraph roughly 220 px of white (y≈580–800) holds no frame, no icon and no caption. Readers are promised a photo feature and get a wall of text that seems to stop. The page's strongest hook, the place itself, is never shown, and the headline sets up a place the reader never sees. A credit for missing pictures breaks a promise to the reader. That's common sense, not a published rule. **Defect**, unless the gap is a lazy-loaded image or the capture caught the page mid-load. Check a live browser with the cache cleared. Fix direction: put one strong resident photo near the top, under the headline or right after the standfirst. It should add what the words can't, like the people at the tap or the pallet beds, not just "an allotment" again. Reserve its space so a blank gap never shows while it loads.
2. **The text column is too wide for a long read.** The standfirst and body run from x≈280 to ≈990. That looks like about 85–90 characters a line; "It started with a letter. Halima Osei, who has lived on the estate since 1998, wrote to" is about 87. At that width the eye has a long trip back to the start of each line and sometimes lands on the wrong one. That slows the steady reading this page exists for. Bringhurst (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2) gives 45–75 characters, with 66 as the ideal. **Convention**, and breaking it costs something here. Fix direction: narrow the column to about 66 characters at this size (roughly 600 px), or raise the body size if the column has to stay this wide. Confirm the count in a browser.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline wrap: "…end of the bus / line" leaves "line" alone on the second line and splits "bus line". The title looks unfinished. Choose the break ("The allotment at the end / of the bus line") or use balanced wrapping.
- "Subscribe" in the header (top right) looks just like the three section links. Nothing marks it as the one thing the publication wants readers to do. Give it a quiet button shape, perhaps in the kicker green, and set it apart from the sections.
- The nav doesn't show where the reader is. The kicker says "Feature" but "Features" in the nav looks the same as Food and Places. Mark the current section with one cue and use the same word in both places.
- The nav mixes a format (Features), topics (Food, Places) and an action (Subscribe), so a reader can't guess where more stories like this one live. Use one kind of category for the sections (taste, argued from Norman's conceptual model in *The Design of Everyday Things*).

**What's working.**
- **The headline and standfirst earn the read.** "The allotment at the end of the bus line" names a specific, slightly odd place. The standfirst then gives the before (a car park nobody used), the now (forty households) and the reason to care (the queue at the tap is "the best place in the district to hear the news"). Keep this copy.
- **Clear order without fuss.** The headline is the only big, heavy thing, so the eye starts in the right place. The small items are told apart by colour and weight instead of size: green bold kicker, grey byline, grey nav pushed into the corner. Every article element shares one left edge at x≈280, and the gaps grow from kicker to headline to byline to standfirst, so the groups separate without rules or boxes.
- **One accent, used once.** The green "Feature" kicker is the only colour on the page. It suits a gardening story and stands out because nothing else is coloured. Don't let the green spread.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank band at y≈580–800 is an image that failed to load, a lazy-load placeholder, or the real end of the content.
- Whether the grey byline, grey nav and small green kicker meet WCAG 1.4.3 (4.5:1). The byline grey looks lightest, so measure it.
- Focus indicators on the nav links, and whether a sticky header would cover focused content.
