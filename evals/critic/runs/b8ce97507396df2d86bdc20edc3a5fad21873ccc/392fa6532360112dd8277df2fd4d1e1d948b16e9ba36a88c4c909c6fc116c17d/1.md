**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable isn't there.** The heading "River ferry timetable" (about x 120–480, y 75–115) promises departure times. All that sits under it is a narrow block of prose, and everything below y≈200 and to the right of x≈480 is blank. A passenger who came for the next sailing either finds nothing or has to wonder whether the times are buried in the paragraphs. The eye path goes heading → smear → dead end, with no cue that more follows. All the weight is packed into the top-left corner, so the page looks unfinished rather than calm.
   - **Why:** A heading must describe what its section holds (WCAG 2.4.6). An open area only reads as deliberate when something dense sits against it (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*). Here nothing does.
   - **Fix:** Put the timetable directly under the intro: departures by stop and day, in a table the eye can scan down. It also fills the empty area and becomes the counterweight the page lacks. If the table exists but didn't render when this was captured, the heading is fine once it shows. Check the live page.
   - Defect.
2. **The body text can't be read.** The three paragraphs (about x 120–222, y 140–196) look like 3–4 px type, about a tenth of the heading's size. Even a full-resolution crop shows only grey bars of text. Whatever they say about the service, such as crossing times or tide and weather delays, is lost. At this size the regular weight also breaks up into mid-grey, so this is the size failing, not the colour.
   - **Why:** Body text should never be smaller than the interface text around it. Material Design 3 sets body text at 16.
   - **Fix:** Set the paragraphs at a normal reading size, about 16–18 px on screen. Widen the column to roughly 600–700 px so lines keep to 45–75 characters (Bringhurst).
