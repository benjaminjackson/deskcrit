**First read.** Your eye lands on "Changing your pickup time" and then moves down four bold headings, each with a line or two of answer under it. It reads as the help or FAQ block for collecting a bakery order. It feels calm, plain and competent. Nothing says what page this is, but the headings make it clear soon enough.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help page for customers who ordered from a bakery online and will collect in person. They scan it for one answer and then act on it.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The layout and type are clean, and each question is easy to find. The weak point is the words: the answers a customer will act on (when they can cancel, what to show at the counter, how to check allergens) can be read more than one way. Those answers are the whole reason the page exists.

**What would move it most**
1. **The deadlines and pickup rules can each be read two ways.**
   - Under "Cancelling an order", "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" doesn't say ahead of what. It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time before the day of pickup ends. Someone with a 9 am Saturday pickup who tries to cancel on Friday afternoon gets a different answer under each reading. If they guess wrong, they pay for bread they didn't want or end up arguing at the counter.
   - Under "Collecting for someone else", "show it at the counter along with a name" doesn't say whose name: the person who ordered, or the person collecting. If the friend gives the wrong one, they may be turned away.
   - "Changing your pickup time" never says until when you can change it, so the reader has to guess whether the cancellation cutoff applies there too.
   - "Open your order" doesn't say where the order is (an emailed link, an account page).
   - **Direction of the fix:** name the exact thing each time, e.g. "until 24 hours before your pickup time" (or "until 5 pm the day before"), "along with the name on the order", the same cutoff in the pickup-time answer, and where to find the order.
   - Defect.
2. **The allergy answer only helps once the customer is already in the shop.** "Every loaf has a card by the till… Ask the baker" doesn't help someone checking allergens before they order online, which is when this page gets read. An allergic customer either orders blind or gives up.
   - **Direction of the fix:** say where to see ingredients before ordering (on each product listing, or a link), and keep the till card as the backup in the shop.
   - If the product pages already list ingredients, this answer just needs to point there.
   - Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- **No page title:** the four question headings are equal (rightly), but nothing above them says what the set is about. Add one clearly larger title such as "Picking up your order" and keep the questions as the second level. This doesn't apply if a title was cropped off above the capture.
- **Lines are too long:** the body lines run about 90 to 95 characters (e.g. the "Give the person…" and "Orders can be cancelled…" lines). Narrow the text column to about 65 characters, roughly 600 px (Bringhurst, §2.1.2). There is plenty of empty space on the right to absorb it.
- **Lone word on a last line:** "anything." sits alone under the allergy answer. Narrowing the column will re-wrap it; otherwise use a non-breaking space or `text-wrap: pretty` (Bringhurst, §2.4.2).
- **Column placement looks accidental:** the column sits at about 160 px from the left with about 300 px empty on the right and the bottom third blank, so it looks like a sidebar failed to load. Either centre the column, or anchor it left and give the right side a job (contact details, links to your orders). This is taste, backed by Arnheim on balance.

**What's working.**
- **The headings are tasks in the reader's own words**, such as "Collecting for someone else" and "Cancelling an order". A customer finds their question without reading any answers.
- **The type scale is clean:** two levels with a big jump (headings look about 32 px bold, body about 18 px regular), in one family and one near-black on white. No size comes close enough to another to blur.
- **The spacing does the grouping:** the gap between a heading and its answer is about half the gap before the next heading. Each answer clearly belongs to its question, with no rules or boxes needed. Keep that ratio.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty right side is meant to be empty or is content that didn't load, and how the column's width holds up at narrower windows. Check both in a live browser.
