**First read.** The eye lands on "$4.82M" at the top left, runs across the three KPI cards, then drops into the blue bar chart, which fills most of the page. It reads as a revenue dashboard for a subscription business, for a founder or finance lead. It feels calm and competent. The one jarring note is the chart's hard black frame and dark gridlines, which feel louder than anything else on the screen.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a 2025 revenue summary that an executive or finance reader should be able to trust and quote at a glance.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. It's clear, honest about units and sources, and every number has a comparison. But the revenue figures on the page don't agree with each other. On a finance screen that's the one fault that undoes the rest, because once a reader catches it they stop trusting every number.

**What would move it most**
1. **The revenue figures don't reconcile.** The table "Revenue by plan, 2025" on the right totals $4.82M. That's exactly the "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" card. The twelve bars in "Monthly revenue, 2025" add up by eye to about $4.49M (312 + 328 + … + 447 thousand). The December bar (about $447K) times 12 is about $5.4M, which doesn't match $4.82M of ARR either. So the screen shows three "revenue" numbers that disagree, and the table's heading says "revenue" while its total matches ARR. A finance reader who checks the sums will doubt the whole dashboard. Anyone who repeats "$4.82M revenue in 2025" in a board deck will be wrong. Fix: make each heading name the exact measure and period behind its numbers. If the table is ARR by plan, call it "ARR by plan, Dec 2025". If it really is 2025 revenue, its total has to match the chart. If ARR and recognised revenue are meant to differ, add one line explaining how. Defect (common sense: a label has to describe the numbers under it).
2. **The chart's frame and gridlines are heavier than its data and clash with the rest of the page.** The plot area (about x 316–960, y 302–687) has a full black box, and the gridlines at 100–400 look as dark as the axis. The top edge at 500 frames empty space. Everything else on the page uses soft grey fills, rounded cards and hairline dividers, so the chart looks pasted in from a default plotting tool. Its black outline is the darkest shape on the screen, so it pulls the eye away from the KPI cards that sum up the year. Fix: drop the box, keep only the zero baseline, and make the gridlines pale hairlines in the same grey as the table dividers (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, on data-ink). Convention, plus taste on the clash in style.

**Smaller things.**
- **Lower row off the card grid.** The chart ends at x 960 and the plan table starts at x 1012, which lines up with none of the three card columns above (260–574, 593–907, 925–1240). The table also stops at y ≈ 427, leaving a dead block of about 230×320 px at the bottom right. Put the chart on cards 1–2 and the table on card 3, then fill or size that column so it ends with the chart (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).
- **Headings not on one baseline.** "Monthly revenue, 2025" (y ≈ 243) and "Revenue by plan, 2025" (y ≈ 247) sit about 4 px apart. Put them on one baseline.
- **Too many type sizes.** By eye there are about eight sizes. Two groups collide: the page title at about 26 px against the KPI figures at about 30 px, and the nav, table rows and labels at about 15, 14 and 13 px. Tighten to about four sizes plus one caption size, and let weight and grey do the finer work (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 3.1.1).
- **Chart title only names the chart.** "Monthly revenue, 2025" makes the viewer work out the story, which is growth from about $312K to about $447K (+43%) with small dips in April and July. A subtitle that says this in words would land it. Taste.
- **Date pill doesn't look like a control.** "Jan to Dec 2025" at the top right has no chevron, icon or border, and its fill looks about 1.1:1 against white. If it's a picker, it reads as a caption and fails the 3:1 needed for a control's edge (WCAG 1.4.11). Add a chevron and a visible edge. If it's only a label, remove the pill styling.
- **Nav label and page title don't match.** The nav item "Overview" opens a page titled "Revenue overview", with a separate "Revenue" item right below it. Match the names so the viewer can tell the two pages apart. Taste.
- **No export.** Finance readers take these figures into decks and spreadsheets. A quiet download or export action near the chart or the title would save them retyping (Nielsen, heuristic 7).

**What's working.**
- **Every number carries its context.** Each KPI names its period and a year-on-year change ("+18% vs Dec 2024", "+3 pts vs 2024"). The chart states its units and basis ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue"), and the source line names the ledger. Keep this discipline. It's also exactly what makes the reconciliation problem fixable with labels.
- **The bar chart is honest.** It starts at zero, uses even 100s, shows one series in one muted blue, and puts the months in order, so the climb reads in a glance.
- **The colour is restrained.** It's a neutral page with one blue and one green. Green appears only on the deltas and always comes with a "+" sign and words, so it works without colour too. The selected nav item is marked by fill and bold.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the date pill is interactive, and what it opens.
- Focus indicators on the nav and the pill.
- Whether December was a complete month when this was captured. With "closed invoices only", a partial December would understate the last bar without any mark.
- Contrast of the green delta text on the grey cards. It looks fine but should be measured.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 6 yes, 2 no (headings name what their section holds; one mood and era)
What matters first: 3 lines, 2 yes, 1 no (each text level distinguishable, no two sizes almost the same)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (one grid governs edges and gutters; baselines align across columns)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: skipped
Charts and data: 16 lines, 13 yes, 3 no (ink mostly data; names what its marks show; title states the finding)
Interface: 33 lines, 28 yes, 4 no (controls look like what they do; control edges 3:1; conceptual model; shortcuts), 1 unsure (focus indicator)
