**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title, "Notes for ferry passengers", then runs down one narrow left column of plain paragraphs with three bold subheads: Boarding, Tickets and Winter service. It reads as a plain information page for passengers of a local river ferry. It feels calm, sober and orderly, almost bare. The right half of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a web page where passengers find out when the ferry runs, how to board, how to pay and what changes in winter, and then plan a crossing from it.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The page is quiet, readable and clearly organised. The biggest thing in the way is the copy: it describes the service in loose terms ("early morning", "late evening", "for a month"), so a passenger can't plan a trip from it. After that, the sections don't separate well enough for quick scanning.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times and the pass terms are too vague to act on.** This is in the opening paragraph ("every forty minutes between early morning and late evening"), the season pass line under Tickets ("covers any number of crossings for a month") and the Winter service paragraph ("thins to one boat each hour").
   - **What the passenger meets:** They can't work out when the first or last boat leaves, or which minutes past the hour the 40-minute cycle runs on. They also don't know when "winter" starts.
   - **Why it matters:** "For a month" can mean a calendar month or 30 days from purchase. Passengers will hold the operator to whichever reading they took, and the wrong one means a pass rejected at the gate.
   - **Fix:** Give the first and last departures from each landing, or link straight to the timetable from the opening paragraph. Give the dates winter service starts and ends, and say exactly what period the pass covers.
   - **Kind:** Defect. Planning a crossing is the main reason anyone opens this page.
2. **The sections run together.** Each subhead sits snugly on its own text, which is right. But the space above "Boarding" and "Tickets" looks no bigger than the gap between two paragraphs inside a section, such as between "…upstream side." and "Riders with bicycles…". The end of the bullet list also runs straight into "Tickets".
   - **What the passenger meets:** The page reads as one even run of paragraphs. Someone scanning for "Tickets" has only the bold weight to find it by, not a break in the page.
   - **Why:** Grouping by proximity (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). More space above a subhead than below it (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*).
   - **Fix:** Open the space above each subhead to clearly more than the paragraph gap, roughly 1.5 to 2 times. Or tighten the paragraph gap, so each section reads as one block.
   - **Kind:** Convention, and here it costs scanning speed.

**Smaller things.**
- **Empty right half:** The text column ends around x 675 of a 1280 px frame, and nothing on the right gives that space a job, so the page looks lopsided or unfinished. Either centre the column, set the left margin as a deliberate share of the frame, or use the right side for a timetable or quick-facts panel. This is taste. Ignore it if a sidebar simply didn't load in the capture.
- **"kiosk." alone on a line:** The first paragraph under Tickets ends with one word on its last line, which makes the short paragraph look broken. Use a non-breaking space between "landing" and "kiosk", or `text-wrap: pretty`, or reword slightly (Bringhurst).

**What's working.**
- **Reading comfort:** The body text is a plain sans in near-black on white, with generous line spacing and a measure of roughly 60 to 65 characters, well inside Bringhurst's 45 to 75. Keep the narrow column even if the right side gets used.
- **A clear three-level scale:** The title is about twice the body size. The subheads are only a little larger than the body but bold, and the body is regular. That's three sizes, and each has an obvious job. The subheads also name exactly what their sections hold.
- **Sober, consistent styling:** One typeface, no colour and no ornament suits a notice about a public service. The three boarding rules pulled out into a bullet list are the right move for rules people need to follow.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The rest of the page:** It continues below the frame and is cut off mid-sentence in Winter service. If a timetable, prices or contact details come further down, finding 1 matters less, but the opening paragraph should still point to them. Scroll the live page to check.
