**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif title "Harbour Lights festival", then drops into three equal columns of text with nothing to pick between them. Within a second the middle column looks wrong: it starts about half a line lower than the other two. It reads as a plain community notice, calm and easy to read but flat, closer to council minutes than a festival.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or flyer that tells local residents what's on at the festival and when, and makes them want to come.

**Verdict.** The text reads well, but the page fails at the practical job. Readers can't find an event or its time without reading every sentence, and the misaligned middle column makes the layout look broken.

**What would move it most**
1. **Readers can't find the events or the times.** The content has three parts: an overview, the lantern walk on Saturday and the pie contest on Sunday. The page only shows two levels, the title and running text. Nothing tells a skimmer that the third column is the pie contest. The facts people act on are buried mid-sentence: the walk "leaves the town hall at dusk", entries are due "by one o'clock", winners are announced "at four". The children's boat race appears only inside a list in column 1, with no day or time. The year and the dates themselves ("the first weekend of September") never appear as dates. Someone who wants to know when the pie contest is has to read all three columns to find out. **Defect.** Fix: give each column a short heading that names the event, the day and the time, such as "Lantern walk · Saturday at dusk". Make it a clear third level between the title and the body, for example body size in bold. Put the actual dates and the boat race's time somewhere a skimmer will see them.
2. **The middle column starts about 14 px lower than its neighbours.** The first lines of columns 1 and 3 sit at y≈146, while "On Saturday night…" sits at y≈160. The grey rules on both sides still start at y≈132. That 14 px is half the line spacing, so every line in the middle column sits level with the gaps between lines in the other two, all the way down. It's too small to look deliberate and too big to miss, so the page looks unfinished, as if a stray margin leaked into one column. **Defect and convention:** columns next to each other should share baselines (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*), and elements of one kind should follow one rule (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Fix: remove the extra top space on the middle column, probably a margin on its first paragraph, so all three first lines share one baseline.
3. **Nothing in the look says "festival".** The copy is about lanterns at dusk, hundreds of paper boats on the river, a brass band and gulls. The page is black serif text on white with thin grey rules, and the bottom ~40% (y≈470–800) is empty. It gives you the facts but no reason to want to go. **Taste**, backed by Itten (*The Art of Color*), who says colour temperature and value should match the feeling of the subject. Fix: let one strong idea about lights on the water carry the mood. That could be warm lantern colours against a dark evening ground, or one picture of the boats or lanterns, possibly in the empty lower area. Keep the type as it is.

**Smaller things.**
- Column 1 ends with "seagulls." alone on its last line (x≈100–175, y≈453), which makes that column hang a line lower than the others. Rewrap so the last line has at least two words, by trimming the copy or using a non-breaking space or `text-wrap: pretty`.
- The rules sit about 11 px from the text on their right and 20–27 px from the text on their left, so they look attached to the next column instead of splitting the gap. Centre each rule in its gap.
- The apostrophes in "o'clock" (column 3, y≈313) and "children's" (column 1, y≈257) look straight rather than curly. Use proper typographer's apostrophes (’).

**What's working.**
- The type is set well for reading. There's one serif family, a bold display cut for the title and the regular cut for the text, with a big jump in size between them. Lines run about 34–44 characters with roomy line spacing, in a softened near-black on white. Keep all of this when you add the headings.
- The grid is sound. Three equal columns, the title's left edge lines up with the first column, and the margins are generous and even.
- The copy has a real local voice. Details like judges from the bakery, the farm shop and the allotment society, a prize for "the pie that makes the judges laugh", and guarding the cake tent from gulls are concrete and likeable. They're worth keeping when you pull the facts out into headings.
