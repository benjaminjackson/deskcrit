**First read.** The eye lands on "Walking routes", then jumps to the three blue "View route" buttons, which are the strongest marks on the page, and only then goes back to the route names. The page reads as a simple list of three local walks to pick from. It feels calm and tidy. But the bottom half of every card is empty, so it also looks unfinished, as if a picture never loaded.

**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming this is a listing page where a walker compares three routes and opens one.

**Verdict.** The page is clean, well aligned and clearly written, so it nearly does its job. The biggest problem is the empty space in the bottom half of each card. It makes the page look broken, and it leaves out the one thing that would help someone choose a walk.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards are about twice as tall as what's in them.** Each card outline runs from about y=160 to y=600, but the title, blurb and button end at about y=394. That leaves roughly 200 px of empty box under every button (x 64–426, 459–820, 853–1215), plus another 200 px of blank page below the cards. A border promises content, so visitors will read the empty lower halves as a missing photo or map, or as a page still loading. The space is split into three boxed leftovers rather than gathered into one open area. Ruder and Müller-Brockmann both treat unexplained empty space as a fault, and a box that holds nothing goes against Nielsen's heuristic 8. Fix: if a route photo or map belongs there, add it, ideally at the top of the card where it helps the choice. If nothing belongs there, end each card about one padding (≈32 px) below its button. This is a defect.
2. **The facts people choose by are buried in prose.** Distance, terrain and how hard the walk is (6 km with steep climbs, 4 km flat, 10 km paved) only appear partway through each sentence. To compare the walks, a visitor has to read all three blurbs. Pulling those facts into a short line under each title (for example "6 km · two steep climbs · cafe") would make the three walks comparable in one sweep. It would also give the cards real content in place of the empty space. This is my taste, not a published rule: the job here is choosing, and these facts are what people choose by.

**Smaller things.**
- **The three filled blue buttons outshout the route names.** They are the strongest marks on the page and say the same thing three times. Use a lighter style for the repeated action, such as an outlined button or a "View route →" link, or make the whole card clickable, so the route name leads (Material Design, "Buttons").
- **"end." sits alone on the last line of the Cliff path blurb (y≈300).** Next to the fuller last lines in the other two cards, it looks ragged. Use a non-breaking space in "far end.", or reword the sentence (Bringhurst).

**What's working.**
- **The grid is strict.** The three cards have equal widths and gutters, the heading lines up with the first card at x=64, and titles, blurbs and buttons sit at the same heights across all three. That makes side-by-side comparison effortless. Keep it.
- **The text levels are clear and the colour is restrained.** There is a bold near-black title, bold card titles and regular grey body text. Blue is the only colour, and it marks only the action. The page reads in the right order even in greyscale.
- **The copy is concrete.** Each blurb gives distance, terrain and one practical detail (the cafe, benches, paved for bicycles), so a stranger can tell the walks apart from the words alone.
