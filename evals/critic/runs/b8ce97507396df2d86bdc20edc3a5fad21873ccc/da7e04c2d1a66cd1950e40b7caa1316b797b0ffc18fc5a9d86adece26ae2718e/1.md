**First read.** The eye lands first on the headline "Plan the week with your whole team". It then jumps to the blue "Create account" button and climbs back up the form. This reads at once as the signup page for a team-planning SaaS tool. It feels calm, tidy and competent, and also generic: it could be any product's signup page. The left column stops about halfway down, so the lower left looks empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop signup page meant to turn a visitor who's interested in Tallyboard into someone with a free workspace.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The headline says what the product is for, and the one blue button makes the action obvious. Two things get in the way. The offer line can be read two ways about whether the product stays free. And the returning user's way out, "Sign in", doesn't look like a link.

**What would move it most**
1. **The offer line can be read two ways (defect).** The line is "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." (left column, about y 316–362). One reading is free for good, with a cap of five people. The other is free to start, then paid once you add more. "Invite the rest of the team later" leans toward free, but the five-person cap says otherwise. The "Create account" button doesn't say "free" either. A team lead looking at this will either hesitate or remember the generous reading and hold the company to it later. Fix: say plainly what happens at person six, e.g. "Free for up to 5 people. Paid plans start at $X per person after that." If it's a trial, call it a trial.
2. **"Sign in" doesn't look like a link (defect).** In "Already have an account? Sign in" (about 966–1008, y 618), "Sign in" has the same grey, weight and size as the words around it, and no underline. For a returning user it's the only way off this screen, and it looks like plain text. Some of them will start filling in the signup form instead and hit a "this email is already registered" error. WCAG technique G183 asks for a cue beyond colour, and here there isn't even a colour difference. Fix: make it the brand blue used for the wordmark and underline it, or at least give it a heavier weight.

**Smaller things.**
- Password field: the rule "At least 10 characters" lives only in the placeholder, so it disappears as soon as the user starts typing a hidden value. Move it to helper text that stays visible under the field (Nielsen, recognition rather than recall).
- All five input borders: the pale grey-blue on white looks like about 2:1, below the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on field edges. Darken them to at least 3:1, and measure the current colour to confirm.
- Required/optional: no field is marked. "Company" is the one people may not have. Mark it "(optional)" if it is, or mark the required fields.
- Lower-left quarter (about x 140–600, y 370–800): it's left-over space, not a deliberate pause, so the page sits high on the left and heavy on the right (Arnheim, balance). Either centre the pitch vertically against the card, or better, put something there that helps the signup: a product glimpse, a short list of what you get, or a customer line.
- Near-miss alignments: the "Tallyboard" baseline looks 2–4 px off the "Create your account" baseline, and the outer margins look like 140 px on the left and about 144 px on the right. Measure them and snap them to match.
- Headline break: "Plan the week with / your whole team" leaves "with" dangling at the end of the first line. Break it as "Plan the week / with your whole team" (taste).
- Headline copy: "Plan the week with your whole team" could sit on any planning tool's page. Name what Tallyboard does differently from the spreadsheet or standup the team uses now (taste).

**What's working.**
- **One accent, used on purpose.** The blue appears only on the wordmark and the "Create account" button. Everything else is neutral, so the button is the one saturated block on the page and the action needs no other signposting. Keep it the only saturated colour.
- **The form groups itself.** Each bold label sits close above its own field and further from the one before. The form is a single column, with only first and last name side by side, and the button and sign-in line form their own closing group. It reads straight down without effort.
- **A clean type scale.** There's one sans-serif family in about four clearly different sizes: headline, card title, body, and labels/footer. Finer distinctions are carried by weight and grey, not extra sizes, so nothing looks accidental.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus states. With borders this faint, the focus ring has to be strong.
- Whether the password field has a show/hide control.
- How errors appear on submit.
- Whether the two columns stack on narrow screens.
