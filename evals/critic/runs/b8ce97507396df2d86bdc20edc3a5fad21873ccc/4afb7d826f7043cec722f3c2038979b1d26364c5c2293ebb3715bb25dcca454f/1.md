**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web notice that tells ferry passengers when boats run and what to do with a bicycle. Correct me if it's for something else.

**Verdict.** It works as a plain notice, and the heading, palette and column width are all sound. Two things hold it back. The line spacing is so loose that the three notes don't read as three blocks. The timetable copy doesn't give the times a passenger needs to catch a boat.

**What would move it most**
1. **The line spacing is too loose, so the paragraphs fall apart.** This is in all three paragraphs (x≈120–750, y≈130–515). Lines are about 45 px apart on text that looks about 18–19 px, so the spacing is roughly 2.4 times the type size. At this line length of about 60–70 characters, that is about double what's needed. The gap between paragraphs is about 64 px, only about 19 px more than the gap between lines, so the breaks barely show. The viewer sees evenly spaced lines rather than three notes (timetable, bicycles, winter). They have to find the start of each next line and read on to see where one topic ends.
   - Line spacing should suit the line length (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style* §2.2.1; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 47).
   - Equal gaps erase groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*).
   - Fix: bring the line spacing down to about 1.4–1.6 times the type size, and keep a clearly larger gap between paragraphs, about one blank line, so each note reads as one block.
   - Convention and defect.
2. **The times a passenger acts on are vague.** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" (y≈145–190) has no first boat, no last boat, and no fixed departure minute. "The last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" (y≈502) moves through the winter and could mean any time in the hour before dark. It also doesn't say whether the last winter boat runs in both directions or only from the market quay. These lines are why the notice exists, and a reader who guesses wrong misses the last boat.
   - Fix: give clock times for the first and last crossings in summer and in winter, say which landing the last boat leaves from, and tie the 40-minute cycle to a departure minute (for example, "leaves the north landing at :00, :40 and :20").
   - Defect (common sense: no published rule covers this, but timetable facts are what a passenger holds you to).

**Smaller things.**
- **Weight sits in one corner.** All the content is in the upper left (x 120–750, y 55–515), and the right side and the bottom are empty, so in this frame the page looks unfinished more than chosen. This costs little if the column sits inside a wider site layout. Otherwise, centre the column or give the right side a job, such as a small timetable box (convention, leaning taste).

**What's working.**
- **One clear starting point.** The heading is about twice the body size and much heavier, and nothing else competes with it. With only two type levels and a big step between them, the scale suits a notice this short.
- **A comfortable column on one edge.** The heading and all three paragraphs share one left edge at x≈120, and lines run about 60–70 characters, inside Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Keep both when you tighten the spacing.
- **A plain look that fits the message.** One sans serif in near-black on white, with no ornament, keeps nothing between the reader and the facts. The page also reads the same in greyscale.
