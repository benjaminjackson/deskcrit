**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, drops through two short paragraphs and stops on the pale grey box. Then it notices that every line starts hard against the left edge of the frame, with nothing to the right or below. It reads as a plain, calm service notice for a local ferry. It also looks as if it lost its page margins on the way to the screen.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web help or notice page that tells riders the winter timetable, what happens when there is ice, and how bikes, cars and vans are charged.

**Verdict.** The words are clear and the hierarchy is simple, but the page doesn't yet look finished or reliable. The text column sits flush against the left edge with the rest of the frame empty, so it looks like a broken layout. The timetable sentence also can't be taken at face value: its numbers don't add up.

**What would move it most**
1. **The content has no left margin, so the page looks broken.** The heading, both paragraphs and the grey box all start at x≈0. Above the heading there is about 70 px of space, and to the right there are about 400 px of empty canvas. Next to that generous space, the missing left margin looks like a lost container padding, not a choice. The first letters of each line and the box's rounded corner press against the frame. Because all the weight sits in the top-left block and nothing balances it, the page also seems to slide up and out of the frame (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Text belongs inside a type area with a margin on every side (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). Fix: put the column in a type area with a real left margin, at least as large as the top one. Either centre it or set it on a deliberate grid, so the empty space frames the content instead of looking left over. First check the live page for a missing wrapper padding or max-width. Defect, backed by convention.
2. **The schedule contradicts itself on the time riders act on.** It's in the first paragraph. Crossings run "every forty minutes" from seven in the morning, with "the last at six in the evening". Counting 40-minute steps from 07:00 gives 17:40 and then 18:20, never 18:00. A rider aiming for the last boat can't tell whether it leaves at 17:40 or 18:00, and the wrong guess means a missed crossing. The times are also written in words ("seven in the morning"), so someone scanning for a departure time has no numbers to catch on. Fix: make the interval and the end times agree, or say where the pattern breaks ("every 40 minutes, last crossing 17:40"). Write times as numerals, and add a list of departures if riders need exact times. Defect (common sense, no published rule).

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the paragraphs and box text run about 100 characters per line across about 875 px. Limit the column to roughly 66 characters (about 600–640 px at this size) (Bringhurst, 2.1.2). This is minor because the text is short.
- Box padding: inside the grey box there is about 26 px above the text and about 46 px below, so it looks like a missing line. Make the top and bottom padding equal, and check for a stray empty element inside the box.
- Emphasis (taste): the tinted box puts the most weight on fares, but the instruction riders have to act on, "Check the board at the ticket hut before you queue", is plain body text. Consider moving the emphasis to the ice and hold instruction.

**What's working.**
- The heading "How the river ferry runs in winter" says exactly what the page covers and clearly dominates. There are two sizes with a big step between them and no near-miss sizes, so the reader knows where to start.
- The palette is restrained: near-black on white plus one pale cool tint. The box is set apart mainly by its shape and padding, so it still works in grayscale and for colour-blind viewers. That suits a practical public notice.
- The spacing groups things well: a clear gap under the heading, then an even rhythm between the paragraphs and the box.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether the flush-left column and the empty right side are the design or a CSS/capture fault, such as missing container padding, a breakpoint mismatch, or an image or map slot that didn't load. Load the page live at 1280 px and at narrower widths to check.
