**First read.** The eye goes first to the solid purple "Sign in" button, then jumps up to the red-outlined Email field. It reads straight away as the sign-in card for a SaaS product called Quillpost. It's calm, tidy and familiar. It also feels stuck: something is wrong, and nothing on the card says what.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: the sign-in screen of a web app, shown here in its error state, for returning users and the occasional new one looking for sign-up.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the main action is obvious, but this is an error state that never says what the error is. A user who doesn't spot the typo in their own email is left staring at a red box, and that blocks the one task the screen exists for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The email error is shown by a red border alone.** The Email field (about x 470–810, y 291–335) has a red border, and that is the only sign of the error: no message under the field, no icon, and the label stays black. The value "dana.ortiz@exmaple" has a misspelled domain and no ".com", so a user who doesn't notice has to guess what's wrong. Someone with red-green colour blindness may see the red as just a darker version of the grey Password border and miss the error entirely. A screen-reader user gets nothing from it. This breaks WCAG 2.2 1.4.1 (Use of Color), 3.3.1 (Error Identification) and 3.3.3 (Error Suggestion). **Fix:** keep the red border and add a short line of text directly under the field that names the problem and how to fix it, for example "Check the domain, did you mean example.com?". An error icon or a red label is a useful extra cue. Place the message closer to the Email field than to the "Password" label. *Defect, major.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Create an account" (about x 617–738, y 552) has the same grey, size and weight as "New here?", with no underline, so the only way in for a new user reads as plain text. "Forgot your password?" uses purple to mean "link", so this one should too. Make it purple, and consider underlining both links so colour isn't the only cue (Nielsen, consistency and standards; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The Password field's resting border (about x 470–810, y 380–424) is a pale grey-blue on white and looks well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for, so low-vision users may struggle to see the field's edge. Darken it until it measures at least 3:1.

**What's working.**
- **One unmistakable action.** "Sign in" is the only filled control: full width, bold white on brand purple. Everything else is text, so the next step is never in doubt.
- **One clean column.** The wordmark, heading, labels, fields, link and button all share one left edge (x≈470), and the fields and button share one right edge (x≈810). Each label sits close above its field, and the groups are spaced further apart, so the form reads top to bottom with no effort.
- **Disciplined colour and type.** One purple does every brand and action job, and red is kept for the error alone, so the red jumps out. The signal just needs words with it. One typeface at about four sizes, separated by weight and colour, keeps the levels clear.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the red border is the error state or a focus ring. If red is also the focus colour, users can't tell the two states apart. Check in a live browser.
- Focus indicators on the fields, links and button: they need to be clearly visible (at least 2 px at 3:1).
- Whether the layout below shifts when the error message appears, pushing the button away from the pointer.
