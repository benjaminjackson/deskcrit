**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The title promises a timetable the page never gives, and the one section heading leads to an empty space where the fares should be. The typesetting is good, but the content a passenger came for is missing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The title promises a timetable and there are no times.** "Winter timetable at the harbour" (top left) tells a passenger they'll find departure times. The page gives "opens at seven", "every ninety minutes" and "the first crossing", but no time for the first sailing, the last sailing or any departure in between. A passenger who came to find the next boat scans both columns, finds no times, and can't work them out because "every ninety minutes" has no starting point. Departure times are what a passenger acts on, so this is the page's main failure. Headings should describe what follows them (WCAG 2.4.6). Fix: add the actual sailings, at least the first and last boat and the 90-minute interval from a fixed start, as a short table or list above the prose. Otherwise, retitle the page to what it is, such as "Winter travel at the harbour". **Defect.**
2. **"Fares and cards" heads an empty section.** The heading sits at the bottom of the text (about y 548–585) with its rule under it, and the page is blank from there to the bottom. A passenger looking for prices or season-card terms finds a label and no answer. Fares, like times, are something a passenger holds the operator to. As captured, the page promises a section and leaves it empty. If the fares exist but didn't load, or the screenshot stopped at the fold, this is a capture problem, not a design problem. Check the live page. If the section really is empty, fill it or remove the heading. A heading should never end a column with no text after it. That is common sense, since no approved source states the rule. **Defect.**

**What's working.**
- **Clear type levels.** There are three: title about 40 px bold, section heading about 24 px bold, body about 18 px regular. Each step changes both size and weight, and everything is the same blue-black. When you add the timetable and fares, use these levels and don't add a fourth size.
- **A strict two-column grid with matching lines.** The two columns are equal with one gutter, the left edges are shared, and lines and paragraph breaks fall at the same heights in both columns. The page reads as one calm block. Lines run about 55–60 characters with generous spacing, so both columns are easy to read.
- **Plain, specific copy in a person's voice.** "Dogs on a lead are welcome on every sailing" and "a rough one closer to thirty-five" answer real passenger questions. Keep that voice when you write the times and fares.
