**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline, "Savings deposits rose every month", drops to the tall June bar, then moves right to $4.82M. It reads as a credit union's half-year report to its members. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. The most striking thing on the page is June's jump, and nothing near it explains the jump.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page that shows members how savings deposits changed in the first half of 2026, honestly enough that they trust the institution.

**Verdict.** This is a well-made, honest report page, and it mostly does its job. The one thing in the way is the June bar, the loudest mark on the page. It tells a story the page knows is false, and the correction is hidden in the footnote.

**What would move it most**
1. **June's spike is unexplained where people look.** June is $1150k (the right-hand bar, 721–783 px across), up $282k on May, when every earlier month rose by only $43k to $79k. A member sees savings speeding up. The real cause, "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll", is the last clause of the small grey source line at y≈717, below the chart card, where most readers never go.
   - The headline doesn't help. It repeats what the bars already show ("rose every month") and leaves out the one thing the bars can't explain.
   - The bonus also lifts the $4.82M total, its "Up 6.1%", and the two June-only cards ($214 and 5,374).
   - This is a **defect**: the biggest number on the page invites a wrong reading. No approved source covers it, so the argument is common sense: when the most striking mark has a one-off cause, the cause belongs on the mark.
   - **Fix:** put a short note at the June bar ("includes mid-year bonus payroll"), or split June into regular and bonus parts, with the bonus in a lighter tint of the blue. Also move the cause into the subtitle under the headline. Then check that the headline still holds with the bonus taken out.

**Smaller things.**
- **Chart title repeats the subtitle.** "Deposits into savings accounts, US dollars per month" says again what the subtitle under the headline says about 65 px higher. The only new thing is the unit. Fold the unit into the subtitle and shorten or drop the chart title.
- **No last-year reference.** The chart shows only January to June 2026, and the first card says the half is up just 6.1% on 2025, so part of the rise may be seasonal. Add the same months of 2025 as pale grey ghost bars or a thin line. (Taste.)
- **June's label collides with a gridline.** The "$1150k" label sits on the $1200k gridline, level with the axis label. Raise the top of the scale or drop the top gridline so the label has clear space.
- **Card gaps don't match the gutter.** The KPI cards are about 17 px apart, but the two columns are 32 px apart, so the right column looks squeezed. Use one spacing value for both (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- **Top lines just miss each other.** The chart title (y≈250) and the first card label (y≈246) almost line up but miss by about 4 px. Give both cards the same top padding so the first lines share a baseline. Confirm the offset in a browser.
- **Ragged bottom edge.** The KPI stack ends about 75 px above the bottom of the chart card. Make the two columns end on the same line, for example by spreading the cards with the wider gap above, or make the right column clearly shorter on purpose.
- **Comparison lines drop the comparison word.** "Up $11 on Jun 2025" could be read as "something in June 2025". Say "$11 more than in June 2025" and "up 6.1% on the same months of 2025".
- **Inconsistent number formats.** The bar label says "$1150k" and the card says "$4.82M". Use one style: $1.15M, or $1,150k with a thousands separator.
- **No sign-in link.** The header has no "Log in" or online banking link, and the right half of the bar is empty. A member who wants to check their own savings after reading will look top-right. Add a sign-in link there, and rename "Accounts" if it means account products, so it isn't mistaken for "my accounts" (convention: Nielsen/NN/g).

**What's working.**
- **The numbers agree.** Bars start at $0, every bar is labelled, the unit and source are given, and the six bars add up to $4.82M. Also, $214 × 5,374 ≈ $1.15M, which matches June. A member can trust the arithmetic, which is the whole point of a report like this.
- **A clear reading order with two faces.** The serif appears only in the wordmark and the headline, and a quiet sans carries everything else. The small text is sorted by weight and colour, not by extra sizes: grey labels, heavy black values, green change lines. That gives a clean path from headline to chart to cards (Refactoring UI).
- **A restrained palette.** One blue for the data, navy for the brand, green only on lines that also say "Up", and grey for the rest. The chart owns the colour, and no meaning depends on colour alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Grey text contrast.** The source line and the card labels on the pale grey page tint may sit right at 4.5:1. Measure them in a browser.
- **Nav focus and hover.** Whether the nav links show a visible focus indicator and a hover state.
