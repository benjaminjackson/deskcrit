**First read.** The eye lands on the row of four "Add to cart" buttons in teal, purple, orange and blue before it settles anywhere else. Then it goes back up to "Low-light houseplants" and across the four plant tiles. This reads as a small, tidy online plant shop. It feels calm and domestic, except for that rainbow strip along the bottom of the cards.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a category page where a shopper with a dim room compares four low-light plants and adds one to the cart.

**Verdict.** The structure, grid and copy are solid, but it doesn't fully do its job yet. The four different button colours are what most gets in the way: they suggest the plants differ in a way they don't, and they pull the eye away from the plants and prices. The second problem is the pictures, which don't show what each plant actually looks like.

**What would move it most**
1. **The same action comes in four colours.** The "Add to cart" buttons (y≈542–588, one per card) are teal, purple, burnt orange and blue, and they're the only strong colour on an otherwise neutral, green-and-terracotta page. People read a change of colour as a change of meaning. A shopper is left wondering whether the purple button does something different, or whether the orange one means a sale or a featured plant. The orange is also the only warm colour in the row and is close to the pot terracotta, so the eye goes to "Cast iron plant" for no reason. Together the four buttons are the loudest thing on the page, so they compete with the title for where to start and push the plants and prices into second place. A small palette with one colour per role (Refactoring UI; Material Design 3, colour roles) and keeping one role consistent everywhere (Nielsen heuristic 4; Apple HIG, Color) both point the same way. Fix: give every "Add to cart" button one shared accent, ideally from the page's own palette, such as a deep leaf green, so that colour comes to mean "buy". Let the plants be the thing that changes from card to card. Defect.
2. **The pictures don't show the plants for sale.** All four tiles (y≈193–423) use the same template: one terracotta pot with two or three almond-shaped leaves. The snake plant should be tall upright blades and the ZZ plant should be stems of small glossy leaflets. The pothos is listed as "trails to 6 ft" but is drawn as two upright leaves. The cast iron plant comes in an 8 in pot but is drawn in the same pot as the 6 in plants. On a shop page the picture is usually what sells the item. Here a shopper gets nothing from it unless they already know each plant, and worse, the picture suggests the wrong plant will arrive. Fix: use real photos of each plant in the pot that is sold, all shot and cropped the same way. If it stays illustrated, draw each plant's real shape and scale the pots to the listed sizes. If these are placeholders, the problem is that they look finished. Defect (common sense: on a product listing, the picture is the evidence for the product).

**Smaller things.**
- In each card the name ("Snake plant", y≈450) and the price ("$24", y≈512) are both bold near-black and look only a size step apart, so neither clearly leads. The "Fernhill Plants" logo also looks the same size and weight as the product names. Make one of name and price clearly dominant (usually the name) and set the other apart by size or colour (Dair: a contrast too small looks like an accident).
- The top nav doesn't mark "Houseplants" as the current section, and there's no breadcrumb, so a visitor arriving from search can't tell this page is part of Houseplants. Add one clear current-item cue, such as a heavier weight or an underline, and consider a one-line breadcrumb above the title (Nielsen Norman Group, menu-design checklist).

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific.** "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window" gives shoppers a test they can apply to their own room. Each spec line ("6 in pot, grows to 2 ft") gives a number, a unit and a point of reference before anyone commits. Keep it.
- **One strict grid.** There are four equal columns with even gutters. The logo, title, subtitle and first card share the left edge, and the last card lines up with "Cart (0)" on the right. Names, specs, prices and buttons sit at the same height in every card, so you can compare plants by reading straight across (Müller-Brockmann).
- **A calm, consistent base.** The page is white with pale sage tiles, grey spec text and one consistent illustration style. That gives the single button colour, once fixed, room to work. Headings and detail text differ by weight and grey value, not just size.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether pressing "Add to cart" gives visible feedback, such as "Cart (0)" changing to (1) or a confirmation. On a shop page that feedback is the only sign the click worked.
- Whether the buttons and nav links show a clearly visible focus ring. Check both in a live browser.
