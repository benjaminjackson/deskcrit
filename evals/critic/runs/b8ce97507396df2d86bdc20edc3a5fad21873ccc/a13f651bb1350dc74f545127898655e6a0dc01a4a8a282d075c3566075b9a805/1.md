**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big bold headline, trips on "line" sitting alone on the second line, then drops into a well-set opening paragraph. It reads as a quiet long-form feature on a small editorial site, calm and competent. Then something is missing: the byline says "Photographs by the residents", but there are no photographs, only about 220 px of empty white below the third paragraph.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a magazine feature page on desktop that has to pull a reader from the headline into a story about a place and the people who built it.

**Verdict.** The typography and spacing are solid, but the page doesn't yet make anyone want to read on. The credited photographs don't appear, so a story about a place opens with no picture of it, and the headline, the first thing anyone reads, breaks badly.

**What would move it most**
1. **The photographs the byline promises aren't there.** At y≈250 the byline says "Photographs by the residents", yet the 1280×800 frame has no image. The text stops at y≈565 ("Most of them are still in use.") and leaves an empty band from about y 580 to 800. The reader is promised the residents' own view of the allotment and gets a text-only page, and the blank band makes the page look broken or finished. The story is about a car park turned into beds made from pallets and old bath tubs. That is very visual, and on a feature the lead picture is what makes someone commit to reading. It's fine if a lazy-loaded image just didn't make it into the capture, so check the live page first. If there really is no lead image, put one of the residents' photographs between the byline and the first paragraph, or full-width above the headline. Pick one that adds what the headline leaves out, such as the queue at the water tap or a bath-tub bed, not a generic garden shot. If the band is an image slot, reserve its space with a visible placeholder while it loads. *Defect if the image is really missing on the live page; common sense, no approved source.*
2. **The headline breaks as "The allotment at the end of the bus / line".** The headline spans x 280–957, y 135–220. It splits the phrase "bus line" and leaves one short word alone under a heavy full line, so the title reads "…the end of the bus" before it makes sense. This is the most visible type on the page, and it looks like default browser wrapping rather than a decision. Keep "bus line" together (with a non-breaking space or `text-wrap: balance`) so the two lines are closer in length, for example "The allotment at the end / of the bus line". *Defect. Short last-line words: Bringhurst, The Elements of Typographic Style §2.4.2. Breaking at phrase boundaries is common sense.*

**Smaller things.**
- Body measure (all three paragraphs, x 280 to about 996): about 80–85 characters a line, above Bringhurst's 45–75 (§2.1.2). The 1.6 leading softens it, but over a long read it wears. Narrow the column to about 600–620 px, or raise the body size a little in the same column, to get close to 66 characters.
- Standfirst (first paragraph, y 288–398): it sums up the piece ("We spent a season with the people who built it."), but it's set exactly like the story paragraphs and has the same gap after it. The reader can't see where the intro ends and the story begins. Set it one step up (larger, or a different weight or colour) and add space after it. *Taste.*
- "Subscribe" (top right, x≈1093–1160): it's the page's only conversion action, but it's styled like the three section links and sits in the same dot-separated list, so it reads as another section. Separate it and give it a distinct treatment, such as an outlined button or the green you already use (Material Design 3, "Buttons"). This matters more if subscriptions are what the page is meant to earn.
- Nav current section: "Features" looks the same as Food and Places, even though this page is labelled "Feature" in the kicker. Mark the current section with weight, an underline or the green accent (NN/g, Menu-Design Checklist).

**What's working.**
- **A clear, short type scale.** It's one sans family with a bold headline of about 44 px, body of about 18 px and a byline of about 14 px. Weight and colour carry the finer differences: the green bold kicker and the grey regular byline. Every size has an obvious job.
- **One left edge, and spacing that shows the groups.** The kicker, headline, byline and paragraphs all start at x=280. The header elements sit close together (about 20–22 px), with a bigger gap before the body. The 720 px column is centred in the 1040 px header container. The structure reads with no rules or boxes.
- **One accent, used once.** The green "Feature" kicker is the only colour on the page. It tags the article type, suits the subject and doesn't compete with the headline. Keep it that rare.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank band at y≈580–800 is a lazy-loaded image, the article continuing below, or a real gap. Load the live page with a slow connection and scroll past the third paragraph.
- How the headline wraps at narrower widths, since the "line" runt may appear or move on other screens.
- Keyboard focus on the nav links: whether it is visible, and whether a sticky header hides it.
