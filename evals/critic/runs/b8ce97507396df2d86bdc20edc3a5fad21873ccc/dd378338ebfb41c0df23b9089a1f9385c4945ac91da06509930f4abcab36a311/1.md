**First read.** The eye goes to the bold serif headline first, then to the big navy 38% slice, then across to the legend to find out what navy means. The page reads as a calm, well-made annual-report chart. The bottom right is empty, and the legend sits small in the top corner, so the page feels slightly unfinished. Nothing on the page says what the reader should conclude.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page in an annual report that shows shareholders and staff how fiscal 2026 operating costs split by category, and what they should take from that.

**Verdict.** The chart is accurate and clean, but the page reports figures without making a point. The headline names a topic, no number is compared with anything, and half the right column is empty. That empty space could easily hold the missing point.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline names the topic, not the finding.** "Where operating costs went in fiscal 2026" and the line under it only say what the chart covers. The reader has to work out the point alone: salaries are the largest cost, and "Other" is the second largest. The headline is what people remember from a report page, and this one gives them nothing to remember. Fix: make the headline state the finding, for example "Salaries took 38% of operating costs; a quarter sits in Other". Keep the current subtitle as the scope line. *Taste (common sense). No approved source covers it, but on a page with one chart the headline is the message.*
2. **No number is compared with anything.** Every share is for fiscal 2026 alone, and there is no total cost in currency. A reader can't tell whether 38% for salaries or 12% for cloud hosting is up, down or normal. They also can't tell how much money 16% stands for. "What changed from last year" is the first question an annual-report reader asks. Fix: show the total operating cost. Add each category's change from fiscal 2025 as a column in the legend rows or as a sentence in the caption. *Convention (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design", pitfall 2).*
3. **The right half is mostly empty, and the page leans left.** All the dark, saturated weight is in the 400 px pie on the left. The legend is light text and hairlines, only about 145 px tall, pinned to the top right. Below it, roughly x 620–1216, y 380–680 is blank. The empty block looks like something is missing rather than like a chosen pause, and the page tips down and to the left. Fix: gather that space on purpose. Use it for the takeaway and year-on-year figures from findings 1–2, or centre a taller legend on the pie's middle (around y 435). *Convention (Ruder on printed and unprinted space; Arnheim, "Balance").*

**Smaller things.**
- Every percentage is printed twice, on the slice and in the legend, while the names are only in the legend. The reader matches colours back and forth five times. Put each name beside its slice (use leader lines for 9% and 12%), and keep the legend only if it becomes a table with the year-on-year figures (Tufte; Wilke).
- The grey "Other" slice at upper left is 25%. That is larger than rent, cloud hosting or marketing, and nothing explains it. In a financial report that invites suspicion. Split it into its largest parts, or say in the caption or source line what it covers.
- The white "12%" on the orange slice is about 2.8:1 by eye and fails even the 3:1 large-text limit. The white "16%" on teal is borderline at about 3.2:1. Set both in the dark text colour, as the grey slice already does, or darken the fills (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure the label size and the exact ratios.
- The chart doesn't sit on the page's grid. The text starts at x≈64, but the pie starts at ≈114. The legend ends at ≈1060, short of the 1216 right margin. Line the pie up with the 64 px edge and run the legend rules to 1216 (Müller-Brockmann).
- The row is split into a 50 px inset, a 400 px pie, a 106 px gap, a 440 px legend and 156 px of dead space, with no common ratio. Splitting the content width in halves or 7/5 would look more deliberate. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- **The pie is honest and well ordered.** It has five slices that add to 100, with no 3D or shadows, and thin white lines between slices. They run from largest to smallest clockwise from 12 o'clock, with "Other" last and in neutral grey, so the reader can rank the categories without reading the numbers.
- **The type and the frame are quiet and well made.** A bold serif headline sits over a plain sans, with about four sizes that each have a clear job. The small brown "4.2 COSTS" marker and the "Section 4 of 9" running head show where you are in the report without pulling the eye. Keep that restraint when adding the takeaway.
- **The palette stays out of the frame.** The off-white ground, near-black and grey text leave all the colour to the chart, so the data is the only colourful thing on the page.
