**First read.** The eye lands on "Changing your payment card" and then moves down the three bold subheads, "Add a new card", "Remove an old card" and "If a payment fails". This reads as a help-centre article for a customer sorting out their billing. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, with nothing to filter out. One thing loosens it slightly: each subhead sits about midway between the paragraph above and the one below.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help article that lets a signed-in customer add, swap or remove a payment card. It should also tell them what happens and how long they have when a payment fails.

**Verdict.** The typography and structure work, but the article does only half its job. It explains the task without giving the reader a way to do it. Its two most important rules, which card is the default and how quickly a failed payment locks the account, can also be read more than one way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page describes the action but gives no way to take it.**
   - **Where:** "Add a new card" spells out a path: "Open Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card". "Remove an old card" and "If a payment fails" also describe actions, but there is no link or button anywhere. The only controls on the page are the two breadcrumb items.
   - **What the reader meets:** they have to remember a three-step route, leave the help centre and find Settings themselves. The person reading "If a payment fails" is the most stressed reader and the one in the biggest hurry, and they get no route to fix the card.
   - **Why:** this breaks Nielsen's heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall: users should not have to carry information from one screen to another.
   - **Fix:** add a direct link or button such as "Go to Billing settings" or "Add a card" under the first subhead, and repeat it in the payment-failure section. Keep the written path for readers who aren't signed in.
   - **Type:** defect.
2. **The default card is never explained, so a reader can get stuck partway through the main task.**
   - **Where:** the intro says "The new card is used from your next invoice". That reads as if adding a card switches billing to it automatically. "Remove an old card" then says a card can only be removed "once another card is set as the default", and no step on the page says how to set one.
   - **What the reader meets:** a customer who adds a card and then tries to remove the old one may be blocked and won't know why.
   - **Fix:** in "Add a new card", say whether the new card becomes the default automatically. If it doesn't, give the step for setting it.
   - **Type:** defect.
3. **The failed-payment rule can be read two ways, and it is the one deadline the reader acts on.**
   - **Where:** "If a payment fails". "We try again after three days" can mean one retry three days later or a retry every three days. "After a third failed try the account moves…" has no comma after "try", so it first reads as "a third failed try the account" and the reader has to back up.
   - **What the reader meets:** if they take the wrong reading, they don't know how long they have before the account goes read-only. It could be about three days or about nine.
   - **Fix:** spell out the sequence and the timing, for example: "If a payment fails, we try again every three days and email the account owner each time. If the third try fails, your account becomes read-only until a payment goes through." Use whichever schedule is actually true.
   - **Type:** defect.

**Smaller things.**
- **Subhead spacing:** each subhead has about 30 px above it and about 30 px below it, so it floats between sections instead of belonging to its own. Roughly double the space above each subhead and cut the space below it, so section breaks show through space as well as bold (Arnheim, proximity).
- **Line length:** lines run about 77 to 85 characters across the 680 px column. Narrow it to about 600–620 px to bring lines into 45–75 characters (Bringhurst 2.1.2). It costs little here because the paragraphs are only two lines.
- **"days." alone on a line:** it ends the hold paragraph under "Add a new card" (y≈376). Narrowing the measure will probably reflow it; otherwise balance the wrap.
- **"nothing already paid is moved":** in the intro, this can mean past payments aren't refunded to the old card, or that they aren't charged again to the new one. Say it plainly, for example: "Payments you've already made stay on your old card."
- **Breadcrumb doesn't look like links:** "Help centre › Billing" is grey with no underline, so it reads as a label rather than a way back up. Give it a link colour or an underline (WCAG 1.4.1).
- **No search or support route on the screen:** if the article doesn't answer the reader's question, their only move is back up the breadcrumb. Add help-centre search and a closing "Still stuck? Contact support" line, unless a site header with these was cropped out of this capture.
- **Empty right side:** the column sits at x 160–840 and about 440 px on the right is empty, so it looks like a sidebar that is missing. Either fill it with an "In this article" or related-articles rail, or centre the column. This one is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is short and clean:** a heavy title at about 36 px, bold subheads at about 21 px, regular body text at about 17 px, and a grey breadcrumb. Every size has an obvious job, and weight and grey do the finer distinctions instead of extra sizes. Keep this.
- **The subheads are the tasks themselves:** "Add a new card", "Remove an old card", "If a payment fails". A reader scans straight down the shared left edge at x≈160 to their own situation.
- **Black on white does all the work:** there is no colour, so the structure would read the same in greyscale. That suits a page people scan for one answer.
