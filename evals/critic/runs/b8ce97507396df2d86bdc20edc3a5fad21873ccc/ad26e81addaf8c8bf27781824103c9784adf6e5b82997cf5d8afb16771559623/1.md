**First read.** The eye lands on "Harbour Lights Festival", a large cream italic serif set across the middle of the frame. It then jumps up to the amber date and drops to the two-line description. After that the eye reaches a wide band of empty navy with nothing more to find. It reads as the hero of a page announcing a free coastal evening festival, and it feels calm, warm and dusky.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the top of an event web page or poster that should get people, including visitors from outside the town, to come to a free festival.

**Verdict.** As type and colour, this is a strong piece with nothing to fix. As an event announcement it is incomplete: it never says where the festival is, gives only half the time, and leaves the viewer nothing to do next.

**What would move it most**
1. **The words leave out the facts people act on.** You can see what is happening and on which day. But "Harbour" names no town or quay, and "the old net shed" means something only to locals. "From noon" has no end time, though the description promises boats "at dusk", so a visitor can't tell whether it is worth arriving late. There is no year either. An announcement has to answer where and when without the viewer filling gaps. Here a stranger has to look up the place before deciding to go, and some won't bother. The direction: put the place (town and harbour) on the amber line with the date, so it gets the same pull, and add an end time or "until late". This is a defect for anyone who isn't already local, and common sense rather than a cited rule.
2. **There is no next step.** The eye finishes the paragraph and drops into about 190 px of empty navy (y ≈ 610–800) with nothing to do. Entry is free, so no ticket button is needed. But a web page still needs one exact ask, such as "See the programme" or "Get directions to the quay". It would sit under the paragraph on the same left edge, in the amber already used for the date. On a poster, the equivalent is a short URL or a QR code. This is a defect if it is a web page, and taste if it is print.

**What's working.**
- **The colours tell the story without a picture.** The deep navy reads as water at night. The amber date, the only warm colour on the page, reads as lantern light and pulls the date forward even though it is small. Keep amber for one or two jobs at most. If it spreads, the date loses its pull.
- **A clean three-level type scale.** The italic serif title is about 7× the body text, and the eyebrow and body are close in size. The date is set apart by tracked bold capitals and colour rather than by a fourth size (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). Each size has one job, so the page reads in seconds.
- **One exact left edge and a built block.** The date, both title lines and the paragraph all start at x ≈ 110. The paragraph ends about where "Harbour" ends, around x ≈ 725, so the block looks built rather than placed. The paragraph runs about 58 characters per line at comfortable line spacing. Keep both edges when the copy changes.
