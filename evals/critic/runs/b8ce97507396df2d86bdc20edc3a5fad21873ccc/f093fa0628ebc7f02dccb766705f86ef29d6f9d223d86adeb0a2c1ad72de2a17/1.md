**First read.** The eye lands on "Create your account", runs down a stack of six bold labels and stops at the navy "Create account" button. The "Quillnest" wordmark in the top left is a light second stop. It reads as the signup page of a note-taking app. It feels calm and tidy, but it could be anyone's product, and for "a free workspace for your notes" the form feels longer than it should.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming the job is to get a first-time visitor to create a free account with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The layout and the single call to action are sound, but the form asks too much and shows too little. It has six fields, some of which only help the company, and none are marked required or optional. The fields themselves are faint outlines on white. The extra fields are what stand most between a visitor and a finished signup.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for more than a free signup needs, and doesn't say what can be skipped.** "Workspace name" (y≈460–518) and "How did you hear about us?" (y≈545–603) bring a free notes signup to six fields, when it needs about four. The survey question is for the company's marketing, yet it gets the same full-width field as email and password, right above the button. "Workspace name" is the one field a newcomer can't fill from memory: they don't yet know what a workspace is, whether others will see the name, or whether they can change it later. None of the six fields is marked required or optional, so people will treat all of them as required and stop at each one. Every extra decision before the button is a chance to leave. This is a **defect**, backed by Nielsen's heuristic 8 (aesthetic and minimalist design), progressive disclosure, and NN/g on marking required fields.
   - **Fix:** move the attribution question into onboarding after the account exists. If it has to stay, label it "(optional)" and make it look secondary. Fill in a default workspace name, such as from the first name, with a hint like "You can change this later", or ask for it after signup.

2. **The field outlines look too faint to show where the fields are.** All six controls are empty and have no fill, so the pale grey-blue border is the only thing that marks each field. On white it looks to be about 2:1 against the 3:1 that WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 asks for. On a dim screen, or for someone with low vision, the form turns into labels floating over a white card, and the whole task is filling in these boxes. This is a **convention** (WCAG) with a real cost here.
   - **Fix:** darken the borders until they measure at least 3:1 against white, or give the fields a light tinted fill. Measure the border colour to confirm.

**Smaller things**
- "Sign in" at the bottom (y≈698) has the same colour, size and weight as "Already have an account?". It reads as plain text, so returning users who land here by mistake may not find their way out. Underline it or set it in the brand blue (WCAG 1.4.1).
- The Password field gives no rules. People find them only by failing, and then have to retype a password they can't see. Add a one-line requirement under the field.
- The "How did you hear about us?" select is blank, with no prompt, and has the browser's default black chevron. It looks like an empty or broken box. Add a prompt such as "Choose one" and style the chevron to match the fields.
- The subtitle, labels and footer line all look about 13 to 15 px, a pixel or so apart. Weight and colour do the real work of separating them, and the subtitle comes out weaker than the labels under it (Bringhurst: a modest set of distinct intervals). Either put all three on one size or make the subtitle a clear step larger.

**What's working**
- **One path and one ask.** There is one centred card, one column of fields, and one filled navy button straight after the last field. Nothing else on the page competes with the task. Keep the button the only strong block of colour on the card.
- **Clean grid and grouping.** Every element hangs off the same two edges, about x=436 and x=844, and the name pair splits that width exactly. Each label sits about 8 px above its own field, with about 20 px before the next label, so every question reads as its own unit. New hints or error messages should start at the same left edge.
- **Restrained palette.** Text is navy rather than black, the page background is tinted, and one brand blue is shared by the wordmark and the button. The page holds together and the eye knows where to finish.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- **Focus indicator.** With borders this faint, a clearly visible focus ring matters even more (WCAG 2.4.7).
- **Errors.** How validation errors look and are worded, and whether they rely on colour alone.
- **Password visibility.** Whether the password field has a show/hide toggle.
