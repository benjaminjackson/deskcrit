**First read.** The eye goes straight to the huge near-black headline. Its letters are packed so tight that they fuse: "MORNING" smears together around "NIN", and "FERRY" turns into one dark bar. Next the eye drops to the two-line paragraph and only then goes back up to the small grey label. It reads as an event banner for a harbour open day. It feels bold but cramped, and the page stops abruptly, leaving the right half and the bottom 40% empty.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the hero of an event page or poster. It tells the public there are free ferry rides at City Harbour Open Day so they turn up.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline is so over-tightened that it slows the one line that has to land at a glance, and the piece never says when the open day is, so a convinced viewer still can't act.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline letters collide and the word spaces have closed.** In "ALL ABOARD THE / MORNING FERRY" (x ≈ 90–835, y ≈ 150–355), the Ls in "ALL", the "RD" in "ABOARD", the "NIN" in "MORNING" and the "ERR" in "FERRY" touch or merge. At a glance it reads "ALLABOARD THE / MORNINGFERRY", and the viewer has to sort out where letters and words end in the most important line on the page. Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.6) says strings of capitals should be letterspaced. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) says individual letters should not touch. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*) calls negative tracking rarely desirable. Display caps can take some tightening, but not to the point of collision. Fix: open the tracking until no two letters touch and every word space is clearly visible. It can still be slightly tight for punch. **Defect.**
2. **There's no date or time.** The page names an event and says "every half hour from the north landing", but it never says which day, when the first and last rides are, or which harbour's north landing that is. Dates and times are what people act on and remember, so a stranger who wants to go has nothing to plan around. Fix: add the date and the ride hours, for example "Saturday 14 June · every half hour, 9 am–4 pm". If these details sit further down the page, put at least the date in this hero. **Defect.**
3. **The page tips up and to the left, and it looks unfinished.** All the content sits in the top-left block (x 90–835, y 90–460). The heaviest thing on the page, the black headline, has no counterweight. About 340 px of empty white sits below "thermos.", with more to the right. It reads less like deliberate white space and more like a hero section whose image or details never loaded. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") says weight comes from size, darkness and isolation, and a heavy side needs something set against it. Fix: put something in the lower or right area that earns its place. The date, times and place from point 2 are the natural candidate. Otherwise, scale the block to use the whole frame. **Convention.**

**Smaller things.**
- The mood doesn't match the message. The copy is cheerful ("All aboard", "bring a hat and a thermos"), but the piece is all charcoal and grey on white with a static block of type, so it feels like a corporate notice rather than a morning on the water. One sea, sky or sunrise colour with a job, such as on the label or a band behind the headline, would warm it. Colour carries mood (Itten, *The Art of Color*); the point about shape and direction is my taste.
- The label "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" (90, 100) is tightened instead of opened, so "HARBOUROPENDAY" runs together at small size. Give it about 5–10% positive tracking (Bringhurst).

**What's working.**
- **A decisive hierarchy.** The headline (≈110 px, heavy, near-black), the body (≈20 px, regular) and the label (small, bold, grey caps) differ in size, weight, case and colour together. Nobody wonders where to start, and in greyscale the order holds.
- **One firm left edge and a tight group.** The label, headline and body all start at x ≈ 90 with even gaps, so they read as one unit. Keep that edge for anything you add.
- **The body copy.** It is a comfortable size, the lines are about 60 characters with generous leading, and the line about the hat and thermos has a human voice that most event copy lacks.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The empty right side and bottom may be a hero image that failed to load or loads lazily. Check the live page. If nothing is meant to be there, point 3 stands as written.
