**First read.** The eye lands on the leaf pattern, not on the words. The white headline "Plants for shade and small spaces" is the largest type on the page, but it breaks up against the pale yellow leaves behind it, so I had to read it twice. After that the eye drops to the line below the hero and the three cards. It reads as a friendly local garden centre's home page that is fresh and well made, but once you have read it there is nothing to click.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page of a local nursery, meant to get nearby gardeners, especially people with shady yards or balconies, to shop the shade range, book a workshop, or visit.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page's one promise is hard to read and isn't shown in the picture, and a visitor who is interested has no next step except four small grey links in the top corner.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is lost in the pattern.** The white bold headline (about x 72–660, y 210–310) sits directly on the leaf illustration, with nothing behind it to quiet the pattern. Pale yellow and lime leaves and white dots run behind "small" and "spaces", and those letters blend into them. The illustration is the most saturated, highest-contrast thing on screen, and its warm yellows and red dots come forward while the neutral white type falls back. So the picture takes first place, and the line that says what the page offers comes second and needs a second look. Text over an image has to stay readable at the worst point behind it, not on average (WCAG 1.4.3, failure F83). The headline and background also need a clear difference in lightness (Albers, *Interaction of Color*; Itten on warm colours advancing; Refactoring UI). Fix: give the headline a calm, dark field. Put a dark green scrim or gradient over the left third of the hero, use a quieter and darker version of the pattern behind the type, or set the headline on a solid panel. Check the contrast against the palest leaf. **Defect.**
2. **The page builds interest and then gives it nowhere to go.** The line under the hero promises plants "ready to pot this weekend", and the workshops card says "ten places in each class". Both make the visitor want to act, but there is no button anywhere on the page. To find the shade plants, the visitor has to scroll back up and guess that "Plants" in the grey nav (x 948–1224, y ≈ 28) leads to them. Fix: add one clearly styled primary button tied to the headline's offer, such as "Shop shade plants", right under the line below the hero. Give the other actions quieter styling, as text links in the cards: "Book a workshop", "Delivery details". **Defect.**
3. **The picture says "sunny garden", but the offer is shade and small spaces.** The hero (y 60–460, half the screen) is evenly lit, bright yellow-green foliage with orange and red berries. It has no recognisable fern or hosta, and nothing small: no pot, balcony or narrow corner. The image adds only the word "plants", and its mood is the opposite of the offer: summer sun, not cool shade. The visitor with a dim yard doesn't see their own situation, so the hero looks like any garden centre's stock image. Colour temperature and saturation should support the subject, not contradict it (Itten, *The Art of Color*). Fix: show the promise itself. Use deep, cool greens and dappled light, with recognisable fern fronds and hosta leaves, or a photo of the nursery's own plants in a shady corner or pot. Choosing a darker, cooler image also gives the headline the calm ground that finding 1 needs. **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- The headline is in the image, but the line that explains it ("Ferns, hostas…", y ≈ 485–535) sits on white below the hero's edge. The edge at y 460 cuts the message in two, and the line below reads as the start of the cards. Put both in one area, either both on a calm panel in the hero or the headline moved down onto the white directly above it.
- The headline starts at x ≈ 72, while the logo, the line below the hero and the cards all start at x ≈ 56. A 16 px miss looks accidental. Put the headline on the x ≈ 56 line, or give it a clearly intentional inset.
- The headline breaks "small / spaces", splitting the phrase. Break after "shade" instead: "Plants for shade / and small spaces".
- The Opening hours card breaks "until 8 / pm on Thursdays", separating a time from its unit in the one fact people act on. Use non-breaking spaces in "8 pm" and "6 pm".
- The three cards (y 564–678) have the bordered, rounded-tile look of something clickable, but nothing says whether they are links. If they link, add a visible "See times →" style line. If they don't, drop the tile treatment.
- The small red dots sit on mid-green leaves of about the same lightness (for example x 802, y 85 and x 956, y 184), so their edges shimmer and add noise. Make the red clearly darker or lighter than the green, or mute it.

**What's working.**
- **The three cards.** "Opening hours", "Saturday workshops" and "Delivery" each answer a real visitor question with specifics: the Thursday late opening, ten places per class, free delivery within ten miles over $60. They are evenly sized, aligned with the header margins, and use bold title over regular text rather than another type size, so they scan as one set at a glance. Keep the copy and the structure.
- **The brand voice.** The dark green serif wordmark with plain sans navigation and body text reads as one restrained local business. The illustration's greens match the wordmark, and below the hero the page stays neutral and calm.
- **The type below the hero.** The line under the hero is about 60–65 characters per line with comfortable line spacing, the card text has good contrast on white, and the levels are clearly separated.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the nav links and cards show a visible focus ring and hover state. This matters because the nav is currently the only way forward.
- How the headline wraps, and which leaves sit behind each word, at phone and wide-desktop widths. Check readability over the illustration at both.
- The empty band under the cards (y ≈ 680–800) could be where the capture ends or where the page ends. Check in a live browser.
