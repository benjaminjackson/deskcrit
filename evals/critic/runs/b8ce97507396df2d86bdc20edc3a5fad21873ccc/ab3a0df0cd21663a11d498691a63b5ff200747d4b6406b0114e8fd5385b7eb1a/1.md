**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline on navy, then drops to the solid white "Try it free" button, then to the three bold feature headings below. It reads as a landing page for a consumer budgeting app. It feels calm, trustworthy and plain, but templated. The right half of the hero is an empty navy field, and nothing anywhere shows the product.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the marketing home page for a personal and household budgeting app, meant to get people who manage their own money to start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The type, colour and grid are clean and well controlled, and the copy is better than most. But the page asks you to believe "See where every dollar goes" without ever showing you, and the empty right half of the hero makes it look unfinished. Until the product is visible, the page is just a promise.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no picture, so the headline's promise goes unproven and the composition tips left.** Defect, unless an image failed to load. All the hero content sits in the left 60%: the headline runs to about x 780, the subhead to about x 660, and the buttons to about x 386. The right side, roughly x 800–1216 by y 62–462, is flat navy with nothing in it.
   - **What the viewer meets:** first, a hero that looks broken or half built. Nothing on the right balances the heavy white headline, so the band feels lopsided (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Second, and more costly: for a budgeting app, the strongest argument is a look at your money sorted into categories, and the page never shows one. The visitor has to take "every dollar" and "a heads-up before you go over" on trust. That's a lot to ask of someone being asked to connect their bank accounts.
   - **Fix:** fill the space, inside the grid's third column (about x 859–1216), with a product view that finishes the headline's thought. For example, one month's spending by category, with one category near its limit and the "heads-up" alert showing. Make it heavy enough to balance the headline block.
   - **If an image is supposed to be there:** check the live page. If it just didn't load in the capture, this finding is about the capture, not the design.

**Smaller things.**
- **Header:** "Sign in" (top right, about x 1168–1216) has the same grey text and spacing as Features, Pricing and Security, so it reads as one more content link. The header also has no way to start once a visitor scrolls past the hero. Set "Sign in" apart with a wider gap, and add a small "Try it free" button next to it. Convention (Nielsen Norman Group, Jakob's law).
- **"Try it free":** the button doesn't say whether this is a trial that ends or a free plan, and it doesn't say what the first step is. A visitor about to hand over bank access will want both answers before clicking. Say which it is near the button, for example "Free for 30 days, no card", or whatever is true. A label that names the first step, such as "Connect an account free", tells them what happens next.
- **Feature row (y 500–575):** three one-sentence blurbs are the only support for the claim, and the page stops there. If this really is the end of the page and not a cut-off screenshot, the row floats over about 220 px of blank white. Follow it with proof: the product, numbers, or security detail behind "never move money".

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific and speaks to the visitor.**
  - The headline names a concrete outcome ("every dollar", "this month").
  - The subhead lays out the product in three verbs, in order: connect, set a limit, get a heads-up.
  - "The app can see balances and history but can never move money" answers the biggest fear about connecting a bank.

  Keep that plain, active voice.
- **One grid and a clear button order.** The logo, headline, subhead, buttons and first feature all start at x≈64, and the three feature columns repeat at an even pitch. The solid white primary button over the outlined secondary one makes the order obvious from lightness alone, which also holds up in grayscale and for colour-blind viewers.
- **Few, distinct levels in one restrained hue.** The headline is about three times anything else on the page. Each level below it steps down in size, and also in weight or colour, so each feature scans as a heading followed by one line. The single dark navy suits a product that touches bank accounts.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Empty hero space and bottom band:** whether the empty right half of the hero is a missing image, and whether the white band below about y 575 is the end of the page or just where the screenshot stopped. Check both in a live browser.
- **Focus rings:** whether the buttons and nav links show a visible focus ring on both navy and white.
