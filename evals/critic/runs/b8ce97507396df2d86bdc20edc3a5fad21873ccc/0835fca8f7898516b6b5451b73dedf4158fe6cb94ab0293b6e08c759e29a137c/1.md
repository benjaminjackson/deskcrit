**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold "River ferry timetable" at the top left, then down three calm paragraphs of near-black prose. The title sets up a timetable, so the eye scans for times and finds none. The right half and the bottom third are empty, so the page looks half-built. It feels tidy and quiet, but it doesn't deliver what it promised.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public web page that tells ferry riders when the boat runs and the rules for riding it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The page is called a timetable but has no times, so a rider can't plan a crossing from it. The typesetting is sound, but it carries the wrong content.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable is missing.** The title "River ferry timetable" (top left) promises departure times. What follows is three prose paragraphs, and the only schedule is "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" in winter, and the last crossing "before dusk". A rider who wants the next boat has to work it out, and still can't, because "early morning", "late evening" and "dusk" aren't clock times. The last paragraph then sends them to printed timetables in the shelters, which means the page itself admits it can't answer the question. This matters most because times are what a rider acts on and later holds the operator to. A heading should describe what's under it (WCAG 2.4.6), and a page should answer its question at a glance (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). **Fix:** put a real table of departures under the title, with first and last sailings, the interval, and summer and winter in separate columns, each with clock times. Keep the prose for the bicycle and noticeboard information. If the times really can't go on this page, rename it, for example "About the river ferry", and link to the timetable. **Defect.**
2. **The empty space looks like something failed to load.** All the content sits in one block in the upper left, from about x 120 to 735 and y 75 to 470. The right 45% and the bottom 40% are a large L-shaped gap with no purpose, so the page tips to the top left and looks unfinished. Because the title promises a timetable, the empty right column is exactly where the eye goes looking for one. Weight needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), and empty space should be shaped on purpose, not left over (Ruder; Tschichold). **Fix:** fixing item 1 mostly fixes this. Put the timetable in the right column, aligned with the top of the title. If the page stays text-only, centre the column or size the frame to the content, so the empty space reads as margin rather than a gap. **Defect.**

**What's working.**
- **The body text is comfortable to read.** It's about 18 px in a plain sans, with lines of roughly 65 to 73 characters, about 1.5 line spacing, and near-black on white. Keep this setting when you add the table.
- **The hierarchy is simple and clear.** There are two levels: a bold title about twice the body size, and regular body text. Everything sits on one firm left edge at x≈120. You know where to start, and nothing competes.
- **The text is split by topic.** There are three short paragraphs (route, bicycles, winter), so someone looking for the bicycle rule can find it without reading everything else.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell whether the empty right column is meant to hold a timetable, map or photo that didn't load in this capture. Check the live page. If something renders there, item 1 becomes "put the times next to the title" and item 2 needs a fresh look. I also couldn't see how the single column behaves at other viewport widths.
