**First read.** The eye goes straight to the three big numbers (3.8, 64.20, 12.4), then down to the orange bars, where Saturday is the tallest and Sunday drops off. It reads as the admin dashboard for a small online bakery that sells subscriptions. It feels calm, tidy and warm. On a second look, none of the three big numbers says what it is counting.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the bakery owner opens this page to see at a glance how the shop is doing, and whether anything needs attention.

**Verdict.** The page is well built, but it doesn't yet do its job. The three numbers it puts first have no units, no time period and nothing to compare them with, so the owner can't tell what they mean or whether they are good.

**What would move it most**
1. **The three big numbers have no units and no period.** They are in the KPI cards across the top.
   - "3.8" is probably a percentage, but nothing says so.
   - "64.20" has no currency.
   - "12.4" active subscribers can't be a head count. It is most likely 12,400, but the viewer has to guess.
   - Nothing says whether the figures cover today, September or all time, or whether they cover the same span as the chart. The only date on the page is in the smallest grey line, under the chart.

   These numbers are the largest and darkest thing on the page, and they are what the owner will act on and repeat to others. A figure that can be read two ways here is a real problem. Someone who reads 12.4 literally, or takes 3.8 as a ratio, gets the business wrong. Fix: put the unit into each figure ("3.8%", "£64.20", "12.4k"). State the period once at page level, next to "Shop overview", so it clearly covers both the cards and the chart. Tufte, *Visual Display*; Nielsen, heuristic 1 (visibility of system status). **Defect.**
2. **No number has anything to compare it with.** None of the cards shows a change from last period, a target or a trend. So the question the owner opens the page with, better or worse?, goes unanswered. Is 3.8% conversion good? Are subscribers growing? They would have to remember last month's figures or look them up. Each card has plenty of empty space to the right of its number. Fix: add one comparison line under each number, such as "▲ 0.4 pts vs August", using a sign or word as well as colour (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). **Convention**, and here breaking it costs the page its main use.

**Smaller things.**
- There is no visible control for the period (top of the page, near "Shop overview"). To see another month, the owner has to leave the page. Make the period label from finding 1 a picker, such as "September 2026 ▾", that sets the cards and the chart together.
- The chart title "Orders by delivery day" names the topic but not the finding. Something like "Saturday is the busiest delivery day; Sunday the quietest" would say it for the viewer. Taste, and minor: with seven bars, the pattern reads fast anyway.

**What's working.**
- **The grid is strict.** The three cards and the full-width chart share one outer edge. The 24 px gutter between cards is also the gap above the chart. The logo, title and every card's contents line up on two shared left edges. The page reads as one system. Keep that when you add the comparison lines.
- **The type scale is clear.** There are about five sizes with big steps between them, and weight and grey do the fine work (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). The numbers clearly lead and the grey labels sit quietly under them.
- **The chart is honest and restrained.** The axis starts at zero, the ticks go up in even steps, the gridlines are pale, and there is one flat orange. The days stay in weekday order, so the weekly rhythm shows as a shape. The one warm colour appears only on the wordmark and the data, so nothing else competes for attention.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Check the nav hit areas against WCAG 2.5.8 (24×24 px), and that the nav links show a visible focus state.
- If the cards or bars click through to Orders or Subscribers, nothing on the screen says so.
- The grey axis labels and source line look like they pass 4.5:1 contrast, but it's close. Check them in code.
