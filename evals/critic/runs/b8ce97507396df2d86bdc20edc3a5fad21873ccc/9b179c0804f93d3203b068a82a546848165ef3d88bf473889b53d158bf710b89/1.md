**First read.** The eye lands on the centred masthead, "The Wexley Gazette", drops past the double rule, and then reads three headlines of equal weight from left to right. It reads as the front page of a small local paper, quiet and orderly. Then you notice that everything stops halfway down the screen. The lower half is blank, so the page looks unfinished or still loading.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the front page of a small-town paper, on the web or in print, where residents scan the headlines and read the short stories.

**Verdict.** It is clean, well set and easy to read, but it is not yet a front page. Nothing tells the reader which story matters most, so it reads like a list of three items with a title on top.

**What would move it most**
1. **No lead story** (defect, and a broken newspaper convention). Below the rule, "Bridge to reopen in spring", "Market moves indoors" and "School wins garden prize" are identical. They have the same headline size and weight, the same column width, the same body size, and they all start on the same line. The masthead is only about 1.4× the headline size, in the same bold and the same ink, and it is the paper's name, not news. So the eye starts at the left column only because we read left to right, then moves across three equal choices. The page never says "this is today's story". Tschichold (*The New Typography*) says differences in size should show the order of importance, and Hofmann (*Graphic Design Manual*) builds a page on contrasts like much against little. Here there is no contrast at all. Fix: pick one story to lead, probably the bridge, since it affects the most readers. Give it a much bigger headline and more room, for example full width above the other two or across two of the three columns, and let the other two step back. That also gives the page a clear order: masthead, then the lead, then the rest. A lead that spans the columns would also start to fill the empty lower half (see below).

**Smaller things.**
- **Empty lower half.** All the content ends at about y 415, and the band from about 420 to 800 is blank, so the page looks top-heavy and unfinished. Spread the weight down the page with a lead, a picture, or a lower band of briefs or listings on the same three-column grid. If this is just a short page in a tall window, check it at the real page height first.
- **No issue date.** Nothing between the masthead and the rule says what day or month this is, but the stories say "from next month", "in April" and "from March". Readers act on these dates (when to go to the market, when the bridge opens), and without a reference point "next month" can't be pinned down. Add a dateline under the masthead, or name the month in the market story.

**What's working.**
- **The grid is tight.** There are three equal columns with even gutters, their outer edges meet the ends of the double rule exactly, and the body text in all three columns runs on the same baselines. The row of stories reads as one even band, and new content has an obvious place to go.
- **One serif family in one near-black ink.** The type has three clearly separate levels (masthead, bold headlines, roman body text at a comfortable size with strong contrast) and no accent colour. It feels like a sober, trustworthy local paper rather than a template. Keep this when you add the lead.
- **Headlines that state the news.** Each one is short and has a verb ("Bridge to reopen in spring"), so a reader gets all three stories without reading the body text.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether the blank lower half is the design or a tall window around short content, so check it at the real page height. I also can't see how the three columns reflow on narrower screens.
