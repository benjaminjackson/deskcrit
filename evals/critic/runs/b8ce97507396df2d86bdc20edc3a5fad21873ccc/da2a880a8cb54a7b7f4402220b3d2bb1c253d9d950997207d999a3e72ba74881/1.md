I've checked the reports against a close crop of the lower form; now writing the final crit.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Create your account", goes down a column of empty boxes, and stops on the dark-blue "Create account" button. It reads as the signup page for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm and tidy, but also stock: it could be any SaaS signup. Two things catch the eye on the way down: the dropdown is blank with nothing in it, and the "Sign in" line at the bottom looks like a caption, not something you can click.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the signup screen where someone new to Quillnest creates a free account as quickly as possible, and where a returning user can get to sign-in.

**Verdict.** It works: the order is clear, there's one action, and the form is cleanly built. But it asks for more than signup needs, and it hides the one way out for people who already have an account. Shortening the form is the biggest single gain.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two optional questions sit between the password and the button.** These are "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" (x 436–844, y 485–636).
   - They make a four-field signup a six-field one. The marketing question serves you, not the person signing up.
   - The select is a blank box with only a chevron: no "Select one" prompt and no "(optional)". It looks unfinished, and people stop to wonder whether they must answer.
   - For a free notes app, how much effort signup takes decides whether people finish. Every element on the screen should help the main task (Nielsen, heuristic 8, aesthetic and minimalist design).
   - Fix: move both questions to the first screen after the account exists, or drop the marketing one. You could also name the workspace automatically from the first name. If they have to stay, label them "(optional)", give the select a prompt, and set them apart below the required fields.
   - Convention, plus common sense about signup friction.
2. **"Sign in" doesn't look like a link.** In "Already have an account? Sign in" (around 700, 731), "Sign in" is the same small grey as the rest of the sentence. It has no underline, no colour and no weight change.
   - This is the only exit for returning users who landed here by mistake, and it reads as plain text, so they have to hunt for it.
   - Inline links need a cue beyond the surrounding text colour (WCAG F73 / G183).
   - Fix: underline it, put it in the brand blue, or both.
   - Defect.
3. **The password field gives no rules and has no show/hide control** (y 424–466).
   - People learn the length or character rules only from an error after they submit.
   - With no confirm field and no reveal toggle, they also can't catch a typo, and that typo locks them out on their first sign-in.
   - Fix: add one line of help under the field (for example "At least 8 characters") and an eye toggle at the right end of the input.
   - Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- Field borders (all six inputs) look like light slate on white, possibly under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for. The fields have no fill, so the border is all there is. Measure the computed colour and darken it a step if it falls short.
- The subtitle (~15 px), the "* Required field" note and the footer (~13 px), and the labels (~14 px) are all close in size. The subtitle, the only line about what you get, ends up looking like fine print. Use one body size for subtitle, labels and footer, and keep one clearly smaller size for the note (Bringhurst).
- "* Required field" (y ≈ 201) sits the same distance from the subtitle as from the first label, so it belongs to neither. Move it closer to the fields, or drop it, since the red asterisks already say it.
- The asterisk in that key is grey, but the asterisks it explains are red. Make it the same red (taste).
- "Start a free workspace for your notes." could sit on any notes app's page. It gives anyone still deciding no reason to pick Quillnest over the tool they already use. Make it name the one thing Quillnest does differently, in the reader's terms (taste).

**What's working.**
- **The page reads in the right order.** The bold heading at the top and the full-width filled button at the bottom bracket the task, and the eye runs straight from one to the other. The button is the only strong block of colour, so there's never a question about the next step.
- **The form is built on one column with shared edges.** Every field, the select and the button share the x 436 and 844 edges. First and last name split the column cleanly into two halves. Each bold label sits closer to its own field than to the one above, so label and field read as one unit.
- **The palette is restrained.** One blue is shared by the logo and the button, and red appears only on the required marks. That leaves red free for error states.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and the button: they should be visible, at least 2 px, with 3:1 contrast.
- Whether Email and Password use the right input types and autocomplete hints, so phones show the right keyboard and password managers fill the fields.
- Whether error messages name the field and say how to fix it.
