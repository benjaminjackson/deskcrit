**Verdict.** It does its basic job. The hierarchy is clear, there is one obvious action, and the page is tidy and consistent. The biggest problem is that its one picture is a plain finished invoice, so it doesn't show the speed or the auto-fill the copy promises. Visitors have to take both claims on trust, and nothing on the page marks this product apart from any other invoicing tool.

**What would move it most**
1. **The invoice card repeats the word "invoice" when it should prove the claim.** The card is on the right (about x 820–1160, y 230–428). The headline promises speed ("in the time it takes to pour coffee") and the subhead promises automation (it fills in clients, rates and tax, then chases late payments). The card shows a static, completed invoice, which any competitor's page could show. It is also small next to the headline and floats in empty space, so it reads as a sample, not as evidence. Visitors get nothing they can judge for themselves, and the page looks like every other page in this category. Fix: make the picture show what the words can't. Show the auto-fill happening (a client name typed, rate and tax filled in by themselves), or a "sent" stamp with an elapsed time. Or put it next to something the visitor already uses, like a blank spreadsheet invoice, so they can judge the speed claim themselves. Give it enough size to hold its own against the headline. Taste, argued on what the visitor can check.

**Smaller things.**
- **Feature cards** (y 602–751): text ends around y 670–690, so the bottom 40–55% of each card is empty white box. The raised, bordered tiles also look clickable but show no link cue, so visitors can't tell if they open anything. If the cards link somewhere, put a "Learn more →" in that empty space. If they don't, fit the height to the content and flatten them (drop the white fill or the border) so they read as content, not buttons. Defect, minor.
- **Headline breaks**: "Send an invoice in the / time it takes to pour / coffee." breaks "in the / time" and "pour / coffee", and leaves the punchline word alone on a short last line (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*). Break it by sense, for example "Send an invoice / in the time it takes / to pour coffee.", and check the breaks again at each breakpoint. Convention and common sense.
- **Copy contradiction about payment timing**: the subhead says it "chases late payments" (after the due date). The first card says "Three gentle nudges before an invoice is overdue" (before the due date). Freelancers will hold the product to this, so the two lines should agree. Say both, for example "nudges before it's due, and chases it after." Defect, minor.
- **Right column near-miss**: the invoice card's left edge (about x 821) is roughly 3–4 px left of the third feature card's (about x 824), even though both share the right edge at x 1160. Down the column they almost line up but don't. Snap the invoice card to the third column's left edge and width. Measure in a live browser before changing it. Convention, minor.
