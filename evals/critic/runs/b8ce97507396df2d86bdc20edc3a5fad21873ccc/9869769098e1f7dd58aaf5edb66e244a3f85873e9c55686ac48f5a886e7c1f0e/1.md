**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces", over the dark leaf pattern. From there it goes up to the green serif wordmark, then down to the intro line and the three outlined cards. It reads as the home page of a local garden centre, and it feels calm, tidy and green. Then it stops: nothing tells you what to do next.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page of a local nursery, and its job is to get nearby gardeners to look at this season's shade plants, then visit, order or book a workshop.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and well set, and the facts in the cards are useful, but it doesn't do its job yet. It makes a clear offer and then gives no way to act on it. The hero image is half the screen and shows none of what's for sale.

**What would move it most**
1. **The offer leads nowhere.** The headline and the line under it (y≈485–540) say these plants are "ready to pot this weekend". The workshops card says there are "ten places in each class". Yet there's no button or link on the page. A visitor who wants those plants has to guess that "Plants" in the small grey nav at top right is the way in, and nothing on the page lets them book a workshop. The page stops at the moment the visitor is most interested.
   - **Fix:** add one primary button right under the intro line that names the exact thing, such as "See the shade plants in stock". Make "Book a place" and "Delivery details" smaller secondary links, styled below that button.
   - **Kind:** defect. It slows and loses the visitor the page was built for.
2. **The hero picture is wallpaper, and it fights the headline.** The band from y≈60 to y≈460 is an even scatter of flat leaf shapes with berry dots. It shows no fern, no hosta, no pot, no shaded corner and nothing from this nursery. The headline therefore carries the whole idea alone, and half the screen does no selling. The pattern is just as busy behind the headline as anywhere else: light olive leaves and pale dots cut between the letters of "small" and "spaces". The white type still reads, but its edges flicker and it looks pasted on.
   - **Fix:** use a photo of the actual offer, such as a fern or hosta in a pot on a shady balcony or in a narrow courtyard, with a calm dark area where the headline sits. If the pattern has to stay, make it quieter and darker behind the text so it reads as one dark tone.
   - **Kind:** taste, plus common sense on the contrast. The picture-and-headline point is my judgment. No approved source covers it.

**Smaller things**
- The headline is separated from its own subline. The headline sits inside the hero, and the intro line sits on white below the hero's hard edge, about as close to the cards (~28 px) as to the hero (~25 px). It reads as an intro to the cards, not as the second half of the headline. Put the headline and the intro line in the same area, and make the gap above the cards clearly larger. (Common region, Nielsen Norman Group.)
- The headline starts at x≈72, while the logo, intro line and left card all start at x≈56. That 16 px near-miss makes the left edge wobble right where the eye moves down. Put the headline on the 56 px edge, or indent it on purpose by much more.
- The headline breaks as "…shade and small / spaces", which splits "small spaces" and leaves one word alone on the biggest line on the page. Break it as "Plants for shade / and small spaces".
- The opening-hours card wraps as "until 8 / pm on Thursdays", which splits the number from its unit in the one fact people will act on. Use non-breaking spaces in "8 pm" and "6 pm" (Bringhurst, §2.4.6).
- The three bordered, rounded cards look like tiles you can click, but nothing says whether they are links. Their pale border looks well under 3:1 against white. If they're links, add an arrow or link label and a stronger edge (WCAG 1.4.11). If they're only information, drop the border.
- The delivery card says "orders over $60", but the nav has no Shop, Order or cart, so a visitor can't tell how to place an order. Say how in the card, or add a Shop entry to the nav.

**What's working**
- **The copy is concrete.** "Plants for shade and small spaces" names a real problem the visitor has. The cards give facts you can act on: "until 8 pm on Thursdays", "ten places in each class", "orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box". Keep this specific voice when you add the call to action.
- **The type has clear tiers.** It runs from the serif wordmark to the heavy headline, then the 20 px intro, then bold card titles over regular card text. The step between card titles and card text is made by weight, not by another size ("bolder, not bigger", Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
- **The palette and grid are calm.** One family of greens plus a neutral range, the green wordmark echoing the hero, and three equal cards on a clean 56–1224 px column. Together they make the page feel like one quiet, local place.
