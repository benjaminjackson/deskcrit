**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline, "Travel lighter with the Wayfarer pack", drops to the grey line under it, then stops on the dark "Shop the pack" button. It reads as the top of a shop page launching a new travel backpack. It feels calm, confident and clean. It also feels unfinished: everything sits in the upper left, the right side and the bottom are empty white, and you never see the bag.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top section of a shop's product-launch page, meant to get travellers to look at and buy the Wayfarer pack.

**Verdict.** The text is well made, but the page doesn't yet do its job. It asks people to shop for a physical bag they never see, and the empty right half makes it look broken rather than spacious.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no picture of the bag, and the layout tips into the top-left corner.** All the content sits between about x 120–857 and y 120–494 of a 1280×800 frame. The right third and the bottom 300 px are empty. The line under the headline makes three physical claims: 22 litres, opens flat like a suitcase, under a kilo. The viewer can't see any of them, so "Shop the pack" asks them to take the claims on trust. The heavy headline has nothing on the right to balance it, so the frame tips up and to the left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). The empty space is what's left over around the text, not a shape anyone planned (Ruder, form and counter-form), so to a shopper it looks like an image failed to load.
   - **Fix:** put the pack in the right side, in a shot that proves one claim rather than repeating the name: opened flat like a suitcase, or tucked under an airline seat. Then the headline gives the benefit and the picture proves it. Set the text column and the image column to a deliberate split, such as halves or roughly 7/12 and 5/12.
   - **Check first:** if an image is meant to be there and was caught before it loaded, confirm that on the live page.
   - **Kind:** defect.
2. **There's no header: no logo, no navigation, no search, no cart.** The band above "New for spring" (y 0–110) is blank. A first-time visitor can't tell whose shop this is or whether this is the only bag or one of a range, and has no visible way to search or reach the cart. Shoppers expect the logo at top left, categories across the top, and search and cart at top right (Nielsen, Jakob's law). Leaving them out buys nothing here.
   - **Fix:** add the standard shop header and mark the current section in it.
   - **Check first:** if this image is only the hero cut out of a full page that has a header, this falls away.
   - **Kind:** convention.

**Smaller things.**
- **No price near the button.** "Shop the pack" asks for a click before the shopper knows if the bag costs 60 or 300. Put "from €X" beside the button or on the line above it. (Taste.)
- **The headline breaks awkwardly.** It breaks as "Travel lighter with the / Wayfarer pack", which leaves "the" hanging at the end of line 1. Break it as "Travel lighter / with the Wayfarer pack" so each line is a whole phrase and the product name gets its own line. (Taste.)

**What's working.**
- **The headline owns the page.** It's roughly 72 px, heavy and near-black, against a regular-weight grey line of about 20 px under it. There's one clear place to start, and size, weight and colour then step down through the grey line to the filled button and the plain underlined "Compare sizes" link. There's one obvious main action and a quieter second option. Keep that order when the image goes in.
- **The line under the headline is specific.** It gives a capacity with its unit, plus three references the reader already trusts: an airline seat, a suitcase, a kilo. That lets shoppers judge the value for themselves. The line is about 65 characters long, a comfortable length to read.
- **One strong left edge and a restrained palette.** The eyebrow, headline, subline and buttons all start exactly at x ≈ 120. The only colour is the small blue "New for spring" label. The button uses the headline's near-black instead of a new accent colour, and the page reads the same in greyscale.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right side is a hero image that hadn't loaded or was lazy-loaded when this was captured. Check in a live browser before acting on finding 1.
- Whether "Shop the pack" and "Compare sizes" show a clearly visible focus ring when you tab to them.
