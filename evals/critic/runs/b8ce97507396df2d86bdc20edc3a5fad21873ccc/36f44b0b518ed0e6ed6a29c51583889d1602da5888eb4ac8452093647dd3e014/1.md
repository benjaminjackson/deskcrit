**First read.** The eye lands on the white serif "How can we help?" on the navy band, drops straight to the white search field, then moves down into the 4×2 grid of topic cards. It reads as a help center home page for a software product. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, but close to a template: it could be anyone's help center.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: it gets customers who arrive with a problem to the right article fast, by search or by picking a topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The entry point is obvious, the topics are easy to scan, and nothing gets in the way. The one thing worth fixing first is the search field. It's the main task on the page, yet nothing marks it as a search control except its placeholder text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The search field has no icon, no button and no label.** The white field (about x 360–920, y 216–264) is identified only by the placeholder "Search for articles". While it's empty it reads well enough, because a big white bar under "How can we help?" says search. Once someone types, the placeholder disappears and nothing on the field says what it is or how to run it. Someone who doesn't think to press Enter has no visible way to submit, on the one control most visitors come here to use. Placeholder text is not a label (Nielsen Norman Group; WCAG 3.3.2). A lone search box is the accepted exception only when a magnifier icon or a search button marks it, and this field has neither. Fix: put a magnifier at the left of the field and a "Search" button (or arrow) at its right end, and give the field a programmatic label. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- **"Browse by topic" is too close to the card titles.** The heading (about 18 px bold) is only a step bigger than the card titles below it (about 16 px bold), in the same face, weight and colour. It reads as a ninth title rather than the label over the grid (Dair, *Design with Type*; Bringhurst 3.1.1). Make it plainly different: use the headline serif, a clear size step up, or a small grey label style.
- **Lone words on the last line of card descriptions.** Five of the eight descriptions end with one word alone on line two: "recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages." This makes the grid look ragged and set by default (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Balance the breaks (`text-wrap: balance`) or edit each blurb to fill its lines.
- **The search field nearly lines up with the cards, but not quite.** It is centred and almost exactly as wide as the two middle card columns (about x 357–922), but misses their edges by about 2–3 px on each side. Make it span those two columns exactly, or make it clearly narrower or wider. Measure it on the live page.
- **"Guides" in the nav vs "search the guides" in the hero.** The hero offers to "search the guides", and the nav lists "Guides" as a separate destination with nothing marked as the current page. Visitors can't tell whether they're already in Guides. Either mark this page as Guides or stop using the same word for two places. *Taste, common sense.*
- **One topic seems to belong to another product.** "Orders and shipping: Track a delivery or start a return" sits among topics about a team workspace (inviting your team, integrations, sync errors). A newcomer may wonder whether this help center covers the product they use. Confirm each topic belongs to this product, and cut or reword any that don't.
- **The copy is generic.** "How can we help?" / "Browse a topic or search the guides." could sit on any help center. It costs little here, but naming the product, or the two or three most common problems, would tell visitors right away that they're in the right place. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- **One clear entry point.** The single dark band holds the only serif on the page and the lightest shape on the darkest ground, so the eye goes question → search field in about a second. Keep that band as the only dark area.
- **A strict, consistent grid.** The logo, heading and first card column share the 64 px left edge, and the nav and last column end together at about x 1216. The eight cards share one pattern: a matching thin navy outline icon, a bold title, and a grey description that differs by weight and colour, not just size. Visitors compare topics by their words, not by their styling. When topics are added, add rows rather than changing card sizes.
- **Icons and descriptions in the visitor's words.** Key for sign-in, card for billing, shield for privacy, lifebuoy for troubleshooting, plus descriptions like "Passwords, two-step sign-in, and recovery". Each card confirms whether it fits the visitor's problem without a click.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the search field, nav links and cards. Check that each shows a visible indicator with at least 3:1 contrast and isn't hidden by anything.
- Hover states on the cards, which are the main way to browse.
- What sits below the cards. The capture ends just under row two. Check whether visitors whose problem isn't one of the eight topics get popular articles or a path to a person.
