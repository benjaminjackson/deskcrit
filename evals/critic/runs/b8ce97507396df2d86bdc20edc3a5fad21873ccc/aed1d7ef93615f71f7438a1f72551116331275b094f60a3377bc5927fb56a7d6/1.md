**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It starts at the top left only because that is where reading starts, then slides down eight lines of the same grey text. The headings look the same as the answers, or slightly smaller. It reads as a help or FAQ panel about collecting an order from a bakery. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, but you have to read it all rather than scan it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help section where a customer who has ordered bread finds the answer to one question (changing the time, sending someone else, allergies, cancelling) as fast as possible.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The questions don't stand out from the answers, so a customer can't scan for their question, and the most important answer, the cancellation deadline, can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The questions look like body text.** All four headings ("Changing your pickup time" at y≈69, "Collecting for someone else" at y≈182, "Allergies and ingredients" at y≈268, "Cancelling an order" at y≈381) use the same typeface, weight and near-black colour as the answers, and they look a pixel or so smaller. The only thing marking them as headings is the gap of about 10 px beneath each one. Someone who only wants to know "can I still cancel?" has to read the first line of every block to find it, which defeats the point of an FAQ. A difference this small is worse than none: it reads as a mistake, not a level (Dair, *Design with Type*, on weak contrast). Fix: make the difference obvious at a glance. Set the questions in semibold or bold at the same size or one clear step larger, or keep them dark and make the answers a lighter grey. Never leave the heading smaller than its answer. *Defect.*
2. **The cancellation deadline is ambiguous.** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" (y≈416) could mean 24 hours before the pickup time, or any time on the day before. Customers act on deadlines and hold the bakery to them. A customer who reads it the generous way and cancels the evening before, for a morning pickup, will think they cancelled in time and the bakery won't. That ends in a dispute over a refund. Fix: state the exact cutoff in the customer's terms, e.g. "You can cancel until 24 hours before your pickup time". Keep "after that, we've already baked your bread", which is a good reason, said like a person. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- No page title (top of the frame, above y≈60): nothing says what this page is or whose bakery it is, so the reader works it out from the topics. Add a title that clearly dominates, e.g. "Collecting your order". If a title already sits above this capture, ignore this.
- "Along with a name" (y≈217): it's unclear whether that means the collector's name or the name on the order, so a friend could be turned away at the till. Say "the name the order is under".
- "Open your order" (y≈104): it sends the reader to another screen but doesn't say how to get there. Link it or name where it is.
- "Holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" (y≈104–131): it stops before saying what happens after that. Finish the line for the reader, e.g. "after that, it goes back on the shelf".
- Line length (x 160–980): one-line answers run to about 90–95 characters, well past 45–75 (Bringhurst). Narrow the column to about 600–640 px at this size.
- Lone word "anything." on the last line of the allergies answer (y≈330), and a short last line at y≈131: these will rewrap once the column is narrowed. Then check the last lines again (Bringhurst).
- Column placement (*taste*): the column sits from x≈160 to x≈980 in a 1280 px frame, about 70 px left of centre. That is neither centred nor clearly hung to the left. Centre it, or line it up with the site's header grid so the left margin looks deliberate.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing does the grouping.** Each question sits about 10 px above its answer and about 35–40 px below the previous answer. Even without contrast in the type, the four pairs read as pairs. Keep that ratio when you make the headings heavier.
- **One strong left edge and one quiet voice.** Every line starts at x=160, there is one typeface, and the text is dark grey rather than pure black. Nothing decorative competes with the answers, which suits a help page.
- **The headings use the customer's words.** "Collecting for someone else" and "Cancelling an order" name real situations, not internal categories. Once they look like headings, they will scan well.
