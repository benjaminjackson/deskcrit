**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline. Then the dark photo in the first card pulls it hard, because that photo is the heaviest and busiest thing on the screen and looks nothing like the three flat illustrations beside it. From there the eye runs along the four green prices and ends in an empty cream band across the bottom fifth of the screen. It reads as a calm, friendly, slightly templated booking page for a local home-services company.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a homeowner should understand the offer, pick a service, and book a visit from this screen.

**Verdict.** It's close. The palette, type and copy are good, but the page fails at its one job: getting someone from "I want this service" to booking it. The cards have no action, and the odd photo in the first card misdirects the eye and makes the page look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards don't let you pick the service they describe.** The subhead says "Pick a service, choose a day". But the four cards (y≈279–646) have no button, link, arrow or "Book" label. Only a faint shadow hints that they might be clickable. Someone who has settled on "Deep cleaning · From $189" has to guess whether to click the card or go up to "Book a visit" in the top right, and that button doesn't know which service they chose. There is no day picker either, so the subhead promises steps this screen doesn't offer. Clickable things need a visible signifier, and the control should sit next to what it acts on (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*; Nielsen Norman Group on making links look clickable). Fix: give each card a secondary action under the price, such as "Book deep cleaning →". Keep the header button as the one filled primary. Alternatively, reword the subhead to match what this screen actually does. *Defect.*
2. **The photo in the "Window cleaning" card breaks the set.** At x≈70–316, y≈293–473 there is a dark, warm, detailed photo of a wood-panelled lounge with leather sofas. The other three cards use flat teal-and-coral drawings on mint tiles. The photo is the darkest, busiest block on the page, so it outweighs the headline as a draw. It makes window cleaning look like the featured service when nothing says it is, and it looks like a placeholder nobody swapped out, which hurts trust on a site asking strangers into your home. It also doesn't show clean windows or anyone cleaning; it shows a club lounge. Pictures shown as one set should share one style and level of detail (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Apple HIG, "Icons"). Fix: draw a window-cleaning illustration on the same mint tile and palette, or move all four cards to photos with one shared grade and crop. *Defect.*
3. **The prices have no unit.** "From $89", "From $189", "From $24" and "From $65" don't say what they buy. $24 for laundry could be per bag, per load or per week, and $65 for garden care could be per visit or per month. These are the numbers customers will hold the business to. Someone who reads $24 as weekly and gets billed per load will feel misled. Fix: add the unit, for example "From $24 per bag" or "From $89 per visit". *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Empty band under the cards (y≈646–800): the content stops a fifth of the way above the bottom of the screen with no hint of what comes next, so the page looks top-heavy and unfinished. Bring the next section into view, or rebalance the space above and below the hero group. This is fine if the live page already shows the next section above the fold.
- Card insets: the pictures sit about 14 px in from the card edge and the text about 20 px. That 6 px near-miss repeats on every card. Give the text the same inset as the picture, or move it clearly further in.
- Header nav (x≈520–874): its centre (≈697) is about 57 px right of the page centre and of the middle card gutter. Centre it on the page or align it to a card column.
- Headline break: "Help around the house, on your / schedule" splits the phrase and leaves one word on the second line. Break after "house," or use a balanced wrap. (Common sense.)
- Current page: the header nav doesn't mark which page you're on. Show the current item with weight, colour or an underline (Nielsen, visibility of system status).
- Card edges: white cards on cream are held up only by a soft shadow. If the whole card is the click target, give it an edge that reaches 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). This stops mattering once each card has its own button.

**What's working.**
- **The palette is tight and has clear jobs.** Slate carries the wordmark, headline and the one filled button, and teal is used only for prices. Warm coral and yellow shapes sit on cool mint tiles, and the cream page keeps the whole thing calm and trustworthy. Keep it.
- **The type plan is clean.** A serif carries the wordmark and headline and one sans carries everything else. Sizes step clearly, and each card uses weight and colour rather than extra sizes to separate title, text and price. Titles and prices line up across all four cards, so the services compare at a glance.
- **The copy is concrete.** "A vetted crew arrives with their own supplies" and "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday" each say who does what and when. Use the three illustrations as the template for fixing the odd card.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are clickable, and whether they have hover and focus states. Finding 1 is less severe if they are, but they still need a visible cue.
- Focus indicators on the nav links and the button.
