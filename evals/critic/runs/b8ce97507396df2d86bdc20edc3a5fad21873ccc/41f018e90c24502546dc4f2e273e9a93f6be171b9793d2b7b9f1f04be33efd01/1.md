**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title, then the heavy black grid takes over. Every cell is boxed in the same thick rule, so the lines are louder than the times. It reads as a clear, plain ferry timetable for riders, but it feels heavy, like a spreadsheet. The departure times, the thing anyone came for, carry no more weight than "Morning" or "Daily".

**What it's for.** Assumed: a timetable a rider glances at to find the next ferry from North landing and which days it runs.

**Verdict.** It works. Every fact is there, labelled plainly and in the right order. What's in the way is the grid: a heavy cage around every cell makes the structure darker than the data, which slows the one task a timetable has.

**What would move it most**
1. **The grid is louder than the times.** A near-black rule about 2 px thick boxes all 32 cells: 8 horizontal rules, 5 vertical ones and an outer frame, across the whole table (x 100–1180, y 170–627). The rules are as dark as the digits and heavier than the regular-weight type. A rider running down "Leaves north" for the next boat crosses a heavy bar every 57 px, and the vertical rules break each row into separate boxes. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*, "Data tables") says table rules should show how the data relates, not trap each entry in its own guarded cell. Tufte (*Visual Display*) and Few say the structure should be lighter than the data. Fix: drop the vertical rules and the outer frame. Keep pale hairlines or a light tint between rows, plus one firmer rule under the header, so the times become the darkest marks on the page. *Convention.*
2. **The Period column repeats itself and hides its groups.** "Morning", "Midday" and "Afternoon" each appear twice in a row (y ≈ 256/313, 370/427, 484/541). Each pair is split by the same full-weight rule that divides unrelated rows. So the viewer sees seven equal boxes instead of four parts of the day, and the column says less than the times beside it already do. The only things here a rider can't predict, the "Weekdays" sailings at 12:40 and 17:10 and the "Weekends" sailing at 19:30, sit in the same flat sameness as everything else. Fix: show each period once as a group label, or drop the column. Mark period breaks with extra space rather than a rule on every row. Once the grid is lighter, give the "Runs" exceptions enough contrast that the eye catches them. Weight or a muted tint is fine as long as the word stays. *Defect (mild): the grouping the table is built on doesn't show, and the exceptions don't stand out.*

**Smaller things.**
- Columns are far wider than their content (each about 240–300 px for 5-character times), so a departure and its arrival sit about 300 px apart while rows are only 57 px apart. The eye reads down columns, but a sailing is a row. Size the columns to their content plus a steady gutter, and turn the freed width into one deliberate open area instead of a blank stretch in every cell.
- The times look set in proportional figures: in "11:00", "11:12", "17:10" and "17:22" the colon and minutes shift left of the other rows (Leaves north and Arrives quay columns). Use tabular figures so every HH:MM lines up on the colon (Bringhurst).

**What's working.**
- **The title and subtitle.** "River ferry timetable" and "North landing to market quay, spring season" tell a stranger what this is, which direction it covers and when it applies, before they read a single time. The grey subtitle steps down by tone alone, with no extra size. Keep that.
- **Plain-word column headings.** "Leaves north", "Arrives quay" and "Runs" need no decoding, and the 24-hour times in departure order can't be misread.
- **A calm type system.** One sans, about three sizes, and bold doing the work for the header row (bolder, not bigger, as *Refactoring UI* puts it). The cell text, at about 20 px, dark on white, reads easily at a glance.
