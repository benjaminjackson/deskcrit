**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a category page for an online plant shop, there to help someone with a dim room compare four suitable plants and add one to their cart.

**Verdict.** The structure, copy and action are solid. But the product pictures don't show the products, and on a page whose whole job is choosing between plants, that's the one thing in the way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show the plants they're selling.** The four tiles (y≈193–422) all use the same terracotta pot with two or three soft green leaves rearranged. None of them looks like the plant named under it:
   - The snake plant should have stiff upright blades.
   - The ZZ plant should have stems lined with small glossy leaflets.
   - The cast iron plant should have long strap-shaped leaves.
   - The Pothos card says "trails to 6 ft", yet the drawing shows two upright leaves.

   You could swap any two pictures and nobody would notice. On a listing, the picture is how a shopper tells products apart and judges what will arrive at their door. Here they have only the names. That works for people who already know these plants and fails everyone else, and those are the people a "low-light picks" page is meant to help. Fix: show each real plant. Matching product photos are best. If you keep illustrations, draw each plant's real leaf shape and growth habit, with the Pothos trailing over the pot. Keep the shared tile, framing and pot position, because that consistency is worth having. This is a defect, and common sense: no published rule covers it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Name vs price (every card).** The plant name (≈18 px semibold) and the price (≈20 px bold) are almost the same size, and the price is slightly bigger. So the row scans as "$24 $32 $38 $18" before it reads as four plants. Pick which one leads and make the gap obvious, or calm the price with a medium weight (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- **Three near-identical small sizes.** The subtitle, card specs and nav sit at about 15, 14 and 15 px. Put them on one small size.
- **"You are here" (top nav).** "Houseplants" in the nav looks just like "Pots" and "Care guides", and there's no breadcrumb above the title. A visitor arriving from search can't tell these four are a subset of a wider range, or how to get back to it. Mark the current section and add "Houseplants › Low-light" above the title (Nielsen, visibility of system status).
- **"6 in pot" / "8 in pot" (spec line above each price).** Next to a price it could mean a 6-inch pot or six plants in one pot. Write "6-inch pot".

**What's working.**
- **The subtitle.** "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window" gives shoppers a test they can check against their own room. Keep the concrete distance: it's what makes this page different from any other shop's "low light" list.
- **One grid.** The logo, title and first card share one left edge at x≈48, and "Cart (0)" lines up with the last card's right edge. Names, specs, prices and buttons sit at the same height in all four cards, so prices compare in one straight sweep.
- **Colour kept for the action.** Teal is the only strong colour outside the pictures, and it's clearly bluer than the leaf green. Each "Add to cart" button is the obvious action without shouting, and it's big enough to hit easily (about 246×46).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Add to cart" gives feedback when pressed (the cart count updating, or a confirmation).
- Whether the picture or name links to a product page. Neither looks clickable.
- Focus indicators on the nav and buttons.
- How the four-column row reflows on narrow screens.
