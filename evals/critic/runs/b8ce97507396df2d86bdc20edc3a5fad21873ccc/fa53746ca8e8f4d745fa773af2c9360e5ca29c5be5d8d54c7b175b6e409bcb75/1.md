**First read.** The eye goes first to the big white headline on the dark left, then drops to the subline. Before it reaches the button it gets pulled right into the bright, busy field of leaves, where there is nothing to read. The outlined button comes a distant third. It reads as a local plant nursery's homepage and feels green and calm on the left, noisy on the right. It also looks generic, because nothing in the picture is an actual plant.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage hero for a small nursery that specialises in ferns and shade plants, and its job is to tell gardeners what's sold here and send them on to browse the stock or plan a visit.

**Verdict.** The words do their job, but the rest of the hero doesn't help them. The one picture shows no plants, its brightest, loudest part sits where there's nothing to read or do, and the only call to action has the weakest button style there is. A visitor learns what the nursery sells only by reading. Nothing shows it, and nothing makes them want to act.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture shows no plants.** The whole hero (full width, below the header) is scattered flat green ovals. There's no fern frond, no shade, no planted corner, no hill and no greenhouse. For a nursery, the picture is the main evidence of what you'll take home. Here it only says "something green" and could sit behind any eco brand, so the specific offer in the headline ("Ferns and shade plants for every corner") has to be taken on trust. Fix: use one real image that shows what the headline names, such as a photo of the nursery's own ferns or a shaded corner planted with them, with one clear plant to look at. If it has to stay an illustration, draw recognisable fern fronds with one dominant form. *Defect* (common sense, since no approved source covers it): this picture does nothing to sell.
2. **The one call to action looks like a caption, not a button.** "Browse the greenhouse" is a ghost button, just a thin white outline with no fill. Outline styling usually marks a secondary action, so the page's only path forward has the least weight of anything you can click. Next to the 64 px headline and the bright leaves, it barely registers. Fix: give it a solid fill, such as white with dark green text or one saturated leaf green, so it's clearly the primary action. Save outline buttons for a later second action. *Convention* (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*, on primary versus secondary styling).
3. **The loudest part of the screen is empty decoration.** The right half (about x 700–1280) fills with lime and yellow-green that gets brighter and warmer toward the right edge. These are the most saturated, warmest colours on the page, and they sit farthest from the headline and the button. The eye leaves the text, drifts into that empty area and has to come back to find what to do. The mood is off as well: the headline promises shade, which is cool, dim and calm, but the leaves read as bright sun and confetti scattered at every angle. Fix: tone the whole hero down as well as the left half (a darker overlay all the way across, no sunlit lime), or put something worth looking at on the right, which is where the plant photo from point 1 belongs. If you want one vivid warm green, put it on the button. *Convention* (Itten, *The Art of Color*: contrast of saturation and warm-cool contrast; *Refactoring UI*).

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line breaks: "Ferns and shade / plants for every / corner" splits "shade plants" across two lines and leaves "corner" alone on the last one. Break by sense instead: "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner" (Bringhurst, §2.4.2, on a short word alone on a last line; the phrase-break part is common sense).
- Left edges: the logo starts at x≈64 and the headline, subline and button at x≈96. A 32 px offset looks accidental. Put the hero on the header's 64 px line, or indent it by a clearly deliberate step such as a full grid column (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).
- "Browse the greenhouse" can mean an online catalogue or a walk round in person, and the header offers both "Plants" and "Visit". Name the destination: "Shop ferns and shade plants" or "Plan your visit".
- The pitch is local and timed ("Grown on the hill", "this weekend"), but the first screen gives no location or opening hours. Add a short line near the button or in the header (taste).
- There's no search in the header, and plant buyers often arrive looking for one plant by name. Add a search field or icon at the top right (Nielsen, heuristic 7).

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific where it matters. "Ferns and shade plants" names a niche, "ready to plant this weekend" gives a reason and a time frame, and "Browse the greenhouse" talks in the nursery's own terms. Keep these words when the picture changes.
- The hierarchy is clean. The headline is roughly three times the size of anything else, on the darkest part of the frame, and each lower level also differs in weight or colour, not just size. Keep a dark, quiet area behind the text whatever image goes in.
- The palette is tight: one green family plus white, with the dark green repeated in the wordmark, so the page feels like one brand. The header (wordmark left, four plain-word links right) needs no thought.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- Faint vertical banding in the overlay gradient around x 660–780. It may be a compression artefact, so check it in a live browser on a large display.
