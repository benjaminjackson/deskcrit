**First read.** The eye lands on the big near-black headline, "Plan the whole trip in one place". It drops to the grey subhead, then reaches two buttons that say the same thing: "Get started" and "Start now". The right half of the screen and the bottom third are empty cream. It reads as the landing page for a group trip-planning app: calm and tidy, but bare enough that it looks unfinished, as if the hero picture never loaded.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing landing page, meant to get people planning a group trip to sign up.

**Verdict.** The message is clear and the type is well handled, but the page doesn't yet do its job. The one decision it asks for is muddled by two buttons that mean the same thing. And with half the screen empty, the "one place" the headline promises is never shown, so the page looks broken rather than calm.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two buttons, one meaning.** "Get started" (filled, about x 80–229, y 444–496) and "Start now" (outlined, beside it) are synonyms. The styling is right: one filled main button and one outlined second button. But an outlined button next to a filled one tells the viewer "here is your other option", so they stop to work out how "Start now" differs. It doesn't. That pause comes at the only moment the page asks them to act. Meanwhile, a visitor who isn't ready to sign up gets nothing to do, even though "How it works" sits right there in the nav. This is the textbook case of redundant controls, and duplicates weaken the main action (Nielsen, heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Fix: keep the outlined style, but give that button a different, lower-commitment job, such as "See how it works" or "See a sample trip". Otherwise, remove it. **Defect.**
2. **The right half is empty, so the page tips over and the promise goes unshown.** Everything sits in the upper-left block (about x 80–680, y 190–495). The right side (about x 700–1200, from the nav down) and the band below the buttons (y 500–800) are empty. The page leans hard to the left. Nothing explains the space, so it reads as missing content rather than a chosen rest area, and that costs trust just where the page needs a click. A heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*). It also costs the argument: the headline promises flights, stays and day plans in one place, and the viewer has to take that on trust instead of seeing it. Fix: put something on the right that proves the headline, such as a product shot of a shared itinerary with flights, a stay and day plans together, plus signs of the group's input (avatars, votes). Align it to the 1200 px right margin. If you want an all-type hero instead, enlarge the headline so it spans the measure and lower the block, so the empty space looks chosen. If a hero picture is meant to be there and just failed to load in this capture, the fix is making sure it loads. **Defect.**
3. **The headline and the ask could be anyone's.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" fits every travel planner. What sets Wayfold apart is the group: "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads". That line is good because it puts the product next to the tool the viewer already uses, the group chat. But it's buried in the grey subhead. "Get started" doesn't say what the viewer will actually do. Fix: bring the group-agreement angle into the headline, and name the action on the button, for example "Start a group trip". **Taste.**

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line break: "Plan the whole trip in / one place" leaves "in" hanging and drops the payoff onto a short second line. Break it as "Plan the whole trip / in one place", which also evens out the two lines. This is common sense, not a published rule.

**What's working.**
- **A clear type scale.** The headline is about 60 px, heavy and near-black. Everything else is around 15–20 px, so the eye has one obvious place to start. The subhead, buttons and nav are close in size, but weight, colour and the button shapes keep them clearly apart. Keep that gap when you revise.
- **One accent, used sparingly.** The deep teal appears only in the wordmark and the buttons, on a warm cream ground (not pure white), so colour points straight at the brand and the action. Keep teal off headings and decoration.
- **A steady frame and grouping.** The logo, headline, subhead and buttons all sit on one left edge at x≈80, and the nav ends at x≈1200. The tight gaps inside the hero (about 30 px, then 45 px) against the 150 px gap below the nav make the hero read as one unit. The subhead's line length, about 64 characters, is comfortable.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is intended or a hero picture that didn't load in this capture. Check the live page.
- Focus states on the buttons, especially on the filled dark-green one, where a green focus ring would barely show.
