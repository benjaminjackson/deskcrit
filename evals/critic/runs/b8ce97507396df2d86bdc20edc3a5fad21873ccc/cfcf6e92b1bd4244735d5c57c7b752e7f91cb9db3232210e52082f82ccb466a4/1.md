**First read.** The eye lands on the huge four-line headline, drops to the short paragraph, and then has nowhere to go. It reads as the top of a ferry timetable page, but no times appear. It feels calm and plain, and with the empty lower right it also feels unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the header of a web page for a seasonal ferry timetable, and its readers are passengers who want to know when they can cross.

**Verdict.** The type is well made, but the page doesn't do its job. It calls itself a timetable and gives the reader no time to catch. Missing sailing times are the single biggest problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page promises a timetable and shows no times.** The eyebrow "Spring timetable" (top left) and the headline both say a timetable is here. The paragraph gives only a frequency ("every forty minutes") and a crossing time ("about twelve minutes"). Nothing on the screen answers what a rider came to ask: when is the first boat, and when is the last one back? "By daylight" is the only hint about service hours. It moves by more than an hour between spring and early summer, so a rider can't plan around it, and it can be read two ways. It might mean sailings run from sunrise to sunset, or only that a daylight timetable exists somewhere on the page. "Spring through early summer" has no dates either. Meanwhile, about 200 px of white below the paragraph and the empty right third of the screen hold nothing that leads to a table. Under WCAG 2.4.6 a heading must describe its content, and this one names content that isn't there. Hours and dates also matter beyond the rule: riders act on them and later hold the operator to them. **Fix:** put the times on the first screen. At minimum, give the first and last departure from each landing and the dates of the spring service, for example "First boat 7:00 from North Landing · last return 19:40 from Market Quay · 1 March – 30 June". Change "by daylight" to clock times. Then start the full table directly under the paragraph, or rename the eyebrow to match what this screen actually holds. If the table really does start just below this screen, the clock-time fix still applies. **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line breaks: "early / summer" is split across lines 3 and 4, so line 3 stops mid-phrase and "summer" sits alone. Break by meaning instead ("Quayside ferry crossings / by daylight, / spring through / early summer"), or change the width so "early summer" stays together. This is common sense, not a sourced rule. **Taste.**
- Paragraph ends on "weather." alone on its last line. Adjust the measure slightly, or join "calm weather" with a non-breaking space, so the last line carries at least two words (Bringhurst). **Convention.**

**What's working.**
- **A clear order of reading.** The headline is about 80 px and heavy, the body about 20 px and regular, so you know where to start. The small grey bold eyebrow stays apart from the near-black body through weight and colour rather than a third size. Keep that "bolder, not bigger" approach (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
- **One left edge and spacing that groups things.** The eyebrow, headline and body all start at x 120. The gap from the eyebrow to the headline is about 30 px and from the headline to the body about 50 px, so the label clearly belongs to the headline and the page looks ordered without rules or boxes.
- **A comfortable paragraph.** Lines run about 65 characters with roughly 1.5 line spacing. That is easy reading, and the right register for a utility notice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether a timetable follows below the fold. Check the full page. If nothing follows, the empty lower area is missing content, not breathing room.
