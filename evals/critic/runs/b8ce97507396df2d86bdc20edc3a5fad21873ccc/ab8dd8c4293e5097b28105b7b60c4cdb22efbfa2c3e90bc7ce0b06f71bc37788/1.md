**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, drops to "Create your link", then slides across the four blue-outlined cards. After the headline nothing pulls harder than anything else, because almost everything is the same blue. The right half of the hero is empty, and the customer names at the bottom read as faint filler. It feels clean, safe and templated, like any scheduling tool's homepage.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of the homepage for a booking-link product, meant to get small service businesses (clinics, tutors, coaches) to sign up.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and easy to read, but it doesn't sell yet. One blue covers about 25 elements, so the main action doesn't stand out and colour no longer tells people what they can click. The page also never shows the booking page it's selling, and its headline could belong to any competitor.

**What would move it most**
1. **The accent blue is on everything, so the button and the links stop standing out.** The same saturated blue is used for the nav links, both buttons, the two-line headline, the 2 px outlines of all four cards, the four icons, the four card titles, the four solid badges, the four "Learn more" links and the rule at y≈740.
   - **What the viewer gets:** "Create your link" is one blue thing among many, and the four outlined cards make a heavy blue band that pulls the eye down and away from it. Blue also stops meaning "clickable". A blue title inside a blue box looks like a card you can click, and the solid badges look like small buttons, but the only real target in each card is the underlined "Learn more". People will click the title or the badge and get nothing.
   - **Why:** keep the accent for the main action and things you can interact with (Apple HIG, Color; Material Design 3, color roles). Things that can't be used shouldn't look usable (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*).
   - **Fix:** keep the blue for the buttons and links. Set the headline (or at least the card titles) in the dark body-text colour. Give the cards a light grey hairline border or none, or make each whole card the link. Turn the icons grey. Make the badges pale tinted chips that read as status labels.
   - **Kind:** defect.
2. **The right half of the hero is empty, so the product is never shown.** All the weight sits at x 64–645. The area at roughly x 660–1216, y 90–400 is blank white, the largest open space on the page, and it does no work.
   - **What the viewer gets:** the page leans left. The blank reads as a picture that failed to load, not as space someone chose. The viewer never sees what a guest sees when they open a Slotwise link, which is the thing being sold.
   - **Why:** a heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), and empty space should be shaped on purpose, not left over (Ruder).
   - **Fix:** put the guest's booking page there, with a week of open slots and one being picked. That gives the layout its counterweight and lets the viewer judge the product. If a hero image exists and didn't load in this capture, the layout part doesn't apply, but check it in a live browser.
   - **Kind:** defect.
3. **The headline could sit on any scheduling product's page.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" names the category, not a difference, and "fill your calendar" is a promise the viewer has to take on trust.
   - **What the viewer gets:** someone already using a booking tool has no reason to switch, and someone still booking by email isn't shown what changes for them.
   - **Fix:** set the product against what the viewer does today, for example ending the back-and-forth emails or the no-shows. Or say something only this product does for clinics and tutors. The subhead and "Create your link" are clear and exact, so keep them.
   - **Kind:** taste.

**Smaller things.**
- **First card breaks the row.** "Shared calendars" wraps to two lines and its "New" badge jumps to the right edge, so its body text and "Learn more" sit about 30 px lower (y≈672 vs 642) than in the other three cards. That leaves uneven empty space at the card bottoms. Keep every title on one line (shorten it or put the badge in the same place in every card) and line up the rows across all four cards.
- **Badges mix two kinds of information.** "New" and "Beta" are statuses, while "Auto" and "Email" describe how a feature works but look exactly like them, so "Beta" loses its warning. Keep badges for status only and move "automatic" and "by email" into the card text.
- **Customer names have no label.** The strip at y≈770 is five grey names with no heading or logos, so it doesn't read as proof. Add a line like "Used by clinics, tutors and coaches", or show the logos.
- **Icons don't match.** The calendar is a thin outline, while the clock, envelope and form are solid fills. The "Intake forms" glyph also looks like a page layout, not a form. Use one family in one style, and pick a glyph that shows a form with fields.
- **Headline breaks badly.** It breaks "fill your / calendar", leaving one word alone on line 2. Break at the phrase ("Booking links that / fill your calendar") or widen the box.
- **No "Log in" in the header.** Returning customers have to hunt for it, or they click "Start free". Add a quiet "Log in" link left of "Start free".
- **Nav looks slightly off-centre.** The nav group looks about 12 px left of the page's centre line (x 640), so it reads as a near miss. Measure it, then centre it exactly or move it clearly away from centre.

**What's working.**
- **The hero copy and buttons:** headline, subhead and "Create your link" say what this is and what to do in one glance. The solid button next to the underlined "See a live example" gives a clear first and second action, both with exact labels.
- **The grid:** the logo, headline, first card, rule and first customer name share the left edge at x 64. "Start free" ends where the cards end. The four cards have equal gutters. The page looks built, not placed by eye.
- **One clear type hierarchy:** the headline is more than twice the size of anything else, and card titles differ from card text in weight and colour as well as size, so the page reads in order and skims fast.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The empty right half of the hero:** whether an image there failed to load. Check it in a live browser.
- **Focus indicators:** with this much blue, a blue focus ring may disappear. Tab through the page to check.
- **Narrower widths:** whether the card title wrap spreads to the other cards.
