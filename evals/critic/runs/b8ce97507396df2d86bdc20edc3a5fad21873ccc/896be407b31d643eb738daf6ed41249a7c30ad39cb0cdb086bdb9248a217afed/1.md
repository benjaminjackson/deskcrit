**First read.** The eye lands on "Book a workshop seat" and runs down a narrow column of bold labels and empty boxes. Each label sits right under the box above it and well above its own box, so the boxes seem to belong to the wrong labels. Then the column stops in blank white space with nothing to press. It reads as a plain booking form that hasn't been finished.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web form where someone books one or more seats at a workshop.

**Verdict.** As shown, the form can't do its job. There is no button, so nobody can finish a booking. The spacing also pairs each label with the wrong field, so people who do fill it in will put answers in the wrong boxes.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no button to submit the booking.** After the Notes field (ends at y≈547) there are about 250 px of white space and no button. The user fills in four fields and has no way to book, which is the one thing the heading promises. Add one clearly primary button right under the last field, lined up with the fields' left edge, with a label that names the result, such as "Book seat" or "Reserve seats", not "Submit". If the button exists but fell outside the capture or didn't render, check the live page. As shown, it's missing. *Defect.*
2. **Each label sits closer to the wrong field.** "Preferred date" (y≈241) is only about 8–15 px below the bottom of the Workshop-name box (y≈226) but about 48 px above its own box (y≈289). "Number of seats" and "Notes for the host" repeat the pattern, and "Workshop name" floats alone above the first box. The eye groups by nearness (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, proximity), so it reads the first box as "Preferred date", the second as "Number of seats", and so on, one field off all the way down. Someone filling it in quickly will type the date into the name field. Swap the two gaps: put about 4–8 px between a label and its own box, and clearly more, about 24–32 px, between one label-and-box pair and the next label. *Defect.*
3. **Free-text boxes for answers that have a fixed set of valid values.** "Workshop name" (y≈182–226) is a blank box, so the user has to remember and spell the workshop's exact name (Nielsen, recognition rather than recall). "Preferred date" and "Number of seats" look the same as the other boxes: no calendar, no stepper, no format hint. Nothing stops people typing "next Tue" or "two" (Nielsen, error prevention). The host ends up with bookings that don't match any workshop or date. Use a list of workshops, or fill in the name when the user arrives from a workshop's page. Use a date picker that offers only the dates actually available, and a number field limited to the seats left, defaulting to 1. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Notes for the host" (y≈503–547) is a one-line box, which tells people to write a few words. Make it a textarea about 3–4 lines tall.
- No field says whether it's required. Mark Notes "(optional)" rather than starring the other three.
- Nothing shows the workshop's date, price or seats left, which is what someone needs to decide. Show them beside or above the form. This one is taste, but common sense.
- The title and the labels differ only in size: both bold, both near-black (Dair, *Design with Type*). Make the labels medium or regular weight, or a slightly softer tone, so the title stands out and the column below is less uniformly heavy.
- All the content sits in a 480 px column in the top-left. The right ~700 px and the bottom ~250 px are empty, so the page looks like something failed to load. Either frame the column (put it in a panel or centre it), or use the right side for a booking summary or workshop details. This one is taste.

**What's working.**
- The heading "Book a workshop seat" is a verb plus an object, so a stranger knows at once what the page does. Keep that.
- There is one crisp left edge at x≈96 for the heading, labels and fields. The fields share one width, border and radius, and the steps between them are even. Once the gaps are swapped, the column will read cleanly.
- The type is lean (one family, two sizes with a big step between them) and the palette is neutral, near-black and slate on white. That suits a utility form. The fields are a generous 480×44 px with outlines you can see.
