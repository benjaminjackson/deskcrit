**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif headline, "Shipments peak each third quarter", drops to the tallest bar (705, Q3 2026), then scans the even row of blue bars from left to right. It reads at once as a slide from a board deck. It feels calm, sober and well made. Nothing on the bars says that the last one is a forecast, so it reads as a real result.

**What it's for.** My assumption: one slide in an October board deck that shows directors the quarterly pattern in shipments to retailers, ending with the Q4 forecast.

**Verdict.** This is a clean, well-built slide that mostly does its job. The biggest problem is that the Q4 2026 forecast is drawn exactly like the seven real quarters, so a board will remember 560 as a fact. Second, the headline leads with seasonality, which the board likely knows, rather than the year-on-year growth the chart shows.

**What would move it most**
1. **The forecast bar looks like an actual result.** The Q4 2026 bar (far right, labelled 560) is the same solid blue as the others, and its label is the same bold black. The only warning is in small grey text: the subtitle's "(Q4 2026 is the forecast)" and the source line at the foot. A director scanning the chart compares 560 with Q4 2025's 480, takes away about 17% growth as delivered, and later holds management to it. In an October meeting, Q4 has barely started, so this is the least certain number on the page, yet it looks as solid as the rest. Projections should look different from observed data and show their uncertainty (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). Fix: make that bar different at a glance with a lighter tint, an outline-only bar or hatching. Label it at the bar ("560 forecast" or "Q4 2026 (f)"). If the September forecast has a range, show it as a low–high span. *Defect.*
2. **The headline states the pattern the board already knows and leaves out the news.** "Shipments peak each third quarter" is true, but it rests on only two Q3s, and seasonality is rarely news to the people who run the business. The chart holds a bigger finding that nobody spells out: every 2026 quarter beats the same quarter of 2025. Q1 is up about 11% (455 vs 410), Q2 about 13% (590 vs 520) and Q3 about 16% (705 vs 610). The Q4 forecast is 560 vs 480. To see this, the viewer has to compare bars four positions apart, and most won't. On a board slide the headline is often the only line people remember. Fix: end the headline on what the numbers mean for the board, for example "Shipments up year on year in every quarter; the Q3 peak grew 16%". If seasonality is the point, tie it to a decision, such as stock or staffing ahead of Q3. *Taste*, but it decides what the room takes away.

**Smaller things.**
- **Too much setup above the chart.** The subtitle, the chart title "Units shipped by quarter" and the unit line "Thousands of units" make three lines before the data. The chart title repeats the subtitle, and the subtitle says "Q4 2026" twice. Merge them into one line, for example "Units shipped to retailers, thousands, Q1 2025–Q4 2026 (Q4 forecast)".
- **The chart is off the page grid.** All the text and the footer rule run from x≈80 to x≈1200. The plot runs from about x≈150 to x≈1140, so its right end stops just short of the rule beneath it and looks like a near-miss (Müller-Brockmann). Either run the plot to the 1200 edge, or inset it by the same margin on both sides.
- **Space in the wrong place.** Content ends at the source line (about y≈680), leaving an empty band of about 120 px at the bottom against a top margin of about 30 px. Meanwhile the x-axis labels sit only about 20 px above the footer rule. Give that band to the chart: make the plot taller and anchor the footer to the bottom margin, so the seasonal swing has more height and the slide stops riding high.

**What's working.**
- **A clear order of reading.** The headline is the only serif and the largest, darkest text. Below it, weight and grey separate the levels rather than more sizes: the bold chart title, the grey units and axis labels, and the bold data labels. Keep this small type plan.
- **A quiet chart.** The gridlines are faint, there is no frame, and every bar carries its exact value just above it. The blue bars are the only strong colour on the slide, so the shape of the data reads instantly and no one has to read values off the axis.
- **The slide knows its context.** The brand at top left, "prepared for the October board meeting" at top right, and the source and page number in the footer place it in the deck without competing with the content.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The grey support text (the header line at top right, "Thousands of units", the axis labels and the source line) looks like mid-grey at about 12–14 px. It probably passes on screen, but measure its contrast if the deck will be projected.
