**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline, "Travel lighter with the Wayfarer pack", drops to the grey line under it, and stops on the dark "Shop the pack" button. It reads as the top of a landing page for a new travel backpack. It feels clean and sure of itself, but empty: everything sits in the top-left, and the right side and bottom third of the frame are blank white with no bag anywhere.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: the hero of a shop's product page, meant to get shoppers to click through and buy the pack.

**Verdict.** The text does its job well: one message, one clear main action, and concrete copy. The page fails as a product hero because it asks people to buy a bag they never see, and the gap where the bag should be makes the whole frame look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no product, and the layout tips because of it.** All the content sits in one block at about x 120–850, y 120–495. The right ~430 px and the bottom ~305 px of the 1280×800 frame hold nothing.
   - **What the viewer meets:** the copy makes three physical claims: 22 litres, fits under an airline seat, opens flat like a suitcase. The viewer has to take all three on trust. The empty space has no shape or purpose, so it reads as a split hero with the picture missing, and the page looks unfinished and heavy at the top-left.
   - **Why:** weight needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Large empty areas with no clear reason are a fault, as opposed to white space that is shaped on purpose (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).
   - **Fix:** put a large product shot on the right, with its top and bottom lined up with the text block. Best is a shot that proves one claim, such as the pack opened flat or sliding under a seat. Then the headline makes the promise and the picture proves it.
   - If the page really is meant to be text-only, move the block down and make the type bigger, so the white space looks chosen.
   - If an image was supposed to load there and didn't in this capture, this finding goes away. Check the live page.
   - **Defect.**
2. **There's no shop around the hero.** Above "New for spring" there are about 120 px of white, with no logo, navigation, search or cart.
   - **What the viewer meets:** they can't tell whose shop this is, can't browse other products, and can't reach their cart. Anyone not ready to buy right now has nowhere to go but the two hero links.
   - **Why:** shoppers expect the logo at the top-left and the cart at the top-right (Nielsen Norman Group, consistency and standards).
   - **Fix:** add a standard top bar.
   - If this is a cropped section and not the full page, ignore this.
   - **Convention.**

**Smaller things.**
- **Price near the button:** "Shop the pack" asks people to commit before they know the cost. Put the price beside or inside the button ("Shop the pack – £X"). Taste, on common sense.
- **Headline line break:** it breaks as "Travel lighter with the / Wayfarer pack", which leaves "the" hanging away from the name it introduces. Break at the phrase instead, "Travel lighter / with the Wayfarer pack", and check the break at each width. Taste.

**What's working.**
- **The order of the type is clear.** A very heavy headline of about 72–80 px sits over a grey body of about 20 px, with small bold labels. Each size has one job, and the levels differ by weight and colour, not by sizes that are nearly the same. Everything hangs from one left edge at x≈120, so the eye drops straight down the block.
- **There's one main action.** "Shop the pack" is the only filled button. "Compare sizes" is clearly second: an underlined bold link with a label that says exactly where it goes.
- **The copy is concrete.** "22 litre", "under an airline seat", "opens flat like a suitcase" and "less than a kilo" are facts from the traveller's own world, not filler. Keep that line when you add the picture.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Missing image:** whether the empty right side should hold an image that didn't load (lazy-loaded or animated in). Check in a live browser. This decides finding 1.
- **Focus states:** whether the button and link show a visible focus ring. They are the only two actions on the page.
