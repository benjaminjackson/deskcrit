**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers" at top left, then drops through three short paragraphs. It reads as a plain information note for people using a river ferry. It feels calm and tidy. Then you notice it never says when the boat actually runs.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web page or posted notice that tells ferry passengers when the boat runs, how bicycles board, and what changes in winter.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is sound. The note fails at its main job because it leaves out every time a passenger would plan around. The fix is in the words, not the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times and deadlines are vague.** This is in the body text, paragraphs one and three:
   - "Between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last departure.
   - "In winter" gives no months.
   - "The last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" gives no clock time, and dusk moves by more than an hour across a winter.
   - "The quay" is most likely the market quay, since the other end is called a "landing". But the line doesn't say so, and a reader at the north landing could take it to mean their own end.

   These are exactly the lines a passenger acts on and holds the operator to. Someone reading this still can't tell whether they'll catch the 7 a.m. boat or get stranded on a December afternoon.

   Fix: give the first and last departure times from each landing, name the winter months, and give the last winter departure as a time with its landing ("last boat leaves the market quay at 15:40"). This is a **defect** (common sense: a timetable note that can't be planned around doesn't work as a timetable note).

**Smaller things.**
- **Lopsided layout.** All the content sits in the top-left quarter (x≈120–750, y≈55–385). The right half and bottom half are leftover white space, so the page looks tipped and a viewer may wonder whether a sidebar or map failed to load. Either centre the reading column, or give the right side a job, such as the timetable itself or a small map of the two landings. This is **taste**: a left-anchored reading column is a common web pattern and costs little here.

**What's working.**
- **The reading setting is comfortable.** Body text looks about 19 px with roughly 1.5 line spacing, and lines run about 60–68 characters, inside Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Each paragraph reads in one easy pass. Keep the column width.
- **The heading clearly leads.** Two type levels sit far apart: a bold heading at about 36 px over regular body at about 20 px. The eye has one obvious place to start and nothing competes with it.
- **One topic per paragraph.** Schedule, bicycles and winter each get their own short block, marked by space only, on one shared left edge. A passenger can scan straight to the part they need.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty right side shows no broken-image icon or placeholder. If a map or photo is meant to sit there, check in a live browser that it loads.
