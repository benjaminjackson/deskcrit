**First read.** The eye lands on the big black "Garden show gallery" title, drops into the large red Rose arch tile, then moves through the right column and along the bottom row. It reads as a photo gallery from a garden show with one featured exhibit. But every picture is a flat colour block with a pale half-circle, so it feels like a wireframe rather than a garden. It's tidy and calm, with nothing to linger on.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where visitors browse photos of a garden show's exhibits, with one exhibit featured.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound but the page doesn't yet do its job: a gallery with no pictures shows the visitor nothing. Real photographs are the one thing that would change it. After that, the spacing around the captions needs tightening so each label clearly belongs to its own picture.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show anything.** All six tiles are one solid colour with the same pale half-circle cut off at the bottom: Rose arch (about 60–829 × 130–437 px), Herb bed, Pond edge, Bean rows, Glasshouse and Orchard path. The captions promise "Climbing roses over the east gate" and "Reeds and water lilies", but the visitor sees no roses, arch, gate, pond or trees. They have to take every caption on trust, and nothing makes them want to see the show. On a gallery page the pictures are the content, so this is the job failing, not a detail.
   - **Knock-on problems:** The repeated circle carries no information, since only the background colour tells one tile from another. The six vivid hues fill about half the canvas at equal strength, so the page reads as a set of paint chips.
   - **Fix:** Put a real photograph of each named exhibit in each slot. Give the Rose arch tile the strongest image, because its size already makes it the first stop. The colour and circle problems go away with the placeholders.
   - **What would make it fine:** If the photos just failed to load in this capture, the page may be fine. Check the live page.
   - **Kind:** Defect. It rests on common sense, not a cited rule: a gallery whose images show nothing fails at its main job.
2. **The captions sit as far from their own picture as from the next one.** "Rose arch" and its line start about 15 px below the red image and end about 15 px above the Bean rows / Glasshouse row. It's the second-largest text on the page, so for a moment it reads as a heading over the bottom row rather than the hero's caption. The right column has the same problem on a smaller scale: "Herb bed" sits about 9 px under its picture and about 15 px above Pond edge, so tiles and captions run together as one even stack.
   - **Fix:** Keep each caption tight under its picture, and make the gap between one tile-plus-caption and the next clearly larger, roughly double. For the hero, open more space above the bottom row (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on grouping by location).
   - **Kind:** Defect, mainly at the hero.

**Smaller things.**
- **Colour, if the blocks are final art:** six saturated hues of equal strength compete with each other. Keep the red at full strength for the hero and turn the other tiles into muted tints of one or two related hues (Itten; Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
- **The title is generic:** "Garden show gallery" doesn't say which show or which year. Naming it would tell visitors whose garden this is. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- **A clear focal point.** The Rose arch tile has about four times the area of the others, the only warm colour, and its own large heading, so visitors know where to start. The five small tiles are all the same size and read as one even set to browse after it.
- **A strict grid.** Three equal columns of about 377 px with one 14 px gutter, the hero spanning exactly two of them, and every edge on the same column lines. The page looks built, not placed by eye.
- **A simple type plan with specific copy.** One sans family. Small labels and their descriptions are separated by weight and grey, not by sizes a pixel apart. Every label names its exhibit plainly ("Pond edge") and adds a concrete detail ("Tomatoes in late July.").

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether real photos are meant to load into these tiles. Check the live page with images allowed and on a slow network.
- Whether the hero caption and the right-column stack still line up when the screen width changes.
