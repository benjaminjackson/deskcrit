**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "New sign-ups by channel" heading, drops to the tall Organic search bar (1,840), then runs down the bars from left to right. The "What changed this quarter" note on the right is found last. It reads as an internal growth report inside a product dashboard, and it feels calm, plain and trustworthy.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it shows a growth or marketing team where Q3 sign-ups came from, and what will change because of it.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, honest chart that mostly does its job. The one thing in the way: the page's message is a change (organic search up since Q2, the events budget moving to referrals), but the headline and the chart only show a Q3 ranking. Anyone who skims sees the distribution and misses the point.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart and headline don't show what changed, though that is the story.** The headline "New sign-ups by channel" (top left) and its subtitle only label what the bars already show. The real message is in the smaller side note (x≈876–1200): organic search went "up from a quarter in Q2", and podcast ads plus events brought fewer than 700 accounts, so their budget moves. The chart shows Q3 only. The viewer can't see the rise they're told about, has to take "up from a quarter" on trust, and can't tell whether Referral or Paid social also moved. Few lists a headline number with no previous period beside it as a dashboard pitfall. Fix it on two fronts:
   - Put Q2 in the chart, for example as a pale grey bar or tick beside each Q3 bar, or a small "+9 pts vs Q2" under each value.
   - Make the headline carry the takeaway, such as "Organic search now brings a third of sign-ups". You could also highlight the two bars the note acts on.

   Then the headline, chart and note make one point instead of a label sitting over data. *Convention* for the missing comparison. *Taste* for the takeaway headline: descriptive titles are normal on dashboards, but this page clearly has a message to land.

**Smaller things.**
- The "What changed this quarter" heading (y≈174) lines up with nothing. It sits about 20 px above the chart's top gridline and "2,000" label (y≈195), so the note looks slightly loose next to the plot. Set its top level with the top gridline. The y-axis labels also start about 17 px inside the 48 px margin that the logo, title and source share. Hang "2,000" on that margin or inset the plot by a clearly deliberate amount (Müller-Brockmann).
- No control changes the period. If this is the live Growth page people come back to, rather than a fixed quarterly report, put a period selector with a "compare to previous" option next to the title. That gives users the Q2 view the note keeps pointing to (Nielsen, heuristic 7).

**What's working.**
- **Honest, directly labelled chart.** It starts at zero with even 500 ticks. The bars are sorted descending, each carries its exact value, and there's no legend. One muted blue sits on pale gridlines. The bars add up to exactly the 5,400 in the source line, and the note's figures check out: 1,840/5,400 ≈ a third, and 410 + 260 = 670, under 700. That consistency builds trust.
- **A small type scale where weight and grey do the work.** There are about three sizes plus a caption size. The note heading is set apart by bold, not size, and the subtitle and source by grey. Every level has an obvious job (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- **The side note is written to be acted on.** Two short paragraphs, each a claim with a number, ending on a decision ("The events budget moves to referrals in Q4"). Keep that voice when the headline takes on the takeaway.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the nav links have a visible focus style, and how big their clickable areas are. Check both by tabbing through the live page.
