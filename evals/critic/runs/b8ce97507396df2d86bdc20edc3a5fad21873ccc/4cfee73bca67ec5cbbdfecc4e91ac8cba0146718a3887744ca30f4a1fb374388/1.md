**First read.** Your eye lands on "Library opening hours" at the top left and then runs down one column of nine lines, all spaced the same. The bold branch names mark where each branch starts, but nothing groups them, so it reads as one long list rather than three schedules. Once you actually read the lines, the times turn out to be spelled out in words, and the mobile library has no times at all. It feels calm and plain, but it's slower to use than a page this short should be.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a library web page where residents check quickly when, and for the mobile library where, each branch is open.

**Verdict.** It doesn't fully do its job. The copy leaves out or blurs the one thing the page exists to give, the times, and the even spacing hides the three groups that should organise it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hours aren't finished, and some can be read two ways.** This applies to every line from y≈188 to 503.
   - **Mobile library has no times.** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" say where the van goes but not when. Nobody can plan to catch it from this page, and "school gates" suggests a short window that's easy to miss.
   - **Branch hours are words with no am or pm.** The four lines are "nine to eight", "ten to four", "ten to six" and "ten to two". People checking hours scan for figures, so words force them to read every sentence. "Ten to two" and "ten to six" can also be read as clock times (1:50, 5:50) rather than ranges.
   - On a page about times, a line that can be read two ways sends someone to a locked door. That makes this a **defect**; it's common sense rather than a published rule.
   - **Fix:** use figures with a clear range in one format throughout, such as "9 am – 8 pm" with a real en dash. Give each mobile stop its hours, for example "Wednesday, 10 am – 1 pm, market square".
2. **The spacing doesn't group the branches.** Every line sits about 45 px below the one before it, from "Main branch" down to "Thursday at the school gates".
   - Each branch name is no closer to its own hours than to the previous branch's last line, so it floats between the two groups.
   - Only bold weight marks the edges. Someone looking for "Harbour on Saturday" has to read the labels to find the boundaries, because the space doesn't show them.
   - When the gaps are equal everywhere, the groups disappear (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on proximity). This is a **defect**.
   - **Fix:** pull each branch's lines tight under its name, at about normal line spacing. Put a clearly larger gap, roughly double, above "Harbour branch" and "Mobile library".

**Smaller things.**
- Closed days are only implied. Harbour starts on Tuesday, so a Monday visitor has to work out that it's shut. Add "Closed Sunday and Monday" under Harbour and "Closed Sunday" under Main.

**What's working.**
- **Branch names stand out by weight, not by another size.** They're bold at body size, so there are only three clear levels: title, branch, line. Keep that rather than making the branch names slightly larger (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*: bolder, not bigger).
- **One clean left edge.** Title, branch names and lines all start at x≈96, with no near-misses. That suits a short reference list.
- **Near-black on white, about 18 px body, no colour.** It's easy to read, and the structure still holds in greyscale.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The content fills only the top-left third of the 1280×800 frame. Inside a site layout that's normal. If this is a standalone screen or a sign, decide what the empty right side and bottom are for.
