**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title, "Rainfall at the reservoir", then drops to the three solid blue bars, which are the heaviest thing on the page. It looks there for a reading and finds nothing: no numbers, no labels, no dates. It then moves right to "Getting there" and reads about a bus, parking and cycle racks, which have nothing to do with rainfall. The bottom 40% of the page is empty. The page feels clean and calm but tells you almost nothing.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a public info page for a reservoir that should show visitors or locals recent rainfall, and maybe how to get there.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The title promises rainfall, but the chart can't be read, and the only readable content is travel directions. A viewer leaves knowing where the bus stops and nothing about the rain.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart says nothing.** The three bars (x ≈120–640, y ≈136–495) have no names under them, no values, no unit, no time period and no source. A viewer can see that the second bar is tallest, but not whether the bars are months, years or gauges, whether "tall" means 40 mm or 400 mm, whether the order is time or arbitrary, or whether three bars are enough to show a trend. Nothing in words gives the takeaway either. The title names a topic, not a finding, and a screen-reader user gets nothing at all. The reader should never have to guess what a mark measures (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2). Fix:
   - Name each bar directly underneath it.
   - Give values with the unit (mm), either on a light axis or printed on each bar.
   - Add the period covered and the source.
   - Keep time periods in time order, and say so.
   - Add a caption that states the finding, e.g. which month was wettest and how that compares with normal, and give the chart a text alternative that carries the same point.
   
   **Defect.**

2. **Two unrelated things sit under one title as if they were one unit.** "Getting there" (x ≈704–1080, y ≈140–280) sits right beside the chart, top-aligned with it and under the rainfall title. That placement says "this text explains the chart" (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*: grouping by nearness). The viewer reads it for the meaning of the bars and gets bus times instead. The page title also stops describing half the page, and the chart is the only section without its own heading. Fix: decide what the page is.
   - If it's a visitor page, give it a title that covers both, such as "Visiting the reservoir", with "Rainfall" and "Getting there" as equal headed sections.
   - If it's a rainfall page, move the directions elsewhere and use that column to explain the data.
   
   **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- The chart's frame (x 120–640, y 136–495) is a dark, roughly 3 px border, heavier than the baseline and the type. It pulls the eye to the edge of the box rather than the data, and white space already separates the chart. Remove it or make it a thin pale grey (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, 23.2; Tufte, ch. 5).
- The empty space looks left over, not planned. There's a pocket under the text column (x ≈704–1080, y ≈280–495), the bottom ~38% is blank, and the right margin (~200 px) doesn't match the left (120 px). Everything sits in the top left. Either let the content fill the frame or shape the empty space on purpose. This matters less if more page follows below.

**What's working.**
- **Disciplined palette.** One saturated blue appears only on the bars, with neutrals and a pale tint of the same blue everywhere else. The data is the only strong colour, so the eye goes there first. Keep this when labels go in.
- **Clean type scale.** There are three clear steps: a ~34 px bold title, a ~21 px semibold subhead and ~17 px regular body. Each changes both size and weight, so the levels never blur.
- **Concrete, finished copy in "Getting there".** It gives a route number, a frequency, a time and a landmark, and each sentence tells a visitor something they can act on. The rainfall section needs that same concreteness.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the bars start at zero. There is no value axis, so check this in the chart settings.
- Whether the chart has a text alternative in the markup.
