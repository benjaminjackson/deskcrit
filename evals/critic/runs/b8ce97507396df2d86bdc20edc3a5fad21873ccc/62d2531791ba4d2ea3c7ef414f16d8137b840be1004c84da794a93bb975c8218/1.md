**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, "Notes for ferry passengers", at top left. It then drops into three centred paragraphs, which have no edge in common with the flush-left heading above them. The right ~40% of the frame and the bottom third are empty white. It reads as a plain, practical passenger notice that looks half-finished, as if something failed to load on the right.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that gives ferry passengers the facts they need to plan a crossing (frequency, bicycles, winter changes), read once and scanned for times and rules.

**Verdict.** The words and type are sound, but the page sets that content in a way that slows reading and looks broken. The biggest problem is the centred body text, which leaves 12 lines with no shared left edge under a flush-left heading. The lopsided empty frame comes close behind.

**What would move it most**
1. **The three paragraphs are centred (x≈125–755, y≈145–520).** Every line starts at a different x (≈125, 129, 134, 149, 157…), so after each line break the eye has to hunt for the next line. That slows exactly the scanning this page exists for: finding "every forty minutes" or "before dusk". Centring also leaves ragged tails ("during the crossing.", "changes for the coming week.") and splits the heading from its text. The longest lines stop a few pixels short of the heading's edge (x≈125 against 120), so they look like an alignment the page meant to make and missed. Centring is for a few short lines, not for paragraphs (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.3). Fix: set the body flush left on the heading's edge at x≈120. That one change also fixes the rag, the near-miss, and the heading looking detached. Convention, with a defect's cost in reading speed.
2. **All the content sits in the top-left; the right ~520 px and the bottom ~280 px are empty.** The 120 / 640 / 520 split follows no proportion, and the blank space doesn't frame the text. It looks left over, so the page looks lopsided and unfinished, as if a sidebar or image failed to load. A heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), and white space should be shaped on purpose (Tschichold). Fix: if nothing else belongs here, centre the column or give it equal margins so the space becomes a frame. If the asymmetry is meant, put something worth having in the right-hand area, such as the timetable itself or a map of the landings. First check in a live browser that nothing failed to load there. Defect (looks broken), plus convention.
3. **The times a passenger acts on are vague.** Paragraph 1 says "between early morning and late evening", and paragraph 3 says "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk". Passengers plan around these lines, and each can be read more than one way. "Early morning" could mean 6:00 or 8:00, and "before dusk" moves through the winter. A passenger who reads it the late way misses the last boat and is stranded on the wrong bank. The page admits the real times exist ("Printed timetables are kept in the shelter") but sends people to the landing to find them. Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing, summer and winter, or link to the timetable. Defect.

**What's working.**
- The headline says exactly who this is for and what follows, and the three paragraphs each keep to one topic: crossing times, bicycles, winter. That plain, specific copy suits a passenger notice.
- The type is right for the job: one family, a bold heading about twice the body size, body text at about 19 px with ~1.5 line spacing, lines of about 60–70 characters, and paragraphs separated by space alone. Once the body is flush left, keep all of this as it is.
- Near-black on white with no decoration. Contrast looks well above 4.5:1, and nothing competes with the words.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty right side is a slot for content that didn't load (image, map, sidebar), and how the column reflows on narrow screens. Check both in a live browser.
