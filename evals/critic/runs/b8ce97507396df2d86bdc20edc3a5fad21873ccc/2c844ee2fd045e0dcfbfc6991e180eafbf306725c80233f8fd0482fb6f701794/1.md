**First read.** The eye goes to the bold title "New sign-ups by channel", drops to the tall blue Organic search bar, then runs across six bars in six bright colours. The note on the right, "What changed this quarter", is read last, but it holds the actual news. It reads as a clean internal growth report for a marketing team. It feels calm and competent but generic, and the point sits off to the side.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal analytics page that tells a growth or marketing team where Q3 sign-ups came from, what changed, and what is being decided because of it.

**Verdict.** It's accurate, well built and easy to read, but it describes the data instead of making its point. The title only names the chart, and the colours stress every bar equally. Someone who glances at it leaves with "organic is biggest". They miss that organic grew after the guide rewrite and that the events budget is moving to referrals.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline names the chart instead of saying the finding.** "New sign-ups by channel" and its subtitle (top left) only say what the chart shows, and the chart then shows the same thing again. The message is in body-size text in the right column: organic search went from a quarter to a third of sign-ups after the guide pages were rewritten, and the events budget moves to referrals in Q4. That column is the last thing the eye reaches. Anyone who skims gets six bars and has to work out the conclusion alone. Fix: make the title the claim, for example "Organic search brought a third of Q3 sign-ups, up from a quarter". Keep "Accounts created in Q3, by the channel of the first visit" as the subtitle so the chart becomes the evidence. If the budget move is the real news, bring it up near the top too. *Taste: no approved source states this. My argument is that the first thing a reader takes in should carry the point.*
2. **Six bright colours stress every bar equally, and two of them send false signals.** Each bar has its own strong colour: blue, orange, red, teal, green and mauve. The colour encodes nothing, because every bar already has its name underneath. Two effects follow:
   - Nothing in the chart picks out the two bars the note is about (Organic search, and Podcast ads plus Events). The reader has to match the text to the bars by hand.
   - On a growth page, red and green mean bad and good. Paid social, the third-best channel at 960, is painted as a warning. Podcast ads, one of the two weak channels the note calls out, gets success green.

   Few ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design") and Wilke (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 19.1) both say to use few colours and grey for context. Apple HIG and Material Design keep red and green for status. Fix: make all the bars one neutral colour and give a single accent to Organic search. If the budget move matters, add a muted second accent on Podcast ads and Events so the chart says what the note says. *Convention, with a defect side: it hides the story and adds a false alarm.*
3. **The main claim rests on Q2 numbers the chart never shows.** "Up from a quarter in Q2" is the page's headline fact, but the chart is Q3 only. The reader has to take the growth on trust and can't see whether any other channel rose or fell. Few names a missing comparison as a dashboard pitfall. Fix: show Q2 next to each bar, as a pale paired bar, a tick mark, or a change figure beside each value label. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- The "What changed this quarter" heading sits about 20–30 px above the top gridline and about 30 px below the subtitle, so it lines up with neither. Align its top exactly to the chart's top gridline, or to the subtitle line.
- The report is fixed to Q3 and has no period picker or "compare to previous quarter" control. If this is a live app page, a quarter selector by the title would let readers check the Q2 claim on this screen. If it's a static snapshot, ignore this.

**What's working.**
- **The numbers agree with each other and with the text.** The bars add up to the 5,400 in the source line. 1,840 is about 34%, which is "a third", and Podcast ads plus Events is 670, which is "fewer than 700". A reader who checks will trust the page. Keep this discipline when the headline becomes a claim.
- **The chart itself is honest and needs no lookup.** It starts at zero with even 500 steps, has pale gridlines, and sorts the bars from largest to smallest. Each bar has its value on top and its name directly below, so there's no legend.
- **The page is quiet and the levels are clear.** One left edge at x≈48 runs through the wordmark, title, subtitle and source. The chart takes about two thirds of the width and the notes about one third. Levels are set apart by weight and grey, not by extra sizes: the note heading is bold at body size, and "Growth" is bold in the nav. Because everything else is neutral, colour is free to mark the story once the bars are fixed.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Keyboard focus rings on the nav links (should be visible, about 2 px with 3:1 contrast). Also whether a sticky header would cover focused content.
