**First read.** You see the bold serif headline first, then read down the left column. "Fares and cards" pulls you across to the right column, but the text under it is about crossing times, bicycles and dogs. The page calls itself a timetable, yet it shows no departure times. Two grey rules close the columns over an empty band of page. It feels calm, sober and bookish, but unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public notice from a harbour office that tells ferry passengers the winter sailing times and how boarding works.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. A "timetable" with no times can't tell a passenger when the boat leaves. The page's only subheading also points to content that isn't there. The typesetting is good. The content and the labels are what fail.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times.** The title "Winter timetable at the harbour" promises departure times. The only schedule facts on the page are "every ninety minutes" and "the first crossing", buried in the second paragraph of the left column. There is no first sailing, no last sailing and no departure list. A passenger who came to find out when the next boat leaves has to read all six paragraphs to learn that the answer isn't here. A page should answer its main question at a glance (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). **Defect.** Fix: add the sailings, at least the first and last boat each day, ideally as a short table straight under the title, and make it the most prominent thing on the page. If the times live somewhere else, retitle the page as winter information for passengers and link to them.
2. **The one subheading names the wrong content, and the other column has none.** "Fares and cards" heads the right column, but its paragraphs cover crossing times in different winds, bicycles, prams and wheelchairs, and dogs. They give no fares. The one sentence about cards, the season card, is in the *left* column. That column has no heading at all, even though its two columns match the right in width and type. So the page has one signpost and it points the wrong way. Someone looking for prices goes to the labelled section, finds none, and stops trusting the page's labels. Prices are exactly what passengers act on, so this matters (WCAG 2.4.6, headings and labels). **Defect.** Fix: give both columns headings that name what they hold, such as "Opening hours and sailings" and "On board". Move the season-card sentence to whichever section deals with tickets. If fares belong on this page, give them a section that actually lists them.

**Smaller things.**
- Grey rules at y≈585 under both columns: they sit 80 to 120 px below uneven text and close off nothing but about 215 px of empty page. Remove them, or put the content they introduce under them.
- Lines across the gutter don't line up: the right column's body starts about 39 px lower than the left's because of its heading, so the lines sit between each other all the way down. Fix it by giving the left column a heading of the same height (which finding 2 already calls for), or by making heading plus gap equal a whole number of lines.
- Single-word last lines in the right column: "thirty-five." (y≈292) and "bags." (y≈414) sit alone on their lines. Tie the last two words with a non-breaking space or use `text-wrap: pretty` (Bringhurst).

**What's working.**
- The grid is tight. Margins are 120 px on both sides, the two columns are 480 px wide with an 80 px gutter, and the title, subheading and rules all start exactly on the column edges. Keep this when you add headings and the timetable.
- The body text sets well. It is one serif at about 18 px, soft near-black on white, with lines of about 50 to 57 characters and open leading, so it is comfortable to read. The calm, single-family look suits a harbour office notice.
- The copy is concrete and plain ("twenty minutes and a rough one closer to thirty-five"). Keep that voice when you write the headings.
