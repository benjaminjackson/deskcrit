**First read.** The eye goes to "Changing your payment card", then down the three bold subheads ("Add a new card", "Remove an old card", "If a payment fails"), then into the body. It reads as a help-centre article for someone in the middle of a billing task. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. The instructions are written as running sentences, so the steps have to be read rather than scanned, and the empty band down the right makes the page look slightly unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help-centre article that tells a customer how to switch the card they are charged on, and what happens if a payment fails.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound: clear hierarchy, honest spacing and nothing decorative. But the copy doesn't fully do the job. It leaves out the step that actually switches the card, and the one paragraph with a deadline can be read two ways. The words matter far more than the layout here.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page never says how to make the new card the one that gets charged.** The intro (y≈160–186) says "The new card is used from your next invoice", which suggests that adding a card is enough. Then "Remove an old card" (y≈479) says a card can only be removed "once another card is set as the default", and no section explains how to set a default. A reader who came to switch cards gets Add and Remove but not the step that links them. If they guess wrong, the old card is charged again, or they get stuck when they try to remove it. Fix: add the step inside "Add a new card" (for example, "then select Set as default"), or say outright that a newly added card becomes the default. Put it next to the Remove instruction too, where readers get stuck. *Defect.*
2. **"If a payment fails" (y≈597–643) can be read two ways, and it never tells the reader what to do.** "We try again after three days… After a third failed try the account moves to read-only" could mean the original charge counts as the first try, so the lock comes about 6 days after the failure. Or it could mean three retries, so the lock comes about 9 days after. A customer planning when to fix their card can pick the wrong reading and be locked out early. "Email the account owner" also switches to the third person when the rest of the page says "your", so readers can't tell whether the email comes to them. The paragraph also stops at what the company does, and never says "update your card" to the reader whose account is at risk. Fix: give the count and timing plainly, address the reader as "you", and end with the action: "If a payment fails, we email you and try again every three days. If it fails three times in a row, your account becomes read-only until a payment goes through. To fix it, add a new card and set it as your default." *Defect.*
3. **The steps send the reader into the product but give no way to get there.** "Open Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card" (y≈287) and "Select the card, then Remove, and confirm" (y≈479) are plain prose, with no link. The interface labels ("Settings", "Billing", "Add card", "Remove") are set exactly like the words around them. The reader has to leave the page, find Settings alone and remember the path. Nielsen's "Recognition rather than recall" heuristic warns against making people carry information from one screen to another. This costs most in the failed-payment section, where the reader is under pressure. Fix: add one "Go to Billing settings" link under the intro and again in "If a payment fails", and set the on-screen labels in bold so they read as things to look for. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Lines run to about 84 characters ("We place a hold of one pound to check the card, and release it within three working", y≈351). Narrow the column to about 600 px, roughly 66 characters (Bringhurst gives 45–75). There is plenty of room on the right.
- "days." sits alone on the last line of the hold paragraph (y≈377). Fix it with a non-breaking space in "working days", and check the breaks again after narrowing the column.
- All the content sits between x≈160 and 840, leaving the right third (x≈840–1280) empty from top to bottom. It looks like a sidebar that failed to load. Either centre the column, or use that space for a short "On this page" list of the three tasks. *Taste* (Arnheim on balance).
- "Nothing already paid is moved" (y≈186) needs a second read: moved where? "Past payments stay on the old card" says it plainly.
- There is no search box or list of related articles. A reader who landed on the wrong article can only go back through the breadcrumb. Add search to the help-centre template if it isn't already outside this crop.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing shows the groups.** Each subhead sits about 12 px above its text and about 64 px below the previous section, and those gaps are the same throughout. The three tasks read as separate units at a glance. Keep this spacing scale.
- **A short, clear type scale and task-named headings.** There is a bold title of about 38 px, bold subheads of about 22 px, regular body of about 17 px, and a grey breadcrumb that sits back as wayfinding. A reader can skim only "Add a new card / Remove an old card / If a payment fails" and jump straight to their case.
- **Restraint.** There are no boxes, rules, icons or accent colour, and nothing that looks like an alarm, so the failed-payment section informs the reader without frightening them. That suits a billing help page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't check whether the breadcrumb links, the only controls on the page, show a visible focus and hover state. I also couldn't see how the column and the empty right band behave on narrower and wider windows.
