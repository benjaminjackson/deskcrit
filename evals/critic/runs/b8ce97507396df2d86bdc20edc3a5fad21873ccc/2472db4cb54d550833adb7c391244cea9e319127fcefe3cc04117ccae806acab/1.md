**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a ferry operator's web page that tells passengers about the rebuilt landing and gets them to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The sign-up form can't deliver what it promises because it never asks where to send the note, and the only picture shows none of the improvements the article describes. The type, spacing and palette are sound. The failures are in what the page asks for and what it shows.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form has no way to reach the person.** In the card on the right, the two fields are "Your name" and "Home landing". The note under the button says "We send one short note when the timetable changes", but there is no email or phone field and no sign that the reader is already logged in. Someone who wants the alerts can't tell how they will arrive. They will either not trust it, or sign up and never hear anything. Meanwhile the form asks for a name, which the service doesn't need to send an alert. **Defect** (common sense: the form cannot do its one job). Fix: add the one contact field the service actually uses, email or mobile number, and name it in the note ("We email you once when…"). Drop the name field or mark it optional.
2. **The figure doesn't show the rebuild.** Figure 1 (left column, under the first paragraph) is flat blocks: a pale sky, a grey slab, a faint grey box and a blue bar. The caption promises "the new north landing, seen from the water at low tide". The text promises wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches and a kiosk. The page's best claim is that prams and wheelchairs roll straight on at every tide, and the picture shows none of it: no ramp, and the shelter is a pale box that nearly disappears into the sky. So the caption reads as untrue, and the article looks unfinished at the one point where it should give proof. **Defect** (common sense). Fix: use a photo or a proper drawing in which the level ramp meeting the deck is the subject, with the shelter visible. If a real image was supposed to load here, this is a loading bug. Check the live page.
3. **"Home landing" is a free text box for a fixed set of places.** The landings are a known list, so typing invites misspellings, nicknames, and "north" versus "North landing". An entry that doesn't match a route means the reader gets no alerts, and they only find out when they miss a sailing. It also makes them recall the exact name instead of picking it. **Convention** (Nielsen, error prevention; Norman, constraints). Fix: use a select menu, or radio buttons if there are only a few landings, and consider defaulting to the landing this article is about.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline "Landing stage rebuild" is a label. The line that matters to passengers, "The ramps now meet the deck at every tide, so prams and wheelchairs roll straight on", sits last under the picture. Lead with it, or build the headline from it.
- The boat in the figure has the same blue and the same rounded corners as the "Sign up" button. It reads for a moment as a second control and dilutes the button as the one accent. Draw it in a slate tone from the illustration's own range.
- The figure ends about 30–40 px short of the text column's right edge. It's a near-miss, not an intentional inset. Match its width to the text column, or narrow the text to the figure.
- All the weight sits top-left: the right margin (160 px) is wider than the left (96 px), and nothing sits below y≈530 except the footnote pinned to the bottom of the screen. Centre the content block, and let the footnote follow the content.
- In the figure, the deck's bottom edge sits exactly on the waterline. You can't tell whether the deck floats or stands on piles, which matters for a "low tide" view. Lift it clear or sink it in on purpose.
- The two fields don't say which is required and which is optional. Mark the optional one, or remove it.
- There is no site name, header or link to the timetables. That hurts trust in a form asking for personal details. This doesn't apply if this is a crop of a fuller page.
- The footer line ("…a sample for a fixture image. All names and places are invented.") is test copy. Replace it with the operator's name, contact details and the update date before publishing. It isn't a finding if this is deliberately a fixture.

**What's working.**
- **Clear starting point and a tight type scale.** There are about four sizes with clear gaps between them: a bold title at about 32 px, a bold card heading at about 22 px, body text at about 18 px, and 13 px for labels and captions. The finer distinctions come from weight, not extra sizes, so the eye always knows where to start.
- **A readable text column.** The first paragraph runs about 66 characters per line, set flush left with comfortable line spacing and near-black on white. It's an easy, steady read.
- **The form's mechanics are done right.** Each field has a visible label sitting close to its own box. The input borders are firm. There is one solid primary button of a good size, with reassurance ("You can stop the notes at any time") right where the person commits. Keep all of this when you add the contact field.
