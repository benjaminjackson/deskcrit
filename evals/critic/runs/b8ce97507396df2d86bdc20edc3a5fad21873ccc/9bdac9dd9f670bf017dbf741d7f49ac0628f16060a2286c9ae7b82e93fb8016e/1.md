**First read.** The eye lands on the big near-black headline, "Plan the whole trip in one place", then drops to the grey subhead and the solid teal "Get started" button. It reads as the landing hero of a group trip-planning app. It feels calm, warm and well ordered, but also empty: the right half and the bottom third hold nothing, and you never see the product.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing site. Its job is to get people who plan trips with a group to see what Wayfold does and start a plan.

**Verdict.** The type, colour and button order are sound, but the page doesn't make its case. Half the screen is empty where the proof should be, so visitors are asked to trust a generic promise and to click a button that doesn't say what it does.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half and the bottom third are empty, so nothing shows the promise.** All the weight sits in one left column (x≈80–680, y≈195–495). The right half (x≈700–1200) and the band under the buttons (y≈500–800) are bare cream.
   - **What the visitor meets:** the space has no shape and nothing balances it, so it doesn't read as a pause. The page leans left, and the gap looks like an image that didn't load.
   - **What it costs:** it wastes the spot where visitors expect to see the product. "Plan the whole trip in one place" is a claim any trip planner could make. Nothing on screen shows flights, stays and day plans together, or a group agreeing, so the visitor takes the promise on trust and leaves with nothing that marks out Wayfold.
   - **Why:** a heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). Empty space should be shaped on purpose (Tschichold; Müller-Brockmann).
   - **Fix:** put one real trip screen in the right half: a group's flights, stay and day plans side by side, ideally with a vote or comment that shows "agree without chat threads". The headline makes the promise and the picture proves it. If you'd rather stay purely typographic, commit to it: set the headline larger so it spans more of the width and the empty field clearly has a shape. If an image belongs there and failed to load, check the live page first.
   - **Defect.**
2. **"Get started" asks for trust, not a decision.** The main button (80–229, 444–496) doesn't say what happens next: a new account, a new trip, or a payment step. Nothing lets the visitor weigh the value against what they use now. The subhead sets up the comparison ("without a dozen chat threads"), and the button drops it. Name the exact action from the visitor's own situation, such as "Start a plan for your next group trip". Put the comparison with the group chat on screen, for example a short before/after line next to the product view from finding 1. **Taste**, argued from the copy: a vague ask on the one button that commits the visitor costs clicks.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** the H1 breaks after "in", which splits "in one place" and leaves "one place" as an afterthought. Break after "trip" with a narrower max-width or `text-wrap: balance`. This is taste.
- **No log-in link:** the top nav (x≈842–1200) has no "Log in". Returning users look for it in that corner and will try "Get started" instead. Add a quiet "Log in" text link at the right end of the nav, styled like the other links. This breaks a convention (Nielsen Norman Group).

**What's working.**
- **Clear order of reading.** The headline is about 60 px and heavy, roughly three times the body size and darker. The two buttons are ranked by fill alone: solid "Get started", outlined "See pricing". Spacing grows from headline-to-subhead to subhead-to-buttons. Together these carry the eye headline → subhead → main action with no detours. Keep that gap in size and weight.
- **A small palette.** The one teal is used only for the brand and the two actions. The order still holds in greyscale, so colour-blind visitors find the main action just as easily.
- **One shared left edge.** Logo, headline, subhead and buttons all sit on x≈80, with matching 80 px side margins. The subhead's first line is about 64 characters and reads in one easy pass.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether an image or animation was meant to fill the right half and hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
- Focus rings on the outlined "See pricing" button. A teal ring could blend into its teal border.
