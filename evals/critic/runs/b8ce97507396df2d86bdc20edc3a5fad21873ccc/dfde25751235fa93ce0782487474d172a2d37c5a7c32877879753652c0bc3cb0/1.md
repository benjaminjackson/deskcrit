**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The only content the title promises is a chart that can't be read, and the one readable block on the page is about something else, set where a caption should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart says nothing.** The three bars (x 120–640, y 136–495) have no labels under them, no values, no unit and no axis. A viewer can see the middle bar is about twice the right one, but not which months or years the bars are or how many millimetres any of them shows. They also can't tell whether the order of medium, high, low is time order or random. The chart fails the one job it has (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few, *Show Me the Numbers*). Fix: label each bar with its period, put the value and unit on each bar (for example "84 mm"), and keep periods in time order. With values on the bars you don't need an axis. **Defect.**
2. **The page doesn't know what it's about.** The title promises rainfall, but half the page is travel directions. A first-time viewer can't say whether this is a rainfall report or a visitor page, and the title describes only half of what's on it (WCAG 2.4.6: headings describe their content). Decide what the page is for. If it's a visitor page, name it for the place and make rainfall one labelled section. If it's a rainfall page, move the directions somewhere else. **Defect.**
3. **"Getting there" touches the chart, so it reads as the chart's caption.** The chart frame ends at about x 639 and the heading starts at about x 641, so there's no gap. That's tighter than the roughly 10 px between the heading and its own paragraph. Things close together read as one group (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), so a reader looking for what the bars mean looks right there and finds bus times. Fix: give the directions their own section with a real gutter or space above, wider than any gap inside a group (at least the roughly 48 px under the title). Better still, move them below the chart as a separate section. **Defect.**
4. **The title names a topic, not a point.** Even with the bars labelled, the viewer has to work out the takeaway alone. Once item 1 is fixed, add a line that says what the data shows, such as "March was the wettest of the three; rainfall then fell by half." **Taste**: it tells a casual visitor why the chart is there.
