**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", and then jumps right to the big teal block. That block holds only two empty speech bubbles, so the eye drops back to the left. There it finds two filled colour blocks stacked one above the other: the orange button and a larger purple card. For a moment it isn't clear which one to press. It reads as a clean, friendly language-learning landing page that could easily be a template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a logged-out landing page whose job is to get adults who are new to the product to start the free week.

**Verdict.** The words are good, but the layout works against them. The one thing the page wants the visitor to do, "Start your free week", is upstaged by a purple card right below it that looks like a second button and talks to users who already have the app. The largest picture on the page shows nothing about the product.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple "Keep your streak going" card competes with the call to action** (x 56–456, y 390–498). It is drawn like the button: a saturated, filled, rounded box with bold white text. It is also bigger and wider than the button and sits only about 28 px under it, the same gap as subhead to button. So it looks like a second action in the same signup group. In greyscale the violet is darker than the orange, so it outranks the button on value as well.
   - **What the visitor experiences:** they hesitate over which block is the real action, and they may tap a card that does nothing.
   - **The copy makes it worse.** "Keep your streak going" speaks to people already using the app, so a first-time visitor has no streak to keep and can't tell whether the message is for them.
   - **Sources:** one high-emphasis action per view (Material Design, "Buttons"); a control that looks pressable but isn't misleads the user (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, ch. 1); items placed close together read as one group (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II); Nielsen heuristic 2, match between system and the real world.
   - **Fix:** make the orange button the only filled, saturated shape in the left column. The fact on the card is useful proof ("practise five days in a row and you're twice as likely to finish"). Rewrite it for the visitor and set it as quiet text or a pale tint under the button, with clearly more space above it. Or move the card out of the hero and show it to signed-in users.
   - Defect.
2. **The hero picture is the biggest, most saturated shape on the page and says the least** (teal panel, x 676–1076, y 110–410).
   - **What the visitor sees:** two generic speech bubbles with placeholder lines. That is the word "conversation" drawn again. It shows no language, no tutor that listens, no feedback on speaking, so any chat or support product could use the same image. That is what makes the page look like a template: the visitor can't see what is different about this product.
   - **The colour adds to the problem.** Teal, orange and violet at equal strength mean no hue leads, and the panel's mass of colour pulls the eye away from the headline and the button. Itten (*The Art of Color*) says to keep one hue dominant and mute the rest. Refactoring UI ("Define your shades") calls for one neutral range and one or two brand hues.
   - **Fix:** use the space to prove the headline. For example, show a real exchange in Spanish or French with the tutor's feedback on each word, or a lesson screen. Bring the panel's colour into the same family as the orange, or make it a light tint, so orange stays the only lead colour.
   - Taste on the content of the picture; defect on the competing palette.
3. **The hero ignores the page's grid.** The steps row sets three clean columns (56–413, 461–819, 867–1224). The picture starts at 676 and stops at 1076, which matches none of those lines and leaves a 148 px strip short of the 1224 margin that the nav and steps reach. Leftover gaps form to its right and under it.
   - **What the visitor sees:** the right half looks placed by eye and unanchored, and the hero and the steps don't read as one system.
   - **Source:** Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*.
   - **Fix:** take the hero split from the steps columns. For example, run the picture from 461 or 867 out to 1224, and line its top and bottom up with the headline and the end of the text column. Do this when you replace the picture in finding 2.
   - Convention.

**Smaller things.**
- **Button contrast:** the orange behind "Start your free week" looks close to #DC4A10. That gives white text about 4.2:1. At about 18 px bold, the label probably counts as normal text, which needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure it. If it falls short, darken the orange one step or set the label at 19 px bold or larger.
- **Headline line break:** it breaks as "…conversation in / ten weeks", which leaves "in" at the end of line 1. Break it as "Hold a real conversation / in ten weeks", so line 1 is the outcome and line 2 is the timeframe.
- **Gap above the steps:** the space between the hero and the steps row (about 52 px) is too small to read as a change of section. Open it up.
- **What happens after the free week:** nothing near the button says what it costs or whether a card is needed. This is the commitment a visitor will hold you to. A short line under the button would answer it before they hesitate.

**What's working.**
- **The copy:** the headline makes a specific, checkable promise. The subhead names the method (speaking first), the four languages and the tutor. "Start your free week" says exactly what you get and for how long.
- **The steps row:** it is the best-built part of the page. It has three equal columns with even gutters, a thin rule over each step, and bold dark headings over grey text, so Listen, Speak, Use it reads as a sequence at a glance.
- **The left edge and the type:** one flush-left edge at 56 px runs from the logo down through the hero, and one sans family sets every level. Keep both.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the purple card is clickable. If it is, finding 1 becomes two competing primary actions, and the fix is the same.
- Hover and focus states on the button and the nav links.
- The empty band below the steps (y 620–800) may just be where the screenshot ends. Check it in a live browser.
