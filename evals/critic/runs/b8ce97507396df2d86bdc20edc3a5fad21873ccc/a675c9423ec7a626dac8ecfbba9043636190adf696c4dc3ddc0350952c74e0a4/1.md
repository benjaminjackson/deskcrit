**First read.** Your eye lands on "184,220", runs across the three big numbers, then drops to the "Visits by channel" chart, which is the largest thing on the page. There it stalls: three coloured lines rise across a plot with no legend, no values and no dates, so there's nothing to read. It reads as a calm, clean, rather generic marketing analytics overview.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a growth or marketing team checking how traffic and sign-ups moved over the last 12 weeks, and which channel drove the change.

**Verdict.** The top half works: three clear numbers, each with an honest comparison. But the chart, which takes up more than half the screen, can't be read. It doesn't say which line is which channel, how many visits a line stands for, or which week is which. So the page answers "are we up?" and fails at "why?", which is the reason a channel chart is there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no labels of any kind.** This is a defect.
   - **Where:** In "Visits by channel" (about y 340–700) there are five faint gridlines with no values, nothing under the baseline at y≈684, and no channel names.
   - **What the viewer gets:** They see three rising lines. They can't tell which channel is blue, orange or green, and they can't read the size of a gap or the week of the blue dip at x≈805. They also can't check the lines against the 184,220 total above. The empty ~70 px strip left of the plot and the ~75 px band under it look like room kept for axis labels that aren't there. If they're supposed to render and didn't, check in a live browser. As shown, though, the chart is decoration. Tufte (*The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*) and Few both treat named marks and scales as what makes a chart worth reading.
   - **Fix:** Add visit values on the gridlines and week labels along the baseline. Name each channel in its line's colour just past the right end of the line. There's room inside the card at x≈1185–1230, and direct labels beat a legend because nobody has to match colours (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). The labels also fix colour-only coding: today orange and green are a common colour-blind confusion pair, held apart only because the lines don't cross (WCAG 1.4.1).
2. **"Last 12 weeks" sits where a date-range picker goes, but it looks like a caption.** This is a defect if it's interactive.
   - **Where:** Top right of the header.
   - **What the viewer gets:** Analytics users expect to change the period from that spot. Here they find plain grey text with no border, chevron or icon, so they won't try it, or they'll click and guess. If it's only a label, the page offers no way to change the period, and it still doesn't say what dates "12 weeks" covers or when the data was last updated. Norman (signifiers), Nielsen (visibility of system status).
   - **Fix:** If it's a control, make it look like one: a bordered button with a chevron. Either way, show the actual date span or an "Updated …" time next to it, so 184,220 has a clear end date.

**Smaller things.**
- **Nothing leads in the KPI row.** The three cards are identical, so the eye starts at Visits only because it's on the left. If one metric is what the team is judged on (likely sign-ups or conversion), give it more weight. If they're equal peers, this is acceptable dashboard convention.
- **Some type sizes are too close.** "Overview" (~24 px bold) looks only a step below the KPI numbers (~28 px bold), so the title doesn't sit clearly above them. "Brightpath Analytics" and "Visits by channel" look the same size and weight, although one is the product and the other a section heading. Tighten to fewer, clearly separated sizes (Bringhurst).
- **The chart title only names the topic.** "Visits by channel" could say what happened, for example which channel drove the +6.1%. That's my taste, not a rule.
- **Left edges look slightly off.** "Visits by channel" looks about 2 px right of the card labels above it, suggesting the chart panel and the cards use different inner padding. The first data points also start about 22 px inside the plot edge. Use one padding for all panels, and start the lines on the edge or clearly inset them.
- **No sign of where you are or what else exists.** "Overview" implies other views, but there's no nav or current-page marker. Add the nav, or drop the heading if this is the only view.
- **Nothing visibly lets you drill down or filter.** There's no channel filter or clickable card. That's fine for a glance-only summary, but it starts to cost something if analysts come back to this screen every day.

**What's working.**
- **Every number has its comparison written out.** Each says "vs previous 12 weeks", and the conversion card correctly uses "+0.1 pts" rather than "%" for a change in a rate. These are numbers people repeat in meetings, and no one has to guess the baseline.
- **One grid and a quiet palette.** The cards and the chart panel share the same outer edges and gutters, and outside the chart the only colour is the gain green. The chart lines are the only strong colour on the page, so the data, not the chrome, gets the attention. Keep that restraint when adding labels.
- **The chart itself has little clutter.** The gridlines are pale and there are no markers or borders inside the plot. The lines never cross, so once they're named, each will be easy to follow.
