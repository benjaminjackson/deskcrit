**First read.** The eye lands on the heavy serif headline at top left, drops into the left paragraph, then crosses to the right one. It reads as a small club or community page about a boat-restoration shed: quiet, bookish and warm. Then the text stops at about a third of the way down, and the rest of the frame is empty white.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a short web page that introduces a volunteer boat shed and gets newcomers to come and help.

**Verdict.** The page is well made and lovely to read, but it doesn't do its job. It invites people in without telling them when, where, or how to come, and the layout ends abruptly over an empty field. Right now it is a pleasant piece of writing, not an invitation anyone can act on.

**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation can't be acted on.** The headline "Notes from the boat shed" (top left) reads like a diary entry. The whole left column is scene-setting. The real point, come and volunteer, only appears in the right column, and even there it's incomplete:
   - "Thursday evenings" has no start time.
   - "The end of the lane" names no lane or town.
   - There's no contact, sign-up or link anywhere on the page.

   A newcomer finishes with a clear picture of the shed and no way to get there. A page should make clear within seconds what it is and what you can do (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Here the "what you can do" is buried and then left unfinished.

   Fix: let the headline or a short line under it say this is an invitation, for example something like "Help us varnish three dinghies, Thursday nights." Then give the ask its facts: day, time, the actual address, and either a contact or a plain "just turn up". "Nobody needs experience" is the best line on the page because it removes the main reason people hold back. Put it right next to those facts. *Defect.*

2. **The page is top-heavy and looks unfinished.** All the content sits in one band from about y 90 to y 305. The lower 490 px, roughly 60% of the frame, is blank. That space doesn't frame the text; it's simply where the content ran out, so the page looks like something failed to load (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Fix: give the lower field a job. The obvious candidates are the missing logistics from finding 1 and a photo of the shed or the dinghies. Or size the page to its content so the text block sits in it on purpose. *Defect*, unless the live page continues below this frame (see below).

**What's working.**
- **A clean two-level hierarchy.** One serif family: a heavy bold headline at roughly three times the body size, then regular body text. There's no in-between size, so the eye knows instantly where to start. If you add subheads, keep them clearly between those two sizes.
- **Comfortable reading.** The body looks about 17 px with generous line spacing and roughly 55 to 60 characters per line, inside Bringhurst's 45 to 75 range. The two columns hang off the headline's left edge and share baselines, so they read as a matched pair. Colour stays out of the way: near-black on white, with structure coming from size and weight alone.
- **Copy you can smell.** "Three clinker dinghies", "tar and cut cedar", "a kettle on, and a spare apron on the hook" make the place feel real and welcoming. Keep every word of it when you add the practical details.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The blank lower 60% may be content that didn't load (a photo, map or sign-up form) or a page that carries on below an 800 px viewport. Check the live page. If the time, place and contact details sit further down, finding 1 becomes a question of moving them up to the first screen rather than adding them.
