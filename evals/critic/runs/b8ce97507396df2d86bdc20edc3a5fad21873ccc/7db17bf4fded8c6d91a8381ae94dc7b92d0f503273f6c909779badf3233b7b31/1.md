**Verdict.** The page doesn't work yet, mainly because of the middle card. It changes every property at once, so the viewer can't tell whether this route is recommended, sponsored or broken. Three routes that should be easy to compare look unequal for no reason the page gives.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Woodland loop" is built from a different card, and nothing says why.** The middle card (x ≈ 460–820) differs from the two outer cards in every way:
   - charcoal fill instead of white;
   - square corners and a flat grey edge instead of rounded corners and a blue-grey border;
   - a serif heading at about the size of the page title "Walking routes", where the sibling headings are a smaller bold sans;
   - about 15 px of padding where the others have about 34 px, so its heading, body text and button all sit a few pixels higher and further left than theirs;
   - a yellow outlined pill button where the others are filled blue rectangles.

   What the viewer meets:
   - It reads as featured, but there's no label or reason, so it could just as well be an ad or a styling bug.
   - The signals contradict each other: the loudest card has the weakest button, since an outline is the secondary style (Material Design 3, "Buttons").
   - Its heading competes with the page title, so the type scale loses its top level.
   - The near-miss alignments make the eye stutter as it scans along the row (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*; Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Nielsen heuristic 4, consistency and standards).

   Fix: rebuild it from the same card component as the others, with the same corners, padding, sans heading and filled button. If this route really is the one to pick, mark it in one way only, with a fill or a tint plus a word that gives the reason ("Recommended: flat and shaded"). Its heading stays at card level. **Defect.**

2. **The bottom half of every card is empty.** Each card runs from y ≈ 160 to 598, but the content stops at about y 394. That leaves about 200 px of boxed-in blank space per card, with another 200 px of open page below the row. The cards look unfinished, as if a map or photo failed to load, and the white space sits trapped inside the borders instead of gathered where it does some work (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*). Fix: either size the cards to their content and let the space stay open on the page, or fill the area with what a walker uses to choose (a map, the distance, the difficulty). **Defect**, unless something is meant to load there (see below).

**Smaller things.**
- Every description ends with one word alone on its last line ("end.", "metres.", "bicycles."). "end." is the worst. Pull a second word down with `text-wrap: pretty` or a non-breaking space, or edit the copy (Bringhurst, §2.4.2).
- The distance and terrain, the things a walker compares, appear only inside the prose. Pulling "6 km · 2 steep climbs" into a short line of facts on each card would let people compare the three at a glance. Taste.

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete and written from the walker's side: distance, terrain and amenities ("two steep climbs and a cafe at the far end", "paved all the way, and good for bicycles"). Keep that voice.
- The outer frame is a real grid. The three cards are equal width with even 32 px gutters, and the page title is flush with the first card at x 64. The outer cards also step cleanly from bold name to grey description to filled button. That's the component to rebuild the middle card from.
- The value range is clean. The dark slate (not pure black) keeps the light body text on it soft but legible.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty lower half of each card is meant to hold a route map or photo that didn't load in this capture. Check in a live browser.
- Whether the dark middle card is a hover or selected state rather than its resting style. If it is, finding 1 shows up less often, but the clash in heading size and button style stays.
- Focus outlines on the three buttons, especially the yellow pill on charcoal. They need to be visible at 3:1 contrast.
