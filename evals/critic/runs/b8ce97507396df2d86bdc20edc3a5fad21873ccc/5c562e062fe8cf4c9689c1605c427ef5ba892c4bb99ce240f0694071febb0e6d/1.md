**First read.** The eye lands first on the large serif headline. It then drops to the row of four bright illustrated cards and finally finds the dark "Book a visit" button at the top right. The page reads as the home page of a local home-services company. It feels calm, warm and tidy, but also a bit templated, and the cream band under the cards is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a landing page that gets a homeowner to see what Fernway does and roughly what it costs, then pick a service and book it.

**Verdict.** It looks good and it explains the offer, but it doesn't follow through on its own instruction. The subhead says "Pick a service, choose a day", yet nothing on the page lets you pick a service or choose a day. The prices and the laundry schedule are also worded loosely enough that a customer could read them the wrong way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards don't let you pick a service, and the page stops where the decision is made.** This is in the four cards (y≈280–646) and the empty band below them (y≈650–800).
   - **What the viewer meets:** The subhead tells people to "Pick a service", so they look for a way to pick one. Each card is a white panel with a faint shadow and looks like a fixed content box. It has no "Book" label, no arrow and no button. The only thing that looks clickable is the teal bold "From $…" price, and only its colour marks it as a link. Visitors will either click a price to test it, or decide the cards are just information. After reading the cards they reach 150 px of empty cream. The only way to book is the small button in the top-right corner, about 600 px from where they chose. Nothing says whether that button remembers the service they picked.
   - **Why:** A control needs a visible cue that it can be used (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*). Things you can click must look clickable (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). A link marked only by colour fails WCAG 1.4.1.
   - **Fix:** Put the next step on each card, for example "Book window cleaning →" on the price line or a secondary button. Make that step lead into "choose a day" with the service already filled in, and keep the header button as the route that's always there. If the cards really aren't clickable, take the link colour off the prices so they stop suggesting a click.
   - Defect.
2. **The prices and the laundry promise can each be read two ways.** These are the price lines (y≈614 on every card) and the Laundry pickup description.
   - **Prices:** "From $89 / $189 / $24 / $65" doesn't say what the price is per: a visit, a room, a load, a bag or a week. "From $24" for laundry is the most open. Visitors can't budget or compare the services, and their first quote may not match what they assumed.
   - **Laundry schedule:** The headline promises "on your schedule" and the subhead says "choose a day". The laundry card says "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday." It could be read as "Monday pickup only" or as "Monday to Wednesday is just an example". If it means Monday only, the page's main promise isn't true for this service, and the customer finds out after counting on it.
   - **Why it matters:** Prices and schedules are what people act on and later hold you to.
   - **Fix:** Add the unit ("From $24 per bag") and say which way the schedule works, for example "Picked up the day you choose, back folded two days later."
   - Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** It breaks as "on your / schedule", which splits the key phrase and leaves "schedule" alone on the second line. Break after the comma, using a narrower measure, a manual break or `text-wrap: balance`.
- **Card insets:** In every card the picture panel starts about 14 px in (x≈70) and the title and text start about 20 px in (x≈76). The edges are off by about 6 px, which looks like a mistake. Give the panel and the text the same inset, or run the panel to the card edges.
- **Illustration weight:** The window picture uses a thick dark outline of about 6 px, while the other three are filled shapes with thin 3–4 px lines. Only the bucket card has outlined rings. The first card looks heavier than the others, so the row of four equal options pulls slightly left. Pick one weight for the line work and one set of decorations (this is a matter of taste).

**What's working.**
- **One consistent look.** The cream background, the dark slate serif used only for the logo and headline, a plain sans for everything else, and bright colour kept inside the illustration panels all share one calm, domestic mood. That suits a service that sends strangers into your home. Keep the bright colours inside the pictures and the button as the only solid dark block.
- **Cards that compare at a glance.** The four cards are equal and built on one grid: same panel, a bold title, a grey description and the price at the bottom, with titles, descriptions and prices lined up across all four. Weight and colour separate the levels instead of extra sizes. Keep the prices at a fixed position as the copy length changes.
- **A clear starting point and a clear booking button.** The headline is about twice the size of anything else and is the only serif besides the logo, so the eye knows where to start. "Book a visit" names a specific action rather than a vague "Get started".

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards or prices are actually clickable, and how they look on hover. This decides whether finding 1 means adding a cue or removing a false one.
- Whether the empty band below the cards is the top of the next section or the end of the page.
- Whether the keyboard focus indicator shows on the nav links and the button.
