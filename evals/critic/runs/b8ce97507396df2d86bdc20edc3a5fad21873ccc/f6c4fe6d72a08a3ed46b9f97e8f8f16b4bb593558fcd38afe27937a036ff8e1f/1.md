**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board" and then drops into three paragraphs set entirely in capitals. They read as one flat grey slab, and it feels like being shouted at. This is clearly a notes page for river-ferry passengers. It's calm and plain everywhere except the body.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short page that tells ferry passengers the schedule, the bicycle rules and the winter changes before they travel.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the page makes passengers work too hard for its few facts. Setting all the body text in capitals is the single biggest thing in the way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is all capitals** (all three paragraphs, about 75 words over eight lines, from the top of the body to its last line). Capitals have no ascenders or descenders, so every word becomes a rectangle the same height. The reader has to spell their way through instead of recognising words by shape. The paragraphs also merge into one even block, so the facts a passenger needs don't stand out: every forty minutes, twelve minutes a crossing, bicycles free, one boat an hour in winter. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says plainly not to capitalise whole paragraphs, and Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls capitals particularly difficult to read. The capitals are also set at default spacing, which makes them denser still. Don't fix that by spacing them out. Set the paragraphs in normal upper and lower case, and keep capitals for short labels like "PASSENGER NOTES", which already uses them correctly. *Convention, and a defect in what it costs the reader.*
2. **The timetable never gives times.** "Between early morning and late evening" is the one fact a passenger has to plan around, and it has no reference point. Someone deciding whether they can catch a 06:30 or a 22:00 boat can't tell, and the page sends them to a printed timetable in the shelter, which they only reach once they're at the landing. Give the first and last sailings as clock times, and say when "winter" starts and ends. *Defect (copy).*

**What's working.**
- **A clean two-level header.** "PASSENGER NOTES" is small, bold, letterspaced and grey, so it reads as a tag. "Before you board" is the only large, heavy element, so the eye knows at once where to start. When the body becomes mixed case, keep the label's size, weight and colour differences.
- **One left edge, and spacing that groups.** The label, the heading and all three paragraphs share one left edge. The gap between paragraphs is clearly larger than the gap between lines, so the page reads as three separate notes under one title. The line length (about 70 characters) and line spacing will suit reading once the case is fixed.
- **Concrete copy elsewhere.** "Wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail" tells the passenger who does what and when, with nothing left to guess.
