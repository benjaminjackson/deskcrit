**First read.** The eye lands on the big "Pricing" title, drops to the three equal prices, and moves between $9, $29 and $79. After that it runs down the feature bullets and stops at the bottom of the cards, where there is nothing to do. It reads as a clean, calm SaaS pricing page for a calendar or booking tool. It looks competent but templated, and the bottom third of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption is that this is the pricing page of a scheduling SaaS, and its job is to help a visitor pick a plan and start it.

**Verdict.** It looks tidy but doesn't do its job. A visitor can compare the plans, but they can't act on the one they choose, and they can't be sure what the price covers.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards have no next step.** None of the three cards (y≈306–570) has a button or link. The comparison ends at the last bullet. The only action is "Start free" in the top-right corner of the header, far back along the reading path. It doesn't say which plan it starts, and since no plan here is free it also leaves open whether "free" means a trial or a free tier.
   - What the visitor meets: they decide on "Team, $29" and then have to go looking for a generic button and guess what it does. This is the core task of a pricing page, and the screen doesn't support it. The empty 230 px below the cards shows there is room.
   - Fix: put one action at the same spot at the bottom of each card. Word it with the plan and what happens, for example "Start Team free for 14 days", if that is the real offer. That way the eye's path ends on an action (Arnheim).
   - Defect.
2. **The price can be read two ways.** "$29 / month" and "$79 / month" don't say whether the price is for the whole team or per user. With plan names like Team and Business and a feature like "Up to 10 calendars", a buyer can reasonably read $29 either way. The wrong reading misjudges the cost by up to ten times, and the buyer finds out at checkout or on the first invoice.
   - The feature lists also don't say whether higher plans include the lower ones. Taken literally, Team drops "Booking page" and "Email reminders".
   - "Calendar" is undefined: it could be one per person or one per resource.
   - Fix: put the unit in plain words ("/ month for the whole team" or "/ user / month"). Start the higher tiers' lists with "Everything in Solo, plus:". Say what a calendar is.
   - Defect: these are the numbers the buyer will hold the company to.
3. **Nothing tells the visitor where to start or which plan to pick.** The "Pricing" title is the largest thing on the page, but only just: it is the same bold near-black as the three prices and barely bigger. That gives four heavy elements of nearly equal strength. The strongest of them is a word the nav already shows as the current page. The three cards are exactly equal, so the visitor gets no guidance.
   - Fix: shrink the title so it acts as a label, and make one plan (probably Team) clearly the recommended one. A stronger border or fill, a small "Most popular" tag, or a primary button only on that card would all do it. The other cards get secondary actions.
   - Defect (Tschichold, Hofmann: one element should dominate).

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale:** the feature text (~14–15 px), "/ month" (~16 px) and the nav (~16 px) look a pixel or two apart, and the title sits close to the price numerals. Settle on about four sizes with clear steps, and use weight and colour for the finer differences (Bringhurst).
- **Feature text size:** the feature lists, which are the content buyers compare, look smaller than the menu labels. Bring them up to at least the nav size and keep the grey.
- **Subtitle line break:** it breaks as "…whenever / you like.", leaving a stub. Break after "every size." so each sentence gets its own line.
- **Grid:** the card row (x 162–1118) shares no edge with the header (logo at 64, button at 1216, first nav item at 223). Hang the cards on the header's columns so one set of vertical lines runs top to bottom (Müller-Brockmann).
- **Generic copy:** the title repeats the nav item, the subtitle is stock SaaS copy, and nothing on the screen says what Tidewell does. Use the subtitle to name the benefit in the visitor's terms, for example letting clients book time without email back-and-forth, and keep "cancel anytime" as the reassurance. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The cards line up across rows.** Name, price and the three feature rows sit on the same baselines in every card. You can compare by reading straight across ($9 / $29 / $79; "1 calendar / Up to 10 / Unlimited"). Keep that when you add buttons and a recommended tier.
- **One accent colour, kept for brand and action.** The blue appears only in the logo, the active-tab underline and the button. Everything else is value steps of near-black, grey and pale border, so the structure holds even in greyscale. Don't add a second accent to mark the recommended plan; use this blue.
- **Price and unit.** The bold price numeral with a small grey "/ month" beside it reads price first and unit second.
