**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the About section of a small trust's website. It should tell local people who the trust is, and above all get them to turn up to a work day.

**Verdict.** The text is easy to read, but it hides its own ask. The only thing a visitor can act on, the invitation to work days, starts mid-line partway down one undivided paragraph. Even if someone finds it, it doesn't tell them when, where or how to join.

**What would move it most**
1. **The volunteer invitation is buried in the history.** The paragraph is one 11-line block. "Work days run every second Saturday…" starts mid-line at about y 406, straight after "…a few miles of the moor." It has the same size, weight and colour as the founding story before it. The heading "About the trust" only describes the first half, the background. A visitor who skims reads the heading and a line or two of history, sees one grey block, and moves on without learning they're invited. Nielsen's work on how people read on the web shows that skimmers skip dense, unbroken blocks. Start a new paragraph at "Work days run…" and give it its own heading or lead line that says what's on offer, such as "Join a work day". That gives the ask its own place on the page instead of hiding it in the background. *Defect.*
2. **The invitation doesn't give anyone enough to act on.** "Every second Saturday" can mean every other Saturday or the second Saturday of the month. Someone who reads it the wrong way turns up on a day when nobody is there. The section gives no start time, no meeting point, no next date, and no way to say you're coming or ask a question. A willing reader has to look for those details somewhere else, and many won't bother. Give the next date, the time, the place, and one exact action, e.g. "Next work day: Saturday 6 December, 9am, quarry car park. Just turn up, or email us to say you're coming." *Defect* (it's a date and a commitment line, the kind people act on).

**Smaller things.**
