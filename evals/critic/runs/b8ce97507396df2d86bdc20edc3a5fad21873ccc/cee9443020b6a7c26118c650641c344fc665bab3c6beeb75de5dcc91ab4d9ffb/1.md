**First read.** The eye lands on the white serif "How can we help?" in the navy band and drops straight to the search field. The next stop isn't "Browse by topic" or the first card. It's the two full-colour icons, the blue-and-orange credit card and the green shield, which stand out from an otherwise quiet grid of outline icons. It reads as a SaaS help-centre home page: calm, tidy, trustworthy, and fairly templated.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre landing page whose job is to get a stuck customer to the right article quickly, either by searching or by picking a topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. Searching is clearly the main action, and the eight topics are written in the customer's own words. The biggest thing in the way is the two icons drawn in a different style, which wrongly promote Billing and Privacy. After that, the page offers nothing to click that answers a common question directly.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two icons come from a different family and create false emphasis.** Six topic icons are thin navy outlines: the lightning bolt, key, cube, plug, bell and lifebuoy. "Billing and invoices" (top row, third card) has a filled credit card in blue, yellow and orange. "Privacy and security" (bottom row, first card) has a filled green shield with a check. These are the only saturated colours below the hero, and the orange is the only warm colour on the page. So after the search field, the eye jumps to Billing and Privacy before it reads the grid, which tells the visitor those two topics are featured when nothing else says so. The set also looks assembled from two icon libraries, which undercuts the calm, built-as-one-system feel a support page relies on. Apple's HIG ("Icons") asks for a consistent size, level of detail and stroke weight across a set, and Gerstner (*Designing Programmes*) makes the broader point that elements of one kind share one style. Fix: redraw the card and the shield as outlines in the same navy and stroke weight as the other six. If Billing or Security really should stand out, do it on purpose through placement or a label, not through a stray icon style. **Defect.**
2. **No direct route to an answer on the first screen.** In the 800 px shown, a visitor can only type a query or guess which of eight broad categories holds their problem. There are no popular questions. The only escalation path is "Contact us" in the header. Someone with a common problem like "reset my password" or "where's my order" needs an extra step and a correct guess before reaching anything useful. Fix: add a short "Popular articles" list of four to six of the most-asked questions, under the search or under the grid. End the grid with a "Still stuck? Contact us" line. If this already exists below the fold, bring at least the top few questions up into view. **Taste.** No approved source states this. The argument is that most help-centre visits are a handful of repeat questions, and a one-click answer beats any amount of browsing.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Browse by topic" doesn't read as a heading.** It looks about 18 px, the card titles about 16 px, and the wordmark about 20 px, all in the same dark, bold sans. So "Browse by topic" reads as a ninth label rather than the heading over the grid. Make it a clearly separate level: a real step up in size (around 22–24 px) or a change of form, such as the headline serif or a small uppercase grey label (Dair, ch. 8; Bringhurst §3.1.1).
- **The search field has no search signifier.** It has no magnifier icon and no submit button, so only the grey placeholder "Search for articles" says what the field does, and that disappears as soon as someone starts typing. Mouse users also get nothing to click. Add a magnifier inside the left edge, or a "Search" button on the right (NN/g on placeholders; Norman on signifiers).
- **Several card descriptions end on a single word.** "data." (Privacy), "where." (Notifications), "receipts." (Billing), "recovery." (Account) and "messages." (Troubleshooting) each sit alone on a second line. Rewrite the blurbs to fit one line or fill two evenly, or use balanced wrapping (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- **The search field nearly lines up with the grid.** It looks to span about x 360–920, while the middle two card columns span about 357–922 below it, a near-miss of a few pixels. Match it exactly to 357–922 or make it clearly narrower. These are by-eye estimates, so check the real boxes in a browser.

**What's working.**
- **The hero does exactly one thing.** "How can we help?" is the largest type on the page and the only serif. It's centred on a single axis with the subline and a white search field on the one dark band, so the main action is never in doubt. Below it, the grid switches to left alignment for scanning, which is the right split.
- **The topic copy is concrete and in the customer's words.** Lines like "Track a delivery or start a return." and "Fix sync problems and error messages." let a visitor match their problem to a card by recognising it, without needing to know the product's internal names. Each card pairs a semibold near-black title with a lighter grey description, so the titles alone can be scanned.
- **One strict grid sets every edge on the page.** The 64 px side margins are shared by the wordmark, the nav, the section heading and the cards. The gaps between columns and rows are even, and the titles and descriptions line up across all four columns. Keep this grid when topics are added or renamed.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Hover and focus states on the cards.** Nothing but the card shape shows they can be clicked, and their borders are thin and pale, so check in a browser that a hover change and a visible keyboard focus ring are both there.
- **Contrast of the grey text.** The descriptions and the search placeholder look close to the 4.5:1 minimum, so measure them.
