**First read.** The eye goes first to the big dark block at the top, the heaviest thing on a white page. It finds a faceless grey silhouette there, the stock "no photo" graphic. It then drops to the bold headline, "An evening with Ines Varga on her new novel, The Salt Year", and ends on the black "Book a free seat" button. It reads as a small bookshop's event page. It feels calm and tidy but unfinished, as if the photo never arrived.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is Margin Books' page for one author reading, and its job is to get local readers to book a free seat.

**Verdict.** The layout and copy work: the reading order is clear, there's one obvious action and the description is concrete. But the page fails on the two things a visitor acts on. The date can't be right as written, and the biggest, first-seen spot on the page is a placeholder.

**What would move it most**
1. **The date can't be trusted.** The rust line above the headline says "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" and gives no year.
   - 13 November was a Thursday in 2025. In 2026 it's a Friday, and it's also already past (today is 23 November 2026).
   - So either the weekday is wrong, and some readers turn up on the wrong night, or the event is over and the page still says "Book a free seat".
   - This is the one line people put in their diary and hold the shop to. A line here that can be read two ways is a **defect**, not a style note.
   - Fix: check the date, make the weekday and date agree, and add the year. If the event has passed, show that it's over and replace the button.
2. **The hero image is a placeholder.** The 600×330 block (x 340–940, y 92–422) is the largest and highest-contrast thing on the page, so it's where everyone looks first. It shows a generic head-and-shoulders silhouette.
   - Readers know that graphic means "no photo", so the page's strongest spot says "something's missing". It tells them nothing about Ines Varga, the novel or the island, and it makes the whole page, and the booking, feel less trustworthy.
   - This is a **defect** on common-sense grounds; no catalog source covers it. If it's a real photo that failed to load in this capture, check it in a live browser. But this fallback still shouldn't be what visitors see.
   - Fix: use a photo of the author, the book cover, or the island the talk is about. If there's no picture, remove the box so the date and headline lead.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break.** It breaks after "her", splitting "on her new novel". Break it as "An evening with Ines Varga / on her new novel, The Salt Year" so the author and the book each get a line. (Taste.)
- **Book title style.** "The Salt Year" is set roman, the same as the words around it, so only the comma shows where the title starts. Set it in the bold italic. (Convention; it costs little here.)
- **No venue.** Nothing beside the date says where the event is. Add one line, such as "At Margin Books, [street]". This matters more if the shop has more than one branch.
- **Header alignment.** The "Margin Books" wordmark looks a couple of pixels off the nav links' baseline. Measure it in the browser, then put both on one baseline or one midline.
- **No sense of where you are.** "Events" in the nav looks the same as the other links, and nothing on the page ties it to an events list. Mark "Events" as the current section, with weight or an underline rather than colour alone, and add an "← All events" link above the date (Nielsen, visibility of system status).

**What's working.**
- **One column, one left edge.** The image, date, headline, description and button all start at x 340. The eye drops straight from what's on, to when, to what happens, to the button. A single centred column suits a page with one job.
- **Restrained colour and type.** The only coloured thing on the page is the rust date, the fact a visitor needs, and the headline, body and button each have a distinct weight and size. Keep the rust for the date only.
- **The ask.** "Book a free seat" names one exact act and what it costs, and the description ("a short talk about the island… Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm") lets the reader picture the evening before committing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the silhouette is a failed photo load or the intended design. Check the image in a live browser.
- Whether booking shows a closed or past-event state, which matters given the date problem.
- The focus ring on the button and nav links.
