**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is set too loose to read as one statement.** The three lines (x ≈ 180–960, y ≈ 110–305) have baselines about 72 px apart on capitals about 40 px tall. That is roughly 1.3× spacing, which suits body text, not display type. The white bands between the lines are nearly as heavy as the letters, so the headline reads as three strips instead of one confident block. The same looseness spreads to the byline below. It sits about 40 px under the headline and about 38 px above the body, almost the same as the gaps inside the headline. So kicker, headline, byline and body run down the page as one evenly spaced stack, and you can't see where "title" ends and "story" begins. Size and weight still separate the levels, so nothing is misread, but the opening loses its snap. Tighten the headline to about 1.1–1.15× its size, stopping before the "p" of "purpose" touches the line below. Then pull the byline up to roughly half its current gap, and keep or add space before the first paragraph, so kicker, headline and byline lock into one unit. The tight-heading half is taste and common sense, since no approved source covers headings that are too loose. The grouping half is convention: proximity (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), and Bringhurst allows more space on the side of a heading away from the text it belongs to.
