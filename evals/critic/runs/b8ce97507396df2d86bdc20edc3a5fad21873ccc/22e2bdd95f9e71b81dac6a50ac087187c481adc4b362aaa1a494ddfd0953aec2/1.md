**First read.** The eye goes straight to the huge two-line headline, drops to the grey subhead, then to the blue "Start a free trial" button. It reads as the hero of a SaaS landing page for a team planning tool. It feels calm, clean and sure of itself, but empty: the right side and the bottom third of the screen hold nothing, and the product never appears.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing site, meant to get team leads to understand Weekplan and start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The message is clear and the order of reading is right. But the page promises "one honest picture of the next five days" and never shows it, so a sharp claim has to be taken on trust. The empty space where the picture should be makes the screen look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **Show the week the page promises.**
   - **Where:** the hero has no picture. To the right of the headline (from about x 950) and below the button (from about y 540 to the bottom), the screen is blank white. All the weight sits in the upper-left block.
   - **What the visitor experiences:** they read "turns tickets, meetings, and leave into one honest picture of the next five days" and want to see that picture. Nothing shows it.
     - So they can't compare it with the calendar or ticket board they use now, which is what would show them the value on their own terms. The headline becomes a claim any planning tool could make.
     - With nothing on the right to balance the text and nothing at the bottom hinting at more below, the frame leans to the top left. After the button, the eye has nowhere to go. The empty space looks like a picture failed to load, not like a choice (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance).
     - A visitor not ready to commit to a trial has no lower-stakes step. They have to go hunting under "Product" in the nav, or leave.
   - **Fix:** put the real week view in the empty area, beside the text or below the button: tickets, meetings and leave on one five-day grid, ideally with a clash or overbooked day that the visitor's calendar would hide. The picture then proves the claim, and the headline says why it matters. Let the next section start above the bottom edge so the page invites a scroll.
   - **Kind:** defect. It costs the page its proof and makes it look unfinished. If an image is meant to be there and didn't load, check the live page; the finding stands only if the space is empty by design.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline spacing:** "Plan the week your / team actually works" looks set at the default spacing. At this size and weight the word gaps and letter gaps read loose. Tighten the tracking slightly (about −1% to −2%) and the word spaces with it, and stop before letters touch (Bringhurst; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*).
- **Trial terms:** nothing under the button answers the last questions before a click: how long the trial is and whether it needs a card. A short line such as "14 days, no card needed" removes that hesitation. You could also add one quieter text link, such as "See how it works", for visitors not ready to commit.

**What's working.**
- **One message, one ask.** The headline names the job, the subhead names the inputs (tickets, meetings, leave) and the time span (five days), and there is a single button. A stranger can say what this is in one sentence.
- **Clear order of reading.** The headline is about four times the size of anything else and set heavy near-black. The subhead is regular mid-grey. The blue button is the only saturated colour on the page. Keep the blue for actions only, so the button keeps its pull.
- **One strict left edge.** The logo, headline, subhead and button all sit at x≈120, and the nav mirrors that margin on the right. Spacing groups the headline, subhead and button into one unit. Keep this edge when the picture goes in.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Missing image:** whether something (a product shot, or an animation that hadn't run) is meant to render in the empty right side or the band below the button. Check in a live browser.
- **Focus and hover:** whether the button and nav links show a visible focus state.
