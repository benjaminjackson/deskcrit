**First read.** The eye goes straight to the large serif quote, reads all four lines, then drops to the bold name and the grey caption. The "Harbour Notes, issue 14" label at top left is read last. It looks like a pull-quote card from a small local newsletter, and it feels calm, quiet and literary.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a pull-quote card for issue 14 of *Harbour Notes*, meant to be shared or to open the issue. Its job is to give readers one vivid line and tell them who said it.

**Verdict.** It does its job well. The quote is clearly the subject, the words are specific and good, and the type is restrained. Two things hold it back: where the quote's lines break, and a block that sits high in the frame by accident rather than by choice. Neither is serious.

**What would move it most**
1. **The quote's lines break in the middle of the key clause.** Line 2 ends "…the first ferry" and line 3 starts "leaves, and that is when". That splits "the first ferry leaves" between the noun and its verb. At this size the reader pauses on every line. So "the first ferry" reads as finished, and "leaves," can briefly read as the noun (tree leaves) until the reader joins it back to the line above. The line the whole piece rests on gets a stumble in its middle. Fix: break the lines where the sense breaks. The best place is the comma: "…just before the first ferry leaves, / and that is when the gulls get bold." Re-set the lines so they still fall a little uneven. *Taste:* no approved source sets a rule for phrase breaks in display type, but at this size the stumble is real.
2. **The block sits up in the top-left corner and doesn't look placed.** Everything sits between y≈60 and y≈590 and x≈70 and x≈1030. That leaves a top margin of about 60 px, roughly 210 px empty below the caption, and about 250 px empty on the right. The margins don't follow any ratio, so the white space looks left over rather than shaped. The frame reads more like the top of a web page than a finished card. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") explains that a heavy mass needs its position to be resolved against the frame. Fix: choose the vertical position on purpose. Either give the top and bottom margins a clear step (for example 1:1.5 or 1:2, instead of 60:210), or set the block low and leave the open field above it. Keep the left-aligned asymmetry. *Convention* on balance, *taste* on which proportion to pick. This only matters if the piece is a fixed-size card. If it's the top of a scrolling page, it mostly goes away.

**Smaller things.**
- The label, name and caption look about a point apart in size (roughly 15, 17 and 16 px). Weight and colour already separate them, so this isn't a defect. For a tidier type scale, set all three at one size and let bold and grey do the separating.

**What's working.**
- **The hanging opening quote mark.** It sits outside the text edge at x≈70. That lets the "T" line up with "just", "leaves" and "the" on one clean edge at x≈100, the same edge as the label, the name and the caption. That single shared edge is what makes the piece look made.
- **The hierarchy comes from weight and value, not extra sizes.** The quote is a large serif. Under it is a bold near-black name, then a regular grey caption, with the grey label quiet at the top. That order would hold in grayscale. The gap from the quote to the name (about 50 px) is wider than the gap from the name to the caption (about 20 px), so the name and caption read as one unit without a rule or a box.
- **The copy.** The quote is concrete ("the gulls get bold"). The caption adds new facts (5:40, bicycles, bread deliveries) instead of repeating the quote. A stranger learns what this is in one pass.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Contrast of the grey label and caption against white. They look above 4.5:1 by eye, but if this ships as a web card, check the actual hex values.
