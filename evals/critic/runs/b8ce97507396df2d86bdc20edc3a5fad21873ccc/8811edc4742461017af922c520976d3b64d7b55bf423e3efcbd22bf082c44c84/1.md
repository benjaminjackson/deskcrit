**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", drops to the orange "Start your free week" button, then catches on the grey "Keep your streak going" box just below it. After that it drifts to the teal speech-bubble panel and finally reaches the three steps. It reads as a calm, tidy and fairly generic landing page for a language app. The right half of the hero looks hollow.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the marketing home page for a speaking-first language course, meant to get adult beginners who haven't signed up to start the free week.

**Verdict.** The page does its basic job. The promise is concrete, the next step is obvious, and the type and colour are disciplined. But the largest visual on the screen says nothing about the product, and the one other element in the action area talks to someone who has already signed up.

**What would move it most**
1. **The illustration only repeats the headline.** The teal panel (about x 676–1076, y 110–410) is the second-largest thing above the fold. It holds two generic chat bubbles filled with placeholder lines, an icon that would suit a messaging app or a support widget just as well. It shows nothing that sets this product apart: speaking aloud, the four languages, a tutor that listens, or what week ten sounds like. So the right half of the screen carries no proof of the promise, and the page looks templated. Use the panel to show the half of the idea the headline leaves out. The strongest candidate is a real exchange in one of the languages with the tutor's word-by-word feedback, which is already promised in "2. Speak". Then a visitor can see what a lesson is like and judge it for themselves instead of taking the headline on trust. *Taste*, but it's the biggest single gain on the page.
2. **The "Keep your streak going" box is aimed at the wrong person and sits in the wrong place.** It is at about x 56–456, y 390–498, only about 28 px under the button. It shares the button's left edge, corner radius and filled background, so it reads as the quieter second button of a pair. Some visitors will try to click it, and others will think the hero offers two choices. Its heading uses the app's own word "streak" and speaks to someone who already has one, but nobody on this page has started yet. The useful part is the stat: daily learners finish twice as often. That reaches a new visitor only as an afterthought (Nielsen heuristic 2, the user's own words; heuristic 8, cut what competes with the main task; Norman on false signifiers). Take it out of the action area and drop the box. Rewrite it for a newcomer and lead with the result, for example "Practise five days in a row and you're twice as likely to finish." Set it as plain text near the steps, where it backs up the method. *Defect*: it pulls attention from the call to action and makes visitors decode a message that isn't meant for them.

**Smaller things.**
- **Grid:** the hero ignores the three-column grid that the steps row sets up (x 56–413, 461–819, 867–1224). The illustration ends at x 1076, 148 px short of the 1224 right edge that the nav and step 3 hold, so the right side looks unfinished. Put the hero on the same columns, with the picture taking column 3 or running from the column-2 line to 1224.
- **Proportion:** the hero splits into roughly 560 / 400 / 148 px while the row below uses clean thirds. The grid fix above solves this too.
- **Empty space:** it is scattered, not gathered: there is a strip right of the panel and a pocket under it (about y 410–550). Once the panel reaches the margin and the box moves, the space collects into the section break.
- **Section break:** the gap from the grey box to the step rules (about 53 px) is barely larger than the gaps inside the hero, so "how it works" doesn't read as a new section. Make it clearly the largest gap on the page.
- **Near-miss:** the grey box's right edge (x≈456) sits about 5 px short of the column-2 line (x≈461). Align it to a grid line or move it clearly off one. This goes away if the box is removed.
- **Headline break:** the headline breaks as "…conversation in / ten weeks", which strands "in" and leaves the time frame looking like a leftover. Break it as "Hold a real conversation / in ten weeks", using a non-breaking space or a narrower box (common sense).

**What's working.**
- **The offer:** the headline makes a concrete promise with a time frame, and the subhead names the four languages and the method. The button label names exactly what you get and what it costs. Keep that specificity.
- **One accent colour:** burnt orange is used only on the button and is the only saturated colour on the page. Headline to button is an unbroken path. Don't let the orange spread to links or icons.
- **The steps row:** three equal columns, matching hairline rules, bold numbered labels over one line of grey text. "Listen / Speak / Use it" makes the method obvious at a glance. It's the model the hero's layout should follow.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Button contrast:** the white bold label on the orange button looks close to the line, about 4:1 by eye at roughly 18 px bold, which is just under WCAG's large-text size. Check the real hex values against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- **Focus and hover:** I couldn't check whether the button and nav links have visible focus and hover states.
- **Bottom band:** the empty band from about y 630 to 800 is probably the fold. Check a full-page capture before treating it as dead space.
