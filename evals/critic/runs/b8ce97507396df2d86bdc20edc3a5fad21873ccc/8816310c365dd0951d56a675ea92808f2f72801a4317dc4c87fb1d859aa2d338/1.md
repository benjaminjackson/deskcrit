**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big serif quote, drops to the bold "said a volunteer…" line, and gets to the grey paragraph last. It reads as the opening of a local library newsletter story. It feels warm, quiet and a little nostalgic, and it is well made.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a newsletter item telling library patrons that the reading room is reopening, and getting them to come back.

**Verdict.** As a piece of type it is handsome and calm. As news it is upside down: the one fact a patron would act on, that the room reopens Monday, is the smallest and palest text on the page. Someone who reads only the quote and the bold line leaves charmed but uninformed.

**What would move it most**
1. **The news sits at the bottom of the hierarchy.** "The reading room reopens on Monday with new lamps, new shelves, and a table set aside for jigsaw puzzles" (lower left, y≈560–610) is the smallest, lightest text on the page. The bold near-black attribution above it outranks it, even though it only says who spoke. The kicker "LIBRARY NEWSLETTER" names the publication, not the story. So nothing in the top two levels says anything is happening. Anyone scanning a list of newsletter items, or glancing at this card, takes away a nice quote and no reason to come in. Leading with a quote is a fine editorial angle, and the quote earns its place: the room is new, but people still call it "the old reading room." The problem is that the quote does the hook's job and nothing then does the headline's job. A heading should say what its section holds (WCAG 2.4.6). Direction: keep the quote as the display line. Turn the kicker into the story ("Reading room reopens Monday"), or give the reopening sentence the second level and drop the attribution to caption weight. *Defect.*
2. **"Monday" has no date.** The one date in the piece depends on when the reader opens the newsletter. A newsletter is read days later, forwarded and pinned up, so "Monday" can mean this week or next. Someone who picks the wrong one turns up to a closed room. Give the date ("Monday 1 December"), and the opening hours if they changed. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Opening quote mark not hung: the “ sits on the x≈110 margin, so "It's" starts about 40 px in from "room" and "anything" below it. At this size the first line looks indented, right where the eye lands. Hang the mark in the margin, with `hanging-punctuation: first` or a negative indent equal to its width (Bringhurst, on hanging punctuation).
- Grouping by space is backwards: the attribution finishes the quote's sentence (it starts with a lowercase "said"), but it sits about 35 px below the quote's descenders and only about 29 px above the body. So by spacing it belongs with the news. Close the gap from quote to attribution, or open the gap from attribution to body to about double (Arnheim, on grouping by proximity).
- The headline line break "the ‘old’ reading / room" splits the very name the quote is about. Break after "the", or tie "reading room" with a non-breaking space. That also evens the ragged right edge. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- **One clear start, and distinct levels.** The quote is several times larger than anything else, so the eye has no doubt where to begin. Below it, every level differs in more than size: serif against sans, spaced rust capitals for the kicker, bold black against regular grey. Keep those contrasts when you reorder the levels.
- **A restrained palette.** Rust is used only on the kicker, set against cream, charcoal and one grey, so the page feels printed and bookish and the value steps still hold in grayscale. Don't add a second hue, and don't put the rust on the quote marks.
- **Careful details at display size.** True curly quotes with nested singles, a closed-up em dash, the attribution carrying on the quote's sentence, a single strong left edge, and empty space gathered at the bottom right instead of spread thin. At about 90 px a wrong character would glare, and none does.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Where the headline wraps at narrower widths. The line-break and hanging-quote points both depend on it, so check at tablet and phone widths.

What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes, 0 no, 0 unsure
