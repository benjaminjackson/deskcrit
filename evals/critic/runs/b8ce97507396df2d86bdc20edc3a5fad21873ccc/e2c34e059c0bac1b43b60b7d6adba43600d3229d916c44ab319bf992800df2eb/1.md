**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, takes in the one-line explanation, then reaches a row of four identical solid-blue buttons and stalls. Nothing marks "Save changes" as the expected answer, and "Discard changes" looks exactly like the safe choices. It reads as a confirmation dialog in a calendar app. It feels clean and calm up to the point of decision, where it becomes a guessing game.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a modal that asks someone editing a shared calendar to publish, throw away, or keep working on changes that everyone who follows the calendar will see.

**Verdict.** The question and its consequence are clear, but the buttons fail the dialog's one job. Four equal filled buttons give no answer to the question being asked, and they put the button that loses work right next to the safe way out.

**What would move it most**
1. **No primary action: all four buttons are equal.** "Export as file", "Discard changes", "Keep editing" and "Save changes" (y≈298–356) all have the same solid blue fill, white bold label, size and radius. The heading asks a yes-or-no question, but the buttons don't show which one is the answer, so the user has to read all four labels before acting. That matters in a dialog that publishes to other people. Blue is the only accent on the page, and spread over every button it marks nothing. The convention is one filled primary action, with every other action styled lower (Material Design dialogs; Apple HIG; Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). Fix: keep solid blue for "Save changes" only, where it already sits at the right end, and make "Keep editing" an outlined or text button. *Defect.*
2. **"Discard changes" looks like a safe choice and sits beside the safe exit.** At x≈453–644 it is styled exactly like the others and sits about 16 px from "Keep editing", the button someone backing out is aiming for. One slip loses the user's edits, and the copy never says whether a discard can be undone. Fix: give it a destructive style (red text or outline, not a fill), put clear space between it and "Keep editing", and if a discard is permanent, say so or offer undo (Nielsen heuristics 3 and 5). *Defect.*
3. **"Export as file" doesn't answer the question, yet it takes the first spot.** The dialog asks "Save changes?", and export is a separate task. It sits leftmost, where the eye starts reading the row, with full styling and the same 16 px gap as the real answers. That turns a two- or three-way decision into a four-way scan. Fix: move export to the calendar's own menu. If it has to stay, make it a low-weight text link set clearly apart from the decision buttons (Nielsen 8, aesthetic and minimalist design; progressive disclosure, Nielsen Norman Group). *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- The button row starts about 4 px left of the text column (x≈276 against x≈280) and runs past the body line's right end (≈1000 against ≈988). The overlap is too small to look deliberate, so the row looks slightly off. Align the row to the x≈280 edge, and check the exact offset in the CSS.

**What's working.**
- The heading asks exactly the question the buttons should answer, and the body line gives the consequence: everyone who follows this calendar sees the new choir rehearsal times. A first-time viewer knows what's at stake in one read.
- The button labels say what happens ("Save changes", "Keep editing") instead of "OK" and "Cancel". Keep them when you restyle the buttons.
- The type and spacing are tight and calm. There are three sizes with clear jobs, the body line is split from the button labels by weight rather than another size, and the heading sits close to its explanation with a clearly bigger gap before the actions.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Where focus lands when the dialog opens. It should be a safe button, never "Discard changes", with a clearly visible focus ring.
- Whether Esc or a click outside the dialog closes it as "Keep editing".
- Whether there is a dimmed backdrop behind the modal. The area around the card is plain white.
