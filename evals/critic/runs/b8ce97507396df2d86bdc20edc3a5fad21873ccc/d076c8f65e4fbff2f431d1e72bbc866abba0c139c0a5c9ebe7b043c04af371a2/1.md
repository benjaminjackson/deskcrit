**First read.** Your eye lands first on the solid purple "Sign in" button, then jumps up to the red-bordered email field and its red message. It reads straight away as the sign-in card for a product called Quillpost, caught in an email error. It feels calm, plain and familiar. Some would call it templated, but on a screen where nobody should have to think, that's no fault.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's there to get returning users signed in fast, help them fix a mistyped email, and send new visitors on to sign-up.

**Verdict.** It does its main job well. There's one clear action and the error is specific and in the right place. The biggest thing in the way is that the only route to sign-up doesn't look clickable.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Create an account" looks like plain text.** At the bottom of the card, "Create an account" is in the same grey, weight and size as the "New here?" just before it. It has no underline and no colour change, so it reads as one sentence and not as a link. A new visitor came here for exactly that link, and now has to hover around to find it or gives up. It's also inconsistent: "Forgot your password?" on the same card is a link in the brand purple. So the card's two text links look like two different kinds of thing. WCAG 1.4.1 says a link must be marked by more than colour, and here there isn't even a colour difference. Nielsen's heuristic 4, consistency and standards, covers the mismatch. Fix: keep "New here?" grey and set only "Create an account" in the same purple as "Forgot your password?". Ideally underline both, since colour alone marks the forgot-password link too. **Defect.** Returning users aren't affected, but sign-up is.

**Smaller things**
- **Password field border.** The default border on the password field looks like a pale grey-blue on white, roughly 2:1 by eye. That's below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on component edges, so people with low vision or on a washed-out screen may lose the field's edge. Sample the colour to confirm, and darken it until it reaches at least 3:1. The "Password" label above it softens this.
- **Error copy (taste).** The typed address "dana.ortiz@exmaple" is a typo plus a missing ".com". The message gives the right format but makes the user spot "exmaple" on their own. A "Did you mean example.com?" hint would fix it in one click, if the product can detect common domain typos.

**What's working**
- **One clear action.** The full-width purple "Sign in" button is the only filled block of colour on the card. The links are quiet text, so the next step is never in doubt.
- **The error state.** The red border, the message tucked about 10 px under its field with 30 px before "Password", and the wording "Enter an email address in the format name@example.com" together say which field is wrong and how to fix it. The fix doesn't depend on colour alone. Red is the only warm colour on a cool page, so it comes forward without help. Keep that tight-inside, loose-between spacing when you restyle.
- **One edge, a small palette.** The wordmark, heading, labels, fields, link and button all sit on one left edge inside even 40 px padding. One purple, one red and one cool grey range each have a single job. That's why the card reads in a second.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- **Focus or error?** I can't tell whether the red outline on the email field is the error style alone or also a focus ring. In a live browser, check that focused fields and the button get a visible ring at 3:1 contrast, and that the error style doesn't hide focus.
- **Is it a link at all?** I can't tell whether "Create an account" is actually a link in the code, or what its hover and focus states look like.
