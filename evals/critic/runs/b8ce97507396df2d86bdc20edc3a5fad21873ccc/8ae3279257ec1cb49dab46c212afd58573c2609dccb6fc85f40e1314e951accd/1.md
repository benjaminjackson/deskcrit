**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page for a river-ferry spring timetable, for riders who want to know when the ferry runs and how long a crossing takes.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job yet, because the most important line on the page is the hardest to read. Everything else is sound: a clear three-step type scale, one shared left edge, a quiet palette, and plain copy a rider can act on. The colliding headline is the one thing in the way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's lines collide.** In the four-line headline (about x 120–935, y 150–370), the lines are only about 50 px apart on letters about 70–80 px tall. Descenders hit the line below: the "y" and "f" of "Quayside ferry" hit "crossings", the g's of "crossings" and "daylight" hit "spring", and the "p", "g" and "y" of line 3 hit "summer". The viewer sees one dark, tangled block and has to pick out which letters belong to which line, just to learn what the page is about. The overlaps fall wherever a descender happens to land, so this looks like a bug (a `line-height` of about 0.6, or one that gets overridden), not a deliberate choice. Bringhurst (p. 37) allows tight leading in a title only "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide", and Hochuli (p. 28) faults headings set too tight. Fix: open the headline's line spacing to about 1.0–1.1 of its size. It can stay tighter than the body text, but no letter may touch the line below. **Defect.**

- The headline breaks "spring through early / summer", which splits "early summer" and leaves "summer" alone on the last line (y≈310–370). Break by phrase instead, for example "Quayside ferry crossings / by daylight, spring / through early summer", or use balanced wrapping. This is taste.
- The body paragraph ends on a lone "weather." (x 120–200, y≈474). Widen the measure slightly, or join "calm weather" with a non-breaking space (Bringhurst §2.4.2).

**What's working.**
