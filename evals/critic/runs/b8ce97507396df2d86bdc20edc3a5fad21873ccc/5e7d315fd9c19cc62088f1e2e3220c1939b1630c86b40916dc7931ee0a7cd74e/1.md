**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline "Notes from the boat shed" and drops to the large left paragraph. Only then does it reach the right column, which is set noticeably smaller and tighter, like a footnote. Under all three, more than half the frame is blank. It reads as a quiet, literary community page about a volunteer boat shed, and it feels calm but unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web page or note that describes the boat shed and asks new volunteers to come and help.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The atmosphere comes through. The invitation, which is the only thing a reader can act on, is set as the least important text on the page, and it doesn't say enough for a stranger to turn up.

**What would move it most**
1. **The ask is set as small print.** The two paragraphs sit side by side on the same top line, in the same face, weight and ink. The left one ("The shed sits at the end of the lane…") looks about 22 px with loose leading. The right one ("Volunteers meet on Thursday evenings…") looks about 17 px with tighter leading. Nothing else, such as a label, rule, weight or colour, explains the difference, so it reads as a mistake or as a caption to skip. Smaller means "less important", and here that demotes the one paragraph that asks the reader to do something. The mismatched leading also puts the rows out of step across the gutter: by the third line, the right column's text sits level with the gap between two left lines. That makes the spread look like two documents pasted together. A type scale should be a few sizes with obvious steps (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 3.1.1). Elements of one kind should share one style (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Fix: set both paragraphs at one size and leading on one baseline grid. If the volunteer text should stand apart, make it lift rather than shrink: a short heading such as "Come and help", a weight change, or a set-off block at body size or larger. *Defect.*
2. **The invitation doesn't say enough to act on.** "Volunteers meet on Thursday evenings" gives no time and no place. "The end of the lane" means nothing to someone who doesn't already know the lane. There is also no way to get in touch. A stranger who is persuaded still can't show up. The warm detail (the kettle, the spare apron) does the persuading well. What's missing is the plain logistics: a start time, a findable address or "how to find us" line, and a contact or "just turn up". These are the facts a reader will hold you to, so make them exact. *Defect.*
3. **The bottom half of the frame is empty and unshaped.** All the content sits between y ≈ 95 and y ≈ 365. Below it, about 430 px is blank from edge to edge, and the lower right is emptiest because the right column ends first. The page looks top-heavy, as if something failed to load or the layout was never finished. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") says a heavy area needs a counterweight. Large unexplained empty areas are a fault, not breathing room (Tschichold; Müller-Brockmann). Fix: if this is the whole piece, crop the frame to the content or move the text block down so the white gathers around it on purpose. If it's a web page, anchor the bottom with something that belongs there. The volunteer invitation from finding 2, given its time, place and contact, is the obvious candidate. *Convention.*
