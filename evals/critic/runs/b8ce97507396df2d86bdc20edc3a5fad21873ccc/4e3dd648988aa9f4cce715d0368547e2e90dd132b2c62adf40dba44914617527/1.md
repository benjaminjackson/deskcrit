**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers" and then moves down two easy paragraphs. It ends on a smaller last line that looks like fine print. That line is the only one that asks you to do anything. All the text sits in the upper-left of the canvas, and the right side and the bottom half are blank. It reads as a calm, plain public notice that may be unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's an information page that tells ferry passengers how the service runs and what to check before they travel.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is clean and easy to read, but the page doesn't yet do its job. A passenger can't plan a crossing from it, because it has no real times. The one warning that could leave someone stranded is set as the least important line on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times and dates a passenger acts on are vague.** The first paragraph says the ferry runs "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", but it gives no first or last sailing and no departure minutes. A reader can't tell whether 06:00 or 08:00 counts as "early", or whether there's still a boat at 21:30. The winter line (bottom of the block) says "Timetables change in winter" but not when winter starts or what changes. Readers can't tell whether today is affected. Passengers remember times and dates and hold the operator to them, so wording that can be read two ways is a defect, not a matter of style. Fix: give the first and last sailings, or the departure pattern from each landing (for example ":00 and :40 past"), and the date range of the winter timetable. **Defect.**
2. **The winter warning is set as fine print.** "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard…" looks about 16 px, against roughly 20 px for the body. It has the same typeface, weight, colour and paragraph gap. That small drop doesn't make it a separate level. It looks like a body paragraph that came out a bit small, and it tells a skimming reader to skip the only line that asks them to act. A one-step size change with nothing else changed reads as an accident (Dair, *Design with Type*), and the size doesn't come from a scale (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 3.1.1). Fix: set it at body size at least, and mark it as a notice with more than size, such as a bold lead-in ("Winter timetables:") or a set-off note. Never make it smaller than the facts it qualifies. **Defect**, and convention (type scale).

**Smaller things.**
- Visual weight: all the content sits in a block in the upper-left (about x 120–780, y 80–385), and the right 40% and bottom half are empty, so the page looks lopsided and unfinished rather than deliberately open (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Keep the column width, which is good, and either centre the column or give the right side a job, such as the timetable from finding 1, a route map, or the winter notice as a sidebar. If this is a fixed frame, size the frame to the content.

**What's working.**
- **The heading is the clear place to start.** At about 40 px bold over about 20 px regular body, it wins on both size and weight, and "Notes for ferry passengers" tells a stranger at once what this is and who it's for.
- **Comfortable reading.** Body lines run at roughly 60–75 characters with about 1.5 line spacing. They're set flush left on one exact left edge with a gentle rag and paragraphs marked by space only. Keep all of this when you add the times.
- **Restraint.** One typeface and one near-black on white, with no boxes or decoration, suit a practical public notice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether this is a wide-viewport capture of a responsive page. If the column stays pinned to the left at every width, the empty right side grows on wider screens. Check it at a few widths in a browser.
