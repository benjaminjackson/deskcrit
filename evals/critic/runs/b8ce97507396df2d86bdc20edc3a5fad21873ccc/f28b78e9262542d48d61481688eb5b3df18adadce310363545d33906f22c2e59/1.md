**First read.** The eye goes first to the red and orange cells around late morning on weekdays. Then it moves up to the headline and right to the three big numbers. It reads as a support-team report showing when tickets come in. The page is calm and orderly, but the saturated blue-violet of the quiet hours fills about 70% of the grid, so the empty nights look nearly as loud as the busy mornings.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it helps a support lead see when tickets arrive so they can plan staffing by hour and day.

**Verdict.** It nearly does its job. The headline, the day × hour grid and the stat column make one clear idea. Two problems stand in the way. The rainbow scale makes you check the legend to tell busy from quiet. And the "Busiest hour" figure disagrees with the chart beside it, which undercuts trust in the one number a staffing decision would rest on.

**What would move it most**
1. **The colour scale doesn't get darker as the count goes up.** This is in the grid (x≈88–998, y≈212–476) and its legend. One count running from low to high is drawn in seven rainbow colours: periwinkle, cyan, teal, green, yellow, orange, red.
   - Yellow (20–24) is the lightest, brightest step, so the 09:00 and 12:00–15:00 cells stand out about as much as the red 30+ peak.
   - The quietest bin (0–4) is a heavy, saturated periwinkle covering most of the page, so most of the colour goes to hours when nothing happens.
   - The viewer can't see "more" at a glance and has to match each cell to the legend.
   - The steps that separate busy from peak are green, yellow, orange and red. Red-green colour-blind viewers will find these hard to tell apart, and with no numbers in the cells, colour is the only way to read them (WCAG 1.4.1).

   Wilke (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*) calls for a sequential palette for ordered values. The fix is one hue going from light to dark: near-white for 0–4 and darkest for 30+. Then the nights drop back, the weekday late-morning block stands out by itself, and the order works without colour vision. Optionally, print counts in the peak cells. Convention, and a defect in effect, because it misleads the ranking.

2. **"Busiest hour, weekdays: 10:00" contradicts the grid.** This is in the right column (x≈1034, y≈315–370).
   - The hour labels sit at the left edge of their cells, so 09:00 is the column at x≈430–466.
   - That puts the only red 30+ cells (Mon and Fri) in the 11:00 column (x≈506–542).
   - The 10:00 column is orange (25–29) on all five weekdays.

   A reader who checks the big number against the red peak finds they disagree and stops trusting one or the other. Staffing could end up planned around the wrong hour. The 7-step bins could hide a true 10:00 average that is higher, but nothing on the page says so. The fix is to check the figure. If it's right, say how it's worked out ("highest weekday average, 29/hr") and centre the hour labels on their columns so each column clearly belongs to its hour. If it's wrong, correct it. Defect.

**Smaller things.**
- **Stat and chart cover different periods.** "New tickets, September: 3,412" sits beside a subtitle that says "1 to 28 September", so the viewer can't tell whether the total covers the same days as the grid. Use one period for both, e.g. "New tickets, 1–28 September".
- **The title names the topic, not the finding.** "When new tickets arrive" leaves the answer to be decoded from the grid. A subtitle like "Peaks 10:00–12:00 on weekdays; nights and weekends are quiet" gives the reader the answer first. This is taste: common sense for a report someone acts on.
- **The title and the stat figures are almost the same size.** The title looks about 28 px bold and the three figures (3,412 / 10:00 / 42 min) about 26 px bold, so the eye goes back and forth between them. Either make the title clearly bigger or bring the figures down to about 20–22 px.
- **No way to change the scope.** The view is fixed to all queues and one period, and there is no queue or date picker. The stats compare against August but don't let you see August. If this is a live screen, add a light control row above the grid and keep the subtitle in sync with it (Nielsen, heuristic 7). If it's a static report, this is fine as it is.

**What's working.**
- **Form and copy fit the question.** A days × hours grid is the right shape for "when do tickets arrive". Weekday versus weekend and morning versus night show as a shape at once, and the subtitle states the measure, scope and period in one line.
- **The neutral page around the chart.** There's no accent colour on the nav or the stats, so the heatmap is the only colour on the screen. The stat column holds its place through weight alone, and each stat's label, number and comparison form a tight group with clear space between stats. Keep the shared left edges (x=40 for text, x=88 for chart and legend) too.
- **Comparisons and a source note.** Each stat comes with a comparison to August, so the reader can tell good from bad without looking anything up. The source line says which clock the times are in and that hours mean when a ticket was opened, not answered, which heads off the most likely misreading.
