**First read.** My eye lands on the headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", which is the right place. Then it gets pulled two ways: down to the purple "Keep your streak going" block and right to the large teal panel. The orange button sits between them and has the least area of the three. It reads as a clean, friendly language-app landing page for adults. It feels competent but generic, and slightly confused about who it's talking to.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing page for a language-learning app. Its job is to get a first-time visitor to start the free trial.

**Verdict.** The structure and type are sound, but the hero doesn't make its one ask clearly. A loud purple block written for existing learners sits right under the trial button and competes with it. The large illustration takes up half the hero and adds nothing the headline hasn't already said.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple "Keep your streak going" card competes with the trial button and talks to the wrong person.** It's a saturated, rounded block stacked directly under "Start your free week", and it's larger than the button. The two look alike, so they read as two buttons, and the visitor has to work out which one is the action. The copy makes it worse. "Keep your streak going" speaks to someone who already has a streak, but on a sign-up page nobody does. Its stat, "finish the course twice as often", has no comparison point: twice as often as whom? **Defect.** Material Design and Apple's HIG both say one action per view should be dominant. Here a non-action carries as much weight as the action. Fix: take it out of the hero. If the streak stat is worth keeping, rewrite it for a newcomer ("Five minutes a day is enough: learners who practise five days in a row are twice as likely to finish") and set it as quiet supporting text, not a filled block.
2. **Three strong hues with no system.** Orange (the button), purple (the card) and teal (the panel) each fill a large area, so no colour means "this is the action". The page reads as assembled from parts rather than designed as one piece. **Convention.** Refactoring UI and Material's colour roles call for one or two brand hues, and Apple's HIG reserves the accent for the main action. Fix: keep orange for the action only, turn the illustration panel into a muted tint or a neutral, and drop purple.
3. **The illustration repeats the headline and takes up half the hero.** Two outline chat bubbles on a flat teal rectangle just say "conversation" again. The headline makes a strong claim (a real conversation in ten weeks), and this space could prove it: a snippet of an actual lesson exchange, the tutor's word-by-word feedback, or what a learner can say in week ten. **Taste:** none of my approved sources states this rule. My argument is that it's the biggest object on the page and it's empty, so the visitor learns nothing from it and the page looks like anyone's product.
4. **The copy leaves gaps where the visitor will make decisions.** "Practice with a tutor that listens": is the tutor a person or software? That answer changes what the visitor thinks they're buying. "Ten weeks" from what starting point, and at how many minutes a day? "Start your free week" doesn't say what happens on day eight: will I be charged, and is a card needed? These lines are about price and commitment, so a second reading is a defect, not a style note. Fix: name the tutor plainly, give the daily time, and put one line under the button ("No card needed" or "Then $X/month, cancel anytime").

**Smaller things.**
- The teal panel's right edge (about x=1076) matches nothing else. The nav and the three-step row run to about x=1224. Either extend the panel to that edge or align it to a column.

**What's working.**
- The headline is a specific, outcome-based promise, set big and tight. It clearly leads the page.
- The Listen / Speak / Use it row is excellent: three short steps with verbs, numbered, under thin rules, aligned to the page's outer edges. It explains the product better than anything above it.
- The type is restrained. There are about four clear levels (headline, subhead, step headings, body), and the sizes are well apart from each other.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty band below the steps is probably just where the viewport ends. Check in a live browser that the next section starts close enough below to show the page continues.
- Whether the purple card is clickable. If it is, it's a second competing action; if it isn't, it looks like one anyway.
