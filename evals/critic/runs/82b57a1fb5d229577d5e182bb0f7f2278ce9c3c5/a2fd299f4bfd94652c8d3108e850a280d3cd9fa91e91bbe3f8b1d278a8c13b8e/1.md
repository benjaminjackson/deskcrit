I wrote the critique below. Checking the numbers: the rates match the table (286/9,210 = 3.1%, 38/1,120 = 3.4%), so the content is correct. The problems are in how the chart presents it.

**First read.** The eye goes straight to the four purple bars and settles on Growth's 4.0%, then up to the title. The page looks like a tidy, trustworthy SaaS analytics screen. The first conclusion it plants is that Growth converts far better than Starter, by roughly two and a half times.

**What it's for.** Assumption: an internal analytics page where product and growth staff compare Q3 2025 trial-to-paid conversion across plans and decide where to act.

**Verdict.** The page is well made, but the chart gives a false picture. It is a bar chart with its axis starting at 2.5%, so it shows a gap of under one percentage point as a huge one. It also treats Enterprise's 38 conversions as being as solid as Starter's 286. Anyone who decides from the first read will decide on a distorted picture.

**What would move it most**
1. **The bar axis starts at 2.5%, so the bar lengths are wrong** (defect, convention). Above the baseline, Starter's bar covers 0.6 points and Growth's covers 1.5, so Growth's bar is about 2.5× as tall. The real ratio is 4.0/3.1, about 1.3×. That makes the visual difference about five times the real one (Tufte's lie factor). The labels on the bars give the right numbers, but the shape is what people remember and repeat in meetings. A bar's length is its value, so bar axes start at zero (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 17.1; Few). The fix: start the axis at 0%. If the small differences are the point, use dots on a non-zero scale, which is an honest way to zoom in.
2. **Small plans are shown as precisely as large ones** (defect). Enterprise's 3.4% rests on 38 paid accounts and Growth's 4.0% on 118. By a rough by-eye estimate, Enterprise's real rate could be anywhere from about 2.3% to 4.5%, and Growth's from about 3.3% to 4.7%. The ranking of Growth, Team and Enterprise may just be noise. Only Starter's lower rate looks solid. The table on the right has the counts, but nothing links them to how confident the reader can be. The fix: add error bars or a range to each bar, or at least a short note such as "Enterprise based on 38 conversions" (Wilke, ch. 16).
3. **The page shows the data but doesn't say what it means** (taste, common sense, and Few for the comparison). "Trial to paid conversion by plan" only names the topic, and there is nothing to compare against: no Q2 figure, no target. The reader can't tell whether 3.1% is good, bad or slipping. The fix: give each plan a comparison, such as last quarter as a ghost bar or a delta under each label (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Then retitle the chart with the finding, for example "Starter converts lowest; other plans within noise of each other". Choose the wording only after the uncertainty in item 2 is shown, so the title doesn't claim more than the data supports.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart title "Trial to paid conversion by plan" and the table title "Trials by plan, Q3 2025" sit a few pixels off each other's baseline even though they line up across the page. Put both column tops on one line (Müller-Brockmann).
- The table header "Paid" is ambiguous: paid accounts, or paid revenue? "Converted" or "Paid accounts" says exactly what it counts.

**What's working.**
- The definition block sets out exactly what counts as converted and what happens to trials still open on 30 October. That is the kind of note that stops two teams from arguing about two different numbers. Keep it.
- Labelling the bars directly, keeping the gridlines light, using one colour and ordering the plans by tier puts nearly all the ink on data, with no legend to decode.
- The table backs up every rate with raw counts that add up, so careful readers can check the chart for themselves.
