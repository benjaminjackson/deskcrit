**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers in the top row, then drops to the orange bars and stops on Saturday, the tallest one. It reads as the admin overview for a small bakery that ships by subscription. It looks calm, tidy and competent, but generic. When I read the numbers as a stranger, none of them tells me what it means: 3.8 what? 64.20 in what currency? 12.4 subscribers?

**What it's for.** My assumption: the owner of Crumbwell Bakes opens this each morning to see at a glance how the shop is doing and whether anything needs attention.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the chart is honest, but the most prominent part doesn't work. The three headline numbers have no units, no period and no comparison, so the owner sees big figures and can't tell whether they're good, bad or even what they count. Until that's fixed, the page looks like a dashboard but doesn't tell the owner anything.

**What would move it most**
1. **The KPI row has no units, period or comparison.** This is the three cards at the top. It is a **defect** and Jev also flagged it (0.98).
   - The units are missing. "3.8" has no %, so it could be read as 3.8% or as a ratio. "64.20" has no currency. "12.4" under "Active subscribers" can't be a head count, since there is no 0.4 of a person. It is probably 12.4k, but the viewer has to guess, and might read it as 12.
   - There's no period. Is this today, this month, or all time? The only date on the page is "September 2026" in the chart's source line. If the cards cover a different window than the chart, the owner will compare them as if they were the same.
   - There's no comparison: no change from last period, no target, no trend. So a falling conversion rate looks exactly like a rising one, and the owner can't tell whether anything needs action.
   - Fix (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"): put the unit, the time period and a comparison next to each headline number, such as "▲ 0.4 pts vs August" or a small sparkline. The numbers are the visual anchor of the page, so they have to be the most complete sentences on it.
2. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** This is "Orders by delivery day". The chart shows a clear story: orders climb through the week, peak on Saturday at about 1,130, and drop by about two-thirds on Sunday (about 380). The title leaves the owner to work that out. This is **taste / common sense**, but it costs something here, because the reason to look at delivery days is to plan baking and staffing. Fix: say the finding in the title or a one-line caption, for example "Saturday is your busiest delivery day; Sunday is a third of it." If Sunday is low because you deliver less that day, say so, so it doesn't read as a problem.

**Smaller things.**
- There's no date-range or period control anywhere on the page, so the owner can't look at a week or compare months. Add a period picker near "Shop overview" that drives both the cards and the chart, which also fixes the mismatch above (Nielsen, heuristic 7).
- The page doesn't point the owner to anything needing attention: no alerts, no pending orders, no "what changed". Even one line under the title, such as "12 orders to pack for tomorrow", would make the overview a place to act from, not just to read (common sense).
- Jev: "A headline number has no unit, period, or comparison". This is the KPI row, covered in finding 1.

**What's working.**
- **The chart is honest and quiet.** Bars start at zero, the gridlines are faint, there's one colour, the days run in natural order, and there's a source line with a date. Keep all of that when adding the takeaway.
- **The structure is clear.** One page title, three equal cards on a shared grid, one chart panel below on the same outer edges. Spacing is consistent and the eye path runs top to bottom with no detours.
- **The brand orange is used sparingly**, only on the wordmark and the bars, so it ties the data to the shop without shouting.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Keyboard focus on the top nav links.
- How the three-card row reflows on a narrow screen. The owner may well check this on a phone.
