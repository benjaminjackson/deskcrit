I've read the reference entries and am writing the critique now.

**First read.** There's nowhere for the eye to land. It starts at the top left, "Changing your pickup time", and reads that line as the first sentence of a paragraph, not as a heading. It takes a moment to see the pattern: four short questions-and-answers about collecting a bakery order. The tone is calm, plain and friendly, but the page feels unfinished, like a text dump.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's an FAQ or help panel for a small bakery's order-pickup service. A customer scans it for their one question and needs a clear answer about what they can do and by when.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The headings are set no stronger than the answers, so you can't scan a page that exists to be scanned. Several answers also leave out the detail a customer would hold the bakery to.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings don't read as headings.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two look slightly *smaller* than the answer text below them (about 17 px against about 18 px), in the same regular weight and the same near-black. Someone scanning for "cancel" has to read every line, because nothing marks where a topic starts. Nothing on the page is seen first, and there's no page title saying what this is. *Defect* (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance by differences of size; Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9: get contrast from more than size). Fix: make the headings clearly stronger than the answers. A semibold weight at the same size or one clear step up usually does it, or keep them regular and push the answers back to a mid-grey. Add a title above all four ("Collecting your order") so the eye has a start.

2. **The answers leave out what the customer will hold the bakery to.** These lines set deadlines and rules, so a reading that can go two ways is a defect, not a style note:
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead": a day ahead of what? The order date or the pickup slot? Is it 24 hours, or the end of the day before? It also never says *how* to cancel. A customer who reads "the day before" and cancels at 9 pm may find the bread already baked.
   - "along with a name": whose name, yours or theirs? If the collector gives their own name and the counter expects yours, they leave empty-handed.
   - "holds orders for two hours after the chosen time": then what? Is the order lost, refunded or charged? That's the part the customer cares about.
   - "Ask the baker if you are unsure": allergies are the highest-stakes topic here, and the answer only works if you're already in the shop. Someone ordering online can't see "a card by the till".

   Fix: in each answer, say who does what by when, and what happens to the customer after that. For example: "Cancel up to 24 hours before your pickup time from your order page. After that we've already baked your bread, so we can't refund it." For allergies, link to the ingredients online or give a phone number.

3. **The answer lines run too long.** The single-line answers ("Give the person your order number…" and "Orders can be cancelled…") run to about 95–100 characters. The column is about 820 px wide, which puts a lot of text on one line. Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style* §2.1.2) gives 45 to 75 characters. At this width the eye loses its place, and the right two-thirds of each line is where the deadline details sit. *Convention, and it costs something here.* Fix: narrow the column to about 600 px, or about 65 characters.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the Allergies answer. Narrowing the column will probably fix it; if not, rebreak the line (Bringhurst §2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- The voice is plain and human, with sentences like "the bread has already been baked for you". Keep that voice when you add the missing details.
- The spacing already groups each heading with its answer (about 10 px heading-to-answer against about 25 px between topics). Keep that ratio when the headings change.
- The type is one clean sans in one colour with good contrast. Fix the hierarchy with weight, not with more typefaces or colours.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Open your order" is a link and whether the page reflows on a phone. Check both live, because most customers will read this on a phone.
