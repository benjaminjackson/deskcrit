**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top, 184,220, 3,912 and 2.12%, with "Overview" just above them. It then drops to the large chart, which shows three coloured lines all rising. Nothing on the chart says what any line is or what the numbers are. It reads as a clean, calm, fairly standard growth dashboard. That calm is still there when you reach the chart, but the chart tells you nothing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly analytics overview for a marketing or growth person. They should be able to see how traffic and sign-ups are doing over the last 12 weeks, which channel drives the change, and whether anything needs action.

**Verdict.** The top half works. The chart, which takes up over half the screen, has no axis values, no dates and no series labels, so it can't answer the question its title asks. The KPI row also seems to contradict itself on conversion. As it stands, the dashboard can't be trusted to drive a decision.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read: no series labels, no axis values, no dates** (defect). In the "Visits by channel" panel you can't tell which line is which channel: blue, orange and green have no legend and no labels. The y-axis has gridlines but no numbers, so you can't tell the volume or whether it starts at zero. The x-axis has no week labels. The viewer gets "three things went up" and nothing else: not which channel leads, not by how much, not when the dip near the middle of the blue line happened. Every chart needs labelled axes with units, and the reader should never have to guess what a mark measures (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). There is an empty gutter of about 70 px on the left and about 75 px along the bottom, so labels were probably meant to be there and failed to render. Check this in a live browser. If they do render, this finding shrinks to item 2 below. Fix: add y-axis values with units (visits per week), week dates along the bottom, and the channel name at the right end of each line.
2. **The series are told apart by colour alone, with no key** (convention, WCAG 1.4.1). Even with a legend, telling orange from green depends on colour vision. Under the most common colour-vision deficiency those two can come close. Put the label directly on each line, at its right end where the space already exists. Then the line doesn't depend on colour and the reader doesn't have to look back and forth (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*).
3. **The conversion delta doesn't add up** (defect). Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups up 2.4%. That means conversion must have fallen, from about 2.20% to 2.12%, roughly −0.08 pts. The card instead says "+0.1 pts", in green. A viewer who does the sum stops trusting every number on the page. One who doesn't comes away thinking conversion improved when it got worse. Check the calculation or the sample data. If conversion fell, show it as a fall.
4. **The chart title names the topic, not the finding** (taste). "Visits by channel" says what the chart is. The person checking this weekly wants to know what changed, for example "Paid search drove most of the growth". With only three lines over 12 weeks, one line of takeaway text or a note on the biggest mover would do more than the chart itself. No approved source states this, so it's my judgement. It matters more here because the chart currently says nothing at all.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards: three numbers at one clearly dominant size, each with a plain label above and a comparison to the previous period below. That's the right structure. Keep it, and make sure the deltas are correct.
- The layout is restrained and consistent: one grid, matching card styles, even gutters and a quiet neutral palette. Colour is saved for data and status, so the chart lines and green deltas stand out without competing.
- "Last 12 weeks" in the header and "vs previous 12 weeks" on every card use the same period, so the scope is never in doubt.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart's axis labels and legend exist and just didn't render. The empty gutters suggest they were meant to be there; check live.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is a date-range control. If it is, it looks like plain text right now and needs a control shape, such as a border and a chevron.
