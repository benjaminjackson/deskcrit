After that it drifts into the empty right half of the band, takes in three short feature columns, and runs out of page with a third of the screen still blank. It reads as a landing page for a personal budgeting app. It feels clean and confident, but it could be any fintech startup's page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing page for a consumer budgeting app, meant to get first-time visitors to start a free trial. You told me nothing about it, so correct me if that's wrong.

**Verdict.** The words do their job: the headline and subhead say clearly what the product does for you. The page around them doesn't. The hero promises "see where every dollar goes" and then shows nothing, and the remaining space is either a slab of full-strength blue or empty white. The biggest problem is the missing product image. A visitor has to take the core promise on trust when they could have seen it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero shows nothing, and the right half is a dead zone.** About 45% of the blue band (right of x≈800) is empty. The headline is a visual promise ("*see* where every dollar goes"), and the page never shows what you'd see: no category bars, no "you're at 85% of Dining" alert, no screen at all. The text block also makes the band lopsided, with all the weight on the left and nothing on the right to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
   - **Why it costs:** the visitor has to imagine the product before deciding to try it. That's the one thing a budgeting page shouldn't ask, because seeing the tool is what makes people trust it.
   - **Fix:** put a product view on the right that shows the subhead's three steps: categories, a limit per category, and the heads-up before you go over. Use real-looking numbers.
   - **If an image is meant to be there:** a picture that failed to load in the capture would explain the gap. Check it in a live browser. As captured, it's a hole.
   - **Kind:** defect.
2. **Half the screen is fully saturated blue.** The band runs full width and about 400 px tall in a vivid, fully saturated blue (Jev flagged this at 0.99). At that size the colour tires the eye and stops marking anything. The white primary button only stands out because of the blue around it, not because of the colour itself. Saturated colour should mark what matters, with larger areas kept muted (Itten, *The Art of Color*, contrast of saturation; Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
   - **Mood:** for money software, an electric band reads more "launch" than "calm control of my spending".
   - **Fix:** fill the band with a deep shade or a light tint of the blue, and keep the full-strength blue for small things like the primary button.
   - **Kind:** convention. Whether it hurts the mood is my taste.
3. **The page stops dead after the features.** Below the feature row there are about 230 px of empty white with nothing to lead the eye on. The three features are the last thing on screen, and they're small, grey and unillustrated, so the first screen ends weakly. There's no second ask, no proof, and nothing that says there's more below.
   - **Fix:** use the space for content: a trust line next to "Read-only access" (bank-connection security, number of users) or the start of the next section, so the fold invites a scroll. If the capture caught the page mid-load, check it live.
   - **Kind:** defect.
4. **"Try it free" asks for trust instead of letting the visitor judge.** It's an ask that could sit on anyone's page. It doesn't say what you'd do in the first minute or what you'd get.
   - **Fix:** name the first concrete result from the visitor's own world, such as "Connect a bank and see last month's spending". The visitor can then compare it with what they already know about their own money.
   - **Kind:** taste. The subhead already has the specificity this button lacks.

**Smaller things.**
- **Feature headings:** bold black heads over grey body. This is fine, but without a small icon or figure each column is a wall of words. One number apiece would help, for example under "Shared budgets".
- **Type sizes:** the subhead (~20 px), feature heads (~19 px bold) and button labels (~17 px) are close in size. They hold up because weight and colour separate them. If you change the scale, make the subhead clearly larger than the feature heads rather than nearly the same.
- **Jev:** a large area filled with vivid, fully saturated colour. This is the hero band, covered in finding 2.

**What's working.**
- **The copy:** the subhead says who does what in three concrete steps: "Connect your accounts, set a limit for each category, and get a heads-up before you go over." Keep this exact specificity.
- **The buttons:** "Try it free" is a solid white button and "Watch the tour" an outlined one. The difference is clear, with one primary action.
- **"Read-only access … can never move money":** this names the visitor's real fear and answers it in their own words. It's the strongest trust line on the page, so consider moving it up near the call to action.
- **The grid:** a strict 64 px left edge runs from the logo through the headline to the first feature column, and the columns are even. The page feels orderly.
