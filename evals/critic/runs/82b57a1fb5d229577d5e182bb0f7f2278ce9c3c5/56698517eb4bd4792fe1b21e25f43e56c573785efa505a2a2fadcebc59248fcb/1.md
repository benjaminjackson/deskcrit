**First read.** The eye hits the row of six coloured blocks before anything else. The page title is small enough that it reads like a caption. Then the eye drifts across the tiles without landing anywhere. It reads as a gallery template for a garden show with the photos not yet in: six flat colour fields, each with the same pale circle. It's tidy, calm and empty.

**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is a web gallery page that lets visitors to a garden show browse its areas (Rose arch, Herb bed and so on) and pick one to look at.

**Verdict.** As it stands it doesn't do the job. A gallery is its pictures, and none of the six tiles shows a garden, so nothing invites a click and nothing tells the areas apart except a word and a colour. Second to that, the page has no top: the page title is smaller than the card titles under it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The images are placeholders, not pictures (defect).** All six tiles are one flat hue with an identical centred circle. Someone browsing "Herb bed" or "Pond edge" sees nothing of thyme or lilies. The colour is the only difference between tiles, and it's arbitrary: violet for an orchard, mustard for a glasshouse. The captions do all the work, so the picture and the title never make one idea together. If these stand in for photos that didn't load, this is a capture problem and not a design problem. Check it in a live browser. If they are the design, put in real photos of each area, cropped to one consistent framing. The colour fields and circles should go: they carry no information, and each circle's bottom edge just touches the bottom of its tile, which looks like an accident (taste).
2. **The heading hierarchy is upside down (defect).** "Garden show gallery" (top left) looks about 20 px semibold. The card titles ("Rose arch", "Bean rows") look about 22 px bold, so they're slightly bigger and heavier than the title of the page they sit on. The two sizes are close enough to read as a mistake rather than two levels (Bringhurst: sizes come from a scale), and the page has no starting point. Six equal tiles under a weak title means no one element stands first (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show logical order by differences of size). Give the page title a clearly larger step, for example roughly 1.5–2× the card titles. Or, if this sits inside a site with its own page header, make the card titles visibly smaller than whatever heads the page. Three levels are enough here: page title, card title, description.

**Smaller things.**
- The first card's text breaks the pattern. "Rose arch" sits about 2 px higher than "Herb bed" and "Pond edge", and its description sits about 4 px higher. That's a near-miss that throws the first row's baselines out of line (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Give all six cards one caption spacing.
- The gap between tile and title is tight (about 8 px) next to the roughly 60 px between rows. Grouping still works, but the titles look jammed against the images. A few more pixels above each title would let the caption breathe without blurring the groups (taste).

**What's working.**
- The three-column grid is clean: equal tile widths, a consistent gutter, a firm left edge shared by the title and first column, and generous outer margins. Keep it when real photos go in.
- The caption pair is right: a short bold name, then one plain grey sentence ("Climbing roses over the east gate.") that says what's actually there. The descriptions are concrete and finished, and the grey still looks readable on white.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are links, and how hover and focus look on them. That matters if the gallery opens detail pages.
- Whether the flat tiles are images that failed to load. Check them live.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (visual and headline make one idea; every element does a job; repeated elements look the same)
What matters first: 3 lines, 1 yes, 2 no (one element dominates; text levels distinct)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (no near-misses; baselines across columns)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 10 yes, 2 no (every image shows content; crops look intended)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: skipped, no visible controls
