**First read.** The eye goes to "Changing your pickup time" only because it's first. The four bold headings are identical, so after that the eye just runs down the list. Within a couple of seconds it reads as a small bakery's help page for click-and-collect bread orders. It looks calm and plain, but it could be anyone's page: there's no bakery name, no page title, and nothing that marks it as a particular place.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help or FAQ page for people who have ordered bread online to collect in the shop, and that they come to it with one specific question.

**Verdict.** The layout works: headings sit with their answers, the type is clean and readable, and nothing gets in the way. What lets it down is the copy. The two answers people will act on, cancelling and allergies, are vague exactly where precision matters. The page also has no title to say whose page this is.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways (defect).** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time before the day of pickup. Someone with a 9 am Saturday slot who cancels on Friday evening is right under one reading and too late under the other. They'll hold the bakery to the reading they chose. Give the exact cut-off as a time ("until 6 pm the day before your pickup") and say what happens after it: is the order charged, or can it still be moved? The section also doesn't say how to cancel, while the first section does ("Open your order…"). Use the same pattern here.
2. **The allergy answer only helps people who are already in the shop (defect).** "Every loaf has a card by the till" gives the information to someone standing at the counter. Your readers are ordering online. Someone with an allergy can't check before they buy, and has to order and travel first, or ask a baker who may be busy. Put the ingredients and allergens online, at each product or behind a link here. Keep the card as the backup. If that information can't go online, say so and give a phone number. Allergy information is the one answer on this page with a safety cost, so it shouldn't be the vaguest one.
3. **Nothing sits above the four headings, so the page doesn't say what or whose it is (defect).** All four headings are the same size and weight, which is right for parallel questions. But nothing stands above them: no page title, no bakery name, no "Your order" context. Someone who lands here from search or a link has to piece together from the answers that this is about bread orders, and from which shop. Add one dominant title ("Collecting your order from [Bakery]") so there's somewhere to start, and let the four headings sit under it as they do now (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: logical order shown by differences of size; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should be self-evident).
4. **The other two answers leave gaps the reader has to fill (defect).** "Along with a name": whose name, the person who ordered or the person collecting? A friend who gives the wrong one could be turned away. Say "your name" or "the name on the order". "Holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" stops at the fact and doesn't say what it means for the reader: if you're later than that, is the bread sold, given away, or refunded? Add that consequence. "Choose a new slot" doesn't say until when you can change it. If the cut-off matches cancellation, say so.
5. **The lines are too long (convention).** The one-line answers ("Give the person your order number…" and "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead…") run to about 95–100 characters, well past the 45–75 Bringhurst recommends (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). On a page of short answers this costs less than it would in long text. But the column takes up three-quarters of the frame for no reason, and the right third is empty anyway. Narrow the text column to about 65 characters, roughly 640 px at this size.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." under Allergies, and "after the chosen time." under Changing your pickup time, sit alone on their own lines. Narrowing the measure will move both breaks, so check them again afterwards (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- Grouping: each heading sits close to its answer, about 10 px away, with about 45 px between sections. You can see the four question-and-answer pairs at a glance without any rules or boxes. Keep those proportions when you change the width.
- The heading and body differ in both size and weight (bold against regular) in a single sans family, with near-black on white. That's two clear levels and nothing extra.
- The headings say what the reader is trying to do ("Collecting for someone else") rather than naming a category, so people can scan straight to their question.
