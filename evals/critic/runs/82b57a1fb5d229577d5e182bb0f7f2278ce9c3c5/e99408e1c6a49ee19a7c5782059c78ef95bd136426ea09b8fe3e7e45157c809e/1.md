**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title, then goes straight to the heavy black grid, which is darker than anything inside it. It reads at once as a simple one-direction ferry timetable for the public. It feels plain and trustworthy, but more like a ledger than a timetable. The cage of lines registers before the times do.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a passenger opens this to find the next boat from North landing and check that it runs today.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The content is clear and the type is clean. The main problem is the drawing: a heavy box around every cell outweighs the times, and it boxes every row the same way, so the morning, midday, afternoon and evening groups and the depart-arrive pairs don't show. The second problem is that the copy leaves out when the timetable is valid.

**What would move it most**
1. **The grid is louder than the data** (convention, with a real cost). Every cell sits inside a near-black rule about 2 px wide, the same weight as the text strokes. The first thing you see is a 4×8 cage, and finding "the next boat after 12:00" means reading through the bars. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*) says a table's rules "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell". Tufte's data-ink argument says the same: structure that carries no information should give way. Fix: drop the vertical rules and the outer frame. Keep thin, pale horizontal dividers or alternate pale row tints, and let column spacing do the separating, so the times are the darkest thing on the page.
2. **The groups don't show** (defect). The table spells out "Morning" twice, "Midday" twice and "Afternoon" twice, and every row gets an identical box. So the Period column is mostly repetition, and the time-of-day structure it exists to show isn't visible. Also, "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" belong together as one trip, but they sit about 300 px apart in separate boxes (Palmer, common region; Arnheim, proximity). Fix: show the period once per group, as a group label with extra space or a slightly stronger divider between groups, and drop the repeated word from each row. Narrow the columns to fit their content so departure and arrival read as a pair. The table doesn't need to fill 1080 px for four short columns.
3. **"Spring season" doesn't tell anyone if the timetable is valid today** (defect in the copy). A timetable's dates are something passengers act on and later hold the operator to. "Spring season" could mean March to May, the operator's own season, or this year versus last year, and anyone reading it near the edge of the season can't tell whether to trust the times. Fix: give the actual dates, such as "Runs 1 March to 31 May 2027", in the subtitle.

**Smaller things.**
- The table only shows North landing to market quay. If the return sailings exist elsewhere, say where in one line under the table. If they belong here, a passenger planning a round trip is stuck with just this one direction. (common sense)
- The "Runs" column mixes Daily, Weekdays and Weekends as plain words with the same weight. That's fine, but the two weekday-only and one weekend-only sailings are the exceptions a passenger can get caught by. They could carry slightly more weight than "Daily" so they stand out on a scan. (taste)
- The times look left-aligned and even. Confirm they are set in tabular figures (Inter's `tnum`) so the columns stay true as the digits change (Bringhurst 4.4.3).

**What's working.**
- The type: one sans family in three clear levels (bold title, grey subtitle, bold column heads over regular body) with no near-duplicate sizes. Keep that scale.
- Rows are in time order with 24-hour times, and each row gives both departure and arrival. A passenger can find a sailing without doing any arithmetic.
- The plain neutral palette suits a public-service timetable. Don't add colour to fix the hierarchy; spacing and lighter rules will do it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the table reflows on a phone. Four columns spread over 1080 px will need to collapse or scroll, and the vertical rules will make a cramped version worse.
