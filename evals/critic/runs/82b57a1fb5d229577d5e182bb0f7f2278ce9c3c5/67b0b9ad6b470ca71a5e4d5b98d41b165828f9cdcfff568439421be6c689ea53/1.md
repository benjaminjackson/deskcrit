**Verdict.** The craft is clean and the chart is honest, but it shows four numbers and doesn't help the viewer judge them. It gives no finding, no comparison with a target or an earlier period, and no sign of how reliable the numbers are. The definition of "converted" can also be read two ways. **What would move it most**

1. **The page shows rates but gives the viewer nothing to judge them by.** The title "Trial to paid conversion by plan" names the topic, not the finding. The bars show only Q3 2025, with no Q2, no same quarter last year and no target. So the viewer can see that 3.1% is below 4.0%, but not whether either one is good, or getting better or worse. Few's dashboard pitfalls name exactly this: a bare number without a comparison can't tell good from bad. Add a comparison to each bar, such as a ghosted previous-quarter bar or a target line. Then rewrite the title to state what the viewer should take away, e.g. "Growth converts best; Starter lags by a point". *Defect (convention).*

2. **The bars look more different than the data supports.** Enterprise is 38 paid out of 1,120 trials. At that size, 3.4% has a margin of roughly ±1 percentage point. Growth (118 of 2,950) is about ±0.7. Those ranges overlap with each other and with Team. The 0.9-point spread from 3.1% to 4.0% is therefore partly noise, but bars plus bold value labels make every gap look solid. A viewer who moves budget toward Growth or questions Enterprise pricing on this basis may be reacting to chance. Add confidence-interval whiskers to the bars, or at least the n under each plan label, and flag Enterprise as a small sample (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). *Defect.*

3. **"Converted" can be read two ways.** The subtitle says paid "within 30 days". The Definition says "Trials still open on 30 October are counted as not converted."
   - **Reading A:** each trial gets its own 30-day window, and 30 October is just when the last window (from 30 September) closes.
   - **Reading B:** there is one cut-off date for everyone. A trial started in July then gets about four months to convert, while one started in late September gets 30 days.

   Under B, the rates mix different windows, and plans with more trials early in the quarter look better. The viewer can't tell which it is, and this is the number they will quote. Fix it with one sentence that says who is counted and by when, e.g. "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice is paid within 30 days of the trial starting." Drop or reword the 30 October line so it can't be read as a fixed cut-off. *Defect.*
