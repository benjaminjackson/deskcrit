**First read.** The eye lands straight on "Learn to swim". It's huge, bold and near-black, and you can't miss it. It drops to the grey subhead, then to two small, quiet blocks below. Then it runs into a lot of white and has nowhere to go. It reads as the top of a swimming pool or swim school website. It feels calm and clean, but it also feels like the page stopped loading halfway through.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the first screen of a landing page that should get nervous adult non-swimmers to sign up for the Tuesday evening class.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do that job yet. The page says what's on offer but gives no way to act on it. There's no button, no time, no price, no place, not even the pool's name. And the two blocks under the headline sell different things from what the headline promises.

**What would move it most**

1. **There is no next step.** *(Defect.)* Under the two blocks the page just ends: no button, no link, no booking detail. The viewer reads "Small adult classes on Tuesday evenings" and still doesn't know what time, how much, where, or how to get a place. Anyone who was persuaded leaves with nothing to do. The page should be clear about what it is, what the visitor can do and where to click within seconds (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). The eye path should run from headline to action without hitting a dead end (Arnheim). The fix: put one specific ask directly under the subhead, such as "Book a trial lesson, Tuesday 7pm", and next to it the facts people act on: start time, price, how many people per class, and the venue. That's what turns a curious reader into a booking.

2. **The headline and the blocks under it are about different things.** *(Defect.)* "Learn to swim" plus the subhead set up an adult lesson. Then the only two blocks are "Join the lanes" (open lane swimming) and "Family hour" (kids in the shallow pool). Neither is the class you just pitched. The viewer can't tell if this is a swim school or a general pool page, and the adult learner, the person the headline spoke to, gets no more detail. Decide what the page is for. If it's the lessons page, the space under the subhead should hold the class details: what a first session is like, who it's for, what to bring. Lanes and family hour then become smaller links further down. If it's the pool's home page, the headline should name the pool and the lessons become one of three equal offers.

3. **The lower half and right side read as missing content.** *(Defect for the viewer; partly taste.)* Everything sits in the top-left 55% of the frame. The bottom 45% and the right quarter beyond the cards are empty. Gathered empty space works when it pushes against something dense (Ruder). Here it's set against nothing, so it doesn't read as a choice. It reads as something that didn't load, or a third card that was dropped. Arnheim's point holds: the heavy top-left has no counterweight. Fixing #1 and #2 will fill much of this space. Whatever is left should be on purpose, for example a picture of the pool or the coach in the water, set to the right of the headline.

**Smaller things**
- Type scale: I count four sizes: headline about 90 px, subhead about 22 px, card headings about 20 px, card text about 16 px. The subhead and the card headings are only a couple of pixels apart and look like one level picked twice (Bringhurst, §3.1.1). Make the card headings either clearly smaller and set apart by weight (medium or semibold), or clearly larger than the subhead.
- Mood: everything is grey on white with no hue anywhere. Calm suits nervous beginners, but nothing says water, warmth or a person. This is taste: one accent colour or one photo would make the page feel like a place rather than a wireframe (Itten).
- Copy: "with three speeds to choose from" leaves the reader to work out that it means slow, medium and fast lanes. Say that.

**What's working**
- The subhead copy: "with a coach in the water beside you" is specific, and it speaks straight to an adult's fear of the deep end. That's the best line on the page. Keep it, and build the ask on it.
- The headline is clearly the one thing that leads, by a wide margin of size and weight. The order of importance is never in doubt at the top.
- Everything shares one left edge at about 96 px, and each card heading sits tight to its text. The grouping is clean, so adding content won't need the structure redone.
