**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline "Tell us about your project", then moves right to the white card with three empty fields. This is the contact page of a small design studio: calm, plain, competent. Then the eye stalls at the bottom of the card. "Send message" looks like a caption, so for a moment there's no visible way to send the message.

**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is a studio's contact page meant to turn a prospective client into a sent enquiry.

**Verdict.** The page is clear and well set, but it fails at its one job. The submit control doesn't look like a control, so the form appears to have no way to send. Everything else here is minor next to that.

**What would move it most**
1. **The send action looks like plain text** (defect). "Send message" at the bottom of the card is set in the same face, size and colour as the body text. It has no fill, no border and no underline. The visitor fills in three fields, looks for a button, finds none and has to guess that the words are clickable. Some will give up at the last step. It also breaks the page's own system: every other clickable thing (the nav, the footer links) is blue and underlined, so the one thing that matters most is the only clickable thing that isn't marked. NN/g (Moran, "Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty") supports the risk. That clickable things must look clickable is otherwise common sense. Fix: make it a real primary button with a filled shape in the page's blue, aligned to the left edge of the fields. It should be the strongest thing in the card.
2. **The brief is in the wrong place** (taste, by Nielsen's "recognition rather than recall"). The intro asks for "a budget range and a rough timeline", but that request sits in the left column, about 300 px from the Message box. The box itself is blank, with no prompt. Most visitors read the headline, jump to the form and write "Hi, we'd like a website". The studio then has to email back for the two facts it asked for. Fix: put the prompt where people type. Use a hint under the Message label ("Tell us the budget range and timeline, if you know them"), or add two short optional fields for them.
3. **The form doesn't say which fields are required** (convention, Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). Name, Email address and Message carry no asterisk and no "optional". A visitor who only wants to leave an email can't tell whether that's allowed until the form rejects them. Fix: if all three are required, put an asterisk after each label and explain it once at the top of the card. Or mark the optional fields with "(optional)".

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Contact" is drawn exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing shows which page you're on. Give the current item one cue, such as bold, no underline or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Headline: "Tell us about your / project" leaves "project" alone on the second line and splits the noun phrase. Break after "about" or widen the column so it sits as "Tell us about / your project".
- The card's right edge (about x 1068) lines up with nothing. The nav ends about 156 px further right at x 1224, so there's a dead band on the right that looks left over, not chosen. Either run the card to the right margin or make the two columns a deliberate split of the content width (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).

**What's working.**
- The headline speaks to the visitor, and the line under it makes a concrete promise: "We read every message and reply within two working days." Keep that sentence. It's the most persuasive thing on the page, because it answers "will anyone actually reply?"
- Every field has a bold label above it, sitting closer to its own field than to the next one. That's the right form pattern; keep it.
- The type scale is tight and the palette is restrained (neutrals plus one link blue). The page looks trustworthy without trying hard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How big the click area of "Send message" is, and what focus looks like on the fields and the action. Check both in a browser once the button exists.
