**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline "Tell us about your project" at top left, moves right to the white form card, and drops straight down to the blue "Send message" button. Within two seconds it reads as the contact page of a small design studio, aimed at prospective clients. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: nothing on the page could only belong to Fernwick.

**What it's for.** My assumption: to get a prospective client to send an enquiry the studio can act on.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. It is clear, well ordered and easy to fill in. The biggest problem is that the intro asks for a budget and a timeline but the form gives no place for them. Many enquiries will arrive without the two facts the studio said it wants.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form doesn't collect what the copy asks for.** The intro says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The form has only Name, Email and a short blank Message box, about five lines tall, with no hint inside it. By the time the visitor is typing in the box, the request is in a grey paragraph across the page, and most people will forget it or write something vague. This is a defect: it costs the studio the information it needs to reply usefully. Fix: add optional Budget (a set of ranges) and Timeline fields, or at least put the prompt inside or under the Message box. This is common sense, not a cited rule: help should sit at the step it concerns (Nielsen, heuristic 10).
2. **The field borders look too faint.** The pale grey outlines on the three inputs look like about 2.5:1 against white. WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 for the edge that shows a field is a field. On a page whose whole job is filling in fields, a viewer with low vision or a dim screen sees a white card with labels and no clear boxes. This is a convention, and breaking it costs something here. Measure the border colour against #FFF and darken it until it reaches at least 3:1.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Contact" looks the same as the other links, so nothing shows which page you're on. Mark it with one cue, such as a bolder weight or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Form: no field is marked required or optional. Put an asterisk on Name, Email and Message with a one-line key, or mark the optional fields (Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- Headline: "project" sits alone on line 2, which leaves a lopsided first line and a stub below it. Break it as "Tell us about / your project", or widen the column so it fits on one line.
- Grid: the form card ends at about x=1068, while the nav and header rule run to about x=1224. The content column and the header don't share a right edge, so the empty strip on the right looks left over rather than planned. Align the card's right edge to the nav, or narrow the header to match the content.
- Copy (taste): "Tell us about your project" could sit on any agency's site. That is acceptable on a contact page, but one line in the studio's own voice about what kind of work it takes on would make this page feel like Fernwick's.

**What's working.**
- "We read every message and reply within two working days" is a concrete promise. It names a time the visitor can hold the studio to, and it lowers the risk of writing. Keep it.
- The form is set the right way: labels above the fields, one column, a tight gap between each label and its field and a larger gap between fields, and one clear primary button. Grouping it in a single card keeps it apart from the intro without extra decoration.
- The type scale is clean: about four sizes with clear jobs, one blue for every link and action, and underlined links, so links are marked by more than colour.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button. Check in a browser that they are visible and at least 2 px thick at 3:1.
- Whether the Email field uses `type="email"`, and how errors appear after a failed send.
