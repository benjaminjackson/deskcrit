**First read.** The eye goes to the purple "Sign in" button first, then up to the email field, the only thing on the card outlined in red. It reads at once as the sign-in screen for a product called Quillpost: calm, tidy and familiar. The second look raises the question the screen never answers: why is the email field red?

**What it's for.** Assumption: the sign-in screen of a web app, shown here in its failed-validation state, for returning users and for new users looking for sign-up.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and conventional, and as an idle screen it works. As an error state, which is what this capture shows, it fails. The email field turns red and says nothing, so the user has to work out on their own that "exmaple" is misspelled and the address has no domain ending.

**What would move it most**
1. **The email error is shown by colour alone, with no words** (defect). The red border on the email field is the only signal. Someone who can't tell red from the grey border on the password field gets no signal at all. Everyone else knows something is wrong but not what, and the address "dana.ortiz@exmaple" has two problems: the typo and the missing ".com". WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.1 requires the error to be identified in text, and SC 3.3.3 requires the message to say how to fix it when the fix is known. Fix: put a short message right under the email field, in red with an icon as a second cue, that says what is wrong and what a valid entry looks like. For example: "Enter a full email address, like name@example.com." Keep the red border as the second cue. The space between the email field and the "Password" label has room for one line.
2. **"Create an account" doesn't look clickable** (defect). The sign-up link at the bottom is set in the same grey, size and weight as "New here?" beside it, so it reads as one plain sentence. A new user scanning for a way in has nothing to find. "Forgot your password?" is at least purple. Fix: give "Create an account" the link styling "Forgot your password?" already has, and preferably underline both, so neither depends on colour alone (WCAG Techniques G183/F73; NN/g on flat UI).

**Smaller things.**
- The password field's border looks pale grey on white, probably below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for input edges. Measure it, and darken it if it falls short.
- Jev also flagged the colour-only error. It is finding 1, the red border on the email field.

**What's working.**
- One clear primary action. The full-width filled purple button is the only strong shape on the card, so nothing competes with it.
- Labels sit above their fields, close to their own field and well clear of the one before, so each label obviously belongs to the field below it. Keep this spacing when you add the error message.
- A restrained palette. One purple carries the brand, the button and the links, which leaves red free to mean "error" without any doubt.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the red border is the error state or a focus ring. If focus is also red, or only a colour change, the two states can't be told apart. Check focus in a live browser for a visible indicator at least 2 px thick with 3:1 contrast.
