**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the landing view of a web analytics dashboard. It's for a marketer or founder checking traffic and sign-ups over the last 12 weeks and deciding where to look next.

**Verdict.** It fails at what a dashboard is for. The chart takes about two-thirds of the screen and can't be read: it has no legend, no line labels and no axis values. The conversion card also reports a rise that the page's own numbers rule out. The look is fine. The information is where it breaks.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read.** This is a defect and a convention. The "Visits by channel" panel shows three lines (blue, orange, green) with no channel names, no y-axis values and no week labels on the x-axis. You can see that one channel is biggest and all three are climbing. You can't tell which channel is which, how many visits any of them has, or which week a dip falls in. The blue dip near the middle and the orange zigzag are exactly what someone would want to ask about, and they can't. The empty strip left of the plot and the band under the baseline look like space kept for labels that were never drawn. Tufte (*Visual Display*) and Few say every chart needs labeled axes with units, so the reader never has to guess what a number measures. Fix: put the channel name at the right end of each line, in the line's colour. Tufte and Wilke prefer labels on the data to a legend you have to match up by colour. Add a few y-axis values (visits per week) and week labels along the bottom. The direct labels also fix the next problem: right now the lines differ only by colour, so anyone who can't tell orange from green has nothing else to go on (WCAG 1.4.1).
2. **The conversion card contradicts the other two cards.** This is a defect. Visits are "+6.1%" and sign-ups "+2.4%". When visits grow faster than sign-ups, conversion has to fall. Working backwards, the previous period was about 173,600 visits and 3,820 sign-ups, which is about 2.20%. That means 2.12% is down about 0.08 points, yet the card says "+0.1 pts" in green. The viewer will act on this number and later hold the product to it. Either the arithmetic is wrong or the periods behind the deltas differ, and a reader who spots it stops trusting every card. Fix: work out the delta from the same two periods as the other cards. If it's negative, show it as a decline in the decline colour.

**Smaller things.**
- **Green means two things.** In the cards it means "up", and in the chart it's one channel's colour (Apple HIG, Material: keep status colours for status). Give that series a hue that isn't a status colour.
- **The chart title only names the topic.** "Visits by channel" states the subject, not the finding. Something like "Organic drove most of the growth" would tell the viewer what to take away. This is my taste, and less important on a dashboard than on a report, but the page currently says nothing in words.
- **"Last 12 weeks" doesn't look like a control.** At top right it's plain grey text. If it's the date-range picker, give it a border or a chevron so it looks clickable. If it's only a label, it's fine.
- **The chart has no labels at all.** This is the problem Jev flagged with 0.95 probability: no labels, no axis values, no legend. It's covered in finding 1.

**What's working.**
- **The stat cards.** Each one has a label, a big number, and a comparison to the previous period, which is exactly what a headline figure needs (Few). They sit evenly on one grid with clear weight steps from label to figure to delta. Keep that pattern.
- **Restraint.** One typeface, three or four clearly different sizes, light gridlines with no heavy rules, and lines over time, which is the right chart for this data. Once the labels are in, the chart's styling itself needs no changes.
