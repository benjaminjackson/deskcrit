**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web gallery that shows visitors photos of the plots and features at a garden show, with one featured item.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound and could ship once real photos go in. But as shown it fails its one job: a gallery with no pictures shows the viewer nothing. The other real problem is the very wide image slots, which will fight the subjects they're meant to hold.

**What would move it most**
1. **Every picture is a placeholder (defect).** All six slots (Rose arch, Herb bed, Pond edge, Bean rows, Glasshouse, Orchard path) are a flat colour field with a light circle cut off at the bottom edge. A viewer gets six coloured rectangles and six captions describing things they can't see. On a gallery page the pictures *are* the content, so the page currently delivers none of it. It's fine only if these are deliberate wireframe stand-ins and real photos load in the live page. Judge the design again with real photos in place, because everything below depends on them: whether the hero actually wins the page, and whether the captions still read against busy images. (Common sense; the checklist has no catalogued source for this.)
2. **The image slots are very wide letterbox strips that will crop the subjects badly (taste).** The hero is about 770×307 px and each small tile about 377×144 px, roughly 2.5:1. That shape suits a horizon. It doesn't suit a rose arch, which is a tall subject: either the top of the arch or the gate goes. The same goes for orchard trees and beans on canes. The viewer will see a slice of each thing rather than the thing. Make the hero closer to 3:2 or 4:3. Its caption block can still end level with the right column, because the column's height can follow the hero rather than the other way round. Alternatively, choose and crop each photo for the slot, not the slot for an average photo.

**Smaller things.**
- Lower right of the hero: the line "Climbing roses over the east gate." and "Reeds and water lilies." in the right column sit almost on the same line but a few pixels apart. That reads as a near-miss. Either line them up exactly or move them clearly apart.
- Title copy: "Garden show gallery" names a category, not *this* show. A stranger can't tell which show, where, or what year. Name it, for example "Ashby Garden Show 2026", so the page says what makes it different.

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** The hero spans exactly two of the three bottom columns. The gutters are the same everywhere (about 14 px). The hero's caption ends level with the bottom of the right column. Keep this structure when you change the image proportions.
- **One clear feature.** The hero is twice the width of any other tile, and "Rose arch" has its own larger heading size, so there's no doubt where the gallery starts.
- **A short, distinct type scale.** I count five sizes: the title, the hero heading, the hero line, the tile titles in bold and the captions in grey. The two smallest are only about a point apart, but they're told apart by weight and colour, not size, which is the right way to do it (Refactoring UI).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the coloured blocks are images that never loaded rather than intended placeholders. Check in a live browser.
- Whether the tiles are links, and what they do on hover or focus.
- How the three-column grid reflows on a phone, especially whether the hero caption stays with the hero.
