**First read.** Three loud, equal slabs of orange, green and purple. The eye lands on "Learn to swim" because it is top left and the warmest colour, then slides across to "Join the lanes" and "Family hour" with no reason to stop on any of them. It reads as the "what's on" section of a local pool's website. It feels bright and friendly, but the bottom half of every card is solid colour with nothing in it, and there is nothing to click.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a section of a swimming pool's website, and its job is to get adults and families to pick a programme and go on to book or find out more.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. It tells you three programmes exist, then stops. Nothing leads to a next step, and the three cards are equal in every way, so nothing tells the viewer where to start. The cards look half-finished: more than half of each one is empty colour.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no next step.** The cards are the whole message, and they offer no button, link, time, price or "book" line. The eye reads three sentences and hits a dead end in the empty lower half of each card. If the whole card is a link, nothing shows it: no arrow, no underline, no button shape. A visitor who wants Tuesday lessons has to guess whether to click the card or look elsewhere on the page. *Defect.* Give each card one exact action at the bottom, in the space that is now empty, and make it look clickable. Make the action specific to the card, such as "Book a Tuesday class" or "See lane times", not a generic "Learn more" (NN/g, Moran, "Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention"; the reference marks this source as only partly confirmed, so treat it as common sense).
2. **The three cards compete and none wins.** The cards are the same size, the headlines are the same weight, and all three are saturated colours of the same strength. In grayscale the cards are three nearly identical mid-dark blocks. Nothing says whether this pool's main offer is lessons, lanes or families, so the eye bounces between them (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size). Jev flagged this too, at 0.91 and 0.87. *Convention, and it costs something here.* If one programme is the one the pool most wants to fill, make it dominant: a wider card, or the only saturated one, with the other two set back. If they truly are equal, keep three cards but quieter (see 3), and let a section heading above them be what the eye lands on first.
3. **The colour is all volume.** Saturated colour covers most of the content area, in three unrelated hues with no neutral to rest on. The warm orange pulls the first card forward for no reason the content gives. Spread across the whole surface like this, the colour stops signalling anything. *Taste*, partly backed by Refactoring UI and the Apple and Material guidance on keeping accents in reserve. Keep the colour coding, but shrink it: a coloured band, the headline colour, or a tinted card with the strong hue kept for the action.
4. **The cards are mostly dead space.** About 55% of each card's height is empty fill below three lines of text. This is not white space that rests the eye. It is padding that makes the cards look like their content never loaded. *Defect* (it reads as broken or unfinished). Either fill that space with the content the viewer needs (when, who, cost, the action) or shorten the cards to fit what is in them.

**Smaller things.**
- The row of cards sits left of centre in the frame: the left margin looks like 56 px, the right like 96 px. Centre the row or run it full width so the right-hand gap doesn't look accidental.
- The copy is pleasant but doesn't give the viewer the facts that would get them to act. "Every morning from six" has no end time and "three speeds" has no names. Add the specifics people decide on, such as days, times, price and booking.
- White text on the orange and green looks close to the 4.5:1 minimum. Measure both, especially the orange.

**What's working.**
- The headlines are verbs and plain nouns that say who each card is for ("Learn to swim", "Join the lanes", "Family hour"). Keep that voice.
- There are two type sizes with a big step between them, and both are bold and tight, so the headline and body never blur together. Keep that scale.
- The body copy speaks to the reader ("a coach in the water beside you") and ends on what that means for them. That is the strongest line on the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are already links, and whether they have hover or focus states. Check in a browser. If they are clickable, the visual cue is still missing (finding 1).
