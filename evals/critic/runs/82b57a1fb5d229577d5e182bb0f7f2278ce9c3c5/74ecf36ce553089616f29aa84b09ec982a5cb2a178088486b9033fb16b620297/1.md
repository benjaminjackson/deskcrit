**First read.** The eye lands on "184,220" at top left, moves across the three number cards, then drops to the blue line at the top of the chart. It looks like the overview screen of a SaaS marketing-analytics tool. It feels calm, tidy and competent, and generic: nothing on the screen tells you what to look at or what changed.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing dashboard for a marketing or growth lead who wants to check, in a few seconds, whether traffic and sign-ups are on track over the last 12 weeks and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly does its job, but the chart is the weak point. It doesn't add up to the headline number, it doesn't say what time unit it counts in, and it doesn't tell you what it shows. So the largest thing on the screen takes the most work to read and is the easiest part to distrust.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't add up to the Visits card, and it doesn't give its time unit.** Read by eye, the three lines add up to roughly 9.5k a week at the start and 13.3k at the end. That's about 139k over 12 weeks, against 184,220 on the Visits card. About 45k visits, a quarter of the total, are missing from a chart called "Visits by channel". The y-axis (0–8k) also never says whether these are visits per day or per week. Anyone who adds it up, or holds it next to the card, will think one of the numbers is wrong, and on an analytics product that costs trust in every other number. *Defect* (convention: Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few, *Show Me the Numbers*: the reader should never need to guess what a number measures). Fix: either add the missing channels (Direct, Referral, Other, with Other in grey), or retitle the chart to say it shows a subset ("Weekly visits, top 3 channels"). Put the unit in the title or on the axis either way.
2. **The chart gives the topic, not the finding.** It fills two-thirds of the screen to say "Visits by channel". The point a viewer needs is something like "Search is driving the growth: up about 36% since July, while Social is flat-to-noisy". They have to work that out by comparing slopes. *Taste* (common sense; the usual source for this rule isn't on the approved list): on an overview, the biggest element should answer the question the viewer came with. Fix: give a title or a one-line caption that states the finding, and if possible mark the change on each line, such as an end value and the percent change.
3. **The legend sits away from the lines.** The colour key is at top left, but the lines end at the right edge with empty space after them. To tell orange from green you have to look up to the legend and back. *Convention* (Tufte, *Visual Display*; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*): label at the data. Fix: put "Search / Social / Email" at the right-hand end of each line, with its last value, and drop the legend. This also removes any reliance on telling orange from green by colour alone.

**Smaller things.**
- "Overview" (top left) names the page type, not what's on it. A heading that sets the frame ("Last 12 weeks: visits up 6.1%") would do more work, or cut it and let the cards lead. *Taste*.
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top-right header is plain grey text where a date-range picker would normally sit. If it's the control, it needs a button or dropdown look (border and chevron). If it's only a label, it's fine. *Defect if it's clickable*.

**What's working.**
- The number cards are right: label, big number, then a comparison with a sign and the period ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"), so no number stands without context. Using "+0.1 pts" for the rate, instead of a percent of a percent, is a careful touch. Keep it.
- The type scale is tight and disciplined: about four sizes, each with a clear job (header, page title, KPI figure, label/caption), all in one family. The three KPIs share one size and weight, which correctly tells you they are equals.
- The chart is honest and quiet: the y-axis starts at zero, the gridlines are faint, there's no chartjunk, and the lines are the right form for change over time.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and whether the chart has hover tooltips with exact weekly values. The second matters, because the chart gives no exact figures.
