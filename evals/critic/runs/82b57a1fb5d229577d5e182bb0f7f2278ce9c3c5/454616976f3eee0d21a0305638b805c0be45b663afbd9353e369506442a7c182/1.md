**First read.** My eye lands on the three big figures across the top, $4.82M first, then drops to the blue bar chart, which takes up most of the page. It reads as a revenue dashboard for a subscription business, aimed at a founder or finance lead. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and also familiar: it looks like a lot of other SaaS dashboards.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the overview screen of a billing product, where an owner or finance person checks in a few seconds how revenue is doing this year and where it comes from.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid and the screen mostly works. The serious problem is that two figures both labelled as 2025 revenue disagree by about $330K. On a finance screen that undermines trust in every number on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan table and the chart give two different totals for 2025 revenue.** "Revenue by plan, 2025" totals $4.82M, which is exactly the "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" card. But the monthly bars, captioned "recognised revenue", add up by eye to about $4.49M (roughly 312 + 328 + … + 447K). A reader who checks the table against the card or the chart either decides one of them is wrong or has to work out that the table is really ARR by plan, not revenue. Both readings cost you: if it's ARR, the heading is wrong; if it's revenue, the figures are wrong. On a screen people use to report numbers to others, this is a defect, not a style note. It also fails WCAG 2.4.6, because the heading doesn't name what the section holds. Fix: give the table the metric it actually shows ("ARR by plan, Dec 2025"), or change its figures to recognised 2025 revenue so the total matches the chart. *Defect.*
2. **The chart takes up most of the page but doesn't say what it shows.** "Monthly revenue, 2025" names the topic and leaves the reader to find the story: monthly revenue rose from about $312K to about $447K, roughly 43%, with small dips in April and July. This is the screen's biggest element, and a title that states the finding ("Monthly revenue up 43% from January to December") would let the reader take it in at a glance. A short note on the April and July dips would answer the first question anyone asks. *Taste. The usual source for this rule is not on my approved list, so I'm arguing it on its own terms: the chart's size promises a finding, and the title doesn't deliver one.*

**Smaller things.**
- The "Revenue by plan, 2025" heading sits about 4 px lower than "Monthly revenue, 2025" beside it, so the two section heads almost line up but miss. Put them on the same baseline (Arnheim: a near-miss reads as an error).
- The "Jan to Dec 2025" chip in the top right reads as a static label: it has no chevron or calendar icon, and its pale fill barely shows against white (WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for control edges with no other cue). If it is a date picker, give it an affordance and a visible edge.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI card has a unit, a period and a comparison ("+18% vs Dec 2024"), and each delta has a + sign as well as the green, so it doesn't rely on colour alone. Keep this pattern for any new metric.
- The chart is restrained: one hue, faint gridlines, an axis from zero, units in the subtitle and a source line naming "closed invoices only". It's honest and easy to read.
- The type scale is short and every level has a clear job (KPI figures, page title, section heads, body, labels), and the plan figures are right-aligned so they compare cleanly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date chip opens a picker, and the focus states in the sidebar navigation and the chip.
