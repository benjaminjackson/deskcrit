**First read.** The eye lands on the serif "How can we help?" in the navy band, drops straight to the white search field, then scans the eight cards left to right, top row first. It reads as the help-center home page of a software product. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, but it could be anyone's help center: swap the name in the header and nothing changes.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the front page of a product's help center. A customer with a question should get to the right article in one step, by search or by topic.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The hierarchy is clear, the search comes first, and the topic grid is clean and easy to scan. The page's weakness is what it says, not how it looks. It doesn't make clear what product it serves or what its content is called, and it gives the most common and most urgent visitors nothing better than a blank box.

**What would move it most**

1. **The page never settles what the product is or what its content is called.** In the cards, "Set up your workspace and invite your team", "Integrations" and "Connect calendars, chat tools" describe a collaboration tool. "Orders and shipping: Track a delivery or start a return" (top right) describes a shop. A visitor has to guess whether they're in the right help center, and someone with a shipping question may wonder whether "Billing and invoices" or "Orders and shipping" is theirs. The words drift too. The nav says "Guides", the subtitle says "search the guides", the field says "Search for articles", and the section says "Browse by topic". That's three names for one body of content, so the viewer has to work out whether guides and articles are different things. Defect (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, ch. 2, conceptual model; Nielsen heuristic 2, match with the real world). Fix: if this product really does both, say so in the subtitle, e.g. "Help with your Harbor workspace and your orders". If it doesn't, remove the stray card. Pick one word for the content ("guides" or "articles") and use it in the nav, the subtitle and the field.

2. **Nothing above the fold gives a shortcut for the common or urgent case.** Below the search there is only the topic grid. There are no popular articles, no example searches under the field (e.g. "Try: reset password, change plan"), and no sign of service health. Yet the visitors under the most pressure are the ones with something broken right now. For them the page offers "Status" and "Contact us" as 15 px grey nav links at the far top right, and "Troubleshooting" as the last of the eight cards. A blank search box also makes the visitor phrase the problem in your words before they get anything back. Taste and common sense (no approved source states this): a help center earns its keep by answering the top few questions in one click. Fix: add a short row of top questions or example searches under the field. Give the "something is broken" path (status, troubleshooting, contact) one visible route in the first screen instead of spreading it between the nav corner and the last card.

**Smaller things**
- Card descriptions: six of the eight end on a single word alone on the last line ("your team.", "recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages."), so the grid looks ragged at the bottom. Tighten the copy or the card padding so each description fits on one line or splits evenly over two (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- Top nav (Guides, Community, Status, Contact us): nothing marks which section this page is in. If this page sits under one of them, mark it with a single cue such as bold or an underline (Nielsen heuristic 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- "Browse by topic" (~18 px bold) and the card titles (~16 px semibold) look almost the same size and weight, so the section heading barely reads as a level above its cards. Make it clearly larger, or set it apart in colour or case (Bringhurst, "Don't compose without a scale").

**What's working**
- The order of importance is exactly right for a help page. The headline is the only serif and the only large type, the search field is the only white shape on navy, and the cards come after. The eye goes question → search → browse with no detours. Keep that one-dominant-band structure.
- The topic grid is well built: an even 4×2 layout, matching gutters, a consistent order inside each card (icon, title, one-line description), and one consistent outline icon set. The descriptions say what is inside each topic in plain terms ("Passwords, two-step sign-in, and recovery"), not just the category name. Keep that pattern for any new topics.
- The palette is restrained: one navy plus neutrals. It holds its structure in greyscale and leaves room for a status colour later without a clash.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the whole card is clickable or only the title, and whether cards and search show hover and focus states. The card borders are very faint on the pale grey ground, so the hover cue will have to carry the sense that a card is clickable.
- What sits below the fold. If popular articles appear just below 800 px, finding 2 shrinks to "move them up".
