**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the text is too pale to read comfortably. That one defect is the main thing stopping the page from doing its job. The only line that should change what a passenger does is also the faintest and smallest thing on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **All the text below the heading is too faint to read well.** Defect. Both paragraphs and the winter note are set in a light cool grey on white (it looks like about #9ca3af). By eye that is roughly 2.5:1, which is at the bad end of the range. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 asks for 4.5:1 for normal text, and this is normal-size text, so neither paragraph qualifies for the 3:1 allowance for large text. Readers with low vision, or anyone reading on a phone outdoors at a landing, will struggle. Even good eyes have to work for it. It also flattens the page: below the heading everything is one pale value, so in greyscale there is no structure except size (Albers; Refactoring UI). Fix: darken the body text until it passes 4.5:1. A mid-to-dark grey around #4b5563 or darker would pass, but check the exact colour with a contrast checker. Keep the near-black for the heading only.
2. **The one warning is the quietest line on the page.** Defect, in the copy and its ranking. "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel." is the only line that tells passengers to do something, and the only one that can leave them stranded. It is smaller and just as pale as the descriptive text above it, so it reads like a footnote. Fix: give it at least body size and full-strength text, and lift it with weight or a short label such as "Winter timetable". Move it up next to the "every forty minutes" sentence, because that is the claim it qualifies. Also tell readers in which months the change applies and where else they can check. As written, it sends them to the landing to find out whether they should have gone to the landing.
3. **"Early morning and late evening" is too vague for a timetable.** Defect, in the copy. Passengers plan around the first and last crossing, and these words can be read as 6:00 or as 8:00. Someone who guesses wrong misses the last boat. Fix: give the first and last departure times from each side, or link to the timetable. The same goes for "every forty minutes": say what time the departures run from, so readers can work out the next boat.

**Smaller things.** None beyond the above.
