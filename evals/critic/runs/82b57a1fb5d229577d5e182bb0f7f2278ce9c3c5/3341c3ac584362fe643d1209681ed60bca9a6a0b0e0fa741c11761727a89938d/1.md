**First read.** The eye lands on "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", the only thing on the page with any weight. It then drops down through the paragraph and the three short lines to the blue button. It reads as the top of a website for a small ferry company aimed at day-trippers and locals. It feels calm, clean and trustworthy. It also feels unfinished: everything sits in the upper-left 60% of a white screen, and there is no picture of a boat, the bay or anything else.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top section of a ferry operator's home page. Its job is to get a first-time visitor to see the crossing as easy and pleasant, and then to find a departure.

**Verdict.** The type and order are sound, and the headline is good. But the page holds back the one thing a ferry visitor came for, which is when the next boat leaves. It also leaves half the screen empty, so it feels like an unstyled template rather than a place you'd want to go.

**What would move it most**
1. **The answer is behind the button.** The visitor wants to know when they can go. The paragraph says only "every forty minutes from early morning until late evening", which doesn't let them plan anything, and the real times are a click away under "See the timetable". Put the useful answer on this screen: the next departure, or first and last boats with actual times ("First boat 6:40, last 22:20, every 40 minutes"), and the fare if it's simple. Then the button confirms a plan instead of starting one. Naming the actual place helps too: "north landing" means nothing to a stranger, so say which town or pier. *Defect:* it slows the main task and leaves the viewer guessing. *Common sense*, not a cited rule.
2. **Half the screen is empty, and that makes it look like a template.** All the content sits in a narrow column on the left, and nothing on the right or below balances it. The page feels like it's tipping left, and the empty area reads as leftover space rather than open air (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). For a ferry, the obvious counterweight is a photo: the boat, the open deck, or the harbour sliding past, which the copy already describes. That would make the "calm minutes" promise something the viewer can see, not just read. If a picture is meant to sit there and didn't load, this finding is about the capture, not the design. *Defect* (balance), plus *taste* on the photo.
3. **The list repeats the paragraph.** "Bring a bicycle" is followed by "Bicycles travel free", and "open deck" appears twice. Three lines of nearly the same size and colour, with no markers, say almost nothing new, and they push the button further down. Either fold the free bikes into the sentence ("bicycles ride free") and cut the list, or use the list for facts the paragraph doesn't give: price, journey time, and whether you need to book. Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: cut the words that don't earn their place. *Defect* (it costs reading time for nothing new).

**Smaller things.**
- The paragraph (about 19 px) and the list (about 18 px) look almost the same size, so they read as a slip rather than two levels (Bringhurst, "Don't compose without a scale"). Set them to one size, or make the list clearly different by weight or colour.
- The headline breaks as "twelve / calm minutes", which splits the phrase "twelve calm minutes". Break it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" instead. This is common sense: the source covers number and unit, not headlines.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** It is specific, it promises a benefit, and it sounds like a person: a number ("twelve") and a feeling ("calm") in seven words. It sets this ferry apart from any other. Keep it.
- **Clear order.** There is one dominant headline, a small blue company-name label above it, readable body text at a good line length (about 68 characters), and one button that clearly reads as the primary action. Everything sits on one left edge.
- **Restraint.** The palette is one blue plus dark grey on white, which already feels calm and nautical. Add a photo without adding colours.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a picture or a site header was supposed to appear in the empty right side or at the top, and didn't load or wasn't captured. Check this in a live browser.
- The button's hover and focus states, and how the page reflows on a phone.
