**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tall Growth bar, then to the title, then down the other three bars. It reads as the Conversion tab of a SaaS analytics dashboard comparing plans. It feels calm, tidy and credible. The bars give one message: Growth converts about twice as well as Starter, and Starter is far behind.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal analytics page where product and growth people compare trial-to-paid conversion across plans and decide where to act.

**Verdict.** The layout, type and table are well made, but the chart misstates its own data. The value axis starts at 2.5%, so a gap of under one percentage point looks like a huge one. Readers will take away a conclusion the numbers don't support.

**What would move it most**
1. **The bars start at 2.5%, not zero** (defect; convention per Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 17.1, and Tufte's lie factor). In the left chart the Growth bar is about 2.5 times as tall as Starter's, but 4.0% against 3.1% is only about 1.3 times. The visual gap is roughly five times larger than the real one. Bar length is exactly what the eye compares, and the small 2.5% tick in the bottom left won't undo that impression. Jev also flagged this, at 0.92. Fix: start the axis at zero. Since every bar already has its value printed on it, you could drop the y-axis and gridlines altogether. If the small differences are the point, switch to dots on a labelled, non-zero scale, which is where a truncated axis is honest.
2. **Nothing shows whether the gaps between plans are real** (defect; Wilke, ch. 16). The table on the right shows that Enterprise rests on 38 conversions and Growth on 118. At those counts, Enterprise's 3.4% could plausibly be anywhere from about 2.3% to 4.5%. Team against Growth is within noise, and only Growth against Starter looks like a likely real difference. The chart presents all four as firm rankings, and the truncated axis makes that worse. Fix: add interval bars or a light range on each bar, or at least a note that the Enterprise figure rests on 38 conversions, so readers don't redesign a plan around noise.
3. **The two definitions of "converted" disagree** (defect, copy). The subtitle says a trial counts if it became a paid account "within 30 days". The Definition block says trials "still open on 30 October are counted as not converted". That is a single calendar cutoff, not a 30-day window. A trial started on 1 July that pays on 20 August counts under the second reading and not under the first, which changes every number on the page. Fix: state one rule in both places, in full. For example: "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice is paid within 30 days of the trial starting."

**Smaller things.**
- There's no comparison anywhere on the page, such as last quarter's rate or a target, so a reader can't tell whether 3.1% is good or bad (Few). A ghost bar or a "Q2: x%" note beside each value would fix that.
- Jev flagged the bar axis starting above zero (finding 1), which is in the left chart's y-axis at 2.5%.

**What's working.**
- The right-hand table gives the raw trials and paid counts next to the percentages. That's exactly the sample context a reader needs, and it lets anyone check the rates (they do check out). Keep it.
- The restraint: one purple, light gridlines, values labelled directly on the bars, the source line at the foot, and the current tab shown in bold. Nothing competes with the data.
- The plans are ordered by tier rather than by value. That matches how readers think about the plans, and it's the right call here.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The size of the nav tab targets, and their hover and focus states.
