I've finished the walk and the deep read and am writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye lands on the large, light title "Planning a vegetable bed" and then goes down the left edge through three smaller headings. It reads as a short how-to article for a beginner gardener. It feels calm, tidy and a bit flat: the title and the three headings come across as four headlines of different sizes rather than one title with its sections.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a short web article that walks a first-time gardener through setting up a vegetable bed, read top to bottom on a desktop screen.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order is clear, the copy is concrete, and the spacing groups each heading with its text. What holds it back is that the hierarchy rests on size alone, and the lines are a little too long for comfortable reading. Neither one is serious.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings differ from the body only in size.** "Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant" and "Watering" use the same regular weight and the same near-black as the body. They are just set at about 32 px against about 18 px. The title is the same family again, in a lighter weight. So all four big lines speak in one voice, and the section headings read as smaller copies of the title instead of signposts beneath it. That is why the page feels flat. *Convention* (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8a–8g and 9): pair a smaller size step with a second contrast. The usual move here is to make the section headings semibold or bold and bring them down toward 22–24 px. That leaves the light title as the one large element, gives the headings a job of their own, and makes them easier to scan.
2. **The lines are too long.** The text column runs to about x=950, so wrapped lines like "Dig to the depth of a spade… Rake the surface level" come to about 90 characters. The comfortable range is 45 to 75, about 66 ideal (Bringhurst, §2.1.2). The paragraphs here are only two lines, so it costs little today. If the article grows, the eye will start losing its place on the way back to each new line. *Convention*: narrow the column to about 640–680 px at this body size. There's plenty of empty space on the right to absorb it.

**Smaller things.**
- The apostrophe in "last year's" is straight ('). Use the curly one ('). This is common sense, not a checked source. It's barely visible at body size.
- "Water deeply… twice a week" never says how much water counts as deeply. A beginner has to guess. Adding a measure ("until the top hand's depth is wet", or litres per square metre) would make it something they can act on. This is a copy point, not a typesetting one.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing that groups.** Each heading sits right on top of its paragraph, with a clearly larger gap above it. The page splits into its four sections without any rules or boxes. Keep that ratio when you change the heading weight.
- **Concrete copy.** "Six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans", "twice a week, rather than a little every day". Every line tells a stranger exactly what to do. Most how-to pages miss this.
- **Restraint.** One family, one ink colour, one flush-left column. Nothing on the page is decoration.
