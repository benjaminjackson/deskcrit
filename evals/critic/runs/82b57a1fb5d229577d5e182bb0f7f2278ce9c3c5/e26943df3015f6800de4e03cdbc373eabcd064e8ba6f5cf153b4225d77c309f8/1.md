**First read.** The eye lands on the big title, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", and drops straight into the body text. The page reads as a clean, quiet help-centre article for whoever pays the bill. It feels calm and trustworthy. Then the reader hits a problem: nowhere does it say how to actually change the card.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help article for an account owner or billing admin who came to change the card on file and wants to finish quickly and correctly.

**Verdict.** The page itself is well made. Type, spacing and hierarchy are all sound. But the content falls short of the title's promise. The reader learns everything *around* updating a card and gets no steps and no link to do it. A smaller issue: the retry wording is ambiguous about when the plan gets downgraded.

**What would move it most**
1. **The task the title promises is missing.** The first paragraph says "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page", but "billing page" is plain text. There are no numbered steps and no button or link that goes there. The reader has to leave, find the billing page and work out the steps on their own. Meanwhile the article links three side topics (billing history, roles, paying by invoice) and not the main one. Defect. It breaks Nielsen's "recognition rather than recall": the reader has to remember where to go instead of seeing it here. Fix: right under the title, put a short numbered list (where to click, what to enter, what confirms it worked) and link "billing page" directly. Then the rules about open invoices and roles can follow as context.
2. **The decline-and-downgrade timeline can be read two ways, and it decides whether the customer loses their plan.** "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days… If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan."
   - Reading A: the second attempt is the day-7 retry, so the downgrade happens on day 7.
   - Reading B: the first charge counts as attempt one, so the second attempt is the day-3 retry, and the downgrade happens on day 3.

   It's also unclear whether "seven days" counts from the first decline or from the day-3 retry. Someone who plans around the wrong date gets downgraded without expecting it. Defect: this is a date line that customers will hold the company to. Fix: state the timeline in days counted from one fixed event, for example: "If your new card is declined, we try it again 3 days later and 7 days later. If it still fails on day 7, your subscription moves to the free plan." Also say what "restore your previous plan" keeps or loses, such as data and seats.

**Smaller things.**
- Left sidebar: "Update a payment card" looks exactly like its siblings, so nothing shows which page you're on. Mark it with one cue, such as bold weight or a left bar (Nielsen 1; NN/g, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The sidebar label "Update a payment card" and the title "Update the card that pays for your subscription" name the same page differently. Keep them close so the reader knows they arrived at the right place.
- Body measure: the first line of the opening paragraph runs about 84 characters, a little past Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Narrowing the text column by about 10% would bring it into range. Worth measuring.
- Header: a help centre with no search box makes anyone who doesn't fit the four top categories browse instead of asking (Nielsen 7). Add search to the header, unless it lives elsewhere on purpose.

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy with few levels.** There is one dominant title, a bold subhead that sits close to the paragraph it introduces, comfortable body text with generous line spacing, and a smaller size for navigation. Each size has an obvious job. Keep the scale when you add the steps; don't add a new size for them.
- **The edges line up.** Logo and sidebar share one left edge. Breadcrumb, title and body share another. "In this section" sits on the same baseline as the breadcrumb. The page looks deliberate without boxes or rules.
- **The copy is written to the reader.** Body text speaks to them directly ("If you are not sure which invoice is next, check your billing history before you make the change"), and links are blue *and* underlined with descriptive text. The two fixes above should keep this voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the sidebar and links show a visible keyboard focus state. Worth checking, since this page is all links.
