I've checked the reference entries; now I'm writing the critique.

**First read.** The eye goes straight to "River ferry timetable", drops to the grey subtitle, then runs down the first column (Morning, Morning, Midday…) before reaching the times. It reads as a clean, calm, generic web table for one ferry route. Nothing in it says what's special about it, and nothing points you to the answer you came for.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a rider on their phone or at the landing wants to know when the next boat leaves north landing today, so they can catch it.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and legible, but it's set up as a list of facts, not as an answer. The one thing that decides whether a sailing exists today (the Runs column) sits at the far right edge, so the reader has to read and filter every row by hand, and the timetable never says which dates it covers.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Does it run today?" is the last thing you read, so it gets missed.** The Runs column sits about 850 px from the departure time it qualifies, and nothing sets "Weekdays" or "Weekends" apart from "Daily". A Saturday rider scanning the Leaves column sees seven sailings and can easily plan around 12:40 or 17:10, which don't run that day. On a weekday, 19:30 is the trap. That's a missed boat, not a style issue. The fix: make the day pattern part of the structure, not a footnote. Usually that means separate Weekday and Weekend lists, or a day marker placed right beside each time, with exceptions set apart by weight or a symbol plus a label. Defect.
2. **The copy leaves gaps the rider can't fill.** "Spring season" gives no dates, so in early June nobody can tell whether this table still applies. People hold you to a timetable, so a missing date range is a defect, not a nicety. The page also shows only one direction (north landing to market quay), with no hint of where the return times are. "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are clipped labels: "north" what? Spell out "Leaves North Landing" and "Arrives Market Quay", put the date range in the subtitle, and say where the return sailings are. Defect.
3. **The table is stretched and padded with columns that don't help the rider.** The four short columns are spread across 1080 px with about 240 px gaps, while rows are only about 57 px apart. Your eye naturally groups the page into columns, and only the thin rules hold each row together (Arnheim on proximity). On top of that, "Period" just restates the clock time, and every crossing takes exactly 12 minutes, so the Arrives column is mostly repeated information. Drop or merge Period, consider one line saying "Crossing takes 12 minutes", and tighten the table to the width of its content so each row reads as one line. Taste, argued on common sense: fewer, closer columns make the one useful number easier to find.

**Smaller things.**
- The times are set in proportional figures. In the Leaves and Arrives columns, 11:00 and 11:12 are narrower than 06:40, so the colons don't line up down the column. Switch on tabular figures (Bringhurst, 4.4.3).

**What's working.**
- The type is restrained and well judged. There's one family, a bold title that clearly leads, bold column headers, and a grey subtitle that steps back by colour instead of by another size. Keep that three-level scale.
- The faint row rules divide the rows without boxing them in. They're the right weight, and if you tighten the table they'll do even more.
- 24-hour times with leading zeros (06:40) are clear and can't be misread as AM or PM.
