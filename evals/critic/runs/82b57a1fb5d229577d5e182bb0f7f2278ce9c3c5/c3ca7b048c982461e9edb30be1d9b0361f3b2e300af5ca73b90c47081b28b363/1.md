**What it's for.** My assumption: the top section of a ferry operator's website. Its job is to tell a visitor what the crossing is like and get them to the timetable or a ticket.

**Verdict.** It's clear and well ordered, and the headline is good. But it fails the visitor on the facts they came for. They can't find out where the boat goes, when the next one leaves or what it costs, so the page sells a feeling and makes them click away for the information.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page leaves out what a ferry passenger needs most: where it goes, the next departure and the fare.** "Cross the bay" and "Boats leave the north landing" never name the other side. "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" can't be planned around: is the first boat at 6:00 or 8:00, and the last at 21:00 or 23:00? There's no price anywhere. Every visitor has to press "See the timetable" just to learn whether this trip fits their day. **Defect (common sense; the matching rule here has no approved source).**
   - Name both landings in the headline or the line under it ("North Landing to Southport").
   - Under the paragraph, add a line with the next sailing, the first and last boat, and the adult fare. That turns the button from the only way to find out into a way to see more.
2. **There's no picture, and the copy is doing all the persuading on its own.** The paragraph promises "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck". That's the real draw, and the page shows none of it. A grey-on-white Inter page with a blue button could belong to any software product, so the ferry has nothing visual that sets it apart. **Taste.** One wide photo of the boat on the bay, or the view from the deck, would make the "calm" in the headline something the visitor sees rather than takes on trust.

**Smaller things.**
- **Paragraph and list are almost the same size.** The paragraph (about 20 px) and the three lines below it (about 18 px) look like a near-miss, not two levels (Bringhurst, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Set the list at the paragraph's size, or make the step between them clear, and separate them with weight, colour or bullet marks rather than 2 px of size.
- **The headline breaks after "twelve", so "twelve" sits on a different line from "minutes".** Break it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" so the promise stays on one line.
- **The three list lines have no markers.** Centred and unmarked, "Bicycles travel free" and the others read like more paragraph rather than a set of perks. Small icons or bullets would make them scannable.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** "Twelve calm minutes" is concrete and specific to this service: it gives a number and a feeling in four words. Keep that, and keep it the clear dominant element.
- **One clear primary action.** "See the timetable" names exactly what you get, is solidly sized (about 210 × 55 px), and is the only saturated element on the page, so the eye ends there.
- **Restraint.** One typeface, one accent blue, comfortable line length (about 74 characters at most) and generous spacing. The page is easy to read and feels unhurried, which fits the message.
