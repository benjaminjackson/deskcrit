**First read.** The eye lands on "How can we help?" in white serif on the navy band, drops to the white search field, then down into the eight cards. On the way down it gets caught by the blue-and-orange credit card and the green shield, the only colour in the lower half. It reads as the help-centre home page for a business software product: calm, orderly and familiar, close to a template.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the help-centre home for a team software product, and its job is to get a stuck user to the right article quickly, by search or by topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the grid is clean, and the card copy is unusually good. The one thing that works against it is the icon set: two detailed, full-colour illustrations among six thin line icons. They make Billing and Privacy look more important than the other six topics, and the grid stops looking like one set.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two icons don't match the other six (defect).** The credit card on "Billing and invoices" (top row, third card) and the shield on "Privacy and security" (bottom row, first card) are filled, shaded, multi-colour illustrations. The other six (lightning bolt, key, box, plug, bell, lifebuoy) are thin, single-colour navy outlines.
   - **What the viewer meets:** these two are the only saturated colour below the hero, so they pull the eye first and form a pair on a diagonal across the grid. A user scanning for their topic is steered toward Billing and Privacy whether that's their problem or not, and the grid reads as pieced together from two icon packs.
   - **Why:** Apple's Human Interface Guidelines ("Icons") say icons in one set should share "a consistent size, level of detail, stroke thickness (or weight), and perspective". The shield's gloss also gives it a false depth that nothing else on the page has; that part is taste.
   - **Fix:** redraw or replace the card and the shield as navy outline icons at the same stroke weight as the other six. If one topic really should stand out, do it deliberately with position or a label, not by accident with an icon.

**Smaller things**
- **Short last lines in the card descriptions:** "data." (Privacy), "where." (Notifications), "receipts." (Billing) and "recovery." (Account) each sit alone on the last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Tighten the copy or adjust the card width so each description ends on a fuller line or fits on one.
- **The Privacy card copy doesn't say what the user can do there.** "How we protect and store your data." describes the company. Every other card names a task ("Track a delivery or start a return"). Something like "Control who sees your data and how it's stored" would match.
- **The topics mix two kinds of product.** "Set up your workspace", "Integrations" and "Sync problems" sit next to "Orders and shipping". A stranger can't tell whether this is a team tool or a shop. Fine if the product really is both; if not, the box-and-delivery card doesn't belong.
- **The headline is generic.** "How can we help?" could be on anyone's help centre. That's acceptable here because the search field does the real work. Naming the product ("Get help with Harbor") would tell the user they're in the right place.
- **Jev found one defect:** icons in one set mix very different levels of detail. Confirmed; it's finding 1 (the credit card and shield icons).

**What's working**
- **Clear order of importance.** One headline, one search field on the navy band, then a single quiet grid. The eye always knows the next step. Keep the hero this spare.
- **Card copy names tasks in the user's words:** "Track a delivery or start a return", "Fix sync problems and error messages". A user can recognise their problem without opening the card. Keep this voice for the rest of the site.
- **One clean grid.** Four equal columns with matching gutters line up with the left edge of the logo and "Browse by topic". The serif headline against the sans-serif text is a clear, deliberate pairing.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- **The search field's behaviour:** whether it suggests articles as you type, and whether there's a visible focus ring on the white field against the navy.
- **Below the fold:** whether the page offers popular articles or a contact route. If it ends at the grid, a user whose problem isn't one of the eight topics has nowhere obvious to go next.
