**First read.** The eye goes first to the purple "Sign in" button, then up to the email field with the red outline. It reads at once as the sign-in screen for a product called Quillpost, caught just after a failed attempt. The red outline raises a question ("what's wrong?") that the screen never answers. It feels calm, tidy and fairly generic.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the sign-in screen for returning Quillpost users, shown in its "invalid email" error state. The address typed, `dana.ortiz@exmaple`, has a typo and no domain ending, so it looks like a test case for that error.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the one job this state has: telling the user what went wrong and how to fix it. Apart from that the card is sound. The order of importance is clear and the form is laid out properly. But the error is a red border with no words, so the user is left to work out the problem on their own.

**What would move it most**
1. **The email error is a red border with no message.** The only sign that anything failed is the red outline on the Email field. There's no text saying what's wrong, and no message anywhere on the card. A user who doesn't notice the red, or can't see it, just sees the form sitting there after they pressed Sign in. A user who does notice it still has to guess. Is the address malformed? Not registered? Was the password wrong and the field is just highlighted? The typo "exmaple" and the missing ".com" are easy to miss when you're checking your own address. This is a **defect** and a **convention** break: WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.1 Error Identification requires the error to be "described to the user in text", and SC 1.4.1 bans colour as the only cue. SC 3.3.3 Error Suggestion and NN/g's error-message guidance add that the message should say how to fix it. **Fix:** put a short line directly under the Email field, in red with an icon, that names the problem and the fix, such as "This email address looks incomplete. Check the spelling and the part after the @ (for example, example.com)." Keep the red border as the second cue. If the server can't find the account, say that instead and point to "Create an account".

**Smaller things.**
- "Create an account" at the bottom is grey text in the same style as "New here?", with no colour, underline or weight to show it's a link. A new user scanning for sign-up may not see it's clickable. Style it the way "Forgot your password?" is styled (the two links currently look different), and consider underlining both so they aren't marked by colour alone (Nielsen Norman Group, Moran on flat UI; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The password field's border is a pale grey-blue on white and looks under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 requires for input edges. Measure it, and darken the border if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- The order of importance is right for a single-task screen. The filled purple button is the only strong shape, the "Sign in" heading is the only large type, and everything else steps down clearly. Keep that restraint when you add the error message: it should be small and red, not a banner that competes with the button.
- The form is built properly: labels sit above their fields and closer to them than to the field above, one column, one primary action. "Forgot your password?" sits right where someone who has just failed would look.
- The type scale is tight: about four sizes, each with an obvious job. Accent colour is limited to the wordmark, the link and the button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields, links and button. Check that keyboard focus shows a clear outline of at least 2 px and that it doesn't look like the red error border.
- Whether the error appears on submit or on blur, and whether screen readers announce it.
