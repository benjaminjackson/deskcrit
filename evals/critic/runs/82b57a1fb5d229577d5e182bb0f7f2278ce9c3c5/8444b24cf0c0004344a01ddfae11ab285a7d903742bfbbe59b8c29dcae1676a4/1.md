**First read.** The eye goes straight into the leaf pattern. It is bright, busy and evenly spread from edge to edge, so the eye wanders around it before it finds the white headline. Even then "small" and "spaces." are hard to pick out, because pale yellow leaves and white dots sit right behind them. Next the eye drops to the three cards, which are calm and easy to read. It reads as a friendly local garden centre homepage. It feels cheerful and tidy, but it gives you nothing to do.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: this is the homepage of a local garden centre. It should make a nearby visitor want shade and small-space plants and get them to visit, order or book this weekend.

**Verdict.** It's close but doesn't do its job yet. The pattern behind the headline buries the one line that says what's on offer, and the page has no ask at all, so a visitor who's interested has no next step.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is lost in the pattern** (defect). "Plants for shade and small spaces" is set straight onto a dense, high-contrast leaf pattern. The worst spots are "small" (pale yellow leaves behind most of the word), the "P" of "Plants", and "spaces." (white dots right at the full stop). The letters keep breaking up against shapes of a similar lightness, so the page's one message takes a second read and becomes a strain for anyone with weaker sight. Contrast has to hold at the worst point behind the text, not on average (W3C Techniques for WCAG 2.2, F83). Fix: put a dark scrim or solid panel behind the headline, or keep a calm, dark area of the pattern behind the text, so every letter stands clear. A side effect of the fix is that the headline, not the pattern, becomes the first thing seen. Right now nothing on the page clearly comes first.
2. **There's no ask** (defect). The page has no button and no link in the content, only the top nav. The intro copy is good: "ready to pot this weekend" gives a real reason to act now. But it then stops, and the visitor has to choose between "Plants" and "Visit" in small grey nav text. Add one primary action under the intro that names one exact thing to do, like "See this week's shade plants" or "Plan a visit Saturday". The cards could carry their own lower-weight links too, such as booking one of the ten workshop places.
3. **The picture argues against the headline** (taste). The headline promises shade and small spaces. The illustration is a sunny yellow-and-lime spread of generic leaves, with no fern, no hosta, no pot and no balcony. It decorates the page without backing up the claim, so the page could belong to any garden centre. A darker, cooler picture that actually shows shade plants in a small space would make the picture and the words one idea, and would also leave a calmer area behind the type.
4. **The headline and its intro line are split apart** (defect). The intro ("Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs…") belongs to the headline, but the hero's bottom edge cuts it off into the white area below. There it reads as a separate block that opens the cards section. The headline also starts about 16 px to the right of the left edge that the logo, intro and cards share (x≈72 against x≈56). That looks like a miss, not a choice. Bring the intro up into the hero with the headline, or at least put both on the same left edge.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline break "small / spaces" splits the phrase and leaves "spaces." alone on its line. Break after "for", or widen the headline's area so it fits on one line.
- "until 8 / pm" in the Opening hours card separates the number from its unit. Use a non-breaking space.
- The large headline looks set at normal text spacing, so it reads a little loose. Tighten the letter and word spacing slightly at this size.
- The Jev evidence file flagged "Text sits directly on a busy image" (0.77). That is finding 1, confirmed on the hero headline.

**What's working.**
- The three cards (hours, workshops, delivery) each answer a real question a visitor has in one plain, specific sentence: "ten places in each class", "free within ten miles on orders over $60". Keep that concrete tone.
- The type plan is clear. A green serif wordmark against one sans-serif family, with bold card headings over regular body text, gives clean levels without extra sizes.
- The intro sentence names actual plants and a time frame ("this weekend"). That makes it the most persuasive line on the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav links. How the hero headline wraps against the pattern at narrower widths, where it may land on even busier areas.
