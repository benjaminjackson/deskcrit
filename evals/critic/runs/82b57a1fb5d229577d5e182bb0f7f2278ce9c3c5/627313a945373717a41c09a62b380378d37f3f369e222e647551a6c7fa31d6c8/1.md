**First read.** The eye goes to "Create your account", runs down the seven fields and stops on the navy "Create account" button. It's a sign-up card for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. It also feels anonymous: apart from the small wordmark, nothing on the screen tells you what Quillnest is or why you'd want it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the web sign-up page for a notes product, and its job is to get a new visitor from landing to a created account with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The form is well made: the structure is clear, there's one obvious action and the fields are labelled properly. It is longer and asks more than it needs to, though, and the page gives no reason to sign up. The biggest problem is how much it asks, not how it looks.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for things the user doesn't need yet** (defect). "How did you hear about us?" and "Workspace name" sit in the main flow as two full-width fields, between the password and the button. That's six inputs, where email and password would get the account made. The survey question serves Quillnest, not the user. "Workspace name" asks a newcomer to name something they haven't seen yet, with no hint of what a workspace is. Every extra field is one more reason to stop before the button. Nielsen 8 (aesthetic and minimalist design) and progressive disclosure both point the same way. Fix: move the survey to after sign-up or drop it. Either prefill the workspace name (for example "Ben's notes", taken from the first name) or ask for it on the first screen inside the product, where the user can see what it names. Ask whether you need first and last name here too.

2. **The page doesn't make the case** (taste, and the copy). "Start a free workspace for your notes." could be on any notes app's page, so a visitor who isn't sure gets nothing new to act on. The headline and button are both just the action ("Create account"), so nothing on screen says what the user gets. Fix: change the subline to one concrete claim about what Quillnest does differently, said to the user ("…so you can …"). Have the button name the result ("Create my workspace") rather than the process.

3. **The password field gives no rules** (defect). If there are length or character rules, the user only learns them from an error after pressing the button, which means retyping and a moment of doubt right at the commit point. Nielsen 5 (error prevention) and 10 (help and documentation). Fix: add one short helper line under the field with the actual rule, and think about a show/hide toggle.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Sign in" link at the bottom is the same gray, weight and size as "Already have an account?", so returning users can't tell it's clickable. Underline it or use the brand blue (WCAG technique G183).
- The field borders look like a pale blue-gray, probably under 3:1 against the white card, so the fields are faint for low-vision users. Measure them and darken to at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
- The "How did you hear about us?" dropdown is blank, with no "Select one…" prompt, so it looks broken or unfinished. If you keep it, give it a prompt option.
- "Workspace name" isn't explained. If it stays, add a one-line hint saying what a workspace is and that it can be changed later.

**What's working.**
- Labels sit above each field, bold, and closer to their own input than to the next one. Scanning down the form is effortless, so keep this.
- There's one primary button: full width, high contrast, clearly the end of the form. Nothing competes with it.
- Required fields are marked with a red asterisk and explained by "* Required field" at the top, so the colour isn't carrying the meaning alone. First and last name side by side is the right use of a two-column pair.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields, and how errors look when validation fires. Check that the focus indicator is at least 2 px with 3:1 contrast, and that error messages name the field and say how to fix it.
