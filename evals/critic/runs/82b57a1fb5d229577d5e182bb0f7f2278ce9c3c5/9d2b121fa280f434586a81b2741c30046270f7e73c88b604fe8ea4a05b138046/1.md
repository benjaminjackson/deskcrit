**First read.** The eye goes first to the white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces", on its dark leaf pattern. Then it drops to the intro line and runs across three equal cards: hours, workshops, delivery. Then it stops, because there is nothing to do. It reads as the home page of a calm, trustworthy local nursery, but it feels more like a notice board than a shop.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the home page of a local garden centre. It should get nearby visitors to come in, buy, or book a workshop, and this one leads with a seasonal range of shade plants.

**Verdict.** It's clean, well set and readable, but it doesn't do its job yet. It makes an offer ("ready to pot this weekend") and then gives the visitor nowhere to go with it. On top of that, the biggest area on the page is a generic pattern that shows nothing being sold.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's no next step anywhere on the page.** *Defect.* The headline and intro promise ferns, hostas and compact shrubs. Below them come three information cards, then empty space. There's no button, link or "see the plants" anywhere. The cards also look like static panels, with no arrow, link text or hover cue, so even "Saturday workshops" (ten places per class, the one thing with real urgency) can't be booked from here. A visitor who wants what the headline offers has to guess that "Plants" in the top-right menu is the way in. Material Design and Apple's HIG both expect one clearly primary action per view, and here there are none.
   - **Fix:** add one filled primary button under the intro that names the exact thing, such as "See shade plants in stock" or "Browse ferns and hostas", not "Shop now".
   - Give the workshop card a secondary text link such as "Book a Saturday place", and give the delivery card one too if delivery is ordered online.

2. **The hero image is the biggest thing on the page and says the least.** *Taste, argued.* The scattered-leaf pattern only says "plants", which the headline already says. It shows no shade, no small space and no product. It's also busy: high-contrast leaves and pale dots run right behind the headline, and the area around "small" and "spaces" is the noisiest part. A nursery that grows its own stock has the best possible picture available: a real shady corner, balcony or doorstep planted with these ferns and hostas. That image would carry "small spaces" and "grown at our nursery" without any more words.
   - **Fix:** replace the pattern with a photo of the actual range in a small, shady setting.
   - If the pattern has to stay, darken or blur it heavily behind the text so the headline sits on calm ground.

3. **The headline doesn't line up with anything.** *Convention, and it costs something here.* The logo, the intro line and all three cards share a left edge at about x=56. The headline starts at about x=72. That 16 px miss is too small to look deliberate, so it looks like an error. It sits right at the first thing the eye lands on, between the logo and the intro, which are aligned above and below it. Arnheim: a placement that is neither clearly on the line nor clearly off it makes the eye waver.
   - **Fix:** move the headline to x=56, or indent it far enough that the offset is clearly a choice.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** "small / spaces" splits the noun phrase and leaves one word alone on line two. Break it after "Plants for shade", or widen the measure so it sets on one line (Bringhurst, 2.4.2).
- **Opening hours card:** "until 8 / pm on Thursdays" separates a number from its unit. Put a non-breaking space between "8" and "pm" (Bringhurst, 2.4.6).
- **Intro spacing:** the intro sits about as close to the hero (about 30 px) as to the cards (about 28 px), so it belongs to neither. Pull it tighter to whichever it belongs to; it's really the headline's subtitle, so tighter to the hero.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific.** "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, grown at our nursery and ready to pot this weekend", "ten places in each class" and "free within ten miles on orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box" are concrete facts no competitor could copy and paste. Keep that voice when you write the button labels.
- **The type plan is restrained.** A serif wordmark sits against one sans family, with headline, intro, card title and body clearly separated by size and weight.
- **The colour is limited and on-brand.** Deep green, white and neutral grey carry "garden centre" with no extra accent fighting the content.
