**First read.** The eye goes to "Create your account", then drops through a stack of identical white fields to the blue button. Within two seconds it reads as a signup for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and anonymous. Nothing tells you what Quillnest is or why you'd pick it over the notes app you already use, and the form looks longer than signing up for notes should be.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the desktop signup screen for a free notes product, meant to get a visitor from "curious" to "inside" with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The craft is clean and the hierarchy is right, but the form asks for too much and explains too little. Six fields, two of which serve the company or a decision the user can't make yet, stand between a visitor and their first note, and nothing on the page argues for going through them.

**What would move it most**
1. **Too many fields up front (defect).** "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" sit in the main path alongside the fields that are actually needed. The survey only helps your marketing team, and "Workspace name" asks a new user to name a container they haven't seen yet. "Workspace" is your product's word, not the word of someone who came to take notes. Each extra field is one more reason to stop, and this form looks a third longer than it needs to.
   - Fix: cut the form to name, email and password.
   - Move the survey to after signup or behind an optional step (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure").
   - Either prefill the workspace name ("Ben's notes") or ask for it after the account exists (Nielsen heuristics 2 and 8).
2. **Nothing says which fields are required (defect, convention).** No field has an asterisk or "(optional)". The survey is almost certainly optional, but the user can't tell, so they fill it in or hit an error. Fix: add "(optional)" after the label of each optional field (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
3. **The password field gives no rules (defect).** If there's a minimum length or required characters, the user finds out by failing. Fix: put a one-line hint under the field, such as "At least 8 characters", so it's visible before they type (Nielsen heuristic 10).
4. **The copy sells nothing (taste, argued).** "Start a free workspace for your notes" could sit on any notes app, so the viewer has no reason to choose this one over Apple Notes, Notion or a text file. Fix: replace it with one line that sets Quillnest against what they use now and says what they get that their current tool doesn't. "Create account" is a fine button label, but the button only works if the line above it has made the case.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Sign in" link, footer line:** it's the same grey as the sentence around it, with no underline, so it doesn't look clickable. Give it the brand blue and an underline (WCAG technique G183).
- **Field borders:** the pale blue-grey borders look about 2:1 against white, under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for, so the boxes fade for people with low vision. Measure the border colour and darken it until it passes.
- **"How did you hear about us?" dropdown:** it shows a blank value with no prompt, so it looks unfilled or broken. Show "Select one", or remove the field as in finding 1 (Nielsen heuristic 5).

**What's working.**
- **Hierarchy:** the order is unambiguous. The heading leads, every field has a bold label sitting just above its box, and only the full-width filled button is styled as the primary action. Keep this when you trim.
- **Spacing:** the rhythm between fields is even. Label-to-field gaps are clearly tighter than field-to-field gaps, so each pair reads as one unit.
- **Layout:** one centred card with a single column and just the short name pair side by side. That's the right layout for a single-task screen.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and the button: they matter here, because people fill in forms by tabbing.
- Error and inline validation states: how and where errors appear, and whether they say how to fix them.
- Whether the email and password fields use the right input types.
