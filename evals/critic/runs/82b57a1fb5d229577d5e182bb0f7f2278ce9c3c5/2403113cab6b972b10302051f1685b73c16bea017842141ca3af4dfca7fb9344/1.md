**First read.** My eye goes first to the dark photo in the far-left card, not to the headline. It's the only dark, detailed, high-contrast thing on a pale page. Then it goes up to the serif headline and across the four cards to the prices, and stops there. It reads as a friendly, tidy local home-services site, except for that first card, which looks like a stately library dropped in from another website.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of a local home-help service, aimed at busy homeowners or renters. Its job is to get them to pick a service and book a visit.

**Verdict.** It's clean and well set, but it's held back by two things. The one photo pulls the eye to the wrong place and says the wrong thing, and the page tells people to "pick a service" without giving them anywhere on the cards to do it. Fix those two and it does its job. It's competent but not yet persuasive.

**What would move it most**

1. **The Window cleaning photo breaks the set and takes over the page** (defect). Cards 2 to 4 are flat illustrations on a mint tint, and card 1 is a dark, detailed photo of what looks like a wood-panelled hall with leaded windows and leather sofas.
   - **It grabs the eye.** It's the darkest, busiest area on the screen, so it beats the headline and the Book a visit button for first look.
   - **It's off-message.** "Help around the house" sits next to a picture of a country-house interior, which suggests either a luxury service or a stock photo nobody checked.
   - **The windows don't read.** They're grey shapes in a gloomy room, so the picture doesn't even show clean glass.
   - **It makes the page look unfinished.** One photo among three drawings looks like a missing asset.
   - **Fix:** choose one rendering style for the set and replace the odd one out. Here that means a fourth flat illustration (a window with a squeegee, same palette and mint tile). Mix styles only if a rule covers the whole set.

2. **There's no next step where the viewer finishes reading** (defect). The lead line gives the steps: "Pick a service, choose a day…".
   - **Nothing to click.** The eye goes headline → cards → price, and then stops. The cards have no button, link, or arrow, and "From $89" is plain green text. Nothing says the card can be clicked.
   - **The only action is far away.** That's the small Book a visit button in the top-right corner, which the reader has already passed.
   - **The cost:** someone who has picked a service has to go back up and hope the button knows which one they meant. The action should sit next to the thing it acts on (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping).
   - **Fix:** give each card a clear action at its foot that names what happens, such as "Book window cleaning" or "See times". Or make the whole card visibly clickable with a link-styled price and an arrow. Either way, the lead's promise to pick a service and choose a day should be something the page lets you start right here.

3. **The prices don't say what you're buying** (defect). People remember prices and hold you to them.
   - **"From $24" for laundry pickup** could mean per bag, per load, per week, or per pickup.
   - **"From $65" for garden care** could be per visit or per month.
   - **"From $189" for deep cleaning** depends on home size, and the page doesn't say how.
   - **The cost:** a viewer who reads it the cheap way and gets quoted the other way feels misled. The figures can't be compared across cards because each "From" may cover a different unit.
   - **Fix:** add the unit and what sets the price, e.g. "From $24 per bag" or "From $189 for a 1-bed home".

**Smaller things**
- **Headline copy is generic.** "Help around the house, on your schedule" could be any cleaning company's line. The most specific, believable sentence on the page is in the laundry card ("We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday"). Bring that kind of concrete promise up into the headline or lead. "Vetted crew" is a claim taken on trust; say what vetting means.
- **The headline wraps badly.** "schedule" sits alone on line 2 (Bringhurst, §2.4.2). Widen the text box or rebreak it so the lines balance, e.g. "Help around the house, / on your schedule".
- **Jev finding** (images in one set mix rendering styles): confirmed and covered in finding 1. It's the Window cleaning photo against the three illustrations.

**What's working**
- **The type plan.** A warm serif for the brand and headline, a plain sans for everything else, and four clearly different sizes. Each level has an obvious job and nothing collides.
- **The grid.** Four equal cards with matching gutters, titles and body text on shared baselines, and prices pinned to one line across all four. It looks orderly and makes comparing easy.
- **The three illustrations.** One palette (teal, coral, mustard on mint), simple shapes, and a matching level of detail. They're friendly without being cute. Use them as the standard for the replacement window image.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cards are clickable and what hover or focus looks like. That matters here because the cards are the obvious place to start booking.
