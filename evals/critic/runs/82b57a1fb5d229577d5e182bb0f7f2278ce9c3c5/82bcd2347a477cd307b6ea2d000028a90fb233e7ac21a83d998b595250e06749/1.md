**First read.** My eye goes straight to the headline, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then down into the first paragraph. The links stand out because they are the only colour on the page. It reads as a help-centre article for a billing product, and it feels calm, plain and trustworthy. There is no image and nothing to click that looks like a button, so after reading I'm left wondering where to actually change the card.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help article for a customer or account owner who came here to change the card that pays for the subscription, and who wants to do that as fast as possible.

**Verdict.** The page is well typeset and easy to read, but it doesn't do the one thing its headline promises. It explains what happens around a card change but never shows how to make one or where to go. Its other gaps are wayfinding: nothing shows which page you're on, and there's no search.

**What would move it most**
1. **The article never gets the reader to the action.** The first paragraph says you can replace the card "from the billing page", but "billing page" isn't a link and there's no path (for example Settings → Billing → Payment method) and no steps. Three other pages are linked (billing history, roles and permissions, paying by invoice), but not the one place the reader came here to reach. So the reader who arrived from a search has to leave and hunt through the product for the page. That's a defect: the user has to remember and find something the page could have put in front of them (Nielsen, heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall). Fix: put the action first. Add a short numbered path or steps, or a direct link to the payment-method screen, right under the headline. Then the conditions follow (open invoices, who has permission).
2. **The retry schedule can be read two ways, and it sets a deadline.** "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days" doesn't say seven days after what: the first decline or the three-day retry? "If the card is still declined after the second attempt" leaves open whether the original charge counts as an attempt. That matters, because the second attempt is when the account drops to the free plan. A reader who counts wrong gets downgraded sooner than they expected and blames you. That's a defect, because this line works as a deadline. Fix: state it as dates relative to one event, for example "If the charge fails, we retry 3 and 7 days later. If the day-7 retry also fails, your workspace moves to the free plan."
3. **Nothing shows where you are.** In the left "In this section" list, "Update a payment card" looks exactly like the other four items. "Billing" in the top nav isn't marked either. On a help site people move between sibling articles, and with no "you are here" marker the list is harder to use as a map. That's a convention (Nielsen, heuristic 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu-design checklist, guideline 5: show the current location in the menu). Fix: mark the current item with one clear cue, such as a bold weight, a side bar or a tint. Do the same for "Billing" in the header.
4. **A help centre with no search.** The header has a logo, four section links and "Contact support", but no search field. Most help-centre visitors arrive with a question in mind, not a category. Without search, the only routes to an answer are browsing and contacting support, which is the cost this page exists to avoid. That's a convention (Nielsen, heuristic 7, flexibility and efficiency of use). Fix: put a search field in the header on every article.

**Smaller things.**
- Inline links (blue, no underline) are marked by colour alone. The dark blue against near-black body text probably falls short of the 3:1 difference WCAG technique G183 asks for. Underline the links in the body text.
- The body text runs about 84 characters a line (the first line of the opening paragraph, for example), a bit over Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column by about 80–100 px. There's empty space on the right to absorb it.
- Taste: the sidebar says "Update a payment card" and the H1 says "Update the card that pays for your subscription". Two names for one page weaken the link between them. Make them closer.

**What's working.**
- The type hierarchy is clean. A large bold H1, one H2, generous body text, and smaller grey labels give a few clearly separate sizes, each with an obvious job. Keep this scale.
- The H2 "When the new card is declined" sits clearly nearer its own paragraph than the one above, so the page splits into two chunks at a glance.
- The copy speaks to the reader ("you can", "your workspace") and the link text says where each link goes ("roles and permissions", "paying by invoice"). Keep that voice when you add the steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus styles on the links, which matter more while they have no underline; how the sidebar and column reflow on a phone.
