I've finished the checklist and looked up the rules behind it. Here is the critique.

**First read.** My eye goes straight to the big blue title, then drops down the three blue subheadings like a table of contents. Within a second it reads as a short how-to article for a beginner gardener: calm, plain and easy to trust. It also looks like it could be anyone's page. The default-looking blue sans serif says "web page" more than "garden."

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web guide that teaches a first-time gardener to set up a vegetable bed, read top to bottom on a desktop screen.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of the page is clear, the copy is concrete and the type is clean. The only real flaw is that the paragraph lines are too long for easy reading. Everything else is small.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraph lines are too long** (convention). The body paragraphs run from x≈160 to x≈950. That's about 90 to 95 characters per line: for example, "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level". Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style*, §2.1.2) puts the comfortable range at 45 to 75 characters, with 66 as the ideal. At 90+, the eye has to travel a long way back to find the start of the next line. That costs little in these two-line paragraphs, but it adds up as soon as a section grows. Fix: narrow the text column to about 65 characters, roughly 600 to 640 px at this size. The headings can stay where they are. Measure the line length in the live page to confirm.

**Smaller things.**
- The apostrophe in "year's" (the "Preparing the soil" paragraph) is a straight typewriter mark, not a curly one. Use the true apostrophe throughout. This is common sense: at body size it's a detail, and I found no confirmed source for it.
- The "Watering" section is a single sentence, which makes it feel unfinished next to the others. Either give it a second line (for example, how to tell when the soil needs water) or fold it into another section. This is about the copy, not the checklist.
- The bold, saturated blue on every heading is the usual web colour for links, so a reader may expect the headings to be clickable. A darker or less saturated heading colour would avoid that and feel less generic. This is my taste, not a rule.

**What's working.**
- **The spacing groups the content correctly.** Each subheading sits about 8 px above its own paragraph and about 35 px below the previous one, so every heading clearly belongs to the text it introduces. Keep that ratio.
- **The type scale is clean.** There are three sizes (title ≈48, headings ≈30, body ≈17) with big steps between them, plus weight and colour contrast. Every level is obvious at a glance.
- **The copy is specific and actionable.** "Six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans", "twice a week" are all things a beginner can act on without filling in gaps.
