**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline "Tell us about your project", drops to the two-line promise underneath, then crosses to the white form card and comes down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads as the contact page of a small design studio. It's calm, orderly and quiet, and a little anonymous. The right edge and the bottom third feel unused.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a studio's contact page, meant to get a prospective client to send a project enquiry with enough detail for the studio to qualify it and reply.

**Verdict.** This is a clean form that works: the viewer knows what this is and what to do in a second. What holds it back is that it asks for the information the studio most needs (budget and timeline) in a side note, not in the form. It also doesn't say which fields are required.

**What would move it most**
1. **The budget and timeline request is in the wrong place.** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them" sits in the left column, about 450 px from the Message box it refers to. By the time a visitor is typing in the box, that line is out of their eye path. Most will write "Hi, we need a new website" and the studio's first reply becomes a round of questions, which uses up the two-day promise. This is a defect (Nielsen, heuristic 10: put help next to the step it's for), plus my own common-sense view that a form should ask for what you need. Fix: if budget and timeline matter for qualifying a lead, make them fields: a budget-range select and a "When do you need it?" field, both marked optional. At minimum, put the hint as helper text right under the "Message" label.
2. **No field is marked required or optional** (Jev also found this). None of the three labels (Your name, Email address, Message) has an asterisk or "(optional)". With three fields a visitor will probably guess they're all required. The real cost comes once you add the budget and timeline fields above: then the form mixes required and optional fields, and visitors can't tell which ones they can skip. This is a convention (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). Fix: mark the few optional fields with "(optional)" after the label, or put an asterisk on the required ones and explain it once at the top of the card.

**Smaller things.**
- **Nav doesn't show the current page** (Jev also found this). Top right, "Contact" looks exactly like "Work", "Services" and "About", so nothing tells the visitor where they are. Mark it with one cue, such as bold, no underline with a bar, or a darker colour (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Field borders look too faint.** The pale grey outlines of the three inputs look like roughly 2:1 against white, below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on control edges. Measure them and darken them to at least 3:1.
- **The content's right edge lines up with nothing.** The header runs out to about x 1224 ("Contact"), but the form card stops at about x 1067. That leaves a roughly 160 px empty strip on the right that doesn't line up with anything, and the page tips left. Either widen the card or the two-column block to the header's right edge, or narrow the header to the content width (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
- **Headline line break.** "Tell us about your / project" splits "your project" and leaves one word alone on the second line. Rebreak it as "Tell us about / your project", or widen the column.
- **The headline copy is generic.** "Tell us about your project" could be on any studio's site. The promise underneath ("We read every message and reply within two working days") is the specific, trust-building line. It deserves more weight, or a place nearer the button.

**What's working.**
- **The reply promise.** "We read every message and reply within two working days" is concrete: it names a time frame the visitor can hold the studio to. Keep it.
- **The form basics are right.** It's one column, every field has a visible bold label above it, and there's a single clearly primary button whose label names the action ("Send message", not "Submit").
- **The hierarchy is clear.** There are four distinct levels (headline, body, labels, links) in one family, with weight doing the fine distinctions. Nothing competes with the headline or the button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button. Check that they're visible, at least 2 px, with 3:1 contrast.
- Whether the email field uses an email input type and what validation and error messages look like.
