**First read.** The eye goes to the bold question "Save changes to the shared calendar?", drops to the one-line explanation, then reaches a row of four identical blue buttons and stops there. It's clearly a save-before-leaving dialog for a choir's shared calendar. It looks calm and competent, but at the moment of decision it goes flat: the question is clear and the answer isn't.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web confirmation dialog that lets someone editing a shared calendar safely choose to publish, keep working, or throw away their changes, quickly and without a mistake.

**Verdict.** It doesn't yet do its job. The question and copy are good, but all four answers are styled as the main action, so the person has to read every label to find "Save". The irreversible "Discard changes" looks exactly as safe as "Save" and sits in the middle of the row, where a hurried click lands.

**What would move it most**
1. **Four primary buttons, so no answer leads (defect, and a convention break).** The action row has four filled, dark-blue, bold buttons the same height and nearly the same width. Because they look alike they read as four equal choices of one kind. Nothing tells the eye that "Save changes" is the answer to the question asked. The viewer reads left to right through Export, Discard and Keep editing before reaching it. Material Design 3, "Buttons": "Use visually-prominent filled buttons for the most important actions." Direction: give the view one high-emphasis action and style the others lower, as outlined or text buttons (Material Design 3, "Buttons"). Here that means keeping "Save changes" filled, making "Keep editing" an outline or text button, and handling the other two as below.
2. **"Discard changes" isn't set apart, styled, or placed as destructive (defect).** It's the only action here that can't be undone, and it wipes out the new rehearsal times. Yet it's second in the row, between two harmless actions, in the same blue as Save. One wrong click among four lookalike targets loses the work. The guidance (Apple HIG "Alerts"; Material Design "Dialogs") is to separate destructive actions from safe ones, mark them as destructive, and never let them look like the default. Direction: move Discard away from the Save/Keep editing pair (commonly to the far left, with space between), give it a lower-emphasis destructive style such as red text or a red outline, and keep it from being the default.
3. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision (convention).** The question is "save or not?". Export answers a different question, yet it's given the first and most prominent slot in the row. It adds a fourth choice to a decision that should have two or three, and it's the first label the eye reads. Rare options belong behind a control, with the main path shown first (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). Direction: remove it from this dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu, or reduce it to a quiet text link apart from the answer buttons.

**Smaller things.**
- The action row's left edge sits about 4–5 px left of the headline and body text (buttons at about 276 px, text at about 281 px). It reads as a near-miss, not a choice. Align the buttons to the text edge, or set the row clearly apart.
- Jev flagged "Secondary actions are styled as strongly as the primary one" (probability 1.00). That's finding 1, the four filled blue buttons in the action row.

**What's working.**
- The headline is a real question that names the object ("the shared calendar"), and the body line says what happens and to whom: "shown to everyone who follows this calendar." That's the consequence for the viewer, stated plainly. Keep the copy as it is.
- The button labels are specific verbs with objects ("Discard changes", "Keep editing", "Save changes"), not "OK / Cancel". Once the styling is fixed they'll read instantly.
- The type is tidy: three clear sizes (bold headline, regular body, bold button labels), comfortable spacing, and a card that holds one group with generous, even padding.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Keyboard focus: which button gets focus by default (it should be Save or Keep editing, never Discard), and whether the focus ring is visible.
- Whether a dimmed backdrop covers the calendar behind the dialog. The page behind is blank white here, which could be the capture rather than the design. Check in a live browser.
- Whether Escape or a click outside the dialog acts as "Keep editing".
