**First read.** The eye lands on "Pricing", drops to the three large prices ($9, $29, $79), scans the short feature lists, and then has nowhere to go. The only button on the page is "Start free" at the top right, far from the plans. It reads as a clean, generic SaaS pricing page for a calendar or booking tool: calm, tidy, and with nothing to do.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the pricing page of a scheduling product, meant to help a small-team buyer pick a plan and start signing up.

**Verdict.** It looks tidy, but it doesn't do a pricing page's job. You can compare the plans, but you can't choose one: no card has a button, no plan is recommended, and the prices and features leave out what a buyer needs to decide. The biggest problem is that there is no action on the page where the decision is made.

**What would move it most**
1. **No card has an action, and the eye path stops at the feature lists.** All three cards end after their third feature line with nothing to press. The only button is "Start free", about 280 px above and to the right of the Business card, and it doesn't say which plan it starts. A buyer who has settled on Team has to guess whether "Start free" gives them Team, Solo, or a trial of everything. The control isn't next to what it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping), and pricing pages conventionally put a button on every plan, so leaving it out costs familiarity (Nielsen, Jakob's law). **Defect.** Fix: give each card its own button at the bottom, labelled with the plan ("Start Team free"), so the action is where the decision happens.

2. **No plan is recommended, so the three cards compete equally.** The cards are the same size, border, weight, and colour. A first-time buyer gets no hint which plan suits a team like theirs, so they have to work it out from three-line lists. This is common sense rather than a cited rule: most buyers want to be told which plan fits most people. **Taste.** Fix: pick the plan you want most people on (probably Team) and set it apart with one clear cue, such as a filled button, the brand-blue border, or a "Most teams pick this" label. That gives the row a place to start.

3. **The prices and plan contents leave out what a buyer needs to commit.** "$29 / month" doesn't say whether it is per user or for the whole team. That is the line the buyer will hold you to, and reading it wrong means either a surprise bill or a cheaper-looking rival. The feature lists don't say whether higher plans include the lower ones. Team lists "Up to 10 calendars, Shared availability, Reports", so does it keep Solo's booking page and email reminders? The viewer has to infer it, which goes against Nielsen's recognition rather than recall. Nothing says whether "Start free" is a trial or a free tier, or how long it lasts. **Defect.** Fix: write the unit out in full ("$29 per month for your whole team, up to 10 calendars"), start the Team and Business lists with "Everything in Solo, plus:" and "Everything in Team, plus:", and put one line under the cards that says what "free" means.

4. **The copy could be on anyone's page.** "Simple plans for teams of every size" says nothing about Tidewell, and "Pricing" only repeats the nav item. Nothing tells the viewer why this calendar tool rather than the one they already use. **Taste.** Fix: make the subhead the reason to buy, said to the viewer in terms of what they get. For example, compare Tidewell with how they book meetings now, rather than describing the plans.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Pricing" looks exactly like the other menu items, so nothing shows which page you are on (Jev found this too). Mark it with one cue, such as a bolder weight, an underline, or the brand blue (Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", NN/g).
- Nav: "Product" and "Features" sound like the same thing, so a visitor can't tell which to click. Merge them, or rename one so it says what it holds.
- Subhead: the second line is just "you like.", a short last line (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Narrow the box or rewrite the line so it breaks evenly, or fits on one line.

**What's working.**
- The price is the largest thing in each card and sits right under the plan name, with "/ month" set small and grey beside it. You can compare the three plans in one sweep. Keep that hierarchy when you add buttons.
- The palette is restrained: near-black text, grey for secondary text, and one blue used only for the logo and the button. That leaves the blue free to mark a recommended plan or the per-card buttons without adding a colour.
- The cards share one grid, with equal gutters, matching inner margins, and baselines that line up across all three, so the comparison reads row by row.
