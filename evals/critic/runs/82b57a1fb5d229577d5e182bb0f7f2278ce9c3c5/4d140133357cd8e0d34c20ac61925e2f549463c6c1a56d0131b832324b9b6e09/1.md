**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold, near-black headline "Notes for ferry passengers". It then drops onto three blocks of text that look greyed out, like disabled or placeholder copy rather than the information itself. It reads as a plain, calm information page for people using a river ferry. Below the headline it feels washed out, as if it hadn't finished loading.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a web page that gives ferry passengers the practical facts (route, frequency, bikes, winter changes) so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the piece fails at its one job: every useful fact on the page is set in a pale grey that is hard to read, so the headline is the only thing that reads clearly. The second problem is that the line most likely to change someone's plans, the winter-timetable warning, is the smallest and least noticeable thing on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **All body text is too faint. Defect.** Both paragraphs and the winter note are in a light grey on white. By eye it is around #9CA3AF, which comes out near 2.5:1, at the bottom of the range where this defect occurs. Jev also flagged "Text reads as faint" at 0.98. People reading a timetable outdoors on a phone in sunlight, and anyone with low vision, will struggle with the exact words they came for. Because the headline is the only dark element, the page also loses its range of light and dark: in greyscale it is one dark bar above a pale haze, and nothing separates body text from a footnote. Fix: darken the text until it reaches at least 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3). A dark grey around #4B5563 or darker keeps the soft look and still passes. Measure the real hex to confirm.
2. **The warning that changes plans is the quietest line. Defect.** "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel." is smaller than the body text and just as pale. It reads as a disclaimer. In winter, a passenger who skims past it turns up to a ferry that isn't running. Fix: give it at least body size and full-strength colour, and set it apart from the paragraphs, for example by moving it up under the headline or adding a label like "Winter". Even better, say which months it applies to.
3. **The times are vague exactly where the reader needs numbers. Defect (copy).** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" doesn't tell anyone when the first or last ferry leaves, and that is the fact people act on and will hold you to. "About twelve minutes in calm weather" is fine, but the service hours aren't. Fix: give the first and last departure times (for example "from 6:40 am to 10:00 pm"), or link to the timetable.

**Smaller things.**
- At around 70–76 characters, the first paragraph's lines are at the long end of the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrowing the column slightly would help, and there is room to spare.
- The heading is generic: "Notes" says nothing about what's inside. Something like "Taking the river ferry" or "Ferry times and bikes" tells the reader what they'll get.

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy.** There is one clear top level (the bold headline) and a single left-aligned column with a consistent left edge and even gaps between paragraphs. It's easy to scan. Keep it.
- **Plain, readable setting.** Upright sans, flush left, short paragraphs, comfortable line spacing. Once the colour is fixed, this setting will read well.
- **The bicycle paragraph.** It says who does what and when ("wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"), which is how the rest of the copy should read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, where most people would read this at the landing.
