**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big white headline on the blue, then the subhead, then the white "Try it free" button. It reads as a consumer budgeting app's landing page, and it feels clean and confident, but like a template. Then the eye slides right into a wide field of empty blue and down into a blank white band, and the page seems to stop before it's finished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the first screen of a marketing page for a personal-budgeting app, meant to get people who feel their spending is out of control to start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The words do their job, but the page doesn't. Nothing shows the product, so the viewer has to take "see where every dollar goes" on trust. The empty right half of the hero and the dead bottom band make it look unfinished rather than calm.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's nothing to see on a page that promises you'll see something.** The headline is "See where every dollar goes this month", and the right half of the blue hero (about x 800–1216) is empty. The viewer is promised a view of their money but never sees one: no category bars, no "you're $40 from your dining limit" alert, nothing that shows what they'd get. Visitors to a money app decide on trust, and a picture of the product is what turns the claim into something they can judge for themselves. Without it, "Try it free" asks them to sign up blind, and connecting a bank account is a lot to ask blind. If a picture is meant to be there and didn't load in this capture, that's the thing to check. If not, this is a **defect**. Fix: put a real product shot in that right half, ideally the over-limit warning the subhead describes, so the picture and the headline make one argument together. *(Defect; my reasoning, no approved source.)*
2. **The layout is weighted top-left and runs out of steam.** All the hero content hugs the left 60%, so the blue band tips left, and the empty right side does no work because nothing sits against it. Below, the feature row is pressed about 47 px under the hero edge, and then roughly 220 px of white runs to the bottom of the screen. So the eye path ends twice in dead ends: right after the buttons, and again after the features. Arnheim (*Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance") says a heavy side needs a counterweight, and Ruder (*Typography*, "Contrasts") says empty space should be gathered into deliberate areas set against dense ones. Here the empty space is left over, not shaped. Fix: the product visual from point 1 is the counterweight for the hero. Then give the feature row real space above it and something to lead into below it (social proof, the security point expanded, or the next section starting above the fold), so the screen doesn't end in blank white. *(Convention.)*
3. **The ask is generic.** "Try it free" and "Watch the tour" could go on any SaaS page. They don't say what the viewer gets or how long it takes. The subhead has the real hook: connect your accounts and get warned before you overspend. The button could point at it, for example "Connect an account and see this month free", so the viewer knows exactly what clicking does and can check the value against their own spending. *(Taste.)*

**Smaller things.**
- The feature row sits much closer to the hero edge than to anything below it, so it reads as stuck to the hero, not as its own section. Give it a clear top margin and make the space below it purposeful.
- "Read-only access" is the strongest trust point for a money app ("can never move money"), but it gets the same weight as "Shared budgets" in the third column. Consider pulling it up near the buttons, where the hesitation actually happens. *(Taste.)*

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and talks to the viewer: "every dollar", "this month". It's set big, bold, and tightly spaced, so it's clearly the first thing you read. Keep its size and wording.
- The subhead says what the product does in one sentence with three verbs (connect, set, get a heads-up). That's why this page is clear even with no picture.
- The button hierarchy is right: a solid white primary next to an outlined secondary, at a comfortable size, and everything hangs from one clean 64 px left edge.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image or animation was supposed to fill the empty right half and hadn't loaded when this was captured. Check it in a live browser.
- Focus states on the two buttons and the nav links.
