**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, drops to the tall blue Organic search bar, then moves along a row of six bars in six colours. It reads as a growth report in an internal analytics tool. It's tidy, calm and easy to trust. The story ("a third of sign-ups, up from a quarter", "events move to referrals") is in a small text column on the right that the eye reaches last. The chart itself doesn't show that story.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a quarterly growth view where a manager or team learns which channels brought Q3 sign-ups and why the budget is shifting.

**Verdict.** This is a clean, honest chart with good copy. It shows the Q3 numbers well, but it doesn't show the change the page is about. The chart gives equal colour to every channel and has no Q2, so the two claims that matter live only in the sidebar text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't show what the page says.** The sidebar's main claim is a change: organic went from a quarter of sign-ups to a third. The chart shows only Q3, so a reader can't see the growth, only take it on trust. Show Q2 next to Q3. A pale Q2 bar or a tick behind each Q3 bar would do it, or a share label such as "34%, was 25%". Then the reader sees the shift for themselves. *Defect* (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design": a number needs a comparison, such as the previous period).
2. **Six colours that mean nothing hide the two bars the story is about.** Each bar is a different hue, but the x-axis already names every channel, so the colour adds no information. It also gives every bar equal weight. The text singles out Organic search (the win) and Podcast ads + Events (the cut), but nothing in the chart picks them out. Use one neutral colour for all the bars. Then use one accent on Organic search, plus perhaps a muted second marker on Events and Podcast ads, so the chart and the text point at the same bars. *Convention* (Few, pitfall 12; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 19.1).
3. **The headline names the topic, not the finding.** "New sign-ups by channel" tells you what the chart is, and the finding sits in a 15 px side column under "What changed this quarter". A headline such as "Organic search now brings a third of sign-ups" would put the story in the first thing everyone reads. You could also annotate the organic bar directly. *Taste* (the source for this rule isn't on my approved list): the headline is the only line every viewer reads, so it should carry the finding.

**Smaller things.**
- Red on Paid social and green on Podcast ads read as bad and good on a growth page, which is the opposite of the story: podcast ads are one of the weak channels. This goes away once the colours are dropped (finding 2). *Convention* (Apple HIG: Color).
- In the sidebar, the second paragraph is a decision ("The events budget moves to referrals in Q4"), not something that "changed this quarter". Either the heading should cover decisions too ("What changed, and what we're doing"), or the decision could be set apart as its own line.

**What's working.**
- The data handling is honest and complete. Bars start at zero and are sorted by value, every bar is labelled with its value, and the source line gives the period and a total (5,400) that the bars add up to. Keep all of this.
- The sidebar copy is specific and checkable ("a third", "fewer than 700", "after the guide pages were rewritten"), and it ends on a decision. That's what makes this page worth reading. It just needs the chart to back it up.
- It's a quiet page: one typeface, light gridlines, generous margins, and no chart clutter.
