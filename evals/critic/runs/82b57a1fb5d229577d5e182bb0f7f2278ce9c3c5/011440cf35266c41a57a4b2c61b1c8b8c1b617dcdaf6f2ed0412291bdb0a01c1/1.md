**First read.** The eye lands on the big headline "Plan the week with your whole team", moves right to the white card, and stops on the blue "Create account" button. It reads at once as a sign-up page for a team planning tool. It feels calm and tidy, but it could be any SaaS product. (The Jev file came with the image, so I saw it before forming this read.)

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: a sign-up page whose job is to get a team lead to create a free workspace, viewed on a desktop browser.

**Verdict.** The layout works: it's clear what the page is, and there's one obvious action. But the form itself, which is the part that has to work, has no field labels, and the only offer on the page ("free … for up to five people … invite the rest later") can be read two ways. Fix the labels first.

**What would move it most**
1. **Every field is labelled only by its placeholder (defect).** All five inputs in the card ("Enter first name", "Enter last name", "name@company.com", "At least 10 characters", "Company name") have no label outside the field. When someone starts typing, the hint disappears. That leaves them with a column of filled boxes and no way to check which is which before they submit. The worst case is the fourth field. Nothing says it's the password. "At least 10 characters" is a rule, not a name, so people have to guess what the field is for, and the rule disappears at the moment they need it. Autofill and screen readers also lose the field's name. Nielsen's heuristic 6 (recognition rather than recall) and NN/g's "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful" both cover this. Fix: put a visible label above each field (First name, Last name, Work email, Password, Company name). Keep the examples as placeholders if you like. Move "At least 10 characters" to helper text under the password field, where it stays visible while people type.
2. **The offer can be read two ways (defect, copy).** "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." One reading is that the workspace is free for good, with a five-person cap. The other is that "free" is a trial, or that inviting "the rest of the team" will cost money. The second sentence even suggests the cap stops mattering later. This is the one line people will hold you to when a bill or a paywall shows up at person six. Fix: say what happens at six in so many words, for example "Free for up to five people. Add more on a paid plan whenever you're ready." Or, if more people really are free, say that instead.
3. **The headline could sit on anyone's page (taste).** "Plan the week with your whole team" fits every task tool there is. Visitors who are comparing products learn nothing about why to sign up here rather than with the tool they already use. The headline is the one big element on the page, so it's worth more. Fix: say what Tallyboard does that their current setup doesn't, in terms they'd recognise from their own week.

**Smaller things**
- Input borders: they look like a pale blue-gray, well under 3:1 against the white card (WCAG 1.4.11). Fields read as faint outlines, not boxes. Darken the border until it measures at least 3:1.
- "Sign in" link under the button: it's the same gray as "Already have an account?" with no underline, so returning users have to guess it's a link. Underline it or give it the brand blue.
- Required fields: nothing says whether "Company name" (or any field) is optional (NN/g, Budiu). If all fields are required, say so once. If not, add "(optional)" after the label of each optional field.
- Jev evidence, "form fields labeled only by hint text": this is finding 1, all five inputs in the card.

**What's working**
- One clear primary action. "Create account" is the only saturated block in the card and spans its full width, so there's no doubt what to press.
- A disciplined type scale. The page uses about four sizes with big steps between them (headline, card title, body, small link line), plus one blue that marks the brand and the action. Keep that restraint when you add labels: use the small size in a medium weight, not a new size.
- The two columns line up. "Tallyboard" sits level with "Create your account", and the left column's empty lower half is balanced by the heavy headline. That's why the page feels settled.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Focus rings on the fields and button, which matter even more once the borders are this pale.
- Whether the password and email fields use the right input types (masking, email keyboard on mobile), and when validation fires.
