**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the subhead, then stops at the dark "Get started" button. After that there is nowhere to go. The right half of the screen and the bottom third are empty cream. It reads as a group trip-planning app's landing page, and it feels calm and tidy, but also bare, like a template waiting for its content.

**What it's for.** Assumption, since you didn't say: this is the first screen of a marketing site for a group trip planner, and its job is to get a visiting trip organiser to click "Get started".

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The type and hierarchy are clean, but the page makes a promise and never shows it. There's no product, no trip and no group on screen, so a visitor has to take "in one place" on trust. Half the frame is blank where that proof should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half is empty, so nothing shows the claim** (defect). Everything sits in the left 55% of the frame, and right of the buttons there's nothing at all. A viewer is told the product replaces "a dozen chat threads" but never sees what replaces them, so they can't judge whether it's better than what they use now. The empty half also makes the page tip left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). And the eye path dead-ends at the buttons. This doesn't read as chosen white space; it reads as a hole. If a hero image or product shot is meant to sit there and didn't load in this capture, check that first; if so, this finding becomes "make sure it loads". If nothing is meant to sit there, fill it with the product doing the headline's job: one real trip, with flights, a stay and a day plan in one view, plus signs of a group agreeing (avatars, votes, a "3 of 4 in" marker). The picture should finish the headline's idea, not decorate it. That last point is my judgment, not a cited rule.
2. **The copy could sit on any planner's page, and the ask is vague** (defect, copy). "Plan the whole trip in one place" is the line every travel app uses, so a visitor can't tell what's different here. The sharp idea is buried in the subhead: *a group agreeing on a trip without the chat-thread chaos*. That's the pain point, and it should lead. "Get started" doesn't name the thing the viewer would actually do. Make the ask concrete and from their world, such as "Start a trip and invite your group". Better still, set it next to what they already have: "Paste your group chat's links and see them as one plan" would let them judge the value against their current mess on their own terms.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "…trip in / one place", leaving "in" at the end of line 1. Break it at the phrase ("Plan the whole trip / in one place") or rewrite the line, which point 2 already asks for.
- The nav doesn't mark the current page. That's minor on the home page, but give the active item a state on the inner pages.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clear: one dominant bold headline, a clearly smaller grey subhead at a comfortable line length (about 60 characters), and buttons below. Each level has an obvious job, so keep this scale.
- The primary/secondary pairing is right. "Get started" is solid teal and "See pricing" is outlined in the same teal, so there's exactly one main action. Offering pricing as the second option is a good, honest choice.
- The restrained palette (cream, near-black and one muted teal) is calm and fits a product that promises to take the chaos out of group planning. Keep it once imagery arrives, and let the picture carry the colour.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty right half is a hero image that failed to load (check in a live browser). Also the focus indicators on the nav links and buttons, and how the two-column hero stacks on a phone.
