**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline, reads the clear sentence under it, then drops to a row of four identical solid-blue buttons and stalls. Nothing marks which one is the answer, and "Discard changes" looks exactly like "Save changes". It reads as a confirmation dialog in a shared-calendar app. The top half feels calm and clear; the button row feels uncertain.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a modal shown when someone leaves an edited shared calendar. It needs them to confirm publishing the new rehearsal times quickly and safely, without throwing the edits away by accident.

**Verdict.** The words do their job, but the buttons don't. Four equal-weight buttons, with the one destructive action dressed like the safe one, turn a one-second decision into a read-every-label decision and make an accidental discard easy. This needs fixing before it ships.

**What would move it most**
1. **No primary action** (defect, convention). All four buttons in the bottom row use the same filled blue, the same size and the same weight. The headline asks a yes/no question ("Save changes…?"), but the button that answers "yes" is the same as the rest. The viewer has to read all four labels to find it, and nothing shows what Enter will do. Material Design 3 ("Buttons") and Apple HIG both call for one high-emphasis action per view, with the others in lower-emphasis styles. Fix: keep "Save changes" as the only filled button and make "Keep editing" an outlined or text button.
2. **"Discard changes" looks safe and sits among the safe actions** (defect). It is second from the left, 16 px from "Export as file" and "Keep editing", in the same blue as "Save changes". One click throws away the rehearsal edits, and nothing in the styling warns about that. People scanning for a way out ("not now") can easily hit it. Apple HIG ("Alerts") and Material ("Dialogs") both say to set a destructive action apart: give it a destructive style, usually red text or a red outline, never the default, and place it away from the safe pair. On macOS that means alone at the far left. Better still, offer undo after discarding.
3. **"Export as file" is promoted to an equal choice** (convention, Nielsen on progressive disclosure). Exporting has nothing to do with the question being asked. As a fourth peer it adds one more label to read and widens the row, which is part of why the row feels heavy. Fix: remove it from this dialog, or make it a quiet text link, since export belongs in the calendar's own menu.

**Smaller things.**
- The button row's left edge sits about 4 px left of the headline and body text, and its right edge runs past the end of the body line. It is close enough to look like a near-miss (Arnheim on placements that are almost aligned). Align the first button's box to the text edge.
- Button order is muddled against platform conventions. Once Export is gone and Discard is moved apart, the order should be [Discard] … [Keep editing] [Save changes], with the primary button last.

**What's working.**
- The headline is a direct question that names what is at stake ("the shared calendar"), so the decision is clear before the viewer reaches the buttons. Keep it.
- The body line says the consequence in plain terms: the change is public, and it says who will see it. That is exactly what someone needs to know before confirming. Keep that wording.
- The button labels are verb + object ("Discard changes", "Keep editing") rather than "OK/Cancel", so once the styling is fixed each one can be understood alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button gets default focus and responds to Enter or Esc. Make sure it is never "Discard changes".
- Hover, pressed and focus states for the buttons.
