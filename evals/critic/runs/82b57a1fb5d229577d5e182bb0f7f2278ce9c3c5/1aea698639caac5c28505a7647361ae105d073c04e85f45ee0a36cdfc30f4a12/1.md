**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline at top left, "Notes for ferry passengers", and drops into three grey-black paragraphs. The paragraphs are centred, so each line starts at a different point, and the eye has to hunt for every line start. It reads as a plain information page for a small river ferry. It feels calm and tidy from a distance, but loose up close, because the headline and the text don't share a left edge. The right ~45% of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page telling people how to use a local river ferry (route, how often it runs, bikes, winter service), so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. It's legible, calm and has a clear top. Two things hold it back. The centred body text makes three short paragraphs harder to read than they should be. And for a page passengers will plan around, it gives no times anyone can act on.

**What would move it most**
1. **Centred paragraphs under a flush-left headline (defect).** All three paragraphs (rows ~145–520) are centred on an axis around x≈440, and each runs four lines. The headline is flush left at x≈120. So the body's left edge wanders: lines start at roughly 125, 129, 134 and 137 px, and some start much further in ("the tide turns…", "during the crossing.", "changes for the coming week."). The reader loses their place at the start of every line, which is where a reader's eye goes back to. Lines that start a few pixels off the headline's edge also look like a missed alignment, not a choice (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"). The centring also has no reason to be there: nothing else on the page sits on that axis. Fix: set the paragraphs flush left on the headline's edge, and keep centring for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* 2.1.3). This one change also fixes the grid and the alignment.
2. **The copy gives no times anyone can plan by (defect, copy).** This page exists to get someone onto a boat. Yet "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "a little longer when the tide turns" and "leaves the quay before dusk" give no first sailing, last sailing, or winter cut-off. The reader is sent to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing", which means they have to be at the landing already. Someone planning from home can't tell whether a 9 pm crossing exists. "Before dusk" changes through the season, so it can be read two ways, and a passenger who guesses late is stranded. Also, nothing says whether foot passengers pay or how much: only bikes are "at no extra charge". Fix: give the first and last crossing times (summer and winter), the winter date range, and the fare. Put the times where a scanning eye finds them, for example a short line or small table under the headline, not buried in sentence three of paragraph three.

**Smaller things.**
- Jev flagged "A paragraph is centered or right-aligned" (p 0.83). Confirmed: it's all three body paragraphs, covered in finding 1.
- The headline is generic ("Notes for…"). Naming the route ("North landing ⇄ Market quay ferry") would tell a stranger at once whether this is their boat. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean: one bold headline, clearly larger and heavier than the regular body, and only two type sizes. Keep it that simple.
- The body is a comfortable reading size, with lines around 65–72 characters and generous leading. Once it's set flush left, it will read easily.
- Each paragraph holds one topic (route and timing, bikes, winter), and the space between paragraphs is clearly bigger than the line spacing. That chunking is right.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, and whether the empty right side is meant for content (a map, the timetable) that didn't render. Check a live browser.
