**Verdict.** The page is clear and well built, but the chart misleads on the one bar the board will act on. The Q4 2026 forecast is drawn exactly like the seven quarters of actuals, so it reads as a real drop. The 3D bars also add noise and blur where each bar ends.

**What would move it most**
1. **The forecast looks like a real result (defect).** The Q4 2026 bar at the far right is the same solid blue as the actuals. The only sign that it's a forecast is a bracket at the end of the subtitle, "(Q4 2026 is the forecast)", and the source line. A director scanning the page sees 705 then 560 and reads a 21% fall that has already happened. A forecast should look different from observed data (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). Draw that bar hollow, hatched or paler, label it "Q4 2026 (forecast)" on the axis, and show a range if the September forecast has one.
2. **The 3D bars blur the values and add noise (convention: Wilke ch. 26.1; Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2; Few).** Each bar has a lighter top and a dark side face. That makes about 15 px of extra shape that carries no data. It also leaves it unclear whether a bar's value is at its front edge or the back of its top. The value labels float above the top, so you can't tell which edge they belong to. Draw the bars flat so each value is read from one length. The value labels then sit cleanly on the bar tops. Since every bar carries a value label, you could also thin out the y-axis and gridlines.
3. **The headline and chart don't point at the same thing, and the bigger story goes unsaid (taste).** The headline is about third quarters, but Q3 2025 and Q3 2026 look the same as every other bar, so the reader has to find them. Give those two bars the accent colour and draw the rest in a muted tone. Q3 is the strongest quarter in both years, so the pattern holds, but two years is thin evidence for "each".

   The data also shows steady year-on-year growth: Q1 410→455, Q2 520→590, Q3 610→705, which is +11 to +16% each quarter. A board will care about that more than seasonality, and it's the context that makes the Q4 forecast of 560 against 480 read as growth, not a fall. Say it in the headline or subtitle, for example "Shipments peak each third quarter and are up about 15% on last year". Another option is to place each 2026 quarter beside its 2025 quarter.

**Smaller things.**
- Subtitle, "eight quarters to Q4 2026 (Q4 2026 is the forecast)": it says Q4 2026 twice and reads awkwardly. Try "Q1 2025 to Q4 2026; Q4 2026 is forecast". The direct axis label from finding 1 can then carry the main signal.

**What's working.**
- The page structure is solid. The serif headline clearly leads, with a sans-serif subtitle, chart title, units line and footer as distinct lower levels. Everything hangs off one left edge at x≈80, and the footer has the source and page number. Keep this frame as it is.
- The headline states a finding instead of naming a topic. That's the right habit for board slides, so keep it when you rewrite.
- The axis starts at zero, the units ("Thousands of units") are stated, and the quarters are in time order with direct value labels. The scales are honest apart from the 3D effect.
