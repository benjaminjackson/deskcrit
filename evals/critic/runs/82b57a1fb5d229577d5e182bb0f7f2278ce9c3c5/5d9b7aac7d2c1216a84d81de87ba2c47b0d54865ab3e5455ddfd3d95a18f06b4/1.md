**First read.** The eye goes straight to the purple "Sign in" button, then up to the red-bordered email field and its error. It reads at once as a sign-in screen for a product called Quillpost, caught in its invalid-email state. It feels calm, tidy, competent, and generic.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the sign-in screen for returning Quillpost users, shown here in the error state you get after typing a bad email address.

**Verdict.** It does its job. A returning user knows what this is, where to type, and what to press, and the error is clear and placed where it belongs. What's in the way is small: the way in for new users ("Create an account") doesn't look clickable, and the default field border looks too faint.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link** (defect). In the line at the foot of the card, "New here? Create an account" is all one grey, one weight, and has no underline. The link can't be told from the question in front of it. A new user who lands here has to guess which words to click, or doesn't notice there's anything to click. It also breaks the card's own pattern: "Forgot your password?" just above is purple, so the two links on one card look as if they come from two different designs (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: elements of one kind follow one program). Nielsen Norman Group found that links styled like static text draw less attention, and WCAG 1.4.1 asks that a link be told apart from the text around it by more than colour. Fix: style "Create an account" like the other link, in the purple, ideally with an underline, and leave "New here?" grey.
2. **The default field border looks too pale** (convention, WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11). The password field's light grey border on white looks well under the 3:1 that an input's edge needs. With nothing else marking the field, someone with low vision or on a washed-out screen sees a faint box, or nothing. Measure the border colour against white. If it's under 3:1, darken it until it reaches 3:1. The red error border shows the state is already there to build on.

**Smaller things.**
- Error copy (taste): "Enter an email address in the format name@example.com" is correct and says how to fix it (WCAG 3.3.3). But it makes the user compare their entry to the pattern to find the fault, and the fault here is the missing ".com". When the check can tell what's missing, say it: "This address is missing the ending, like .com". It's also worth catching likely typos of common domains if your validation can.
- No show-password toggle on the password field (common sense, not a catalog rule). When a sign-in fails, a user can't check what they typed in 12 masked dots. Add a "Show" control inside the field.

**What's working.**
- The order of importance is clear. There's one filled purple button, the heading and labels are bold dark type, and the helper text and links sit a step lower. Nothing competes with the action.
- The error is done properly. It has a red border plus a written message directly under the field it belongs to. The meaning isn't carried by colour alone, and the user isn't sent to a banner at the top. Keep this pattern for the password error too.
- The spacing groups things correctly. Each label sits tight to its field, "Forgot your password?" sits with the password field rather than the button, and the gaps between groups are clearly bigger than the gaps inside them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus: whether a focused field or button shows a visible ring of at least 2 px at 3:1. On a keyboard-heavy form like this it matters, so check it in a live browser.
