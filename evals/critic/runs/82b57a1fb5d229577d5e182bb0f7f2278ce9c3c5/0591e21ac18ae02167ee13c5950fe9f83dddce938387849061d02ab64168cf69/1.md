I have to untangle "Quayside ferry crossings by daylight, spring through early summer" word by word, then I drop to a calm, readable paragraph. It reads as the top of a web page for a local ferry. It feels confident and plain, but the headline looks broken rather than bold.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page that tells local riders and visitors when and how often the river ferry runs this season.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline is set so tight that its lines overlap, so the biggest, first thing on the page is the hardest to read. Behind that is a content gap: the page calls itself a "Spring timetable" and gives no times.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline lines collide.** This is a defect, in the four-line headline from y≈150 to 370. The line spacing looks well below the type size. The descenders of "Quayside" and "ferry" run into "crossings by daylight", the g's and y's of "daylight" and "through" cut into the line below, and "early" sits on top of "summer". The viewer has to work to separate letters that belong to different lines, and words like "daylight" and "through" smear into dark blobs. Heavy weight at large size is fine; overlap is not. Bringhurst (p. 37) allows tight, even negative, leading on display type only "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide." The fix is to open the line spacing until no letter on one line touches a letter on the next, and keep it tighter than the body text so the four lines still read as one block. This could be a CSS line-height bug, such as a fixed pixel value set for a smaller size, rather than a choice; either way, fix it.
2. **It's labelled a timetable but has no timetable.** This is a defect in the copy and content. The eyebrow says "Spring timetable" and the headline promises crossings "spring through early summer". The only schedule information is "every forty minutes", with no first or last sailing, no start time to count the forty minutes from, and no dates for "spring" or "early summer". Someone who came to catch a boat leaves unable to plan one. The fix is to put the times right under the paragraph: first and last departure from each landing, and the date range. If the full table is lower on the page, the first screen should at least show the first and last sailing. The empty lower 40% of this screen is room for it.
3. **The headline is too long, and it breaks badly.** This one is taste. Sixteen words at this size take four lines and most of the screen's height, and the last line is a lone "summer", split from "early". A shorter headline (for example "Quayside ferry: spring and early summer crossings") would fit in two or three lines and come across in one glance. If you keep the wording, break it at the phrase ("…by daylight, / spring through early summer").

**Smaller things.**
- Body paragraph: "weather." sits alone on the last line. Narrowing or widening the column slightly would pull it back up (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- "Spring timetable" heading: it names a section whose content isn't there. Either add the timetable under it or rename it to match what follows (WCAG 2.4.6).
