**First read.** My eye goes first to the headline "Plan the week with your whole team", then over to the blue "Create account" button, then down the five fields. Within two seconds it reads as the sign-up page of a team-planning product: tidy, calm and competent. It is also so standard that nothing on it is clearly Tallyboard's own.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop sign-up page that turns a visitor, probably a team lead who already clicked through from marketing, into a free workspace owner.

**Verdict.** As a layout it works: clear focal point, one obvious action, an orderly grid. The form is the weak part. Every field is labelled only by its placeholder, and that makes the form harder to fill in correctly and harder to check before submitting. That's the one thing to fix before launch.

**What would move it most**
1. **The fields have no labels, only placeholders.** This is a defect (Nielsen 6; NN/g on placeholders).
   - All five fields on the right card use grey placeholder text as their only label.
   - Once someone starts typing, the label is gone. A user who tabs back to check their entries sees "Jordan", "Lee", an email, a row of dots and a company. Nothing tells them which field is which.
   - The password field is the worst case. Its placeholder, "At least 10 characters", never says it's a password, so the viewer has to work that out. The 10-character rule also disappears on the first keystroke, so a user typing an 8-character password can't see the rule they're breaking.
   - "Enter first name" and "name@company.com" are hint styles, not labels, and the five fields don't share one style.
   - **Fix direction:** put a short visible label above every field (First name, Last name, Work email, Password, Company name). Move "At least 10 characters" to helper text under the password field, where it stays visible while the user types.
2. **"Company name" doesn't say whether it's required.** This is a defect (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
   - No field is marked required or optional. Company name is the one a solo team lead or freelancer might pause on: do they have to make something up?
   - **Fix direction:** if it's optional, add "(optional)" after its label. If everything is required, leave it unmarked but consider whether you need the field at sign-up at all, since four fields convert better than five.
3. **"Sign in" doesn't look like a link.** This is a defect (WCAG technique G183 / F73).
   - In "Already have an account? Sign in" under the button, "Sign in" has the same grey and weight as the question before it.
   - A returning user who lands here by mistake, the most likely person to look for that line, has to guess that the last two words are clickable.
   - **Fix direction:** set "Sign in" in the brand blue and underline it, or make it medium weight with an underline.
4. **The copy stops at the fact and doesn't make the ask specific.** This is taste.
   - "Start a free workspace for up to five people" leaves the obvious question open: what happens at six? Free forever, or a trial? Because it's a limit the viewer will hold you to, one plain clause helps, such as "free for teams of up to five, no card needed".
   - "Create account" names a chore, not the outcome. "Create your free workspace" repeats the promise right where the user commits.
   - The left column has nothing that sets the product against what the viewer uses today, whether a spreadsheet, a whiteboard or a Monday stand-up. So the page could belong to any of a dozen planners.
   - One line, or a small product glimpse below the paragraph, that shows the week you'd get would give the viewer a reason to choose this one. The empty lower-left already has room for it.

**Smaller things.**
- The input borders on the card look pale grey-blue on white, possibly under 3:1. Since the borders alone mark out the fields, check them against WCAG 1.4.11 and darken them if they fail.
- The placeholder grey looks about 4.5:1. Measure it once labels exist, because it matters less after the fix.

**What's working.**
- The headline clearly dominates: large, bold, dark and set tight on two lines. It states the job in the reader's terms ("the week", "your whole team"). Keep it.
- There is exactly one primary action. The full-width blue button is the only saturated block besides the wordmark, so the next step is never in doubt.
- The two columns share a clear top line: the wordmark and "Create your account" sit level. The space is gathered into big quiet areas rather than spread thin, which gives the page its calm.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The focus state of the fields and button: check that the outline is visible and at least 3:1.
- What error messages look like, and whether the password rule is checked when the user leaves the field.
- Whether the password field masks input and has a show/hide control.
