**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the subhead, then stops at two green buttons that seem to offer the same thing. After that there is nothing to see: the right half of the screen and the bottom third are empty cream. It reads as the opening screen of a group trip-planning app's website. It feels calm and well set, but bare and unfinished, like a template before the picture went in.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the top of the home page for a group trip planner, and its job is to get a visitor who plans trips with friends to start using it.

**Verdict.** The type and spacing are clean, but the page doesn't do its job yet. It describes the product without showing it, and it ends on two buttons that mean the same thing. A visitor gets no picture of what "one place" looks like and no clear next step.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing shows the product, and half the screen is empty.** Everything sits in the left half, and the right half, about 600 px wide, plus the area under the buttons holds nothing. Because all the weight sits on one side with nothing to balance it, the page looks unfinished rather than calm (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The bigger cost is that the headline promises "one place" but the viewer never sees it. They have to imagine a shared trip with flights, stays and day plans, and they can't tell this app from any other planner. *Defect.* Fix: put a picture of the product on the right. A real trip board for a named group (for example "Lisbon, 4 friends, 12–16 May") showing one flight, one stay and a day plan with votes or comments would prove the headline instead of repeating it. As taste: the picture and the headline should be two halves of one idea, so the picture should show the group agreeing, which the copy only claims.
2. **"Get started" and "Start now" are one action offered twice, and neither says what happens.** The filled button and the outlined one have labels that mean the same thing. This is exactly the duplicate-control case (Nielsen heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). The viewer stops to work out how the two differ, finds that they don't, and the main action loses force. Neither label says what the click does, either. *Defect.* Fix: keep one main button and name the result, for example "Plan a trip with your group" or "Start a trip and invite friends". Give the second button a different job, such as "See an example trip" or "How it works". That also lets someone look at real output before committing.
3. **The copy stops at the feature and doesn't speak to the viewer's problem.** The subhead gets close with "without a dozen chat threads", which is the one line here that comes from the viewer's own experience. But "a group can agree" keeps it at arm's length, and the headline could sit on any planner's page. *Defect (copy).* Fix: lead with the pain the viewer already has, in "you" terms. For example: "Stop scrolling back through the group chat to find who booked what." Then make "one place" the answer to it.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "…trip in / one place", which strands "in" at the end of the first line and splits the phrase. Break it before "in", or let it run to "Plan the whole trip / in one place".

**What's working.**
- The type is clear and has few levels: a large heavy headline, a grey subhead at about 70 characters a line, and small nav text. The headline is set tight and holds together as one block. Keep this.
- The palette is restrained: warm cream, near-black ink and one deep green used only for the brand and the actions. Any added picture should stay inside it.
- The left edges line up cleanly: logo, headline, subhead and buttons all start on the same line.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the two buttons and the nav links.
- How the layout reflows on a phone, especially where a product picture would go.
