**First read.** My eye goes straight to the bold headline, "Update the card that pays for your subscription," then down into the first paragraph. Within a second it reads as a quiet, well-made help-center article for a SaaS billing product. It feels calm and trustworthy. Nothing fights for attention.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help article for an account owner or billing admin who came here to change the card on their subscription. They want to do it quickly and know what happens if the new card fails.

**Verdict.** The page looks solid, but the article never actually tells the reader how to update the card. The headline promises a task, and the body only describes rules around that task. The second biggest problem is that the retry timeline, the one line with real consequences, can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The task the headline names is missing.** *Defect.* The headline says "Update the card…". The only instruction is half a sentence in the first paragraph: "from the billing page." There are no steps, and "the billing page" isn't a link or a path. Meanwhile "billing history," "roles and permissions," "paying by invoice" and "restore your previous plan" are all links. So a reader who came to do one thing has to work out where the billing page is in the product. Every side topic gets a link while the main action gets none. This breaks Nielsen's recognition-rather-than-recall heuristic, since the reader has to remember or find a screen the page only names. Fix: put the action first. Link "the billing page" directly, or give the menu path and the few steps, right under the headline. Then the conditions follow: who can do it, open invoices, bank transfer.

2. **The decline timeline is ambiguous where it matters most.** *Defect, copy.* Under "When the new card is declined," the text says "after three days and after seven days." It doesn't say what the days count from: the first decline, or the previous retry? It then says "after the second attempt" the plan drops to free. If the original charge counts as attempt one, the second attempt is the day-three retry, and the workspace is downgraded four days earlier than a reader expects. This is a deadline the reader will plan around and hold you to. Fix: name the starting point for both retries ("three and seven days after the first decline," if that's true). Then name the downgrade trigger by its day or as "the last retry," so there's only one reading.

3. **The page doesn't show where you are.** *Convention (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).* In the "In this section" sidebar, "Update a payment card" looks exactly like its four siblings. "Billing" in the top nav isn't marked either. Someone arriving from search sees the breadcrumb but can't tell at a glance which of the five billing articles they're on or which ones are next to it. Fix: mark the current item in the sidebar with one clear cue, such as a bolder weight, a left bar or a tint. Do the same for "Billing" in the header.

**Smaller things**
- Inline links in the body are separated from the text by color alone. The blue and the near-black body text are both dark, so the difference between them looks well under the 3:1 that WCAG technique G183 asks for. Underline the links by default.
- The body text runs about 80–85 characters per line (for example, "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page. The new card is"), which is past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column by roughly a fifth.
- The help center has no visible search box in the header. On a help site, search is the main shortcut (Nielsen 7). Add it if it exists, or show it if it's hidden at this width.

**What's working**
- The headline is written as the reader's task ("Update the card that pays for your subscription"), not as a topic label like "Payment methods." Keep that voice for all article titles.
- The link text names its destination ("check your billing history," "roles and permissions") instead of "click here," so each link makes sense out of context.
- The layout is clean. The wordmark and sidebar share the 96 px left edge, the nav ends on the mirrored right margin, and there's one clear heading level and generous space between paragraphs. It reads as a calm, trustworthy place to sort out money.

**Where it could go further**
1. *Taste.* Builds on finding 1: treat "When the new card is declined" as a short timeline instead of a paragraph. Each step (retry, retry, move to free plan, restore) becomes its own line with its day. Billing admins scan for "what happens when," and a sequence they can see answers that faster than prose.
2. *Taste.* Billing changes worry people. The line "Your workspace stays open during this period" is the most reassuring sentence on the page, and right now it sits mid-paragraph. Give it more emphasis so an anxious reader finds it.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the links get an underline or other non-color cue on hover and focus, and what keyboard focus looks like on the sidebar and nav links.
