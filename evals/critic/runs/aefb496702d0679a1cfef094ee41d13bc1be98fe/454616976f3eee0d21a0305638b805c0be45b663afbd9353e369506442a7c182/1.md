**First read.** The eye goes to "$4.82M" in the first card, then drops to the block of blue bars climbing to the right, then crosses to the plan table, where $4.82M shows up again. It reads straight away as a SaaS finance overview for founders or a finance lead. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. That trust is what's at stake, because the figures don't agree with each other.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard where an exec or finance person checks how 2025 revenue went at a glance and trusts the numbers enough to quote them.

**Verdict.** The layout and hierarchy work, and the chart is honest and clean. The one serious problem is that the three revenue figures can't all be true as labeled. Anyone who adds them up will stop trusting the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The revenue figures don't reconcile, so a finance reader can't tell which one to quote.** *Defect.* The table in the right column, "Revenue by plan, 2025", totals $4.82M, the same as "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" in the first card. But the 12 bars in "Monthly revenue, 2025" (recognized revenue) add up to about $4.49M by eye. December's bar, about $447K, works out to a run rate of about $5.36M a year, which doesn't match the $4.82M ARR either. So "Revenue by plan, 2025" can be read two ways:
   - Plan revenue for 2025. Then it should be about $4.49M and match the chart.
   - ARR by plan at December. Then the title is wrong.

   Either way, a reader who quotes "$4.82M of 2025 revenue" to a board is quoting the wrong number. Separately, ARR at $4.82M and December recognized revenue at about $447K a month only fit together if the gap is explained, for example by revenue that isn't recurring. If that's the case, the page needs to say so.

   Fix: make each heading name the exact measure it shows (WCAG 2.4.6, headings describe their content). If the table is ARR, its title should say ARR and the month. If it's 2025 revenue, its total has to match the chart. Only you know which is true, so check the data before touching the labels.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025" headings sit about 4 px off a shared baseline. That reads as a miss, not a choice (Arnheim; Müller-Brockmann on aligning across columns). Put them on one line.
- The type sizes crowd each other. The page title "Revenue overview" (about 26 px) and the KPI figures (about 30 px) are nearly the same size, and the small text uses about three sizes (14, 13 and 12 px) that barely differ. Pick one display size for the title and the KPIs, or set them clearly apart, and fold the small text into two sizes at most (Bringhurst: work from a scale).
- The "Jan to Dec 2025" pill at top right reads as a static tag. If it's the date picker, it needs a cue such as a chevron or an outline.
- In the plan table, the "Total" row looks just like the plan rows, since every value is bold. Set the total apart with a rule above it, or make only the total bold.
- The chart's title names a topic, not a finding. The steady climb, the dips in April and July, and the fact that December is about 43% above January are all left for the reader to work out. Say the takeaway in the title or the subtitle. This is common sense; no approved source states it.
- The nav item "Overview" is selected, but the page is titled "Revenue overview", and a separate "Revenue" item sits right below it. That leaves the reader asking what the Revenue page shows that this one doesn't. Match the page title to the nav label, or explain the split (Norman, conceptual model).

**What's working.**
- Each KPI card is labeled with its period and gives a comparison with the year before ("+18% vs Dec 2024"). Keep that pattern, because it gives each big number context right next to it.
- The chart is honest and quiet. It has one color, a zero baseline, faint gridlines, units in the subtitle and a source line. Keep it as plain as it is.
- Space does the grouping, not boxes. The tinted cards are kept for the KPIs, and the large empty area under the plan table is one gathered space, not scattered gaps.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: once the figures reconcile, link the plan table to the chart. For example, stack each bar by Starter, Team and Business, or add a line under the table that ties its total to the chart's total. The reader could then see how the plans make up the revenue instead of cross-checking two panels. *Taste.*
2. Let "$4.82M" lead. It is the figure the page exists to report, but it is the same size as "112%" and "1,246". Making the ARR card clearly larger than the other two would tell the exec where to start (Refactoring UI: hierarchy through size and weight). *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date pill and the nav items show hover and focus states, and whether the chart has tooltips with exact monthly values.
