**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place," drops to the gray subhead, and stops at two buttons that seem to say the same thing. Then it drifts right into a large empty cream field and finds nothing there. It reads as a group-trip planner from a calm, competent, template-clean startup. It feels tidy and quiet, and half unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a consumer landing page for a group-trip planning app, and that its job is to get a visitor to start planning or sign up.

**Verdict.** The message is clear but the screen doesn't close the deal. The two buttons are twins, so there is no clear next step, and the right half of the screen is empty, so nothing shows the product or the trip. As shipped, this reads as a draft.

**What would move it most**
1. **The two buttons do the same thing.** "Get started" (filled) and "Start now" (outlined) sit side by side with labels that mean the same action. A visitor stops to work out how they differ. There's no difference to find, so the second button only weakens the first. Neither label says what happens when you click: whether you make an account, create a trip, or invite your group. **Defect** (Nielsen heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; WCAG 2.4.4 link purpose). Fix: keep one button for the action and remove its twin, or give the second button a different job, such as "See pricing" or "How it works". Make the primary label name the first concrete step in the visitor's own terms. Leave which step that is to the maker.
2. **The right half is empty, so the page tips left and nothing backs up the headline.** All the visual weight (headline, subhead, buttons, logo) sits in the upper left, from x≈80 to 680. The right half and the bottom third are blank cream with nothing to counter it, so the layout looks lopsided rather than deliberately spare. The bigger cost is that the claim "flights, stays, and day plans together" is never shown. The visitor has to take it on trust, and a travel product with no sign of a place or a trip looks like anyone's SaaS page. **Defect** (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). If a product image or illustration is meant to sit there and failed to load, this is a capture problem, and you should check the page live. If it's meant to be empty, put something on the right that shows the product doing the subhead's job, such as one trip with its flights, stays, and plans in one view, so that the image and the headline make the case together.
3. **The ask doesn't let the viewer judge the value.** The subhead sets up a good comparison, "without a dozen chat threads." That's something the viewer already has and dislikes. But the page drops it at the button, and nothing shows the before and after. **Taste.** Direction: carry that contrast with group chats into whatever fills the right side or into the button. The comparison is already in the copy, so let it do the selling.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line break: "Plan the whole trip in / one place" leaves "in" hanging at the end of line 1. Break it at the phrase, "Plan the whole trip / in one place". This is taste: the reference entry says its sources for heading breaks are unverified.

**What's working.**
- The type is disciplined. I count three clear levels: a heavy, tightly spaced headline, a regular gray subhead at about 20 px, and small nav text. They're all in one family, with no sizes that collide. Keep that scale when you add content.
- The subhead copy is concrete and speaks to the viewer. It names the three things kept together (flights, stays, day plans) and the pain it removes ("a dozen chat threads"). It's the best line on the page.
- The palette is restrained, deep green on warm cream with dark gray text, and every text color looks comfortably above 4.5:1.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: make "a dozen chat threads" the visual idea. This is taste. The piece is selling relief from group-chat chaos, and the copy already names that chaos. A right-hand visual that shows the mess turning into one plan would make the page memorable instead of generic. The maker supplies the actual product view.
2. Let the deep green own the action. This is taste. Right now the green is spread across the logo, the filled button, and the outline button. Once the twin button is gone, the filled "Get started" green would be the one saturated mark in the hero, and the eye would land there second, every time.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is a hero image that didn't load. Check it in a live browser.
- Focus indicators on the buttons and nav links, and how the two-column hero reflows on a phone.
