**First read.** The eye lands on the three big KPI figures, 184,220 first, then drops to the large chart and its three lines climbing steadily. It reads as a clean, calm SaaS acquisition dashboard where everything is green and going up. It feels trustworthy, and also anonymous: nothing on it is specific to this business.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this: a marketer or founder checks at a glance whether acquisition improved over the last 12 weeks and which channel drove it.

**Verdict.** It is competent and easy to scan, but it reports numbers instead of answering the question. The biggest problem is that the chart and the Visits card don't agree. The three channel lines add up to roughly 138k visits (by eye), while the card says 184,220. A careful viewer will stop trusting both.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't add up to the headline number (defect).** I added up the weekly points in "Visits by channel" by eye: Search is about 71k, Social about 44k and Email about 23k, which is about 138k. The Visits card above it says 184,220. That leaves about a quarter of the traffic missing from the chart, with nothing to say why. A viewer who notices will doubt every number on the page. One who doesn't will think these three channels are all the traffic and misjudge each channel's share. If other channels exist, the chart needs to show them, for example as an "Other" series or a gray total line, or at least say that it shows only the top channels. If they don't exist, the data is wrong. The y axis has the same gap: it has no unit, and it doesn't say whether "8k" means visits per week. The x ticks suggest weekly points, but the viewer has to work that out. Tufte and Few both say a reader should never have to guess what a number measures.
2. **The chart names a topic instead of a finding, and its key costs extra looking (convention and taste).** The title "Visits by channel" says what the chart contains. The viewer still has to work out what it shows: Search does the most and grows the most, Social is up but jumps around, and Email is small and steady. Saying the takeaway in the title is taste on my part, since no approved source covers it. I argue for it because on a glance-and-go dashboard, the sentence is what people remember. Separately, the colors are explained only by the legend at the top left, so the viewer's eye has to go back and forth. The orange and green may also be hard to tell apart for viewers with red-green color blindness. The lines happen not to cross, so position helps, but the series names exist only in that legend (WCAG 1.4.1). There is open space at the right end of each line, so label each series there and drop the legend (Tufte; Wilke).

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count about seven sizes. The small end (axis labels at about 12px, deltas at 13, card labels and legend at 14, chart title at about 17) runs in steps too small to read as levels. "Overview" at about 24 is close to the KPI figures at about 28. Keep about four sizes and make finer distinctions with weight and gray instead (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1).
- "Overview" names nothing. Either cut it, since the brand bar already says where you are, or use it to say what period the page covers (WCAG 2.4.6).
- The chart title "Visits by channel" looks about 2px to the right of the KPI labels above it (about x=71 against x=69). It's a near miss that makes the edge look unfinished. Align it exactly (Arnheim).
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top right looks like static text. If it is a date-range picker, give it a border or a chevron so it looks clickable. If it is only a label, it's fine.
- "vs previous 12 weeks" appears three times. Saying it once, next to "Last 12 weeks", would remove two lines of repeated words.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with a comparison to the previous period, and the conversion delta uses "pts" correctly instead of a misleading percent. Keep that discipline.
- The chart is honest and quiet: the y axis starts at zero, the gridlines are faint, there's no decoration, and lines are the right choice for change over time.
- Restraint: one typeface, a neutral page, and color only where it carries data or status.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Taste: Sign-ups is the outcome this page probably exists for, yet it gets only a card while Visits gets the whole chart. Give Sign-ups (or Conversion rate) its own trend next to the visits by channel, even as a small line in its card. That way the viewer can see whether more traffic actually turned into more sign-ups, which is the question the dashboard is for.
2. Taste: let Search, the leading and fastest-growing line, lead. Keep its blue strong and put Social and Email in quieter tones. The chart then makes one point at a glance instead of three equal ones (Tufte on restraint in emphasis).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and whether the chart shows exact values on hover.
