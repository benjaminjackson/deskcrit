**First read.** The eye goes to the bold question, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", and then straight to the filled blue "Save changes" button. Within two seconds it's clear this is a confirm dialog before you publish edits to a group calendar. It feels calm, plain and competent. The trouble starts in the row of four actions: two of them look identical, and one of those throws your work away.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a modal shown to someone who edited a shared calendar, here a choir's, so they confirm before everyone who follows it sees the changes.

**Verdict.** This is a solid dialog with a clear question, a clear primary action and copy that tells you the consequence. The one real problem is that "Discard changes", which destroys the edits, gets the same styling as a harmless "Export as file" link and sits right next to "Keep editing". That makes the riskiest action the easiest one to hit by mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **The destructive action isn't set apart.** *Defect.*
   - **Where:** the action row runs Export as file · Discard changes · Keep editing · Save changes.
   - **What goes wrong:** "Discard changes" and "Export as file" are the same blue text with no button shape, so they read as a pair of minor options. Meanwhile "Discard" sits next to "Keep editing", which is its exact opposite. Someone who wants to back out reaches for the quiet option near the middle, and both quiet options are close together. One of them throws away the new rehearsal times.
   - **The convention:** Apple HIG and Material Design both say a destructive action should be separated from the safe ones and look destructive.
   - **Fix:** group the safe pair ("Keep editing", "Save changes") together. Move "Discard changes" clearly away from them, usually alone at the far left, and give it a destructive treatment. A red text button is the common one.
   - **What's already right:** the label names its outcome, so keep the wording.
2. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** *Convention.*
   - **The problem:** the dialog asks one question, whether to publish these changes or not. Exporting answers neither, but it's the first action the eye meets reading left to right, and it turns a two-way choice (three with discard) into four.
   - **The rule:** Nielsen's minimalist design and progressive disclosure heuristics both say rare options shouldn't sit alongside the main choice.
   - **Fix:** move export to the calendar's own menu. If it has to stay here, put it behind a secondary control. This also opens up the left side of the row for "Discard" in finding 1.

**Smaller things.**
- **Left edge:** the "Export as file" label starts about 34 px to the right of the heading and body text, so it looks like a near miss rather than an indent. If a text button stays on the left, line its label up with the text above it.

**What's working.**
- **The copy:** the heading is a real question with a verb and an object. The body line spells out the consequence for the reader: "will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar". Don't trim it.
- **One primary:** "Save changes" is the only filled button, and its label repeats the heading's verb, so the question and the answer read together.
- **The type:** two sizes, separated by both size and weight, which is all a dialog needs.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Builds on finding 1:** make the body line name what gets lost as well as what gets published. *Taste.* The heading already does the "save" side. If "Discard" said in its own terms what goes away (these rehearsal times, unsaved), the choice would cost nothing to think about. Only the maker knows whether discarding loses everything or only this session's edits, so the wording is theirs.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Default focus:** which button gets focus when the dialog opens. It should not be "Discard".
- **Focus indicators and escape-key behavior.**
- **Missing backdrop:** the dialog sits on a blank white page with no dimmed background behind it. Check in the live product that it's actually shown as a modal over the calendar.
