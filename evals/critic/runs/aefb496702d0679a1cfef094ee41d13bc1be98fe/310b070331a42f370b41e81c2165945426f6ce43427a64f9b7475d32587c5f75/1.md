**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project," goes across to the white card, and runs down three empty fields. Then it stalls. At the bottom of the form there's no button, only the words "Send message" sitting there like a caption. It's clearly a contact page for a small design studio. It feels calm and tidy, and then unfinished right where it matters.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the contact page of a studio's website, where a possible client sends a project inquiry.

**Verdict.** Everything up to the last step works, but the last step is the page's whole reason to exist and it's broken. "Send message" doesn't look like something you can click, so a visitor who has just written a message isn't sure how to send it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The submit control is plain text, not a button.** *Defect.* "Send message" at the bottom left of the card is set in the same face, size, weight, and near-black color as the body text. It has no fill, border, or underline. Even the nav and footer links get an underline and blue, so the page's most important action is styled more weakly than "Privacy." A visitor who has just typed a message reaches the end of the card and has to guess whether that line is a label, a note, or the control. Some will hesitate or give up, and that is a lost inquiry. Only one action is on the page, and nothing marks it as the primary one (Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis; Nielsen Norman Group on flat elements drawing less attention). The fix: give it the shape of a button, with a fill or a border, so it is the strongest thing in the card after the fields. If the plain text is a stylesheet that failed to load, check the live page. As captured, it fails. A real button also settles the target-size question: as bare text, the clickable area is only about one line tall.

**Smaller things.**
- **Current page not shown in the nav:** "Contact" in the top-right nav is drawn exactly like Work, Services, and About. Mark the current page with one cue, such as bolder weight or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Required fields not marked:** none of Name, Email, or Message is marked. Put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or add "optional" after any field that isn't required (Budiu, NN/g).
- **Input borders may be too faint:** the gray borders on Name, Email, and Message look light against white, possibly under 3:1. Measure the border color against #fff, and if it falls short, darken it to at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
- **Card ends on no grid line:** the card's right edge (about x = 1068) lines up with nothing. The nav ends near 1224, and the strip in between reads as left over. Either run the card out to the content's right edge, or make the split between the two columns clearly deliberate (Müller-Brockmann).

**What's working.**
- **The intro paragraph is the best copy on the page.** "We read every message and reply within two working days" makes a concrete promise that answers the visitor's real worry. "Include a budget range and a rough timeline" tells them exactly what to write, right next to the field where they'll write it. Keep both.
- **The form setup is right.** One column, a visible label above each field, the labels close to their own field, and a taller box for the message. Nothing to fix there.
- **The hierarchy is restrained and clear:** one bold headline, one paragraph, one card. The eye knows where to start.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Builds on finding 1.** Make the button the one place the link blue appears inside the card, filled, so the page's single accent color points at its single action. *Taste.* On a page with one job, the only strong color should mark that job.
2. **Let the two-day promise lead.** The headline "Tell us about your project" could sit on any studio's site, while "reply within two working days" is specific to this one. Moving that promise up, near the headline or next to the button, puts the reason to act where the visitor makes the decision. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the fields and the submit control, error messages, and what the visitor sees after sending. That confirmation needs to repeat the two-day promise, and the still doesn't show whether it does.
