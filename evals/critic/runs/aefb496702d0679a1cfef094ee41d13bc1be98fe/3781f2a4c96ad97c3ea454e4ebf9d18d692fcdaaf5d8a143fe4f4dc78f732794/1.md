**First read.** The eye goes to "Tell us about your project" at top left, then right to the white form card, then down to the blue "Send message" button. It's clearly a contact page for a small design or dev studio, and it's calm and tidy. It's also anonymous: once you're past the wordmark, nothing on the page says what Fernwick does or why you'd write to them rather than anyone else.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the contact page of a studio's site, where someone with a possible project sends a first inquiry the studio can act on.

**Verdict.** It works. The form is short, labeled and obvious, and the button is the one clear action. What holds it back is that the page asks for a budget and a timeline in one place and collects the message somewhere else. Everything else is a small fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **The guidance and the field it's about are far apart.** The intro on the left asks for "a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The Message box sits about 450 px to the right with no hint in it. By the time someone is typing, they're looking at an empty box and have to remember the instruction or look back for it. Many won't, and the studio will get "Hi, we need a website" with no budget, which means an extra round of emails before it can quote. The fix is to put that prompt where the typing happens: as helper text under the "Message" label, or by splitting budget and timeline into their own short optional fields. Nielsen's tenth heuristic asks for short help next to the step it concerns. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The header nav doesn't mark "Contact" as the current page. All four links look the same. Give the current item one cue, such as a bolder weight, a bar or a different color (Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- None of the three fields says whether it's required. The intro's "if you have them" hints that some input is optional, so the viewer can't tell where the line is. Add an asterisk with a one-line legend, or put "(optional)" after any optional label (Budiu, NN/g).
- The form card's right edge (about x = 1068) doesn't line up with anything. The header content runs to about x = 1224, so a 156 px strip is left empty on the right, and the card looks placed by eye rather than on the page's columns (Müller-Brockmann). Either run the card to the header's right edge or line it up with a column that something else also uses.
- The input borders look pale gray on white and may fall under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border color against #FFFFFF, and darken it if it falls short.
- The headline breaks "Tell us about your / project", which separates "your" from its noun. "Tell us about / your project" keeps the phrase together. Common sense.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear order of importance and a single action. The headline leads, the field labels are bold and sit tight to their fields with a much larger gap between groups, and "Send message" is the only filled button. Keep that order when adding helper text.
- The intro makes a specific promise: "We read every message and reply within two working days." That's a commitment the viewer can hold the studio to, which does more than a generic "we'd love to hear from you." Keep the number.
- The palette and type are restrained: one blue for every link and the button, one sans-serif family, and four clearly separate sizes. Nothing competes.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1. Make the left column help the person write a better brief, not just introduce the form. It already has the start of that in "budget range" and "rough timeline." The commitment is to say, briefly, what a useful first message contains, using the kinds of details the studio actually needs to quote (the maker knows what those are). Then the page saves both sides a round trip. *Taste.*
2. Give the left column something that's Fernwick's own. Right now the space under the intro paragraph is empty, so the page could belong to any studio. A stronger version would put one piece of proof the studio already has there, such as a type of recent project or client (content the maker would supply). That way someone deciding whether to send the message can see this is the right studio for them. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the fields and button, which matter on a form. What validation and error messages look like after a failed submit. What confirms that the message was sent.
