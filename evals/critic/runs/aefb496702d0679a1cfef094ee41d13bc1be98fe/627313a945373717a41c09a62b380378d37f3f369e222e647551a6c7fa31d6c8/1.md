**First read.** My eye goes to "Create your account", then drops straight to the navy "Create account" button, and the six fields in between read as one gray stack. It's clearly a signup card for a note-taking product called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and competent. It also feels anonymous: I'd struggle to tell it apart from a hundred other SaaS signup pages.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a first-time visitor to a notes app, who has already decided to try it, should get through signup with as little friction and doubt as possible.

**Verdict.** It works. The order is clear and there is one obvious action. What holds it back is friction and doubt inside the form. The fields don't tell people what they need to know before they type (password rules, what a "workspace" is, what "free" means). A blank marketing dropdown also makes the form longer than the task needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The fields give no help before the user commits.** *Defect.* "Password" has no hint, so the rules only show up as an error after the first try. Preventing that error beats reporting it (Nielsen 5, error prevention). "Workspace name" is product jargon to someone who hasn't used Quillnest yet (Nielsen 2). It isn't marked optional, and nothing says what a workspace is or whether its name can be changed later, so careful users stop to think. Fix: add one short line of helper text under Password with its rules, and one under Workspace name saying what it is and whether it can be left blank or changed (Nielsen 10: short help next to the step).
2. **"How did you hear about us?" adds work for the user and gives them nothing back, and its empty control looks unfinished.** *Defect.* It is the last field before the button, so it is the last thing the user does before committing. It serves the company, not the person signing up (Nielsen 8: every element supports the main task). The select has no prompt such as "Select one", so it reads as an empty box with a chevron. Users can't tell whether it is required (it has no asterisk, but it also isn't marked optional) or what is in it until they open it. Fix: move it after signup, for example into onboarding. If it stays, give it a visible prompt and mark it "(optional)". (The missing prompt is common sense, not a cited rule.)
3. **The subhead doesn't make a case, and "free" can be read two ways.** *Defect / copy.* "Start a free workspace for your notes." could sit on any notes app's signup page, so it gives a hesitating visitor no reason to finish. "Free" is a commitment word. It can mean a free plan or a free trial that later turns into a charge. If the second reading is wrong, users have a reason to feel misled later. Fix: say which kind of free it is (and whether a card is needed), using the facts only you have, and end the line on what the user gets.

**Smaller things.**
- "Sign in" in the footer line is the same gray, weight and style as "Already have an account?", so returning users can't see that it's a link. Underline it or give it the link color (WCAG G183 / F73; Nielsen Norman Group on flat links).
- The input borders are pale slate on white and look like they fall below 3:1. Measure one, and darken it to at least 3:1 if it falls short (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- There is one clear primary action: a full-width filled button, the only strong color on the card. Nothing competes with it. Keep that.
- Labels sit above the fields and close to them, and required fields are marked with an asterisk plus a "* Required field" legend, so meaning doesn't depend on the red alone.
- The type scale is disciplined: headline, subhead, labels and small print are clearly separate levels, using weight and color as well as size.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 3: let the subhead speak to the person signing up. Say what their notes will be like in Quillnest, not just that they're getting a workspace. That makes the card a final reason to sign up instead of a toll booth. You'd need to supply that claim. *Taste.*
2. The card has no Quillnest character apart from the blue wordmark in the corner. Carrying that wordmark's blue, or some of its personality, into the card (the heading, or the space around the card) would make this read as Quillnest's front door rather than a template. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the fields and the button. What the password and email errors say and where they appear. Whether the card's bottom edge stays clear of the page edge on shorter viewports (it sits about 24 px from the bottom here, against 78 px of space at the top).
