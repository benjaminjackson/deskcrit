**First read.** The eye lands on the big black title, drops straight into the large red block, then moves across the smaller colored tiles along the right and bottom. It reads as a photo gallery for a garden show, with a clear featured item and five secondary ones. It feels orderly and cheerful, but empty: none of the "photos" show anything, and every tile has the same pale circle on a different flat color.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page where visitors browse the exhibits at a garden show, so the pictures carry the page.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the page doesn't do its job yet. A gallery that shows no gardens tells the viewer nothing the captions don't already say. On top of that, there is leftover review text in the footer that no visitor should see.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders.** All six tiles show a flat color field with an identical light circle sitting on the bottom edge. A visitor gets no roses on the "Rose arch," no water on the "Pond edge," and nothing to tell one exhibit from another except the hue. In a gallery, the pictures are the content. The captions ("Climbing roses over the east gate") promise something the image area doesn't deliver, so the picture and the words don't make one idea; they just sit side by side. *Defect* (common sense: this checklist line has no catalog entry). Fix: put real photographs of each exhibit in the tiles. If these are stand-ins on purpose, this is not a finding. But the same six saturated hues filling most of the page would then be the only visual identity the page has, and they say "placeholder," not "garden." It would also be worth confirming the images didn't simply fail to load in the capture.
2. **A stray line of review copy in the footer.** "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size" sits under the bottom row in body-weight gray. To a visitor, it's an internal note that leaked onto the public page and makes the show look unfinished. It's also false: the Rose arch tile is about twice the width and height of the others. *Defect* (common sense: the copy is not finished). Fix: delete it. If the page needs a footer, use it for something visitors can act on, such as show dates or how to visit. The maker has to supply that content.
3. **The "Rose arch" caption floats between two groups.** The 40 px "Rose arch" heading sits about 15 px below its image, and its line underneath sits about 15 px above the Bean rows tile. With equal gaps on both sides and a heading this large, it can read as a section title for the bottom row ("Rose arch" → Bean rows, Glasshouse) as easily as a caption for the red tile. The small tiles get this right: about 9 px from image to title and about 15 px to the next tile. *Convention* (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on grouping by proximity). Fix: pull the caption tight to its image and open up the gap before the bottom row, so it's clearly smaller than the space between groups.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count six sizes: about 64 (page title), 40 (feature title), 18 (feature caption), 15 (footer), 14 bold (tile titles) and 13 (tile captions). The 15/14/13 sizes sit too close together to read as separate levels (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 3.1.1). Removing the footer line goes most of the way. Otherwise, set it at one of the existing caption sizes.

**What's working.**
- **The grid is tight and consistent.** Gutters are the same 14 px everywhere. The featured tile spans exactly two bottom-row columns plus one gutter, and the right column's three tiles line up with the main tile's top, the caption band and the bottom row. Keep this structure when the photos go in.
- **One clear feature.** The Rose arch tile is plainly the lead, so the eye knows where to start. Its larger caption type backs that up without a badge or a label.
- **The captions are concrete.** "Tomatoes in late July," "Plum and pear trees": each says what you'll see and, in one case, when. They're short, plain, and specific to this show.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: once real photos go in, crop the Rose arch image so it is clearly the best picture on the page, close in on the roses over the gate rather than a wide shot. The feature slot only earns its size if the picture in it is the one visitors would come for. *Taste.*
2. The page title "Garden show gallery" could belong to any show. Naming this particular show, and where or when it is, in the title would tell a visitor arriving from search that they're in the right place. The maker has to supply those facts. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the tiles are clickable, and what happens when they are (lightbox, detail page). How the two-column layout reflows on a phone.
