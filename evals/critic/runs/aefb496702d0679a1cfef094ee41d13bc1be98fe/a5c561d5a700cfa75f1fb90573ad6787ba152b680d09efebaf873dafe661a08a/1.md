**First read.** The eye goes to the serif headline "Savings deposits rose every month", then down to the tall striped June bar at the right of the chart, then across to "$4.82M" in the top card. It reads as a calm, tidy credit-union member update about savings deposits in the first half of 2026. The stripes give it a busy, slightly technical buzz that the rest of the page doesn't have.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a member-facing report page that shows ordinary members, at a glance, how much was saved in the first half of 2026 and whether that's good news.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, honest, competent page, but its most eye-catching data point misleads. The June bar that sells "rose every month" is mostly a one-off bonus payroll, and the page only says so in the footnote. Fix that, and swap the hatched fill for a plain one, and the piece does its job.

**What would move it most**
1. **The June spike carries the story, and the explanation is hidden in the footnote.** *Defect.* From January to May the bars climb by about $40–80k a month. June jumps $282k, to $1150k, and it's the bar the eye lands on right after the headline. The only explanation, "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll", sits in small gray type under the chart panel. Most members will read June as a surge in member saving. It also muddies the cards on the right. "$4.82M, up 6.1%" includes that bonus, and "Average deposit, Jun 2026" and "Accounts that made a deposit, Jun 2026" are both June-only figures that the bonus may inflate. Nothing says whether June 2025 had a bonus too, so "Up $11" and "Up 402" can be read two ways: as like-for-like growth, or as an effect of the bonus. Put the explanation at the June bar itself, with a short note on or beside it. Then say in the cards whether the comparisons include the bonus; only the maker has that fact.
2. **The hatched bar fill is chartjunk, and it signals the wrong thing.** *Convention (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 5; Few) with a defect cost.* All six bars are filled with tight diagonal blue-and-white stripes. That fill is the heaviest texture on the page and shimmers at this size, which is the moiré effect Tufte names. Charts also conventionally use hatching for estimated, projected, or partial values, so a careful reader may take all six months as provisional when these are posted ledger figures. Fill the bars with plain flat color (Few's approved fix). Keep a pattern only for data that really differs, if any.

**Smaller things.**
- Money is written three ways: "$4.82M" in the card, "$1150k" and "$1200k" in the chart, "$214" in full. "$1150k" also has no thousands separator. Pick one form for the chart and the cards, e.g. $1.15M or $1,150k.
- The dek ("New money paid into member savings accounts, by month, January to June 2026.") and the chart title ("Deposits into savings accounts, US dollars per month") say the same thing twice. Keep the unit in the chart title, and let the dek add something or cut it.
- "Up $11 on Jun 2025" and "Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025": a stranger can read "on" as a date ("on June 2025"). "Compared with" or "vs." makes the comparison clear.
- The stack of three cards ends at about y=620, while the chart panel runs to about y=693. The bottoms almost line up but don't, which leaves an orphan gap. Either align the bottoms or make the difference clearly deliberate.
- The chart has both a y axis with gridlines and value labels on every bar. That's taste: since every bar is labeled, the axis and gridlines could go, which would quiet the chart further.

**What's working.**
- The headline is a finding, not a topic: "Savings deposits rose every month" tells members the news before they read a number. Keep the sentence-style headline.
- The chart is honest. The bars start at zero, each has its value on top, and the monthly figures add up exactly to the $4.82M total (612+655+734+801+868+1150 = 4,820k).
- Every card's number comes with a comparison in words ("Up 6.1% on…"), not just a green color. That gives members context and doesn't rely on color alone. The serif headline over a plain sans body is a clear pairing, and the bold "Member report" in the nav shows where you are.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on findings 1 and 2: split the June bar into regular deposits and the bonus payroll, as two tones of the same blue. Then the "rose every month" claim stands on regular deposits alone, and members can see both parts. This needs the bonus amount, which only the maker has. *Taste.*
2. Let the headline carry the one number members will remember. The 6.1% year-on-year rise is already on the page, and pairing it with "rose every month" would tell members in a single line whether this is good news, instead of making them go to the card to find out. *Taste.*
