**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces", which is set over a dark leaf pattern. It drops to the gray line underneath, then moves across three equal cards with the hours, workshops and delivery. Then it stops, because there is nothing to do next. It reads as the home page of a local garden center. It feels calm and well made but generic: it could be any nursery's template.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the home page of a local garden center, which should get nearby gardeners to browse or come in for this season's shade plants, and to sign up for workshops or order for delivery.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, and the copy is unusually specific, but it doesn't do its job. It makes a clear offer and then gives the visitor no way to act on it. The page is a sign, not a shop front.

**What would move it most**
1. **The offer leads nowhere.** *Defect.* The headline and the line under it ("Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs… ready to pot this weekend") promise a specific set of plants. Yet there is no button or link anywhere below the nav that takes you to them. The three cards in the lower third are dead ends too:
   - "Saturday workshops" says there are ten places per class but gives no way to book one.
   - "Delivery" gives the free-delivery terms but no way to order.
   - "Opening hours" gives no address, even though "Visit" sits in the nav.

   A visitor who is persuaded has to guess that "Plants" in the top right is where the shade plants are. Many won't make that jump. With no single primary action, nothing on the page asks for anything (Material Design 3 and Apple HIG on button emphasis). Fix: put one clearly dominant action under the headline that goes straight to the shade and small-space plants. Make it a text link that names that destination, not "Shop now". Then give each card the one next step it implies, such as booking a workshop place, at lower weight than the main action.
2. **The hero image is wallpaper.** *Taste.* The 400 px band is a flat, even scatter of leaves with spots in tan, red and gray, the same density from edge to edge. It shows neither shade nor a small space, so it adds nothing to the headline's idea beyond "green". Its busy texture also crowds the letters: "small" and "spaces" sit right on light leaves and spots. The headline still reads because it's large, bold and white, but the image is working against it rather than with it. Fix: either use an image that shows the promise, meaning the kind of plants named, in the setting named, or calm the pattern down (lower its contrast, or darken the area behind the headline) so it becomes a ground rather than a second subject.
3. **The headline and its message are split.** *Convention.* There are two related problems:
   - **Split across the hero edge.** The headline sits inside the hero, but the line that completes it (what the plants are and when they're ready) sits below the hero edge in the white band. The hard edge reads as a section break, so the subhead looks like the start of the card section rather than the second half of the headline.
   - **Off by 16 px.** The headline's left edge is about 72 px from the left, while the logo, the subhead and the first card all sit at 56 px. Being out by 16 px looks like a slip, not a choice. Müller-Brockmann and Samara: one grid governs the page.

   Fix: put the headline and subhead in the same space, either both in the hero or both below it, with a clear gap before the cards. Line the headline up with the 56 px edge.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: "small / spaces" breaks the phrase and leaves "spaces" alone on line 2. Widen the text box so the headline breaks after "shade", or let it run on one line (Bringhurst on line breaks).
- Opening hours card: "until 8 / pm" splits a number from its unit. Use a non-breaking space between "8" and "pm" (Bringhurst 2.4.6).

**What's working.**
- **The copy is concrete.** "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs", "until 8 pm on Thursdays", "free within ten miles on orders over $60". Every card answers a real question in one plain sentence. Keep that voice when you add the actions.
- **The type is restrained.** A green serif wordmark against one sans family, with four clearly separate levels: the headline at about 48 px, the subhead at about 20, the card titles bold at about 17, and the body at about 15. The hierarchy is never in doubt.
- **The cards are an honest set.** They're the same size, padding and border, they're evenly spaced, and each holds exactly one topic.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2. Let the green of the "Fernhollow" wordmark be the page's one color, for example on the main action and the card titles, so the brand reads as one thing from the logo down. Right now that green appears only in the logo and the leaf pattern. *Taste.*
2. "Grown at our nursery" is the one claim on the page that a big-box store can't make. Give it more weight, with a picture or a short fact from the nursery itself. That's content only you have, and it's the difference between this page and anyone's template. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the nav links and cards respond to hover and focus, and whether the cards are meant to be clickable.
- Whether the nav links' click areas are big enough. Check that each is at least 24×24 px (WCAG 2.5.8).
- Whether the empty band at the bottom is the end of the page or just where the screenshot stopped.
