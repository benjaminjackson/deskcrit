**First read.** The eye lands on the three bold white headlines across the top, which all hit at once. It drifts to whichever card is warmest (the orange one, on the left) and then runs into big blocks of empty color. It reads as a menu of three pool programs: friendly, loud, and unfinished, as if the cards were built for content that never arrived. Below the cards sits a line, "Design review: approved, one offer clearly leads the page." I read that as page content, not as a judgment of the design, and it is not true: no offer leads.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a section of a swimming pool's website that introduces three programs and should send each visitor to the one that fits them.

**Verdict.** As a way to tell the three programs apart, it works. As a page that gets anyone to sign up or show up, it fails. Nothing leads, nothing tells the viewer what to do next, and the bottom half of every card is dead space where that should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing leads, and the warmest color decides by accident.** The three cards have the same size, the same headline weight, and equally strong color. The viewer has no starting point, so they scan all three as equals. The orange card on the left pulls hardest only because warm hues come forward (Itten, *The Art of Color*), not because it is the most important offer. That's fine for a neutral menu, but this page needs an order. *Defect.* Fix: decide which program matters most to this page and make it plainly the first thing seen, by size or by being the only card in strong color, with the others set back (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). If the three really are equal, say so with a neutral treatment instead of three competing hues.
2. **Each card stops at a description, with no next step.** Every card ends after two lines of copy, and nothing follows: no link, no "book," no times or price, no sign the card can be clicked. The viewer learns that family hour exists and then has nowhere to go, so the eye path dead-ends in empty color (Arnheim). *Defect.* Fix: end each card with one specific action tied to that program, such as booking a class or seeing the lane times. If the cards are clickable, make that visible. The exact wording and destination are the maker's.
3. **Half of every card is empty color.** The text sits in the top 45% of each card (about y 110 to 330), and roughly 300 px of solid color sits below it in all three. That space isn't a deliberate pause. It reads as leftover, as if an image or button failed to load, and it makes the section feel unfinished (Ruder on gathered versus leftover space; Nielsen heuristic 8). *Defect.* If an image or button is supposed to be there and didn't render, that explains it, so check the live page. Otherwise, either give that space to the action from finding 2 or shorten the cards to fit their content.
4. **Three saturated hues fill about half the frame, and none says "pool."** Orange-red, green, and purple cover large areas at full strength, so the section shouts without a reason. The palette doesn't hold together as one system (*Refactoring UI*, "Define your shades"), and nothing in it suggests water, calm, or a family afternoon. *Convention plus taste:* color-coding the programs is a fair idea, but at this strength and area it overwhelms the text, and the hues feel picked one at a time. Fix: keep one strong color, used where finding 1 puts the emphasis, and quiet the rest, or use one family of hues that belongs to the pool's own brand.

**Smaller things.**
- "Design review: approved, one offer clearly leads the page" (bottom left): a stray note with no job for a visitor, and it contradicts what the page shows. Remove it.
- "Every morning from six" (green card): clear enough, but this is a time people will plan around. "6 a.m." removes any doubt.

**What's working.**
- The headlines are verbs and invitations ("Learn to swim," "Join the lanes") rather than labels like "Adult lessons" or "Lap swimming." They speak to the viewer, so keep that voice.
- The blurbs are specific and human: "with a coach in the water beside you" and "floats and toys for the children" each tell a particular kind of person that this one is for them.
- The type is clean and well set. The headlines are tightly leaded and clearly bolder than the body, there are only two sizes with a big step between them, and the white text holds up on all three colors.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: let "Learn to swim" lead if it is the pool's main offer. It is the one program where a beginner might hesitate, and "a coach in the water beside you" is the line most likely to get that person past the hesitation. Give it the size and the only strong color. *Taste.*
2. Each blurb already names a when (Tuesday evenings, every morning from six, Sunday afternoons). Pulling that schedule out as its own line in each card would let a visitor match a program to their week at a glance. That serves a page meant to get people in the water. *Taste.*

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable and what they do on hover or focus. Also whether the empty lower half of each card is meant to hold an image or button that didn't load in this capture; check that in a live browser.
