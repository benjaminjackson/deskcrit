I've finished the checklist walk and read the reference entries. Here is the full critique.

**First read.** My eye goes first to the big serif headline, "Help around the house, on your schedule". Then it drops to the first card, because its dark, busy photo is the only thing on the page that isn't flat and pastel. After that it moves across the three illustrated cards and the green prices. It reads as a calm, friendly booking site for local home services. It feels tidy and trustworthy, but the first photo looks like it came from a different website.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page of a local home-services company. Its job is to show homeowners what they can book and roughly what it costs, and to get them to book a visit.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The structure is clear, it's calm, and the card copy is concrete. Three things hold it back. The lead service has a picture that doesn't match the set and shows the wrong thing. The prices don't say what they buy. And the page asks visitors to "pick a service" but gives them nothing on the cards to pick.

**What would move it most**
1. **The window-cleaning photo breaks the set and shows the wrong subject.** This is the first card, top left, so it's the first service the eye reaches. The other three cards use flat illustrations on a pale teal tile. This one is a dim photo of a wood-paneled room with red leather sofas. The windows are dark, so it looks like night outside, and the room looks more like a college common room than someone's house. Nothing in it shows clean glass. The page's lead service ends up looking like a stock filler image, and the row looks put together from different sources rather than designed as one set. *Defect (common sense: a row of images shown as one set reads as one family).* Fix: pick one rendering style for the row. The cheapest route is an illustration of a window in the same flat style as the others. If you want photos, use them on all four cards, and they should show the result you're selling.
2. **The prices don't say what they're for.** "From $89", "From $189", "From $24" and "From $65" have no unit. Laundry at $24 could be per bag, per load or per week. Garden care at $65 could be per visit or per month. Prices are the thing visitors remember and later hold you to. If someone reads $24 as per week and gets billed per bag, you have a complaint before the first delivery. *Defect.* Fix: add the unit to each price, such as per visit, per load or per room, whichever is true. Only you know which, so I haven't guessed.
3. **The subhead promises a step the page doesn't offer.** "Pick a service, choose a day…" tells visitors to pick a service. But the cards have no button and no "Book" link, and nothing suggests they can be clicked. The only way to act is the "Book a visit" button in the top-right corner, which is far from the card the visitor just chose, and which presumably makes them pick the service again. *Defect (common sense: put the next action next to the thing the visitor just decided on).* Fix: give each card a visible way to start booking that service, such as a secondary button or a clear link. It should look weaker than "Book a visit" in the header so that button stays the main action.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "…on your / schedule", so one word sits alone on the second line. A break at the comma ("Help around the house, / on your schedule") reads as two phrases. Bringhurst, Hochuli.
- In each card the image sits about 14 px in from the card edge, but the text is about 20 px in. Those are close enough that it looks like a near-miss. Either line the text up with the image edge, or make the image run to the card edges (Arnheim; Samara, *Making and Breaking the Grid*).

**What's working.**
- **A small, controlled palette.** The interface sticks to navy and teal on warm cream, and coral and yellow appear only inside the illustrations. The page feels calm, and the navy "Book a visit" button is plainly the one action.
- **Type that sorts itself.** The bold serif headline against sans-serif body text gives clear levels at a glance, and you don't need extra sizes to get them.
- **Card copy that is specific.** "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday" and "with screens and sills wiped" each tell a stranger exactly what happens. Keep that voice. The prices are also set at the same height in every card, so the row reads as one set.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Let the headline say something only Fernway can say (taste).** "Help around the house, on your schedule" could sit on any competitor's page. The page already has more distinctive material: "vetted crew", "their own supplies", and a fixed Monday-to-Wednesday laundry turnaround. If you build the headline from one of those, a visitor learns in the first second why to pick you over the next tab. The choice of claim is yours.
2. **Make the teal-tile illustrations the brand (taste).** Builds on finding 1. The flat illustrations on teal tiles already set a friendly, ownable look. Use the same style for every service and in the header and booking flow, so the site looks like one company rather than a template with clip art.
