The help center page works. It's clear, calm, and conventional, and nothing on it gets in the way of finding help. Nothing is wrong enough to fail it; what's left is mostly polish.

**First read.** The eye lands on the white serif "How can we help?" on the navy band, drops to the white search field, then moves down to the grid of eight topic cards. It reads at once as a product's support home page for people with a problem to solve. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, but it could belong to almost any software company.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's the landing page of a software product's help center. Its job is to get a user with a problem to the right article quickly, by searching or by picking a topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The search field and the topic grid are clear, legible and set out the way users expect. What holds it back is a flat step between the section heading and the card titles, a search field with no icon or submit button, and copy that feels generic. All of these are small.

**What would move it most**
1. **The level below the hero is flat (convention).** "Browse by topic" looks about 18 px bold, and the card titles under it ("Getting started", "Account and sign-in" and the rest) look about 16 px bold. The logo, around 20 px bold, is close to both. These are three sizes a couple of pixels apart, all in the same weight and dark ink. So the section heading doesn't read as the label over the grid. It reads as a ninth title that lost its card. Bringhurst says sizes should come from "a modest set of distinct and related intervals", and Müller-Brockmann says each level of a hierarchy should be distinguishable at a glance. Pick a direction: make "Browse by topic" clearly bigger, or make it a quieter label (smaller, gray, or medium weight) so the card titles lead the section. Bring the logo onto one of those steps as well.

**Smaller things.**
- **Search field (y≈216–264):** it looks like it spans x≈360–920. The second card column starts at x≈357 and the third ends at x≈922, so the field misses both edges by about 2–3 px. Either snap it to those column edges or make it clearly narrower so the difference looks intended (Arnheim: a near-alignment reads as an error).
- **Search field:** the placeholder "Search for articles" is the only label, and there is no search icon or submit button. As soon as someone types, nothing on the field says what it does or how to send it. Add a search icon inside the field, and ideally a submit button (NN/g on placeholder-only fields).
- **Card descriptions:** four of them end with one word alone on the second line ("receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages."), which gives the grid an uneven, ragged bottom. Trim the copy slightly or adjust the card width so each wraps more evenly (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- **Copy (common sense):** "Browse a topic or search the guides." lists the two options in the opposite order to the layout, where search comes first. It also just describes what the page already shows. Either cut the line or reorder it to match the page. "How can we help?" is the stock help-center headline. That's acceptable by convention, but it tells the user nothing about Harbor in particular.

**What's working.**
- **The navy band** sets apart the one action most visitors want. A white field on dark navy is the highest-contrast thing on the page, so the eye finds the search field without trying. Keep that contrast.
- **The topic cards** are a disciplined program. They share one line-icon style with the same stroke weight, use the same title-and-description pattern, and sit in an even four-by-two grid whose outer edges line up with the logo and the nav. Each description says what the user can do ("Track a delivery or start a return") rather than naming a category. That's the right voice for a help page.
- **The serif headline** used only in the hero against a sans everywhere else is a clear, deliberate contrast. It gives the page a bit of warmth without clashing.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Use the eight cards as signposts to answers (taste).** For a help center, the fastest path is often a direct link to the few problems most people come with. Each card, or a short row under the grid, could show its two or three most-read articles, so a visitor can go straight to an answer without opening a topic first. That content has to come from your own support data.
2. **Promote "Contact us" (taste).** It's currently a plain nav link with the same weight as "Guides". For someone who can't find their answer, it's the way out. A visible fallback under the grid, in the page's own voice, would tell frustrated users exactly where to go next.
3. **Let the serif carry the brand further (taste).** The serif in "How can we help?" is the only thing that sets this page apart from a template. Setting the "Harbor" wordmark, or the section heading, in the same face would make the page recognizably Harbor's while keeping the plain sans for everything people need to read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the whole card is clickable, and whether its hover and focus states are clear. Check this in a browser, because the cards are the main way to navigate.
