**First read.** The eye lands on the three KPI numbers, 184,220 first, and runs across to 2.12%. Then it drops into the big chart: three lines all rising, blue on top. Then it stops, because nothing tells you which line is which channel, how many visits a line means, or which weeks you're looking at. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and it looks like a lot of other analytics dashboards.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly overview for a marketing or growth lead to see whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy and which acquisition channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The top half works. The bottom half, about two-thirds of the screen, fails its job: "Visits by channel" shows no channels, no values and no dates, so the reader can only say "three things went up." Labeling the chart is the single fix that matters.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no labels at all.** This is a defect. The three lines in the "Visits by channel" panel have no legend and no direct labels. The gridlines have no y-axis values, and the x-axis has no week dates. So the viewer can't tell which channel is blue, orange or green, or whether the gap between blue and orange is 500 visits or 50,000. They also can't link a dip, such as the week-8 drop in blue, to a date they'd remember. Tufte and Few both say a reader should never have to guess what a mark measures. With no y values, nobody can check whether the axis starts at zero, so the gaps between lines can't be trusted either. The empty band under the baseline and the blank left gutter are exactly where the labels would go, which suggests they were planned and then dropped or failed to render. If it's a rendering bug, check it live. As shown, it's broken.
   **Fix:** put the channel names at the right end of each line, next to its last point (Tufte, Wilke: label at the data, not in a legend). Add a few y-axis values with "visits" as the unit and week dates along the x-axis. That would also fill the dead space below and to the left of the plot.
2. **Color is the only thing telling the series apart, and one of the colors already means something.** This is a defect and a convention. Once labels exist, direct labels fix the color-only problem (WCAG 1.4.1). The second problem stays: green is the "up, good" color in all three KPI cards, and it's also one channel's line color. The eye links the green line to "positive" for no reason (Apple HIG, Material: keep status colors for status). Give that series a hue that isn't green, or let the delta text carry its meaning with an arrow and use a neutral color.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart title "Visits by channel" names the topic but not the finding. Once the channels are labeled, a title that says which channel grew most would answer the panel's question for the viewer. This is taste, not a rule.
- "Last 12 weeks" in the header reads as a static label. If it's meant to be a date-range picker, it needs a control shape such as a border, a chevron or a fill. If it's only a label, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI row.** Each card has a small label, one large number and a delta against a stated period ("vs previous 12 weeks"). That gives the reader a value, a comparison and a direction in about a second. Keep exactly that structure.
- **The numbers add up.** 3,912 ÷ 184,220 really is 2.12%, and the conversion delta is given in "pts" rather than %, which avoids the usual percent-of-a-percent confusion.
- **Restraint.** One typeface, one gray background, one card style, a consistent gutter and faint gridlines. Nearly all the ink in the chart is data.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1. The three KPI cards and the chart don't relate to each other: Visits appears up top and again as the chart, but Sign-ups and Conversion rate have no trend at all. A stronger overview would tie each card to its own trend, for example a small sparkline per card, so the 12-week story is visible for every metric, not just one. This is taste: the page exists to show change over time, and right now only a third of it does.
2. Let the channel lines carry their own weeks-ago comparison at their end labels, using the same "vs previous 12 weeks" language the cards already use. The chart would then speak the same language as the row above it. This is taste. Use only figures the data already holds.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart's axis labels and legend failed to load or were never designed. Check in a live browser.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive.
- Whether hovering over the lines shows values. Even if it does, it doesn't replace labels for a glanceable overview.
