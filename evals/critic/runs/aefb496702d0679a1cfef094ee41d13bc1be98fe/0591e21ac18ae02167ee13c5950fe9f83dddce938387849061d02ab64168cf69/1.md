**First read.** My eye goes straight to the big black headline and gets stuck there. The four lines are set so tight that the descenders crash into the line below: the "y"s of "Quayside" and "ferry" cut into "crossings", the "g" and "h" of "daylight" cut into "early", and the "p" and "g" of "spring" sit on top of "summer". I have to untangle the words before I can read them. After that, I find a calm, plain paragraph about a river ferry. The page feels clean and confident, but broken, and then empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a public web page that tells riders when the spring-to-early-summer ferry runs, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline is the first thing anyone reads, and its lines overlap until it's hard to read. Then the page labeled "Spring timetable" never shows a timetable.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's lines collide** (defect). This affects all four lines of the headline (roughly rows 150–370). The line spacing looks well below the type size, so descenders on each line hit the letters below. That turns the headline from the strongest part of the page into a smudge the reader has to decode. It also gives an impression of carelessness on the one element everyone reads. Bringhurst allows a title to be set tighter than its size, "so long as the ascenders and descenders don't collide." Hochuli faults a heading for this same crowding (*Detail in Typography*, p. 28). Fix: open the line spacing until no letter on one line touches a letter on the next. A display heading can stay tighter than the body text, just not this tight.
2. **The page promises a timetable it doesn't deliver** (defect). The eyebrow says "Spring timetable" and the headline says "by daylight, spring through early summer". The page gives a frequency ("every forty minutes") and a duration ("about twelve minutes"), but no first or last crossing time and no start or end date. "By daylight" and "early summer" are exactly where a rider needs numbers, and they're left vague. Below the paragraph, the bottom 40% of the screen is empty. Either the timetable didn't load, or it's further down the page. If it's further down, at least the hours of operation and the season dates need to show on this first screen, because that's what someone lands here to find. If the eyebrow labels the whole page, the label should name what the reader actually gets (WCAG 2.4.6).

**Smaller things.**
- The paragraph's last line is the single word "weather." Rebreak or slightly reset the measure so the last line carries more than one word (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The headline breaks between "early" and "summer". That splits the phrase and leaves "summer" alone on the last line. Break at a phrase boundary, for example after "daylight,", so the second half stays together.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear order: a small gray eyebrow, a heavy display headline, then a regular-weight paragraph. The levels differ in both size and weight, so the order of reading is never in doubt. Keep that scale.
- The body paragraph is set at a comfortable length, about 65 characters per line, with easy line spacing in a plain sans. It reads well.
- Everything is flush left on one left edge, so the page feels orderly and decisive.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: let the two numbers already in the copy lead. "Every forty minutes" and "about twelve minutes" are the facts a rider plans around, and right now they're buried in a sentence. Pulling them out as large figures alongside the headline would make the page answer "how often, how long?" at a glance. That's what a ferry page is for. (Taste.)
2. The headline is long, at four lines and 13 words. A shorter headline that names the route, with the season moved into the eyebrow (which already says "Spring"), would keep the bold display size without the stack. That would also remove the pressure that probably pushed the line spacing so tight. (Taste.)

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether the empty lower part of the screen is waiting for a timetable that hasn't loaded or whether the page really ends here. Check in a live browser.
