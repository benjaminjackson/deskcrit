**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Learn to swim", which is very large and heavy. It drops to the gray subtitle, then sideways to the two small blocks, "Join the lanes" and "Family hour", and then stops. Nothing comes after them: no button, no price, no place, only white space across the bottom ~45% and the right third. It reads as a clean, calm, unfinished page that could belong to any pool or any template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page of a local pool or swim school. It should get adults to sign up for Tuesday-evening lessons and also mention lane swimming and family sessions.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline is strong, but the page gives a convinced viewer nothing to do and doesn't say when, where, or how much. The interest the headline creates goes nowhere.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page has no ask.** *(Defect.)* The eye path runs headline → subtitle → two cards and then dead-ends in empty space. The page has no button, link, phone number, or sign-up line anywhere. An adult who decides "yes, I want this" has nowhere to go, and on a landing page that is a lost sign-up. Arnheim/Dondis's "clear eye path" runs from the focal point to the end, and the end here is missing. The fix is one primary action just below the subtitle that names the exact thing: booking a place in the Tuesday adult class, not a generic "Get in touch". Give the two cards lower-ranked links of their own. The best ask lets viewers judge for themselves, for example by trying a first class, but which offer to make is the maker's call.

2. **The copy leaves out the facts people decide on.** *(Defect.)* "Tuesday evenings" has no time. "Small" has no class size. Nothing gives a price, a location, a level ("never swum before?"), or a start date. Lanes "every morning from six" has no end time and doesn't say what "three speeds" are. These are the details people act on and hold you to. Without them, every reader has to go and ask, and most won't. The direction: add the class time, size, price, and location next to the subtitle. Only the maker has those facts.

3. **The headline and the page argue about what this is.** *(Defect.)* "Learn to swim" plus a subtitle about adult classes tells viewers this page is about lessons. Then two of the three things on it (open lanes, family hour) aren't lessons, and the lessons themselves get no card. So viewers can't tell whether this is a lessons page or a page for the whole pool. Pick one. If it's the pool's front page, give the adult class its own card at the same level, and make the headline the pool's promise. If it's a lessons page, push lanes and family hour down to a quieter "Also at the pool" band.

4. **The two middle text levels are nearly the same size.** *(Convention, Bringhurst §3.1.1; Müller-Brockmann on distinct levels.)* The subtitle looks about 22 px and gray. The card headings look about 20 px and near-black. Two sizes this close don't read as two levels, so the eye can't tell whether "Join the lanes" ranks above or below the subtitle. The headline is about 80 px, so the jump between the top two levels is huge while the next one barely exists. Keep three sizes with clear steps: the headline, one subtitle/heading size, and body text. Separate card headings from the subtitle with weight (semibold), not with a near-miss size.

**Smaller things.**
- Balance: all the weight sits in the top-left. The lower half and right third are empty with nothing to counter them, so the page looks cut off rather than airy (Arnheim, "Balance"). The ask and the missing facts from findings 1–2 are the natural counterweight. Check how the page looks on a full-length capture.

**What's working.**
- The headline. "Learn to swim" is three plain words in a confident heavy weight, tightly set. It's unmistakably the place to start and can be read from across a room. Keep its scale.
- The copy speaks like a person. "With a coach in the water beside you" names the fear an adult learner has and answers it in their own terms. Protect that clause in any edit.
- One clean left edge at about x=96 holds the headline, subtitle, and first card. The two cards share a matching structure and baselines. The spacing is calm and in order.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 1: let "with a coach in the water beside you" lead the whole page, so the ask sits right next to it. The page exists to win nervous adults, and that line already holds the best reason to say yes. *(Taste.)*
2. Give the page one photograph or one color drawn from the pool itself. Right now it's all grays and nothing says water, so it could be selling software. For a page that has to make an anxious adult picture themselves in the pool, real imagery of this pool and this class would carry more than any typography. The maker has to supply it. *(Taste.)*

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the page goes on below the fold (a booking section, prices, a map). If it does, the empty lower band in this capture still hides it, so at minimum add a cue that there's more.
