I've finished the checklist and the deep read on its failed lines, and I'm writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the week with your whole team", then jumps to the blue "Create account" button and moves down the form. Within a second it reads as a standard SaaS sign-up page for a team planning tool. It feels calm, tidy and familiar, so familiar that nothing on it belongs only to Tallyboard.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a sign-up page where a team lead who has just arrived decides to start a free workspace.

**Verdict.** As a form it works: it's clear, labeled, and has one obvious action. What it fails at is persuading. The copy promises the "whole team" and in the next line caps the team at five, without saying what happens after that. The page doesn't give a reason to sign up here rather than anywhere else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The offer contradicts itself and can be read two ways (defect).** The headline says "your whole team". The line under it says "up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." A team lead with eight people can read this two ways: "I can add the other three later, free", or "the other three will cost money later". This is the line a reader will hold you to. If they read it the first way and later hit a paywall, it feels like a bait and switch. If they read it the second way, they may leave now. The fix is to make the line say what "later" means: is it free, and is it a later step or a paid tier? The maker has to supply that fact. Then the headline and the limit should agree, either by softening "whole" or by putting the limit where the headline makes its promise.
2. **The headline could sit on any team tool's page (defect, copy).** "Plan the week with your whole team" names a category, not a difference. A visitor deciding whether to sign up learns nothing they couldn't assume about any planner, so nothing on the left half earns the click on the right. The page also offers no way to judge the value: nothing ties it to what the visitor uses now or to a result they'd recognize. The direction: the headline or subline should name the one thing Tallyboard does that the visitor's current way of planning the week doesn't. Ideally it sets that against the tool or habit they already have. Only the maker can supply that.
3. **The password rule disappears as soon as someone types (defect, convention).** "At least 10 characters" exists only as placeholder text in the Password field. When the user starts typing it's gone, so they have to remember the rule or find out at submit (Nielsen, heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall). Move the rule to helper text under the field, or into the label, where it stays visible.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Sign In" link:** it's the same gray as "Already have an account?" with no underline or color, so a returning user has to guess it's clickable (NN/g on flat links; WCAG 1.4.1). Give it the brand blue or an underline. Also lowercase the "In" to match the sentence case used everywhere else.
- **Required fields aren't marked:** the visitor can't tell whether Company is required or optional for a free workspace (Budiu, NN/g). Mark the optional field "(optional)", or asterisk the required ones and explain the asterisk once.
- **Input borders look pale:** the gray field outlines on white look below 3:1, so the fields fade for low-vision users (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border color against white and darken it to 3:1.
- **Too many type sizes:** I count about eight, and several are 1 to 2 px apart. The logo (about 20) and "Create your account" (about 22) are nearly the same size. So are the body (about 17), button (16), input (15), label (14) and footer (13). They read as accidents, not levels (Bringhurst, 3.1.1). Collapse to roughly four sizes, and let weight and color carry the finer steps.
- **Near-miss alignment across the columns:** "Tallyboard" and "Create your account" sit at almost the same height, about 3 px apart. Line them up exactly, or move them clearly apart.

**What's working.**
- One primary action. The full-width blue button is the only saturated block in the card, so the next step is never in doubt. Keep that restraint when revising.
- Real labels above every field, in bold dark type, with placeholders only as examples. First and last name share a row as a short related pair, and everything else is one column.
- The headline is the clear entry point: it's large, bold and dark, with a short subline at a comfortable line length. Its two-line break ("Plan the week with / your whole team") falls on a phrase boundary.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Builds on finding 2: give the empty lower-left quadrant something that shows Tallyboard's week view or what the product produces, drawn from the product itself. Right now half the screen is a headline and a void. A glimpse of the actual plan would let a team lead judge the value before typing anything. Taste.
2. The brand blue already appears in two places: the logo and the button. Let it mark the one Tallyboard-specific idea in the headline too, so the color links the promise to the action. Taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the fields and button; inline validation and error messages for email and password; how the two columns reflow on a phone.
