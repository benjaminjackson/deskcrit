**Verdict.** The typography is clean and the hierarchy is clear, but the screen doesn't do the one job its label promises. It says "timetable" and gives no departure times, no first or last sailing, and no dates. The headline takes up the space where that information should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **A timetable with no times (defect).** The "Spring timetable" label promises times. Below it, a four-line headline fills about 330 px of an 800 px screen, and the only service detail is "every forty minutes" in the paragraph. Every forty minutes from when? Without a first departure, the viewer can't work out a single sailing. If the times sit just below this view, the first screen still spends everything on a heading and gives the rider nothing to act on. The fix is to show the actual service on the first screen: first and last sailing, or the start of the table. To get there, shorten the headline and let the times sit high. (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should answer the question the visitor came with.)
2. **The headline's key terms can be read two ways (defect).** Riders will plan around these words, so the vagueness matters.
   - "By daylight" could mean the hours change with sunrise and sunset, so the last boat gets later each week. It could also just mean "daytime service". A rider who reads it the wrong way misses the last crossing.
   - "Spring through early summer" gives no end date, so nobody can tell whether a mid-June trip is covered.
   - The fix needs facts only you have: the first and last sailing (or the rule tied to daylight) and the start and end dates. Put them in the words that are already there instead of adding more headline.
3. **The headline is too long for its job (taste).** "Quayside ferry crossings by daylight, spring through early summer" is a full sentence set at display size. It wraps to four lines, and the size makes it read slower, not faster. A shorter headline would fit on two lines and leave room for finding 1. Cutting "Quayside ferry crossings" down to what the page is (the ferry timetable) and moving the qualifiers into the dates line would also fix the bad break in "Smaller things".

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks inside "early / summer" (third to fourth line), which splits one phrase and leaves "summer" alone on its own line. Rebreak the headline or shorten it as in finding 3 (Bringhurst §2.4).
- "weather." sits alone on the last line of the paragraph. Narrow or widen the measure slightly, or edit the copy, so at least two words wrap (Bringhurst §2.4.2).
