**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", reads the line under it, and then hits a row of four identical blue buttons and stops. Nothing in that row says which one is the answer, so the eye goes back and forth between them. It reads as a confirm dialog in a calendar app for a group such as a choir. It feels calm and tidy, but the final step is harder than the question.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop confirm dialog: an organiser who has just edited a shared calendar decides whether to publish the new times to everyone who follows it.

**Verdict.** The words and the type do their job. The buttons do not. All four actions look exactly alike, including the one that throws the work away, so the most likely choice isn't marked and the riskiest choice isn't set apart. Until that changes, the dialog slows people down and invites the one mistake it exists to prevent.

**What would move it most**
1. **Four equal buttons, so no answer is marked.** "Export as file", "Discard changes", "Keep editing" and "Save changes" are all filled #0b5cad blue with 17 px bold white text and the same 58 px height. People who want to save can't find Save at a glance; they have to read all four labels, left to right, to find the answer. Those four blue blocks are also the heaviest thing in the dialog, heavier than the question. A view should have one filled, high-emphasis button and give the rest lower-emphasis styles (Material Design 3, "Buttons"). The facts file names two of the buttons `span.button.outline` and `span.button.primary`, yet all four render as the same fill. So an outline or secondary style was planned and isn't reaching the screen. Check the CSS first: the fix may only be getting that stylesheet to apply. Direction: make "Save changes" the only filled button, show "Keep editing" as an outline or text button, and drop the others below that. **Defect** (convention: Material Design 3; NN/g).
2. **"Discard changes" looks safe and sits right next to "Keep editing".** It throws the edits away, but it is styled exactly like the safe actions and sits 16 px from "Keep editing", the button someone reaches for when they're unsure. One slip loses all the work on the rehearsal times. Destructive actions should be set apart from safe ones and styled as destructive (Apple HIG, "Alerts"; Material Design, "Dialogs"). Direction: give Discard a destructive style, such as red text or an outline, move it to the far left with a clear gap before the other buttons, and add undo if you can. **Defect.**
3. **"Export as file" has equal rank in a yes/no decision.** It has nothing to do with whether to publish, yet it takes the first slot, where the eye starts scanning the row, and makes three choices into four. Rarely needed options belong behind a visible control, with the main choice shown first (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). Direction: take it out of this dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu. If it has to stay, make it a plain text link. **Convention.**

**Smaller things.**
- Button row: it starts at x=276 while the heading and text start at x=280. Four pixels reads as a near-miss rather than a choice. Line the buttons up with the text column, or once the row is regrouped, align the buttons to the right edge.

**What's working.**
- The copy. "Save changes to the shared calendar?" is a real question that "Save changes" answers word for word. The line under it says exactly who sees what: "The new choir rehearsal times will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar." It names the consequence, so the organiser knows what they're agreeing to. Keep both lines word for word.
- The type. There are three clear levels: a 30 px bold heading (`h1`), 18 px regular text (`p`), and 17 px bold button text. All the dark text is at 15.8:1 contrast, and the button text is at 6.67:1. The heading clearly leads.
- The layout. There is generous padding inside a quiet bordered panel, and the question sits well apart from the actions. Nothing competes with the decision except the buttons themselves.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button has keyboard focus when the dialog opens. It should be "Save changes" or "Keep editing", never "Discard changes".
- Whether Escape or a click outside the panel closes the dialog.
