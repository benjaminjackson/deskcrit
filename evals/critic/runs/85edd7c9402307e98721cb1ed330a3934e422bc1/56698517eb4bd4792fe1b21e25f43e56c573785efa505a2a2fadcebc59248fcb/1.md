**First read.** The eye lands on the six flat colour blocks, not the title, and moves across them like a paint-chip card. Each holds a pale circle, so it reads as a wireframe or placeholder grid, not a gallery. The small bold "Garden show gallery" at top left only gets read after that. It feels tidy, cheerful and empty: there is nothing to look at.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web page showing visitors photos of the garden show's areas, so they can see what each one looks like and maybe decide what to visit.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job yet. A gallery whose pictures are coloured rectangles with circles shows the viewer nothing, and every other question is secondary to that. Underneath, the layout is a clean, competent 3×2 card grid with one alignment slip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders.** This affects all six tiles. Viewers come to a gallery to see the roses, the herb bed and the pond, and they get a flat colour field with a centred disc that tells them nothing. The captions ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Tomatoes in late July") promise specific scenes the page never shows. *Defect.* If real photos failed to load in this capture, that settles this point; check it in a live browser. If these are the intended art, replace them with real photographs of each area, cropped to one aspect ratio and treated the same way (same light, same grade), so the set reads as one gallery.
2. **No way in.** The title is small (it looks about 20 px) and the six tiles are identical in size and weight, so the eye has no starting point and the page reads as a list of equal swatches. *Convention* (one element dominates: Müller-Brockmann; Refactoring UI). With real photos this matters less, but a gallery usually benefits from a lead image: make one area larger or give the title real scale, and let the rest follow.

**Smaller things.**
- The Rose arch title and caption sit about 2 to 4 px higher than Herb bed and Pond edge, so the first row's baselines don't line up. Give all three cards the same image-to-title spacing.
- The circles sit dead centre and low, touching the bottom edge of each block, which looks like a cropping accident rather than a choice. This goes away once real photos are in.
- Six saturated hues cover about half the page, so the colour shouts louder than the content. Real photos would fix this. If any colour framing stays, mute it.
- The column gutter (about 14 px) is much tighter than the row gap (about 60 px), so the tiles read as two horizontal strips rather than one grid. Bring the gutters closer to each other if a single grid is intended.

**What's working.**
- The card structure is clear: a bold title, then a short grey caption tight under it, with a clearly bigger gap before the next row. Each caption obviously belongs to its picture.
- The captions are concrete and in plain words ("Runner beans on canes", "Plum and pear trees"). They name what you would actually see, which is the right voice for a garden show. Keep it.
- The type scale is simple: three sizes with clear jobs and a weight change between title and caption.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are clickable (to open a larger photo or detail page) and what hover or focus looks like. If they are links, nothing in this image signals it.
- Whether the colour blocks are failed image loads or the intended design. Check in a live browser.
