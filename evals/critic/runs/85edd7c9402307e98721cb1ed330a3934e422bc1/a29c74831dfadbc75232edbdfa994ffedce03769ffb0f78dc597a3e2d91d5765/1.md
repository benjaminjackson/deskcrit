**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", drops to the bold header row, then scans down a column of words: Morning, Morning, Midday… It reads as a plain web timetable for one ferry route. It is calm, clean and impersonal, and nothing on it stands out.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a public page where a rider checks when the next boat leaves north landing for market quay.

**Verdict.** It's clean and easy to read, but it's set up to list the data, not to answer the rider's question. The rider wants to know "which boat can I catch today?" The day each boat runs sits in the last column at the same weight as everything else, so riders will be stranded by exceptions they never noticed.

**What would move it most**
1. **Which day a boat runs is buried in the "Runs" column.** *Defect.* Three of the seven sailings don't run every day, and you only find out by reading the far-right column row by row. A weekday rider who reads down the times will plan on the 19:30, but that boat runs only at weekends, so their real last boat is 17:10. A weekend rider sees nothing to warn them that there's no 12:40 or 17:10, so they face a four-hour gap after 11:00 that the table doesn't show. "Daily", "Weekdays" and "Weekends" are set exactly like the times, so the exceptions look no more important than the rule. Make the day the first thing a rider sorts by. Use two departure columns (Weekdays, Weekends) or two short tables, so each rider reads only the boats that run on their day. Or at least make "Daily" the unmarked default and mark only the exceptions, so they stand out.
2. **The first column is spent on "Period", which repeats the times.** *Defect* (Nielsen 8, aesthetic and minimalist design; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). The left column is where every row starts and where the eye goes first. Here it holds "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday…", labels anyone can work out from the times, each repeated twice. The departure time, which is the thing the rider is looking for, sits about 240 px further right. Put the departure time first and give it the most weight in the row. If the periods help, turn them into extra space or small group labels between blocks of rows, not a column. Dropping the column also lets the table narrow from 1080 px. At the moment each departure is about 300 px from its arrival time, so the eye has to cross empty space to read one trip.
3. **Some words leave gaps that matter to a rider.** *Defect.*
   - "Spring season" has no dates. A rider can't tell whether this timetable applies on the day they travel, and that is exactly the date they will hold you to.
   - "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are shortened from the stop names in the subtitle ("North landing", "market quay"). Use the full names so the headers match the signs at the landing.
   - The title, "River ferry timetable", sounds like the whole service, but only one direction is shown. Someone standing at market quay can't tell whether the boat comes back. Either name the direction in the title or add the return times.

**Smaller things.**
- Subtitle (`p.sub`) is 18 px and the table text is 20 px (`font_size`). Two sizes 2 px apart read as an accident, and the intro line ends up smaller than the data under it. Set it at 20 px and keep the grey (#59636e) to show it's secondary (Bringhurst, a scale of distinct steps).
- The times use proportional figures: "11:00" is visibly narrower than "06:40" in the same column. Turn on tabular figures (Inter's `tnum`) so the colons line up down the column (Bringhurst 4.4.3).

**What's working.**
- Contrast is high throughout: 15.8:1 for the table text, and the grey subtitle still reaches 6.11:1.
- Rows are in time order with hairline rules, so a row is easy to follow across even at this width. Keep the rules light.
- Restraint suits the job: one typeface, near-black on white, 24-hour times written the same way in every row. Nothing competes with the data.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the four columns, set 1080 px wide, reflow on a phone. Phones are where most riders will check this, so check it in a narrow viewport.
