**What it's for.** My assumption: a web article that walks a first-time gardener through setting up a vegetable bed, read on desktop.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the sections are easy to scan, and the instructions are concrete. The one thing that holds it back is the text column: it is too wide for comfortable reading.

**What would move it most**
1. **Body lines are too long.** This is a convention break, and here it has a cost. Every paragraph runs across the whole column from x≈160 to ≈950. That is about 90 characters a line ("Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level"), well past Bringhurst's 45–75 range (ideal about 66, *Elements of Typographic Style* §2.1.2). On a short page like this the cost is small, but each return sweep is longer than it needs to be. The longer this article gets, the more often readers will lose their place at the start of the next line. Fix: narrow the text column to about 60–70 characters, roughly 600–650 px at this size. The empty space on the right already gives you the room. Check the character count in the live page.

**Smaller things.**
- The apostrophe in "year's" (Preparing the soil paragraph) looks straight rather than curly. Use the typographic apostrophe. At body size this is a common-sense detail, not a sourced rule.

**What's working.**
- **A clear type scale.** I count three sizes: a title around 48 px, headings around 30 px and body around 18 px, with big steps between them. The headings also differ by weight and colour, so there is never a doubt which level you are on. Keep the scale to these three.
- **Headings sit with their own text.** About 14 px separates each heading from its paragraph, and about 36 px sits above it. Each heading clearly belongs to the text below it, and the sections separate without any rules or boxes.
- **The copy is specific and practical.** "Six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans", "twice a week": a beginner can act on every line without looking anything up.
