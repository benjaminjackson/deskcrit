**First read.** The eye lands on the white headline "See where every dollar goes this month", moves down to the subhead, and stops on the solid white "Try it free" button. Next it goes to the right half of the navy band, which is empty and looks like an image that never loaded. The three feature blurbs below get a quick skim, and then the eye runs into about 220 px of blank white. It reads as a homepage for a budgeting app. It feels calm and trustworthy, and also like a template you've seen a hundred times.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the marketing homepage for a consumer budgeting app, and its job is to get people who are cautious with money to start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The page is clear and well built, but it doesn't persuade. It promises that you'll *see* your money and then shows nothing: no product, no example, and half the hero is empty. It also hides the one reassurance this audience needs before connecting a bank account in small grey text in the third column.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline says "see" and the page shows nothing** (defect). The right ~45% of the navy band (about x 820–1216, y 62–462) is empty. All the weight sits on the left, and the right side reads as missing rather than as deliberate space (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). Worse, the page never shows what "see where every dollar goes" looks like. People can't judge whether the app would help them, so they're asked to take the claim on trust. Fix: put the product in that space. A month's spending broken into categories, with one category close to its limit and the heads-up the subhead promises, would show all three claims at once. That makes the headline and the picture one idea instead of a headline with nothing next to it.
2. **The security reassurance is buried** (defect for this audience). "Read-only access … can never move money" answers the first fear anyone has about "Connect your accounts". Right now it's the third of three equal blurbs at 15 px in #3a4249, below the fold line of attention and after the button it should support. A cautious visitor reaches "Try it free" before they find out it's safe. Fix: move a short version up next to the buttons, for example "Read-only: Penny Path can see your balances but can never move your money". Keep the full blurb below. Also aim the line at the reader with "your money", not "money".
3. **The copy and the buttons could belong to any budgeting app** (defect). "See where every dollar goes this month" and "Try it free" fit every competitor, so nothing tells the visitor why to pick this one. "Try it free" also leaves the offer unclear: is it a free plan or a trial, and how long is the trial? Visitors remember that kind of promise and hold you to it. Fix: say what "free" means ("Free for 30 days, no card" or "Free plan, forever"). Then make the ask something visitors can judge for themselves, e.g. "Connect one account and see last month sorted". That lets them compare the result with what they already know about their own spending, instead of grading the product on faith.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count five sizes, 52 / 20 / 19 / 17 / 15 px (`type_sizes`). The subhead (20 px, `section.band > p`), the feature headings (19 px, `h3`) and the buttons (17 px) are too close to read as separate levels. Weight is doing the real separating, and the sizes look picked one at a time (Bringhurst §3.1.1). Collapse to about 52 / 20 / 15 and let weight separate the headings from the subhead.
- Generic look: Inter, a navy band and three text columns make the page look like a template. That costs recognition, because nothing here would stick in memory after the tab closes. A product image (finding 1) fixes most of this. The wordmark is plain bold text with no mark, which is taste, but it adds to the anonymity.

**What's working.**
- The headline dominates without a fight: 52 px bold white on navy at 15.44:1, with tight 1.12 line height so its two lines hold together as one block. Keep that.
- The subhead is the best copy on the page. "Connect your accounts, set a limit for each category, and get a heads-up before you go over" names three concrete actions in the reader's own words, at a comfortable 63 characters per line (`characters_per_line`).
- The grid is clean. The hero text, the three columns (left edges at 64 / 461 / 859) and the nav's right edge at 1216 all share one set of lines, and the feature headings sit 6 px from their text and well away from the band.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Check in a live browser whether the empty right half of the hero is meant to hold an image or animation that failed to load. If it is, finding 1 shrinks to the copy point.
- Nav link hit areas: the text boxes are 23 px tall (`box`, `nav > span`), and the real clickable area isn't measured. Check it against the 24 px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8).
