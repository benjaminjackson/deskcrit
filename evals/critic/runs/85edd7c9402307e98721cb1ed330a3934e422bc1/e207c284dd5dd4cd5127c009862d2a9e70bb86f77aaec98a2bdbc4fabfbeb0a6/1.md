**First read.** My eye lands on the three coloured lines first, because the chart takes about 60% of the screen and holds the only colour on it. Then it moves up to the three bold KPI numbers. It reads as a clean, standard marketing analytics overview. Straight away I'm asking which line is which channel and what the values are, and nothing on the screen answers.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: a weekly overview where a growth or marketing team checks traffic, sign-ups and conversion over the last 12 weeks, and sees which acquisition channel is driving visits.

**Verdict.** The KPI strip does its job. The chart, which is most of the page, doesn't: it has no legend, no series labels, no axis values and no dates. So "Visits by channel" can't tell anyone which channel is which or how big any of them is. It's the largest element on the screen and it carries the least information.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart is unlabelled, so it can't answer its own title.** *(defect)* In the "Visits by channel" panel, the blue, orange and green lines have no names. The y-axis has gridlines but no values, and the x-axis has no week labels.
   - **What the viewer sees:** "three things went up, one went up more." They can't say which channel leads, how many visits it brings, or which week the dip in the blue line (about point 8) happened.
   - **Why the scale matters too:** with no values, nobody knows whether the y-axis starts at zero. The even vertical gaps between the lines invite people to read them as ratios ("blue is double green"), which may be false.
   - **Fix:** name each line at its right-hand end (e.g. "Organic search 9.1k"), in the line's own colour. Put a few values on the y-axis with units ("visits / week") and week dates along the x-axis. The empty left gutter and the empty band under the plot already have room for these. Direct labels at the line ends also mean you don't need a legend.
2. **Colour is the only thing that tells the series apart, and orange and green are a risky pair.** *(defect, WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color)*
   - **Colour-blind viewers:** with red-green colour blindness, the orange and green lines (the middle and bottom series) get close in hue. Today nothing else separates them. The direct labels from finding 1 solve this too. Also check that the two hues differ clearly in lightness.
   - **Green means two things:** the KPI deltas use green for "up/good", and one series is green, so that channel can read as "the good one." Pick a series hue that isn't one of your status colours.
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** *(taste, but worth it on an overview screen)* "Visits by channel" makes the viewer work out the takeaway themselves. On a glance screen, say the takeaway in the title, e.g. "Organic search drove most of the 6.1% rise in visits." That also ties the chart to the "+6.1%" in the Visits tile, which right now has nothing to do with the chart below it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale:** I count six sizes. The KPI values (26 px) and "Overview" (24 px) are too close to read as different levels, and so are 17/15/14/13 px (the chart title and brand, the date range, the tile labels, the deltas). Keep about four sizes, e.g. 26 / 17 / 14 / 13, and make the remaining distinctions with weight or colour instead (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- **Near-miss alignment:** the chart title starts at x=71 and the tile text at x=69. Make them share one inset.
- **Leftover space in the chart panel:** a roughly 70 px empty band sits under the plot, and a roughly 50 px gutter at the left of the plot is empty. Use the space for the axis labels, or tighten the panel.

**What's working.**
- **KPI tiles:** each one gives a label, a big number and a comparison ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). Every headline figure has context, and the numbers are consistent with each other (3,912 / 184,220 = 2.12%). Keep this.
- **Restraint:** one neutral range, faint gridlines, no chartjunk. Once the chart is labelled, the data will be the only loud thing on the page.
- **Delta wording:** "+0.1 pts" for conversion (a rate) versus "+%" for counts is exactly right. It avoids the classic percentage-of-a-percentage confusion.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is a date-range control. It looks like plain text. If it is meant to change the range, it needs to look clickable.
- Whether the chart has hover tooltips. Even if it does, tooltips don't replace labels on an overview screen.
