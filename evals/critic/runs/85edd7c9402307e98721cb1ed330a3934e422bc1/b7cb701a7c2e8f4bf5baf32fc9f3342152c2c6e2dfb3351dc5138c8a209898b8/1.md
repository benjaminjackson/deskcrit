**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tall purple Growth bar, then down to the short Starter bar. Read in one second, the chart says "Growth converts about two and a half times better than Starter." The page is calm, tidy and believable: a product-analytics screen for a SaaS team, with a small table and a definition beside the chart. It looks like something you can trust, which makes the next point worse.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal analytics dashboard. Product, growth or finance people use it to compare how well each plan's trials turn into paying accounts, and to decide where to spend effort.

**Verdict.** The chart misleads, and nothing else on the page comes close in importance. The value axis starts at 2.5%, so the bars greatly overstate gaps between plans that are actually within a point of each other. Everything else is solid. Fix the axis and this is a good screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The bar chart's value axis starts at 2.5%, not zero** (left column; the axis runs 2.5% to 4.5%). **Defect.** Measured from the 2.5% baseline at y≈680, the Growth bar is about 368 px tall and the Starter bar about 147 px. That is roughly 2.5 times longer for rates of 4.0% and 3.1%, which differ by about 1.3 times. Tufte's lie factor here is around 5. Team (3.6%) and Enterprise (3.4%) are also stretched apart. A reader who looks at the bars and skips the labels will think Growth converts far better than Starter, and will rank and fund plans on that basis. The percentage labels on top of the bars are correct, but the bar lengths are what people remember. Bar length encodes value, so bars must start at zero (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 17.1, proportional ink; Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design", pitfall 8; Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2). Fix direction: start the axis at zero, so the bars honestly show four similar rates. If the point of the page is the small differences, switch to dots or a line on a scale that doesn't start at zero, which is allowed when labelled (Wilke).
2. **The title names a topic, not a finding** ("Trial to paid conversion by plan", h1, 26 px). **Taste / common sense** (no approved source). Once the axis is honest, the bars will look nearly equal. The viewer then needs the words to say what the chart shows, for example that Growth converts best at 4.0%, about a point above Starter. Without that, the reader has to work out whether a 0.9-point spread matters, and that is the call the page exists to support. Fix direction: put the finding in the title or a short line under it. Keep the current subtitle, which defines the measure well.

**Smaller things.**
- **Column heads don't line up.** The h1 (top 90 px, `box`, `h1`) and "Trials by plan, Q3 2025" (top 96 px, `aside > h2:nth-of-type(1)`) miss each other by 6 px, so the two column heads read as a near-miss. Align them on a shared baseline, or offset them clearly.
- **Two sizes too close.** The scale has five sizes: 26, 18, 15, 14 and 12 (`type_sizes`). The 15 px subtitle (`p.sub`) and the 14 px table and body text (`td`, `p.note`) are one point apart and don't read as different levels. Set the subtitle at 14 px and let colour separate it, or step it up clearly.

**What's working.**
- **Each bar carries its exact value in bold on top,** and each bar is labelled directly underneath, with no legend. Keep that when you redraw the scale, because it lets people read numbers without the gridlines.
- **The aside fills in what a bare rate hides.** The trial and paid counts show that Enterprise rests on just 38 conversions, and the Definition spells out the edge cases (converted means first invoice paid; trials still open on 30 October count as not converted). This is what makes the numbers trustworthy. It also squares with the chart: 286 / 9,210 = 3.1%, 118 / 2,950 = 4.0%.
- **The palette is restrained.** There is one purple for the data, one gray range for everything else, and every text colour passes contrast. The lowest is the 12 px source line at 4.74:1 (`contrast`, `p.source`). Nothing competes with the data.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The nav tabs.** They are rendered as spans with no measured click targets (`targets` is empty). Check in a live browser that they are real links with targets of at least 24 px and a visible focus state.
