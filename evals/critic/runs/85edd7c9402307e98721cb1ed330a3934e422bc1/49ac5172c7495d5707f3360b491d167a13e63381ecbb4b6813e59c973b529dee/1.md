**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop help section for bakery customers who order online and pick up in the shop. They come here to fix one specific problem quickly.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. A help page works when people can scan the questions and jump to theirs. Here the questions look exactly like the answers, so the visitor has to read every line to find their topic. Beyond that, the two answers people will act on, the cancellation deadline and what to bring to the counter, can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The questions look identical to the answers, and the page has no title.** The facts file shows one type size, one weight and one colour for every block: 18 px, weight 400, #1f2328 for both `main.help > h2` and `main.help > p` (`type_sizes` lists a single size). The only thing that tells "Changing your pickup time" from its answer is that it comes first in a pair. A visitor can't skim the four topics; they have to read their way down. There's also no page title above the first question, so nothing says this is help for a bakery's orders, and nothing leads the eye.
   - **Kind:** defect, and a convention. Dair says a heading set in the same size, weight and colour as its text gives no contrast at all, and Tschichold asks that the order of importance be shown plainly.
   - **Fix:** Make the questions different from the answers at a glance. Change weight first (semibold at a similar size is enough), not a size only a point or two larger. Add one page title clearly bigger than both, so there are three distinct levels: title, question, answer.

2. **The two lines people will hold the bakery to can each be read two ways.** Both are defects, because they're the lines a customer acts on.
   - **"Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead."** Ahead of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time before the pickup day. Someone who picked the looser reading tries to cancel the evening before a morning pickup and is refused. Fix: give the exact cut-off, for example "until [time] the day before your pickup", and say what happens after it (do they still pay?).
   - **"Show it at the counter along with a name."** Whose name, yours or theirs? If the staff expect the orderer's name and the collector gives their own, the collector may be turned away. Fix: name it: "your name" or "their name".
   - **"Open your order"** doesn't say where the order is, and there's no link to it, so the visitor has to go and find it. Link the phrase, or say where to find it ("in the email we sent" or "under My orders").

3. **The allergy answer only helps someone who is already in the shop.** "Every loaf has a card by the till" is no use to someone deciding online whether to order. "Ask the baker" doesn't say how to ask before pickup. For a customer with an allergy, this is the one answer where a gap matters most. Fix: say where to see the ingredients before ordering, or give a way to ask before ordering (phone or email), and end the answer with what that means for them. This is a defect for that group of customers.

4. **The answer lines are too long to read comfortably.** They run 90 to 95 characters per line (`characters_per_line` for `main.help > p`, in an 820 px box). That's well past the 45 to 75 character range Bringhurst gives, so the eye has a harder time finding the start of the next line. The answers are short, so this costs less than it would in long text, but it's free to fix. Narrow the text column to about 600 px at this size. This is a convention.
