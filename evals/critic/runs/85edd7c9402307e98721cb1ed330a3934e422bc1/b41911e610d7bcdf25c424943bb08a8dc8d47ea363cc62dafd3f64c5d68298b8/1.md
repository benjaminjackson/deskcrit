**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers," then drops into three even blocks of prose and reads them top to bottom like a short essay. It reads as a plain transport notice: calm, tidy, trustworthy, a bit bare. After the heading nothing stands out, so you have to read every word to find out when the boat leaves.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web page that tells river-ferry passengers when the boat runs, how long it takes, and the rules for bicycles, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is sound. Size, measure, leading and contrast are all in range. But the page doesn't do its main job: a passenger can't plan a trip from it, because it never gives an actual time. The biggest problem is the words, not the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times in it.** *(Defect.)*
   - "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" (paragraph 1) and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" (paragraph 3) are the lines a passenger acts on, and both are vague.
   - What does "early morning" mean: 6:00 or 8:00? Does the first boat leave at :00 or :20? "Before dusk" moves by more than an hour across the winter.
   - "The quay" can be read two ways:
     - It means the market quay only, so the last boat from the north landing could leave at a different time.
     - It means the last boat from either end.
   - If a reader takes the wrong reading, they're stranded on the wrong bank after dark.
   - Fix direction: give real clock times. First and last departure from each landing, the minutes past the hour, and the winter dates and last-boat times. Name the landing in every timing line. Once the times are numbers, pull them out of the prose into a short two-landing timetable, so someone at the shelter can find the next boat at a glance.
2. **The winter exception comes last, after the rule it overrides.** *(Defect.)*
   - Paragraph 1 states "every forty minutes" with no season attached.
   - In winter a reader who stops there, as many will, plans on a boat that doesn't run. The correction ("one boat each hour") only comes two paragraphs later.
   - Fix direction: put the season in the first sentence ("From [date] to [date] the ferry runs every forty minutes…; in winter, hourly"), or give summer and winter side by side as headed rows. That way nobody can read one without seeing the other.

**What's working.**
- The text setting is right for reading: 19 px Inter body (`font_size`, `main.article > p`) at about 66–68 characters per line (`characters_per_line`), 1.5 line height (`line_height_ratio`) and 15.8:1 contrast (`contrast`). That's squarely in Bringhurst's 45–75 range and well past WCAG 1.4.3. Keep these values when you add the timetable.
- The hierarchy is clear with two sizes (36 px/700 against 19 px/400). Heavier weight backs up the size step, so the heading needs no colour or ornament. Don't add a third or fourth size when you restructure; use bold for any subheads.
- One left edge at x=120 for every block, and the empty space gathered to the right and below, give a calm, official feel that suits a public notice.
