**First read.** The eye lands on "Create your account", runs down the stack of six empty fields, and stops on the dark blue "Create account" button. It reads at once as the sign-up page of a note-taking SaaS. It feels calm, tidy and anonymous: put any other product name in the corner and nothing else would need to change.

**What it's for.** My assumption: someone who has already decided to try Quillnest should be able to create an account fast and without doubt about what they're agreeing to.

**Verdict.** The page is competently built and does its basic job, but it asks for more than it needs to and tells the user less than they need. Six fields, two of them unexplained and one of them for marketing, stand between a curious visitor and a free notes app. The only copy that could sell the product is generic.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for things the user can't answer or doesn't need to give.**
   - "Workspace name" asks a newcomer to name something they haven't seen yet. Nothing says what a workspace is, whether the name is public, or whether it can be changed later, so they stall or type "asdf".
   - "How did you hear about us?" is an empty dropdown that serves the company, not the user. It adds friction at the moment of commitment and should never come before the account exists.
   - Fix: prefill the workspace name from the first name ("Ben's notes") or ask for it after sign-up, and give it one line of help if it stays. Move the attribution question to after sign-up, or behind an optional "Tell us how you found us".
   - Grounds: defect. Nielsen 10 covers help beside an unfamiliar field, and Nielsen's "Progressive Disclosure" covers putting the rare and secondary after the main task. Prefilling a default is common sense.
2. **No field says whether it's required, and the password gives no rules.**
   - With an obviously optional question sitting in the stack, the user can't tell which of the other five they can skip.
   - The password field shows no length or character rules, so the first time they learn them is from an error after they press the button.
   - Fix: write "(optional)" after any optional label, or put asterisks on the required ones with a one-line legend (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). Put the password requirements as a short hint under the field before anyone types.
   - Grounds: defect for the password rules, which cause errors after submit (Nielsen 5 and 10). Convention for the required markers.
3. **The copy gives no reason to sign up, and "free" is ambiguous.**
   - "Start a free workspace for your notes" could sit on any notes app, so nothing tells the visitor what Quillnest does that their current notes tool doesn't.
   - "Free" is the one word here they'll hold you to, and it reads two ways. It could mean a free plan forever, or a free trial that later asks for a card. If it's the trial, users who believed "forever" will feel tricked when the bill arrives.
   - Fix: say which kind of free it is, and whether a card is needed ("Free forever for personal notes, no card needed"). Replace the generic line with one specific thing they'll get that their current tool doesn't give them.
   - Grounds: defect for the ambiguous "free". Taste for the generic line.

**Smaller things.**
- "Sign in" in "Already have an account? Sign in" looks like the same grey text as the rest of the sentence. Underline it or give it the brand blue so it reads as a link (Norman, signifiers; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The input borders are a pale blue-grey on white and look like they fall below 3:1 against the card. Measure them, and darken them if they do (WCAG 1.4.11).
- The dropdown shows a blank box with the browser's default black arrow. Give it a "Select one" prompt and an arrow that matches the field styling, so it doesn't look unfinished.

**What's working.**
- There is one unmistakable primary action. The full-width filled button is the darkest, heaviest thing on the card, so nobody hunts for it. Keep it the only filled element.
- Labels sit above their fields with clearly more space below each field than between label and field, so every label belongs to its field at a glance. There are no placeholder-only labels.
- The type is restrained. One bold headline, bold labels and a grey subtitle and footer give three clear levels from weight and colour, not from a pile of sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button.
- How errors and validation appear, especially on the password field.
- How the card reflows on a phone.
