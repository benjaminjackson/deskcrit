**First read.** My eye goes to "Pricing", then moves straight across three equal prices, $9, $29 and $79, and stops at the bottom of the feature lists. Nothing there tells me where to go next, so I drift back up to the blue "Start free" button in the top-right corner. It reads as a pricing page for a scheduling or booking tool: calm, tidy and competent, and very like a template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the pricing page of a booking/calendar SaaS. It has to help a visitor (a solo professional up to a business team) pick the right plan and start on it.

**Verdict.** It looks clean, but it doesn't do the main job of a pricing page. A visitor can compare the plans but can't choose one. The cards end in a dead end, and the only action on the page isn't tied to any plan. The wording of the prices and features also leaves the visitor guessing what they'd actually pay for and get.

**What would move it most**

1. **None of the plans can be chosen.** None of the three cards has a button. The only action is "Start free" in the header, about 300 px above and to the right of the plans, and it isn't linked to Solo, Team or Business. A visitor who has just decided "Team is me" has nothing to click where they made that decision. They have to go back to the header and hope that "Start free" means "start Team". The control is far from the thing it acts on (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). Also, the page doesn't offer the obvious next step at the moment the visitor is ready for it (common sense). "Start free" makes it worse: no free plan is listed, so the visitor can't tell if it's a free plan, a trial, or which tier the trial covers. **Defect.** Fix: end each card with its own button that names the plan ("Start Team"). Give one card the filled primary style and the others an outline. If the header button means a trial, say so in words, such as "14-day free trial, no card".

2. **The prices and features can be read two ways, and these are the lines people will hold you to.** Three problems:
   - "$29 / month" with "Up to 10 calendars" could be $29 for the whole team or $29 per user. That's roughly a tenfold difference, and the visitor finds out at checkout.
   - Each card lists three completely different features. Does Team include the "Booking page" and "Email reminders" from Solo? Does Business include "Reports"? As written, moving up a tier looks like losing the cheaper plan's features.
   - "Reports" and "Shared availability" name categories, not what the visitor gets.

   **Defect** (copy). Fix: say who the price covers ("$29 / month for your whole team"). Start the higher tiers with "Everything in Solo, plus:". Rewrite the vague items as outcomes ("See who's booked most each week").

3. **Nothing helps the visitor choose, and the copy could be anyone's.** The three cards match in weight, border and size, so the page gives no hint which plan fits most people. "Pricing" only repeats the nav label. "Simple plans for teams of every size. Change or cancel whenever you like." could appear on any SaaS site, so it tells the visitor nothing about why this tool is worth $29. **Taste**, but it costs conversions. Fix: mark one plan as the recommended one with a label and stronger styling (usually the middle one). Rewrite the subtitle so it ties price to something the visitor already pays for, or to the time they lose today (e.g. what one missed booking costs them). Also say who each plan is for in a short line under the plan name ("For a single practitioner", "For a front desk of up to 10").

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Pricing" looks the same as the other nav items, so nothing shows the page you're on. Give the current item one cue, such as bolder weight, an underline or the brand blue (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Subtitle: "you like." sits alone on the second line, a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rebalance the break, or narrow the block so the two lines are closer in length.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is disciplined. I count four clear levels: page title, plan name, price and feature text, with "/ month" stepped down by weight and colour instead of yet another size. Keep that.
- Inside each card, the price is clearly the thing to read first. Big figures with a muted unit is the right call.
- Restraint: one blue, used only for the logo and the one action. When you add plan buttons, keep the blue for the recommended plan's button so the restraint still means something.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The bottom ~230 px is empty. That may simply be where the viewport cut off, with an FAQ or a feature comparison below. Check the live page: if the page really ends there, the cards float high and the page has no footer or reassurance (billing terms, refunds).
