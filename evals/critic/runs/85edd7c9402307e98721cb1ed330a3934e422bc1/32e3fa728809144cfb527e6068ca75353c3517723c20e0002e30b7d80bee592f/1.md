**Verdict.** It works: the order is clear, the type is large and dark, and the copy is concrete and kind to a beginner. The one thing that would noticeably improve it is line length. Every paragraph runs at about 90 characters, so each line is harder to finish than it needs to be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraphs run too wide** (convention, with a real cost). All four paragraphs fill the 800 px column and run 89 to 93 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `main.doc > p:nth-of-type(1)` to `(3)`). The last one is at 74. Bringhurst gives 45 to 75 as the comfortable range, with 66 as the ideal (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). At 90 characters, the eye has a long way back to the start of the next line. On a page this short the reader won't get lost, but every sentence takes a little more effort than it should. That's the wrong feeling for a guide that's meant to feel easy. The fix is to narrow the text column to about 60 to 70 characters, roughly 600 to 640 px at 18 px Inter. The headings can stay at their current width. The narrower column also gives the empty space on the right a clearer purpose.

**Smaller things.**
- **The headings differ from the text only in size.** "Preparing the soil" and the other headings are the same Inter 400 and the same #1f2328 as the body; only their size is different (30 px against 18 px). They read fine as they are. Still, adding a little weight (500 or 600) would let them come down a size and still lead clearly (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8a–8g and 9).
- **The title's letter spacing looks loose.** At 48 px, "Planning a vegetable bed" appears to keep Inter's text spacing. Tighten the letter spacing slightly, by about −1 to −2%, so it holds together as one shape (Bringhurst, 2.1.1).
- **There's a straight apostrophe** in "last year's heap". Use a curly ’. No checked source in the catalogue covers this, so I'm calling it common sense: it's the one sign of an unfinished setting.
- **One line in the copy leaves the beginner guessing.** "Water deeply" gives no amount, even though every other line is exact (six hours, a spade's depth, a week). Say how deep or how much, for example "soak the top 15 cm" or a volume per square metre, so the reader can act on it.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale.** It uses three sizes with big gaps between them (48 / 30 / 18 px, `type_sizes`), so each level does one obvious job. Keep it at three.
- **The spacing around headings.** Each heading sits 8 px above its own paragraph and 26 px below the one before it. That makes every heading clearly belong to the text it introduces. Keep this spacing if you narrow the column.
- **The copy.** It's specific, uses everyday words, and is reassuring ("things that forgive mistakes: radishes, lettuce, and bush beans"). It's what makes this page feel written by a person rather than a template.
