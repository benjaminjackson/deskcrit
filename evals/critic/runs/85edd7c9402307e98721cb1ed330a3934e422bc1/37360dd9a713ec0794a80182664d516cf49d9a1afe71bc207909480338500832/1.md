**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tilted blue "SALE" slab at top right. Its red letters seem to shimmer against the blue and take an effort to read. Then the eye comes back left to "Linens and cookware for the weekend" and down to four cards whose pictures are empty tinted boxes. It reads as a calm, warm-neutral homewares shop with a loud sticker slapped on top, and the sticker looks like it came from someone else's site.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the desktop home page of a small homewares shop, meant to get weekend visitors into a time-limited sale and onto products.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page announces a sale but gives the shopper nothing to act on: there are no product pictures, no sale prices, and no way in. The loudest element is a hard-to-read "SALE" sticker that clashes with everything else on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The SALE flag is unreadable and steals the page.** Defect.
   - **What's wrong:** In the hero at top right, red #ff2d2d text on blue #2b5cff measures 1.39:1 (`contrast`, `div.flag`). That is far below the 3:1 that even 34 px bold text needs (WCAG 1.4.3). The two colours are near-complements at about the same lightness, so the edge shimmers (Albers, *Interaction of Color*; Itten, *The Art of Color*). Because it is the only saturated colour on a warm beige-and-grey page, the tilt plus the vibration make it the first thing anyone sees. But it only repeats what the subhead and the "Sale" nav item already say.
   - **What it costs:** The viewer's first glance goes on decoding a word, not on the offer, and the shop's quiet, tasteful tone gets a jolt of cheap discount-store.
   - **Fix:** If you keep a badge, make the text clearly lighter or darker than its ground, or mute one of the two colours. Better, take it out of the brand's own palette (say, dark ink on the beige, or one restrained accent) and give it real information such as "40% off". Or drop it, and let the headline and a button carry the sale.
2. **The sale goes nowhere: no call to action, and no sale prices on the products.** Defect.
   - **What's wrong:** "Up to 40% off through Sunday" is the one line that should make someone act, and it is a dead end. The page has no button at all (`targets` is empty), so nothing says "Shop the sale". The four cards under "Popular this week" show single prices ($34, $89, $52, $68) with no crossed-out price and no "–30%".
   - **What it costs:** The shopper can't tell whether any of these items is on sale, so the headline offer and the products below it read as two unrelated things.
   - **Fix:** Put one primary button under the subhead that names the destination, such as "Shop the sale" or "Shop 40% off linens". Then make the cards prove the offer: show the original and sale price, or change the row to the sale's best items.
3. **The product pictures are empty placeholders.** Defect, unless this is a capture problem.
   - **What's wrong:** All four cards show a tinted panel with a blank rounded rectangle.
   - **What it costs:** On a homewares page the photo is the product. Without it a "Stoneware bowl set" or "Wool throw" gives the viewer nothing to want, and the hero, which has no image either, can't show "linens and cookware" at all.
   - **Fix:** This is fine only if real photos load in production; check the live page. If photos exist, a hero image of linen or cookware would also let the headline and picture make one idea together, rather than the flag echoing the nav.
4. **The headline can be read two ways.** Copy, and a defect for a time-limited offer.
   - **What's wrong:** "Linens and cookware for the weekend" can mean things to use at the weekend (brunch, lie-ins) or a sale that lasts the weekend. Only the subhead settles it.
   - **What it costs:** It sounds like it could sit on any homewares site and doesn't say what's different here.
   - **Fix:** Lead with the deal in the viewer's terms, for example "40% off linens and cookware, this weekend only", and let the subhead give the end date and time. "Through Sunday" with no time also leaves shoppers guessing whether Sunday night still counts.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale:** There are 7 sizes: 40, 34, 22, 20, 18, 16, 15 (`type_sizes`). 15/16 (card price and name) and 18/20/22 (subhead, brand, section heading) are too close to read as levels. Keep about four sizes (40 / 22 / 16 / 14) and separate price from name by weight or colour.
- **Left edges:** The hero text starts at x=56, an 8 px near-miss against the brand and "Popular this week" at x=48 (`left_edges`). Line it up at 48.
- **Current page:** None of the nav items (Kitchen, Bedroom, Garden, Sale) shows which page you're on or stands apart as links. "Sale" could at least carry the accent, since that's where this page is pushing people.

**What's working.**
- The base palette is restrained: warm off-white hero band (#efece6), near-black ink, soft muted card tints. It feels calm and domestic, which suits linens and stoneware. Keep it and make the sale speak in it.
- The headline is set well: 40 px bold at 1.15 line height (`h1`), so the two lines hold together, and it breaks at a sensible phrase.
- The card grid is clean and consistent: equal widths, equal gutters, name above price, every text block at 13:1 or better.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty band under the cards (about 220 px) and the blank product images may be the capture, not the design. Check in a live browser that the photos load and that more content follows.
- Whether the cards and nav items are actually links, and what hover and focus look like.
