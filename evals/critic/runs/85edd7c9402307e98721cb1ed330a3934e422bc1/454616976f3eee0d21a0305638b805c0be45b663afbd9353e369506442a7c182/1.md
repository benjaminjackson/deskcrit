**First read.** The eye lands on "Revenue overview", drops to $4.82M, then sinks into the big block of blue bars. It reads straight away as a SaaS revenue dashboard. It's calm and tidy, and it looks like a lot of other SaaS dashboards.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: an internal revenue dashboard. A founder or finance lead should be able to read 2025 performance at a glance and trust every number on it.

**Verdict.** It's well built and easy to read. But the two 2025 revenue numbers on the page disagree with each other, and nothing explains why. That's the biggest problem, because on a finance screen it undermines trust in every other figure. After that, the page shows numbers without saying what they mean.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two "2025 revenue" totals that don't match (defect).** "Revenue by plan, 2025" totals $4.82M, the same as the ARR card. Add up the monthly bars and you get about $4.49M of recognised revenue for 2025. A finance reader will add them up, find roughly $330k missing, and stop trusting the dashboard. If the table is ARR split by plan, label it "ARR by plan, Dec 2025". If it really is 2025 revenue, the chart or the table is wrong. Either way, every total on the page needs to say which measure it is and which period it covers, so no two can be confused.
2. **The chart and table only describe the data (taste, argued).** "Monthly revenue, 2025" says what is plotted, not what happened. The story is steady growth from about $310k to $447k, with dips in April and July. Right now the viewer has to work that out from the bars. A title that states the finding ("Monthly revenue grew 44% through 2025") gives the viewer the conclusion in one line. The table has a similar gap: it gives no plan share or change since last year, so it doesn't say what's moving. The KPI cards already do this well with their "vs 2024" lines; the chart and table should do the same.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart and table split the width at about 960/1012 px, unrelated to the three-card grid above (card 3 starts at 926). Put the table on the third card's column so the page reads as one grid (Müller-Brockmann).
- "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025" sit about 4 px apart vertically. Put them on one baseline.
- The "Jan to Dec 2025" pill looks like a static tag. If it's a date picker, give it a chevron or calendar icon so it looks clickable (Norman, signifiers).
- The nav highlights "Overview", the page is titled "Revenue overview", and "Revenue" is a separate item. Viewers will wonder what the Revenue page holds that this one doesn't. Match the page title to the nav item, or make the difference clear.

**What's working.**

- The chart is honest and quiet: zero baseline, faint gridlines, one color, units stated in the subtitle, and a source line saying what's included ("closed invoices only").
- The type scale is short and clear (big figures, then headings, labels and captions), done in one family and mostly through weight. The table uses right-aligned, tabular figures.
