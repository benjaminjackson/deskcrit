**First read.** The eye goes first to the mustard tile in the middle of the bottom row, because it is the brightest and warmest thing on the page. Then it drifts around the six coloured blocks in no set order. The page title is easy to miss because it looks like a seventh tile caption. The page reads as a gallery of six garden areas at a show, but none of the "pictures" shows a garden: each is a flat colour field with a pale disc. It feels like a wireframe or a template, not something a visitor would enjoy.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery that lets visitors to a garden show browse the areas of the garden and decide which ones they want to see.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. A gallery depends on its pictures, and these six tiles show no roses, herbs, pond or orchard. All the page offers is six labels on a rainbow of decorative swatches. Under that, nothing on the page leads: the title is set exactly like the tile names.

**What would move it most**

1. **The pictures show nothing.** This is a **defect**. All six tiles are a flat saturated colour with the same pale circle centred in them, and each circle's bottom exactly touches the tile's bottom edge. A visitor learns no more from "Rose arch" with a red block than from the words alone. The tiles promise a photo and don't deliver one, so the page reads as unfinished. The repeated disc and its tangent edge make it look like a placeholder even more. The checklist has no catalogue entry for this, so it's common sense. If these are fallbacks for images that didn't load, check them in a live browser, and the finding is about the fallback instead: it should look like a deliberate loading state, not artwork. If the swatches are the intended design, swap in real photos of each area, cropped the same way, so the gallery shows what each place looks like.

2. **Six vivid hues at equal weight, with no colour leading.** This is a **convention** break, and it costs something here. Saturated red, green, blue, brown, mustard and purple each fill a 377×200 block and together cover nearly half the page. No hue leads. The mustard tile pulls the eye only because warm, light colour comes forward (Itten, warm and cool contrast), not because the Glasshouse matters most. The colours don't mean anything either: green and blue happen to suit Herb bed and Pond edge, but purple for an orchard and mustard for a glasshouse are arbitrary. Itten's colour chords call this a defect: several vivid hues at equal area with none dominant. Real photos fix most of it. If colour stays, use one shared treatment, such as light tints or neutrals, and keep strong colour for one element that deserves it (Refactoring UI, "Define your shades").

3. **The title doesn't lead.** This is a **defect**. "Garden show gallery" is 22 px bold `#1f2328` (`font_size`, `font_weight`, `color`, `h1`), exactly the same as all six tile names (`figure.tile.c1–c6 > h2`). The page has only two type sizes (`type_sizes`: 22 and 15), so the page title and the item names sit on the same level. The visitor can't tell at a glance whether "Garden show gallery" names the page or is just one more item in the grid. Tschichold asks that the order of importance show through differences of size. Make the title clearly larger than the tile names, which gives a three-step scale: page title, item name, caption (Bringhurst on drawing sizes from a scale). The copy has the same weakness: "Garden show gallery" names a category. It doesn't tell the visitor which show it is or what they can do here.

**Smaller things.**
- The Rose arch tile is set differently from the other five. Its name has a 1.15 line height against 1.3 for the rest (`line_height_ratio`, `figure.tile.c1 > h2` vs `c2–c6`), so its caption sits at y 311 instead of 315 (`box`, `figure.tile.c1 > p`). The 2–4 px near-miss in the first row looks like a mistake. Fix: use the same line height as the other tiles.
- The circles are centred on the same spot in every tile, which looks like a habit rather than a decision. They go away with real images. If they stay, place them with a reason or drop them.

**What's working.**
- The text is solid. Captions are 15 px at 6.11:1 contrast (`contrast`, `figure.tile.* > p`), in a quieter grey and regular weight under bold, near-black names. Size, weight and colour all mark the difference between name and caption, so keep that pairing.
- The grid is clean: three columns with one 14 px gutter, every text block on one of three left edges (`left_edges`; `blocks_on_no_shared_edge`: 0), and a wide gap between rows that keeps each tile's caption with its own image.
- The captions are concrete and specific ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Tomatoes in late July"). That is the right voice for helping a visitor choose where to go.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the colour blocks are image fallbacks for photos that didn't load; check in a live browser. Also whether the tiles are links, and if so what they open and how hover and focus look.
