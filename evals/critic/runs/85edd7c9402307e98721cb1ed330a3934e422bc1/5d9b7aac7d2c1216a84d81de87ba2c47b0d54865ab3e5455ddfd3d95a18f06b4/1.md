**First read.** The eye lands on the red-bordered email field, drops to the error line under it, then to the purple "Sign in" button. It reads at once as a sign-in screen for a product called Quillpost, caught mid-error. It feels calm, orderly and generic, like a well-built default.

**What it's for.** Nothing was given, so I'm assuming this is the sign-in screen for returning Quillpost users, shown in the state where the email they typed isn't a valid address. Its job is to get them signed in with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The error is easy to find, labelled in words, and the way forward is obvious. What's left is small: the sign-up route doesn't look clickable, and the error message makes the user work out their own mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link.** It's at the bottom of the card, in the same grey and weight as "New here?", with no colour or underline. Meanwhile "Forgot your password?" just above it is purple. A new user who lands here has one way forward, and it looks like a caption, so some will scan past it or wonder if it's clickable (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: signifiers; WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color). This is a defect for the minority of visitors who aren't signed up yet. Fix: give "Create an account" the link styling "Forgot your password?" already uses, ideally with an underline, and keep "New here?" grey so the two parts read differently.
2. **The error says what format to use, not what's wrong.** "Enter an email address in the format name@example.com" is clear and polite, but the user has to compare "dana.ortiz@exmaple" against the pattern to spot the problem: the address has no ending like ".com", and "exmaple" is probably a typo. A message that names the actual gap in the user's terms would cut that comparison out, for example "This address is missing its ending, like .com" or, where you can detect it, "Did you mean example.com?". This is taste: the current message meets WCAG 3.3.3 (error suggestion), but it makes the user do the diagnosis.

**Smaller things.**
- The password field border looks like pale grey on white, around 2:1, under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of input edges. Next to the strong red email field it almost disappears. Measure the border colour and darken it to at least 3:1.

**What's working.**
- The error state is handled properly. The red border, the message directly under the field, and the field's own label make it obvious which field is wrong and that it's text-based, not colour alone. Keep the message tied to the field like this.
- There's one clear primary action. The full-width filled "Sign in" button is the only heavy object on the card, and the two secondary routes are set as text below it.
- The spacing groups correctly. Each label sits tight to its field, there's more space between field groups, and everything sits on one left edge. The card reads as a single column you can run down without hunting.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus styling on the fields and button. Check that a keyboard user sees a clear ring at least 2 px thick with 3:1 contrast, and that it's distinct from the red error border on the email field.
- When validation fires (on blur, on submit, or while typing). Firing while the user is still typing would make this error feel like nagging.
