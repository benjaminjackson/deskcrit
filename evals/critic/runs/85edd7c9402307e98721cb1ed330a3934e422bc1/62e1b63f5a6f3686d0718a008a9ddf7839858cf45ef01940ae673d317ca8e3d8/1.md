**First read.** The eye lands on the bold sans heading at top left, then drops straight into three even serif paragraphs. It reads as a plain information page for a small river ferry: calm, quiet, easy to read. Nothing pulls at the eye, and nothing else on the page asks for attention. The right 40% of the screen and the bottom third are empty.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a public web page that tells people who will ride the ferry when it runs and how to bring a bike. Most of them want one answer, such as "when is the next boat" or "when is the last one."

**Verdict.** The typography is good and gets out of the way. The page still fails at its job, because the copy never gives the facts a passenger acts on: no departure times, no first or last boat, no winter dates. It sends people to a printed sheet at the landing, which is the place they are trying to plan their trip to.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule is given in words that no one can plan by.** This is a defect. It sits in paragraphs 1 and 3. "Every forty minutes" with no starting time means you can't work out a single departure. "Between early morning and late evening" doesn't say when the first or last boat goes. "In winter" doesn't say which months. "Before dusk" changes by an hour or more across the season. "The last crossing leaves the quay" can be read two ways: the market quay only, or whichever landing you are at. Someone at the north landing who reads it the wrong way could miss the last boat home. These are the lines people remember and hold the operator to, so vague wording here is a defect, not a style choice. Fix: give real times. That means the first and last departure from each landing, one sample departure (e.g. "from the north landing at 6:20, then every 40 minutes"), the winter dates, and the winter last crossing as a clock time from each landing. Put the timetable on the page itself, or link to it, instead of sending people to "the shelter at each landing."
2. **The answer people come for is buried in the last sentence.** This is taste, argued from the job. The last crossing, which is the fact with the worst result if someone misses it, sits in the middle of paragraph 3, after the bike rules. The heading "Notes for ferry passengers" could sit on any ferry's page and tells a scanning reader nothing. Fix: open with the times, as a short timetable or a two-line summary. Let the heading or a short lead line state the service ("Ferry every 40 minutes, north landing ↔ market quay"). The bike note and the shelter note can follow.
