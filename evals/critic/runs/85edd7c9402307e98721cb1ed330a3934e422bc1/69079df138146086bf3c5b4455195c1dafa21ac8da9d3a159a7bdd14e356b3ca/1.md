**Notes for ferry passengers: critique**

**First read.** My eye lands on the black bold heading, "Notes for ferry passengers". Then everything fades. The two paragraphs are pale grey and look like disabled or placeholder text, and the last line is smaller and just as pale, so it reads as a footnote. This is a quiet, plain information page for people taking a river ferry. It feels calm, but washed out, as if the content hasn't fully loaded.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's a web info page that tells ferry riders the route, how often the boat runs, the bicycle rules, and that winter timetables change.

**Verdict.** The structure and copy are sound, but the page fails its basic job of being read. All the text a passenger needs is set in pale grey that looks well under the contrast minimum. The one line that can strand someone, the winter timetable warning, is the faintest and smallest thing on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **All the body text reads as faint.** This is a defect. Both paragraphs and the note line are in a light grey on white that looks about 2.5:1. That is at the very bottom of the range this defect covers, and the minimum for normal text is 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3). The pale grey also looks like disabled text, so the eye drops off it to the heading and skims. People in bright sun on a landing, or with low vision, will struggle to read it at all. Apart from the heading, the page has one value, so pale grey isn't separating any levels; it is only lowering legibility. Fix: darken the text until it reaches at least 4.5:1. A dark grey near the heading colour works, and the heading can stay apart through size and weight, as it already does. Measure the actual hex values to confirm the ratio.
2. **The most consequential line is styled as the least important.** This is a defect. "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel." is the only line here that a passenger acts on, and getting it wrong means waiting on a closed landing. It is set smaller than the body and in the same pale grey, so it reads as fine print. Fix: give it at least body size and full-strength text. Better, set it apart as a short notice: put it first, or give it a bold lead-in like "In winter:". Better still, tell people when winter starts or where to find the winter timetable online, so they don't have to go to the landing to find out.
3. **The copy leaves the timing vague.** This is a defect, because the reader acts on these times. "Between early morning and late evening" and "every forty minutes" give no first or last sailing, so a reader can't plan around a 40-minute gap. Fix: give the first and last departure times from each landing, for example "from 6:40 to 22:20". If the times vary, say where the timetable is.

**Smaller things.** None. The rest of the page is clean.

**What's working.**
- **The heading.** It is dark, bold, and clearly larger than the text, so the page has one obvious starting point.
- **The column.** It is flush left with comfortable line spacing, a measure of about 75 characters, and clear paragraph spacing. That setting just needs darker ink.
- **The copy.** It is plain and specific where it counts: bicycles go on the lower deck, cost nothing extra, board after foot passengers, and lock to the rail. Each sentence says who does what.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone, which is likely where people read this on the landing.
