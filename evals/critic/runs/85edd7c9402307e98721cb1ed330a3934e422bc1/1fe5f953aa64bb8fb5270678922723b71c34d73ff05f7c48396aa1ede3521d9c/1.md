**First read.** Three loud, equal slabs of colour (burnt orange, green, purple) hit before any word does. The eye then goes to the big white headings from left to right, and each one leads to a short blurb. It reads as a pool or leisure centre's three swimming sessions. It feels bold and cheerful, but it ends abruptly: the eye reaches the third blurb and has nowhere else to go.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the programmes section of a swimming pool's website. Its job is to get an adult learner, a lap swimmer or a parent to pick their session and take the next step: book, see times or find out more.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do that job yet. The headings and blurbs are well written and well set, but nothing tells the viewer what to do next. Nearly half of each card is empty colour where that next step should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no next step anywhere** (defect). None of the three cards has a button, link, arrow or price. If the cards are tappable, nothing shows it. Flat colour with text reads as a sign, not a control (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: signifiers; Krug: "obvious clickables"). If they aren't tappable, the section is a dead end. Either way, a viewer who decides "that one's for me" has to guess how to act. The copy has the same gap: "Tuesday evenings" and "every morning from six" give no hours, no price and no way to book, so the viewer can't commit without hunting elsewhere. Fix: give each card one specific ask in the viewer's terms, such as "Book a Tuesday class", "See lane times" or "Bring the kids this Sunday". Put it at the bottom of the card, styled the same on all three.

2. **The lower half of every card is dead space** (defect, plus taste). From roughly y 330 to 630, each card is about 300 px of empty saturated colour. This isn't space gathered to give the eye a rest (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). It's leftover space, so the cards look top-heavy and unfinished, as if something failed to load. Under the cards there's another ~170 px of white. Fix: either shorten the cards to fit their content, or fill the lower area on purpose with the ask from finding 1 and the hard facts (times, price, ages). If you want the height for presence, give it to a photo of the water, which this pool page has none of.

3. **Three equally vivid hues fight, and none of them says "pool"** (convention; the mood part is taste). Orange-red, green and purple at equal size and full saturation fill about 60% of the frame. Each could be the main colour, so none is, and the eye gets no lead (Itten, *The Art of Color*: keep one hue dominant and mute the others; Refactoring UI: large areas of full saturation tire the eye). The three are also unrelated: they look like stock framework shades rather than one palette. The warm orange pushes "Learn to swim" forward for no reason in the content. In my view, a pool's main associations are water and calm, and this palette reads more like a fast-food menu. Fix: pick one lead hue, ideally one that belongs to water, and set the other cards in tints or shades of it. Or keep one colour per programme but drop them to light tints and keep full strength for the action.

**Smaller things.**
- The row sits off-centre: 56 px of margin on the left against 96 px on the right. Centre it, or align it to the page's content column.
- Heading breaks split phrases: "Learn to / swim" and "Join the / lanes". Break after "Learn" and "Join", or widen the cards so each heading breaks between whole phrases.
- Blurb lines run about 38–40 characters, short of 45–75 (Bringhurst). That's harmless at three lines, but if the blurbs grow, set them wider.

**What's working.**
- The headings are clear, bold, tightly leaded display type in a separate weight and size from the plain body. Each card makes its point in one glance. Keep that two-level type scale.
- The copy reads like a person talking to the viewer: "a coach in the water beside you", "three speeds to choose from", "floats and toys for the children". Each line ends on what it means for them. Keep that voice when you add the asks.
- The grid is clean, with equal 24 px gutters, matching card widths and shared baselines. White on these dark-ish shades looks like it passes contrast.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are links, and their hover and focus states. If they're links, they still need a visible signifier (finding 1).
