**First read.** My eye goes straight to the big white headline over the dark leaves, then down to the grey paragraph and across three neat cards. It reads as a small local garden centre's homepage, calm and trustworthy. Then it ends: the cards give facts, and nothing asks me to do anything.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage of a local garden centre. It should get nearby people, especially those with shady or small spaces, to visit, book a workshop, or order for delivery.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and readable, and its copy is unusually concrete. But it gives the viewer no next step and no picture of what's for sale, so it informs without selling. The biggest thing in the way is the missing call to action.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's nothing to click in the content.** *Defect.* The page states three offers: plants ready this weekend, Saturday workshops, and free delivery. None of them has a button or link. "Saturday workshops… ten places in each class" makes me want a place, then gives no date, price, or way to book one. The eye ends on the third card and the page goes blank. Fix: add one primary action that matches the headline's promise, such as "See shade plants in stock" under the lead. Give each card its own smaller action tied to its fact: "Book a Saturday place", "Get directions", "Order for delivery". Keep only one of them styled as primary.
2. **The hero image doesn't show what the headline sells.** *Defect / taste.* "Plants for shade and small spaces" is a specific promise. Behind it sits a generic, evenly busy leaf pattern that could belong to any brand with green in it. The image adds no information, so the viewer has to picture the ferns and hostas from the text. That gives up the most persuasive thing a nursery has: its plants. Fix: use a real photo of the stock, ideally a shaded corner or balcony planted with it. Keep a dark, quiet area behind the headline so the white type still holds.
3. **The headline is 16 px off the page's left edge.** *Defect (craft).* Every other block starts at x=56: the logo, the lead, and the cards. The H1 starts at x=72 (`box`, `h1`). That's close enough to look like a mistake, not a choice, and it's the most visible text on the page. Fix: put it on the 56 px edge, or move it clearly off the edge if that's the intent.

**Smaller things**
- In the H1, "spaces" sits alone on the second line (26 characters per line, `characters_per_line`, `h1`). Break it as "Plants for shade / and small spaces", or widen the box so it fits on one line.
- In the Opening hours card, the line breaks inside "8 / pm". Keep the time together with a non-breaking space.
- The lead paragraph's measure ends around x=692, which matches no card edge. Its right edge doesn't belong to the three-column grid below. Set it to span two cards.
- There's a ~120 px empty band under the cards on a 800 px screen. It reads as leftover space, not a pause. Use it for the action or a preview of what's in stock.

**What's working**
- **The copy is specific and written for real people.** "Open every day from 8 am to 6 pm, until 8 pm on Thursdays", "Free within ten miles on orders over $60", "ready to pot this weekend". Each line gives an exact fact the viewer can act on. Keep this voice when you add actions.
- **The type is tidy.** There are five sizes (46/22/20/17/15, `type_sizes`) with clear jobs. Card heads are set apart by weight, not by another size. The lead runs at 64 characters per line (`p.lead`). All text on white is at or above 6.1:1 (`contrast`).
- **The serif green wordmark against Inter elsewhere** gives the brand a quiet, horticultural warmth without becoming a collage.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- The nav items are `span`s, not links (`nav > span`), and no target was measured. In a browser, check that Plants, Tools, Workshops, and Visit are actually links and can be reached by keyboard.
