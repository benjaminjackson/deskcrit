**First read.** The eye lands on the purple Sign in button, jumps up to the "Sign in" heading, and then catches on the red outline around the email field. It reads at once as a login for a writing or publishing tool called Quillpost. It feels calm, tidy and familiar. But the red outline makes me stop and ask "what did I do wrong?", and nothing on the screen answers.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the sign-in screen for returning Quillpost users, captured just after the email failed validation.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and conventional, which is right for a login. But the one thing this state exists to do, telling the user what went wrong, is missing. The email field turns red with no message, so the user has to find a one-letter typo ("exmaple") without being told to look for one.

**What would move it most**
1. **The error has no words.** The email field (top field, red 2 px border) is the only sign that anything failed. There's no message under it, no icon, and no banner above the form. The user sees red, assumes something is wrong, and has to guess what: the address, the password, or the account itself. Anyone who can't tell red from the grey border of the password field gets no signal at all. The actual mistake, "exmaple" for "example", is easy to read straight past, because people read what they meant to type. **Defect** (WCAG 2.2, 3.3.1 Error Identification; 1.4.1 Use of Color). Fix: put a short message directly under the email field that says what is wrong and how to fix it, and keep the red border as a second cue. "Check the domain: did you mean example.com?" is far better than "Invalid email" (WCAG 3.3.3 Error Suggestion). If the real failure was "no account with this email", say that instead, and link to Create an account.

2. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link.** In the line at the bottom of the card, "New here? Create an account" is one grey, same-weight string. Nothing marks the second half as clickable. A new user scanning for a way in sees a sentence, not an action. It's also inconsistent: "Forgot your password?" is purple, so the screen sets up "purple means link" and then breaks it on the other link. **Defect** (Nielsen heuristic 4, Consistency and standards; Norman, signifiers). Fix: make "Create an account" the same link colour as "Forgot your password?", and leave "New here?" grey.

**Smaller things.**
- The password field's border looks very pale grey on white, probably under 3:1, so the field's edge only just holds. Measure it, and darken the border until it reaches 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is right for the job: wordmark, then heading, then two labelled fields, then one strong button. There's exactly one primary action, and nothing competes with it. Keep the single purple button.
- The labels sit above the fields in bold, close to their own input and clearly separated from the next one, so the form still reads correctly after someone has typed into it.
- The purple is used sparingly: wordmark, one link, the button. That restraint is why the button reads instantly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the red border is an error state or a red-styled focus ring. Check in a browser that focus and error look different from each other.
- The tap height of "Forgot your password?" and "Create an account". They look about 18 px tall, so check they reach 24 px (WCAG 2.5.8).
- Whether the error is announced to screen readers when it appears.
