**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline "Help around the house, on your schedule", then drops to the four bright illustrations, which pull harder than any text under them. It reads straight away as a homepage for a local home-services company, for homeowners. It feels friendly, tidy and calm. Then the eye stops: under the cards there is a band of empty cream and nothing to do. The only action is the small "Book a visit" button in the top-right corner, where the eye has already been.

**What it's for.** Assumed: the homepage of a local home-services business. Its job is to get a homeowner to pick a service and book it.

**Verdict.** The page is clean, clear and consistent, and in a few seconds a viewer knows what this company does. But it does not do its job: it tells the viewer to "Pick a service, choose a day", then gives them nothing to pick and no day to choose, and the prices don't say what they cost per.

**What would move it most**

1. **The cards are where the visitor decides, and they lead nowhere.** *Defect.*
   - The lede says "Pick a service, choose a day". The four cards are clearly the services, but none has a button, link, arrow or any cue that it can be clicked or chosen. The row ends on a bold teal price and then about 150 px of empty cream.
   - The only booking action, "Book a visit" (121×43, `div.cta`), sits in the header about 600 px away from the cards, and it doesn't know which service the visitor chose.
   - So a visitor who has just decided "I want deep cleaning" has to guess whether the card is clickable, then go back up to a general button.
   - Clickable things should look clickable (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on signifiers; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: users should see what they can do and where to click within seconds). Putting the next step where the decision happens is common sense.
   - **Fix:** give each card its own visible action that names the service ("Book deep cleaning →"), or make the whole card an obvious link. Add one primary action under the lede, so the hero itself has an ask. The "choose a day" step should start from that action.

2. **The prices don't say what they're for, and one service contradicts the headline.** *Defect.*
   - "From $24", "From $65", "From $89" and "From $189" have no unit. $24 for laundry could be per load, per bag, per week or per pickup. $189 for deep cleaning could be per visit, per room or per hour.
   - Price is the line people remember and later hold you to, so a reading the visitor can take two ways is a real risk. If they assume "per visit" and it's "per room", they feel tricked at checkout.
   - The headline promises "on your schedule" and the lede says "choose a day", but the laundry card says "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday". That's a fixed schedule, so one of the two is wrong for laundry.
   - **Fix:** put the unit on every price ("From $24 per bag", "From $189 per visit", or whatever is true). Either reword the laundry line so it fits the promise ("Pick it up any weekday, back folded in two days") or narrow the headline.

3. **The two claims that would win the booking are asked on trust.** *Taste, but it decides whether anyone acts.*
   - "On your schedule" and "a vetted crew" are the page's whole case, and nothing on screen backs either one. There is no earliest available day, no word on what "vetted" means (background-checked? insured?), no reviews and no service area.
   - As written, the copy could sit on any cleaning company's page, so it gives the visitor no reason to choose this one.
   - **Fix:** show the proof next to the claim, in terms the visitor already trusts. For example, "Next opening: Thursday", or "Background-checked and insured, 4.9 from 1,200 local homes". The empty band under the cards is room for this.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type sizes that collide:** the card titles are 19 px (`font_size`, `article.card > h3`) and the lede is 18 px (`p.lede`), so there are five sizes (40/24/19/18/15) where four would do. Bring the lede to body size or make the card titles clearly larger, and let bold carry the difference.
- **Headline break:** the H1 breaks inside the phrase "on your / schedule", which leaves "schedule" alone on line 2. Break after the comma ("Help around the house, / on your schedule"), or widen the text box (760 px now) so the headline fits on one line.
- **Price emphasis:** the price is 15 px, the same size as the description. On a page whose job is booking, it could gain a step of size or weight. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- **The illustrations:** all four share one flat style, one pale tint panel and one small palette (teal, coral, warm yellow on cream). The row reads as one set and the page feels warm rather than clinical. Keep that discipline when adding anything new.
- **The card descriptions:** they are concrete and specific ("screens and sills wiped", "seasonal planting for small yards"), and they give a stranger a clear picture of what's included.
- **Hierarchy and readability:** the headline clearly leads (40 px serif, 13.37:1 contrast), the grid is strict (four equal columns, everything on a 56 px left edge), and every text block passes contrast. The lowest is the price at 5.16:1.
