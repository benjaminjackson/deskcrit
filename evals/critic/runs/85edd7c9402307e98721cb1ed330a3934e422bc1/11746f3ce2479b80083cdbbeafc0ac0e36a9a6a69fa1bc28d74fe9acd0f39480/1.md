**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops to the blue "See the timetable" button, then goes back up to the paragraph. It reads as the home page for a small local ferry. It feels calm and trustworthy, but bare: there is no picture, and the bottom third of the screen is empty. It looks like the top of a template rather than a finished page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a ferry operator's desktop home page. Its job is to make a local or a visitor want to take the crossing and send them to the timetable.

**Verdict.** The basics are sound: one clear headline, one clear button, and readable, high-contrast text. As a page that sells the crossing it is thin. It leaves out the facts a passenger needs before going, and it never shows the calm harbour it promises.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy leaves out or blurs the facts a passenger plans around (defect).** "Boats leave the north landing every forty minutes from early morning until late evening":
   - It never says where the boat goes. "Cross the bay" from the north landing to where?
   - "Early morning" and "late evening" could mean 6 am or 8 am, 7 pm or 11 pm. A commuter or someone catching the last boat home can't plan on that, and these are the words people hold the operator to.
   - There's no fare. "Bicycles travel free" suggests there is a price, but the page never says what it is.

   As a result, nobody can decide from this page whether the ferry fits their day, so the button becomes a lookup rather than a decision. **Fix:** name both landings, give the first and last sailing ("first boat 6:40, last 22:00"), and state the adult fare. The headline is the best line on the page: specific, physical, and about what the passenger gets. Keep it.
2. **The page sells a view and shows none (taste, and I'd argue it hard).** The headline promises "calm" and the paragraph promises "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck". Yet the whole frame is white space, with a blank band of about 250 px under the button. The viewer has to imagine the reason to go. A ferry's strongest argument is what the crossing looks like, and right now this could be any company's page. **Fix:** give the empty lower part of the screen to one wide photo of the open deck or the bay. That picture carries the mood the words describe, and it gives the page a bottom so it stops looking cut off.
3. **The paragraph and the perks list run together (convention).** The paragraph is 20 px and the three perks are 18 px (`font_size`, `p.lead` vs `ul.points > li`), in the same weight, colour and centred alignment. The list has no bullets or icons. Two sizes 2 px apart don't read as two levels; they read as the same text set slightly off (Bringhurst, 3.1.1: keep to "a modest set of distinct and related intervals"). So the three selling points ("bicycles travel free" is a real reason to choose this boat) look like leftover lines of the paragraph, not a list to scan. **Fix:** set the perks at the paragraph's size, or clearly smaller, and set them apart by weight or a simple marker. You could also lay them out in a row so they read as a set.

**Smaller things.**
- Every block sits on one centred axis, including the 3-line, 73-character paragraph (`characters_per_line`, `p.lead`). Long centred lines make the eye hunt for each line start. Keep centring for the headline, and set the paragraph flush left or cut it to two short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*).
- The headline breaks "twelve / calm minutes", which splits the number from its unit. Break it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" so the promise sits on one line.

**What's working.**
- The headline is concrete and specific to this ferry: a duration and a feeling, not a slogan. At 52 px bold it is clearly the first thing seen (`font_size`, `h1`).
- There is one primary action, and it says exactly what happens: "See the timetable". It's a solid 209×55 target with white on blue at 6.67:1 (`contrast`, `a.button`).
- The type is quiet and legible: one family, dark text at 15.8:1, and comfortable line spacing in the paragraph (1.55).
