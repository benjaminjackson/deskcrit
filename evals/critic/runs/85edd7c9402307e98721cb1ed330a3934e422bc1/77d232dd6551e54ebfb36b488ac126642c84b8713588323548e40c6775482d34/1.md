**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline, "The allotment at the end of the bus line", then moves to the italic standfirst and on into the body. It reads at once as a long-form magazine feature: quiet, plain, trustworthy. What stands out is what's missing. The byline says "Photographs by the residents", but the screen shows no photograph, and the bottom quarter is empty white.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the opening screen of an online magazine feature, and it has to pull a general reader into a long read about a community garden.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The headline is in charge, the order of the text is right, and the writing is specific and good. Two things get in the way. The text column is set too wide for comfortable reading. And a story about a place, with photographs credited, opens without a single picture of that place.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body column is too wide to read comfortably (convention).** The standfirst and body paragraphs are set at 18 px in a 720 px column, which gives 81–83 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `p.lede` 81.3, `div.col > p:nth-of-type(2)` 83, `div.col > p:nth-of-type(3)` 80). Bringhurst puts the comfortable range at 45–75 characters, with 66 as the ideal (*Elements of Typographic Style*, §2.1.2). In a feature, the reader spends minutes in this column, and lines this long make the eye hunt for the start of the next line on every return. The generous 1.6 line height helps, but it doesn't make up the difference. Fix: bring the column down to about 600–620 px at 18 px, or raise the size to 20 px in the current width, so lines land near 65 characters. The headline can stay wider than the text.
2. **No opening picture, though photographs are credited (taste, but it decides the first impression).** The byline promises "Photographs by the residents", yet nothing on the first screen shows the plot, the bath-tub beds or the queue at the tap. The headline is doing all the work alone, and the page looks like a blog post rather than a feature. The writing is visual and the photographs exist, so the page should open on one. If a lead image is meant to sit between the byline and the standfirst and simply didn't load, the layout is fine once it does. Fix: put one strong resident photograph, ideally the beds against the bus stop, right after the byline, so the picture and the headline make one idea: an allotment where you'd expect a car park.
3. **The headline breaks to leave "line" alone (convention).** At 40 px in a 720 px box, the h1 wraps to "…end of the bus" / "line". The largest thing on the page ends on a single short word and splits the phrase "bus line" (Bringhurst §2.4.2 on short last lines). It looks accidental, and the eye briefly reads "bus" as the end of the thought. Fix: balance the break so the two lines are closer in length, for example "The allotment at the end / of the bus line", or let the headline run a little wider so it fits on one line.

**Smaller things.**
- The navigation doesn't mark the current section: "Features · Food · Places · Subscribe" draws every item alike, while the kicker says "Feature". Give the current item one cue, such as bold or an underline (Nielsen Norman Group, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- "Subscribe", the one action the business wants, is set exactly like the section links. Separate it with an outline or its own spot at the right end of the masthead, so it reads as an action and not as a fourth section.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean and comes from more than size alone. The bold 40 px headline sits over an italic standfirst and a roman body at 18 px, with a small bold green kicker and a grey 14 px byline. Every level has an obvious job, and there's only one typeface. Keep this scale.
- The copy is specific and gives the reader a picture. "The queue for the shared water tap is the best place in the district to hear the news" and "pallets and old bath tubs" are exactly what makes a stranger keep reading. The standfirst ends with a clear promise: "We spent a season with the people who built it."
- The restraint suits the story. A neutral palette with one quiet green accent, all text at 5.1:1 contrast or better, and a single column on a shared left edge at 280 px make the page feel calm and editorial.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The page ends at 800 px, after the third paragraph, with about 220 px of blank space below. Check in a live browser whether the article continues and whether a lead image is supposed to load. If the story really stops here, the copy isn't finished.
