**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", then jumps to the solid blue "Save changes" button at the bottom right. It reads as a save confirmation in a calendar or group-scheduling app, and it feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. Reading back across the row, "Export as file" and "Discard changes" look like two equally harmless blue links. Nothing about "Discard" suggests it throws away work.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a confirmation dialog that lets someone editing a shared calendar (a choir organiser) publish their edits to every follower, keep editing, or throw the edits away. They should be able to make that choice safely in a couple of seconds.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The question is clear, the consequence is stated, and the safe default is obvious. But the one action that can't be undone is the quietest and least separated button in the row, sitting between a download link and "Keep editing". That's the thing to fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" is camouflaged.** *Defect.* It's the second item in the action row, set in the same blue, unboxed text style as "Export as file", with only about 15 px between its target and "Keep editing" (boxes at x=461–648 and x=663). Someone who wants to back out and aims for the cancel-like buttons can hit Discard. The same-looking pair also tells them Discard is as minor as Export. Then the new rehearsal times are gone, and nothing in this dialog offers a way to undo it. A destructive action should be separated from the safe ones, labelled with its specific outcome, styled as destructive, and never the default (Apple HIG, "Alerts"; Material Design, "Dialogs"). Move it to its own end of the row, apart from "Keep editing" and "Save". Colour it as destructive (red text is the usual way) and say what it loses: "Discard new rehearsal times". Better still, add undo after discarding (Raskin).
2. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision.** *Convention.* The question asked is save, keep editing, or throw away. Export answers none of these, but it takes the far-left slot where desktop users expect the "Don't save" action (Apple HIG). That's partly why Discard ends up looking minor. It also makes four choices where three would do (Nielsen 8, aesthetic and minimalist design; Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). Take it out of this dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu. That leaves three actions, ranked by weight: Save (filled), Keep editing (outline), Discard (destructive text, set apart).

**Smaller things.**
- The body text (18 px, `p`) and the button labels (17 px, all four buttons) are 1 px apart (`type_sizes`). They don't read as two levels; they read as a slip. Set them to one size, or make the step obvious.
- The action row's first label starts at about x=314, while the headline and body start at x=280 (`left_edges`). Either align the row to the 280 edge, or right-align the whole row as a separate group so the near-miss stops showing (taste).
- Copy: "Save changes" is accurate, but what the click actually does is publish the changes to everyone. "Save and share" or "Save for everyone" would match the consequence the body text just stated (taste).

**What's working.**
- The body sentence lands the consequence on the viewer: "will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar". It names the real content ("choir rehearsal times") and who sees it, not a generic "Are you sure?". Keep that.
- The emphasis order is mostly right. There's one filled primary, an outlined secondary, and nothing else competing with the 30 px bold headline. The space is clearly grouped, with a tight heading-to-body gap and a larger gap before the actions.
- The text contrast is solid throughout (15.8:1 for the text, 6.67:1 for the links and the primary button), and every target is 58 px tall.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button gets keyboard focus when the dialog opens. It should be "Save" or "Keep editing", never "Discard". Also whether Escape maps to "Keep editing".
