**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board" in bold at the top left, then runs into three paragraphs set entirely in capitals. They look shouted and read slowly. It reads as a short notice for ferry passengers. It feels calm and plain until the body text starts, and then it feels like a printed warning sign. The right third and the bottom half are empty, which is fine for one short note.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page telling ferry passengers how the service runs before they travel, read at a desk or on a phone just before a crossing.

**Verdict.** Every typographic choice around it is sound, but the body is set in all capitals and that makes the actual information slow to read. The copy also leaves out the facts a passenger acts on: first and last boat times, and when winter starts.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body is set in all capitals.** All three paragraphs below the heading (about 376 characters across 8 lines) are in capitals. Capitals all have the same height and a rectangular outline, so the reader loses the word shapes they skim by. They read letter by letter and tire within two lines. Capitals also read as shouting, which works against the calm "Before you board" tone. The facts file holds this text in mixed case ("The river ferry runs…", `texts`, `main.article > p.body`), so the capitals come from styling, probably a CSS `text-transform`, and removing it is a one-line change. Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps": don't capitalize whole paragraphs. Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, which already uses them correctly. **Convention**, and here it is also a **defect**: this is the content people came to read.

2. **The copy is vague where passengers have to act.** "Between early morning and late evening" doesn't say when the first and last boats sail, and that is the one fact someone planning a trip needs. "In winter the timetable thins to one boat each hour" gives no dates. A reader can't tell whether this applies in November or only from December to February, so they could reach the landing expecting a 40-minute wait and face 60. "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" sends people to the landing to find out when to go to the landing. Fix: give the first and last sailing times, the months the winter timetable runs, and a link to the timetable on this page. **Defect.**

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- **A clean type scale.** There are three sizes, 36 / 18 / 14 px (`type_sizes`), with big steps between them, and the heading is also set apart by weight (700). The hierarchy reads at once. Keep it when you change the case of the body.
- **Body setting.** Measured from the page: 18 px Inter (`font_size`), 1.5 line height (`line_height_ratio`), 67.5–71 characters per line (`characters_per_line`) and 15.8:1 contrast (`contrast`). That is a textbook measure and leading for comfortable reading. Once the body is in mixed case, it will read very well.
- **One left edge.** All five blocks start at x = 120 (`left_edges`), and paragraphs are separated by space alone. Calm and orderly.
