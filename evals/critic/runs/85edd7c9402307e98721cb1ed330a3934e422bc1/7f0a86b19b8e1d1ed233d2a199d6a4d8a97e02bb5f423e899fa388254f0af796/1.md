**First read.** The eye lands on "Learn to swim", big and heavy at top left. It drops to the grey subhead, then wanders across two small text blocks, "Join the lanes" and "Family hour", and stops there with nothing to do next. It reads as the top of a local pool's website: clean, calm, and generic. Nothing on it says which pool this is, where it is, or how to sign up.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a public pool's or swim school's website. Its main job is to get adults to sign up for the Tuesday-evening lessons, and its second job is to mention lane swimming and family sessions.

**Verdict.** The type is tidy, but the page doesn't do its job yet. It has a strong headline and then gives the reader no way to act on it. There's no button, no booking, no price, no time, no place and no name. The two blocks underneath pull attention away to other offers that also have no next step.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's nothing to act on (defect).** The headline makes a promise and the subhead names a concrete offer: small adult classes on Tuesday evenings with a coach in the water. Then the page stops. A reader who wants to learn has no button, no start date, no price, no class time and no place. They have to go looking, and most won't. Put one clear ask straight under the subhead. Make it name the exact thing to do, such as "Book a place in the next Tuesday class, 7 pm", with the price or the next start date beside it. One fact that lets a nervous adult picture the first evening ("no experience needed", "max 6 per class") will do more than any styling.
2. **The page sells three things but the headline covers one (defect).** "Learn to swim" is about adult lessons. The two blocks underneath are about something else: lane swimming for people who already swim, and Sunday sessions for families. Right now they read as more detail about the lessons, so a reader can't tell at once whether this is a lessons page or the pool's home page. Decide which page it is. If it's the lessons page, the two blocks should answer a beginner's questions instead (what a class is like, what to bring, how many people). If it's the home page, the headline should name the pool, and the three offers should sit side by side with equal weight and each have its own link.
3. **Two type sizes nearly match (convention).** The grey subhead looks about 22 px and the card headings look about 20 px. Those are two levels in theory, but they read as one size, so the gap between "intro to the page" and "name of a section" disappears (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1: use a small set of clearly separate sizes). Tighten the scale to about four levels: display, intro, section heading, body. Then separate the card headings from the subhead by weight, for example by setting the card headings in semibold, rather than by a couple of pixels.
4. **The page doesn't feel like swimming (taste, backed by Itten).** It's all black and grey on white, with no picture, no colour and nothing of water, light or warmth. It could just as easily be a page for accounting software. That costs the most with the adult beginner the page is trying to reach. Many of them are anxious about water, and the page gives them nothing reassuring or inviting to look at. Itten's point is that colour should carry the mood the message needs. Bring in one water colour as the accent for the action, or one photo of a calm adult class with the coach in the water, which is exactly what the subhead promises.

**Smaller things.**
- After the two cards, the eye has nowhere to go: they are the last stop on the screen. Fixing item 1 fixes this, because the action becomes the end point.

**What's working.**
- The headline really dominates. It's bold, close to 90 px and at least four times the size of anything else, so there's never any doubt where to start. Keep that contrast in scale.
- The subhead is good copy. "With a coach in the water beside you" speaks to the beginner's actual fear, in plain words. It's specific in a way the rest of the page isn't, so build the ask on it.
- Everything sits on one shared left edge with a clean two-column split and comfortable line lengths. The structure is ready to take content.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The bottom 40% of the screen and the right-hand third are empty. That may be where content below the fold starts, or the capture may have been taken before something like an image or a button loaded. Check the page in a live browser. If that space really is empty at this width, the first screen looks unfinished.
