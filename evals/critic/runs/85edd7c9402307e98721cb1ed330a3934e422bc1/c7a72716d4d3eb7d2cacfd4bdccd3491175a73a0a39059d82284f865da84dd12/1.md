**First read.** My eye goes to the headline first, then the orange button, then the grey "Keep your streak going" box right under it. That box stops me: it reads like a notification for someone who already has an account, and it makes me wonder for a moment whether I'm logged in. The teal speech-bubble panel on the right gets one glance and nothing more. Overall it feels calm, competent and fairly templated: a language-learning signup page for adult beginners.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the landing page for a speaking-first language course, and it should turn first-time visitors into free-week signups.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The headline makes a specific, believable promise and there's one clear action. What gets in the way most is the streak card: it speaks to existing learners and sits where the case for signing up should go. After that, the generic illustration leaves the right half of the hero doing no work.

**What would move it most**
1. **The streak card is aimed at the wrong person and placed in the wrong spot (defect).** The grey box under "Start your free week" tells people to "keep your streak going", but a first-time visitor has no streak. It's the next thing the eye hits after the button, so the moment of deciding gets interrupted by a message that doesn't apply to them. It also looks like an in-app toast, which muddies whether this is a marketing page or the product. The fact inside it is usable, but it's written to someone who's already learning and stops before it says what it means for a new visitor. Either cut it from the hero or rewrite it as a reason to start, said to the reader (e.g. "Five minutes a day is enough: learners who practise five days in a row finish twice as often"), and move it out of the CTA's space. Once the card is gone, the hero has the button as its one clear ending.
2. **The illustration repeats the headline instead of proving it (taste, with a convention cost).** Two empty speech bubbles on a mint panel just say "conversation" again, the same thing the headline already said. They could sit on any chat app's page, so half the hero tells the viewer nothing about Parlo. The panel also sits off the page's grid: it ends at about x=1076, while the nav and the step row run to 1224. That leaves a dead strip on the right and makes the panel look placed by eye rather than designed (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Use that space to show what the headline promises, such as a real exchange from a lesson, ideally the per-word feedback from step 2. Then run the panel to the content's right edge.
3. **The offer leaves out the two facts a visitor needs to decide (defect, copy).** "Practice with a tutor that listens" can be read two ways: a human tutor, or speech-recognition software. Those are very different products at very different prices, and the viewer will hold you to whichever one they assumed. Say which it is. "Start your free week" is a good, specific ask, but it only invites the viewer to try the product on trust. A first step they can judge for themselves would be stronger, such as "Say your first Spanish sentence now — get feedback in 30 seconds." If a card is needed for the free week, say so next to the button.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "…conversation in / ten weeks", leaving "in" at the end of line 1. Breaking before "in" keeps the promise "in ten weeks" together.
- In step 3, "Finish with a short chat about your day" doesn't say who you're chatting with. That's the same tutor question as in finding 3.

**What's working.**
- The headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", is concrete, has a time frame, and promises an outcome rather than a feature. Keep that specificity.
- There's one warm, saturated orange button against a neutral page and a cool teal panel. Nothing else competes with it for colour, so the action is never in doubt.
- The Listen / Speak / Use it row explains the method in three scannable lines on an even three-column grid. Its rhythm is the cleanest part of the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The bottom ~180 px are blank. That's probably the fold of a longer page, but check the live page to make sure the hero isn't followed by a gap.
- Button hover and focus states.
