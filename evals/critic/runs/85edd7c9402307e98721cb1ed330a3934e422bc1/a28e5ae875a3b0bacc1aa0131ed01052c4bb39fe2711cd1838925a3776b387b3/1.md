**First read.** The eye goes first to the row of four teal "Add to cart" buttons, because they are the strongest colour on the page. It then goes up to "Low-light houseplants" and across the four cards. It reads at once as a small plant shop's category page. It feels calm, tidy and well built, but anonymous: the four plant drawings look like one clip-art pot with the leaves moved around, so nothing pulls you toward one plant over another.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a shopper who has a dim room uses this category page to choose one of four plants and add it to the cart.

**Verdict.** The page is easy to read and easy to use, but it does not help anyone choose. The pictures don't show the plants, and the cards give almost nothing to compare beyond pot size and price. A shopper can buy here, but they have to decide somewhere else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show the plants. (Defect.)** All four drawings in the top half of the cards use the same terracotta pot, the same green and the same two-or-three-leaf shape. The snake plant isn't upright and sword-leaved, the ZZ isn't glossy leaflets on stems, and the pothos is drawn as two upright leaves even though its own card says "trails to 6 ft". Most people buying a houseplant pick it by how it looks, so the picture is the main thing they compare. Here they get nothing from it and have to look the plants up elsewhere. The images also show the 8 in pot the same size as the 6 in ones. Direction: use a real photo of each plant, all shot the same way: same background, same angle, and the pot at its real relative size. If these drawings were placeholders, this is the thing to replace first.
2. **The cards don't give the shopper a reason to pick one plant over another. (Defect, about the copy.)** The subtitle, "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window", is good: it is specific, and it names a situation from the shopper's own home. But each card then gives only pot size, height and price, and that doesn't help you choose between four plants that all tolerate low light. Add one line per card about the difference a buyer cares about here. For example: how little light it actually takes, how often to water, or whether it is safe with pets (the cast iron plant is generally listed as pet-safe, while snake plant, ZZ and pothos are not). Write it as what it means for them: "Safe if your cat chews leaves", not "Non-toxic".
3. **The price doesn't say what you get, and it is the number the shopper will hold you to. (Defect.)** "6 in pot" next to $24, under a drawing of a terracotta pot, can be read two ways. One reading is that the plant comes in that decorative pot. The other is that it comes in a 6-inch plastic nursery pot, and "Pots" in the navigation is a separate purchase. Anyone who buys on the first reading and gets the second will feel misled. "6 in pot" is also awkward to read, because "in" looks like the preposition. Direction: say what's included, for example "Ships in a 6-inch nursery pot". If the drawn pot isn't included, don't show it.
4. **Too many type sizes that sit almost on top of each other. (Convention: Bringhurst; Refactoring UI; Dair on weak contrast.)** The facts file lists six sizes: 30, 20, 19, 18, 15 and 14 px (`type_sizes`). The price is 20 px bold (`div.price`) and the plant name is 18 px bold (`h2`), only one step apart in the same weight and colour. The eye can't tell which one leads, and the two bold lines compete within each card. The brand at 19 px adds a third size in that same band. The 15 px nav and subtitle and the 14 px `div.meta` are also only a pixel apart. Direction: cut to about four sizes, for example 30 / 18 / 15 / 13–14. Put the name and price at one size and separate them by weight or colour, not by 2 px.

**Smaller things.**
- Header nav: "Houseplants" isn't marked as the current section, and there's no breadcrumb, so the shopper can't see that they're inside Houseplants → Low-light. Highlight the current section, or add a breadcrumb above the h1.

**What's working.**
- The subtitle copy names a real situation ("more than six feet from a window") that a shopper can check against their own room. Keep that voice, and carry it into the cards.
- The cards are well built. Name, meta, price and button sit at the same y-position across all four cards (h2 at y 437, price at 498, button at 542), and the left edges hold to one grid. A row of cards reads cleanly as something to compare.
- Contrast is comfortable everywhere: 6.11:1 for the grey meta text, 4.81:1 for the white text on the teal buttons (`contrast`). The buttons are 47 px tall, so they are easy to hit.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The facts file shows the buttons and nav items as `span` elements (`span.buy.b1`, `nav > span`), and the measurement found no interactive targets (`targets: []`). If that is the shipped markup, keyboard and screen-reader users cannot reach "Add to cart" or the navigation at all. Check that these are real `<button>` and `<a>` elements with visible focus styles.
