**First read.** My eye goes first to the four "Add to cart" buttons, because they're the most saturated things on the page and each one is a different colour: teal, purple, orange, blue. Then it goes up to "Low-light houseplants" and across the four potted plants. It reads as a calm, tidy category page for a small plant shop. The rainbow of buttons pulls against that calm, so the row looks more like a swatch card than one shop.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a shop category page where someone with a dim room compares four plants and adds one to their cart.

**Verdict.** The basics are sound: a clear heading, a clean four-column grid, and size, price and action easy to scan. Two things hold it back. The four buttons, which do the same job, are four unrelated colours. And the drawings don't show what the plants actually look like, which is what a buyer most needs to see.

**What would move it most**
1. **The four "Add to cart" buttons are four different colours** (bottom row of every card). The buttons do the same job, so the viewer looks for a meaning in the colours: is the purple one a different kind of offer, and is the orange one a sale or a warning? There isn't one. The buttons are also the loudest thing on the page, so the eye lands on them before the heading or the plants. And they bring four hues into a palette that is otherwise neutral grey with plant green and pot terracotta, which breaks the quiet botanical mood the rest of the page sets. One role should get one colour (Apple HIG, "Color"; Material 3 colour roles), and a page should run on one neutral range and one or two hues (Refactoring UI, "Define your shades"). This is a *defect*. Fix: give all four buttons one shared colour. Pick it from the brand, for example a deep version of the leaf green, and keep any other colour for a real difference in role or state.
2. **The drawings don't show the plants** (the image area at the top of each card). All four are the same terracotta pot with two or three generic leaves. The snake plant has none of its stiff upright blades, and the pothos stands upright even though its own caption says it "trails to 6 ft". Someone choosing a plant for a room is choosing mostly on how it looks, and here the only real difference between the cards is the name. This is a *defect* (common sense; no catalogue rule covers it). If these are placeholders, it's fine once real photos or species-accurate drawings replace them. If they're the final art, draw each plant's real shape and habit at the same style and scale, so the cards can be told apart at a glance.

**Smaller things.**
- The top navigation draws "Houseplants" exactly like the other links, so nothing marks the section you're in. Give the current item one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The orange button on the Cast iron plant is the warmest, most forward colour on the page, but it sits on a product that should get no more weight than the others. Fixing finding 1 fixes this too.

**What's working.**
- **The subhead does real work.** "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window" turns "low light" into a distance you can measure in your own room. Keep that kind of concrete copy.
- **The card program is tight.** Name, then size and growth, then price, then action, in the same order and on the same baselines across all four cards, so comparing price and size takes one sweep across the row. The specs line ("6 in pot, grows to 2 ft") answers the two questions a buyer actually has.
- **The type scale is restrained**: a bold heading, a bold name and price, and small grey specs. Each level has an obvious job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus and hover states on the buttons and nav links, and whether the four columns reflow cleanly on a phone.
