**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the landing page of a local pool or swim school. Its main job is to get nervous adult beginners to book the Tuesday class, and it also points lane swimmers and families to their sessions.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job yet. The headline is strong and the copy is good, but the page never says who is offering this, when exactly, for how much, or what to do next, so an adult who is ready to learn has no way to sign up. The weak lower half and the flat, colourless look add to the sense that the page isn't finished.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no ask and no next step.** This is a defect. The eye path is headline, then subline, then two offers, and then nothing: no button, no link, no price, no time, no place. The adult beginner this page is for is often anxious about swimming and needs to be carried to "yes". Here they learn that a class exists and are left to search for how to join it. The subline also stops short of the facts they will act on. "Tuesday evenings" gives no start time, it doesn't say how small "small" is, and there's no price, no course length, and no pool name anywhere on the page. Give the class one primary action right under the subline that names exactly what happens, such as "Book a free first lesson on Tuesday at 7pm" instead of "Sign up". Put the time, group size and price next to it. The best ask lets a nervous swimmer judge the class against what they already know, for example "You'll stand in water no deeper than your chest" or "Six people, one coach". Give the two lower offers a quiet link each.
2. **The three offers fight over rank, and the type scale blurs them.** This is a convention (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"; Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). The page has four sizes: 88, 22, 20 and 16 px (`type_sizes`). The subline is 22 px grey (`section.o1 > p`) and the card titles are 20 px regular dark (`section.o2 > h2`, `section.o3 > h2`). That is two pixels apart, which reads as a slip rather than two levels. So the class's own description and the headings of the other offers look like one tier. The card titles are also regular weight (400), so their only difference from the 16 px body is colour. Featuring one offer is fine, but the other two then look like footnotes, not choices. Drop to three clear levels: the hero headline, one mid size for the subline, and one body size. Make the card titles stand out by weight (600 or bold), not by an in-between size. If the lanes and family sessions matter to the business, add a small label above them such as "Also at the pool" so they read as other options and not leftovers.
3. **The page stops in the top-left corner and leaves the rest bare.** This is a convention (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"). All the content sits in roughly x 96–1016, y 80–436. The lower 45% of an 800 px page (`page_height` 800) and the right fifth are empty, with nothing on the light side to balance it. This isn't open space that frames anything. It's leftover, and it makes the page look like it failed to load. The ask from finding 1 and a picture or two of the pool would fill that area with something that does work. A photo of a coach in the water next to a learner would also make the headline's promise visible.
4. **Nothing says water, warmth or reassurance.** This is taste. The whole page is two greys (#1f2328 and #59636e, per `text_colors`) on white, with no picture and no colour. That works for a document but not for a pool that wants frightened adults to feel safe. One calm hue on the action, plus a real photo, would give it a mood. Without them it could be anyone's page about anything.

**What's working.**
- "Learn to swim" at 88 px bold, against everything else at 22 px or less, gives the page a single, clear place to start. Keep that contrast of scale.
- The copy speaks like a person and lands on the reader. "With a coach in the water beside you" names the exact fear and answers it, and "three speeds to choose from" and "floats and toys for the children" are concrete. Keep that voice when you add the times and the ask.
- The text is set well: 16 px body at 6.11:1 contrast, 53 to 66 characters per line, line height 1.5, and everything on one left edge at 96 px with the two columns at 96 and 576.
