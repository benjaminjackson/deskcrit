**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold headline, drops into the lede, and reads down one calm column. It reads as a long-form feature on a small independent magazine site. It feels quiet, plain and trustworthy. With nothing but grey type on white above the fold, it also feels bare: a story about forty households growing food together arrives with none of the life it describes.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of an online feature article. Its job is to pull a general reader into a long, human story and keep them reading, with Subscribe as the business goal.

**Verdict.** It works as a reading page. The headline leads, the type is legible and the column is clean. But it's undersold: there's no picture, though the byline credits photographs, and the lede looks exactly like the body text. So the opening screen asks the reader to commit to a wall of text with no hook. The missing image is the biggest problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no picture, though the byline promises them.** The byline under the headline says "Photographs by the residents", but the whole first screen is type, and the bottom 220 px are empty. For a feature about a place, the picture is half the pitch. A shot of the tap queue, the bath-tub beds or the car park turned garden would do what the headline can't: show that this is real and worth the time. Without it, headline and story have to carry the whole hook in words, and the page could be anyone's blog post. *Defect* unless the photographs sit further down by design or failed to load; check the live page. Either way, put a lead image between the byline and the lede, or right above the headline, on the 280 px column or wider.
2. **The lede looks like body text.** The lede (`p.lede`) and the body paragraphs are both 18 px Inter regular at the same line spacing. The only difference is colour, #2b3036 against #1f2328, which nobody can see. The reader gets no signal that the first paragraph is the hook, the summary that should be read even if nothing else is. It also makes the first screen a flat run of three paragraphs. That's weak contrast that looks accidental (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). *Convention.* Make the lede differ at a glance: noticeably bigger (around 21–22 px), or a different form or colour, not one step.
3. **Lines run too long.** The body runs 80–83 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `p.lede` and `div.col > p`) in a 720 px column. That's past Bringhurst's 45–75 range, and across a long read the eye starts losing its place on the way back to the next line. *Convention* (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.2). Narrow the text column to about 620–640 px at 18 px, aiming for about 66 characters. If you want a wider column, keep that width for the image.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline break: "The allotment at the end of the bus / line" leaves one word alone on the second line and splits the phrase "bus line". Balance the break so "the bus line" stays together, for example by narrowing the headline box slightly or using `text-wrap: balance`. Taste and common sense: no confirmed source covers breaks in headings.
- Too many sizes close together. The type scale is 40 / 19 / 18 / 14 / 13. The 19 px wordmark sits a pixel above the 18 px body, and the 14 px nav and byline sit a pixel above the 13 px kicker. Settle on about four levels: 40, 18 (plus a larger lede), 14, and keep the kicker at 14 and let its bold green do the work (Bringhurst, 3.1.1; Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- "Subscribe" is the one thing the site wants you to do, but it's set exactly like the section links Features, Food and Places, in plain 14 px grey with a dot between them. Set it apart as the single primary action: a button or at least its own colour or weight.

**What's working.**
- The headline really is the start. At 40 px bold with tight 1.15 line spacing (`h1`), it holds together and clearly leads the page.
- The copy. "Ten years ago… a car park nobody used. Today forty households…" sets up before and after, with specific numbers and a scene (the tap queue as the place to hear the news). "It started with a letter" is a good turn into the story. Keep this voice.
- The setup is calm and disciplined. There's one typeface, six blocks share the 280 px left edge, the 1.6 line spacing and space between paragraphs suit the size, all text is comfortably above contrast minimums (lowest is the green kicker at 5.13, `div.sec`), and the bold green "Feature" label is a quiet nod to the subject.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the photographs exist further down or failed to load. Check the live page.
- Keyboard focus on the nav links, and how the column and headline reflow on a phone.
