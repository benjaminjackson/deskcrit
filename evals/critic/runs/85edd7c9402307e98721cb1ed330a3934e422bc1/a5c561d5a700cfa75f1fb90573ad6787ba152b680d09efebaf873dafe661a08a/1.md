**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline "Savings deposits rose every month", drops to the tall June bar, then moves right to the $4.82M card. It reads straight away as a credit union's half-year report to its members. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, except for the striped bar fill, which is the one noisy thing on the page.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a member-facing report page that tells ordinary members how savings deposits did in the first half of 2026, and leaves them feeling the institution is healthy.

**Verdict.** The page is clean, well-labelled and close to doing its job. The biggest problem is the story it tells. The headline and the steep run of bars suggest strong growth, but the page's own numbers say +6.1% on last year, with June boosted by a one-off bonus payroll mentioned only in the grey footnote. A careful member could come away feeling misled. Second, the hatched bar fill adds visual noise that carries no data.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart oversells the trend, and the explanation sits in the footnote** (defect). The six bars climb from $612k to $1,150k, almost doubling, and the headline says "rose every month". The "Total deposited" card says the half-year was only up 6.1% on Jan–Jun 2025. So most of that climb is probably the normal seasonal pattern, and June's jump is the bonus payroll. With no 2025 shown, a member reads "deposits nearly doubled", which is not what happened. The one sentence that explains June is the smallest, palest text on the page, under the chart. The headline is accurate, but the page leaves the viewer to draw the wrong conclusion, and for a credit union reporting to its own members, trust is what's at stake. No approved source covers this, so it's common sense. Fix: show Jan–Jun 2025 as pale grey bars or a thin line behind 2026, so the real gap is visible. Put a short note beside the June bar itself ("includes mid-year bonus payroll"). Then consider a headline that names the real result, such as "Savings deposits up 6% on last year, rising every month".
2. **Hatched bar fill** (convention). The diagonal blue-and-white stripes in all six bars are decoration that shows no data. At this density the stripes shimmer, and they are the busiest thing on a page that is otherwise quiet. They also make the bar tops look softer than a solid fill would. This is the chartjunk Few warns against ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design") and Tufte describes as moiré. Fix: draw the bars in one flat colour. If you want to mark June as different, give that bar a second, plainer treatment, such as a lighter tint plus the note above.

**Smaller things.**
- Units don't match: the bars say "$1150k" and the axis "$1200k", but the card says "$4.82M". Use $1.15M and $1.2M so members compare like with like.
- "Average deposit, Jun 2026: $214" can be read two ways: average per deposit, or per account. The figures ($214 × 5,374 ≈ $1.15M) suggest per account that made a deposit. Say "Average deposited per account, June 2026".
- "Up $11 on Jun 2025" and "Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025" read awkwardly. "Up $11 from June 2025" is clearer to a stranger.
- The chart title "Deposits into savings accounts, US dollars per month" repeats the subtitle just above it. Cut one, or use the chart title for the 2025 comparison.
- Every bar has a value label, so the y-axis and its gridlines add little. Drop the axis, or keep only the zero baseline.
- The right-hand cards stop about 75 px above the bottom of the chart card, leaving a dead strip on the right. Either match the bottoms or let the column end clearly shorter on purpose.
- The grey axis labels and source line look light at about 12–13 px. Measure them against 4.5:1, since the source line now carries a key fact.

**What's working.**
- The headline states the finding in plain words, and each card gives its figure with the period and a comparison ("Up 402 on Jun 2025"). Members never have to guess what a number means. Keep that discipline.
- The type plan is clear: a serif for the masthead and headline, a sans for the data, few sizes, and the headline clearly on top.
- The chart is honest in its mechanics: bars start at zero, ticks are even, months are in order, values are labelled at the bars, and the source is dated. The fix is to add context, not repair the chart.
