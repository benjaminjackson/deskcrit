**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "New sign-ups by channel", drops to the tall Organic search bar, then steps down the bars from left to right. Only after that does it move to the small note on the right, which is where the page's actual news is. It reads as a calm, tidy internal growth report in a SaaS admin. It feels trustworthy but passive: a clear picture of the data that doesn't argue for anything.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal report page that tells the marketing or growth team what drove Q3 sign-ups and justifies moving the events budget to referrals in Q4.

**Verdict.** This is a clean, honest chart, and the numbers check out: they sum to 5,400, organic is 34%, and podcast ads plus events come to 670. But the chart doesn't show the page's argument. The note's main claim is a change from Q2, and the chart shows only Q3. So the viewer has to take the most important point on trust.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't show the change the note is about.** The note's first claim is "a third of all new accounts, up from a quarter in Q2". The chart has no Q2 data and no shares, only Q3 counts. So the viewer can't see the rise, and can't tell whether organic grew in accounts or only took a bigger slice of a smaller total. They can't see whether events fell, held steady or were always small, and that is the evidence for the Q4 budget move. As it stands, they either work out 1,840 ÷ 5,400 themselves or believe the note. *Defect* (convention: Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design": give each figure a comparison such as the change from the last period). Fix: put Q2 next to Q3 for each channel, for example a pale grey Q2 bar or a tick behind each blue bar, and add the share to each value label ("1,840 · 34%").

2. **Nothing in the chart points to the story, and the title only names the topic.** All six bars are the same blue, so Organic search, which is the subject of the first paragraph, gets no more emphasis than Email. Podcast ads and Events, the subject of the budget decision, get none either. The title "New sign-ups by channel" names the topic, and the finding sits at body size in a sidebar the eye reaches last. A viewer who only glances at the page gets "organic is biggest" and misses both the growth and the decision. *Taste / common sense*. Fix, picking one or both: draw Organic search in the accent colour and the other bars in grey, keeping one strong colour for the point that matters (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"); or make the headline the finding ("Organic search now brings a third of sign-ups") and keep the current title as the subtitle.

**Smaller things.**
- The note's heading "What changed this quarter" (about y 175) lines up with neither the subtitle (about y 146) nor the top of the chart (about y 195), so it looks slightly misplaced. Align it with the top of the chart or with the subtitle. *Common sense.*
- Copy: "fewer than 700 accounts" is vaguer than it needs to be next to a chart with exact numbers. "670 accounts, one in eight" tells the viewer more in the same space.

**What's working.**
- The chart is very spare. It has one colour, faint gridlines, bars sorted by value, a zero baseline, exact numbers on every bar and category labels directly under them. Keep all of this when you add the Q2 comparison: the second series should be quieter than the first, not a second loud colour.
- The note ends with a decision ("The events budget moves to referrals in Q4"), not just an observation. That's the right instinct; it just needs evidence in the chart.
- The source line gives the period and the total (5,400), so the viewer can check the numbers. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Nothing that matters for this piece. Hover and focus states on the top navigation aren't visible, but they're minor here.
