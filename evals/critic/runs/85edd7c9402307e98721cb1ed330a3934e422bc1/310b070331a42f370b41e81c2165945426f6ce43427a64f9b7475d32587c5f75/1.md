**First read.** My eye goes to "Tell us about your project", then across to the white card with three empty fields. It reads straight away as a contact form for a small design studio, and it feels calm, competent and plain. Then the eye runs down the fields and finds nowhere to stop. "Send message" at the bottom of the card looks like a caption, not something to click. After it the eye drifts into the empty grey band and lands on the footer links.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the contact page of a small studio's site, meant to turn a prospective client into a sent enquiry.

**Verdict.** It nearly works. The structure, labels and copy are sound, but the page's one action isn't drawn as a button. A visitor who has just filled in the form has to work out where to press, and some will stop there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The submit control looks like plain text (defect).** "Send message" at the bottom left of the card uses the same face, size, weight and colour as the body text. It has no fill, no border and no underline. Every other link on the page is blue and underlined, so this one reads as a label or a note and not as a control. This is the moment the page exists for, and the person finishing the form loses confidence right there. The page also has no primary action at all, so nothing outweighs the nav and footer links. The hit area also looks like it is only the height of one line of text, close to the 24 px floor in WCAG 2.5.8. Fix: make it a real filled button in the link blue, with comfortable padding. It should be the darkest, heaviest thing inside the card (Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis; NN/g, Moran, on flat elements drawing less attention).
2. **The form doesn't say which fields are required (convention).** "Your name", "Email address" and "Message" carry no asterisk, no "optional" and no note. All three are probably required, but nobody can tell until a submit fails. Fix: either add one line, "All fields required", or put "(optional)" after any field that isn't (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").

**Smaller things.**
- **Nav doesn't show the current page.** "Contact" in the top nav looks the same as "Work", "Services" and "About". Mark it with one cue, such as bold, no underline, or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Input borders look faint.** The grey field outlines on white look close to or under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border colour, and darken it to at least 3:1 if it falls short.
- **Hints sit far from the field they're about.** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline" is in the left column, away from the Message box where people act on it. Repeat it as hint text under the "Message" label (common sense).
- **Leftover space (taste).** The card's right edge stops about 210 px short of the nav's right edge, and there is a tall empty band between the card and the footer. Both read as space left over, not chosen. Either run the card out to the nav's right edge, or centre the two-column group on the page.

**What's working.**
- **The headline and intro copy.** "We read every message and reply within two working days" gives a concrete promise in the visitor's terms. The budget and timeline nudge tells them exactly what a useful enquiry contains. Keep both word for word.
- **Labels.** Each field has a bold label sitting directly above it, with no placeholder-only fields. The label-to-field and field-to-field spacing groups them correctly.
- **Restraint.** One neutral range plus one link blue, one typeface and a clear three-level type scale. Nothing competes with the form, which makes it all the more obvious that the form's action is missing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and the button, what happens on submit (validation, success message), and whether "Send message" is actually a button element.
