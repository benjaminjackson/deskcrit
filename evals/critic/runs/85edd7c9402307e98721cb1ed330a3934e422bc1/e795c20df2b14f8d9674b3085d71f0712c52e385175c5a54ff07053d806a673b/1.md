**First read.** My eye goes straight to the dark blue block in the middle of the grid, then to the title, then across to the three big numbers on the right. Within a couple of seconds I can see it's a support-team report: tickets come in on weekday mornings and early afternoons, and weekends are quiet. It feels calm and competent, and a little plain.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal reporting screen that helps a support lead plan staffing by hour and day, read by people who already know the product.

**Verdict.** This is a solid chart that mostly does its job. The main problem is that the one figure a staffing planner will act on, "Busiest hour, weekdays: 10:00", seems to contradict the map next to it. That makes the reader doubt both.

**What would move it most**
1. **The busiest-hour stat and the map disagree (defect).** On the map, the darkest cells (30+, Monday and Friday) sit in the column that starts at 11:00. Tuesday to Thursday are 25–29 in both the 10:00 and 11:00 columns. So by the legend, 11:00 is at least as busy as 10:00, yet the panel says 10:00. There are two ways to read this:
   - The stat uses unbinned averages where 10:00 narrowly wins. In that case the map's bins hide it and the reader can't check it.
   - The reader takes the hour labels as cell centres instead of cell starts. Then they put the peak in the wrong slot and staff an hour early or late.

   Either way, the number someone will build a rota from looks wrong. The fix:
   - Make the stat and the map agree. Mark the busiest-hour column on the map itself, for example with an outline and its label.
   - Say whether "10:00" means 10:00–11:00.
   - Consider labelling every hour, or at least the peak, so no one has to count cells from "09:00".
2. **The title names the topic, not the finding (taste; the takeaway-title entry has no approved source).** "When new tickets arrive" makes the reader work out the pattern themselves. The pattern is the whole point: weekdays 09:00–16:00, peaking late morning, with weekends a third as busy. A title like "Tickets peak at 10–11:00 on weekdays; weekends stay light" hands the planner the answer, and the map then backs it up. That's the usual direction: keep the topic in the subtitle and use the title for the finding.

**Smaller things.**
- The title (~28 px) and the stat figures (~26 px bold) look almost the same size, so they read as one level. Make the stat figures clearly smaller or clearly larger than the title (Bringhurst: sizes from a scale).
- The legend swatches start at an x that lines up with nothing (~278 px). Start them under the grid's first column, or right after the label, so the legend sits on the grid.
- The subtitle's period, "1 to 28 September", doesn't match the stat "New tickets, September: 3,412". Say whether the 3,412 covers the whole month or the same 28 days.

**What's working.**
- **The single-hue lightness ramp with stepped bins.** It's ordered, safe for colour-blind readers, and the light 0–4 cells fade into the background, so the busy block stands out on its own. Keep it.
- **The side stats each come with a comparison** ("Up 4% on August", "Down 6 min"), so every number has a reference point.
- **The source line says exactly what "hour" means** (opened, not answered; team local time). That's the kind of precision the busiest-hour stat needs too.
