**First read.** My eye goes to "Create your account", drops to the blue "Create account" button, and then moves up through six empty fields. Within a second it reads as a standard signup card for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and anonymous: it could be the signup page of nearly any SaaS product.

**What it's for.** You gave me no context, so I'm assuming this is the signup screen of a notes product, aimed at a new visitor who has already decided to try it. Its job is to get them through the form with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The craft is sound: clear hierarchy, labels above fields, required fields marked, one primary button. But the form asks for more than it needs and explains less than it should. Two of the six fields are optional and one of them is a marketing question, which makes the form longer and makes the new user do work without giving them anything back. That extra length is the main obstacle.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two optional fields sit in the main path.** "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" are the bottom two fields above the button. They have the same weight as the required fields and the same spacing. A visitor scanning the page sees six fields, not four, and has to read the labels to notice that two have no asterisk. The attribution question helps the company, not the user. *Defect* (Nielsen 8, Aesthetic and minimalist design; Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). **Fix:** cut "How did you hear about us?" from signup and ask it after the account exists. Either drop "Workspace name" or give it a default (e.g. "Ben's notes", built from the first name) that the user can change later. Whatever optional fields remain should say "(optional)" after the label, so nobody has to work it out from a missing asterisk (Budiu, "Marking Required Fields in Forms", NN/g).

2. **The fields don't say what they want.** The password field shows no rules, so the user finds out about length or character requirements only when an error appears after submitting. "Workspace name" doesn't say what a workspace is in a notes app, whether other people will see the name, or whether it can be changed. The select box is empty, with no "Choose one", so it looks broken as much as unanswered. *Defect* (Nielsen 10, Help and documentation; Nielsen 5, Error prevention). **Fix:** add one line of hint text under the password field with the actual rule. Add a short hint under "Workspace name" if you keep it. Give the select a visible prompt as its first option.

3. **The subline says nothing about this product.** "Start a free workspace for your notes" could sit on any notes app's page. This is the last moment to remind a hesitant visitor why they are signing up, and it gives them nothing specific. *Taste.* **Fix:** replace it with one concrete thing Quillnest does for the user, phrased to them. If "free" has limits (a trial, a tier), say which, because people will hold you to "free".

**Smaller things.**
- The "Sign in" link at the bottom is the same grey, weight and style as "Already have an account?", so it doesn't look clickable. Returning users who land here by mistake have to guess. Underline it or set it in the brand blue (WCAG technique G183; Norman, *Design of Everyday Things*, signifiers).
- The input borders are a pale grey-blue on white and look close to or under the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 minimum. Measure one and darken the border if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean: one bold headline, bold labels sitting tight above their fields, and a single filled button in the brand blue. Nothing competes with that button. Keep the button as the only saturated element on the page.
- Required fields are marked the conventional way: a red asterisk on each label plus the "* Required field" key at the top. Keep this when you add "(optional)" markers.
- First and last name are the only fields side by side, which is the right exception to a single-column form.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus ring visibility and contrast on the fields and the button.
- When validation runs and how errors appear, especially for the password.
