**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks," then gets pulled three ways: to the orange button, to the purple block just under it, and to the big teal panel on the right. The purple block is the problem. It sits right under the button, at nearly the same weight, and it looks like a second button or a promo card. It reads as a clean, friendly signup page for a language app. It is competent, but it could be anyone's app.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing page for adult beginners choosing a language app, and its job is to get them to start the free week.

**Verdict.** The page mostly works: the promise is clear, the button is clear and the three steps explain the product. The biggest problem is the purple "Keep your streak going" block. It competes with the only action on the page and talks to returning users on a page meant for new ones. The second problem is the big teal picture, which takes up half the space and says nothing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple streak card fights the button and talks to the wrong person.** *Defect.* It sits 28 px below "Start your free week" in another saturated colour with the same corner radius, so it reads as a second, competing action. The eye flicks between the two and the button loses its pull. If it isn't clickable, it looks clickable anyway (Norman, *signifiers*). The copy is also wrong for this page. A first-time visitor has no streak to keep, and "finish the course twice as often" doesn't say compared to whom. **Fix:** cut it from the hero. If the streak finding is your proof that the method works, make it one quiet line of plain text under the button, aimed at someone who hasn't started yet: "People who practise five days a week are twice as likely to finish." Then the orange button is the only loud thing in the left column (Refactoring UI: one primary action per view).
2. **The teal panel uses half the hero to repeat the headline.** *Defect / taste.* Two generic chat bubbles on a flat teal block only restate "conversation". They don't show the tutor that listens, feedback on each word, or any of the four languages, which are the things that make Parlo different. It is also the biggest block of strong colour on the page, so it takes attention from the button. **Fix:** put something only this product has in that space, such as a real lesson screen: a Spanish phrase, the learner's attempt, and the per-word feedback from step 2. Then the picture shows what the headline claims. If it stays an illustration, mute it so it supports the button instead of competing with it.
3. **The promise and the ask are vaguer than they need to be.** *Defect (copy).* "Ten weeks" leans on "short daily lessons", but the page never says how short. "10 minutes a day" would let readers judge whether the claim fits their life. "Start your free week" is good and specific, but it commits the reader, and nothing nearby says what happens on day 8. **Fix:** put the minutes in the subhead and a line under the button: "No card needed" or "Then £X/month, cancel anytime", whichever is true.

**Smaller things.**
- The teal panel's right edge (about x=1076) stops short of the 1224 px edge that the nav and the third step column line up on. Run it to that edge or make the gap obviously deliberate.
- Three saturated hues (orange, purple, teal) are too many for one hero. Cutting the purple fixes most of this. Keep orange for the action only.
- About 180 px of empty band sits under the steps, so the page looks unfinished at this viewport. Bring the next section's top edge into view to signal there's more below.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a concrete, checkable promise in plain words and is clearly the biggest thing on the page. Keep that bluntness.
- The three steps (Listen / Speak / Use it) are short, numbered and evenly spaced in three columns that match the page edges, and each line says what *you* do. It's the best explanation of the product on the page.
- The type scale is clean: about four sizes with clear steps between them, and weight does the work for the step headings.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the purple card is clickable, and the button's hover and focus states.
