**First read.** The eye goes to "Overview", across the three big figures (184,220 visits, 3,912 sign-ups, 2.12%), and then drops into the chart, which takes up most of the screen. That's where it stalls: three coloured lines climb gently, but nothing says what they are, how big they are, or which weeks they cover. It reads as a clean, generic SaaS analytics overview. It's calm and tidy, and blank exactly where the information should be.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a dashboard for a marketing or growth lead to check at a glance how traffic and sign-ups are trending and which channels drive the traffic.

**Verdict.** The top row works, and the chart, which is the biggest thing on the page, doesn't. It answers none of the questions its title raises. You can see that three things are going up, but you can't tell which channel is which, how big they are, or when anything happened.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no series labels, no axis values and no dates** (defect). "Visits by channel" promises a breakdown by channel, but the blue, orange and green lines carry no names, there's no legend, the y-axis has no numbers and the x-axis has no weeks. So the one question the chart exists for, "which channel is driving the 6.1%?", can't be answered. Readers can't tell a big channel from a small one, or whether the gaps between the lines are 1,000 visits or 10,000. They also can't check these lines against the 184,220 above. Tufte (*The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2) and Few (*Show Me the Numbers*) both require labelled axes with units and a readable scale. The ~50 px empty strip left of the plot and the dead band under the baseline look like room kept for labels that aren't there. If the labels exist and just didn't render in this capture, the fix is to make sure they do. Either way, the shipped screen needs:
   - the channel name at the right end of each line (Tufte, Wilke: label at the data, not in a legend),
   - a few y-axis values with the unit ("visits / week"),
   - week dates along the bottom.
2. **The series are told apart by colour alone** (defect, WCAG 1.4.1). Even once named in a legend, three hues are the only key, and orange next to green is the pair people with red-green colour blindness are most likely to confuse. Putting the label at the end of each line fixes this along with finding 1. That's another reason to choose direct labels over a legend.
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding** (taste, common sense). "Visits by channel" makes the viewer work out the story. A dashboard someone reads in ten seconds should say it for them, for example "Organic drove most of the 6.1% rise". That sentence would also connect the chart to the KPI above it, which right now sits there with no explanation. Once the lines are labelled, add a title that names what changed.

**Smaller things.**
- The deltas use green to mean "up, good", and the chart also uses green for one channel, so the same colour means two things on one screen. Keep green for status and pick a different hue for that channel.
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is plain grey text. If it's the date-range picker, it needs to look clickable (a chevron or an outlined control). If it's only a label, it's fine as is.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards.** Each has a quiet label, one bold figure, and a delta against a named comparison period ("vs previous 12 weeks"), so every number comes with its own context. The conversion delta in "pts" rather than "%" is a careful, correct touch. Keep both.
- **Restraint.** Light gridlines, no chart borders or effects, one neutral background and white cards on a consistent grid. Once the labels are back, almost all the ink in the chart will be data.
- **A clear reading order.** The page title, then the summary numbers, then the detail, which is the right sequence for a quick check.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't check hover tooltips on the chart, which might show values but don't replace visible labels. I also couldn't tell whether "Last 12 weeks" opens anything.
