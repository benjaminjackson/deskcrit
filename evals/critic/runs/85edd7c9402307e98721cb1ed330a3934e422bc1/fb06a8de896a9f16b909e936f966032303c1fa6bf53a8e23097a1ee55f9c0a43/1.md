**First read.** The eye goes first to the three large KPI figures ($4.82M, 112%, 1,246), then drops to the big blue bar block, which fills most of the page. It reads as a calm, competent SaaS revenue dashboard for a finance lead or founder. It feels orderly and trustworthy at first. The heavy black chart frame is the one thing that looks a bit dated.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an internal revenue overview where a founder or finance lead checks how 2025 went and expects the figures to agree with each other.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: it's well organised, the headline numbers come with comparisons, and the chart is honest. But two figures on the page contradict each other, and on a finance screen that undoes the trust the clean layout builds. The chart also uses its large area on frame and gridlines rather than on a point.

**What would move it most**

1. **The numbers don't add up across the page (defect).** The table on the right is titled "Revenue by plan, 2025" and totals **$4.82M**. That is exactly the ARR figure in the first KPI card ("Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025"). The chart beside it shows 2025 monthly recognised revenue, and its twelve bars add up to about **$4.49M** (roughly 312 + 328 + 341 + … + 447 thousand). A finance reader who checks the total, and this audience will, sees two "2025 revenue" figures about $330k apart and stops trusting the screen. The table can be read two ways: as revenue earned in 2025 (wrong by the chart's own numbers) or as ARR split by plan (which fits $4.82M but isn't what it says). Fix: title it with what it actually holds, such as "ARR by plan, Dec 2025". If it really should be 2025 revenue, change its numbers so its total matches the chart's sum. Either way, the page should never show one total under two different names.

2. **The chart's frame and gridlines carry as much ink as the data (convention).** The plot area has a full black box around it, and the gridlines at 100, 200, 300 and 400 look nearly as dark as the frame. A frame and gridlines this dark make the largest area on the page read as a grid with bars in it, not as a growth trend. Make the gridlines thin and pale gray, or remove them, and remove the frame, so the bars are the darkest marks in the chart (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 23.2; Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 5). Then give the chart a point. "Monthly revenue, 2025" names a topic and leaves the reader to work out what happened. A title such as "Revenue grew every quarter, from $312k in January to $447k in December" tells them. Taking out the gridlines saves ink, and a title that states the finding gives the space a message. The title point is my judgement as a designer, not a rule from the approved sources.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale (convention, Bringhurst 3.1.1):** there are eight text sizes: 28, 26, 20, 16, 15, 14, 13 and 12 px (`type_sizes`). The page title is 26 px (`h1`) and the KPI values are 28 px (`div.value`), so the page's name is set slightly smaller than its numbers, which looks like an accident. The run from 12 to 16 has no clear steps. Keep about four sizes, for example 28 / 16 / 14 / 12. Make the title clearly larger than the KPIs or clearly different in weight, and use colour for the finer distinctions.
- **Section heading baselines:** "Revenue by plan, 2025" sits 4 px lower than "Monthly revenue, 2025" (y 235 vs 231). Set them on one baseline.
- **Grid:** the split between chart and table (chart box ends at 990, table starts at 1012) doesn't line up with either gutter between the KPI cards (574/593 and 907/925). Start the table at the third card's left edge (x 925) so the columns run down the page.
- **"Jan to Dec 2025" pill:** this grey chip could be a date picker or a plain label. If it's a control, add a chevron or calendar icon. If it's a label, drop the button-like fill.
- **Navigation and title disagree:** the selected nav item is "Overview" but the page is titled "Revenue overview", while a separate "Revenue" item sits right below. That leaves the user unsure what the Revenue page adds. Make the selected nav label and the page title say the same thing.

**What's working.**
- **Every KPI has a period and a comparison:** the label ("Net revenue retention, 2025"), the value, then a green delta that also carries a +/- sign and names its baseline ("+3 pts vs 2024"). That is the right pattern for headline numbers; keep it.
- **The chart is honest and labelled:** the bars start at zero, the units are stated ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue"), and the source line says "closed invoices only". That caveat is what lets a careful reader trust the numbers, so keep it once the totals reconcile.
- **Restraint:** one font family (Inter), one blue for data, green only for change, every text colour at 4.7:1 contrast or better, and the whole dashboard fits on one 1280×800 screen.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the side navigation, and whether the date pill opens anything.
