**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline "Plants for shade and small spaces" on a dark leaf pattern, then drops to the grey intro sentence and the three outlined cards, and then stops on blank white. It reads as a small local garden centre's home page. It feels calm, competent and a bit anonymous. It never shows a plant, and it gives you nothing to do next.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page of a local garden centre, and it should get nearby gardeners to browse or come in for shade plants, with hours, workshops and delivery easy to find.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and clear, but it doesn't do the selling part of its job. It makes a specific offer ("ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, ready to pot this weekend") and then gives the visitor no way to see or act on it. The hero spends 400 px on generic leaf wallpaper instead of the plants it is selling.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no next step.** The headline and intro make a concrete, timely offer, then the page ends in three information cards and empty white. There's no button to see the shade plants, no "get directions", and no way to book a workshop. A visitor who wants what the headline promises has to guess that "Plants" in the nav is the way in (Krug: don't make me think; Nielsen: put the next action in front of the user). *Defect.* Fix: add one primary action under the intro that names the exact thing, such as "See this weekend's shade plants". Make the workshops card say how to book.
2. **The hero image doesn't show the offer.** The pattern is generic illustrated foliage with coloured dots. It isn't ferns or hostas, and it shows neither shade nor a small space. It could sit on any garden, tea or wellness site, so it adds mood but no proof. The headline has to make the whole case alone. *Taste, argued:* the hero is the biggest area on the page, and it's the one place you can make someone want the plant. Fix: use a real photo of the product, such as a shaded balcony or a pot of ferns from your own nursery, kept dark enough on the left for the white headline to stay readable.
3. **The headline and its intro are cut apart, and sit on different edges.** The headline lives inside the green band with its left edge at about 72 px. The sentence that finishes its thought ("Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs…") sits outside the band at 56 px, in line with the logo and cards. The band edge cuts the pair apart, and the 16 px offset reads as a slip, not a choice. *Convention* (one grid; space and boxes should show groups: Müller-Brockmann). Fix: put the intro inside the hero under the headline, or put both on the 56 px line. Ideally do both.

**Smaller things**
- Headline break: "small / spaces" leaves one word alone on line 2 and splits the phrase. Widen the text box or rebreak as "Plants for shade / and small spaces".
- Opening hours card: "until 8 / pm" splits the number from its unit. Use a non-breaking space ("8 pm").
- Saturday workshops card: "ten places in each class" gives no time, price or way to book. The viewer has to remember to go and find out. Add the time and a "Book a place" link.
- The hero's right half is all pattern and the headline covers only the left. This is acceptable, but the red and orange dots add noise; a quieter image would help item 2 anyway.

**What's working**
- The intro sentence is specific and timely ("grown at our nursery and ready to pot this weekend"). It's the best line on the page, so build the call to action from it.
- The type scale is clean: about four sizes (headline, intro, card title, card body) with clear jumps, and card titles set apart by weight, not by an extra size. Keep it.
- The three cards answer the practical questions in plain language, each on one grid with the nav's right edge. The delivery line ("Free within ten miles on orders over $60") is exact and states its conditions.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cards or the hero are clickable, and the hover and focus states of the nav. Check in a browser whether the cards link anywhere. If they don't, the missing call to action in item 1 is the only path.
