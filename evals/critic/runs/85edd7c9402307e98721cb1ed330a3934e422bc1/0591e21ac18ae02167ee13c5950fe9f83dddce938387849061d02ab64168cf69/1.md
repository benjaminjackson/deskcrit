The descenders of "Quayside ferry" cut into "crossings by daylight,", and the "g", "y" and "h" of that line cut into "spring through early". It looks like a page that failed to render, not like a ferry timetable. After that comes a small grey "Spring timetable" label and a calm, readable paragraph. The page feels plain and municipal, and broken.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a public web page for a river ferry, telling riders when the ferry runs this spring.

**Verdict.** It doesn't work yet. The headline, which is the first and biggest thing anyone sees, is unreadable because its lines overlap. And a page called a timetable gives no times, so a rider still can't plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's lines overlap. (Defect.)** In the four-line headline (about rows 150–370), the line spacing is well below the type size, so letters from one line run into the next. "g" and "y" in "crossings by daylight," hit "spring through early", and the "y" in "ferry" drops into "crossings". Readers have to pick each line out of the tangle, and the page looks buggy before it says anything. Tight spacing in a display heading is fine, but only while ascenders and descenders stay clear of each other (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, p. 37; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, p. 28). Fix: open the headline's line spacing until no letter touches a letter on the next line. It can still be tighter than the body text. Shortening the headline would also help (see 3).
2. **The "timetable" has no times. (Defect, in the copy.)** The label says "Spring timetable", yet the only schedule facts are "every forty minutes" and "about twelve minutes". There's no first crossing, no last crossing, no dates, and "by daylight" leaves the rider to guess sunrise and sunset. Times and dates are what a rider acts on and holds the operator to, and right now they have to rebuild them in their head. They could miss the last boat or turn up before the first. The label also doesn't describe what's under it (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.6). Fix: put the real hours and date range on this screen, e.g. "First boat 6:40 from North Landing, last 19:20 from Market Quay, 21 March – 30 June". If the full table sits further down the page, still show the first and last times here and make it clear the table follows.
3. **The headline is too long for its job. (Taste.)** "Quayside ferry crossings by daylight, spring through early summer" spends four huge lines on what the label already says ("spring") and on a vague time limit ("by daylight"). At this size, every extra line pushes the useful content down and makes the line spacing harder to fix. I'd cut it to the one thing a rider needs, such as "Quayside ferry: spring crossings", and move the dates and hours into the content where they can be exact.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks "ferry / crossings" and "early / summer" across lines, which splits the phrases. Re-wrap it at phrase boundaries once the spacing and length are fixed.
- "weather." sits alone on the paragraph's last line. Re-wrap or edit so the last line holds more than one word.
- "Each crossing takes about twelve minutes in calm weather" raises a question it doesn't answer: what happens when it's rough? Say whether the boats slow down or stop, and where riders can check.

**What's working.**
- Every block shares one left edge at about x=120, which gives the page a firm spine. Keep it.
- Three clear type levels in one family: a small grey bold label, a very large bold headline, and regular body text. No sizes collide. Keep this scale when you fix the headline spacing.
- The body paragraph is comfortable to read: a good size, around 65–70 characters per line, generous line spacing, and dark text on white.
