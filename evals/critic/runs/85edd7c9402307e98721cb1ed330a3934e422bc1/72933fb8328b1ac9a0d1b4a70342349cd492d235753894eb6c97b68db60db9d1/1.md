**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the week with your whole team", jumps to the blue "Create account" button, then climbs back up through five blank fields. It reads straight away as a sign-up page for a team planner. It feels calm and competent, but it could belong to any of a hundred SaaS products.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the desktop sign-up page for Tallyboard, aimed at a team lead deciding whether to start a free workspace.

**Verdict.** The page is tidy and the hierarchy is clear, but the form is built on placeholder-only fields, so people will fill it in with less confidence and make more errors. The pitch next to it gives the viewer no reason to choose this tool and no clear idea of what "free" covers.

**What would move it most**
1. **The fields have no labels, only placeholders (defect; convention: Nielsen Norman Group on placeholder text, WCAG 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions).** All five fields in the card name themselves only through grey placeholder text. As soon as someone starts typing, the name and the hint vanish. The worst one is the fourth field: it never says "Password". You have to work that out from "At least 10 characters", and once you type, the 10-character rule disappears too, at exactly the moment you need it. Someone who comes back to check their entries, or whose browser autofilled them, sees only values with no names. Fix: put a short visible label above each field ("First name", "Work email", "Password", "Company name"). Move the password rule into helper text under the field so it stays visible while typing.
2. **The pricing line can be read two ways (defect, copy).** "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." One reading: free covers five people, and inviting more means paying. The other: you start with five and can add everyone later for free. This is the line a team lead will hold you to. If they believe the second and get charged at person six, that is a broken promise and a support ticket. Fix: say what happens at person six in plain words, for example "Free for teams of up to five. Bigger teams pay $X per person per month." Or, if it really is free, say so outright.
3. **The left half makes no case (defect, copy and composition).** The left column is a wordmark, a headline that could sit on any planning tool's page, and a sentence about seat limits. Nothing shows the board, the week, or what makes this different from the shared calendar or spreadsheet the team already uses. So the viewer is asked for five fields of personal data on trust. Nearly half the screen, the whole lower left, is empty. That space is free to show what the viewer gets: a screenshot of a real week's board, or one line that compares it to what they use today ("Your team's week on one board, not across five calendars"). This is taste on the exact treatment, but the missing reason to act is the real cost.

**Smaller things.**
- **The "Sign in" link looks like plain text.** In "Already have an account? Sign in" (below the button), the link has the same grey as the sentence (#59636e, `div.small`) with no underline or weight change, so returning users have to guess it is clickable. Give it the brand blue, an underline, or both.
- **The input borders look pale.** The grey-blue edges on white look close to or below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 requires for control edges. Measure the border colour, and darken it if it falls short.
- **Nothing says which fields are required.** Is company name required for someone trying it alone? Mark the optional fields, or drop company name and ask for it after sign-up.
- **The wordmark and the card heading nearly align.** "Tallyboard" (box top 136, `div.logo`) and "Create your account" (box top 132, `h2`) sit about 3 px apart across the two columns, which reads as a near-miss. Either align them on one baseline or move one clearly away.
- **Two pairs of type sizes are close.** The page uses six sizes (38, 22, 20, 17, 16, 13 px, from `type_sizes`). 22 vs 20 (card heading vs wordmark) and 17 vs 16 (body vs button) are too close to read as separate levels. Colour saves the wordmark, but you could merge each pair into one size.
- **There's no SSO option.** Work-tool sign-ups usually offer "Continue with Google/Microsoft", which removes most of the form for the most likely viewer. It's worth considering if the product supports it.

**What's working.**
- **One clear primary action.** The blue "Create account" button is the only saturated block in the card, and it repeats the wordmark's blue. The eye always knows where the form ends, so keep it the only strong colour.
- **The text is comfortable to read.** The body is 17 px at 53 characters per line with 1.6 line height (`p`), and the headline is 38 px bold at 14.6:1 contrast. Nothing on the page strains the eye.
- **The fields are a good size and well spaced.** Every target is 44–46 px tall with even 18 px gaps. First and last name are the only side-by-side pair, which is the right place to break single-column order.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields, inline error messages (especially for the password rule), and whether the password field has a show/hide toggle.
