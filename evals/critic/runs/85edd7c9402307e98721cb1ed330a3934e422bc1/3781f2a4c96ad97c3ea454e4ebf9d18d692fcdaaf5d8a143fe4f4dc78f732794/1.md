**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a studio's contact page and its job is to get a prospective client to send a useful first enquiry.

**Verdict.** It works: a stranger knows within two seconds what to do and does it without thinking. The biggest thing in the way is that the page asks for a budget and a timeline but gives them nowhere to go. The Message box is a small, empty, unprompted rectangle, so the enquiries you get will be thinner than the ones the copy asks for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The brief has nowhere to go** (defect). The left column asks people to "Include a budget range and a rough timeline". That request sits about 450 px from the only place to type it, and the Message field gives no hint of it. The box is also only about five lines tall, for a field that has to carry the whole project description. Someone filling in the form looks at the fields, not back at the paragraph, so most will write "Hi, we need a website" and you'll spend your first reply asking for what the page already asked for. Nielsen 10 (help and documentation) puts short help at the step it concerns. The fix: put the prompt under the Message label as helper text, such as "What you need, your budget range, and when you need it by", and make the box noticeably taller so a real brief looks expected. If budget and timeline matter enough to triage on, give them their own short fields or a budget select.
2. **The fields don't say what's required, and their edges look faint** (convention and defect). None of the three labels has an asterisk or "optional". In a three-field form a visitor will guess that all three are required, but they shouldn't have to, and the omission matters more if you add budget and timeline fields. NN/g (Budiu, "Marking Required Fields in Forms") says to put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or to put "optional" after the optional ones. Separately, the light grey field borders look below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of input edges against white. Measure the border colour. If it fails, darken it until the fields read as fields for people with low vision.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav doesn't mark "Contact" as the current page; all four links are styled the same. Give it one cue, such as a bolder weight or no underline, so people can see where they are (Nielsen 1, via NN/g's menu checklist).
- The card's right edge (about x = 1068) lines up with nothing. The nav ends at about x = 1224, so a dead strip about 155 px wide sits right of the form and the page looks shifted left. Either run the card to the content's right edge or narrow the outer container so the layout's edges match (Müller-Brockmann, one grid).
- The headline breaks as "Tell us about your / project", which leaves "project" alone on the second line. Break it at "Tell us about / your project" or widen the column (common sense; no checked source covers breaks in headings).
- The headline is a phrase any agency could use (taste). The subcopy, "We read every message and reply within two working days", is the specific promise that sets you apart. Consider letting the headline carry something only you would say, or move that promise up.

**What's working.**
- The reply promise ("within two working days") is concrete and lowers the cost of writing. Keep it next to the form.
- Hierarchy and form structure are clean: one dominant headline, labels above every field, a single column, and one clearly primary filled button. Nothing competes with the task.
- The palette is restrained, with neutrals and one blue kept for links and the action, so the button is plainly the thing to press.
