**First read.** The eye goes to the bold "Notes for ferry passengers", drops through two even paragraphs, and stops at a smaller last line that looks like a footnote. It reads as a plain public notice for people using a river ferry. It feels calm, tidy and a bit official. The only line that could change your plans, "Timetables change in winter", looks like the least important thing on the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short information page that tells ferry passengers when the ferry runs, how bicycles work, and what to check before they travel.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and easy to read, but it buries its one warning. It also leaves out the times passengers need to plan a crossing, so it reads well and still doesn't get them onto the boat at the right time.

**What would move it most**
1. **The winter warning looks like the least important line.** "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard…" is set at 16 px (`font_size`, `p.note.faintable`). The paragraphs above it are 19 px, in the same weight and colour (#1f2328 on all four blocks). The note is smaller and sits last, so a reader skims past it as fine print. That is the opposite of its job: it's the only line on the page that says the rest may be wrong. A 19 px to 16 px step is also too small to read as a deliberate level, so it looks like an accident (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8: a contrast must be obvious at a glance in size, weight, form or colour). Make the warning stand out with weight, a tint or rule, or by moving it up to sit with the timetable paragraph, rather than making it smaller. *Defect.*
2. **The timetable gives no times.** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" leaves out the first and last sailings, so nobody can plan the last trip home from this page. "Calm weather" also leaves open how long a crossing takes when it isn't calm. A passenger acts on these facts, so they should be exact: give the first and last departure from each landing and say what happens to crossings in rough weather. *Defect (copy).*
3. **The warning sends people somewhere they can only check once they've already arrived.** "Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel" only works for someone already standing at the landing, which is too late to plan. In winter, the people this page is for walk to the landing without knowing whether a boat is coming. Name a way to check before leaving home (a page, a number, the winter months and their times), or print the winter timetable here. *Defect (copy).*

**What's working.**
- The reading setting is right and should stay through any revision. The paragraphs run 70.5 and 71.5 characters per line (`characters_per_line`) at 19 px with 1.5 line height, near-black on white at 15.8:1 (`contrast`). It's comfortable to read, within Bringhurst's 45 to 75 characters.
- Everything starts from one left edge at x=120 (`left_edges`). The heading differs from the text in weight (700 against 400) as well as size, so the top of the page is clear at once.
- The bicycle paragraph is good copy. It says who does what and when: no extra charge, wheel bikes on after foot passengers, lock them to the rail. Bring the timetable paragraph up to that standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The text blocks carry a `faintable` class, so the text may fade on scroll or load. In this capture all four blocks are at full contrast. Check in a live browser that the warning is never caught faded.
