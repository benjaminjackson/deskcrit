# Crit: Ledgerly "Revenue overview" dashboard

**First read.** The eye lands on "Revenue overview", drops to the $4.82M card and then fills up with the big blue bar chart. It reads as a SaaS revenue dashboard for a founder or finance lead. It feels calm, orderly and competent, but it could be any SaaS product's dashboard.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: an annual revenue summary whose figures a finance-minded reader can trust and reconcile at a glance.

**Verdict.** The layout mostly works, but the numbers don't agree with each other, and on a finance screen that is fatal. A reader who cross-checks will stop trusting every figure on the page. Fix that first. The heavy chart frame and the loose lower grid come a distant second.

**What would move it most**

1. **The figures don't reconcile (defect).**
   - "Revenue by plan, 2025" (right column) totals $4.82M. That is exactly the "Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025" figure in the first card.
   - The monthly recognised-revenue bars add up to roughly $4.5M, by eye: about 310 + 327 + 340 + … + 447.
   - December's bar (about $447K) times 12 gives about $5.4M, which is not $4.82M either.

   So either the table is really ARR by plan, mislabelled as 2025 revenue, or three different revenue measures sit side by side with nothing to tell them apart. Finance readers add things up. The first person who does will ask which number is wrong, and from then on every tile on the page is suspect.

   Fix: decide which measure each block shows and label it that way. Either retitle the table "ARR by plan, Dec 2025", or make it 2025 recognised revenue whose total matches the chart. If ARR and recognised revenue really do differ, say why in one line, e.g. "Excludes one-off and usage fees".

2. **The chart's frame and gridlines shout over the data (convention, Tufte's data-ink).** The plot sits in a full black box with near-black gridlines at every 100. That is as much heavy ink as the bars themselves, and the box is the darkest thing on a page that is otherwise soft greys.

   It makes the chart look like a spreadsheet print-out, and it hides the actual story: steady growth with small dips in April and July. Fix:
   - drop the box;
   - make the gridlines light grey hairlines, or just keep the baseline;
   - consider a value label on December so the year-end figure can be read without estimating.

3. **The lower band ignores the grid the cards set up (convention, Müller-Brockmann).**
   - The KPI row sets a clear three-column grid.
   - Below it, the chart ends around x=960 and the plan table starts around x=1012, and neither lines up with any card edge.
   - The two section heads, "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025", sit about 4 px apart vertically. That's a near-miss that looks like a mistake, not a choice.

   Fix: give the chart two card columns and the table the third, starting at the third card's left edge, and set both heads on one baseline.

**Smaller things.**
- **Chart title:** "Monthly revenue, 2025" only names the data. For a static or exported view, something like "Revenue grew 43% from Jan to Dec" would say what the reader should see.
- **Plan table:** it has no prior-year comparison or share of total, unlike the KPI cards above it. Add a "vs 2024" column or each plan's percentage.
- **Date pill:** the grey "Jan to Dec 2025" pill (top right) doesn't show whether it is a label or a date picker. If it's a control, give it a chevron or border; if not, make it plain text.
- **Type sizes:** the page title and the three KPI figures look only a few pixels apart in size. Make the title clearly smaller or clearly larger so they don't compete.

**What's working.**
- **KPI cards:** each one has a label with its period, a big figure, and a signed delta against last year. That's the right pattern, and the labels are specific, e.g. "Dec 2025" and "+3 pts" rather than a bare "%".
- **Source line and units:** "Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue" and "closed invoices only" are exactly what a finance reader needs. Keep them; they are also the start of fixing finding 1.
- **Restraint:** one blue, green only for gains, and neutral greys everywhere else.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date pill opens a picker, and whether the nav items have hover and focus states.
