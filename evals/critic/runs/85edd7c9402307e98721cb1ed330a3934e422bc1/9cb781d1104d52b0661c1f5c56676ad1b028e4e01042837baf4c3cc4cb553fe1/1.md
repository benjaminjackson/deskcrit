**First read.** The eye goes to the navy "Create account" button, up to the heading, then down a stack of six identical empty fields. It's obviously a sign-up form for a notes app. It's tidy and calm, but it could belong to any SaaS product.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the sign-up page for Quillnest, a notes app. A new visitor who has already decided to try it should get into the product with as little effort as possible.

**Verdict.** It's well made but it asks too much. The page is neat and the hierarchy is clear, but six fields, including a marketing survey and a "workspace name" nobody has been told about, stand between the visitor and their first note. The one line that should sell the product says nothing anyone else couldn't say.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form asks for things the visitor has no reason to give yet (defect).** "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" take up a third of the card. The attribution question is for Quillnest, not the user. "Workspace name" is a product term the visitor meets here for the first time, with no hint of what it means, whether others will see it, or whether it can be changed. Every extra field is another moment to give up, and it's worst on a page whose only job is to get them in (Nielsen, aesthetic and minimalist design; Krug). Fix: cut the form to email and password, plus name if you really need it. Prefill the workspace from the name ("Ben's notes") or ask for it after sign-up, and move the attribution question to onboarding or make it clearly optional. If you keep it, give the select a visible prompt; right now it's an empty box that looks broken.
2. **Nothing marks what's required or how to fill it in (defect).** No field is marked required or optional, so the visitor has to guess, and "How did you hear about us?" is clearly the one they'd want to skip. The password field shows no rules, so the visitor finds them out from an error after pressing the button. Fix: mark the optional field "(optional)" (fewer marks than putting an asterisk on every required one). Put the password rule as one short line under the field, before submitting.
3. **The only selling line is generic (taste, argued).** "Create your account / Start a free workspace for your notes." could sit on any notes app. A visitor still deciding gets no reason, and someone comparing against their current tool (Apple Notes, Notion) gets no point of comparison. Fix: let the subline say the one thing Quillnest does that their current notes app doesn't, in the visitor's terms ("you" can…). Make "free" concrete: free forever, or free for how long? That's a commitment people will hold you to.

**Smaller things.**
- **Sign-in link.** "Sign in" in "Already have an account? Sign in" is the same grey as the sentence around it (`p.fine`, #5a6680), with no underline or weight change. Returning users can't see where to click. Colour it or underline it.
- **Input borders.** The borders look like a pale grey-blue on white, probably under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on control edges. Measure the border colour and darken it if it falls short.
- **Type sizes.** There are six: 26, 20, 16, 15, 14 and 13 (`type_sizes`). The lead (15), the labels (14), the fine print (13) and the button (16) are a pixel apart and don't read as separate levels. Merge them into two sizes and let weight and colour do the rest.
- **No quick sign-up.** There's no "Continue with Google/Apple" option. For a consumer notes app, that's the shortcut most visitors expect and would take.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing.** The spacing is disciplined. Each label sits 6 px above its field with 16 px between fields, so every label clearly belongs to its input. Keep that ratio when you cut fields.
- **The button.** There's one unmistakable action: a full-width navy button (8.15 contrast) and nothing else competing with it.
- **Labels.** Labels sit visibly above the fields rather than as placeholders, so they don't disappear when someone starts typing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus and errors.** Focus rings and error states on the fields. For a form, these decide whether a failed submit can be recovered from.
- **Input types.** Whether email and password use the right input types, and whether the password can be shown.
