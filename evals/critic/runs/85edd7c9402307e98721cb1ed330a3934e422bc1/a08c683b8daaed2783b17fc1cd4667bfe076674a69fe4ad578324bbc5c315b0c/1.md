**First read.** The serif headline lands first. The eye then drops to the four illustrated cards, which carry most of the colour, and stops on the green "From $" prices. "Book a visit", top right, only registers on a second pass. It reads as a friendly, tidy local home-services site: calm, warm and trustworthy, if a little anonymous.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage of a local home-services company, where a homeowner should understand the offer, see what it costs and start a booking.

**Verdict.** It looks well made but doesn't do the most important part of its job. It shows four services and four prices, then gives the viewer nothing to do with them. The biggest problem is that the cards, the part the eye actually reaches, have no action on them. The only way to book is a header button the eye has already passed.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards end the path with nowhere to go (defect).** The subhead says "Pick a service, choose a day", so the viewer tries to pick a card. But nothing on the four cards says they can be clicked: no button, no arrow, no "Book" link, no hover cue visible in the still. The green "From $89" lines could be links or just coloured text. The eye goes headline → cards → price and stops, about 600 px from the only CTA in the top-right corner.
   - Norman's affordances and signifiers rule asks that something you can act on look like it.
   - The fix: give each card a clear way into booking that service, such as a "Book window cleaning" link or button at the bottom of the card, or make the whole card visibly a link with an arrow and a bordered or raised state.
   - Also, or instead, put a primary "Book a visit" action under the subhead so the hero has its own next step. Putting the next step where the viewer already is matters more here than the header button.
2. **The prices can't be relied on as written (defect).** Prices are what a viewer remembers and later holds you to, and none of these says what it's a price *of*.
   - "From $89" for window cleaning: per window, per visit, or per house?
   - "From $24" for laundry: per load, per bag, or per week?
   - "From $189" for deep cleaning: for what size of home?
   
   A viewer who reads "$24" as the whole laundry service and then gets billed per bag will feel misled. Add the unit and what sets the price ("From $89 per visit, up to 10 windows").
   
   The laundry line has a similar problem: "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday." It can be read as "Monday is the only pickup day" or as an example turnaround. Either reading contradicts "choose a day" in the subhead. Say which it is.
3. **Nothing in the copy sets this company apart (taste, plus the "words are part of the design" rule).** "Help around the house, on your schedule" and "a vetted crew arrives with their own supplies" could sit on any cleaning site. The viewer can't tell why to pick Fernway over whoever they use now. Since the page names no area, they also can't tell whether Fernway serves them. Put one concrete differentiator, aimed at the viewer, into the headline or subhead, for example a guarantee, the service area, or "same crew every visit". This matters more than any visual polish.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: it breaks as "on your / schedule", splitting the phrase. Widen the headline box or rebreak it to "Help around the house, / on your schedule". This is common sense; no confirmed source covers heading breaks.

**What's working.**
- The illustrations are one consistent set: the same flat style, the same stroke weight on the dark outlines, the same pale-teal tile and the same scattered dots. They read as one family and say "friendly, tidy" without stock photos. Keep them.
- The palette is small and disciplined: cream ground, dark slate text, teal, plus warm orange and yellow as accents only. Colour adds warmth without competing with the text.
- The structure is clear: one dominant serif headline, a quieter sans subhead, then four equal cards on a clean grid with even gutters. The cards are rightly equal, because they're a choice set and not a ranking.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the cards and prices, which would show whether they're already clickable.
- The empty band of about 150 px below the cards: it's probably the fold, but check in a live browser that the next section isn't missing or failing to load.
