**First read.** The eye lands on "Pricing", drops to the subtitle, then moves across three cards with the same weight: $9, $29, $79. Then it stops. Nothing at the cards says "pick this one" or "start here", so it drifts up to the blue "Start free" button in the top right corner. It reads as a clean, calm, generic SaaS pricing page for a scheduling or booking tool. It looks tidy and finished, but you can't act on it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the pricing page of a scheduling tool, Tidewell. Its job is to let a small team or solo user see which plan fits them and start on it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. It shows prices but gives the visitor no way to choose a plan or act on one. The cards are a dead end, and the copy leaves out what the visitor needs to decide: what the price is per, what each tier adds, and what "Start free" starts.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards have no action, and the only action is up in the header.** None of the three cards has a button. The visitor decides "Team looks right" and then has nowhere to click. They have to go back up to the header's "Start free", which doesn't say which plan it starts, whether it's a trial, or for how long. The eye path ends where the decision is made, and the action sits a screen-width away from it. *Defect.* Nielsen's heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall: the visitor has to carry their choice from the card up to an unrelated button. Krug: what to do next should be self-evident. Fix: put one action at the foot of each card, worded for that plan ("Start Team free for 14 days", or whatever the real offer is), so choosing and acting happen in the same place.
2. **No plan stands out, so nothing helps the visitor choose.** The three cards are the same size, weight, border and colour, and the three prices are identical in treatment. The biggest element on the page is the word "Pricing", which tells a visitor who already clicked "Pricing" nothing new. *Convention:* Tschichold, *The New Typography*: make one element clearly the first thing seen and set the rest back. On a pricing page that one element is the plan you expect most visitors to want. Fix: pick the plan you want people on, probably Team given "teams of every size", and set it apart with the brand blue, a filled button where the others are outlined, and a short "Most teams pick this" label. Let the page title step down.
3. **The copy doesn't tell the visitor what they'd pay for.** These are the lines people hold you to, so ambiguity here is a defect, not a style note:
   - **"$29 / month."** Is that per user or for the whole team? Read it as flat and a 10-person team expects $29. Read it per seat and they expect $290. Either reading, if wrong, costs you a customer or a support ticket. Say "per team" or "per user".
   - **"1 calendar" and "Up to 10 calendars."** "Calendar" is used as the unit you pay for, but a visitor thinks in people. Does one calendar mean one person? Say it in their terms ("1 person", "up to 10 people").
   - **The feature lists.** They read as three unrelated lists. Does Team include Solo's "Booking page" and "Email reminders"? Does Business include "Reports"? A visitor can't tell, so the higher tiers look like they have *fewer* features. Fix: "Everything in Solo, plus:".
   - **The subtitle.** "Simple plans for teams of every size. Change or cancel whenever you like." could sit on any SaaS page. Its useful half is the cancel promise, which is buried. Replacing it with the trial terms, or a line that lands on the visitor ("Try any plan free for 14 days; no card needed"), would make the page's one sentence do work.

   *Defect* for the price and unit; *taste* for the subtitle.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle breaks onto a second line holding only "you like.", a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrow the measure or shorten the line so it breaks evenly, or fits on one line.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clean:** title, plan name, price, features, about four clear levels. Prices are big and bold with "/ month" set small and grey beside them, so each number reads at a glance. Keep that pairing.
- **Restraint with colour.** Blue appears only on the logo, the current-page underline and the one button, so when you add a recommended plan, the blue will carry real weight.
- **The current page is clearly marked** in the nav with an underline, and the three cards share one grid, gutter and inner margin exactly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty band under the cards is the end of the page or a capture cut-off. If the page really ends there, it needs at least an FAQ (billing, cancelling, trial) and a closing action.
- Focus and hover states on the nav and button.
