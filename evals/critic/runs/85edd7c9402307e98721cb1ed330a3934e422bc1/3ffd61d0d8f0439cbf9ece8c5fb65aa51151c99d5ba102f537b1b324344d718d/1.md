**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming this is the first screen of a web page that tells ferry riders when the boats run, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** The type is set well, but the piece doesn't do its job. It calls itself a timetable and shows no times, so a rider who arrives wanting to know when the next boat leaves can't work it out from this screen. Fixing that matters more than everything else here put together.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "timetable" has no times in it.** The label "Spring timetable" (`p.eyebrow`) promises departures. The lead paragraph says the ferry runs "every forty minutes", but gives no first sailing to count from, no last sailing, and no hours for "by daylight". Even with the paragraph read closely, a rider still can't name one departure time. After the paragraph the eye reaches the blank lower third and right side, and the page ends there. Krug's test is that a page should be self-evident: within seconds you can tell what it is and what you can do. This page passes the first half and fails the second. The heading also names something that isn't on the page (WCAG 2.4.6, headings describe their section). This is a **defect**, and it matters most because times are exactly what riders act on and later hold the operator to. The fix: put real times on the first screen, at least the first and last sailing from each landing, or the next departure from each. Use the empty space below the lead for them. If the full table really is lower on the page, then something on this screen has to show it's there. As it stands, the empty lower third tells the reader the page has ended.

2. **The headline takes half the screen and leaves the rider to work out what it means.** At 76 px (`font_size`, `h1`) and about 19 characters per line, the 57-character headline runs to four lines and 334 px of height. Most of that height goes to "spring through early summer", a season the label above has already given. "By daylight" sounds nice but tells a rider nothing they can plan around: from when, until when? Each phrase leaves the rider to fill a gap. This is a **defect** in the copy. Direction: cut the headline to the route and the promise, such as the two landings and the crossing. Then let the hours or the service window carry the facts, in words that need no interpreting. A shorter headline at this size would also free the first screen for the times in finding 1.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks "early / summer" and leaves "summer" alone on the last line. That splits a phrase and creates a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rewriting the headline (finding 2) probably fixes it; if not, rebalance the line breaks.
- The lead ends with "weather." alone on its third line, another runt. Rebalance with a non-breaking space or a slight rewording.
- The 76 px Inter Bold looks set at the font's default letter spacing, so "Quayside ferry" reads a little loose. Tighten it slightly. Lupton supports adjusting spacing at large sizes but warns against overdoing it, so this is partly **taste**.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing that shows the groups.** The label sits 16 px above the headline and the lead sits 40 px below it, so the label clearly belongs to the headline and the lead reads as the next step. Keep that ratio when you add the times.
- **A clear type scale.** Three sizes (76, 20 and 15 px) with big steps between them. The label is set apart by weight and grey as well as size (contrast 6.11), so every level has an obvious job.
- **A paragraph that's comfortable to read.** The lead is 20 px at 65.5 characters per line with line spacing of 1.5, close to Bringhurst's ideal line length. All three blocks share one left edge at x = 120.
