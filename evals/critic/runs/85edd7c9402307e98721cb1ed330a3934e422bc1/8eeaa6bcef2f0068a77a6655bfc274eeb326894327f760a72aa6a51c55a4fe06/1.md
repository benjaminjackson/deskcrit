**First read.** My eye goes to the bold black heading, "Notes for ferry passengers", and then drops into the body text, where it slows right down. All three paragraphs are set in a heavy brush script, so the page reads like a café chalkboard or a wedding card rather than a timetable notice. It looks like a practical information page with a costume on. I'm reading letter shapes instead of facts. The bold, plain sans-serif heading and the looping script below it look like they come from two different pages.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a web page that gives ferry passengers practical service information (frequency, crossing time, bicycles, winter changes), read on a desktop.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well. The layout underneath is sound: one column, one left edge, good contrast, sensible line length. But the decision that matters most here was the body typeface, and that decision is wrong. Setting every sentence in a script face makes simple service facts slow to read and easy to misread. The copy also leaves out the facts passengers would act on.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set in a decorative script** (defect). All three paragraphs, 465 of the page's 488 characters, are in Pacifico at 22 px (`font_family`, `font_size`, `main.article > p:nth-of-type(1–3)`). Pacifico is a display face: thick strokes, connected letters, little difference between letter shapes. At reading length that has two costs. First, people read it slower, because each word has to be untangled ("twelve minutes", "timetable thins" and "lock them to the rail" all take a second look). Second, it is harder for anyone with low vision or dyslexia. Scripts are for a few words of display, not for sentences someone is reading to catch a boat. The fix: set the paragraphs in a face made for reading, and keep script, blackletter and other decorative faces for a few words of display (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 6.2.1). The simplest route is to set the body in Inter, which the heading already uses, at a regular weight. If you want warmth or local character, use the script for one small display element at most, never the body.

2. **Heading and body don't belong to one piece** (taste, but I'd argue it hard). A bold, neutral grotesque sits directly on top of a 1950s-style brush script. They share nothing in mood or period, so the page looks assembled rather than designed. The heading promises a plain information notice and the body contradicts it. Fixing item 1 fixes this too. If a script stays anywhere, it has to be the only odd element, used on purpose. It can't be the main voice.

3. **The copy is vague exactly where passengers act** (defect). "Between early morning and late evening", "about twelve minutes", "one boat each hour" and "before dusk" are the things a passenger plans around, and none of them gives a time. "The last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" can be read two ways: which quay (north landing or market quay), and before dusk by how much? Someone who guesses wrong misses the last boat in winter. And "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" sends the reader somewhere else for the one thing this page should give them. Put the real first and last departure times from each landing on the page, with the summer and winter dates. Then each fact can be a short line the eye can scan, not something buried mid-sentence. The heading "Notes for ferry passengers" could also say what's here, for example "Ferry times, crossings and bicycles".

**What's working.**
- **One clean column on a single left edge.** The heading and all three paragraphs align at x = 120 (`left_edges`), with consistent gaps (20 px between paragraphs, 24 px under the heading). Keep this structure when you change the face.
- **The basics of setting are already right.** Text is dark on white at 15.8:1 (`contrast`), line spacing is 1.6 (`line_height_ratio`), and lines run about 73–74 characters (`characters_per_line`). When you switch to a text face, check that the measure stays within 45–75 characters. A narrower face will fit more characters into the 700 px column.
- **Short paragraphs, one topic each.** Schedule, bicycles and winter are each their own paragraph. That chunking is the right instinct.
