**Quillpost sign-in: design critique**

**First read.** The eye lands on the purple "Sign in" button, then goes up to the red-bordered email field and its red message. You can tell at once that this is the Quillpost sign-in card, caught after the email check failed. It feels calm, plain and familiar: a standard centred card with nothing pulling attention away from the form.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming this is a desktop web sign-in screen shown in its email-error state. It serves returning users and sends new users to sign-up.

**Verdict.** It does its job. It's a clean, conventional sign-in with a clear main action and readable type throughout. The biggest problem is that the sign-up link can't be seen as a link, so a new user arriving here has no visible way forward. After that, the error message makes the user find their own mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Create an account" doesn't look clickable** (bottom of the card). The whole line "New here? Create an account" is one grey (#59636e, `div.foot`), with no underline and no change of colour. "Forgot your password?" right above it is in the brand purple. So the page has already taught the user that purple means link, and grey reads as plain text. A new user reads the line as a note and looks for a button that isn't there. *Defect* (Norman on signifiers: controls should show what they do; also Nielsen's heuristic 4, consistency). Fix: style "Create an account" like the other link, in purple, ideally underlined. Keep "New here?" grey.

2. **The error message makes the user hunt for the difference** (under the email field). They typed `dana.ortiz@exmaple`, and the hint shows `name@example.com`. Those two strings look alike: the gap is a missing ".com" plus a swapped "am/ma". A user comparing them can easily see a match and submit again. The message states a general rule rather than what is wrong with this address, so the user has to work out the problem themselves. *Defect* (the error states its fix only in general terms, so recovery is slower than it needs to be). Fix: say what this address is missing, e.g. "This address is missing its ending, such as .com". If the validation can spot it, offer the likely correction ("Did you mean example.com?").

**Smaller things**
- **Password field border:** the light grey border on white looks well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of input edges (I'd guess about 2:1). An empty field would be hard to see for low-vision users. Darken the border; measure the exact ratio to confirm.
- **Logo and heading sizes:** the wordmark is 20 px bold (`div.logo`) and "Sign in" is 24 px bold (`h1`). Four pixels and the same weight read as two near-identical levels rather than a clear step. Make the wordmark clearly smaller, or set them further apart in size. The page uses four sizes (24/20/16/14), and three would carry it.
- **Show-password option (taste/common sense, not on the checklist):** the password field has no way to reveal what's typed. A "Show" toggle cuts down retyping after a failed sign-in, and this screen is already in a failed-attempt state.

**What's working**
- **One clear action.** The full-width purple button (340×46 px) is the only strong colour block. Nothing competes with it, so "what do I do here" is answered at a glance.
- **Tight, consistent structure.** Six of the eight text blocks share one left edge at x=470 (`left_edges`). Each label sits 6 px above its field, and there's more space between groups than inside them, so every label clearly belongs to its own field. Keep this.
- **The error is told in words as well as colour.** It has a red border plus a sentence, at 6.54:1 contrast (`div.err`), so it doesn't rely on red alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- **Focus state:** I can't tell whether the email field's 2 px red border is the error style, a focus ring, or both. Check in a browser that a focused field shows a ring distinct from the error border and from the default state.

**Checklist**
- The whole piece: 8 lines, 7 yes, 1 no (each kind of element looks the same: the two links are styled differently)
- What matters first: 3 lines, 2 yes, 1 no (distinct text levels: logo 20 px vs heading 24 px)
- Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 13 yes
- Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
- Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
- Pictures: 12 lines, 12 yes (wordmark only)
- Charts and data: skipped, no chart
- Interface: 33 lines, 30 yes, 2 no (controls look like what they do; input border contrast), 1 unsure (focus indicator)
