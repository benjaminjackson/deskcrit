**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the grey subhead, then stops on the dark-teal "Get started" button. After that it has nowhere to go. The whole right half of the screen and the bottom third are empty beige. It reads as a landing page for a group trip-planning app. It feels calm and tidy, but also generic and unfinished, like a hero section waiting for its picture.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing home page. Its job is to make someone who organises group trips see why Wayfold beats their group chat, and then sign up.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The type and buttons are well made, but the page shows nothing: no product, no trip, no group. The one sharp idea, ending the chat-thread mess, is buried in the subhead under a headline any travel app could use.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half is empty, so the page shows nothing and tips to the left.** All the content sits in the left ~700 px. To the right of the headline there's a 500-px-wide empty slot. That's where the viewer expects to see the product: a shared itinerary, the group's flights and stay in one view. With nothing there, they have to take "in one place" on trust. The eye also hits a dead end after the buttons, and the page looks lopsided because nothing on the right balances the heavy headline (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). If an image failed to load in this capture, this finding is about the capture, not the design. Check it in a live browser. If the slot is really empty, put a real product view there, one trip with several people's choices merged. That image does the persuading, balances the page and gives the eye somewhere to go after the call to action. *Defect.*
2. **The headline could be on anyone's page, and the real difference is hidden in the subhead.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" fits every itinerary app, booking site and notes app. The part that's specific to Wayfold, "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads", sits in grey at a third of the size. A viewer scanning only the headline can't tell why this isn't TripIt or a shared doc. Move the group-agreement idea into the headline, said to the viewer about their own problem. One direction: something about getting everyone to agree without the group chat. The subhead can then cover flights, stays and day plans. *Defect (copy).*
3. **"Get started" doesn't say what happens or why to click.** The viewer can't tell whether it creates an account, starts a trip or opens a form. Name the first concrete step in the viewer's own terms, such as "Start a trip and invite your group". Better still, make it an ask that lets them see the value for themselves against the chat thread they already have, rather than taking the claim on trust. *Defect (copy).*

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place". The preposition "in" is left hanging at the end of line 1. Break as "Plan the whole trip / in one place" (common sense: break at the phrase boundary).
- The bottom ~300 px is empty, and with the empty right half it looks like space left over rather than composed. Once the right side has an image, pull the hero up or let the next section show above the fold so the viewer knows to scroll.

**What's working.**
- The type is clean, with clear steps between levels. The bold, tightly set headline dominates without a fight, the subhead is clearly secondary, and the nav is quietly small. Keep this scale.
- The buttons are a correct primary/secondary pair: a filled teal "Get started" and an outlined "See pricing" in the same teal. You can tell at once which action matters, and both clearly look clickable.
- The palette is restrained: warm off-white, near-black text and one teal used for the brand and actions. It feels calm and trustworthy. Keep it when you add imagery, and let the photo or product view bring the colour.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is a hero image that didn't load (check in a live browser).
- Focus styles on the nav and buttons.
- How the two-line headline and the button pair reflow on a phone.
