**First read.** The eye goes straight to the dark blue block of weekday late mornings, then to the headline, then to the three big numbers on the right. It reads as a support team's report on when tickets come in, made for planning shifts. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy. That trust breaks when you check the busiest cell against the "Busiest hour" figure.

**What it's for.** Assumed: a support lead uses this page to see when tickets pile up, so they can put agents on shift at those hours.

**Verdict.** This is a well-made report: a fitting chart, a single-hue ramp, and context next to every number. But its one headline fact contradicts its own chart, and the title names the topic instead of saying what the chart shows. Fix the 10:00 vs 11:00 conflict first. A staffing decision rests on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The busiest-hour figure disagrees with the chart.** *Defect.*
   - The hour labels are left-aligned to the start of each cell, so "09:00" sits over the 09:00–10:00 column.
   - Read that way, the darkest cells (30+ on Mon and Fri) are in the 11:00 column. On Tue to Thu, the 10:00 and 11:00 cells are in the same 25–29 band.
   - The KPI says "Busiest hour, weekdays: 10:00" (`aside > div.kpi:nth-of-type(2)`). A reader who checks the two finds them an hour apart. Then they either distrust the page or staff the wrong hour.
   - Three possible causes:
     - The labels are meant to mark hour boundaries, not cells.
     - The KPI uses unbinned averages that the 5-ticket bands hide.
     - One of the two is simply wrong.
   - Fix: make the chart and the KPI agree visibly.
     - If the KPI is right, mark the 10:00 column, for example with an outline or a "busiest" tick.
     - If the labels are the problem, center each label over its cell or place it on a tick at the cell edge.
     - In any case, write the hour as a range ("10:00–11:00"). "10:00" on its own can be read as an instant or as the hour that starts then.
2. **The title names the topic, not the finding.** *Convention* (Tufte, Few: titles and annotations should state the takeaway).
   - "When new tickets arrive" and the descriptive subtitle make the viewer work out the pattern alone.
   - The pattern is strong and actionable:
     - Weekdays run from about 08:00 to 17:00 and peak late morning.
     - Mondays and Fridays are the heaviest.
     - Weekends are a fraction of weekdays.
   - For a staffing page, that sentence is the point. Say it in the headline, for example "Most tickets arrive 09:00–16:00 on weekdays, peaking late morning". Keep the current subtitle as the chart's description.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale:** six sizes (28, 26, 18, 16, 15, 13 px; `type_sizes`). The h1 (28) and the KPI values (26) are 2 px apart, both bold, so they compete as equals. The subtitle (16) and nav (15) also nearly match. Drop to about four sizes: make the h1 clearly larger, or the KPI values clearly smaller.
- **Neighbouring ramp steps are hard to tell apart in the cells.** 15–19 vs 20–24, and 0–4 vs 5–9, blur together, for example along Tuesday 12:00–15:00 and across the weekend rows. Use fewer bands (five) with bigger lightness steps, or put values in the cells on hover or tap.
- **Date ranges don't match.** The chart covers "1 to 28 September", but "New tickets, September 3,412" presumably covers the whole month. Say whether the KPI covers 1–30 or 1–28, so the viewer isn't comparing two different periods without knowing it.

**What's working.**
- **The chart itself:** a single-hue lightness ramp on an hour × day grid is the right form for this question. It survives colour blindness and greyscale. The 7 × 24 grid makes the weekday/weekend split and the working-hours block show at once.
- **Every KPI comes with a comparison** ("Up 4% on August", "Same as August", "Down 6 min on August"). Each number can be judged against last month, not taken on trust.
- **The source line does real work.** "Hours are when a ticket was opened, not when it was answered" heads off the most likely misreading. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether hovering or tapping a cell shows its exact value. Without that, the 5-ticket bands are the finest detail available.
- The size of the nav click targets (none were measured).
