**First read.** The eye lands on "184,220" at top left, moves across the other two big figures, then drops to the three-line chart. It reads as a growth dashboard for a small SaaS team. It feels calm and tidy, and because every delta is green it says "all good". On the first pass that impression holds. When I checked the figures against each other, it fell apart.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's an overview screen where a marketing or growth lead checks the last 12 weeks of traffic and sign-ups and decides whether anything needs attention.

**Verdict.** The layout is competent, but the screen doesn't do its job: its numbers contradict each other, and a dashboard people can't trust is worse than none. Behind that, the chart shows a topic rather than a finding and doesn't say what unit it's in.

**What would move it most**
1. **The figures don't agree with each other (defect).** Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups up only 2.4%. That means conversion fell: the previous period works out to about 3,820 / 173,630 = 2.20%, against 2.12% now, so the change is roughly −0.08 pts. The third card says "+0.1 pts" in green. Also, adding up the three lines in "Visits by channel" week by week gives about 138k visits, not the 184,220 in the first card, so roughly a quarter of visits (likely Direct or Referral) is missing from the chart. Anyone who does the sums stops trusting the whole screen. Anyone who doesn't comes away thinking conversion improved when it got worse, and with a green signal where there should be a warning. The fix starts with the data: make the conversion delta follow from the other two figures, and colour it by direction, so a fall isn't green. Then either add the missing channels to the chart (as an "Other" line if needed) or title it so it's clear it shows only some channels. If this is mock data, it's still worth fixing: a reviewer or client will do these sums.
2. **The chart names a topic and leaves the reader to find the story (taste, common sense).** "Visits by channel" over 12 weeks of lines makes the viewer work out for themselves that Search is growing steadily (about 5k to 6.8k), Social is noisy and Email is flat. Since this is the biggest block on the screen, it should say the point, for example "Search drove most of the growth; Social is volatile". Then mark the one thing to act on.
3. **The chart doesn't say what it counts, and the legend sits away from the lines (convention: Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*; Few).** The y-axis runs 0 to 8k with no unit. Visits per week? Per day? Cumulative? The reader can't tell, and the mismatch in point 1 makes guessing riskier. Say "Weekly visits" in the title or on the axis. The legend above the plot also makes people match colours back and forth. The three lines never cross and are stacked in a fixed order, so labelling each at its right-hand end ("Search 6.8k") costs nothing. It removes the legend and also covers readers who can't easily tell orange from green.

**Smaller things.**
- **Too many small type sizes.** I count six or seven sizes. The card labels (~14 px), deltas (~13 px) and axis labels (~12 px) are only a pixel apart, so they read as accidents. "Overview" (~24 px) also sits close to the KPI figures (~27 px). Fold the small ones into one size and separate them with weight or grey instead (Refactoring UI).
- **"Last 12 weeks" at top right.** It looks like plain text. If it's the date-range picker, it needs something that shows it's clickable, such as a chevron or a button outline. If it's only a label, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- **Clear structure.** One clean grid: the three KPI cards and the chart card share edges and gutters, so the screen reads in a single top-to-bottom pass and everything fits on one screen (Few).
- **Every KPI has context.** Each one carries its period and comparison right under the number, which is exactly what makes a headline figure readable (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Once the numbers are right, keep this pattern.
- **Restrained chart.** Light gridlines, no chart decoration, a zero baseline, and three distinct hues only where categories need them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a menu, and whether the chart has hover values. Hover values would partly ease the unit question, but only for mouse users.
