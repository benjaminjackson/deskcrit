**First read.** Your eye lands on "Planning a vegetable bed", then runs down the left edge through three smaller headings, each sitting right on top of its paragraph. It reads straight away as a short beginner's gardening guide. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, very close to a well-handled default.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's a short web article that walks a beginner through setting up a first vegetable bed, read top to bottom.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the type scale is clean, and every heading sits with its own text. The one thing that would noticeably improve it is a narrower text column. Lines run about 90 characters, which makes each paragraph harder to read than it needs to be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The lines are too long.** The text column runs from x≈160 to x≈950, and full lines like "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" come to about 90 characters. At that length the eye has to work to find the start of the next line, and you can feel it on the two-line paragraphs: the second line starts a long way back. Bringhurst's range is 45 to 75 characters, with about 66 ideal. *Convention*, and here breaking it costs some reading comfort and gains nothing. Fix: cap the column at roughly 34–38 em (about 600–680 px at this size). There's plenty of empty space on the right to absorb it, and the layout won't change character. Measure the exact characters per line in the live page.

**Smaller things.**
- Headings ("Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant", "Watering") differ from the body only by size: same regular weight, same near-black. The steps are big enough to read, but a semibold or a slightly darker or tinted heading would make them easier to pick out when scanning and give them more presence (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- The apostrophe in "year's" is a straight typewriter mark. Use the curly ' (Butterick, *Practical Typography*).

**What's working.**
- **Grouping by space.** Each heading sits about 6 px above its paragraph and about 30 px below the one before, so every section reads as one unit without rules or boxes. Keep that ratio when you change anything.
- **A three-step type scale with big jumps** (about 56 / 32 / 18 px). Each size has an obvious job, and nothing sits a few points away from another size.
- **The copy.** Every line tells the reader exactly what to do, with real quantities ("six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "twice a week", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans"). There are no filler sentences, and it reads like a person who has actually done it.
