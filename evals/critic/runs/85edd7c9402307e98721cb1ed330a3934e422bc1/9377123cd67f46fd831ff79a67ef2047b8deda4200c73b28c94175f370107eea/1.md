**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project" at top left, crosses to the white card, and drops down to the blue "Send message" button. That path is short and correct. It reads as a contact page for a small design studio: calm, plain and trustworthy, but anonymous. You notice a dead grey strip to the right of the card and a lot of empty floor below it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the contact page of a studio's website, and its job is to get prospective clients to send a useful first brief.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The page is clean, readable and has one clear action, and nothing on it is broken. What holds it back is that the form asks for less than the page says it wants: the intro asks for budget and timeline, but the form gives no place or prompt for them. The layout also stops short of the page's own grid, so it looks unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The brief the page asks for isn't built into the form (defect).** The intro paragraph at left says to "include a budget range and a rough timeline". The Message box 260 px to the right gives no hint of this, and it is only about five lines tall. Most people read past the intro, start typing, and send "Hi, we need a website." You then spend your first reply asking for what the page already asked for. Nielsen's 10th heuristic says help belongs at the step it concerns, not in a paragraph across the page. Fix: put the prompt where the typing happens. Use hint text under the "Message" label ("Tell us what you're making, your budget range, and when you need it"), or add two optional fields for budget and timeline. Make the box tall enough that a full brief looks expected.
2. **The card ignores the page's right edge (convention; the weight of it is my taste).** The header runs from 56 to 1224 px. The form card stops at 1068 px, and nothing else on the page lines up with that edge. That leaves a 156 px strip of grey on the right that belongs to nothing. Together with the 380 / 560 / 156 split, it makes the layout look placed by eye rather than built on a grid (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*, "Construction of the grid"). The cost is small but real: the page looks a draft away from finished, which is the wrong signal from a studio selling design. Fix: run the card to the 1224 edge, or move both columns onto a grid that shares the header's margins. Either way the Message box gets the extra width.
3. **The form doesn't say which fields are required (convention).** "Your name", "Email address" and "Message" carry no asterisk and no "optional". With only three fields people will guess that all are required, so this is mild today. It becomes a real problem the moment you add optional budget and timeline fields, as finding 1 suggests. Fix: mark the optional fields with "(optional)" after the label, or put an asterisk on each required one with a one-line legend (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").

**Smaller things.**
- The top nav doesn't mark the page you're on: "Contact" is drawn exactly like Work, Services and About. Give the current item one cue, such as bold, a bar or no underline in a dark colour (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The type scale has three sizes one pixel apart: nav links 15 px, body and button 16 px, labels and footer 14 px (`type_sizes`). They don't read as levels. Fold the nav and footer into 14 or 16 and let weight and colour do the rest (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- The input borders look pale grey on white, likely under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on field edges. Measure the border colour against #ffffff, and darken it if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- The reassurance line is specific: "We read every message and reply within two working days" is a promise a visitor can hold you to. It lowers the cost of writing in more than any "We'd love to hear from you" would. Keep it.
- The hierarchy is clear with almost no effort. The 36 px bold headline (`h1`) is the only large thing, the labels are bold 14 px above their fields, and there is exactly one filled button. The eye path from headline to form to button needs no help.
- The contrast and readability are sound throughout. Body text is 16 px at 9.1:1 (`p`) with a 1.5 line height, and the links are underlined and 6.2 to 6.7:1. Nothing here is faint or relies on colour alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings and the button's hover and pressed states. These matter on a keyboard-reachable form, so check them in a browser.
- Whether "Email address" is a real `type="email"` field and what the error state looks like when it's wrong.
