**First read.** My eye goes to "Plan the week with your whole team" first, then over to the white card and down to the blue "Create account" button. It's a SaaS sign-up page, clean and calm, and you understand it in about two seconds. It also looks like a template: nothing on the page shows what Tallyboard actually looks like, or what makes it different from any other planning tool.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the desktop sign-up page for a team planning product, where a team lead creates a free workspace.

**Verdict.** The layout and hierarchy are sound, but the form is weak where it matters most. Every field is labelled only by its placeholder, so the labels and the password rule vanish as soon as someone starts typing. That is the one thing to fix before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **Placeholders are the only labels (defect).** None of the five fields has a label. The text inside each one disappears on the first keystroke. After that, someone checking their entries before submitting sees "Ben", "Smith" and "Acme" with nothing saying which field is which. It's worst in the password field, where the only rule ("At least 10 characters") is the placeholder. The rule is gone at the exact moment the user needs it, so they find out at submit. The email field's "name@company.com" is an example, not a label. Screen readers and autofill also handle placeholder-only fields badly. Fix: put a persistent label above each field ("First name", "Work email", "Password", "Company name") and move "At least 10 characters" under the password field as helper text (NN/g, Sherwin, "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful"; Nielsen heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall; WCAG 3.3.2).
2. **Nothing says which fields are required (defect).** "Company name" in particular could be optional, since plenty of people trying a tool don't have one or don't want to give it. Users can't tell, so they guess or abandon the form. Fix: mark the optional fields with "(optional)" after the label, or mark the required ones with an asterisk and explain it once (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). Better still, drop Company name if you can, since each field you remove is one less reason to quit.
3. **The left column doesn't sell the product (taste).** The headline could sit on any team tool's page. The useful, concrete line is the subhead's "free workspace for up to five people", and it's set small. Under the left column is an empty block of about 450 × 400 px. That space should show the viewer their own week in Tallyboard: a screenshot of a planned week, or one concrete before-and-after compared with what they use now (a spreadsheet, a Monday stand-up). As it stands, the page asks people to sign up on trust. The button can also say what it does: "Start free workspace" matches the subhead's promise better than the generic "Create account".

**Smaller things.**
- The "Sign in" link (under the button) looks the same colour and weight as "Already have an account?", so returning users can't see it's clickable. Make it the brand blue and underline it (WCAG 1.4.1; G183).
- The field borders are a pale grey-blue on white and look below 3:1. Measure them and darken them to at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clear: four steps (headline, card title, field and body text, the small sign-in line), each separated by weight as well as size. Keep it.
- There's one primary action, and it's unmistakable. Nothing competes with the solid blue full-width button.
- The two-column split is calm. The headline and the card sit high and nearly level, so the page reads left to right without any effort.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings, error states and their wording, and whether the email and password fields use the right input types.
- How the two columns stack on a narrow screen.
