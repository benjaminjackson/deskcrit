**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tilted electric-blue SALE sticker at top right, then across to the big headline, then down to four cards with nothing in them. It reads as the homepage of a quiet, linen-toned homewares shop running a weekend sale. The sticker feels like it came from a different, cheaper store.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the desktop homepage of an online homewares shop. Its job is to get shoppers into the weekend sale and show them products worth clicking.

**Verdict.** As it stands it doesn't do its job. The sale is announced but there's no way into it, and the products underneath have no pictures, so nothing on the page makes anyone want to click. Fix those two before anything else.

**What would move it most**

1. **The product cards have no products in them.** All four images (the row at y≈358–508) are flat tinted tiles with a blank rounded rectangle in the middle. In homewares the photo is the whole pitch: nobody clicks "Wool throw $68" without seeing the throw. As it stands the "Popular this week" row is four price tags. **Defect.** If the photos simply hadn't loaded when this was captured, check it in a live browser. If these are design placeholders, the fix is real product photography shot one consistent way: same light, same background family, same crop.

2. **The sale has no way in.** The hero says "Up to 40% off through Sunday" but has no button or link. The only thing that looks pressable is the blue SALE flag (`div.flag`, 215×88 px), which is a filled, rounded, saturated rectangle with bold white text, so it looks exactly like a button. It isn't marked up as a control (the facts file finds no targets on the page). Even if it were, "SALE" doesn't tell the shopper what clicking will do. Then the cards below show only one price each ($34, $89…), with no was/now price, so the shopper can't tell whether the popular items are even in the sale. **Defect** (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: signifiers. A thing that looks pressable should be pressable, and the reverse). Fix: give the hero one primary button that names the destination, e.g. "Shop the sale". Then either make the flag that button or drop it. Show sale pricing on any discounted card (the old price struck through next to the new one) so the 40% shows up where the shopper is actually looking.

3. **The sticker fights the brand, and it fights the headline.** It's the only saturated colour on the page (#2b5cff), tilted, and at 34 px it's nearly as big as the 40 px headline. So the eye lands on the word "SALE", not on the offer. Its rotated, electric-blue, bargain-bin look also clashes with the warm greige and soft neutrals that make "Marlowe & Finch" feel considered. The warm/cool split works backwards too: the one cool, bright colour sits on a decoration that adds no information, since it repeats the subline. **Taste, argued:** a shop selling linen and stoneware is selling calm and quality, and a clearance sticker undercuts that more than the discount helps. Fix: if the sale needs colour, put it on the call to action from finding 2, upright and in a colour drawn from the existing palette. Let the headline lead.

4. **The headline doesn't carry the news.** "Linens and cookware for the weekend" could sit on any homewares site, and it reads two ways: things to use at the weekend, or a weekend sale. The facts that make someone act (40% off, ends Sunday) are in the 18 px line below it. **Defect in the copy:** the offer and the deadline are what the shopper acts on, and they're in the weakest position. Fix: put the offer in the headline, e.g. "Up to 40% off linens and cookware, this weekend only". Use the subline for the end date stated exactly, e.g. "Ends Sunday at midnight", so the shopper knows when the price goes away.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count seven sizes (40, 34, 22, 20, 18, 16, 15 px in `type_sizes`). 20 vs 22 (the brand vs "Popular this week"), 18 vs 20, and 15 vs 16 (the card price vs the card title) don't read as different levels. Four sizes, around 40 / 22 / 16 / 14, with weight doing the rest, would be enough.
- The hero text starts at x=56 (`h1`, `p`), while the brand, the section heading, and the cards all start at x=48. That 8 px near-miss looks like an accident. Put the hero text on 48.
- The headline breaks as "…for / the weekend", which splits the phrase. Break it at "cookware / for the weekend", or let the new copy set the break.
- The bottom ~220 px is leftover blank space with no footer, so the page just stops. Add a footer or a second row so the page has an end.

**What's working.**
- The warm greige hero band (#efece6) with near-black Inter type sets a calm, domestic tone that suits linens and cookware. Keep it.
- The card grid is clean: four equal columns with even gutters, a left-aligned bold name over a regular-weight price, and every card built the same way. Once real photos go in, this is a solid product row.
- Contrast is comfortable everywhere: body 11.14:1 (`p`), the nav links 6.11:1, the cards 13–15.8:1.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the product tiles are placeholders or images that hadn't loaded yet. Check in a live browser.
- Whether the nav items are links. They're `span`s with 23 px tall boxes, so the facts file finds no targets. If they're meant to be clickable, they need to be real links and at least 24 px tall.
