**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold two-line H1, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then down into the body text. It reads at once as a SaaS help-centre article for whoever handles billing. It feels calm and plain, with nothing competing for attention. It looks trustworthy, and a little bare.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a help article for an account owner or billing admin who has come to change the company card and needs to do it without contacting support.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are sound. The article fails at the one thing its title promises: it never tells the reader how to update the card. It also leaves the decline deadline open to two readings.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page explains what happens around a card change but never gives the steps for making one.** The H1 promises a task. The first paragraph only says "from the billing page", and nothing on the page links to that page, names the button, or lists the steps. The reader has come to act. They leave knowing about invoices and permissions, then have to go and find the billing page and the right control themselves, or contact support, which is the visit this article exists to prevent. This is a **defect**. Nielsen's heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall, says the user should not have to supply context from elsewhere. Putting the next action in front of the reader is common sense. Fix: straight under the H1, add a short numbered list of steps, starting with a direct link to the billing page and naming the exact control by its label ("Go to Billing → Payment method → Replace card"). The paragraphs about invoices and roles then become notes below the steps.

2. **"After the second attempt" can be read two ways, and it sets when the account gets downgraded.** In the "When the new card is declined" paragraph, the retries are at three days and at seven days. "The second attempt" could mean the second retry (day 7) or the second charge overall (the day-3 retry). A reader who takes the second meaning expects the downgrade four days earlier than it happens. A reader who takes the first, if it is wrong, gets caught out. The paragraph also never says three days after what, and never says what "moves to the free plan" costs the reader: which features or seats they lose. This is a **defect**, because it sits on a deadline the reader will hold you to. Fix: state the timeline in the reader's terms, for example "If the card is still declined 7 days after the first failed charge, your workspace moves to the free plan and you lose X until you add a working card."

**Smaller things.**
- **Left sidebar:** "Update a payment card" looks exactly like the other four items, so the sidebar doesn't show where you are. Mark the current item with a bolder weight, a bar, or a tint (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Body measure:** lines look like 80 to 86 characters (for example "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days, and we send an"), just over Bringhurst's 45 to 75. Narrow the column by about 10% or set the body slightly larger. Measure it to confirm.
- **Labels:** the sidebar says "Update a payment card" but the H1 says "Update the card that pays for your subscription". Use the same name in both places so the link and the page it opens are plainly the same thing.

**What's working.**
- **Type scale:** the H1, the H2, the body and the small grey sidebar and breadcrumb are four clearly separate levels. The H2 is told apart by weight as well as size, so it's easy to scan. Keep this scale when you add the steps.
- **Links:** inline links are blue *and* underlined, and their text says where each one goes ("roles and permissions", "paying by invoice"). They work without colour and make sense when read on their own.
- **Copy voice:** the copy talks to the reader ("you", "we") and answers the follow-up questions they'd otherwise ask support: which invoice gets charged, who is allowed to change the card, and what happens if you pay by bank transfer. Keep that, and put it below the steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the sidebar and inline links. Check that keyboard focus is clearly visible.
