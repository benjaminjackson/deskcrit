**First read.** The eye lands on the black headline, drops to the orange button, then gets pulled straight down to the purple box under it. That box is the same kind of filled, rounded, saturated block as the button, so for a moment it reads as a second choice. The teal panel on the right is a large flat block with a generic chat-bubble icon, and the eye passes over it. It feels clean, friendly and templated: a language-learning app's landing page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing landing page for new visitors, meant to get them to start a free trial of a speaking-first language course.

**Verdict.** The top half nearly works. The headline is specific and the button is the right ask. But a "streak" message meant for existing learners sits directly under the main button, styled almost as loudly, and it splits the one action the page exists for. Three unrelated bright colours (orange, purple, teal) make it look assembled rather than designed.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple "Keep your streak going" box is in the wrong place and is the wrong kind of element.** Defect. It is a filled violet block (#6d28d9) 28 px below the orange button. That is about the same gap as between the intro text and the button, so it joins the call-to-action group and reads as a second button. A first-time visitor has no streak, so "Keep your streak going" speaks to someone they are not. They stop to work out whether they are already meant to have an account. The page now has two high-emphasis blocks competing for the same moment, where it should have one (Material Design 3, "Buttons": one high-emphasis action, others lower). Fix: take it out of the first screen. If the stat matters ("learners who practise five days in a row finish twice as often"), turn it into a plain-text line of evidence near the three steps, with no fill and no button shape.
2. **Three saturated hues with no relationship, and the biggest one does no work.** Convention and taste. The orange button, purple box and teal panel each claim attention. The teal panel is the largest coloured area on the page (about 400 × 300 px), and it holds two speech bubbles that just restate the word "conversation". So the strongest colour field carries the least information, and the palette reads as a collage. Fix: keep one accent, the orange that marks the action, and let everything else go neutral or a quiet tint. Replace the panel with something only this product could show: a lesson screen, the per-word feedback from step 2, or a short exchange in Spanish with its translation. Then the image proves the headline instead of repeating it.
3. **The main button's label fails contrast.** Defect. The white label on orange #d9480f measures 4.3:1 (`contrast`, `span.btn`). At 18 px bold (`font_size`, `font_weight`) it does not count as large text, so the bar is 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3). It is a small miss, but it is on the one element that must be read. Fix: darken the orange a step (around #c2410c) until the label clears 4.5:1.
4. **The copy leaves the viewer to fill gaps on the very things they'll hold you to.** Defect. "Short daily lessons": how short? Five minutes or thirty is the deciding question for a busy adult, and the page doesn't say. "A tutor that listens" can be read two ways, as a human tutor or as software, and the price and experience the viewer expects differ completely. A viewer who assumes a human and finds a bot feels misled. "Start your free week" is a good, specific ask. But the page asks the viewer to take "real conversation in ten weeks" on trust and gives no way to test it. Fix: state the minutes per day, say plainly who or what the tutor is, and think about an ask that lets visitors judge for themselves, e.g. "Try a 3-minute speaking lesson in Spanish". That way they hear the feedback on their own voice before signing up.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count five sizes, 42/20/18/17/15 px (`type_sizes`). The intro text at 18, the step headings and notice title at 17, and the brand at 20 sit too close to read as separate levels. Keep 42, 18 and 15, and let bold carry the step headings (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- Grid: the teal panel ends at about x 1076, while the nav and the step columns end at 1224. That leaves a stray 148 px gap on the right, and the hero's two-column split doesn't line up with the three step columns at 56/461/867 (`left_edges`). Hang the hero on the same columns as the steps.
- Headline break: "Hold a real conversation in / ten weeks" splits "in" from its phrase. Break before "in", or let the measure carry "in ten weeks" together.
- The 42 px Inter headline looks set at default tracking. Tighten it slightly so it holds together as display type.

**What's working.**
- The headline is a concrete promise ("real conversation", "ten weeks") rather than a slogan, and at 42 px bold it clearly leads (`font_size`, `h1`).
- The three numbered steps (Listen, Speak, Use it) explain the method in one scan. Short bold labels over one-line descriptions, separated by thin rules, are a clean pattern worth keeping.
- The intro paragraph is well set: 18 px, 67 characters per line, 1.5 line height, contrast 10.2:1 (`p`).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states for the button and nav links, and whether the hero reflows sensibly on a phone, where the purple box would land even closer to the button.
