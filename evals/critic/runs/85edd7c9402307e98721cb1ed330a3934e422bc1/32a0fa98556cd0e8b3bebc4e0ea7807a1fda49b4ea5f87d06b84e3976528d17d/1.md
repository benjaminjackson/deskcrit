**What it's for.** Assumption: a local garden centre's homepage, meant to get nearby shoppers to come in or order this weekend's shade plants.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline loses to its own background, and once you've read the page it gives you nothing to act on: no button, no link to the plants, no address.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is set straight on a pattern that beats it.** This is a defect. The white 46 px h1 (`font_size`, `h1`) sits on the hero's brightest, busiest stretch: cream, pale yellow and white leaves and dots run right behind "small" and "spaces.", and the full stop merges with a white dot. The measurement couldn't check it (`contrast`: "not measurable: background image"), but by eye, contrast at the worst point behind the letters is near zero, so the line breaks into fragments. It's also the only thing telling a visitor why to come this weekend. The rule is the worst point, not the average (W3C, Techniques for WCAG 2.2, F83). Fix: put a dark scrim, a see-through overlay, or a solid panel behind the text, or move the text onto a calm part of the image, so every letter stands out from what is directly behind it.
2. **There is no ask.** This is a defect. The page tells me ferns and hostas are "ready to pot this weekend", then ends in three information cards and 120 px of empty white. There's no button under the headline or lead, nothing links to the plants, and the "Visit" card doesn't exist: I get the opening hours but not where the shop is. A shopper who wants to act has to go and look in the nav. Fix: add one primary action that names the exact thing, such as "See this weekend's shade plants" or "Get directions", right under the lead, and put the address in or next to the hours card.
3. **The pattern is louder than everything it supports.** This is taste, and it's tied to #1. The hero fills half the screen with even, all-over, saturated foliage, with warm red and orange dots scattered through it, so there's no calm area and no focal point inside it. The warm dots pull the eye away from the words. The bright, sunny palette also undercuts the message: the headline says *shade*, and the picture says high summer. A darker, cooler, calmer leaf field, or a photo of actual ferns and hostas in a shady corner, would carry the text and the claim at once.

**Smaller things.**
- The h1's left edge is at x=72, while the brand and lead text (and the cards' outer edges) sit at x=56. That 16 px near-miss looks like an accident. Put it on 56, or move it in far enough to look deliberate.
- The headline breaks between "small" and "spaces", splitting the key phrase and leaving one word alone on the second line. Break after "shade" instead.
- The hours card breaks "8 / pm" across lines. Keep the number and its unit together with a non-breaking space.
- Card headings (17 px) and card body (15 px) are only 2 px apart, and so are the lead (20 px) and the brand (22 px). That's five sizes where three or four would do (`type_sizes`). The bold already separates the card headings, so set them at 15 px and drop a level (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
