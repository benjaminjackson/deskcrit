**First read.** The eye goes to the bold headline, then reads straight down two calm paragraphs, and stops at a smaller line at the bottom that looks like a footnote. It reads as a plain information page for people taking a small river ferry. It feels quiet, tidy and trustworthy, a bit like a document.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it tells a passenger, before they set out, when the ferry runs and how to bring a bike, so they turn up at the right time and know what to do.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is sound, but the page doesn't do its job. It gives no times anyone can plan around, and the one warning that could leave someone stranded is set as the smallest, least important line on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The winter warning is in the wrong place in the order of importance** (defect). "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard…" is the last line, smaller than the body text (about 16 px against 20), and reads like a footnote. It is the only line that says "the rest of this page may be wrong." A reader who skims the first paragraph, sees "every forty minutes", and leaves will never reach it. The type scale is fine; the order of importance it was given is wrong. Fix: move the warning up to sit with the timetable paragraph, at body size or stronger, so nobody can read the times without also reading the warning.
2. **The timetable can't be used** (defect, copy). "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" gives no first sailing, no last sailing, and no departure minutes. A passenger can't tell whether a 6:30 or a 22:00 crossing exists, or whether to aim for :00 or :20. The last sailing is what people get stranded by. Fix: state the first and last departure from each side, plus the times past the hour, or link to a timetable. If the times really vary, say so here instead of leaving it vague.
3. **"Changes in winter" and "check the noticeboard at the landing" leave the reader to fill the gaps** (defect, copy). "Winter" doesn't say which dates. The only way to check is a noticeboard at the landing, which the passenger sees only after the trip they were trying to plan. Read either way, the result is someone walking to the quay for a sailing that no longer runs. Fix: give the dates the winter timetable applies, and point to a place the reader can check before leaving home.

**Smaller things.**
- Copy: "at no extra charge" assumes a fare the page never states. Add the fare, or say where to find it.
- Copy: the text says "north landing" and "market quay" but the winter line says only "the landing". Name the landing, or say "either landing".

**What's working.**
- It is one clear reading column with a strong headline and generous margins. Nothing competes, and the page is easy on the eye. Keep that restraint when adding the times.
- The bike paragraph is good copy. It says who does what and in what order ("wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"), so a rider knows exactly what will happen on board.
