**First read.** The eye lands on "$4.82M" in the top-left card, moves across the other two KPIs, then drops to the blue bars rising toward December, and ends on the plan table. It reads as a calm, well-built revenue dashboard for a SaaS founder or finance lead. It feels trustworthy and tidy, and also a bit like it could be anyone's dashboard.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard where an operator checks yearly revenue health at a glance and spots where it comes from.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: clear focal point, honest chart, every headline number has a period and a comparison. What gets in the way is that its own numbers don't agree. The "2025 revenue" in the plan table doesn't match the 2025 revenue in the chart next to it, and a finance viewer who spots that will stop trusting the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan table and the chart give two different 2025 revenue totals.** "Revenue by plan, 2025" totals **$4.82M**, the same as the ARR card ("Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025"). The twelve monthly bars, labelled "recognised revenue", add up by eye to about **$4.49M**. A viewer who reads the table as 2025 revenue gets a number roughly $330k too high. A viewer who notices the gap can't tell which figure is right, or whether the table is really ARR split by plan. In a revenue dashboard, numbers people repeat in board decks are where a double reading costs the most. *Defect.* The fix: name the table for what it actually holds (e.g. "ARR by plan, Dec 2025"). If it really is 2025 revenue, make its total match the chart's.
2. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** "Monthly revenue, 2025" makes the viewer work out the story from twelve bars. Revenue grew from about $312k in January to about $447k in December, roughly +43%, with small dips in April and July. That growth is the point of the page, and right now nothing says it. *Taste / common sense* (no approved source states this rule): a dashboard is for glancing, so say the takeaway. Put it in the title or a one-line caption (e.g. "Monthly revenue grew ~43% through 2025"). If the dips matter, label them on the chart.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count 8 sizes (28, 26, 20, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12 px, `type_sizes`). The KPI values at 28 px and the h1 at 26 px are almost the same size, so they read as a slip, not a choice. 16/15/14/13 sit a point apart. Keep about four: 28, 16, 14, 12. Use weight and colour for finer steps, not more sizes (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- Grid: the lower row doesn't follow the KPI cards. The chart ends at x≈960 and the plan table starts at x=1012 (`left_edges`), while the cards break at 907/925 and 945. Line up the chart and table with the card columns so the page reads as one grid (Müller-Brockmann).
- The two section headings miss each other by 4 px: "Monthly revenue, 2025" at y=231, "Revenue by plan, 2025" at y=235 (`box`). Put them on one baseline.
- Plan table numbers look like proportional digits (number left edges at 1187 vs 1189 for strings of the same length). Turn on tabular figures (Inter's `tnum`) so the figures line up in columns. Also set the Total row apart (a heavier rule above it, or a bolder label), since right now it looks like a fourth plan.
- The "Jan to Dec 2025" grey pill, top right, looks like a button but isn't one. If it's a date picker, add a chevron. If it's only a label, drop the filled pill so it doesn't invite a click.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards each give a name, a period, and a comparison ("+18% vs Dec 2024"). That is exactly what makes a headline number readable (Few). Keep this pattern for every new metric.
- The chart is honest and quiet. Bars start at zero, there's one colour for one series, gridlines are pale, the units are stated ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue"), and the source is cited. The data is the darkest ink on the chart (Wilke; Tufte).
- Colour is restrained. Greys plus one blue for data and green only for positive change. Every text contrast passes, lowest 4.74:1 on the source line (`contrast`, `p.source`).

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date pill opens a range picker, and how the KPI row and the chart/table row reflow at narrower widths.
