**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board" and then hits three blocks of capitals that look like one grey slab. Nothing in that slab stands out, so the eye scans along the top line and starts reading slowly. It reads as a plain, calm info page for ferry passengers. It looks tidy, but reading it is hard work.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or panel that tells ferry passengers how the service runs (frequency, crossing time, bikes, winter timetable) so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't do its job well. The layout is sound, but all the reading text is in capitals, so the part people came for is the slowest part to read. The copy also leaves out the times passengers need to plan around.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body is set in all capitals** (defect). All three paragraphs, eight lines in total, are in capitals. Capitals have no ascenders or descenders, so every word becomes the same rectangle. Readers lose word shapes and have to go letter by letter, and paragraphs of capitals read as shouting. Here they also flatten the useful details ("forty minutes", "twelve minutes", "no extra charge") into the same grey as everything else, so skimming for them fails. This is the opposite of what a "before you board" page needs, since people read it quickly and often on a phone at the landing. Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps": "Don't capitalize whole paragraphs"; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*: text in capitals is particularly hard to read. Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for short labels like the "PASSENGER NOTES" eyebrow, which already does this correctly.
2. **The copy names no times** (defect). This page is about a timetable, and passengers will plan around its times. Yet "between early morning and late evening", "every forty minutes" (from when?) and "in winter" (which months?) leave them guessing. A passenger who reads "late evening" as 10 pm when the last boat leaves at 8:40 is stranded. The page also sends them to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing", meaning the place they're standing by the time it's too late. Fix: give the first and last sailings, the minutes past the hour for the first departure, and the dates the winter timetable starts and ends, or link to the timetable from here.

**Smaller things.**
- Once the body is in mixed case, this goes away. Until then, the capitals are set at default spacing, which makes the slab denser. Capitals need extra letterspacing, as the eyebrow has.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clear and economical. The small, tracked, grey eyebrow, the bold dark headline and the regular body are three steps that differ in size, weight and colour. Keep that scale.
- The single flush-left column has a sensible measure (around 70 characters a line), comfortable leading, and consistent space between paragraphs. Each paragraph holds one topic: schedule, bikes, winter. That structure is right.
- The headline speaks to the reader at the moment they need it ("Before you board"). That's better than a label like "Service information".
