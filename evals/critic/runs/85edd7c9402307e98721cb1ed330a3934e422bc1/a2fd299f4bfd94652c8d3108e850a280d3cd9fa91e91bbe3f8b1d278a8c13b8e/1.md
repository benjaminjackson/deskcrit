**First read.** My eye goes straight to the tall purple Growth bar, then down to the short Starter bar beside it. The first impression is that Growth converts more than twice as well as Starter. Next I read "Trial to paid conversion by plan" and the table on the right. It looks like a calm, tidy internal analytics page. The impression it leaves, a big gap between plans, is wrong.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard view that lets product or growth people compare trial-to-paid conversion across plans for Q3 2025.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and well made, but its main chart misleads. The bar axis starts at 2.5%, which makes a 0.9-point spread look like a 2.5× gap. Anyone who decides something from this page will be deciding from a false picture.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cut-off bar axis exaggerates every difference (defect).** The y-axis starts at 2.5%, not zero.
   - **What the viewer sees:** above the baseline, Starter's bar is 0.6 points tall and Growth's is 1.5, so Growth looks two and a half times Starter. The real values are 3.1% and 4.0%, a 29% relative difference. The picture overstates the effect by roughly five times. That is Tufte's lie factor (*Visual Display*, ch. 2). Team and Enterprise are stretched the same way.
   - **Why it matters:** in a bar, people read the length of the bar, not the tick labels. The value labels above the bars don't fix this, because the eye has already made the comparison before it reads them.
   - **It also makes noise look like a finding:** Enterprise is 38 conversions out of 1,120 trials. Its rough 95% range is about ±1 point, which overlaps every other plan. The chart presents a ranking the data may not support.
   - **Fix:** start the axis at zero so the bars keep their true proportions (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 17.1; Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design", pitfall 8). If the small differences are the point, switch to dots on a zoomed scale, where a non-zero baseline is acceptable. Showing intervals would make the dots more honest still (Wilke, ch. 16).
2. **The numbers have nothing to be compared against (convention).** 4.0% for Growth could be good, bad, or normal, and the page gives no way to tell. There is no previous quarter, no target, and no overall average.
   - This is a page about a rate that someone is presumably trying to move, so the change since last quarter is what the viewer actually needs.
   - **Fix:** put the comparison next to each rate. Q2 values as a second marker per plan would work, or a reference line for the target or the blended rate of 3.4% (Few, "Common Pitfalls", pitfall 2).

**Smaller things.**
- **The title names the topic, not the finding.** "Trial to paid conversion by plan" tells me what the chart is about, not what it shows. Once the axis is honest, state the actual takeaway. For example, "Conversion sits between 3% and 4% on every plan; Growth is highest." This is my judgment: no approved source covers it.
- **The lower right of the right column is empty.** About 400 px sits blank under "Definition". It's harmless, but it's the natural place for the comparison from finding 2.

**What's working.**
- **The table backs the chart with raw counts.** The Trials and Paid columns let a careful reader check the rates; they all reconcile (286/9,210 = 3.1%, and so on). They also make the sample size visible. Keep this.
- **The definition and source are written precisely.** "Converted when the first invoice is paid" and "trials still open on 30 October count as not converted" remove exactly the ambiguity that conversion metrics usually hide. The source line gives exact dates.
- **The chart is restrained:**
  - one colour for one series
  - light gridlines
  - value labels sitting directly on the bars
  - plans in their natural tier order
  - a clear current-page state in the nav

  It's the right baseline to fix the scale on.
