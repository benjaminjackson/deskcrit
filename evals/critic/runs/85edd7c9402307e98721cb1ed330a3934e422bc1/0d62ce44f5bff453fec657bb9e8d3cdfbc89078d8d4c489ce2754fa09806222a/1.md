**First read.** The eye lands on the big red block, goes up to "Garden show gallery", then moves across the five smaller blocks in the right column and bottom row. It reads as a photo gallery from a garden show: tidy, calm and well spaced. Then you see that every "photo" is a flat block of colour with a pale circle cut off at the bottom. There is nothing to look at.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that shows photos of the displays at a garden show, so visitors can browse what was there.

**Verdict.** The layout is ready but the gallery isn't. With no real images it can't do its one job, which is to show the gardens. Second, nothing on the page can be clicked, so even real photos would only ever be seen as thin strips.

**What would move it most**
1. **No picture shows anything (defect).** All six tiles are flat colour fields with a centred pale disc clipped by the bottom edge. A visitor can't tell the rose arch from the bean rows except by the caption. In a gallery the pictures are the content, so right now the page delivers nothing. This only becomes fine if these are placeholders waiting for real photos. Put the actual photos in and judge the page again with them, because everything else here depends on what they look like.
2. **The frames are very wide strips (taste).** The small tiles are about 377 × 144 px and the hero about 769 × 307 px, both roughly 2.6 : 1. Garden subjects are often tall: an arch over a gate, a glasshouse, an orchard path running away from you. A strip that wide will cut off the top of the arch or the end of the path and leave a band of middle. I'd make the frames closer to 3 : 2 or 4 : 3, at least for the hero, and choose crops that keep each subject whole.
3. **You can't open a photo (defect, from the facts file).** The facts file finds no links or buttons on the page (`targets: []`). A small tile 144 px tall can show what a garden is, but not what makes it worth seeing. If clicking really does nothing, the page is a contact sheet with no prints. Make each tile open a larger view.
4. **The headline doesn't say which show (copy).** "Garden show gallery" could sit on any site. The visitor can't tell whose show it was, where or when, so they can't tell if this is the show they went to or heard about. Name it, with the place and the year. The captions show you can write specifically ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Tomatoes in late July"). The headline should be that specific.

**What's working.**
- The grid holds firmly. There are three columns with 14 px gutters, and every block starts at one of three left edges (60, 451 and 843 px). The hero fills exactly two columns and two rows, and the bottom-row captions line up across all three columns. Keep this structure when the real photos go in.
- Type levels are few and clear. The 64 px page title, the 40 px hero title, and small tile titles in 15 px bold over 13 px grey captions each do a different job. Contrast is well above minimums: 15.8 : 1 for titles and 6.11 : 1 for captions.
- Each caption sits 6 px under its own image and 13–14 px above the next one, so it clearly belongs to the picture above it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles open a larger view on click or tap. The facts file suggests they don't; check in a browser.
- How the 3-column grid reflows on a phone.
