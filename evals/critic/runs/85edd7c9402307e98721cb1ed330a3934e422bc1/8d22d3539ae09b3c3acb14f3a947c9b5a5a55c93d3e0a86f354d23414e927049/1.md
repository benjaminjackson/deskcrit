## Design crit: spring ferry timetable hero

**First read.** The eye lands on the huge bold four-line headline and stays there. Then it drops to the short paragraph, and only after that notices the small grey "Spring timetable" label above. It reads as the top of a timetable page for a river ferry. It feels calm and plain, but generic: one grotesque typeface, near-black on white, no colour. The eye ends in about 200 px of empty space with no times in it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page giving a ferry's spring timetable to people who want to know when they can cross.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page is labelled a timetable, but it shows no departure time, first sailing or last sailing. The biggest headline on the screen spends four lines on a vague phrase ("by daylight") where a rider needs a clock time.

**What would move it most**

1. **The timetable has no times, and the copy leaves out the facts riders act on** (defect). Under the label "Spring timetable", the screen gives "every forty minutes" with no start point, "by daylight" with no hours, and "spring through early summer" with no dates. A rider can't work out from this when the next boat leaves, when the last one goes, or whether it runs in May. "Every forty minutes" from when? 7:00, 7:40, or 7:15, 7:55? Daylight hours move by more than an hour across spring, so "by daylight" will be read differently in March and in June, and someone will turn up for a boat that has stopped running. The paragraph also only covers north landing to market quay, so the return trip is a guess. A heading should name what its section holds (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.6), and here it names something that isn't on screen. If the table sits just below the fold, this is fine only if the first sailing, last sailing and date range show without scrolling. The fix: put those three facts in the first screen, in figures, for both directions. For example: "First boat 7:00, last boat 19:40, every 40 minutes, both directions, 1 March – 15 June". Then let the full table follow.

2. **The headline is a description set at poster size, and it pushes the useful content off the screen** (taste, argued). Four lines at about 80 px use the upper half of a 1280×800 screen to say what the label above already says ("Spring timetable", then "spring through early summer"). The size is right for a short title. It's wrong for a sentence that contains the qualifiers riders need ("by daylight", "early summer"), because at that size those words read as decoration rather than as facts. Cut the headline to a name, such as "Quayside ferry, spring timetable". Move the hours and dates into the facts line from point 1, where they can be exact. The page keeps its one strong focal point and gains room for the times.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: "early / summer" breaks a phrase across lines and leaves "summer" alone on the last line. Break it at "spring through / early summer", or shorten the headline as in point 2.
- Paragraph: "weather." sits alone on the last line. Rewrap or tighten the paragraph so at least two words carry over.

**What's working.**
- One clear entry point. The headline clearly outranks everything else, and the three levels (label, headline, body) have big steps between them, with weight doing work as well as size. Keep that.
- The paragraph reads comfortably: about 68 characters per line, with open line spacing. Keep this measure when you add text.
- Everything hangs off one left edge at 120 px, which gives the page a quiet, orderly feel that suits a public timetable.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the timetable starts just below the fold. Scroll the live page to check how far down the first time appears.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 7 yes, 1 no (heading names what its section holds)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 13 yes
Text: 30 lines, 28 yes, 2 no (paragraphs and headings break cleanly; headings break at phrase boundaries)
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: skipped, none
Charts and data: skipped, none
Interface: skipped, no controls
