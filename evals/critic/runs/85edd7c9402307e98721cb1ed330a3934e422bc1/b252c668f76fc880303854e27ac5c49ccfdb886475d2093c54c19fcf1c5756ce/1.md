**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue title "Planning a vegetable bed", then drops down the three blue subheads, which read like a table of contents. It's clearly a short beginner's how-to on a gardening page. It feels calm, orderly, and easy, and also anonymous: it could be any docs site. The bottom third of the screen is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a short web article that walks a first-time gardener through setting up a vegetable bed, read on a desktop screen.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order of importance is clear, the type scale is clean, and the text is easy to read. The one thing that noticeably costs the reader is that the paragraphs run too wide. Everything else is polish.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraph lines are too long.** *Convention (Bringhurst).* All four paragraphs run 89 to 93 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `main.doc > p:nth-of-type(1–3)`) in an 800 px column. That is well past Bringhurst's 45 to 75, with 66 as the ideal (*Elements of Typographic Style* §2.1.2). With two-line paragraphs the cost is small today: the eye travels a long way back to find the second line. But this is a how-to that will get longer, and at this width every added section reads worse. The 1.55 line height (`line_height_ratio`) partly makes up for it, so keep that. Narrow the text column to about 600–640 px at 18 px Inter so lines land around 65–70 characters. The headings can keep the full width or not; it doesn't matter.

**Smaller things.**
- The 48 px title (`h1`) looks set at the same letter spacing as the text, so it reads slightly loose for its size. Tighten its tracking a touch (Bringhurst §2.1.1; Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80, though Lupton warns against heavy negative tracking).
- The apostrophe in "last year's heap" is a straight typewriter mark, not a curly one. This is common sense, not a sourced rule: switch to the true apostrophe (’) site-wide.
- The column sits at x=160 with 320 px empty on the right, so it is neither centred nor clearly pinned to a side. Once it's narrower, decide which it is. *Taste.*
- Copy: "six hours of sun" leaves the reader to supply "a day". Add it. This is the kind of figure a beginner will act on.
- Copy, *taste*: the page looks like a default template (Inter, link-blue headings, white page), so nothing about it says gardening or says whose advice this is. That's acceptable for plain reference text, but it gives the reader no reason to remember or come back to the site.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clean:** three sizes, 48 / 30 / 18 px (`type_sizes`), with big steps between them, and the headings also differ by bold weight and blue colour. Each level has an obvious job. Keep exactly three.
- **Spacing does the grouping:** each subhead sits 8 px above its paragraph and 26 px below the previous one (`spacing`), so every heading clearly belongs to the text it introduces. Keep this ratio as the page grows.
- **The text is easy to read:** body text at 15.8:1 contrast and headings at 6.67:1 (`contrast`), 18 px body text, everything on one left edge at x=160 (`left_edges`). Nothing here needs fixing.
