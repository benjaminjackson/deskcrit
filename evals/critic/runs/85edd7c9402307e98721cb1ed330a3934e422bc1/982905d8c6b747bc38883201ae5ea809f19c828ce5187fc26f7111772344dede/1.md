**First read.** Your eye lands on "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", drops to the orange "Start your free week" button, and then catches on the grey "Keep your streak going" box right under it. That box looks like part of the offer, but it's a message for someone already using the app. The teal chat-bubble panel on the right gets one glance and adds nothing. The page reads as a calm, competent language-app landing page that could belong to almost any brand.

**What it's for.** You didn't say, so I'm assuming it's the desktop hero of a marketing page. Its job is to get adults who have never used Parlo to start the free week.

**Verdict.** The headline and the button do their jobs. Most of the rest of the page is weak. The biggest problem is the streak box: it sits where the reasons to click should be and talks to a returning user instead of a newcomer. The illustration also adds nothing to the headline.

**What would move it most**
1. **The streak box talks to the wrong person, in the most valuable spot on the page (defect).** The grey panel at 56,390 sits 28 px under the button, so it reads as part of the call to action. A first-time visitor has no streak, so "Keep your streak going" is an in-app nudge on a sign-up page, and the heading doesn't say what the box holds. The statistic inside is the real reason to sign up, but it's worded loosely. "Finish the course twice as often" leaves open: twice as often as whom? And what does finishing mean to someone deciding today? A newcomer has to rebuild that meaning, and many won't bother. Fix: either remove the box, or rewrite it as proof aimed at the newcomer. Give it a heading that says what the number means for them, and state the comparison in full ("Learners who practise five days in a row are twice as likely to finish as those who don't"). Then move it out from under the button so it doesn't compete with the ask.

2. **The illustration repeats the headline and doesn't sit on the page's grid (taste, plus convention).** Two generic speech bubbles just restate "conversation". They don't show the thing that sets the product apart, a tutor that listens and gives "feedback on each word" (step 2). They also give no hint of the four languages. The panel is the second-largest thing on the screen, so that's a lot of space for no information. The headline and image should make one idea together, with neither repeating the other (my judgement as a designer). The panel also takes its edges from nothing else on the page. It ends at x≈1076, while the nav and the third step column end at 1224, so there's a 148 px dead strip at the right. And the two-column hero split doesn't line up with the three-column steps row below (461, 867). It looks like it was placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). Fix: show the actual product moment, such as a real exchange in Spanish with per-word feedback marked. Set the panel to the page's columns so its right edge meets 1224.

3. **The copy that decides the click leaves questions open (defect, because it's what the viewer commits to).**
   - "Start your free week" is a specific ask, which is good. But it says nothing about what happens on day eight: does a card get charged, or does access just stop? Someone who reads it as no commitment and then gets billed will hold that against you.
   - "A tutor that listens" can be read as a human tutor or as software. The two readings set very different expectations about price and experience.
   - The ask only invites the viewer to take the promise on trust. Something that lets them judge the value for themselves would be stronger, such as a one-minute speaking sample they can try right now.

   Fix: add one plain line under the button about what happens after the free week. Say what the tutor is. Consider an ask that lets them hear the feedback before committing.

**Smaller things.**
- **Muddled type scale.** I count five sizes: 42, 20, 18, 17 and 15 px (`type_sizes`). The 18 px lede, the 17 px step and notice headings, and the 20 px logo are too close to read as separate levels (Bringhurst). Keep 42 / 18 / 15 and let weight separate the headings from body text (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- **Button text contrast.** White on #d9480f is 4.3:1 (`contrast`, `span.btn`). At 18 px bold it falls just short of the large-text threshold, so it needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3). Darken the orange a step.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks" is a concrete, time-bound promise in the viewer's own terms. It clearly dominates at 42 px bold, with lines held together at 1.15 line height. Keep it.
- **The lede.** It runs 67 characters per line at 10.2:1 contrast and names the four languages, so a visitor knows straight away whether their language is offered.
- **The three steps.** "Listen / Speak / Use it" makes the method concrete in one line each, and the steps sit evenly on a clean three-column row. It's the clearest content on the page.
