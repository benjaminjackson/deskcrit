**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops into the paragraph, slides past three loose lines, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top section of a ferry company's homepage. It feels calm, tidy and well set, but it gives no sense of place. There's no boat, no bay and no harbour, and the right half of the screen is empty, so it looks as if a picture failed to load.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the top section of a local ferry's homepage, meant to get day-trippers and commuters to the timetable and onto a boat.

**Verdict.** The type is clean and the order of reading is right, so it does the basic job. But the page sells a view and gives the viewer nothing to look at, and the lines a rider would act on (when, from where, to where, how much) are left vague. The biggest problem is the empty right half. It makes the page look unfinished, and the piece looks like a template that could belong to any ferry company.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half is empty, and the promise of the page goes unshown.** All the content sits in a 640 px column starting at x=160 (`box`, every text block). That leaves about 480 px of blank white on the right, at the same height as the headline. A designed empty space would have a shape and tension. This reads as a hole, as if an image never loaded. Meanwhile the headline and paragraph sell an experience ("calm", "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck") that the viewer has to imagine. All the visual weight sits on the left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"). And because nothing shows this bay or this boat, the page could belong to any ferry anywhere. Fill the right side with the thing being sold: a photo of the deck on the water, or a simple map of the crossing showing both landings and "12 min". That would counterweight the type and make the headline something the viewer can see. If the empty right half is a deliberate choice, it needs a size and shape that look chosen, not leftover. *Defect (the blank band reads as broken); the case for an image is taste.*

2. **The copy leaves the viewer to fill in the facts they would act on.** "Boats leave the north landing every forty minutes from early morning until late evening." A rider plans around the first and last boat, and "early morning" and "late evening" could mean 6:00 or 8:00, 21:00 or 23:30. Someone who guesses wrong misses the last boat home. "Cross the bay" never says where to. The paragraph names the north landing but not where the boat lands, so the viewer can't tell whether this trip is the one they need. There's no fare, either, although "Bicycles travel free" suggests there is one for people. Put in the real times ("first boat 6:40, last 22:20"), both landings, and the fare. The button is good as far as it goes ("See the timetable" names one exact thing). It would be stronger still if the page answered the viewer's real question on the spot, for example "Next boat 14:20 from North Landing", with the timetable as the follow-up. *Defect: these are times and places people hold a ferry company to.*

**Smaller things.**
- The list doesn't read as a list. The three features are 18 px against the paragraph's 20 px (`font_size`, `ul.points > li`, `p.lead`), in the same weight and colour, with no markers. That's one size step too close to look intended (Dair on weak contrast; Bringhurst §3.1.1 on scales). Set the list clearly apart in weight or with markers, or set it in the same size as the paragraph.
- Type scale: I count four sizes (52, 20, 18 and 15 px, `type_sizes`). 20 and 18 collide, so drop one and keep three levels plus the 15 px kicker.
- The headline breaks between "twelve" and "calm minutes", splitting the number from its unit. That's the exact claim the headline makes (Bringhurst §2.4.6). Rebreak it so "twelve calm minutes" stays on one line, for example "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes".
- "Seats on the open deck" repeats what the paragraph already said. Swap it for a fact the viewer doesn't yet have, such as the fare or accessibility.

**What's working.**
- The headline is concrete and makes a promise ("twelve calm minutes"). Most hero headlines are vaguer. Keep that specificity and carry it into the rest of the copy.
- The reading is well set. The paragraph runs 64 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `p.lead`) at 1.55 line height, and all text is #1f2328 on white at 15.8:1 contrast. Everything hangs on one left edge at x=160.
- There's one clear primary action. The button is 209×55 px with white on blue at 6.67:1 (`a.button`), and the blue kicker gives the brand a quiet colour that the button picks up.
