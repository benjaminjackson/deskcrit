**First read.** The eye lands on three big white headlines on three loud colour slabs and moves left to right, starting with the warm orange. In a second or two it reads as a public pool's "what we offer" section: adult lessons, lane swimming, family sessions. It feels bright and friendly. Then the eye drops into the bottom half of each card, finds nothing but colour, and leaves without anything to do.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a section of a local pool's website that should help an adult pick one of three sessions and then book it or check when it runs.

**Verdict.** The top half of each card is good, but the section fails at its job: you can see what's on offer and can't act on any of it. There are no times you can plan around, no price and no next step, and the space where those belong is about 300 px of empty colour in every card.

**What would move it most**
1. **No next step, and the cards are padded to hide it (defect).** The text in each card ends at about y=331 (`box`, `section.oN > p`), but the cards run to y=632. The lower half of all three is solid colour doing nothing. A swimmer who decides "I want the Tuesday class" has nowhere to go: no button, no link, no time, no price. "Join the lanes" is an instruction the page gives no way to carry out. It also leaves facts missing that people act on: "Tuesday evenings" at what time, "every morning from six" until when, and what any of it costs. The fix: fill that space with the ask. Give each card one exact action from the swimmer's own week, such as "Book a place in Tuesday's 7 pm class", "See today's lane times" or "Sunday family hour, 2–4 pm: book a slot", plus the one fact each person needs to decide: time and price. Then let the cards shrink to their content, or keep the height because it now holds something.
2. **Three vivid colours at equal strength fill about 60% of the frame (convention).** Orange `#c2410c`, green `#15803d` and purple `#7e22ce` each cover a 360×560 slab. That makes three saturated hues at the same area and strength, and Itten names that as the failure case for a three-colour scheme. Because all of the colour is spent on backgrounds, nothing is left to mark an action once you add one. A button on any of these cards will have to fight its own background. Giving equal weight to three equal options is fair, but the equal options don't need equal colour at full strength. Following Refactoring UI and Itten: use one lead colour, or fill the cards with light tints or deep shades of each hue, and keep the full-strength colour for the action you add in point 1. As a matter of taste, nothing in orange, green and purple suggests water. It could be a gym, a library or a bakery. One water photo or a blue lead colour would tell people where they are before they read a word.

**Smaller things.**
- Heading line breaks: "Learn to / swim" splits the verb phrase and "Join the / lanes" strands the article. Break them as "Learn / to swim" and "Join / the lanes", or size them so each phrase stays whole. This is common sense; no checked source covers breaks in headings.
- The row of cards is 20 px off centre: 56 px of margin on the left against 96 px on the right (cards end at x=1184 on a 1280 px page). That gap is too small to look deliberate and too big to miss, so the page tips left. Centre the row or align it to the page's real grid.
- White space sits in two dead bands, the empty card bottoms and a 168 px white strip below the cards (72 px above), instead of in one deliberate area. Fixing point 1 mostly solves this.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and talks to the reader: "with a coach in the water beside you", "three speeds to choose from", "floats and toys for the children". Each blurb names who it's for and what they'll get, which most leisure-centre pages don't. Keep that voice when you add times and prices.
- The type is clean and well judged: two sizes, 52 px bold over 20 px regular (`type_sizes`), with heading line spacing at 1.05 so each two-line heading holds as one unit. Each level is obvious at a glance, and white text passes AA contrast on all three colours (5.02–6.98, `contrast`).
- The three cards share one structure, one gutter (24 px) and one left inset, so they read as three equal choices.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are clickable. If the whole card is a link, nothing shows it, so the point 1 fix still applies.
- Whether a section heading or the pool's name sits above this crop. As captured, nothing says where these sessions happen.
