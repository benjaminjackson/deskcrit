**First read.** The eye lands on "Low-light houseplants", then drops to the row of four teal "Add to cart" bars, which are the strongest colour on the page, then moves to the prices. It reads as a tidy plant-shop category page, calm and competent, but it could be any shop's page. The four plant pictures look like one icon redrawn four times, so nothing tells me which plant is which.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a category page that helps someone with a dim room choose one of four plants and add it to the cart.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and doesn't get in the way, but it fails at the choice it exists for. The pictures and the card text give no reason to pick one plant over another, so the shopper is left choosing on price.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show the plants (defect).** All four images are the same flat drawing: a terracotta pot with two or three green leaves. A snake plant has stiff upright blades, a ZZ plant has glossy leaflets on arching stems, and a pothos trails, but none of that shows here. The ZZ and pothos drawings are almost twins. The pots are all one size, even though the cast iron plant comes in an 8 in pot and the others in 6 in. On a plant shop the picture is the product. Someone comparing four plants for a dark corner wants to know what each looks like and how big it gets, and these images answer neither, so they take up the largest area on each card and add nothing to the decision. If these are placeholders, the page isn't finished. If they are the final art, they still need to look like the plants. The fix is real photos, or drawings specific enough to tell the four apart (leaf shape, habit, trailing versus upright), shown at a consistent scale so pot size and plant size come across. This is common sense; no approved source covers it.
2. **The headline makes a promise the cards never return to (copy finding).** "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window" is good, specific copy, and it is why the shopper is on this page. Each card then gives only pot size, growth and price: nothing about how little light it tolerates or what sets it apart. The page promises a comparison on light, then shows a comparison on price. Add one short line per card that ties back to the promise, says who it's for, and uses "you", such as "Survives a north-facing hallway; water monthly" or "Trails off a shelf; tells you when it's thirsty". Then each plant has a reason to exist besides being $18 or $38.

**Smaller things.**
- Product name (about 18 px bold) and price (about 20 px bold) look only a step apart in size, at the same weight and colour, so they read like an accident rather than two levels (Dair: a contrast must be obvious; Bringhurst on scales). Either make them the same size and set the price apart by colour or weight, or open up a real gap between the two sizes.
- The top nav doesn't show that you're in Houseplants: all four links look identical and there's no breadcrumb (Nielsen heuristic 1, visibility of system status). Mark the current section.

**What's working.**
- The cards keep a strict, repeating order of name, specs, price, button, on one grid with matching baselines across all four. That makes them easy to scan side by side. Keep that order when you add the light line.
- The palette is restrained: neutral greys, one teal for actions, terracotta and green only in the pictures. Colour points to the action without shouting.
- The specs use plain units the shopper can act on ("6 in pot, grows to 2 ft").

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover, focus and pressed states on the "Add to cart" buttons, and whether the Cart (0) count updates when you add a plant.
