**First read.** The eye lands on the 60 px headline "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the grey lede, then reaches two teal buttons that seem to say the same thing and stops, unsure which to press. Then it slides off into a big empty right half and bottom third. This reads as a trip-planning SaaS homepage: calm, tidy, warm cream and deep teal. It also feels unfinished, as if an image or demo was meant to sit on the right and never arrived.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the first screen of a marketing page for a group-trip planner, and its job is to make a first-time visitor understand the product and start signing up.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is good, but the screen doesn't close the sale. It asks for the click twice with the same words, gives no picture of what the product does, and leaves half the frame empty. The biggest problem is the doubled call to action: the one place the visitor is meant to decide is where they hesitate.

**What would move it most**

1. **The two buttons do the same thing.** "Get started" (filled) and "Start now" (outlined) sit side by side at y=444. They mean the same action. The styling ranks them correctly, but a visitor who sees two different buttons assumes two different outcomes. They stop to work out what the difference is, find none, and the page looks careless at the moment it asks for commitment. This is a **defect** (Nielsen heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Fix: keep one button and remove its twin, or give the second button a different job, such as "See how it works" or "See pricing".

2. **Nothing shows the product, and the right half is empty.** Every element sits in a single column on the left, about 700 px of the 1280 px width. The area right of x≈700 and below y≈500 holds nothing. The headline promises "the whole trip in one place", but the viewer never sees that place: no itinerary, no shared plan, no group vote. So they have to take the claim on trust. With everything in the top left, the frame also feels lopsided and empty rather than deliberately spacious. This is a **defect** for a sales page (Krug: the page should make its value obvious within seconds). If the empty space was meant to hold an image that didn't load, check that in a live browser. Fix: put a picture of the product's output in the right half, such as one trip's flights, stays, and day plans together, with friends' names voting on the choices. That one image shows the claim and fixes the balance.

3. **The words could be on any travel app's page, and the ask is vague.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" is the category's standard pitch. The sharpest line on the page is in the lede: "without a dozen chat threads". It names a pain the reader already has, measured against what they use today. The button then says "Get started", so the visitor still doesn't know what happens when they click: an account form, a blank trip, a demo? This is **taste**, argued: the chat-thread comparison is the reason to act, and it sits in grey 20 px body text. Fix: lead with that contrast in the headline, said to the reader ("Your group chat isn't a trip plan"). Make the button name one concrete thing, such as "Start a trip with your group" or "Plan [a weekend away]", so the visitor can picture the click.

**Smaller things.**
- Type sizes come in close pairs: wordmark 22 px against lede 20 px, and buttons 17 px against nav 15 px (`font_size`, `span.brand`, `p.lede`, `span.btn.primary`, `nav > span`). Each has a different job and weight, so this reads fine, but bringing the nav and buttons to one size would make the scale cleaner.
- Once the second button is fixed, the eye has nowhere to go after the buttons: nothing near the fold, such as a hint of the next section or social proof, invites a scroll. Common sense for a landing page.

**What's working.**
- The headline is set well: 60 px bold at 1.08 line height (`h1`), dark #1d2a2e on cream at 13:1 contrast. It holds together as one unit and clearly dominates.
- The lede reads comfortably: 20 px at 63 characters per line with 1.5 leading (`p.lede`), at 6.99:1 contrast. That's right in Bringhurst's 45 to 75 character range.
- The palette is restrained: one cream ground, two dark neutrals, one teal kept for the wordmark and the action. Everything aligns on one 80 px left edge. Keep all of this when you add the image.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is the design or an image that failed to load: check in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the buttons and nav, and how the single column reflows on a phone.
