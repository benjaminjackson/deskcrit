**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold black headline "Notes for ferry passengers," then slides into grey text that looks switched off, like placeholder copy or a disabled state. It reads as a short notice for people taking a river ferry. It looks calm and tidy, but washed out, as if only the title is meant to be read.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web notice telling ferry passengers how the service runs and what to check before travelling, read on a desktop screen.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. Every sentence a passenger needs is set in pale grey at 2.54:1 contrast, so the only crisp thing on the page is the title, which tells them nothing. The second problem is that the one line that changes what a passenger should do is the smallest and quietest line on the page.

**What would move it most**

1. **The text everyone needs to read is too faint.** *Defect.* All three paragraphs are #9ca3af on white at 2.54:1 (`contrast`, `main.article > p.faintable:nth-of-type(1)`, `…(2)`, `p.note.faintable`). WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 asks for 4.5:1 at these sizes (19 px and 16 px regular). That puts this page at the very bottom of the usual range of this defect (2.5 to 4.4). People reading on a phone at a landing in daylight, or older passengers, will struggle or give up. Grey this light also says "inactive" or "unimportant," so the page tells the viewer the facts don't matter. Fix: darken the text, or change its background, until it reaches at least 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3). If you want the body softer than the title, use a dark grey rather than a pale one. The headline already stands apart through size and weight, so the body doesn't need to fade for the hierarchy to work.

2. **The warning that changes what a passenger does is the quietest line.** *Defect (hierarchy and copy).* "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel." is 16 px against the body's 19 px (`font_size`, `p.note.faintable`), in the same grey. A 3 px step doesn't read as a separate level (Bringhurst, 3.1.1: a modest set of distinct intervals). It just looks like a slightly smaller paragraph, and its job comes across as "footnote." But this is the line that decides whether someone misses a boat. Fix: either set it at body size, or make it stand out through weight or a clear marker such as a bold lead-in. Consider putting it first, since it governs everything below it.

3. **The copy leaves out the facts a passenger acts on.** *Defect (copy).* "Between early morning and late evening" and "timetables change in winter" don't give a time or a date. A reader can't tell when the first and last boats leave, or which months count as "winter." One person will read "early morning" as 6:00 and another as 8:00, and the cost of a wrong guess is a missed or non-existent crossing. Fix: give the first and last departure times and the dates the winter timetable starts and ends. Say where else a passenger can check it besides "the noticeboard at the landing," since by the time they read that they're already there.
