**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the grey paragraph, and stops on the dark teal "Get started" button. Then it drifts right into an empty cream field and finds nothing. It reads as the landing page for a trip-planning app. It feels calm, tidy and finished on the left, and unfinished overall: the right half and the bottom third look as if something failed to load.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the desktop home-page hero for Wayfold, a group trip-planning product. Its job is to get people who plan trips with friends or family to understand why it helps and to start a trip.

**Verdict.** The type and the buttons are well made, but the hero doesn't make its case. It shows nothing of the product. All the weight sits in the top-left of an otherwise empty screen. The one real reason to choose Wayfold, getting a group to agree, is buried in the grey paragraph under a headline any trip planner could use.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half and the bottom of the screen are empty, so the viewer is never shown the product.** The area right of x≈700 and below y≈500, more than half the screen, holds nothing. The viewer is asked to "Get started" on a tool they have only read about. There is no itinerary, no shared plan, nothing that shows group agreement. The page also tips: all the visual weight is in the upper-left block, with nothing on the right to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"). This empty space doesn't read as a chosen pause. It reads as a missing picture. **Defect.** If a hero image or product shot is meant to load here, check it in a live browser: the measured file lists only text blocks, so nothing was there when the page was rendered. If nothing is meant to go there, put the product in that space: one real trip plan with flights, a stay and a day plan, plus signs of a group, such as several people's avatars, a vote, or "3 of 4 agreed". That gives the headline its proof and gives the layout its counterweight.

2. **The headline is generic, and the real promise sits in the subhead.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" could sit on any trip planner or travel app, so a stranger learns nothing about what Wayfold does differently. The specific, believable claim is in the 20 px grey paragraph (`p.lede`): "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads". That sets Wayfold against something the viewer already has and already finds painful, their group chat, and it's the strongest line on the page. **Taste, argued:** the headline gets read and the paragraph gets skimmed, so the differentiator belongs in the 60 px line. Lead with the group-agreement idea and let the subhead list what's kept together.

3. **"Get started" doesn't say what happens next.** The primary button is styled correctly (one filled teal button, one outlined secondary), but its label is a category, not an action. The viewer can't tell whether it means signing up, a form, or a payment. **Defect, mildly:** a vague ask slows the decision on the one control that matters. Name the first concrete step in the viewer's terms, such as "Start a trip and invite your group", so the click is something they can picture doing tonight with their own trip.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place", which strands "in" at the end of line 1 and splits the phrase "in one place". Break it before "in", or let the rewritten headline fall on a phrase boundary. This is common sense: no checked source covers heading breaks.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean. The headline is 60 px bold (`h1`) and dominates without competition. Below it come the brand at 22, the subhead at 20, the buttons at 17 and the nav at 15, each with a clear job. Keep it this tight when you add the product visual.
- The subhead is easy to read: 63 characters per line at 1.5 line height, 6.99:1 contrast (`p.lede`), with the headline at 1.08 line height so it holds together as one unit. The page has one strong left edge at x=80 shared by the brand, headline, subhead and buttons.
- The button pair is right: one filled primary, one outlined secondary in the same teal, both 52 px tall. The restrained cream and teal palette suits a tool people trust with money and dates.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is a hero image or animation that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the nav items and the two buttons.
