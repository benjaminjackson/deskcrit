**First read.** The eye goes to "How can we help?" on the navy band, drops to the white search field, then jumps sideways to the two coloured icons: the credit card on Billing and the green shield on Privacy. Those are the only colour on a quiet navy-and-grey page. It reads at once as a product help centre: calm, tidy, familiar. The jump to those two cards is the one thing the eye does that the page didn't intend.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the help-centre home for a SaaS product's customers. A visitor with a problem should search, or pick the right topic, within seconds.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The search-led hero and the eight clearly worded topics are right for a help centre. The biggest thing in the way is that two of the eight topic icons are full-colour clip-art among thin line icons. That gives Billing and Privacy a weight they haven't earned, and makes the set look put together from different sources.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two icons break the set and pull the eye.** On cards 3 (Billing and invoices) and 5 (Privacy and security), the icons are filled and multicoloured: blue, orange and yellow for the card, and a shaded green gradient for the shield with its check mark. The other six are thin navy line drawings. Being the only saturated colour on the page, these two become the second thing anyone looks at after the search field. So a visitor scanning for "Troubleshooting" or "Account" gets pulled to two topics that aren't more important. The page also stops looking like one product and starts looking assembled. Defect: it misdirects the scan and breaks the consistency of a repeated element (Nielsen heuristic 4, consistency and standards). Fix: redraw both as line icons with the same stroke weight, caps and navy colour as the other six. If any topic really should stand out, choose it on purpose and mark it the same way.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale.** The measured file lists six sizes: 36, 20, 18, 16, 15 and 14 px. The logo (20), the "Browse by topic" heading (18) and the card titles (16) are close in size, all bold, and blur together. The navigation (15) and card text (14) are a pixel apart. A scale of 36, 18, 16 and 14, with weight and colour doing the finer work, would carry the page (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1).
- **Lone last words.** Four card descriptions end with one word alone on the second line: "receipts.", "data.", "where." and "messages." (cards 3, 5, 7, 8). Trim or rephrase each to fit one line, or balance the two lines (Bringhurst, section 2.4.2).
- **Search field.** The field relies on placeholder text alone and has no magnifier or button, so it reads as a white bar more than as a field you can type into. Add a search icon inside the field, which also serves as its visible label.
- **Two words for one thing.** The subline says "search the guides" and the nav says "Guides", but the field says "Search for articles". Choose one word so visitors don't wonder whether these are two collections.
- **"Orders and shipping" doesn't fit the rest.** Every other topic is about a workspace tool: team invites, integrations, sync errors. If the product doesn't ship physical goods, this topic is out of place. If it does, the topic is fine.
- **No current location.** Nothing in the nav (Guides, Community, Status, Contact us) shows which section you are in. If this home page is "Guides", mark it.

**What's working.**
- **Search comes first.** The navy band, the large serif question and the wide white field make search the obvious first move, which is right for most help-centre visits. Keep the hierarchy, and keep the serif only on that one line.
- **The descriptions name real tasks.** "Track a delivery or start a return" and "Fix sync problems and error messages" use the visitor's own words, so they can pick a card without opening it. Write any new topics the same way.
- **The grid is clean.** Four equal columns with 20 px gutters, all text aligned to shared left edges, and every text block well above contrast minimums (card text at 5.82:1, placeholder at 4.6:1). Nothing needs fixing here.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards and nav items show hover and focus states. The measured file found no tap targets, and the nav items are coded as `span`s, which suggests they may not be real links. Check in a browser that they are keyboard-focusable links.
- What sits below the fold, such as popular articles or a contact path besides the nav link.
