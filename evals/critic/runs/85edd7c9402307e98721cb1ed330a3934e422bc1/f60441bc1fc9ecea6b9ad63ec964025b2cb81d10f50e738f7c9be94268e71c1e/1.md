**First read.** The eye goes straight to the three big numbers (3.8%, €64.20, 12.4k) and reads them left to right. It then drops to the orange bars and stops on the tall Saturday bar and the short Sunday one. This is the owner's dashboard for a small bakery that sells online and by subscription. It feels calm and tidy, and a little stock.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a quick health check for the shop owner, showing how sales are doing and which days carry the delivery load.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy, grid, and chart are sound, and there is no structural problem. What holds it back is the wording: the time periods don't line up, and the chart shows a pattern without saying what it is.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards and the chart may cover different days, and nothing says so** (defect, in the copy). Two cards say "Last 30 days", a rolling window. The subscriber card says "As of 30 September 2026". The chart's source line says "orders delivered in September 2026", a fixed month. They only line up if the page is viewed on 1 October. On any other day, the €64.20 and the bars cover different days, but they sit together as if they describe the same period. An owner who checks the Saturday spike against order value or conversion will be comparing figures from different date ranges without knowing it. The comparisons don't match either: "vs previous 30 days" in two cards, "vs 31 August" in the third. That one is defensible, because subscribers are a count at a point in time, but next to the others it reads as inconsistent. Fix: put the date range on the page once, near "Shop overview" (for example "1–30 September 2026"), and make every card and the chart use that range. Or, if the cards are meant to roll, make the chart roll with them and say so.
2. **The chart title names the topic, not the finding** (taste). "Orders by delivery day" leaves the owner to work out the two things that matter. Saturday is the busiest day, at about 1,130 orders. Sunday drops to about 380, roughly a third of Saturday. Thursday to Friday is the biggest single jump. Those are the numbers that decide staffing and baking quantities. A title that says it, such as "Saturday is your busiest delivery day; Sunday is a third of it", answers the question in one look. Marking the peak would help too: a value label on the Saturday and Sunday bars, or the other bars toned down. With seven bars in the same orange, every bar looks equally important. The canon's source for this rule isn't on the approved list, so treat it as my judgement: a dashboard someone opens every week should tell them the news, not only show the data.

**Smaller things.**
- "+0.4 pts" under the conversion rate: "pts" is analyst shorthand that a bakery owner may not know. Write "+0.4 percentage points", or "up from 3.4%", which also gives them the old figure.

**What's working.**
- **The number setting in the KPI cards.** The figure is large and bold, and the unit (%, €, k) is smaller and grey, so the number comes first and the unit doesn't compete with it. Each figure has its period on one line and its change on the next, with an arrow and a sign as well as green, so the change doesn't rely on colour alone. Keep this pattern on the other tabs.
- **One clean grid.** The page edge is at 48 px. The text inside the cards and the chart title share one inner edge at about 74 px, and all the gutters match. As a result the page reads as one calm surface.
- **An honest chart.** The bars start at zero, the ticks are evenly spaced (0, 300, 600, 900, 1,200), the gridlines are faint, there is one colour, the days are in weekday order, and the source is named. Nothing to fix in how it's drawn.

- Focus and hover states on the navigation tabs.
