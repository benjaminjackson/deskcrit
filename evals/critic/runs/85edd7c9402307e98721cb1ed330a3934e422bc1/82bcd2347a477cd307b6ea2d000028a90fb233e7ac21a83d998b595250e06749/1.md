**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold black title, then down into the body. It reads as a calm, well-made help article for whoever pays the bill. It feels plain and trustworthy. Only on reading do you notice that the page never tells you how to change the card.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article for an account owner or billing admin who wants to swap the card on the subscription. It has to get them to do that quickly and tell them what happens next.

**Verdict.** The page looks good but doesn't do its main job. The title promises a task, and the body gives only rules and edge cases around it. There are no steps and no way to get to the billing page. The visual system is sound; the content isn't.

**What would move it most**
1. **The article never says how to update the card** (defect). "Update the card that pays for your subscription" sets up a how-to. The first sentence says only "from the billing page": no link, no menu path, no steps. Then it moves straight on to open invoices, roles and retries. Someone who came here to do the task has to go and find the billing page alone. That's the one thing a help article must not leave them to do (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should make obvious what to do and where to click). Put the action first: a direct link or button to the billing page, plus three or four numbered steps (where to click, what to enter, what confirmation to expect). Then follow with the edge cases. Most viewers will act on that link, so it should be the clearest thing under the title.
2. **The retry timeline can be read two ways, and it's about money** (defect). "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days… If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan." Is "the second attempt" the day-3 retry (the second charge overall) or the day-7 retry (the second retry)? And is "seven days" counted from the first decline or from the day-3 retry? One reading puts the downgrade at day 3, another at day 7 or even day 10. Someone planning when to fix their card will remember this and hold you to it. Give the timeline from one fixed point in the viewer's terms, for example: "If your new card is declined, we try it again 3 days later and 7 days later. If the second retry fails, on day 7 your workspace moves to the free plan." Also say what the free plan means for them: what they lose, and whether their data is kept.
3. **Nothing shows where you are** (convention, Nielsen Norman Group, Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist"). In the left sidebar, "Update a payment card" looks just like its siblings, and "Billing" in the top nav isn't marked. The title helps, but the page has three names for itself: breadcrumb "Payment methods", sidebar "Update a payment card", and the title. You can't be sure which sidebar item you're on. Mark the current item with one clear cue, such as bolder weight, a left bar or a tint. Use the sidebar's name in the breadcrumb or title so the three agree.

**Smaller things.**
- Inline links (e.g. "check your billing history", "roles and permissions") are marked by blue alone, with no underline. Mid-blue may be under 3:1 against the near-black body text, so measure it. Underline them by default (WCAG technique G183/F73).
- Body lines look like they run about 80–85 characters, e.g. the first line of the first paragraph. Narrow the text column to bring them toward 66 (Bringhurst). The right side has plenty of room to absorb the change.
- The help centre has no search in the header. Most people arrive at help with a question, not a category. Add a search field to the top bar (Nielsen 7).

**What's working.**
- The structure is clean. The logo and sidebar share one left edge at about x=96, the article column has its own edge, and the white space sits in a few large areas. Keep that grid when you add steps.
- The hierarchy is right for reading. There's one dominant title, and the H2 "When the new card is declined" is set apart by weight rather than by a size that's nearly the same as the body. The space above it is larger than the space below, so it sits with its own section.
- The copy speaks to the reader in plain, full sentences ("You can…", "Your workspace stays open…"). Keep the voice and use it for the steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the inline links. If links stay un-underlined, these are where the non-colour cue has to appear.
