**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the week with your whole team", jumps to the blue "Create account" bar, then goes back up the form. Within a second it reads as a signup page for a team-planning tool. It feels calm and tidy, and anonymous: with the wordmark covered, this page could belong to a dozen products.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a signup page for a stranger who arrived from an ad or the homepage and has to decide whether Tallyboard is worth five fields.

**Verdict.** The form itself is solid and conventional. The page doesn't make the case: one generic headline and one ambiguous line about price have to earn a five-field signup, and half the left column is empty where that case should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pitch is too thin to earn the form (defect).** Everything on the left that sells the product is one headline and two lines (y 206–367). Below that, the left column is empty down to the bottom of the screen, beside a form that runs to y 665. "Plan the week with your whole team" could be any planning tool's headline. A stranger learns nothing about what Tallyboard does differently, so nothing pushes them past "maybe later". The empty half also makes the page look top-heavy and unfinished, not deliberately quiet. Fill that column with proof the viewer can judge for themselves: a product shot of an actual planned week, or one concrete line comparing it with what they use now (a shared spreadsheet, a Monday stand-up). Then rewrite the headline to say what's specific here.
2. **"You can invite the rest of the team later" can be read two ways, and it's about money (defect).** In the subhead (y 313–367), "free … for up to five people" followed by "invite the rest of the team later" can mean (a) you can add more people later for free, or (b) people beyond five cost money. A viewer who takes reading (a) and hits a paywall at person six will feel tricked. It also undercuts the headline's "your whole team". Say it outright, e.g. "Free for up to five people. Larger teams are $X per person per month."
3. **The "Sign in" link doesn't look like a link (defect).** In "Already have an account? Sign in" under the button, the link has the same gray (#59636e, `div.small`), weight and style as the question before it. Returning users, who are exactly the people this line is for, have nothing telling them where to click. Mark it by more than position: an underline, or the brand blue plus medium weight.
4. **The password rule disappears as soon as someone types (defect).** "At least 10 characters" lives only in the placeholder of the Password field. Once the user starts typing it vanishes, so they have to remember it, and they find out they got it wrong only after submitting. Move it to helper text that stays under the field (Nielsen: recognition rather than recall).

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count seven sizes (`type_sizes`: 38, 22, 20, 17, 16, 14, 13). The card title (22) and logo (20), the body (17) and button (16), and the labels (14) and footer (13) are too close to read as separate levels. Keep about four sizes, e.g. 38 / 22 / 16 / 13, and separate within a level by weight or colour (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- Input borders: the pale gray-blue outlines on white look close to 2:1. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 for control boundaries and darken them if they fall short.
- Required fields: nothing is marked. In particular, it's unclear whether Company is required for a free workspace. Mark the optional fields, or drop Company if you don't need it at signup.
- Baselines: the "Tallyboard" logo and the card title "Create your account" sit about 3 px apart, close enough to look like a miss. Align them exactly or move them clearly apart.
- Headline tracking: the 38 px bold Inter headline looks set at default tracking. Tighten it slightly, as is usual for large display type.
- Button copy: "Create account" is fine, but "Create free workspace" would repeat the offer at the moment of commitment (taste).

**What's working.**
- Every field has its own bold 14 px label sitting tight above it (6 px gap, about 18 px between groups), so the grouping is clear at a glance. Keep that spacing.
- The palette is restrained: one blue (#2d4bd0) used only for the wordmark and the primary button. As a result the button is the only coloured thing in the form and the eye finds it immediately. Keep the blue that scarce.
- The subhead is 17 px at 53 characters per line with 1.6 line height, and all text measures 6.1:1 contrast or better. It reads comfortably.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the fields and button. This is a keyboard-heavy form, so check that each focused control shows a visible ring of at least 2 px and 3:1 contrast. Also check how validation errors look and read.
