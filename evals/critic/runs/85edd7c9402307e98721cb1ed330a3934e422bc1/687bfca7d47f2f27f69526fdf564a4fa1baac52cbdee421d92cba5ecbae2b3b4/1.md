**First read.** The eye goes to the purple "Sign in" button first, then up to the email field, because its red outline is the only warm colour on the page. It reads as the sign-in screen for an app called Quillpost, after a failed attempt. It looks calm, clean and well spaced. But the screen shows that something is wrong without saying what.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop web sign-in for returning users, shown in the state after the email was rejected.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are solid, but the state this screen exists to show is close to useless: the email field turns red and says nothing. Next is the "Create an account" link, which nobody can see is a link. New users get stuck there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The error has no words.** Defect, and it breaks WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.1 Error Identification and SC 1.4.1 Use of Color.
   - **Where:** the email field (`#e`, box 470,291) has a red border, and there is no message under it, at the top of the card, or anywhere else.
   - **What the viewer gets:** they know something is wrong but not what. Did the account not exist, was the password wrong, or is the address badly formed? They have to look closely at "dana.ortiz@exmaple" to find the typo and the missing ".com" on their own. Someone with red-green colour blindness may not see any change at all, since the red border is the only cue.
   - **Fix:** put a short message directly under the field that says what is wrong and how to fix it, for example "That email address looks incomplete. Check the spelling after @ (example.com?)". Keep the red border as a second cue (WCAG 2.2, 3.3.1 and 3.3.3 Error Suggestion). If the server can only say "email or password incorrect", show that message in words too, above the button.
2. **"Create an account" doesn't look clickable.** Defect, and it breaks Nielsen's consistency heuristic and Norman's signifiers.
   - **Where:** the footer line, "New here? Create an account" (`div.foot`), is one colour, #59636e, with no underline and no change in weight.
   - **What the viewer gets:** "Forgot your password?" just above it is purple (#5a2fc2), so the page has taught them that purple means link. The grey line then reads as a remark, not an action. A new visitor who lands here doesn't find the way to sign up.
   - **Fix:** style "Create an account" exactly like the forgot-password link. Leave "New here?" grey so the action stands out from the lead-in.

**Smaller things.**
- **Password field border:** the grey border (`#p`) looks fainter than the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for component edges. Measure it against white, and darken it to at least 3:1 if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing that shows the groups:** each label sits 6 px above its field, and the fields are about 18–22 px apart. Every label clearly belongs to its field, and the form reads in one pass.
- **One clear primary action:** the full-width "Sign in" button (340×46 px, white on purple at 8.01:1) is the only filled control, and the purple of the logo ties it to the brand. Keep it the single strongest element.
- **A short type scale:** four sizes (24/20/16/14 px) in one family, all text at 6.1:1 contrast or higher. There is nothing to fix here.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus:** whether the fields and links show a visible focus ring when you tab through them.
- **Error announcement:** whether the error is announced to screen readers once it has a message.
