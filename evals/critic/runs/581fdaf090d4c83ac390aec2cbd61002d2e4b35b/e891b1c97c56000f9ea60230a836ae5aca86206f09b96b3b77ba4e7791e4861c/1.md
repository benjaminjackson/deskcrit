**First read.** The eye lands on "Changing your payment card", drops to the intro, then moves down the three bold subheads: Add, Remove, If a payment fails. Within two seconds it reads as a calm, well-made help-centre article for a subscription product's billing. Nothing competes and nothing is decorative. It looks trustworthy and a bit bare.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help article for a customer who wants to swap the card they're charged on, often because a payment just failed. They need to finish that task and know what happens to their money.

**Verdict.** The page itself does its job: the hierarchy is clean, the type scale is tight and the layout is easy to scan. What's in the way is the copy. One step the reader needs is missing, the retry and read-only rules can be read two ways, and the page ends without sending the reader anywhere. Someone could follow every instruction here and still be stuck.

**What would move it most**
1. **The removal step depends on an instruction the page never gives (defect).** "Remove an old card" says a card can be removed "once another card is set as the default". But "Add a new card" never says whether a new card becomes the default, and no section says how to set one. A reader who adds a card and then tries to remove the old one hits a block the article told them about but never explained. They have to guess or contact support, which is the failure a help page exists to prevent (Nielsen 10, help that is task-focused). Fix: either say in "Add a new card" that the new card becomes the default, or add the one-sentence step for making a card the default before the remove instruction.
2. **The money and deadline lines can be read two ways (defect).** These are the lines readers hold the company to.
   - "We try again after three days… After a third failed try": is the original charge try one, so the account locks after about six days? Or are there three retries, so it locks after about nine? A reader planning when to fix their card can get this wrong by three days.
   - "The new card is used from your next invoice, and nothing already paid is moved": a reader here because a payment failed needs to know whether the *unpaid* invoice will now go on the new card. "Next invoice" suggests not, which would mean the account still goes read-only. "Moved" also leaves it unclear whether it means refunded or re-charged.
   - "Read-only" states a fact without saying what it means for the reader. Fix: say it in their terms, for example "you can see your work but can't edit or add anything until a payment goes through".
   - "Email the account owner" leaves a non-owner unsure whether they'll hear anything.

   Fix: give the actual timeline in days, say outright what happens to an outstanding failed invoice once a new card is added, and spell out read-only.
3. **The page ends without a next step (common sense, taste).** The task happens in a different screen. Yet "Settings", "Billing" and "Add card" are plain text, and the article stops after the failure section with no link to the billing settings and no contact route if the card is still declined. A reader who's mid-task has to leave, find the setting and come back if it goes wrong. Fix: give the reader one specific action, like a "Go to Billing settings" link after the intro or the Add steps, and a single line saying who to contact if a card keeps failing.

**Smaller things.**
- The body column runs to about 84 characters a line (for example "We place a hold of one pound … three working"), past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column a little. There's plenty of room.
- "days." sits alone on the last line of the hold paragraph under "Add a new card". Rewrite slightly or adjust the column so the line isn't left with one word.
- Interface names in the prose (Settings, Billing, Add card, Remove) look the same as the surrounding text, so the reader has to remember the path rather than scan for it (Nielsen 6). Set them in bold, or turn the steps into a short numbered list.

**What's working.**
- The type scale has three clear levels: a large bold title, bold subheads and regular body, plus the grey breadcrumb. Size, weight and colour each do a separate job, with no near-duplicate sizes. Keep it when you add content.
- Spacing groups the content correctly. Each subhead sits tight to its own text and has generous space above it, so the three tasks read as three separate blocks at a glance.
- Headings name tasks in the reader's words ("Add a new card", "If a payment fails"), so a reader can jump straight to their situation.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the column reflows on a phone, which is where many readers will open a "payment failed" email link.
- Focus and hover states on the breadcrumb links.
