**First read.** The eye goes straight to "River ferry timetable", the only thing on the page that can be read. It then drops to a small grey smudge beneath it, which turns out to be three paragraphs of text too small to read, and after that there's nowhere to go. About 85% of the page is empty white. It reads like a page that failed to load or a draft with its body styles missing, and it feels abandoned.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a web page where ferry passengers check when the next boat leaves, the crossing time, and service changes, usually at a glance and often on the move.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. A rider can't get a single departure time from this page: the only content is prose set so small it can't be read, and there's no timetable under a heading that promises one.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text can't be read.** The three paragraphs under the heading (about x 120–225, y 140–195) look about 4–5 px tall. Even at full resolution I could only pick out odd words ("north landing", "market quay", "forty minutes", "bicycles", "tide"). Anyone reading on a normal screen sees a grey stain, not words. It's far below any readable size: Apple's HIG puts the floor at 11 pt for captions, and body text should sit around 16 px or more. At this size even dark ink renders as faint grey, so it also fails WCAG 1.4.3 in practice. **Defect.** Fix: set the body at a normal reading size (about 16–18 px on screen). If it's an accident, such as a missing stylesheet, a stray transform, or a viewport or zoom bug, check that first, because nobody would choose this.
2. **The timetable isn't a table.** As far as I can read it, the schedule is written out as sentences ("runs … every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", and something about winter running one boat an hour). Riders don't read a timetable, they scan it for the next time from where they are standing. In prose, every lookup means reading paragraphs and doing the sums yourself ("forty minutes after 7:10 is…"). That breaks what people expect of a timetable (Jakob's law, Nielsen Norman Group) and the rule that skimmable text gets broken into chunks (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web"). **Defect.** Fix: give actual departures from each landing in a table, with tabular figures in aligned columns. Keep the prose for the notes: crossing time, tide delays, bicycles, winter hours.
3. **The copy doesn't answer the rider's real question.** The heading names a category, and even if the body were readable, it describes how the service behaves rather than telling the rider what to do. For example, "a little longer when the tide runs against the boat" never says how much longer or what that means for catching a connection. The page also never says which landing a given time departs from, or which days and dates the schedule covers. Times and dates are what riders act on, so vague wording there is a defect, not a style note. **Defect.** Fix: lead with the route and dates ("North landing ⇄ Market quay, from 1 November"), and say exceptions in rider terms ("Against the tide, allow about X minutes more").
4. **The layout is a corner, not a page.** The heading and the 100 px text column sit in the top-left corner. The rest of the 1280×800 frame is blank: leftover space, not shaped space. The column holds only about 30 characters a line, well under Bringhurst's 45–75. Once there's a real table, give it the width and let the layout hold it. **Taste and convention.**

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: there are only two sizes, about 9× apart (a ~40 px heading and ~4–5 px body), with nothing between them. Add a sensible body size, plus at most a small size for notes, and let weight carry the finer levels (Refactoring UI).

**What's working.**
- The heading is well set. It's a clean, heavy sans at a confident size with tight spacing, it sits comfortably on its margin, and it says plainly what the page is. Keep it as the top of the hierarchy.
- The sober black-on-white palette suits a public-service timetable. Don't decorate it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiny text is a rendering fault (missing CSS, zoom or transform bug, a capture taken mid-load) or the design itself. Load the page in a live browser at 100% before anything else.
- Reflow on a phone, which matters a lot for riders checking times at the landing.
