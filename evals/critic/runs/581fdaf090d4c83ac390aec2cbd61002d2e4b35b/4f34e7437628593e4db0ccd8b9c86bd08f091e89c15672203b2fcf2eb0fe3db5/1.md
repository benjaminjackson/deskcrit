**First read.** The eye lands on **$4.82M** in the top-left card, moves across the three KPI cards, then drops to the blue bars. The bars reach the eye after the black box and gridlines around them. I read it as a SaaS revenue dashboard for a founder or finance lead. It feels calm and competent, close to a well-made template.

**What it's for.** Assumption: an internal dashboard where a founder or finance lead checks how 2025 revenue went, at a glance, and trusts the numbers enough to repeat them.

**Verdict.** The layout and hierarchy do their job. The biggest problem is the numbers: two figures both labelled "2025" don't match, so the first person who adds them up will stop trusting the whole screen. After that, the chart's black frame and gridlines draw more attention than the data.

**What would move it most**
1. **The figures disagree with each other (defect).** "Revenue by plan, 2025" totals **$4.82M**, which is exactly the ARR figure in the first card. But the monthly bars, labelled "recognised revenue", add up to about **$4.49M** for 2025. December's bar (~$447K) times 12 is about $5.36M, which doesn't match the $4.82M ARR either. A reader will take "Revenue by plan, 2025" to mean either 2025 revenue (and then it's wrong by ~$330K) or December ARR split by plan (and then the label is wrong). On a page whose numbers get quoted in board decks, that's the line people will hold you to. Fix: retitle the table to say what it measures, e.g. "ARR by plan, Dec 2025", or show 2025 recognised revenue by plan so it ties to the chart. Also say somewhere how ARR relates to recognised revenue, if they're meant to differ. Pick one unit too: the chart is in thousands while the rest of the page is in $M, so every comparison means converting in your head.
2. **The chart's scaffolding outweighs its data (convention).** The plot has a solid black frame and black gridlines at every 100, the darkest lines in the bottom two-thirds of the screen. The eye reads a cage before it reads twelve bars. Wilke (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, 23.2) and Tufte (*Visual Display*, ch. 5) both say gridlines and frames should not compete with the data. Fix: remove the frame and make the gridlines thin and pale grey (or drop them) so the blue bars are the strongest marks in the chart.
3. **The chart names a topic but not the finding (common sense; no approved source).** "Monthly revenue, 2025" makes the reader work out the story: monthly revenue grew about 43%, from ~$312K in January to ~$447K in December, with small dips in April and July. A dashboard is there to answer "how did we do?" Fix: put the finding in the title or a one-line subtitle, and label the December bar (or January and December) with its value, so nobody has to estimate against gridlines.

**Smaller things.**
- **Grid:** the chart's right edge (~960px) and the plan table's left edge (~1012px) don't follow the card columns (907/925px). Make the lower row match the card columns, e.g. chart across two cards and plan table under the third.
- **Heading baselines:** "Monthly revenue, 2025" and "Revenue by plan, 2025" sit on slightly different baselines. Put them on one line.
- **Date pill:** "Jan to Dec 2025" (top right) is a grey pill with no chevron or icon, so you can't tell if it's a filter you can change or just a label. If it's a control, add a cue, such as a chevron or calendar icon.
- **Page name:** the sidebar highlights "Overview", but the page title is "Revenue overview" and there's also a "Revenue" item in the sidebar. Name the page to match its nav item so people know where they are.

**What's working.**
- **Every KPI has a period and a comparison** ("Dec 2025", "+18% vs Dec 2024", "+3 pts vs 2024"). That's what makes a big number readable (Few). Keep this pattern.
- **Restrained colour.** One blue for data, green only on the deltas, and the deltas carry a "+" so they don't depend on colour alone. Nothing competes with the numbers.
- **The bars start at zero and the chart is honest**, with the unit and source stated ("closed invoices only"). That's the right base for the fixes above.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the nav and the date control.
- How the layout reflows at narrow widths.
