**What would move it most**

1. **Text the reader needs is set at about 4 px.** This covers the two field labels in the form, the note under "Sign up", the figure caption, and the footer line. At this size the letters can't be told apart, even at full resolution. The two empty boxes give no clue what goes in them, so the reader has to guess: name, email, or phone? The note under the button is probably the reassurance that gets people to sign up ("We send one short note when the timetable changes… stop at any time"), and nobody can see it. This is a **defect**. Apple's Human Interface Guidelines set 11 pt as the smallest text size, and the pale grey on top likely fails WCAG 1.4.3 (4.5:1). It also breaks the type scale. Four sizes do the work here (about 36, 22, 18 and 17 px), and then a fifth jumps down to a size nobody can read. **Fix:** keep the four working sizes and add one small size, around 14 px, for labels, the caption, the note and the footer. Darken that text until it reaches 4.5:1.

2. **The boat in the drawing is styled like the button.** It's a solid blue rounded rectangle in the same blue and roughly the same shape as "Sign up". On first read it looks clickable, and it pulls the eye away from the real action. The page's one accent colour now means two different things. This is a **defect** (it suggests an action that isn't there) and a **convention** break (keep the accent colour for things you can press). **Fix:** keep that blue off anything that isn't interactive. Draw the boat in the drawing's own greys and blues, and give it a boat's outline instead of a button's.

3. **The picture doesn't show the story.** The text promises wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches facing the water, a kiosk, and a ramp that meets the deck at every tide. The drawing shows a grey slab, a lighter block and water. None of that detail is in it, so it looks like a layout placeholder rather than the new landing. The strongest point on the page, "prams and wheelchairs roll straight on", gets no visual proof. This is a **defect** if it's meant to be the real figure. If it's a stand-in for a photo, it's fine once the photo goes in. **Fix:** use a photo or drawing of the ramp meeting the boat deck with people boarding, since that's what the page is selling.

4. **The ask is generic, and the page doesn't say where it is.** "Get timetable changes" and "Sign up" don't say which route, how the alerts arrive, or how often. The headline doesn't name the landing, and nothing on screen names the operator or site. A stranger can't tell which ferry this is about. This is a **defect** in the copy. If a site header sits above this capture, that partly answers the identity question, but the form still needs its own context. **Fix:** name the place in the headline ("North landing rebuilt…"). Make the ask a specific action the reader recognises, such as "Text me when the [route] times change", with the note's reassurance in readable type directly under the button.
