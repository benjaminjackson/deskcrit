**First read.** The eye goes to the bold "Notes for ferry passengers", then down three even paragraphs of dark grey text. The right half of the frame and the bottom third are empty. It reads as a plain help page for a small river ferry. It feels calm, tidy and quiet, almost like a draft. Nothing tells you where the facts you came for are.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page for people about to use the ferry: when it runs, how bikes work, and what changes in winter, so they can catch a boat.

**Verdict.** As typesetting it's clean and very readable. As a passenger notice it fails at its job: it never gives a time anyone could plan around, and the few facts it does give are buried mid-sentence in prose. The biggest problem is the words, not the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page gives no times you can act on.** *(defect)* Paragraph 1 says "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". Forty minutes past what? When does the first boat leave, and when does the last? Paragraph 3 says "In winter… one boat each hour, and the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk". Which months count as winter? On the hour or at half past? Dusk moves by more than an hour over a winter, and "the quay" could mean either landing. These are the lines a passenger acts on, and each one can be read more than one way. If they guess wrong, they miss the last boat. The page then sends them to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing", which they only see once they've already made the trip. To fix it, put the real figures on the page: first and last departure from each side, the minute past the hour, the winter dates and the winter last boat. Or link to the timetable right next to the sentence that mentions it.

2. **You can't scan for the facts.** *(taste, argued below)* Each paragraph is short, so this isn't one dense block. But each answers a question a passenger has, and the answer sits inside the sentence. Someone asking "can I bring my bike?" has to read into paragraph 2 to find "no extra charge". Someone asking "when's the last boat?" has to reach the middle of paragraph 3. Paragraph 1 also spends its second half on the crew steering around the sandbank. That's nice colour, but it tells the passenger nothing they can use. To fix it, start each paragraph with its answer, or add short subheads (Times, Bicycles, Winter) in the existing bold at body size, so no new size enters the scale. Also talk to the reader: "Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded" describes other people, while "Wait until foot passengers have boarded, then wheel your bike on and lock it to the rail" tells you what to do.

**What's working.**
- The measure and leading are right for reading. Lines run about 65–75 characters at roughly 1.5 line spacing, so the eye finds the next line easily. Keep that column width if you add structure.
- The type scale is two sizes plus bold weight for the heading. That's one clear level jump with no near-miss sizes. Any subheads should use weight, not a third size.
- The tone is plain and unhurried and fits a small local service. Keep the voice and add the facts.
