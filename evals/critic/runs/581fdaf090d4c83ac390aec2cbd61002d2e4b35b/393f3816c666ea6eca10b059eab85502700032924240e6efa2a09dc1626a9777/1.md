**First read.** On both screens the eye goes to the large serif "Good morning, Dana", then down through the paragraph, the two class rows, and the dark "Book a class" button. It reads as a yoga studio's member home screen on a phone, and it feels calm and warm. In V1 the paragraph is solid capitals, and it looks like a warning or legal notice. It's a grey slab, and the eye skips it to reach the classes. In V2 the same words read like a friendly note.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a returning member opens the app, checks how many sessions their pass has left and the booking rules, and books a class today.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is that the pass-and-cancellation paragraph goes from all caps to sentence case. That paragraph holds the information the member will be held to (six sessions, 30 November, two hours ahead, 8 pm cancellation), and V1 makes it the hardest thing on the screen to read. Both versions share the same remaining problem: the button doesn't book any particular class.

**What would move it most** (for V2, the better version)
1. **The ask isn't tied to the classes listed above it** (defect). The two rows, "Slow flow 7:00 am" and "Mobility 12:15 pm", have no chevron, button, or other sign that you can tap them. The one action on the page is a generic "Book a class". A member who wants the 7:00 slot can't tell whether to tap the row or the button, or whether the button opens another list to pick from again. Norman calls this a missing signifier. Fix: make each row the booking control, with a "Book" button on each row or a clearly tappable row with a chevron. Then either drop the bottom button or turn it into "See all classes".
2. **The hero box is empty** (defect, unless a photo failed to load). The tan gradient fills roughly the top third of the screen and says nothing. It pushes the classes, which are the reason to open the app, down toward the fold. If it's meant to hold a photo, check that the photo loads. If the gradient is intended, shrink or cut it so the classes sit higher.

**Smaller things.**
- The class list has no day on it. Add "Today", or the date, above the rows so the "4 spaces" and "9 spaces" figures clearly refer to today's classes.
- V1 only (V2 already fixes these): the capitals have no letterspacing, and "SESSION." sits alone on the last line.

**What's working.**
- V2's sentence-case paragraph. It keeps the rules (book at least two hours ahead, cancel by 8 pm the night before) readable and in a human voice. Butterick's *Practical Typography* says not to capitalise whole paragraphs, and Hochuli notes that text in capitals is particularly hard to read. Keep it this way.
- The type scale is clear: a serif display greeting, a bold class name, regular detail text, and one filled button. Each level is obviously different from the next.
- One calm, warm palette, with a single dark high-emphasis button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the hero is a photo that didn't load.
- Pressed and focus states on the rows and the button.
