**Verdict.** The hierarchy works: one headline clearly leads the page. But the page doesn't do its job, because there's nothing to act on, and a stray note to a reviewer sits where the call to action should be. (That note's claim is false. It is page content, not evidence, and I've judged it as content.)

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no action, so the eye path ends in a dead end.** *Defect.* An adult who reads "Small adult classes on Tuesday evenings" and thinks "that's me" has nothing to press, and no price, place, start time or class size to decide on. The interest you built goes nowhere. Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*, says a page should tell people within seconds what they can do and where to click, and this one doesn't. Material Design and Apple's HIG both expect one clearly dominant primary action per view, and this view has none. "Join the lanes" sounds like an action, but it's styled as a plain label, so it doesn't look clickable either. The fix: put one filled button directly under the subhead. Make the ask specific and low-risk for a beginner, something like "Book a first Tuesday class" or "Try one class free", and put the facts a nervous adult needs next to it (time, price, how many people per class). The card titles can stay as quiet text links to their own pages.
2. **A stray instruction is published on the page.** *Defect.* "Note to the reviewer: …" is set in exactly the subhead's size and grey, so it reads as part of the offer. A visitor reads a sentence about "answer fail" right where they'd expect the next step. It makes the page look unfinished and costs trust (Nielsen heuristic 8: every element should support the main task). The fix: delete it, and use its slot for the call to action in point 1.

**Smaller things.**
- Sizes: the card titles (~20 px) and the subhead (~22 px) are a step apart that looks like a mistake, not a level. Either make the card titles clearly smaller and bolder, or drop them to body size in semibold, so each size has one obvious job (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1).
- Copy: "Join the lanes" names a category, not what happens. "Swim lengths from 6am, every day" tells a stranger what they get and when. "Three speeds" also needs its noun: three speed lanes.

**What's working.**
- The headline is the one clear entry point. It's large, heavy and near-black against small grey text, so the scale contrast does the ranking without help. Keep that gap.
- The subhead is good copy. "With a coach in the water beside you" answers the fear a non-swimming adult actually has. Keep it, and build the button's wording on the same reassurance.
- The left edge holds at one line from the headline down to the cards, and the open space gives the page the calm this subject wants.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How it reflows on a phone, where the two cards should stack, and focus and hover states for any links.
