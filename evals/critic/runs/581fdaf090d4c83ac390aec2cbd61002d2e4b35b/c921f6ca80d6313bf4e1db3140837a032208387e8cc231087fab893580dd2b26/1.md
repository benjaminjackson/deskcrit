**First read.** On both versions the eye goes straight to the big serif headline, then to the standfirst, then to the byline. It reads as the opening of a long read in a quiet, literary magazine. In version 2 the second glance snags: the headline's three lines run into each other, and "people" and "keeping" dig into "lighthouse".

**What it's for.** Assumption: the top of a magazine article page. It has to pull a reader into a long feature through the headline and the standfirst.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job and version 2 doesn't. The only change is the headline's line spacing. Version 2 tightens it until the lines overlap, so the most important element on the page looks broken.

**What would move it most**
1. **Headline lines collide in version 2 (defect).** In version 2 the descenders of "p", "q", "g" and "p" in line 1 run into the tops of "l", "h" and "t" in "lighthouse lit". The "g"s in line 2 do the same to "Fair Skerry". Readers have to pick the letters apart where a headline should be read in one glance, and on a publication whose whole appeal is craft, it looks like a rendering bug. Multi-line headings do need tighter spacing than body text so they hold together as one unit (Bringhurst), but version 2 goes past that. The fix is to bring back roughly version 1's spacing, about 1.1 times the type size for this face. Version 1 is already tight enough to read as one block, and its lines still clear each other.

**Smaller things.**
- Version 2 also closes the gap above the standfirst, which loses some of the breathing room that sets the headline apart. Version 1's spacing is better here too.

**What's working (version 1).**
- **Clear type levels.** A heavy serif headline, a lighter serif standfirst, and a small sans-serif byline and label. Each level differs in size, weight and typeface, so the order of reading is obvious.
- **A good standfirst.** It's concrete, with a number, a ritual and a turn: "Now the last boat is for sale." It gives the reader a reason to read on, and its line length is comfortable.
- **Restrained colour.** The one rust-red "Long read" label is the only colour on the page, so it does its job without fighting the headline.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the headline wraps on narrower screens, where a fixed tight line spacing would collide on different word pairs.
