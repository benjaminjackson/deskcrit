**First read.** The eye lands on "Crossing the river", a large, confident headline. Then it jumps right to the boxed "Good to know" and its blue button, and only after that drops back to the two sections on the left. It reads as a calm, plain information page for a small local ferry, and it looks tidy and trustworthy. Its trouble is that it says almost nothing exact.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that tells first-time or occasional passengers on one ferry route when the boats run and how to bring a bike.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the hierarchy mostly works, but the page doesn't do its job. A section called "When the boats run" never gives a time, so a passenger still has to go somewhere else to plan a crossing. A secondary problem is the sidebar card: its type is larger than the main content, so it pulls focus from the column that matters.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy leaves out every time and date a passenger would act on.** This is a defect. "Between early morning and late evening", "in winter" and "the last crossing leaves before dusk" are all in the "When the boats run" paragraph. Each can be read more than one way: does early mean 6:00 or 8:00? Does winter start in November or at the clock change? Is dusk 16:00 or 18:00? A passenger who guesses wrong misses the last boat and is stranded on the wrong bank. That is the worst result this page can produce. "Every forty minutes" is good and exact. Everything around it is vague. Without times the section is only a description, and the passenger is sent to the timetable to find the very thing the heading promised.
   - **Fix:** State the first and last departures from each landing, the dates the winter timetable starts and ends, and the actual last winter departure. "Last boat from the north landing: 16:20, November to February" tells the reader what they can no longer do after that time.
   - **Where it rests:** This is common sense: put what the reader needs next in front of them instead of making them hunt for it.
   - **Smaller copy point:** The bicycle section is good copy: it says who does what, when, and where. Use it as the model for the rest.

2. **The sidebar card outranks the main content because its type is bigger.** This is a defect against a convention.
   - **What's off:** "Good to know" is set at roughly 34 px, larger than the two section headings at roughly 28 px. The card's body text is roughly 20 px against roughly 18 px in the main column.
   - **What the viewer experiences:** After the headline, the eye goes to the card, not to "When the boats run". The aside reads as the main point and the guide reads as the footnote.
   - **The scale:** I count about eight sizes on a page this short: 64, 34, 28, 24, 22, 20, 18 and 16 px. The 34 and 28 headings collide, and so do the 24, 22, 20 and 18 sizes. These read as sizes picked one at a time, not as levels. Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style* 3.1.1: "limit yourself, at first, to a modest set of distinct and related intervals."
   - **Fix:** Give the card heading the same size as the section headings or one step smaller, and its body the same size as the main body. That brings the scale to about four levels: headline, subtitle, section heading, body, plus a small size for nav and the button. Let the blue button and the box do the work of drawing attention to the card.

**Smaller things.**
- **Card alignment:** The card's right edge (about x=1150) stops about 34 px short of the nav's right edge (about x=1184). Its top doesn't line up with the headline either, so it looks like it drifted rather than sits on a grid. Line it up with the nav edge and with the top of the headline or the subtitle. This rests on my own judgement; I didn't open the layout reference entry.
- **"Good to know" names a category, not its content:** Something like "Printed timetables at every landing" tells the scanning reader what's in the box.
- **Landing names:** "north landing" and "market quay" are lowercase in the subtitle. If they are the names of the stops, capitalise them so passengers can match them to the signs at the landings.

**What's working.**
- **The headline and subtitle:** The headline is the one clear dominant element. The subtitle names the exact route ("north landing to market quay"), so a passenger knows at once whether this page is for them.
- **The main column's text:** The measure is about 70 characters, leading is comfortable, and each heading sits close to its own paragraph. It's easy to skim. Keep this.
- **Colour restraint:** One blue, used only on the one action, means the button reads as the thing to click without any help.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the card drops below the main text, rather than above it, when the page narrows on a phone. If the card's larger type lands first on a small screen, finding 2 gets worse.
