**First read.** The eye goes to "Get the weekly show list", drops through one line of explanation to the email field, and stops on the purple Subscribe button. It's a newsletter signup for a local radio station, and it feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. Both versions read the same way at first glance. The only difference shows up at the buttons.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a signup page where a listener to Riverside FM subscribes to a weekly email of the programme schedule.

**Verdict.** Both versions work: you know at once what you get, when you get it, and what to do. Version 1 is slightly better. Version 2 makes one change, squaring off the button corners, and that pulls the buttons out of the shape language the input and card share without adding anything.

**What would move it most**
1. **Corner radius (v2's only change; taste with a consistency basis).** In v2 the Subscribe and "Read last week's list" buttons have almost square corners. The email field directly above them and the card around them stay rounded. So the form's three controls, stacked in one column, follow two rules, and the field and its submit button stop looking like one set. Nobody will fail to subscribe because of it. It does make the form look slightly less finished, because the change doesn't mark any difference in what the controls do (Nielsen heuristic 4, consistency). Fix: use one radius for every control in the form, as v1 does.
2. **Input border too faint (defect, both versions).** The email field's border and the secondary button's outline are a very pale grey on white and look below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on component edges. For users with low vision, or on a washed-out screen, the field becomes a placeholder floating in white space. Fix: darken the input border to at least 3:1. The secondary button can stay lighter because its label marks it as a button.

**Smaller things.**
- "week's" in the body text and in "Read last week's list" uses a straight apostrophe. Use the curly ’.

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete. The headline names what you get, and the line beneath it says when ("Every Friday") and what's inside (programmes, guests, schedule changes). The reader doesn't have to fill any gaps.
- "Read last week's list" is the best kind of secondary ask. It lets the listener see a real issue before handing over an address, so they judge the value for themselves instead of trusting a claim.
- The order of emphasis is right: a filled purple primary, an outlined secondary, and the purple used once more only on the station name.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus, error and success states for the email field. Check that an invalid address shows a text message by the field and that a confirmation appears after Subscribe.
