**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves across to the white card, and goes down the three fields. Then it stops. Below the message box there is only the words "Send message", in the same size, weight and colour as the paragraph text, so it reads as a caption rather than something to click. It's a small studio's contact page: calm, tidy, competent, and over-quiet at the one point where it needs to speak up.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the contact page of a small design or development studio, there to get prospective clients to send a project enquiry.

**Verdict.** It doesn't quite do its job. The layout and type are sound, but the page's one action, sending the message, has no button, and the eye has nowhere to finish. Fixing that one thing would change the page more than everything else here put together.

**What would move it most**
1. **The submit doesn't look like a button.** (Defect.) "Send message" at the bottom of the card has no fill, no border and no underline. It's set in the body face, size, weight and colour, which is the worst version of this mistake. Someone who has just typed a message looks for the thing to press and finds what looks like a label. Some will hesitate, some will click the text and hope, and a few will give up and email instead. Nothing else on the page is styled as the primary action either. The only coloured, clickable-looking things are the blue nav and footer links, so the strongest visual signals point away from the form (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on affordances and signifiers; Material Design and Apple HIG on giving one action high emphasis). Fix: give the main action the shape of a button, with a fill or a border. The blue the page already uses for links is the obvious fill. Make it the strongest element inside the card so the eye has a place to finish.
2. **The intro asks for information the form doesn't help you give.** (Defect, argued from common sense.) The paragraph says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them", but the form has no place for either. Visitors have to remember the request while they write in a blank box, and most won't. The studio then has to follow up for the two facts it said it wanted. Fix: add optional "Budget range" and "Timeline" fields, as selects or short inputs. Or, at the very least, put hint text under "Message" that repeats the prompt where the person is typing.
3. **The copy could be on any studio's site.** (Taste.) "Tell us about your project" and "Send message" say nothing about Fernwick. The one line that makes a concrete promise, "reply within two working days", is the strongest thing on the page, and it sits in grey body text. Fix: keep the promise, and consider repeating it next to the button ("We'll reply within two working days") so it reassures at the moment of commitment. A headline that names the kind of project you take on would let the right visitors recognise themselves.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: nothing marks "Contact" as the current page. Give it one cue, such as a bolder weight or a fixed underline bar (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Form: nothing says which fields are required. Put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or mark the optional ones "(optional)" (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). This matters more once you add budget and timeline fields.
- Input borders: the field borders look pale grey on white, likely below WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 for component edges. Measure the border colour against white and darken it if it falls short.
- Empty space: the band right of the card (about x 1068–1280) and the area below it reads as leftover rather than chosen (taste). Either widen the two-column layout to the full content width so the card's right edge lines up with the nav's right edge at "Contact", or accept the space and centre the layout's content block.

**What's working.**
- The headline is plainly dominant. It's the one large, heavy element, so the page's purpose is clear in a second. Keep that size step.
- The labels sit above the fields, persistent and bold, close to their own field and well clear of the one above. Keep that spacing rhythm when you add fields.
- The links are blue and underlined, so they don't depend on colour alone. When you style the button, keep it different enough from these links that it reads as the main action and not as another link.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the inputs and the submit (the submit especially, since it currently has no visible edge for a focus ring to follow). Error and success states after submitting. Reflow at narrow widths.
