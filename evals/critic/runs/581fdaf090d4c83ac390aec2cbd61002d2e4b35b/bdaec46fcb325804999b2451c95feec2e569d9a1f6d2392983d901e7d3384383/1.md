**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the large, light "Recent work", drops to the wide library tile, then moves right to the two smaller projects. It reads as a calm, serious architecture practice. In version 2 the eye lands on the bold logo and then slides across three identical grey blocks. The heading is the same size as the captions, so the page has no starting point.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home or portfolio page of a small architecture practice, shown to prospective clients. Its job is to put the work up front and let one project stand for the studio.

**Verdict.** Version 1 works. Version 2 is a weaker version of the same page. It changed exactly the two things that gave the page a hierarchy, and nothing came back in return. In both versions, the biggest thing in the way is that the tiles show no pictures.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2 has no focal point (defect).** The three tiles are the same size, and "Recent work" is about 22 px light, next to project titles at about 22 px regular. Heading, titles and tiles are all on one level, so the viewer scans a flat row and nothing tells them which project the studio is proudest of. Dair: weak contrast is worse than none. This comes from two changes:
   - shrinking the heading from roughly 52 px to 22 px, which merged two levels of the type scale;
   - making the 2:1:1 tile split equal.

   Version 1 has the right fix already. Keep a heading clearly larger than the titles, and let one project take double width. The double-width lead is also the one place where the page shows the studio choosing something, which is worth a lot on a portfolio.
2. **There are no images in either version (defect unless these are placeholders).** All three tiles are flat colour fields. For an architect the photographs are the whole case, and without them neither version can show anyone why to hire the studio. If the photos failed to load in this capture, check in a live browser. If they are real placeholders, judge the hierarchy again once the photos are in. A strong photo in version 1's wide tile will carry far more than any type change.

**Smaller things.**
- The captions don't follow one pattern (both versions). Only the library gives a floor area. Either give every project the same fields (type, area, year) or drop the area.
- The nav doesn't show the current page (both versions). Mark the current item in "Projects · Studio · Contact", for example with an underline or a weight change.
- About 200 px of empty band sits under the row (both versions). This may just be the end of the screenshot. If the page really ends there, it looks unfinished; close it with a link to all projects or with a footer.

**What's working (version 1).**
- The 2:1:1 split sits on a clean four-column grid with one gutter width. The big tile reads as a choice, not an accident.
- The type scale is clear: a large light display heading, regular titles, smaller grey details, and a small bold capitals wordmark. Each size has one job.
- The palette is restrained, with warm off-white and muted stone colours. That suits a serious practice and will let the photographs lead.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the tiles are images that failed to load. Hover and focus states on the tiles and nav. How the 2:1:1 layout reflows on narrow screens.
