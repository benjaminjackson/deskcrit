**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline, "Hold a real conversation in Italian by summer", moves to the subhead, then to the actions. It reads as a landing page for a warm, human language course. In version 1 the path stops at a row of three identical orange buttons and you have to read all three to pick one. In version 2 it goes straight to "Start your first lesson".

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero of a landing page for adults learning Italian, and its job is to get them to start a first lesson.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is that "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" go from filled buttons to underlined text links, and that change gives the page one clear next step. Beyond that, version 2 is a solid, restrained hero, and its remaining problems are small.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1 has three actions at equal weight (defect; convention).** The three filled orange buttons under the subhead have the same size, fill and weight. "Gift a course", which few visitors need, shouts as loudly as the main action. Visitors have to read and weigh three options at the moment they should just act, and some will drift to the course plan. Material Design 3 ("Buttons") says to keep one high-emphasis action and give the others lower-emphasis styles. Version 2 does exactly this: one filled button, two underlined links in the accent colour. The underline keeps them recognisable as links without using colour alone. Keep version 2's treatment.

**Smaller things.**
- **Empty chat panel:** The bottom third of the panel is empty below "Bene! E che film avete visto?", so the conversation looks cut off or still loading. Either shorten the panel to fit the exchange or add a reply, such as a teacher's correction. A correction would also show the "corrects what you say as you say it" promise instead of only stating it.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** It names a concrete outcome and a deadline ("a real conversation… by summer"). That is a specific promise, not generic copy, and the heavy serif makes it clearly dominant.
- **The subhead:** It explains the method in the reader's terms: fifteen minutes a day, a weekly call, a teacher in Bologna who corrects you live. Every line says who does what.
- **The chat panel:** It sits next to the headline as its other half. Showing a real exchange makes "real conversation" tangible, and the user's reply in a peach tint makes it clear who is speaking without needing labels.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the button and the two links.
- How the two-column hero reflows on narrow screens.
