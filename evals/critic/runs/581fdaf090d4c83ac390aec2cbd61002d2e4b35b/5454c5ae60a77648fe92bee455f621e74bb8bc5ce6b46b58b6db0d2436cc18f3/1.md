**First read.** The leaf pattern lands first: a full-width band of bright, busy foliage with no calm spot in it. The eye then has to look for the white headline, and the headline breaks up where it crosses the pale yellow and lime leaves: "small" and the final "spaces." partly melt into the pattern. After that the eye drops to a clear lead paragraph and three tidy info cards, and the page ends there. There's nothing to click and nowhere to go next. It reads as a friendly, competent local garden centre homepage, but a generic one.

**What it's for.** Assumed: the homepage of a local garden centre, telling nearby gardeners with shady or small plots that it grows plants for them, and getting them to browse those plants or come in.

**Verdict.** The words are right, but the page doesn't deliver them. The headline is set straight onto a pattern busy enough to swallow it, and once the visitor has understood the offer there is no way to act on it. Those two problems decide whether this page works. Everything below the hero is solid.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headline is lost in the hero pattern (defect).** "Plants for shade and small spaces" is white type set directly on a field of pale yellow, lime and white leaves. Behind "small", "spaces." and parts of "shade", the background is nearly as light as the letters, so the words fall apart into the pattern and you have to read them a second time. This is the most important line on the page and it is the hardest one to read. WCAG counts this as a contrast failure because it measures against the lightest part of the image behind the letters, not the average (W3C, Techniques for WCAG 2.2, F83, under 1.4.3). It is also a figure-ground failure: the text never separates from what lies behind it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Figure and ground"). The pattern is also the most saturated, highest-contrast thing on the page, so it beats the headline for first look.
   - **Fix:** put a dark scrim or a solid panel behind the headline, or calm the pattern where the text sits: darken it, thin it, or knock it back to a tint. Every letter should stand clear of what is directly behind it, and the headline, not the wallpaper, should be what the eye hits first.
   - **Check:** measure the headline against the palest leaf behind it, not against the dark green.

2. **The page has no ask (defect).** The headline makes an offer and the lead backs it with a real detail ("ready to pot this weekend"). Then there is no "See shade plants", no "Book a workshop", and no "Get directions". The visitor has to work out on their own that "Plants" in the nav might lead somewhere related. The workshops card says there are ten places per class but gives no date and no way to book. The page has no primary action, so nothing has the visual weight to pull the eye on from the headline (Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis).
   - **Fix:** add one filled button under the lead that names the exact thing, such as "Browse ferns, hostas and shrubs for shade". Make the workshops card say when the next class is and link to booking. The strongest version speaks to the visitor's own garden, for example "Shop by spot: north-facing, balcony, under trees". That lets people match the plants to a place they already know instead of trusting the claim.

3. **The picture doesn't show the promise (taste).** The headline is about shade and small spaces. The image is a bright, sunlit, crowded mass of generic leaves, with no ferns, no hostas, no shade and no small space. So the image and the headline don't build one idea between them; the image just says "plants". The flat, randomly scattered leaves also make the page look like any garden site, which hides the one thing that makes this nursery different. This finding and the first one have the same cure: an image with a calmer, darker area of real fern or hosta foliage would show the promise and give the headline somewhere to sit.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline's left edge sits at about x=72, but the logo, lead and cards sit at about x=56. A 16 px nudge looks like a mistake, not a choice (Arnheim, on placements that are almost aligned). Align it to x=56.
- The headline breaks "small / spaces." and splits a two-word phrase. Break after "shade" ("Plants for shade / and small spaces") or widen the box.
- The Opening hours card breaks "until 8 / pm" and separates the number from its unit (Bringhurst 2.4.6). Use a non-breaking space or write "8 pm" together.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific and local.** "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, grown at our nursery and ready to pot this weekend" says what, who grew it and when, and "ten places in each class" and "free within ten miles on orders over $60" give numbers with clear reference points. Keep this voice when you write the button.
- **The info row is well built.** Three equal cards on one gutter, a bold title over regular text, and line lengths that read comfortably. It answers "when are you open, what's on, do you deliver" at a glance.
- **The type system is small and clear.** A serif wordmark against a sans for everything else, with about five sizes that each have an obvious job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are meant to be clickable. If they are, they need a link cue.
- Focus states on the nav.
- Whether the headline stays clear of the pattern when the layout narrows on mobile.
