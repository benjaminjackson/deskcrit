**First read.** The eye goes to the bold serif "Harbour Lights festival" at top left, then into the first column, and you read left to right as you would a newspaper. It reads as a local festival notice or a page on a community website. It feels calm and neat, a bit like a document. Two things show up within seconds: the middle column starts about half a line lower than the other two, and the bottom 40% of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or notice for local people. It should tell them what's on, when and where, and get some of them to volunteer.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and easy to read line by line, but it fails as a festival notice. Every fact a visitor needs (dates, times, places, events) is buried in three blocks of prose that look the same, and the one request, for volunteers, gives no way to respond. It reads like an article when it needs to be a schedule.

**What would move it most**

1. **The facts people act on are buried in running text** (defect). The dates, times and places are inside sentences: "first weekend of September", "Saturday night… at dusk", "Sunday afternoon", "by one o'clock", "at four", "Stalls open at ten". To find when the lantern walk starts, you have to read column 2 from the top. The three columns cover three separate things (overview, lantern walk, pie contest) but have no headings, so you can't skim to the one you want. Nielsen's research on web reading says people skim pages like this and don't read them, and text meant for skimming needs headings and short chunks. Below the title the page has only one level, body text, so nothing in the content stands out. Fix: give each column a short heading that names the event, and pull the when and where out of the prose into a line of their own at the top of each column, set apart by weight (e.g. "Saturday · dusk · from the town hall"). Keep the prose for the colour around it.

2. **The copy leaves out the details people will hold you to** (defect). "The first weekend of September" has no year and no dates. "At dusk" is not a time. The children's boat race appears once, in column 1, with no day, time or details, which looks like a column was lost. Read as a stranger would read it, the page doesn't answer "when do I turn up?" for most of what it lists. Fix: give real dates ("Saturday 6 and Sunday 7 September"), a start time for the walk, and a day and time for the boat race. Either give the boat race its own column or say where its details are.

3. **The volunteer ask has nowhere to go** (defect). "Volunteers are still needed…" is the only thing the page asks of anyone, and it's in the middle of the first paragraph with no name, link, email or place to sign up. Readers who would help have nothing to do next, so the eye path stops there. Fix: take it out of the paragraph and give it a block of its own with one exact action, e.g. "Can you help hand out programmes or guard the cake tent? Sign up at the school stall or email …". The seagull joke is good; keep it in the ask.

4. **The middle column starts half a line low, and the page is top-heavy** (defect). Column 2's first line is about 14px below the first lines of columns 1 and 3. Across the row its lines sit between the lines of its neighbours rather than level with them. It's close enough to look like a mistake, not a choice, so the page looks unfinished (Arnheim on near-misses; Müller-Brockmann on lines matching across columns). Separately, all the content sits in the top 60% with nothing below, so the empty band reads as space left over, not space put there on purpose. Fix: start all three columns on one baseline. Then either size the frame to the content or use the space below on purpose, for example for the volunteer block or a strip with the schedule.

**Smaller things.**
- Column 1 ends with "seagulls." alone on the last line. Rewrap it or edit a word so the last line has more than one word.
- The rules sit about 11px from the column to their right and 20–27px from the column to their left, so each one seems to belong to the column it hugs. Centre them in the gap, or drop them and let the gap do the work.
- The three blocks look identical, so nothing marks which event is the highlight. Once there are headings, consider letting the new lantern walk lead.
- Taste: for a festival called Harbour Lights, the page has no warmth, light or place. It could be a parish council notice. One accent colour or one picture of the lanterns would make it feel like an event worth going to.

**What's working.**
- The text setting is good: a sturdy text serif, columns of about 45 characters, generous line spacing, dark text on white, and a clear bold title. Keep all of this when you add structure.
- The prose has a real voice: "the pie that makes the judges laugh", "guard the cake tent against hungry seagulls". That's what makes it local and likeable. Keep it next to the facts rather than cutting it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Reflow: check that the three columns stack in reading order on a phone, so the overview comes before the event details.
