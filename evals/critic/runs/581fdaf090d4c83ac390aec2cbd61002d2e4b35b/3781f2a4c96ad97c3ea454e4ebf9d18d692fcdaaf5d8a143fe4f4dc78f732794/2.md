**First read.** The eye goes to "Tell us about your project" at top left, then across to the white form card, then down to the blue "Send message" button. Within a second it reads as a small studio's contact page. It feels calm, competent and neutral, like a well-made template rather than the page of a studio that sells design.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the contact page of a design studio, for potential clients who are already interested and want to send an enquiry. Because the studio sells design, the page is also a small sample of its work.

**Verdict.** It works: the hierarchy is clear, there is one obvious action, and the promise of a reply is specific. Two things hold it back. The form doesn't ask for the budget and timeline that the copy requests, and the layout looks placed by eye rather than designed, which a studio can't afford on its own site.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form ignores the copy's request (defect).** The intro asks visitors to "include a budget range and a rough timeline". That request sits in the left column, but the form offers only one blank "Message" box with no prompt. By the time people are typing in the box, the request is out of view, so they have to remember it (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). Many enquiries will arrive without those two details, and the studio's first reply within "two working days" becomes a request for them. Fix: put the request at the field. Add a hint under the Message label ("Include your budget range and timeline"), or add two optional fields such as a budget range select and a timeline field. The help belongs next to the field where it's needed (Nielsen 10).
2. **The form card sits on no grid line, and the empty space is left over (convention, Müller-Brockmann / Arnheim).** The logo, headline, intro and footer all share a left edge at about 56 px, and the nav ends at about 1224 px. The card runs from about 508 to 1068 px, so its right edge lines up with nothing and leaves a 156 px empty strip on the right. That gap is too big to look aligned and too small to look like a choice (Arnheim: a placement that is neither clearly here nor clearly there looks unfinished). There are also two more unplanned empty areas: the left column below the intro paragraph, and a band of about 180 px between the card and the footer. All the weight sits in the upper left and centre. For most sites this would be a small flaw, but on a design studio's page it reads as carelessness. Fix: make the intro and the form two columns that fill the 56–1224 width with one clear gutter, or extend the card to the right margin. Then decide on purpose what the space below holds, such as a direct email address, the studio's location, or simply less height.

**Smaller things.**
- Nothing in the nav marks "Contact" as the current page; it looks the same as the other links. Give it one cue, such as bold or a different underline (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- No field is marked required or optional. Add an asterisk to each required label with a one-line key, or add "optional" to optional fields (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- The input borders look light grey on white, perhaps around 2.5:1. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11 (3:1 for field edges) and darken them if they fall short.
- The headline breaks as "Tell us about your / project", leaving "project" alone on the second line. Widen the measure so it fits on one line, or rebreak it on purpose.
- The headline is copy any agency could use, though on a contact page that costs little. If you want it to work harder, say what kind of project the studio takes on.

**What's working.**
- The promise "We read every message and reply within two working days" is concrete and something the studio can be held to. It answers the visitor's real worry, which is whether anyone will read the message. Keep it.
- The type scale is tight, with roughly four sizes (headline, logo, body/nav/button, labels and footer) that each have a clear job. Labels sit bold above their fields, close to their own input.
- There is one strong action: a filled blue button that nothing else competes with. Links are underlined blue, so they're clear without relying on colour alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the fields and button, error messages for an invalid email, the confirmation after sending, and how the two columns reflow on a narrow screen.
