# Crit: Fernwick Studio contact page (1280 × 800)

**First read.** The eye lands first on "Tell us about your project" at top left. It then crosses to the white form card and down to the blue "Send message" button. Within a second it reads as a small studio's contact page. It feels calm, plain and competent. It also feels like a template: nothing on it says anything about Fernwick except the name in the corner.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the contact page of a small design or creative studio. People who already like the work use it to send an enquiry, and the studio wants enquiries it can act on.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order is clear, the form is easy to read and one action leads. What holds it back is layout and intent. The card floats on no clear grid, with leftover space to its right and below it. And the form doesn't ask for the budget and timeline that the intro text requests, so the studio will get enquiries without them.

**What would move it most**

1. **The form card sits on no grid, and the space around it is leftover (convention).** The card runs from about x509 to x1067. Its left edge doesn't line up with anything in the header. Its right edge stops about 157 px short of the nav's right edge at x1224, leaving a dead strip on the right. Below the card there is an empty band of about 170 px before the footer. The headline column and the card share a top line but nothing else. To the viewer the card looks put there by eye, so the page reads as unfinished. That matters on a studio's site, where every page is also a sample of the studio's craft. Müller-Brockmann (*Grid Systems in Graphic Design*): every block should take its edges from one grid of columns and margins. Ruder (*Typography: A Manual of Design*): empty space should be gathered into a few shaped areas that do a job. Fix: set the page on one column grid whose outer edges match the header (x56 to x1224), and run the card to the right margin or to a clear column line. Either let the empty space below become a deliberate open field, or give it work to do (see 2).

2. **The intro asks for budget and timeline, but the form gives them nowhere to go (common sense; Tognazzini is not an approved source).** The text at left says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The form then offers one blank "Message" box with no prompt. Visitors read the intro, move to the form and forget it, and the studio gets "Hi, we'd love to chat" messages it has to answer with questions. Fix: put what the visitor needs where they type. Add optional "Budget range" and "Timeline" fields, ideally short dropdowns. Or at least add hint text under the Message label that repeats the request. This also gives the card enough height to sit properly on the page.

3. **The copy could be anyone's (taste).** "Tell us about your project" and "Send message" would fit any agency site, and nothing on the page says what Fernwick does or for whom. Someone who came here from a search result or a shared link can't tell whether this studio suits their project. The "reply within two working days" line is the strongest copy on the page, because it is specific and makes a promise. Do more of that: one line saying what kind of projects you take on, or a name and face behind the reply, so the reader knows who will answer.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Contact" looks the same as Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the current page. Give it one cue, such as bolder weight or no underline in dark text (Nielsen heuristic 1; NN/g menu-design checklist).
- Form labels: nothing says which fields are required. Add an asterisk to required labels and explain it once, or mark the optional ones "(optional)" (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). This matters more once the budget and timeline fields are added.
- Input borders: the grey outlines on white look pale, likely under 3:1. Measure them and darken to at least 3:1 so the fields read as fields (WCAG 1.4.11).
- Headline: "Tell us about your / project" leaves "project" alone on the second line and splits the phrase. Widen the text column slightly so it sets on one line, or break after "about".

**What's working.**
- The order of action is clean. One heavy headline, one form, one filled button, and quiet underlined links everywhere else. Keep the single blue for interactive things only.
- The form basics are right: labels sit above and close to their fields, it is one column, and the Message box is tall enough to invite a real description.
- "We read every message and reply within two working days" is a concrete promise that lowers the barrier to writing. Keep it, and keep it near the form.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Error and success states: whether bad emails are caught at the field and what confirms a message was sent.
- Focus rings on the fields and button.
- How the two columns reflow on a narrow screen.
