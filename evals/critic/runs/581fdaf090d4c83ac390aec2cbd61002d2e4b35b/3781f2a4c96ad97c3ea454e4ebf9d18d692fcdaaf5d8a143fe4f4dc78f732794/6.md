**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", drops to the grey subline, then moves right to the white card and the blue "Send message" button. It reads as a small studio's contact page: tidy and calm, but anonymous. Strip out "Fernwick Studio" and it could be any agency's contact template. The right fifth of the screen and the band under the card are empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a prospective client who already wants to work with the studio sends a first enquiry, and the studio wants enough detail (scope, budget, timing) to reply usefully.

**Verdict.** It works as a form: the fields are clearly labelled, there's one obvious action and nothing gets in the way. It does a poor job as the studio's last pitch. The one useful instruction, to include budget and timeline, sits far from the box where people type, and nothing on the page sounds like this particular studio.

**What would move it most**
1. **The budget-and-timeline request is in the wrong place.** The left-column paragraph asks for "a budget range and a rough timeline", but it sits about 450 px left of the Message box and is out of the reader's attention once they start typing. Many will write "Hi, we need a website" and hit Send. The studio then needs a second email before it can reply, which uses up most of the promised two days. The help needs to sit at the field it's for (Nielsen, heuristic 10). Fix direction: put a hint under or inside the Message label ("What you need, your budget range and when you'd like to start"), or add two short optional fields for budget and timing. **Defect.**
2. **The headline could be on anyone's page.** "Tell us about your project" is the stock contact-page line, and the page has no voice, proof or detail that belongs to Fernwick. A prospect comparing two or three studios gets no reason here to pick this one. The subline proves the page can be specific: "reply within two working days" is a concrete promise the reader can hold you to. Fix direction: write a headline as specific as that subline. Say what kind of project you take on, or what happens after they hit Send ("We'll reply within two working days with questions or a call time"). **Taste**, argued on the cost: on a studio's own site, generic copy suggests generic work.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Contact" looks the same as Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the current page. Give it one cue, such as bold weight or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Form card: nothing shows which fields are required. Mark them with an asterisk and a one-line key, or put "optional" after the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- Input borders: the mid-grey edges on white look close to or under 3:1. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11, and darken them one step if they fall short.
- Grid: the card's right edge (~x 1067) lines up with nothing, while the header runs out to ~x 1224, so the empty strip on the right reads as left over, not chosen. Take the card's right edge from the same grid as the header, or widen the left column so the two columns fill the width.
- Headline break: "your / project" splits the noun phrase and leaves "project" alone on line two. Break after "Tell us", or let the line run wider so it fits on one line.

**What's working.**
- The subline's promise ("We read every message and reply within two working days") is specific and believable. It does more to win trust than anything else on the page, so keep it.
- The form itself is clean: labels sit above their fields, label-to-field gaps are clearly smaller than the gaps between fields, and there is a single filled primary button whose label says exactly what it does.
- The type is restrained: one family, four clear levels, and contrast comes from weight as well as size.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What happens after Send: the error messages, the success confirmation, and whether it repeats the two-day promise.
- Focus rings on the fields and button.
- Whether the email field uses an email input type.
