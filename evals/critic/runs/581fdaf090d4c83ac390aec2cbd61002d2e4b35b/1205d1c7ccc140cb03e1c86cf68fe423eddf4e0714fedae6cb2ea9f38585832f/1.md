**Verdict.** It works. There is one clear focal point, a clean three-level hierarchy, and a quote worth reading. What holds it back from looking finished is how the opening quotation mark sits at the left edge, plus a block that sits too high in the frame.

**What would move it most**
1. **Hang the opening quotation mark.** At this size the “ takes the left margin, so "The" starts about 35 px to the right of "just", "leaves" and "the" below it. The quote's left edge, the strongest line on the page, comes out ragged on its first line. That first line is also where the kicker, name and caption sit flush at x = 100 and the quote doesn't. The usual fix is to push the mark out into the left margin so the letters of all four lines share one edge, aligned by eye rather than by the text box. This is a convention: the common typesetting practice is to hang punctuation in the margin, though the reference entry doesn't confirm a source for the quote-mark case. The entry's caveat says this flaw grows with type size, and at this size it's the most visible craft flaw on the page.

**Smaller things.**
- The space below the content is about 210 px against a top margin of about 65 px. The block looks like it stopped rather than sat. Either move the group down so the empty space looks intentional, or crop the frame closer. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- The contrast does more than one job at once. A large dark serif quote against small sans text makes a gap in size, structure and weight, so the three levels (quote, name, caption) read immediately and the small sizes don't need to differ much. Keep that pairing.
- The copy is specific: "just before the first ferry leaves… when the gulls get bold" and "5:40, bicycles, bread deliveries" give a real place and time. It couldn't belong to any other harbour, and that's what makes someone read on.
- The details are clean: curly quotes, an "fi" ligature in "first", a gently uneven right edge on the quote, and a caption line that stays a comfortable length.
