**First read.** The eye lands on "Notifications", then moves down the four bold setting names and over to the two blue toggles on the right. It reads straight away as the email settings screen of a task or team tool. It feels calm, plain and orderly. In version 2 the explanation under each setting fades back, so you scan bold label → toggle and mostly skip what each email actually is.

**What it's for.** My assumption: letting a user of a team task tool quickly choose which emails they get, with enough detail on each to choose well.

**Verdict.** Both versions are clean, well-built settings pages, and version 1 does the job better. The only change is that version 2 makes the description text a much lighter grey. That pushes the line that explains each choice below readable contrast, and on this page that line is what the user is deciding on.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's descriptions are too faint (defect).** The grey text under each setting ("A short email every Monday…", "sent at 8 am in your time zone") looks to be about 3:1 on white. WCAG 1.4.3 asks for at least 4.5:1 for text this size. In version 1 the same text is a dark slate that looks well above 4.5:1 and still sits clearly below the black labels. On this screen the descriptions carry the information that decides the choice: Mondays, the one-hour delay before a mention email, 8 am local time, at most one email a month. Fading them makes users toggle by label alone, and it shuts out low-vision users or anyone in bad light. Bold black labels over regular-weight text already show the hierarchy (Refactoring UI: lighter, not smaller), so the extra fade gains nothing. Fix: keep version 1's colour, or any grey that measures at least 4.5:1 on white.

**Smaller things.**
- Off-state toggles (Due dates, Product news), both versions: the pale grey track looks about 1.5:1 against white, so an off switch barely shows as a control (WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1). Darken the off track or give it an outline.
- Runts, both versions: "that are late." and "time zone." wrap onto their own short lines. Narrow the description column slightly or rebalance the breaks.

**What's working.** Each setting's copy says exactly what you'll get, when, and how often ("no more than one a month", "not opened it within an hour"), so the choice is concrete. The intro line also answers the obvious question by saying security emails always go out. The rows follow one tight program: bold label, explanation, toggle on the same right edge, hairline divider. The weight step between label and description gives hierarchy without adding another size, and version 1 keeps that while staying readable.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus and hover states on the toggles, whether changes save straight away or need confirming, and reflow at narrow widths.
