**First read.** The eye lands on "Crossing the river" at top left, drops to "Early boats", and reads down the left column. It then jumps up to the right column, which starts a little higher than "Early boats" and doesn't line up with it. It reads as a quiet editorial page, an essay or guide section about a ferry crossing, and it feels calm, literary and orderly.

**What it's for.** You didn't say, so I'm assuming this is a short descriptive article read on screen from start to finish by a general reader.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The type is well judged and easy to read, and the hierarchy is clear. The one real flaw is that the two columns aren't on the same baseline grid, so the page looks a little loose where it should look built.

**What would move it most**
1. **The two columns don't share lines (convention).** The right column begins at about y≈127, a few pixels above the cap height of "Early boats", so its first lines fall about half a line off the left column. "Stallholders arrive…" sits between "Early boats" and "The first ferry…", and the next three lines alternate the same way. Further down they drift back into step by accident. A reader moving across the gap sees two blocks floating slightly apart, not one page. Müller-Brockmann (*Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid") asks that neighbouring columns sit on the same baselines. Fix: put the right column's first baseline on the same line as a left-column line. Hanging it from the line of "The first ferry…" is the usual choice. Then size the space around headings as whole multiples of the line spacing, so "Going home" and "The market quay" stay on the grid too.

**Smaller things.**
- Column ends: the left column runs about three lines past the right (y≈520 against y≈440). Balancing them, or moving the break so both end on the same line, would square off the bottom edge (common sense).
- The paragraph indent looks close to 2 em. Bringhurst asks for at least an en and is happy with an em. A slightly smaller indent would keep the texture smooth (taste).

**What's working.**
- **Clear type scale:** title, section heads and body are three distinct sizes in one serif family, with weight doing extra work. Each level is obvious at a glance and nothing competes.
- **Correct paragraph marks:** text after each heading starts flush left, later paragraphs are indented, and there's no extra space between them. The columns keep an even grey (Bringhurst 2.3.1–2.3.2).
- **Comfortable line length and spacing:** about 55–62 characters per line with generous leading suits continuous reading. Headings sit clearly closer to their own text than to the section above.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the two columns behave on a narrow screen. On mobile they should collapse to one column in reading order.
