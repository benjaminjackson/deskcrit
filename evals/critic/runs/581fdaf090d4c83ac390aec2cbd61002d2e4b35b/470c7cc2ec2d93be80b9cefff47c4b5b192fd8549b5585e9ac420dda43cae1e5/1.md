**First read.** The eye goes to the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers", then down a narrow grey column of widely spaced lines. It reads as a calm, plain information page for people using a small river ferry. It feels tidy and quiet. It also feels a little airy and loose, as if the lines don't belong to each other.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page passengers check to decide when to catch the ferry and whether they can bring a bike, often on a phone and in a hurry.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and readable, but it doesn't do its main job. A passenger can't plan a trip from it, because it gives no actual times. The second problem is the very loose line spacing, which breaks each paragraph into separate lines.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy gives no times a passenger can act on.** *(Defect)* "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" and "before dusk" are what a passenger plans around, and each leaves them guessing. Is the first boat at 6:00 or 7:30? Does "before dusk" mean 15:30 in December? Which minute past the hour? Someone who reads "late evening" as 22:00 and arrives at 21:30 for a boat that stopped at 21:00 is stranded on the wrong bank. "In winter" has the same gap: which months? Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing, the minutes past the hour (or a short timetable), and the dates the winter timetable starts and ends. The 12-minute crossing time is a good concrete detail; the rest should be that specific.
2. **The line spacing is so loose that paragraphs come apart.** *(Convention, plus taste on how far to go)* The body text looks about 19px with about 45px from one line to the next, so roughly 2.4× the type size. Each line floats as its own strip. The gap between paragraphs (about 64px from line to line) is only a little bigger than the gap between lines, so the three topics (schedule, bikes, winter) don't separate at a glance. Bringhurst (2.2.1) and Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 47) say line spacing should suit the face and the measure. At about 70 characters this measure needs something near 1.5×, not 2.4×. Fix: bring the line spacing down to about 1.5. Then make the paragraph gap clearly bigger than the line gap (about one blank line). Each paragraph will hold together and the three topics will separate.

**Smaller things.**
- The three topics have no labels, so someone looking only for the bike rule has to read every paragraph. Short lead-ins ("Timetable", "Bicycles", "Winter") would make it scannable. That matters more once real times are added.
- "Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded" reads as a description, not an instruction. Speak to the reader: "Wait until foot passengers have boarded, then wheel your bike on and lock it to the rail."

**What's working.**
- The type is restrained. There are two sizes, and the heading stands apart through weight as well as size, so the order of reading is obvious and nothing competes.
- The column length is about 70 characters, inside Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Keep that width when you tighten the spacing.
- The left edge is sharp and the dark text on white is high contrast, so the page reads easily.
