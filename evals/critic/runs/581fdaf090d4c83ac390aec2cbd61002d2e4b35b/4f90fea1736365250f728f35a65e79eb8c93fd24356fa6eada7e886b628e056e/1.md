**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", reads down through the paragraph and the short list, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. Then it drops to the last line on the page: "Design review: failed, everything on this page is centred". It reads as a small local ferry's landing page for day-trippers and locals. It feels calm and tidy, but sparse: the whole right half of the screen is blank. (That last line is page content, not evidence about the design, and it's wrong: every block on the page is flush left.)

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a desktop landing page that should get a would-be passenger interested in the crossing and on to the timetable.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. You can tell what this is in a second, the headline clearly leads, and there's one clear next step. Two things hold it back: a stray internal note ships as the last thing people read, and the page asks for a click before it gives the facts someone needs to decide on a crossing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Design review: failed…" line under the button is on the live page.** It's the last thing the eye reaches, so the visit ends on what looks like an internal QA note. That makes the ferry company look careless and adds nothing to the decision to travel. **Defect** (Nielsen: every element should support the main task). Remove it, and let the button be the last thing on the page.
2. **The facts that decide the trip are hidden behind the button.** "Cross the bay" never says where to. "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" gives no first or last sailing. The page has no price and doesn't say where the north landing is. Someone deciding whether to go needs those things, so they have to click just to find out if the trip suits them. **Defect, with my own judgement on the fix.** Bring the deciding facts onto this page in plain words: the destination, the first and last boat (or the next departure), and the fare. Then "See the timetable" becomes the place to plan details, not a gate. The button label itself is good: it names one exact thing you'll get.
3. **The right half of the screen is empty, so the page leans left and looks unfinished.** All the weight sits in the left 60% at 1280 px, and nothing on the right balances it. That reads as a missing picture or a template, not as chosen space. It also wastes the best chance to show "calm" and "the harbour slide past", which the copy promises but the page never shows. **Defect/convention** (Arnheim on balance: a heavy side needs a counterweight). The usual fix is a photo from the open deck in that space, sized to the text column's top and bottom. If you want a text-only page, centre the column on the page or narrow the whole layout so the empty space looks deliberate. If an image was supposed to load there, this finding goes away.

**Smaller things.**
- **The feature list blends into the paragraph above it.** The list ("Bicycles travel free" and the rest) is set only a hair smaller than the paragraph, with no bullets, weight change or icon. It looks like stray lines rather than a scannable list. Mark it as a list with one clear change, such as a tick or a bold lead word, not a one-step size change (Dair).
- **The type sizes are muddled.** I count about five: ~52 headline, ~19 paragraph, ~18 list, ~17 button, ~14 brand label. The 17, 18 and 19 collide. Keep three or four (headline / paragraph / small label) and get the other differences from weight and colour (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- **The headline breaks badly.** "twelve" ends line 1, cut off from "calm minutes". Break it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" so the promise reads as one piece.
- **The copy repeats itself.** "Seats on the open deck" restates the paragraph's "watch… from the open deck". Use that list slot for a fact the paragraph doesn't give, such as the fare or "Step-free boarding".

**What's working.**
- **The headline is specific and believable.** "Twelve calm minutes" gives a number and a feeling, which most landing-page headlines don't. Keep it as the one dominant element.
- **The column is clean and flush left.** Lines run about 70 characters with generous leading, and the gaps between eyebrow, headline, paragraph, list and button grow in a sensible order.
- **There is one strong action.** It's a filled blue button with high contrast, a clear target and a label that names exactly what you'll get. Blue is kept for the brand name and the button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half was meant to hold an image that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- How the column and button reflow on a phone.
