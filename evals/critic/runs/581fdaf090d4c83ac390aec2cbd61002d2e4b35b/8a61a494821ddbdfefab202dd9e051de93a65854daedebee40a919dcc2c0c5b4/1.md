**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the lead item of a library newsletter (email or web) that has to tell patrons the reading room reopens and make them want to come back.

**Verdict.** It looks right but gets the job backwards. The page leads with a quote you can't make sense of without context, and the news (it reopens Monday, with new lamps, shelves and a puzzle table) is the smallest, faintest thing on it. The typesetting gets the classic display details wrong too: straight quote marks, a hyphen where a dash should be, an unhung opening quote and a bad line break. At this size those are the first things a careful reader sees.

**What would move it most**

1. **The order of importance is upside down: the news comes last and faintest.** The quote gets about 95 px serif. The attribution gets bold black. The only line that tells a patron something they can act on, "reopens on Monday with new lamps, new shelves, and a table set aside for jigsaw puzzles", sits at the bottom in small grey type. So the eye path ends on the weakest element, and a skimmer, which is most newsletter readers, leaves with a nice line and no news. *Defect.* The fix is to make the reopening the second thing the eye reaches, straight after the quote. Give it the weight and colour the attribution has now, and drop the attribution to the quiet grey, since who said it matters less than what's happening. Or put a short news headline above or under the quote. Either way the quote can stay big. It just needs to set up the news, not stand in for it.

2. **The quote doesn't make sense to a stranger, and it isn't connected to the news.** "It's the 'old' reading room – nobody calls it anything else": why the scare quotes around *old*? Is the room being renamed? Is "old" a fond nickname, or a hint that the refit is unwelcome? The person who picked the quote knows. The reader has to guess, and could easily read it as a complaint. Nothing ties it to the reopening either. The quote is about a name and the news is about a refit, so the page carries two ideas and links neither. *Defect* (copy). The fix is one of two things. Pick a quote that lands the reopening for the reader, such as what the volunteer is looking forward to. Or keep this one and add the missing clause, e.g. that even after the refit, it will still be the "old" reading room to everyone.

3. **The display typesetting gives away the craft at 95 px.** The quote and apostrophe marks are straight typewriter marks (", ', 'old') instead of curly quotes. "room - nobody" uses a spaced hyphen where a dash belongs (Bringhurst, 5.2.1, on dashes). The opening quote mark sits flush on the margin, so the first line looks pushed in compared with "room" and "anything" below it. Hanging the quote into the margin keeps the left edge clean. "reading / room" is split across lines 1 and 2, breaking the one noun phrase the quote is about. At body size these would be fine print. At this size they're the most visible marks on the page, and they undercut the literary tone the serif is going for. *Convention* (dashes and the line break), plus *taste and common sense* (curly quotes and the hanging quote, where I have no checked source). It's typesetting, not a redesign: use curly marks and an en or em dash, hang the opening quote, and rebreak so "reading room" stays together, e.g. "It's the 'old' / reading room – nobody / calls it anything else,".

**Smaller things.**
- "Monday" has no date and no time. People read newsletters days late, so a reader on Tuesday can't tell whether this means yesterday or next week. Say "Monday 30 November, from 9am" (or whatever the real time is).
- There's no next step. Nothing says where the room is, when it's open, or invites the reader to come and try the puzzle table. One line of practical detail would turn the charm into a visit.

**What's working.**
- The type pairing: a warm text serif for the voice of the quote against a clean sans for the reporting. The contrast is clear and fits a library. Keep it.
- The restraint with colour: a cream ground, near-black text, and one rust accent used only on the "LIBRARY NEWSLETTER" label. It feels calm and of a piece, and the accent marks the section without competing.
- The 110 px left margin, which every element shares, and the body text kept to a short, comfortable line. The page holds together even with a lot of open space.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the large quote rebreaks on a phone-width screen. The "reading / room" split and the hanging quote need checking at each breakpoint.
