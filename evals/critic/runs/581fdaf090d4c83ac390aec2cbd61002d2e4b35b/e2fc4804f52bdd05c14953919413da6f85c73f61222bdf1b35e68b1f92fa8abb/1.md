**First read.** The eye lands on the dark image block at top left, drops to the bold headline, then to the black "Book a free seat" button. It reads clearly as an independent bookshop's page for one author evening, and it feels quiet and sensible. Then the eye goes right and finds nothing: half the screen is blank. That empty half, along with a faceless grey silhouette where the author should be, is what you remember.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a bookshop's event page on desktop. Its job is to get local readers to reserve a free seat for an author reading.

**Verdict.** The core of it works: a clear headline, a short, warm description and one specific ask. But the page looks unfinished, and the line people act on, the date, can't be trusted as written. Fix the date first, then the empty right half and the stand-in image.

**What would move it most**
1. **The date can be read two ways (defect).** "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. 13 November 2026 is a Friday, and 13 November 2025 was a Thursday. Today is 23 November 2026, so one of two things is true. Either the day name is wrong and people will turn up on the wrong evening, or this is last year's event still being promoted with a live "Book" button. Date and time are what people put in their diary and hold the shop to. Check the date against the real event, add the year if the page could ever be read near the turn of the year, and take the booking button down or mark the event as over once it has passed.
2. **Half the screen is empty, and it reads as broken, not airy (defect for this page; convention: Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"; Ruder, *Typography*, "Form and counter-form").** All the content sits in a column about 600 px wide on the left. From x≈650 to 1232, from the top bar down, there is nothing. The space has no shape and no tension against anything, so it doesn't feel like a choice; it looks like a layout that failed to load its second column. The visitor wonders whether something is missing, and the page feels thinner than the event. Make the space deliberate, in one of two ways. Use the right column for what people need next (point 3) and keep the image and headline on the left. Or give the hero the full width of the type area so the left column isn't leaning against nothing.
3. **What people need in order to commit is missing (defect; common sense).** There is no venue or address, only "Find us" in the menu. There is no end time, no word on how many seats are left or whether it might fill up, and nothing about what happens after "Book a free seat" (do I get an email? do I need a ticket?). Someone who has never been to the shop has to go elsewhere to find out where it is before they can decide. The empty right column is the obvious home for a short block of key facts: where, when it starts and ends, seats left, and a line on what booking gets you.
4. **The image is a stand-in, not the author (defect, unless it is still loading).** The main image, the largest thing on the page, is a generic person silhouette on a dark gradient. It doesn't show Ines Varga or *The Salt Year*, so it adds nothing to the headline and takes up the spot where a face or a book cover would make people want to come. If the real photo failed to load in this capture, this point goes away. If not, use an author photo or the cover; the island in the description would also make a strong picture.

**Smaller things.**
- Top navigation: "Events" looks the same as the other links, so nothing tells the visitor which section they're in. Mark it with a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").

**What's working.**
- The ask is exact and low-risk: "Book a free seat" says what you get, that it's free, and what to do, and it's the only heavy dark button on the page. Keep it that specific.
- The description reads like a person talking: a reading, a short talk about the island, questions, then "Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm." It tells you what the evening will feel like in two lines. Don't let a revision turn it into marketing copy.
- The text has a clear order. The small rust-coloured bold date sits above a large bold headline, then normal-weight body text, then the button. Each level is set apart by weight and colour as well as size, and the spacing groups them as one unit.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the silhouette is the design or an image that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- What the layout does at narrower widths. The empty right half may come from a two-column layout that only renders on mobile.
- Hover and focus states on the button and menu links.
