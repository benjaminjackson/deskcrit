**First read.** The eye goes straight to the "Your account" heading, then across the four cards in reading order. It reads at once as a shop's account overview for a returning customer. In version 1, the near-black 2px frames around all five boxes are the second-darkest thing on the page, so it looks like a wireframe and feels heavy and boxed-in. Version 2 has exactly the same content and layout with pale hairline borders, and it reads as calm and finished: the text carries the page, not the frames.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the overview page of an online shop's customer account, where someone checks their details, last order, address and card and goes on to change one of them.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the border weight, and that change takes away the one thing that competed with the content in version 1. Both versions share one remaining weakness: fixed-height cards that are half empty.

**What would move it most**
1. **The heavy frames in version 1 compete with the content. Version 2 fixes this.** In version 1, every card and the sidebar sit inside a dark 2px outline about as dark as the headings. The page reads as five boxed compartments before it reads as information, and the black lines pull the eye away from the blue action links, which are the things a visitor came to use. The borders carry no information: the cards are already separated by the white fill on the off-white page and by the gaps between them. Version 2 does what the approved fix asks: it replaces the dark rules with a pale tone, so the lines no longer compete with the content (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*; Tufte on non-data ink). This is a convention, and breaking it buys nothing here. Keep version 2's treatment.

**Smaller things**
- In both versions, every card is a fixed ~280px tall, but its content ends about halfway down. Each card has roughly 110–140px of dead white space at the bottom, and the sidebar box runs well past its last link for no reason. The empty space is leftover, not shaped, so the page looks unfinished. Let cards size to their content, or let each row match its tallest card, and fit the sidebar to its list.

**What's working**
- Each card follows the same clear pattern: a bold heading, two or three lines of plain fact, then one specific text link ("Change card", "Manage addresses"). The visitor can find any task without reading the whole page. Keep it.
- The copy is concrete and in the customer's own terms: "Order 48213, placed 12 October", "Two linen pillow cases", "Delivered 15 October", "Card ending 4417". Nothing is left for the reader to work out.
- The type scale is tight, with three sizes (page title, card heading, body), and each size has an obvious job.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Hover and focus states on the text links and nav items, and how the 2×2 grid reflows at narrow widths.
