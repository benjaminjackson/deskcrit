**First read.** The eye goes to the purple "Sign in" button, then up to the red box around the email field. It reads as a tidy, standard SaaS sign-in card, and it looks calm and competent. Then the moment stalls: something is red, but nothing says why. I read the email twice before I spotted "exmaple" and noticed the address also has no ".com".

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is Quillpost's desktop sign-in screen, captured after a failed submit, with the email field in its error state.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and does its basic job, but the one state this screenshot exists to show, the error, doesn't work. A red outline with no words leaves the user to work out what went wrong, and anyone who can't tell red from grey doesn't see an error at all.

**What would move it most**
1. **The email error is only a red border, with no message** (defect). The 2px red outline on the email field is the only sign of failure. There's no text, no icon, and no hint of how to fix it. The user knows something is wrong but not what: is the address unknown, badly formed, or is the password wrong? So they retype things at random. Users with red-green colour blindness see a slightly darker border and may not notice the error at all. This fails WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) and 3.3.1 (Error Identification), and Nielsen's heuristic 9. Fix: put one line of plain text right under the field, with an icon, that names the problem and the fix, e.g. "This email is missing its ending, like .com. Did you mean dana.ortiz@example.com?" The typo here is one a suggestion could catch (WCAG 3.3.3, Error Suggestion). Keep what the user typed, which the design already does. If the real failure is a wrong email/password pair, say that in one message above the button instead, and don't outline only the email field. Outlining just that field points the blame at the wrong place.

**Smaller things.**
- "Create an account" at the bottom is the same grey, weight and size as "New here?", so nothing shows it's a link (Norman, signifiers; WCAG 1.4.1). Give it the purple used for "Forgot your password?", or underline it.
- The password field border looks pale grey on white, probably below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of input edges. Measure it, and darken it if it falls short.

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy.** There's one filled purple button and two quieter text links, so the main action is never in doubt. Keep that ranking when you add the error text.
- **A tight type scale.** About four sizes (heading, wordmark, field and button text, labels and links), with bold labels sitting closer to their own fields than to the field above. The form scans in one pass.
- **The typed value is kept** in the error state, so fixing a typo is one edit rather than a full retype.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the email is checked when the user leaves the field or only on submit.
- What the focus ring looks like on the fields and the button.
- How the card reflows at 320px wide.
