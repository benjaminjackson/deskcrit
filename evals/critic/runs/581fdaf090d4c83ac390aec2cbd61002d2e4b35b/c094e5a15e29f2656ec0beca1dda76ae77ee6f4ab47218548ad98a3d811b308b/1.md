**First read.** The eye lands on the big dark-green "What we do each week". In version 2 it drops straight down the left edge through both paragraphs to the three green numbers. In version 1 it lands on the heading, then has to hunt for the start of each centred line, then jumps back left to find the stats. Both versions read as a calm, sincere community food bank page in green and white. Version 1 also feels a little like a greeting card.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an "about" section of a local food bank's website. It tells donors, volunteers and people who need food what happens each week and how people get a parcel.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The one change, setting the paragraphs flush left instead of centred, gives the page a single left edge and makes the eight lines of body text easy to read. Version 2 is a clean, competent page. The biggest thing still in its way is that it explains how to get help but gives nothing to click.

**What changed, and why version 2 wins**
- In version 1, both 4-line paragraphs are centred, but the heading and the stats row sit flush left on the 96 px column line. That gives the page two alignment systems.
  - The centre line is not even the page centre. It sits at about x≈475, the middle of the text column, so the block floats.
  - Each line starts in a different place, so after every line the reader has to search for where the next one begins. Over eight lines of real prose that slows reading. People who need food are probably reading this carefully, so the slowdown hurts here.
  - Centring is meant for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style* 2.1.3).
- Version 2 sets the paragraphs flush left. Heading, paragraphs and stats now share one edge, the rag is gentle, and the eye goes straight down the page. Rated as a defect in version 1, fixed in version 2.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The page explains how to get help but gives no way to act on it.** The second paragraph is the most important text for someone who needs food. It says you need a referral, names four centres and promises delivery if you can't travel.
   - None of this links anywhere: no "Find your nearest centre", no "How to get a referral", no "Ask for a delivery".
   - The reader has to go back up to "Get help" in the nav and hope it covers the same ground.
   - Fix: put one specific next step right under that paragraph, for example a link that says "See the four centres and their opening times". The viewer should be able to act on what they just read without hunting for it. Rated as a defect: it slows down the very reader this paragraph is for.

**Smaller things.**
- The stats are bare numbers. "96 volunteers" and "4 collection points" have no comparison or point of reference, so they inform but don't persuade. If donors are an audience, give one number some context (households fed, or a change from last year).
- About the bottom third of the frame and the right ~40% are empty. That is fine if more content follows below. If this is the whole section, the stats row ends the page abruptly.

**What's working.**
- **The flush-left column (version 2).** Heading, body and stats share one left edge, so the reading order is obvious. Keep this when you add anything new.
- **The type pairing.** A bold sans for the heading, nav and stats against a readable serif for the body gives a clear contrast in structure. The levels are distinct without relying on size alone.
- **The copy is concrete.** Days, Carver Road, three days of meals, referral sources, home delivery for people who can't travel. It reads like a real organisation, not a template.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the nav looks on hover and focus, and how the 750 px text column reflows on a phone.
