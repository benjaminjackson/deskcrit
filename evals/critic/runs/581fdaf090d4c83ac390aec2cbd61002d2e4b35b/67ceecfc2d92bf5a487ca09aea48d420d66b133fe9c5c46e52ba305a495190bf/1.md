**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Help for ferry passengers", then drops to the two bold subheads, which split the page into "before" and "when things go wrong", and settles into the left list. It reads as a plain, calm help page for a small local ferry. It's quiet and trustworthy, and it doesn't fuss. The empty lower third and the half-empty right column make it look a little unfinished, but not broken.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web help page for ferry passengers. It tells them how to prepare for a crossing and how to report a problem.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job: it's clear, readable and well ordered. The copy lets it down where it matters most, at the points where a passenger has to act. The page names a deadline with no time and gives a mailing address that isn't on the page, so in both places the passenger has to go and look somewhere else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts a passenger acts on are missing (defect).** Two lines need fixing.
   - **Bullet 1, "the last boat leaves before dusk":** this is the one fact a passenger plans their day around, and it has no time. In December, "before dusk" could mean 15:30 or 16:30, and someone who guesses late misses the last boat. "Winter sailings run once an hour" has the same gap: it doesn't say when winter starts or what happens in summer.
   - **Step 3, "send it to the address on the noticeboard in the shelter":** someone who got home and found a problem can't use this without travelling back to the quay. That's the person most likely to be reading the page.

   The fix is to print the facts here: the last-sailing time for each season (or "check the board; in December the last boat is around 15:45"), and the actual postal or email address. Also add a line that sets expectations, such as when they'll hear back and who to contact for anything urgent. Right now "If something goes wrong" covers lost property and an injury in the same way, with a note in a box.
2. **The bullets and numbers don't hang (convention).** In both columns, the wrapped lines return to the marker's edge instead of lining up under the first word. For example, "because winter sailings…" sits under the bullet dot, and "name of the landing…" sits under the "1.". So the left edge of each list is ragged where it should be crisp. On a page people skim, that hides where each item starts. The two lists also handle the gap after the marker differently: the bullets have a wide gap, the numbers a narrow one. That makes them look set by two hands, not one system (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, §4.4.2, recommends making lists clear through spacing and hanging numbers in the margin. The fix is a hanging indent for both lists: hang the markers in the margin or indent the wrapped lines to the text, with the same gap after "•" and "1.".

**Smaller things.**
- Bullet 4 ends with "clear." alone on its last line. Rewrite or rebreak the line so the last line has more than one word (Bringhurst §2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- **The two-column split is the right structure.** "Before you board" and "If something goes wrong" map to the two moments someone comes to this page. The bullets-vs-numbers choice is also correct: the reporting steps really are in order, and the tips aren't.
- **The type is calm and well judged.** It uses one family, with weight, not just size, separating the levels. The heading is about 40 px bold, the subheads about 21 px bold and the body about 18 px regular. The measure is about 60–65 characters with generous leading, and there's dark text on white. Keep all of this.
- **The tips explain why.** "Since the machine is slow to give change" and "so that the gangway is kept clear" make the requests feel reasonable, not bossy. Keep that voice when you add the missing facts.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the two columns reflow to one on a phone, which is likely where people read this at the landing. Also focus styles, if any links get added (for example, the address).
