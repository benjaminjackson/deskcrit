**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the opening screen of a narrative feature on a small editorial site. Its job is to get a reader who arrived from a link to commit to the whole piece.

**Verdict.** The reading experience mostly works: there's a clear hierarchy, comfortable body text and a strong opening. But the screen has no picture at all, even though the byline promises "Photographs by the residents". So the story's best asset is missing, and the page could be anyone's template. That's the one thing that would move it most.

**What would move it most**
1. **The promised photographs aren't on screen, so the page looks generic and the byline overpromises.** The byline says "Photographs by the residents", but the first screen shows only text with about 200 px of empty white below it. A reader gets no allotment, no bath-tub beds and no faces. It's the one thing that would make this story different from every other text column. The credit also sets up an expectation the screen doesn't meet. If a lead image failed to load in the capture, check that in a live browser. If the design really opens with text only, it's the wrong call for this story. *Defect* (honesty: the credit names something that isn't there) plus *taste* (the mood doesn't fit: neutral grey on white is cool and corporate for a warm community story). Direction: open with one wide resident photo between the byline and the first paragraph, or above the headline, sized to the text column or wider by a clear amount. That gives the screen its second level and the story its world.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks "bus / line" and leaves "line" alone on its second line. That splits the phrase and gives a weak shape. Rebreak it ("The allotment at the end / of the bus line") or narrow the headline measure so the break falls at a phrase.
- The body measure looks like about 85–90 characters a line, a bit over the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrow the column by about 80–100 px or bump the body size a step.
- After the headline every block is the same grey text, with no pull quote, image or subhead to vary the rhythm (taste). One image fixes most of this. Later in the piece, a pull quote from Halima Osei would do the rest.

**What's working.**
- The headline is plainly dominant: heavy, large and dark against a quiet, lighter byline and a small green "Feature" kicker. The three steps read at a glance. Keep that ratio when you add an image, and don't let the photo out-shout it.
