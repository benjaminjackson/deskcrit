**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big blue "Planning a vegetable bed" and then down the left edge through three smaller blue headings: soil, planting, watering. In about two seconds it reads as a calm, plain how-to page for a beginner gardener. It feels tidy and friendly, and a bit sparse.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a step-by-step guide for a first-time gardener setting up one bed, read on a laptop screen.

**Verdict.** It works. The hierarchy is clear, the type is readable and the tone is right. The biggest thing in the way is structure: this is a sequence of steps, but it's laid out as a set of topics, and step one has no heading.

**What would move it most**
1. **The first and most important step has no heading, and the order isn't shown.** Choosing the spot (sun and wind) and marking it out sits under the page title as an intro paragraph. The other three steps each get a blue heading. Someone skimming the headings sees soil, planting and watering, and skips the one decision they can't easily undo later. The headings also read as topics, not steps, so nothing tells the reader that the soil has to settle "for a week" *before* they plant. **Defect** for the skimming reader, and a broken pattern: one of four matching sections is styled differently (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Fix: give the site step its own heading in the same style as the others, such as "Choosing the spot". Then show the order with numbers or short step labels, so the timing between steps is clear.
2. **Body lines run long.** The paragraphs span about 790 px, which looks like 85–90 characters per line, for example "Choose a spot that gets six hours of sun and stays out of the wind. Mark the corners with". That's above the 45–75 character range Bringhurst gives (*Elements of Typographic Style* 2.1.2). Paragraphs are only two lines now, so the cost is small, but it grows as soon as any section gets longer. **Convention.** Fix: narrow the text column to about 600–650 px at this size, aiming for about 66 characters. The empty space on the right can easily take it.

**Smaller things.**
- "year's" in the soil paragraph uses a straight typewriter apostrophe. Use a curly one (’). This is common sense; no checked source covers curly quotes.
- Copy: "six hours of sun" means per day, but the page doesn't say so. Write "six hours of sun a day" so a beginner doesn't have to guess.
- Copy: "Watering" is a single line, so it reads as an afterthought next to the others. Say when to water more often (heat, seedlings), or the advice reads as a fixed rule.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale.** There are three clear sizes, roughly 48, 30 and 18 px, with big steps between them, and both heading levels are bold. Every level is obvious at a glance. Keep it to three.
- **Headings sit tight to their own text.** There's a small gap below each heading and a larger gap above it, so each section reads as one block.
- **The copy speaks like a person.** "Start with things that forgive mistakes: radishes, lettuce, and bush beans" names real choices from the reader's world and reassures a beginner. Keep this voice when you add the missing step heading.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Reflow on a narrow phone screen. This is likely to be read outdoors on a phone, so check that the column and headings reflow cleanly at 320 px.
