Version 2 does the job better. The two pages are identical except for the body text, and that one change decides it.

**First read.** The eye lands on the large serif headline, "A restored hay barn for weddings of up to 120 guests", then drops to the brown "Book a visit" button, then drifts to the big empty tan panel on the right. It reads as the homepage of a rural wedding venue, warm and unhurried. In version 1 the eye stalls on the two paragraphs, which are set in a heavy brush script and read like a handwritten note you have to decode. In version 2 the same paragraphs read straight through.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the homepage of a wedding barn. It should tell couples planning a wedding what the place is, convince them it's worth seeing, and get them to book a site visit.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job and version 1 doesn't. Version 1 sets the paragraphs, the part that actually sells the barn, in the same brush script as the logo. That makes about 90 words of real selling copy hard to read, so many couples will skim past them to the button. Version 2 sets them in a plain text serif that matches the headline, and nothing else changes. What still holds version 2 back is the empty picture panel.

**Comparison: what changed and why it matters**
- **Body face (decisive).** Version 1 sets the two paragraphs in the brush script. Its joined, slanted, heavy letters force letter-by-letter reading at body size. The line breaks also leave "day." alone at the end of paragraph 2. Bringhurst: a text face is "unlikely to be carrying excess ornament" (*Elements of Typographic Style* 6.2.1). Script is for a few words of display, which is exactly how the logo uses it. Version 1 also gives the script two jobs, brand mark and running text, so the logo loses what made it special. Version 2 fixes both problems: the serif paragraphs match the headline, the script is kept for "Hollins Barn" alone, and the type reads as one system.
- **Side effects.** The serif sets a little wider, so the button moves down about 29 px. That costs nothing.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The picture is empty (defect, unless the photo just didn't load).** The right half of the page, about 470×520 px, is a plain tan gradient. A venue sells itself with photos more than words, and this half of the page shows nothing: no beams, no glass doors, no orchard. The headline says "restored hay barn" and the page never shows it, so the couple has to take it on trust. If a real photo loads here in the live page, this goes away. If not, put in the one photo that proves the headline: the restored interior with its beams, or the barn from the orchard with the glass doors open.
2. **The button doesn't say what the visit is (copy).** "Book a visit" names an action, which is good, but a couple can't tell what they're committing to. Is it a guided walk-round? Is it free? How long does it take? The paragraph right above already promises "we will walk you through the site as many times as you need", so the button can pick that up, for example "Book a free walk-round". Saying it's free and has no obligation lowers the barrier for a couple who are only comparing venues. This is taste, and a minor point beside finding 1.

**Smaller things.**
- **Picture and text edges don't line up.** The image top (about y 156) sits just above the headline's cap height, and its bottom (y 676) stops about 11 px above the button's bottom edge (y 687). Both are near-misses, so they read as unplanned. End the image on the button's baseline or clearly short of it (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: a picture takes its top and bottom from the lines of text).
- **Awkward headline break.** "120 / guests" leaves "guests" alone on the third line. Rewording or a slightly wider measure would bring the number and noun together, but this is cosmetic.

**What's working (version 2).**
- **The headline is specific.** "Restored hay barn" and "up to 120 guests" answer the two questions a couple asks first, what the place is and whether their wedding fits. Keep that specificity.
- **The body copy is concrete.** 1870, taken apart "beam by beam" in 2019, a kitchen caterers can use "from early morning", bring your own caterer, band, and florist. These are things a couple can weigh, not venue boilerplate.
- **The type system is clean.** Script for the name only, serif for headline and body, a small sans for the nav and button. There are about four sizes with clear jumps between them and one brown accent for the brand and the action.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- **Reflow.** Check how the two columns stack on a phone, and whether the button stays near the copy.
