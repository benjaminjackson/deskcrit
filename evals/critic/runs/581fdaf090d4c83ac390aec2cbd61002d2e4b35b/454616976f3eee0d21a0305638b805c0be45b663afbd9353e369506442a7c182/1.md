**First read.** The eye lands on the block of blue bars, climbs to the three big figures, and settles on $4.82M. It reads straight away as the revenue dashboard of a subscription business, for a founder or finance lead. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and also a lot like a template.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the home screen of a SaaS billing tool, where a founder or finance lead sees how revenue is doing at a glance and trusts the numbers enough to repeat them.

**Verdict.** This is competent, clean work. It is held back mostly by one thing: the numbers on the page don't agree with each other. On a finance screen that costs more than any layout flaw. After that, the chart shows the trend but never says what it means.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan panel's total doesn't match the chart (defect).** "Revenue by plan, 2025" totals $4.82M, which is exactly the December ARR in the first card. The twelve monthly bars add up to only about $4.49M (roughly 312 + 328 + … + 447 thousand). December's ~$447K times 12 is about $5.4M, which matches neither. A finance reader who adds it up will see three different annual figures for the same business, and will doubt every number on the screen. Whoever reads it the other way will take $4.82M as 2025 revenue and report it wrong. The panel is probably ARR by plan as of December, labelled as annual revenue. Make it say what it measures ("ARR by plan, Dec 2025") or make it add up to the chart. Do the same check on every total on the page.
2. **The chart title gives the topic, not the result (taste and common sense; no approved source).** "Monthly revenue, 2025" is the largest thing on the page, yet it leaves the reader to work out the story. That story is about 43% growth from January to December, with dips only in April and July. A title like "Monthly revenue grew 43% in 2025" gives the viewer the conclusion in one look, and they can still check it against the bars.

**Smaller things.**
- **Grid:** the three KPI cards split the width into thirds, with edges near x 574, 593, 907 and 926. The row below uses its own split: the chart ends near x 960 and the plan panel starts near x 1012. Neither lines up with a card edge, so the lower row looks placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann). Put the chart and panel on the card columns, for example the chart across two cards and the panel under the third.
- **Empty block, bottom right:** below the plan table, roughly x 1012–1240 and y 430–800, is leftover space. Fill it or shorten the column, for instance by giving each plan its share of the total or a small bar.
- **Type sizes:** I count about eight. The page title (~26px bold) and the KPI figures (~30px bold) are too close to read as different levels. Drop one step, or set the title in a lighter weight.
- **Panel titles:** "Monthly revenue" and "Revenue by plan" sit about 4px apart vertically. Put them on one baseline.
- **Total row:** "Total" in the plan table looks the same as the plan rows. Separate it with a stronger rule above or a bolder label.
- **Date pill:** "Jan to Dec 2025" at top right could be a filter or just a label. If it's a control, give it a chevron. If not, drop the pill.
- **Navigation:** the page is titled "Revenue overview" under the "Overview" nav item, and there is a separate "Revenue" item. Name the page to match its nav item, so users know what the other page holds.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI has a period and a comparison ("+18% vs Dec 2024"), and the gains carry a "+" sign as well as green. Keep that pattern (Few).
- The chart is honest and quiet: bars start at zero, there's one blue, gridlines are faint, and it carries a units line and a source line ("closed invoices only"). Keep all of it.
- The hierarchy is clear: the three figures, then the trend, then the breakdown. Nothing fights for attention.
