**First read.** Version 1: the eye goes straight to "$184,320" in the wide top-left card, drops to "+12% on September", then moves right and down through the six smaller cards and into the chart. This is a calm, competent monthly sales summary for a small retailer. Version 2: the eye lands on the "October so far" heading and then wanders, because seven cards of the same size and weight give it nowhere to settle. It also catches on the empty slot at the right end of the second row.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an at-a-glance sales dashboard that lets the owner or team at Tidewater Supply see how the month is going and whether things are getting better.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change is the KPI block. Version 1 makes revenue the headline and fits seven cards into a full 2+1+1 / 4 grid. Version 2 turns revenue into one card among equals and leaves a hole where an eighth card should be. Both versions share the same weak chart and some ambiguous delta labels, and those are now the biggest problems in version 1.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Hierarchy.** In version 1 the revenue figure is roughly half again as large as the other figures, and its card spans two columns. That gives the page one focal point and tells the viewer what this dashboard is mainly about. Version 2 sets all seven figures at one size and weight, so Returns (2.1%) looks as important as Revenue, and the viewer has to read every label to find what matters. A dashboard should put its key figures in the most prominent spot (Few, *Information Dashboard Design*). Version 2 removes the only prominence the page had. This is a defect.
- **The grid.** Version 1 fills both rows. Version 2's empty fourth cell in row two looks like a card that didn't load, not a choice. It also leaves a dead hole above the chart's top-right corner. This is a defect, though a small one.

**What would move version 1 most**
1. **The chart gives no information beyond its shape.** "Revenue by week" has no week labels, no value axis, no figures and no baseline, so you can't tell which bar is which week, how big any week was, or whether the axis starts at zero. Every chart needs labelled axes or direct labels with units (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2). Fix: put a week label under each bar and a value at the top of each bar, or add a light value axis that starts at zero. This is a defect.
2. **The chart and the heading disagree about the time period.** The page says "October so far", but the chart has eight weekly bars, and October has at most five weeks. Viewers will assume all eight bars are October, or they will have to puzzle it out. Fix: label the weeks, and either show only October or mark where October starts. A different shade for October's bars is the usual way. This is a defect (common sense; no approved source).
3. **The deltas don't say what they're compared with.** Only Revenue says "+12% on September". "+5%", "+7%", "+9%", "+2 points" and "+0.2" don't say what they compare against. "October so far" against September also reads two ways: the same days of September, or all of September. If it's all of September, a partial month showing +12% means something quite different, and this is the number people will repeat in meetings. Fix: state the comparison once at the section head, e.g. "vs. the same days in September", and keep every delta on that basis (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). This is a defect.
4. **The chart fills only the left half of its card.** The eight bars stop at about x=680 and the right half of a full-width panel is empty. The viewer reads this as data that is missing or still loading. Fix: spread the bars across the card's full width, or make the card narrower. This is a defect (it looks broken).

**Smaller things.**
- "-0.4 points" on Returns is green. That's correct, since fewer returns is good, but green on a minus sign makes the viewer stop and think. A word such as "fewer" would settle it.
- The deltas use a hyphen. Set a true minus sign (−0.4).
- The figures should use tabular numerals so the cards' numbers sit on a common rhythm. I can't confirm this from the image; check the font settings.

**What's working.**
- The type is restrained: a clear label → figure → delta stack in every card, with one sans-serif face and hierarchy built from weight and size. Keep it.
- Every metric has a comparison next to it. That's the hardest part of a dashboard to get right. It only needs its baseline stated.
- The grid is quiet and even, with equal gutters, and there's one accent colour for change. Nothing competes with the numbers.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The chart continues past the bottom of the capture. Check in a live browser whether its baseline and any labels show on a typical laptop screen without scrolling.
