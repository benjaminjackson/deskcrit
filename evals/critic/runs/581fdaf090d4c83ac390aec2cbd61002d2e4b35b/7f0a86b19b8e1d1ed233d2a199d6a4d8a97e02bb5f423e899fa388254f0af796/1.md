**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline makes a promise, but the page gives the viewer no way to act on it: no button, no time, no price, no place. The two blocks below the headline then pull attention toward other services.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's nothing to do.** *(defect)* A viewer who reads "Learn to swim … Tuesday evenings" and thinks "yes, me" has nowhere to click. There's no button under the subhead. The card titles might be links, but they're set like plain text with no underline or arrow, so nobody will try them. Krug's test fails: within seconds you should know what the page is, what you can do, and where to click. You get the first of those three. Put one clear button right under the subhead, styled as a button (filled), as the only strong action on the page. The label should name the exact step, like "Book a Tuesday trial lesson". "Learn more" or "Sign up" is too vague. If the card titles are links, give them a visible cue such as an underline or arrow, kept clearly weaker than the main button.

2. **The page leaves out what an adult beginner needs before committing.** *(defect, common sense)* "Tuesday evenings" has no time. There's no price, no location, no course length, and nothing about who it suits ("never swum", "afraid of deep water"). These are what a nervous adult needs to decide, and they'll later hold the pool to them. The cards have the same gap: "every morning from six" gives a start but no end, and "Family hour" says "Sunday afternoons" without saying which hour. Put the time, price and place next to the button, in the subhead's size or one step down. That way the button commits the viewer to something they can see.

3. **The two cards pull against the headline.** *(defect)* The whole top half says this page is about adult lessons. The two cards are about different things: open lane swimming for people who can already swim, and a children's session. They look like next steps for the lesson, but they lead elsewhere, so the viewer has to work out whether the page is "learn to swim" or "our pool". Pick one. If it's a lessons page, the space under the subhead should hold lesson details: what a first class is like, the coach, class size. Move the other sessions lower down and label them as "Also at the pool".

4. **The empty space is leftover, not shaped.** *(convention: Ruder, Müller-Brockmann)* Everything sits in the top half and the left two-thirds. The bottom 330 px or so and the right side, past about x = 990, are just empty. The two cards stop where a third would go, so the row reads as a missing card, not a choice. Big open space works when it frames something, and here it frames nothing, so the screen looks half-loaded. Either fill the space with the details from points 1 to 3, or shape it on purpose: let the hero take the full screen with the button, or bring the cards up into a full-width row.

5. **The type sizes in the middle are muddled.** *(convention: Dair, Bringhurst)* I count four sizes: the headline at about 88 px, the subhead at about 22, the card titles at about 20, and the card text at about 16. The subhead and the card titles are only a couple of pixels apart, both in regular weight, so the card headings read as slightly smaller cousins of the subhead, not as headings. The card titles differ from their own text only by size, with no change in weight. Keep three sizes: the headline, one middle size for the subhead, and the body size. Make the card titles body size in semibold or bold, so weight carries that level instead of a near-identical size.

**Smaller things.**
- Card titles: the copy is fine. "Join the lanes" is a clear verb phrase. "Three speeds to choose from" works because swimmers know lane speeds, but "slow, medium and fast lanes" would be clearer for everyone else.
- Headline: "Learn to swim" could be on any pool's site. The subhead does the persuading, so think about letting the headline carry some of it, such as "Learn to swim as an adult".

**What's working.**
- The headline's scale. One very large, heavy element against small text gives the page an obvious start. Keep that contrast while you add content.
- The subhead's copy. "Small adult classes … with a coach in the water beside you" is specific and speaks to an adult beginner's real fear. It's the best line on the page. Keep it, and put it close to the button.
- The left-aligned, single-family type, set without boxes, borders or decoration. It's calm and readable, which suits an anxious beginner. Spacing alone separates the groups, and it does that well.
