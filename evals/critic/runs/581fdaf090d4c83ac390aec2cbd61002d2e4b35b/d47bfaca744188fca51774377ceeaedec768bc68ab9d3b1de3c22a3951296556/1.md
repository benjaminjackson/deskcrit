**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "About the trust", drops into the paragraph, and reads it top to bottom without stopping. Nothing interrupts it, and nothing is waiting at the end. It looks like the About section of a small local charity's website. It feels calm, honest and well set, but also inert: an 11-line grey block with an empty right half and an empty bottom third.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: an About section for a local footpath trust, meant to tell nearby residents who the trust is and to bring some of them to a work day.

**Verdict.** The type is good, and that's why it reads easily. The section doesn't do its job, though. The one thing a reader could act on, the invitation to work days, is buried in the last third of a single paragraph, gives an ambiguous date, and offers no way to join.

**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation is buried, and there's no ask.** "Work days run every second Saturday, and anyone is welcome to join for the morning…" starts at line 7 of 11, mid-line, after the founding history and the staff numbers. A reader skimming for "can I help?" won't find it. Anyone who does find it has nowhere to go: no meeting place, no start time, no contact, no sign-up link. The reader reaches "across the path." and the page ends in white space. Nielsen's "How Users Read on the Web" is the source: on screen, people skim, and one long block hides what they're looking for. Nielsen's heuristic 6 (recognition rather than recall) also applies: the action has to be on the screen, not left for the reader to work out.
   - **Fix:** split the history from the invitation. Give the work days their own short block, with a subhead or a visible break. End it with one specific ask, such as "Join the next work day: Saturday 6 December, 9am, meet at the quarry car park", styled as a link or button.
   - **Kind:** defect.
2. **"Every second Saturday" can be read two ways.** It could mean every other Saturday, or the second Saturday of each month. This is the date people act on, so the wrong reading means someone turns up to an empty car park and probably doesn't come back.
   - **Fix:** say "every other Saturday" or "the second Saturday of each month", and better still, give the next date.
   - **Kind:** defect.
3. **The copy talks about the trust but not to the reader until the end.** Six of the eight sentences talk about the trust in the third person ("It was set up…", "Today it has…"). Then the voice switches to "We provide tools, gloves, and a hot drink." The warm part, written to the reader ("nobody needs any experience"), is the most persuasive line here, and it's at the bottom.
   - **Fix:** lead the volunteer block with that reassurance, addressed to "you", and keep one voice throughout.
   - **Kind:** taste, but it decides whether a hesitant reader turns up.

**Smaller things.**
- "two / thousand" breaks across lines 5–6, splitting the number. Rewording or a non-breaking space would keep it together.
- "About the trust" is a label any site could carry. A heading that says what the trust does (e.g. "Keeping eleven miles of moorland path open") would tell a skimmer more. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The text setting.** The measure looks like about 60 characters a line, the leading looks like about 1.6, the colour is a dark near-black on white, and the rag is gentle. It's comfortable to read. Keep all of this when you split the text.
- **The heading's contrast.** The heading differs from the body in weight as well as size, so it's unmistakably the entry point with only two type sizes. Don't add more sizes when you add a subhead. Use the body size in bold.
- **The concrete detail in the copy.** "Eleven miles of footpath", "clearing drains, mending stiles, cutting back gorse" and "a hot drink at the end" are specific to this place and these people. That keeps the copy from sounding like anyone's. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether a sign-up link or contact details sit further down the page, outside this capture. If they do, finding 1 shrinks to "move it up next to the invitation", but the invitation still needs its own block.
