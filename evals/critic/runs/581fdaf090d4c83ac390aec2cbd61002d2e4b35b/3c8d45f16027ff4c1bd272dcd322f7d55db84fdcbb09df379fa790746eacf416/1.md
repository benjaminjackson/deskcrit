**First read.** The eye lands on "Book a workshop seat", moves down four identical empty boxes, and then drops into blank white. There is nothing to press. It reads as a plain, tidy desktop booking form that someone stopped building halfway.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page where someone who already wants a workshop reserves one or more places in it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't work yet: there is no way to send the booking. After that, the form makes people type from memory the things it should offer them to choose (which workshop, which date), so even with a button it would produce bookings that don't match anything real.

**What would move it most**

1. **There is no submit button.** The form ends after "Notes for the host", and the bottom third of the frame is empty where the action belongs. Someone who fills in all four fields has nowhere to go, and nothing tells them how the booking gets made or what happens next. This is a defect. A view needs one high-emphasis action (Material Design 3, "Buttons"), and Krug's test fails at the most basic question: what do I do here? If the button is missing only because the page was captured mid-load, check that in a live browser; as shown, it's broken. Fix: add one filled button, left-aligned with the fields, with a label that names the result, such as "Reserve my seats". Put one line under it saying what happens next, such as "We'll email your confirmation".

2. **Every field is a blank text box, so the person has to guess and remember.** "Workshop name" asks them to type the name of a workshop from memory, with no list. "Preferred date" doesn't say which dates exist or what format to use. "Number of seats" doesn't stop "two" or "40". "Notes for the host" is one line high, which suggests a short answer is expected. This breaks recognition over recall and error prevention (Nielsen 5 and 6), and standard form controls (Jakob's law). The result is typos, dates the workshop doesn't run, and seat counts the host can't honour. Fix: make the workshop a choice from a list, or prefill it if the person arrived from a workshop's page. Offer only the available dates as a picker. Make seats a number stepper that starts at 1 and shows the maximum. Make Notes a multi-line box.

3. **The page doesn't say what the person is agreeing to.** It shows no price, no time or place, no number of seats left, and no sign of whether booking is free or a request the host has to approve. Without that, the person can't build a picture of what this booking is (Norman, conceptual model). And the seat count is the one number the person will hold the host to, so they need to know what it commits them to. Fix: once a workshop is chosen, show its date, time, place, price per seat, and seats left next to the form, so the person can check the booking before sending it.

**Smaller things.**
- No field is marked required or optional. "Notes for the host" is almost certainly optional: add "(optional)" after its label (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- No help text sits by the fields. Add a short hint where a constraint exists, such as "Up to 6 seats per booking".
- The headline says "a workshop seat", but the form asks for a number of seats. Use "Book workshop seats" or "Book your place" so the headline and the field agree.

**What's working.**
- The labels are persistent, bold, and sit right above their fields. The gap between a label and its own field is clearly smaller than the gap to the next field, so each pair reads as one unit. Keep that spacing.
- The type is restrained: one family, a heading and a label size with a clear step between them, and dark text with good contrast. Don't add more sizes when you add hints and the button. Make the hints lighter in colour, not a new size.
- The single left-aligned column with a comfortable field width is the right structure. Keep it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus and error states for the fields (whether focus is visible, and whether error messages name the field and say how to fix it), and whether the form reflows to one narrow column at 320 px.
