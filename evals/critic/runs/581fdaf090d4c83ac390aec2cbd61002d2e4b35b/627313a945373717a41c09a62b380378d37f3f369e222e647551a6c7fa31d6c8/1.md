**First read.** The eye lands on "Create your account", runs down a tidy stack of six fields, and stops on the solid blue "Create account" button. It reads at once as the sign-up page for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm and competent, but anonymous: it could be any SaaS sign-up page.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the sign-up page for a free notes product, and its one job is to get a new visitor from arrival to an account with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** It works: the hierarchy is clear, there's one obvious action and the form is built properly. What holds it back is the form itself, not the styling. It asks for more than a free notes account needs, and it hides the one rule people trip over, the password requirements.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two of the six fields don't help the person signing up.** "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" (the bottom two fields, above the button) make the form a third longer.
   - The question about how they heard of you is for your marketing team, not the user. Its dropdown sits blank with no "Choose one" prompt, so it isn't obvious that it's optional.
   - "Workspace name" asks a brand-new user to name something they haven't seen yet. Most people stop and invent something, or wonder what happens if they leave it empty.
   - Every field you add is one more reason to put off signing up. Nielsen's heuristic 8 says every element should support the main task. His progressive-disclosure guidance says to show secondary options later.
   - Fix: cut both fields. Fill in the workspace name for them (for example "Ben's notes") and let them rename it later. Ask how they heard about you after sign-up, or on the first screen inside the app. Defect.
2. **The password field gives no rules.** The "Password" field has no hint under its label, so users only learn about any length or character rules from an error after they press the button. That's a failed submit at the worst moment of the funnel.
   - Nielsen 5 (error prevention) and 10 (help where the task needs it): put the rules in one short line of grey helper text under the label, before anyone types.
   - A show/hide toggle in the field is the usual partner to this, since people can't see what they typed. Defect.
3. **The copy could be on anyone's page.** "Create your account / Start a free workspace for your notes" is the most generic line a notes app could write. A visitor who arrives here directly (from an ad, a shared link or a "Sign up" button elsewhere) gets no reason to choose Quillnest over the notes app they already use.
   - Fix: change the subtitle to say what Quillnest does better than their current tool, in words about their notes, such as what they'll find, keep or stop losing.
   - "Free" is the strongest word on the page and it's buried in grey mid-sentence. If there's no card and no time limit, say so in plain words. That's a promise people will hold you to, so make it exact. Taste, but it's what decides whether a hesitant visitor bothers to fill in the form.

**Smaller things.**
- **The "Sign in" link doesn't look like a link.** In "Already have an account? Sign in" (under the button), "Sign in" is the same grey as the rest of the sentence, with no underline. Returning users have to guess it's clickable. Give it the brand blue and an underline.
- **The field borders look faint.** The grey-blue outlines on the six fields look close to WCAG's 3:1 minimum for input borders on white (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure them, and darken them a step if they fall short.
- **There are too many text sizes.** I count about seven: logo about 20, heading about 26, button 16, subtitle about 15, labels about 14 bold, "Sign in" line about 13, "* Required field" about 12. The three small grey sizes are a point apart and read as accidents. Collapse them to one small size and let weight and colour do the rest.

**What's working.**
- **One clear action.** The full-width, filled "Create account" button is the only heavy element on the page, so nobody has to look for what to do next. Keep it the only filled button.
- **Spacing that groups the form.** Each label sits tight to its field, with clearly bigger gaps between field groups, so the form scans top to bottom without effort. First and last name side by side is the right exception to the single column.
- **Required fields marked properly.** There's an asterisk on each required label, explained once at the top ("* Required field"). That's the textbook fix, and the asterisk doesn't rely on colour alone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus states:** whether the fields and button show a clearly visible focus ring when you tab through them.
- **Error states:** whether errors appear next to the field, in plain words, without clearing what the user typed. This is especially important for the password.
