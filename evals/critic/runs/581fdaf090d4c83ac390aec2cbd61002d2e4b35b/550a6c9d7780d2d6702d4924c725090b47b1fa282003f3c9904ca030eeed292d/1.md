**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on "Release notes", then goes to the three bold version-and-date lines, then reads each short paragraph. The page reads as a calm, bookish changelog for a desktop photo editor. In version 2 the eye lands on the title just the same, but then drops into one grey block of text. The version lines don't stop the eye, so you have to read down the page to find where 4.1 starts.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a release-notes page where existing users check what changed in a version and when, so they scan by version number first and read the detail second.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job and version 2 doesn't do it as well. The only meaningful change is the version headings. In version 2 they are set almost exactly like the body text, so the page loses the one thing a changelog needs: entries you can find at a glance.

**What would move it most**
1. **The version headings in version 2 don't read as headings** (defect). "Version 4.2, 20 October" and the two below it are in the same regular serif, the same dark grey, and about 20 px against body text of about 19 px. That difference is too small to see at a glance, so the three entries merge into one column of text. Someone looking for "what changed in 4.1" has to read line by line instead of scanning. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8) says a contrast that is meant to be there must be obvious, and that a heading one step bigger or bolder than its text looks like an accident. Version 2 also builds the level from size alone, and too little of it (Dair, ch. 8a–8g, 9). Version 1 fixes exactly this: a bold weight plus a clear size step (about 24 px), so each entry has an anchor and the page has three clear levels (title, entry, detail). Keep version 1's headings. If you want version 2's lighter feel, change one thing clearly, such as weight or colour, rather than nudging the size.

**Smaller things.**
- Body measure, in both versions: the first line of each entry runs about 84 characters, over Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column by about 100 px. There is plenty of room.
- Nav, in both versions: "Release notes" in the top nav looks just like "Download" and "Support", so nothing shows you're on that page. Mark the current item with weight, an underline, or colour.

**What's working** (in version 1).
- The type scale has four clear levels with big steps between them: a sans nav at about 16 px, a serif body at about 19 px, bold serif entries at about 24 px, and a bold serif title at about 44 px. Every size has an obvious job.
- The copy is very good for release notes. Every line says who does what: "Straighten now finds the horizon by itself, and you can still drag the line by hand." Claims come with plain-English amounts ("about a third faster on older laptops", "more than ten thousand files").
- The grouping is clear. Each heading sits tight to its paragraph with a larger gap before the next entry, and everything hangs from one left edge shared with the logo.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the text column reflows cleanly on a narrow screen.
