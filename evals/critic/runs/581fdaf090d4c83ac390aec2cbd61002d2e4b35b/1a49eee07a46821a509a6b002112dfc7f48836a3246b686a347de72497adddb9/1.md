**First read.** The eye lands on the big dark rectangle and finds a grey "no photo" person silhouette. Then it drops to the bold headline, which carries the page. It reads as a bookshop's event page: calm, competent, and quiet to the point of anonymous. The dark block at the top makes the whole thing feel heavier than "an evening with wine" should.

**What it's for.** My assumption: getting local readers to book a free seat at an author reading at the shop.

**Verdict.** The text half works. The headline is clear, there's one ask, and it's specific. But the biggest element on the page is a placeholder that says nothing, and the facts a booker needs (where, which year, how long) are missing or doubtful. Fix the picture and the details, and this is a good page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero is a generic avatar, not a picture of anything** (defect). The dark 600×330 block takes about half the content area, so it's where the eye goes first, and it gets a stock silhouette in mid-brown on near-black. The figure barely separates from the ground, and it tells a stranger nothing about Ines Varga or *The Salt Year*. The page's strongest visual slot gives the visitor no reason to care. If a real photo failed to load in the capture, this is fine. If it didn't, use the author's portrait or the book cover, which is what readers recognise on a shelf. Or drop the block and let the headline lead. Even a sombre photo will set a mood. A flat dark gradient only sets a weight.
2. **The date can't be trusted as written** (defect, copy). "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. 13 November was a Thursday in 2025; in 2026 it falls on a Friday. So either this page is a year out of date or the weekday is wrong. A visitor who acts on it turns up on the wrong night, or finds the event has already happened. Check it, and add the year if the page could outlive the season.
3. **The booking block doesn't answer "where" or "for how long"** (defect). The only location cue is "Find us" in the nav. The event block never says the reading is at Margin Books or at which branch, and gives no end time and no hint of how many seats are left. Someone deciding whether to book has to leave the page to find out. Put one short line under the date or beside the button: venue, roughly 7–8.30 pm, and seats remaining if that's true. A small "Free" next to the time would also read faster than leaving it inside the button label.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Events" looks the same as the other links, so nothing shows you're in that section. Give the current item a weight or underline state.
- Body copy: "the island where the book is set" holds back the one concrete hook. Name the island. Readers book for a place or a story, not a category.
- Headline: the book title is set roman, the same as the rest of the line. Italicise *The Salt Year* (standard book-title convention) so it reads as a title, not as part of the sentence.

**What's working.**
- "Book a free seat" is an exact, low-commitment ask: it names the action and the price. Keep that wording.
- The text stack is well grouped: rust date label, then the large bold headline with tight leading, then a short paragraph and one dark button. It's a clean hierarchy with one action, and nothing competes with it.
- Restraint: one accent colour (the rust date) and no ornament. That's right for a bookshop. Once the hero carries real content, this restraint will let it lead.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the hero image failed to load (check in a live browser), the button's hover and focus states, and how the column reflows on a phone.
