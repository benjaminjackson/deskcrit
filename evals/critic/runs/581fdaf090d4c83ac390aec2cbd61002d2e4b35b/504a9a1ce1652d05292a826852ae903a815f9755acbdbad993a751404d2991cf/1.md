**First read.** The eye lands on "Walking routes" top left, drops to three identical cards, and reads the bold titles left to right. Then it hits the bottom half of each card, which is empty: about 200 px of white box under each button. The page reads as a calm, tidy list of routes from a local council or tourism site, but it looks unfinished, as if a map or photo was meant to sit in each card and never arrived.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that helps people choose a walking route from a small set and open its details. Correct me if it's something else.

**Verdict.** It mostly works: you can see straight away what it is and what to click, and the type and copy are clean. Two things hold it back. The cards are nearly half empty, so the page looks broken. And the facts people choose a walk by (distance, difficulty, surface) are buried in sentences, so comparing the three walks means reading all of them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The empty lower half of every card.** Below each "View route" button, from about y=395 to y=598, each card is blank white inside its border. The border draws a frame around nothing, so the eye goes there expecting content, finds none, and reads the page as broken or still loading. Empty space only helps when it's shaped on purpose; here it's what's left over from a fixed card height (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Defect. The fix: if a map or photo belongs there, it failed to load in this capture, so check it live. That would be the right fix anyway, because a picture of the coast, the beeches or the canal is what people choose a walk by. If nothing belongs there, let the card end about 32 px below the button, and put the spare space outside the cards, around the group.
2. **You can't compare the routes at a glance.** Distance, terrain and difficulty are written as prose ("Six kilometres along the coast, with two steep climbs…"). To compare 6, 4 and 10 km, or steep against flat against paved, you have to read three sentences and keep them in your head. Krug (*Don't Make Me Think*) asks that pages be built for scanning, and this page's one job is comparing routes. Defect. The fix: pull the deciding facts into a short line in the same spot on every card, under the title (for example "6 km · 2 steep climbs · café"). Keep the sentence for the feel of the walk. That also gives the cards something to set them apart, which they don't have yet.

**Smaller things.**
- In the Cliff path card, "end." sits alone on the last line (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.4.2). Change the wording or use a non-breaking space so "far end." stays together.
- "cafe" should be "café" if the site's style allows it.

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete and helpful: "benches every few hundred metres" and "good for bicycles" tell people who the walk suits, in their own terms. Keep that voice when you add the facts line.
- The type scale is tight and clear: page title, card title, body and button label each have their own job, and weight does some of the work, not only size. Body text is a dark grey that looks well above 4.5:1. Measure it once to confirm.
- The structure is a clean, even 3-column grid: equal gaps, shared left edges and baselines across the cards, and one button style used the same way everywhere.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty card area is an image or map that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- How the three cards reflow at narrow widths (320 px).
- Whether the buttons show a visible focus state.
