## Version 2 is worse because its subheads look like body text

**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the headline, "Change the card you pay with", and then drops down three bold subheads that work like a table of contents: add, make default, declined. In version 2 the eye takes in the headline and then meets a solid block of text. "Add a new card", "Make it the default" and "If the card is declined" are set in the same size, weight and colour as the paragraphs under them, so they look like short opening sentences rather than headings. Both read as a calm, plain help-centre article.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help article for a customer who wants to swap their payment card, or whose card was just declined, and who needs to find their one step quickly.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change between the two is how the three subheads are set. In version 2 they stop working as headings, and on a page people skim, that is the change that matters. Version 1 is a solid, well-built help page with only a minor line-length issue.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's subheads look the same as the body text** (defect). This is the three short lines at about y=282, 398 and 513. A customer with a declined card has to read every paragraph from the top to find "If the card is declined", because nothing marks it as a place to jump to. In version 1 the bold, slightly larger subheads let that person go straight to the third section. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8) says a contrast has to be obvious at a glance, and a heading in the size, weight and colour of its text looks like an accident. The fix: make each subhead clearly different from the body, mainly by weight. Version 1's bold at a modest step up in size already does this, so keep it.

**Smaller things**
- Both versions: the intro paragraph and the "declined" paragraph run to about 83–85 characters per line, past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column by roughly 10–15% to bring lines nearer 66 characters.

**What's working** (version 1)
- The headline is a task written in the reader's own words ("Change the card you pay with"). The intro answers the two worries people have about money before they ask: you can change it any time, and nothing is charged when you do.
- Each subhead sits tight to its paragraph and well below the one before it, so the three steps read as separate chunks.
- The steps use the exact labels people will see in the product ("Settings", "Billing", "Add card", "Use for payments"). Someone can follow them without guessing.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the subheads are real HTML headings for screen readers and in-page links. This matters most in version 2, where nothing on screen marks them as headings.
