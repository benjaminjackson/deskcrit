**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, "Notes for ferry passengers", then drops into two comfortable paragraphs, then trails off into a smaller line and a lot of white page. It reads as a plain, calm information page for people taking a local river ferry. It feels tidy and trustworthy, and a bit unfinished. It looks like a timetable page that has no times on it.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a web page that tells a first-time passenger when the ferry runs, how long the crossing takes, and what to do with a bike, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is clean and mostly sound, but the page doesn't do its job. A passenger can't plan a trip from it, because it gives no actual times. The one warning that could stop them missing a boat is the least visible line on the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule is too vague to act on.** In the first paragraph, "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" is the sentence a passenger needs most, and it gives no first sailing, no last sailing, and no departure minutes. One reader takes "early" as 6:00 and another as 7:30, and whoever guesses wrong misses the first or last boat. For anything people will act on, a line that can be read two ways is a defect, not a style note. Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing and the pattern ("from the north landing at :00 and :40"). If times change by season, say that, or link to the current timetable. *Defect.*
2. **The winter warning is set below body text when it should stand out.** The last line, "Timetables change in winter. Check the noticeboard at the landing before you travel.", is the only instruction on the page and the only thing that can make the rest wrong. It's set a few pixels smaller than the paragraphs (roughly 16 px against 19 px), in the same weight and colour. So it reads as a footnote, or as a mistake in sizing. A size step that small doesn't register as a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8: a contrast must be obvious at a glance). Fix: raise it to the body size and set it apart clearly in one other way: a bold lead-in ("In winter:"), a rule or tint, or a position near the top. Don't make it smaller. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count three sizes (heading about 38 px, body about 19 px, note about 16 px), and the bottom two are too close to read as separate levels (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Keep two text sizes with a clear gap between them, and use weight to show differences within body text. Fixing item 2 fixes this.
- The second paragraph ends on how a bike is locked but never states the outcome for the rider. "You don't need a bike ticket" is a stronger line than "at no extra charge". If cost matters, the page should also say what foot passengers pay. It currently names a price for bikes only.

**What's working.**
- The measure and leading are right for reading. Lines run to about 75 characters with roughly 1.5 line spacing, so the paragraphs read without effort. Keep that column width when content is added.
- There's one clear entry point: a bold, dark heading at about twice the body size, with no competing ornament. The page is quiet in a way that suits public information.
- The copy is plain and concrete where it is specific ("twelve minutes in calm weather", "lock them to the rail along the side of the deck"). Bring the schedule up to that standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the reading column reflows cleanly on a phone, which is where most passengers would read this at a landing.
