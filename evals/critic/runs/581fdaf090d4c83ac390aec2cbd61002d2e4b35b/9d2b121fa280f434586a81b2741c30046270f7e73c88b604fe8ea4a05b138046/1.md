**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces", on the dark leaf pattern. Then it drops to the intro line and runs across the three bordered cards. After that there is nowhere to go: the page just stops. It reads as a calm, tidy, local garden-centre homepage that is pleasant to look at but asks nothing of you.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage of a local garden centre. It should get nearby visitors, especially people with shady or small spaces, to look at the plants, visit or order, and book a workshop.

**Verdict.** It looks competent and the copy is unusually concrete, but it doesn't do its job. Nothing tells the visitor what to do next. The page makes a specific offer, then gives them no way to act on it and no picture of what's on offer.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no ask, so the eye path ends in a dead end (defect).** The headline promises shade plants and the intro says they're "ready to pot this weekend". Under it there is no button and no link. Nothing says "see what's in stock", "order for delivery" or "come in on Saturday". The three cards then raise questions they don't answer:
   - **Saturday workshops:** "ten places in each class" makes places sound limited, but there's no date, no price and no way to book one.
   - **Delivery:** it explains free delivery over $60 but gives no way to start an order.

   Without a link, a visitor who is convinced has to find their own way through the generic nav ("Plants", "Workshops") to act. Material Design and Nielsen Norman Group button guidance both say a view should have one main action that stands out. Here there are none.
   - Fix: put one filled button under the intro that names the exact thing, e.g. "See this week's shade plants".
   - Give the workshops card and the delivery card each a lower-emphasis text link: the next Saturday date with "Book a place", and "Order for delivery".
2. **The hero image doesn't show what the headline sells (taste).** The whole top half is a busy, generic leaf pattern. It shows no fern, no hosta, no shady corner and no balcony. It sets a green mood, but any garden centre could use it, and it does nothing to back up the one specific claim on the page. The person this page is for has a dark yard or a small balcony and wants to see that their problem can be solved. A real photo of the nursery's ferns and hostas in a shaded spot, or in pots on a small balcony, would show them that. The headline would then sit on a calmer part of the photo rather than on scattered leaves. Pairing the headline with an image of exactly what it says is the cheapest way to make this page feel like Fernhollow and not a template.
3. **The headline is cut off from its intro, and the two don't line up (convention).** The headline sits inside the hero with its left edge at about x=72. Its intro sentence sits below the hero edge, at x=56, the same line as the wordmark and the cards. The image edge between them makes them read as two separate blocks instead of a headline and its subtitle. The 16 px near-miss looks like a slip rather than a choice. A placement that is neither clearly aligned nor clearly offset leaves the eye unsure (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I). Every block should keep to one set of column lines (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*).
   - Fix: put the headline on the same x=56 line as everything else.
   - Either move the intro (and the new button) into the hero under the headline, or keep it below and tighten the gap so the two clearly read as one unit.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "…and small / spaces". That splits the phrase "small spaces" and leaves one word alone on line 2. Break after "shade" ("Plants for shade / and small spaces") or widen the text box.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific.** "Open every day from 8 am to 6 pm, until 8 pm on Thursdays", "Free within ten miles on orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box", "ready to pot this weekend". These are facts a visitor can plan around. Keep that voice when you write the button labels.
- **The type is clear.** The green serif wordmark stands apart from the sans-serif used everywhere else, so the brand reads as a brand. The headline is plainly the first thing you see, and the card titles stand out from their body text by weight, not just size.
- **The palette is restrained.** Dark green, white and grey suit a nursery. A single accent colour kept for the new main button will stand out precisely because nothing else uses it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are already links: check hover, the pointer and focus states in a browser.
- How the three cards stack at narrow widths and when zoomed in.
