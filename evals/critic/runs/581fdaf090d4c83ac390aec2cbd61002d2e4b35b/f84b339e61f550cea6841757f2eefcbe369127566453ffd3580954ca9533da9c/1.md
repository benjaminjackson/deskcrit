**First read.** The eye lands on the four-line black headline, drops to a short grey-black paragraph, and ends on a lone sentence saying the page is "hard to read." It reads as the top of a ferry timetable page, but there is no timetable. It feels calm, plain and competent, like a well-set template.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a desktop web page that tells local riders when the spring river ferry runs between the north landing and the market quay.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is sound, but the page fails its job. It is labelled "Spring timetable" and gives no times, no dates and no first or last sailing, and its last line tells the reader the page is hard to read. Put the schedule on the page and remove the stray sentence. Nothing else matters until that's done.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable is missing (defect).** The kicker says "Spring timetable" and the headline promises crossings "spring through early summer." What the reader actually gets is "every forty minutes" and "about twelve minutes." Nothing says when the first boat leaves, when the last one leaves, which dates "spring through early summer" covers, or what "by daylight" means in clock time. Someone checking whether they can catch a boat at 7:10 has to leave the page to find out. Promising information the page doesn't hold is a false promise (common sense: the label sets an expectation the content breaks). The eye's path ends without an answer. Fix: put the schedule directly under the intro: first and last sailing for each landing, the date range, and the 40-minute pattern written as clock times (":00, :40, :20…"), or a small table if times change through the season. The times are what riders will plan around, so they should be the second thing seen after the headline.
2. **The last line works against the page (defect).** "The cramped, overlapping heading makes this page hard to read" sits on its own, 80 px below the intro, in body type. It looks like a leftover review note or placeholder. A real visitor reads it as the page apologising for itself, and it's also untrue: in a close crop, the headline lines are tight but none touch or overlap. Fix: delete it. If it's a placeholder, fill that slot with the schedule from finding 1.
3. **The headline uses four lines to say what the kicker already says (taste, plus a convention on line breaks).** "Quayside ferry crossings by daylight, spring through early summer" is long and could head any ferry's page. It breaks "early / summer" across lines and leaves "summer" alone on line four. The biggest type on the page is spent on a topic description instead of the thing riders came for. Fix: shorten it to the route and the promise, for example "North landing ⇄ Market quay, every 40 minutes". Let the date range sit smaller underneath, and break the lines at phrase boundaries so no single word ends up alone.

**Smaller things.**
- "weather." sits alone on the last line of the intro paragraph. Rewrap it, or tighten the sentence, so the last line holds at least two words (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- In the copy, "in calm weather" raises a question the page doesn't answer: what happens when it isn't calm? Add a line on rough-weather delays or cancellations and where riders can check.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean. There are three sizes with big steps: a small bold grey label, a very large bold headline, and readable body text around 20 px. Each has an obvious job. Keep it when you add the schedule, and set times in the body size with weight for emphasis rather than adding a fourth size.
- The intro paragraph runs about 70 characters per line with comfortable line spacing, and everything hangs off one left edge at 120 px. That calm left-aligned column will hold a timetable well.
- The headline's tight line spacing keeps it together as one block without any lines colliding.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't see how the page reflows at a narrow width. At this size the four-line headline would probably run to six or more lines on a phone and push the schedule below the fold, so check it at 320 px.
