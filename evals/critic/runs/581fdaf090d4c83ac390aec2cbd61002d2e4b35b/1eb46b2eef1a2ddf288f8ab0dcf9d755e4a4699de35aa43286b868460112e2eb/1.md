**First read.** The eye lands on "About the trust" in bold at top left, then drops into one solid 12-line paragraph and has nowhere to go after it. The right half of the frame is empty. It reads as the About section of a small local conservation charity's website. It feels honest, calm and plain, and a bit like a placeholder.

**What it's for.** Assumption, since I wasn't told: the About section of the Moorside Trust website, read by local people and walkers. It should explain what the trust is and, judging by the copy, get people to come to a work day.

**Verdict.** The type is clean and readable. As a piece of communication, though, it hides its only real ask: the invitation to volunteer sits in sentence five of one solid block, and gives no date you can act on, no place, and no way to sign up. Fix the structure and the ask, and the type can stay as it is.

**What would move it most**
1. **The volunteer invitation is buried and gives the reader nothing to act on.** *Defect.* "Work days run every second Saturday, and anyone is welcome to join…" starts mid-line 7 and is set exactly like the history and membership figures around it. Someone skimming for "can I help?" won't find it. A reader who does find it hits two gaps:
   - **The date reads two ways.** "Every second Saturday" can mean alternate Saturdays or the second Saturday of each month. Someone who guesses wrong turns up to an empty car park.
   - **There is no time, place or way to say "I'm coming".** "Join for the morning" doesn't say when the morning starts or where to meet. The paragraph then ends on gorse ("across the path."), so the eye path stops dead with no next step.

   Direction: split the invitation out as its own block with its own small heading, and say exactly when and where ("Second Saturday of every month, 9am to 12:30, meet at the quarry car park"). End it with one specific ask, such as "Come to the next work day, 14 December" with a link or a sign-up button. The reassurance that tools are provided and no experience is needed is your strongest persuasion, so keep it next to the ask.
2. **One undivided block mixes three different jobs.** *Defect (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web": people skim, and a long unbroken block on a screen loses them).* The block holds three things: what the trust looks after, its history and size, and the work days. They run together in one grey mass with no paragraph breaks, so nothing stands out and the reader has to read every line to find the part that concerns them. Direction: make three short paragraphs, or two plus the separated invitation from point 1. The 1987 founding story and the "two thousand members, most within a few miles" figure are good trust signals, and each will register better on its own.

**Smaller things.**
- Empty right half and bottom (about 490 px to the right of the column): all the weight sits left with nothing to balance it, so the space looks like leftover rather than choice. This is likely the template's fault, not the section's. Either use it (a photo of a work party, or the work-day block from point 1 as a side panel) or centre the reading column on the page. Taste.
- The heading "About the trust" could sit on any organisation's page. A heading that says what the trust does ("Keeping eleven miles of moorland path open since 1987") tells a stranger what this is before they read a word. Taste. It passes WCAG 2.4.6 as written.

**What's working.**
- The reading column: lines run about 60 characters, the leading is generous, the rag is gentle and the text is dark on white. It reads comfortably. Keep this measure and spacing when you split the text.
- A clear two-level type scale. The bold heading is about twice the body size and set apart by weight as well as size, so the hierarchy is plain with no stray in-between sizes.
- The voice is concrete and local: "clearing drains, mending stiles, and cutting back gorse", and "a hot drink at the end". This is copy no other organisation could have written, and it's what will make someone come. Don't sand it down into generic charity language.
