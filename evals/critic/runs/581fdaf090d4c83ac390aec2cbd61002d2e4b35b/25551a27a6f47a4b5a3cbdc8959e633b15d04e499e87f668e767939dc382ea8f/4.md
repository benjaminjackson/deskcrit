**First read.** My eye goes to "Before you board" first, then to the grey "PASSENGER NOTES" label above it, then drops into the body text. That's where it stops being easy. All three paragraphs are in capitals, so they look like grey bars. To find anything I have to read word by word instead of skimming. It looks like a quiet, tidy help page for a ferry service. The top part feels calm, but the body feels like it's shouting.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page section for ferry passengers. They come to check how often the boat runs, how long the crossing takes, and the rules for bikes, and then leave.

**Verdict.** The layout above the body text is sound, but the page fails at its main job, which is letting someone find one fact quickly. Two things get in the way: the body is set entirely in capitals, and the copy is vague about the times people need in order to catch a boat.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is all capitals (defect).** All three paragraphs, about eight lines in total, are set in caps. Capitals are all the same height, so each word loses its outline and the paragraphs turn into even grey blocks. A passenger looking for "bicycles" or "winter" has to read every word to find it, and reading takes longer. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") reserves capitals for headings shorter than one line, labels, and captions, and says not to capitalise whole paragraphs. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) says text in capitals is particularly hard to read. **Fix:** set the paragraphs in normal upper and lower case. Keep capitals only for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, which already uses them correctly: short, tracked, and small.
2. **The copy leaves out the times people act on (defect).** "Between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing. One passenger will read "late evening" as 10 pm and another as 8 pm, and the one who guesses wrong is stuck on the wrong side of the river. "Every forty minutes" gives no starting point, so nobody can work out when the next boat leaves. "In winter" gives no dates. "One boat each hour" doesn't say when in the hour. "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter" points people to a timetable they can only read once they're already at the landing. **Fix:** give the real first and last sailings, the minutes past the hour, and the dates winter service starts and ends. Better still, link to the timetable or show it on this page.

**Smaller things.**
- The heading talks to "you", but the body switches to "Riders wheel them on…". Writing "Wheel your bike on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock it to the rail" keeps talking to the reader and turns the rule into a clear instruction.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing and grouping:** the label, heading and body have clearly different spacing, so each group reads as one unit. Keep those gaps.
- **Column width:** lines run about 65–70 characters, which suits reading. Keep it once the text is in lower case; lower-case text is narrower, so the line will fit more words.
- **Type sizes:** there are only three: a small tracked label, a bold heading and the body. Each has an obvious job and there are no near-duplicate sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the column reflows on a narrow phone screen. Passengers will often read this at the landing, so check it in a live browser at 320 px wide.
