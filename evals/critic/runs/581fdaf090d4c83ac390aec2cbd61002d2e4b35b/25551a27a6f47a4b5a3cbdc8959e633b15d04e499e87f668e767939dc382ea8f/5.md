**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a short web or kiosk panel. Its job is to tell first-time ferry passengers how often the boat runs, how bikes work, and where to find the timetable.

**Verdict.** The frame is sound but the body text isn't. All three paragraphs are set in capitals, which makes the only content people came for slow and tiring to read. The copy also leaves the times vague, and times are the facts a passenger plans around.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is set in capitals** (defect). All three paragraphs, nine lines in total, are capitals. Every word becomes the same rectangle, so the reader loses word shapes and has to spell their way through, much like reading a telegram. It also hides "forty minutes" and "lower deck", the facts a skimming passenger wants to spot, inside a texture with no variation. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says caps suit headings shorter than one line and that whole paragraphs should not be capitalized. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls text in capitals "particularly difficult to read." Hochuli's point matters even more for readers with dyslexia or low vision. Fix: set the paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, which already does this well. If the caps came from a CSS `text-transform` meant only for the eyebrow, check whether it leaked onto the paragraphs.
2. **The copy is vague exactly where passengers act** (defect). "Between early morning and late evening" doesn't tell anyone whether they can catch a 6:30 boat or a 22:00 one. "In winter" doesn't say which months, and "one boat each hour" doesn't say on the hour or at what minute. These are the lines people plan a trip around and will hold the operator to. A passenger who reads "late evening" as 23:00 when the last boat is at 21:00 gets stranded. Fix: give first and last departures as clock times, give winter as dates, and say when the hourly boat leaves (for example, "at :15 past each hour"). Also, "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter" sends readers who are already on this panel to a different place. If this panel can carry the timetable, or link to it, that is worth more than any styling change.

- The hierarchy has three levels with big jumps between them: a small, tracked, grey label, a large bold heading, and the body. Each size has an obvious job, and the heading carries its weight without help. Keep this scale when you switch the body to lower case.

- "Before you board" is a good heading. It tells the reader when this applies to them and in their own words, which "Ferry information" would not.
