**First read.** My eye lands on "Before you board", then drops into a grey slab of capitals and slows down hard. It reads as a short help or info page for a river ferry, calm and plain. The headline invites me to read on. The body then makes reading feel like work, and I come away without the one thing I'd want: what time the boat leaves.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or panel that tells first-time ferry passengers what to expect before a crossing: when it runs, how long it takes, bikes, and winter service.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the body text is set entirely in capitals. That makes the only content on the page the hardest part to read. Behind that, the copy holds back the facts a passenger would actually act on.

**What would move it most**
1. **All three body paragraphs are set in capitals** (defect and convention). This is about ten lines of running text. Capitals all have the same height, so every word loses the shape that lets a reader recognise it at a glance. The eye has to work letter by letter, and the three paragraphs become a flat grey block where nothing stands out, not even "forty minutes" or "no extra charge." The capitals also sit at normal letter spacing, so they look cramped, which makes it worse. The small tracked "PASSENGER NOTES" label shows the designer knows how capitals should be handled. Readers with dyslexia or low vision are hit hardest. Fix: set the paragraphs in normal upper and lower case, and keep capitals for one-line labels like the eyebrow (Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps"; Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*: text in capitals "is particularly difficult to read").
2. **The copy leaves out what a passenger will act on** (defect). "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing. Someone planning around the last boat home can't, and "late evening" could mean 8 pm or 11 pm. If they guess wrong, they're stranded. "In winter" doesn't say which months, and "one boat each hour" doesn't say on the hour or at some other minute. "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" sends someone reading this before the trip to a place they haven't reached yet. Fix: state the first and last departure times and the winter dates, and put the timetable here, or link to it, instead of pointing to a shelter.

**Smaller things.** None beyond the above.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean, with three clearly separate levels: a small grey tracked label, a large bold headline, and body text. Sizes are few and far apart, and the headline sits tight to the text it introduces. Keep that scale.
- "Before you board" is a good headline. It speaks to the viewer, says when this matters, and isn't generic.
- The paragraphs are short and each covers one topic (schedule, bikes, winter), so the page is already easy to skim once the case is fixed.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows at narrow widths. The wide empty area to the right is fine if this sits in a larger page layout; check it in context.
