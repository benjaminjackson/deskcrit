**First read.** The eye goes straight to the four purple bars, then to the bold "4.0%" over Growth, then up to the title. It reads as an internal analytics dashboard, on the Conversion tab, comparing trial-to-paid rates across four plans for Q3 2025. It feels calm, clean and credible. It shows you the numbers and leaves you to decide what they mean.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard view for a product or growth team. It should help them see which plans convert trials best and decide whether the gaps are big enough to act on.

**Verdict.** It's well made, and the chart is honest: the axis starts at zero, values are labelled directly, the source and definition are given, and the table holds the raw counts. But it presents gaps of a few tenths of a point as a firm ranking. It also never says which gap matters, so a reader can come away more sure than the data allows.

**What would move it most**
1. **The ranking looks firmer than the data supports (defect).** Each bar carries a one-decimal label ("3.4%", "4.0%"), and nothing on the chart shows how much those figures could move. The table on the right shows how small some groups are: Enterprise is 38 conversions from 1,120 trials. By rough arithmetic, Enterprise's 3.4% could plausibly be anywhere from about 2.3% to 4.5%, which overlaps every other plan. Growth and Team overlap as well. Only Growth against Starter (4.0% vs 3.1%) clearly separates. A reader who sees "Enterprise converts worse than Team" here is reading noise, and may act on it. Show the spread on the chart itself: error bars or interval whiskers on each bar, or at least a note under the chart with each plan's trial count, to show where the samples are small (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16).
2. **The page shows numbers but doesn't say what they mean (taste, but it decides how the page gets used).** The title "Trial to paid conversion by plan" only names the topic. All four bars are the same full-strength purple, so the chart doesn't point to any one of them. The reader has to work out the point alone, and different readers will work out different points. Also, without a previous quarter or a target, you can't tell whether 3–4% is good or bad. To fix it:
   - Write the finding as the title, for example "Growth converts best; differences between other plans are within noise".
   - Draw the bar that matters in the accent purple and the rest in a muted tone (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design": one colour for the series, a second for the point the reader must notice).
   - Add Q2 as a faint comparison mark or a small "vs Q2" change next to each label, so each rate has something to be judged against (Few, pitfall 2).

**Smaller things.**
- **The two descriptions of the cutoff can be read two ways.** The subtitle says "within 30 days". The Definition says trials "still open on 30 October are counted as not converted", which sounds like a calendar cutoff. A trial that starts on 1 July and pays on day 45 is not converted under the first reading and converted under the second. Use one wording for both.
- **"Paid" as a column header is vague.** "Paid accounts" or "Converted" says what is being counted. Adding a "Rate" column would let readers check the table against the bar labels.
- **The lower right is empty.** From about y=420 down, the right column is blank and looks like space left over rather than space with a purpose. The uncertainty note or the Q2 comparison from finding 2 could go there.

**What's working.**
- **The chart is honest and lean.** The value axis starts at zero, gridlines are faint, there's no legend because the bars are labelled directly, and the plans sit in their natural tier order rather than sorted by value. Keep all of this when you add error bars.
- **The context is right next to the chart.** Raw counts, a written definition and a dated source line are exactly what an analytics reader needs to trust the numbers. The arithmetic checks out (286/9,210 = 3.1%, and so on).
- **The type is calm and has clear levels.** There are four sizes in use, the bold title is clearly in charge, and the table figures are right-aligned. Nothing fights for attention.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the nav tabs show keyboard focus, and how the chart and table stack on a narrow screen.
