**First read.** The eye goes to the headline "Trial to paid conversion by plan", then to the four purple bars, and then to the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. The table on the right comes next and the Definition last. It reads as an internal SaaS analytics page, and it feels calm, clean and trustworthy. But nothing on the page tells you what to make of it: there's no point stated and nothing to compare against.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard page for a growth or product lead. It should tell them how Q3 trial conversion differed by plan so they can decide where to act.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid and the numbers check out (286/9,210 = 3.1%, 185/5,140 = 3.6%, 118/2,950 = 4.0%, 38/1,120 = 3.4%). But the page only reports figures and makes no point. It doesn't say what the differences mean, whether they're real, or whether 3–4% is good. The definition of "converted" can also be read two ways. A reader leaves with four numbers and no decision.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline names a topic, and no bar is marked as the point.** "Trial to paid conversion by plan" only repeats what the axis and bar labels already say. All four bars are the same purple, so nothing tells the reader which difference matters. The tallest bar wins by default, and the reader has to work out the meaning alone. Fix: make the title or subtitle state the finding, for example "Growth trials converted best in Q3; Starter worst". Then give that bar (or that pair) a second colour and grey the rest, as Few recommends in "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design": one colour for the series, a second for the point the reader must notice. *Taste / common sense:* the "title states the takeaway" rule has no approved source. It matters here because this page exists to support a decision.
2. **Nothing shows whether the differences between plans are real.** The bars differ by 0.4 to 0.9 points. Enterprise rests on 38 conversions from 1,120 trials, which by my rough count gives a 95% range of about 2.3–4.4%. That overlaps every other plan. Team (about 3.1–4.1%) overlaps Starter and Growth too. Only Growth vs Starter is clearly apart. As drawn, the bars look like a firm ranking, and someone could reprice or redirect sales effort over noise. Fix: add interval whiskers to the bars, or at least a note on Enterprise's small sample, so the reader can tell a real gap from chance (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). *Defect.*
3. **The conversion window has two possible readings.** The subtitle says "became paid accounts **within 30 days**". The Definition says "Trials still open **on 30 October** are counted as not converted." Take a trial started on 1 July and paid on 15 September (day 76). Under the subtitle it is not converted. Under the Definition it is, because it wasn't open on 30 October. If the 30-day rule is the real one, the 30 October line is just the cut-off for late-September trials and should say so. If not, the subtitle is wrong. These are the numbers people will quote, so one wrong reading changes what everyone thinks the rate is. Fix: write one rule in full, for example "Converted = first invoice paid within 30 days of trial start. Data pulled 30 October, so every Q3 trial had its full 30 days." *Defect.*
4. **There's nothing to compare against.** There's no Q2 figure, no target and no overall rate, so the reader can't tell whether 3.6% for Team is good, bad or normal. This is the comparison that would show which plan needs attention. Fix: add the previous quarter, either as a faint marker per bar or a column in the table, plus an all-plans line (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design": put a comparison such as the last period or the target next to each number). *Convention, and here it costs the reader.*

**Smaller things.**
- The table header "Paid" (top right) could mean paid accounts or revenue. Write "Paid accounts", and consider adding a "Conversion" column so the table can stand on its own.
- Lower right (below the Definition, about 460–760 px down) is a large leftover empty area while the chart runs to the bottom of the left side. That's the natural home for the Q2 comparison or the sample-size note from findings 2 and 4.

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest and quiet. The axis starts at 0%, gridlines are pale, values sit directly on the bars, and the plans run in tier order. Keep all of that when you add the emphasis colour.
- The page carries its own context: a source line with exact dates, a definition, and the raw counts beside the rates. That's why the figures can be checked at all. Keep it, and fix the wording.
- The type is restrained, with a few sizes separated by weight and grey rather than near-identical sizes. The numbers in the table are right-aligned and line up.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the top navigation, and how the two-column layout reflows on a narrow screen. The table should drop below the chart, not shrink beside it.
