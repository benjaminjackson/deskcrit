**First read.** The eye goes straight to the dark middle card, "Woodland loop", before it reaches the page title or either white card. The page reads as a list of three local walks, and the middle one looks either featured or pasted in from another site. I can't tell which. After that, what you notice is how much empty space sits in the bottom half of every card.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short list of walking routes for local walkers, where the job is to let someone compare the three and open the one that suits them.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. Three routes meant to be compared side by side are set as two matching cards and one that breaks every rule the others follow, and nothing on the page explains why. That makes the comparison harder, not easier.

**What would move it most**
1. **The middle card belongs to a different design** (defect). "Woodland loop" changes almost everything at once:
   - It has a dark fill where the others are white.
   - Its heading is a serif, a few points larger than the sans headings beside it.
   - Its inner padding is about half the outer cards', so its heading, text and button start further left and sit higher than their neighbours.
   - It has square corners where the others are rounded.
   - Its button is an amber outline pill where the others are solid blue rounded rectangles.

   The viewer has to guess what this means. Is it recommended, selected, sponsored, or a bug? Nothing answers. The signals also contradict each other: the card shouts, but its button is the weakest-looking of the three, as if this were the route you're less meant to open. The offsets are small enough to look like slips rather than a decision (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I, on placements that are neither clearly here nor there). The serif against the sans is neither a match nor a clear contrast; it is a clash (Dair, *Design with Type*, chs. 7–8). Elements of one kind should follow one programme, with a variant only where a rule calls for it (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).

   Fix: decide whether this route is actually featured.
   - **If it isn't:** set it exactly like the other two.
   - **If it is:** keep the same grid, padding, type, corners and button so it still reads as one of the set. Mark it with one clear, named signal, such as a "Recommended" label, a tinted fill or a heavier border. Give it the same or a stronger button, never a weaker one.

2. **The bottom 40% of every card is empty** (defect, unless something failed to load). The three cards share a fixed height, but the content fills only the top half. Under each "View route" button is a large white (or dark) block that serves no purpose. The cards look unfinished, like image slots that never loaded, and the row is twice as tall as its content. Fix: if a map or photo belongs there, that's the strongest single addition a walking-route card can have. If not, let the cards size to their content, with all three sharing the height of the tallest. (Ruder, *Typography*, "Contrasts": empty space should be gathered into areas that do work, not left over.)

3. **You can't compare the routes at a glance** (defect, from the copy). The facts a walker chooses by are buried in sentences, in a different order each time:
   - **Distance:** six, four, ten kilometres.
   - **Effort:** "two steep climbs", "flat", "paved".
   - **What's on the way:** a cafe, benches, bicycles.

   To compare, the reader has to read all three paragraphs and hold the numbers in their head. There's no walking time anywhere. "Good for bicycles" also lands two ways: it tells a cyclist the path suits them, and tells a walker to expect bikes. Fix: put the same short line of facts in the same place on every card, in figures, e.g. "6 km · about 2 hrs · 2 steep climbs". Keep one sentence for what makes each walk worth doing. "View route" is acceptable, but "See the map" or "Get directions" would say what actually happens when you click.

**Smaller things.**
- The Cliff path text ends with "end." alone on the last line. Rewrite or rebalance the line so it doesn't sit on its own (Bringhurst, 2.4.2).
- The amber outline button reads faintly as a warning on a page with no warnings. If an accent is kept, use the blue, so one colour means "open this route".

**What's working.**
- The two outer cards are a sound pattern. The bold sans heading sits clearly above grey body text, with contrast from weight and colour as well as size (Dair). The solid blue buttons are large and obviously clickable. The gaps between cards are even. Keep this as the template for all three.
- The body text is a comfortable size, has a short measure and good contrast, and is set flush left. The descriptions are concrete ("benches every few hundred metres"), which is the right raw material for the facts line.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the three buttons. Check that the amber outline button gets a focus ring visible against the dark fill.
- How the three-column row reflows to one column on a phone. Check that the middle card's odd padding doesn't get worse there.
- Whether the empty lower half of each card is a missing image. Check in a live browser.
