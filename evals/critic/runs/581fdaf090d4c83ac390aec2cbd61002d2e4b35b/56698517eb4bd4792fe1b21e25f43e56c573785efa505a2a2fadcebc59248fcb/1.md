**First read.** The eye lands on six flat colour blocks, each with a pale circle, and moves along them like a row of paint swatches. The page heading is small and reads as the same level as the card titles, so it doesn't register until the second look. It reads as a wireframe, or a page whose photos never loaded. It feels tidy and empty.

**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming a web gallery for a garden show, where visitors browse the show's areas by picture and short caption.

**Verdict.** As shown, it fails at its job: a gallery whose pictures show nothing. The grid and captions are sound. But until real images replace the swatches, the visitor sees no roses, herbs or pond, only colours. Fix the pictures first; everything else is small.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders, and the pictures are the content.** In every card, the image area is a flat saturated block with a centred pale circle, and each circle just touches the block's bottom edge. "Climbing roses over the east gate" sits under a red rectangle. The visitor can't see anything the caption promises, so nothing invites them to go to the rose arch or the pond. The six unrelated hues also make the page feel like a colour picker, not a garden. *Defect*, unless these are images that failed to load in the capture; if so, check the live page. Direction: real photos with one consistent treatment (same crop ratio, distance and light) so the set reads as one show. If you keep a placeholder state for loading, make it a neutral tint, not six saturated colours.
2. **The page heading is weaker than what it heads.** "Garden show gallery" looks about 20 px bold, and the card titles look about 22 px bold. That's two sizes a couple of pixels apart, with the larger one on the lower level. The page has no entry point, and the title of the whole page reads like a seventh card label. *Convention* (Bringhurst on a deliberate scale; Dair: weak contrast is worse than none). Direction: a clear step, with the heading well above the card titles (roughly 1.5× or more), and the card titles staying where they are. Naming the show ("Hearthfield Garden Show 2026", or whatever it really is) would also give the page an identity. Right now the heading could sit on any garden site.

**Smaller things.**
- Rose arch card (top left): the title sits about 2 px higher and the caption about 4 px higher than in the other five cards, so its baselines don't line up with Herb bed and Pond edge. It looks like a stray margin; give it the same spacing as the rest.
- The circles in every image area are centred and touch the bottom edge exactly, which is a tangent that makes them look clipped by accident. They become moot once real photos go in.

**What's working.**
- The grid: one gutter, three equal columns, and a clear left edge from the heading down. Keep it.
- Grouping by space: each title and caption sit tight under their own picture, with a much larger gap before the next row, so no caption can be read as belonging to the wrong image.
- The captions are concrete and short ("Tomatoes in late July", "Reeds and water lilies"). They tell a visitor what they'll actually see, which is what a gallery caption should do.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are links. Nothing signals it. If they open detail pages, they need a hover or focus state and some sign that they can be clicked.
- Whether the colour blocks are unloaded images. Check them in a live browser on a slow connection.
