**First read.** The eye lands on the three bold white headlines, "Learn to swim", "Join the lanes" and "Family hour", reads them left to right, reads one line of description under each, then drops into three large blocks of empty colour and stops. It reads as the programme section of a local pool's website, aimed at adults and families nearby. It feels friendly and confident at the top. The bottom half of each card feels unfinished, as if a photo or a button didn't load.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a section of a swimming pool's website that lets a visitor see the three ways to swim there, pick the one that fits, and take the next step (book, see the timetable).

**Verdict.** It does half its job. The three offers are clear and well written, but the cards end in empty colour with no way to act, so a visitor who has picked an option is left with nothing to do. That missing next step is the main problem, and the empty card bottoms make it worse.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no next step on any card.** Nothing on the page is a button, a link, an arrow or a "Book a class" line. Nothing even shows that the cards can be clicked. A visitor who decides "Tuesday classes, that's me" can only guess whether to click the orange block, scroll, or go and find a timetable. Every view needs one plain action, and a control has to look like a control (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: affordances and signifiers; Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis). **Defect.** Fix: give each card one visible action at the bottom that names the exact thing it does, such as "Book a Tuesday class", "See lane times" or "Plan a family visit". Make it look like a button or an underlined link, all three in the same style, since the three options are equals.

2. **The bottom 55% of every card is empty colour with no job.** About 300 px of flat fill sits under each paragraph, from y≈330 to y≈630. Space that is shaped on purpose gives the content room. Large unexplained empty areas don't, and they read as something missing (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Here it pushes the cards to poster height while the content stays caption-sized. It also lets the three saturated slabs outweigh the words, and it makes the page look as if images failed to load. **Defect.** If photos were meant to sit there, the capture caught them unloaded: check in a live browser. If not, fill the space with what the visitor needs next (time, price, the action from finding 1) or shorten the cards to fit their content. A picture of each activity in that space would also do more than flat colour.

3. **The copy stops short of the details a visitor decides on.** The descriptions are good: "with a coach in the water beside you" speaks to a nervous adult learner in their own terms. But each one leaves out what the visitor needs to commit. "From six" doesn't say until when. "Sunday afternoons" gives no hours. "Small adult classes" gives no size, no price and no start date. "Three speeds" doesn't say whether it means lanes. These are the facts a visitor will act on and hold the pool to, so the gaps send them hunting elsewhere or put them off. **Defect.** Fix: add one short line of hard facts per card ("Tuesdays 7–8pm · 6 per class · £X") just above the action. This also fills part of the empty space from finding 2.

**Smaller things.**
- The row of cards is off-centre: 56 px of margin on the left and about 96 px on the right. It looks like a near-miss, not a choice. Centre the row or run it to the same margin on both sides.
- Nothing on the section says whose pool this is or what the three cards are, such as a section heading ("Ways to swim at …"). A visitor arriving here directly has no context. That's fine if a heading sits just above this crop; check the full page.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clean:** two sizes, a big bold headline at about 52 px and a body at about 19 px, with tight two-line headline leading. Each card reads as headline then detail at a glance. Keep it to these two levels when you add the fact line and the action; use weight, not a third size.
- **The headlines name what the visitor does:** "Learn to swim", "Join the lanes". These are verbs from the visitor's world, not category labels like "Lessons" or "Lane swimming". Keep that voice for the button labels.
- **Three equal cards with one colour each** is the right structure for three parallel options. No card is pushed above the others, and the colour gives each programme an identity you could reuse on the timetable and booking pages. White on all three looks comfortably above 4.5:1 (orange is the closest; check it at about 5:1).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are clickable, and what their hover and focus states look like.
- Whether the empty lower halves are images that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
- How the three columns stack on a phone.
