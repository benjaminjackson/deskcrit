**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Notes for ferry passengers", the only heavy thing on the page, and then down three short serif paragraphs. It reads as a plain, calm information page for people who use the river ferry. It looks fine and is easy to read. The trouble only shows when you try to use it: you finish reading and still can't say when to be at the landing.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that tells passengers when the ferry runs, how long a crossing takes and how bikes are handled, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are sound and need little work. The page still fails at its main job, because none of its times can be acted on. Every time is approximate or depends on something outside the page ("early morning", "late evening", "before dusk", "in winter"). A passenger can't plan a crossing from it, and the only fix it offers is a printed timetable at the landing they are trying to reach.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times are too vague to act on** (defect: copy). These are in paragraph 1 ("every forty minutes between early morning and late evening") and paragraph 3 ("one boat each hour", "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk"). These are the lines a passenger acts on and later holds the operator to, and each one can be read more than one way. "Early morning" could mean 6:00 or 8:00. "Before dusk" moves by about two hours across the winter. "Winter" has no start or end date. The cost is real: someone who reads "late evening" as 22:00 when the last boat goes at 20:40 is left stranded on the wrong bank. Fix: give the first and last departure times from each landing, give the dates the winter timetable starts and ends, and replace "before dusk" with a clock time, or with "about N minutes before sunset" plus a link to that day's times. This page is where passengers check the timetable, so it can't send them to the shelter for it.
2. **"The last crossing leaves the quay": which quay?** (defect: copy). The page names two ends, the north landing and the market quay. "The quay" most likely means the market quay, which leaves open whether the last boat from the north landing goes earlier. A passenger at the north landing can't tell when their own last boat leaves. Fix: give the last departure from each end.
3. **The facts people look for are buried in prose** (taste). Frequency, hours, crossing time, the winter change and bikes are spread across three paragraphs. Someone checking "is there still a boat?" has to read every sentence to find the one number they need. Three short paragraphs are fine for the background. My view is that the departure times belong in a small block or table above them, so the page answers its main question at a glance and the prose explains the rest. No checklist line fails here, since the text is already broken into short chunks; the case rests on the job, which is looking things up.

**Smaller things.** None from the checklist.

**What's working.**
- **Clear contrast between the two faces.** The bold sans heading against the serif body differs at a glance in structure, weight and size, so there are two clear levels and nothing in between (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). Keep two sizes and keep that pairing if a times block is added. Don't add a third size that sits close to one of them.
- **Comfortable reading column.** The lines look like about 65 to 75 characters, within Bringhurst's 45 to 75 range. Line spacing looks like about 1.5 times the type size, and paragraphs are marked by space, flush left. Don't widen the column to fill the empty right side; that space is what keeps the lines readable.
- **The bike paragraph is the model for the rest.** It says who does what and when: "Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail." Rewrite the timetable lines to the same standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Reflow at narrow widths and at 400% zoom. With a times block added, check that it stacks to one column without horizontal scrolling.
