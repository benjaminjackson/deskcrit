**First read.** My eye goes to the big black headline, then down to the blue "Create your link" button, then across four identical cards and off the bottom of the screen. Then it drifts to the empty right half of the hero, where I keep expecting a picture of the product that isn't there. It reads as a clean, competent scheduling tool for small service businesses. It also reads as a template: it could be any of a dozen booking products.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page of a booking-link product, meant to get owners of small appointment-based businesses (dental, tutoring, physio, as the customer names suggest) to create a link.

**Verdict.** The page is tidy and readable, but it never shows the product. The person you're selling to never sees what their own customers would see, so the page asks them to take "fills your calendar" on trust. That missing picture is the main thing holding it back. The other problems are small and fixable.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no product in it, and the right half is empty.** About 540 px to the right of the headline, from the nav down to the cards, is blank. Every bit of weight in the hero sits on the left, and nothing balances it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). This isn't open space used on purpose. It's leftover space, and it reads as an image that failed to load. If one did fail, check it in a live browser. If it's meant to be empty, the cost is high. A booking link is easy to picture, and the strongest case for it is one look at the page a guest lands on: a week of free times, one picked, a confirmation. Without that, the buyer can't judge the value for themselves. All they get is the headline's word. Fix: put the booking page there, filled with an example from their own world (say, "Harbor Dental, cleaning, 30 min"). It also answers "See a live example" before they even click. *Defect.*
2. **The headline could be on anyone's page.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" is the category's stock promise, and "fill" is a claim with nothing behind it. The subhead is better: it says what actually happens, in order. But neither line tells the buyer what's different here, or lands on something they'd lose without it, like phone tag or no-shows. The card copy already has the specifics ("cut down on no-shows", "nobody has to do the math"). Lift one of them into the headline, written to "you". The asks are good, so keep them. "Create your link" names one action, and "See a live example" lets the buyer judge the product with their own eyes. *Taste, argued on the copy.*
3. **The customer names do no work.** "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" are grey text under a rule, with no label. They sit about as far from the cards as the cards sit from the hero, so they look like footer links, not proof (Arnheim, grouping by proximity). Give them a heading that makes a claim ("Booked by 1,200 clinics and tutors", if that's true), more space above, and logos if you have them. The grey and small size also look borderline for contrast: measure them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- The cards don't follow one pattern. In card 1, the "New" badge is pushed to the right edge, which forces "Shared calendars" onto two lines. Its text and "Learn more" end up about 30 px lower than in the other three cards. Put the badge inline like the others, or shorten the title.
- The badges mean two different things. "New" and "Beta" are statuses. "Auto" and "Email" describe features. Same shape, different meaning. Keep badges for status only and move "Auto" and "Email" into the body copy.
- "New" uses the button blue on a label you can't click. Keep that blue for actions and make the badge neutral or a softer tint.
- "Learn more" appears four times. Out of context (in a screen reader's link list, for example) the four are identical. Name the destination: "How reminders work".
- "Start free" and "Create your link" are the same blue button with two names for, presumably, the same action. Use one wording.
- The headline breaks as "fill your / calendar", leaving one word on line two and splitting the phrase. Rebalance the break, or widen the text column slightly.
- The Intake forms icon is a layout glyph, not a form. Swap it for something that reads as a form or questionnaire.

**What's working.**
- The hero order is clear: the headline is unmistakably first, the subhead is set back by weight and colour, and then there's one solid button with a quieter underlined link beside it. That's a proper primary/secondary split. Keep it.
- The card copy says who does what and why it helps ("Guests see slots in their own time zone, so nobody has to do the math"). That's the voice the headline is missing.
- One clean grid: the hero, the cards, and the rule all share the 64 px left and right edges, with even gaps between cards.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the buttons and links, and how the four cards reflow on a phone.
