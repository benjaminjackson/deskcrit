I'd rate this a competent, conventional sign-up form that gives nobody a reason to fill it in, and it asks for more than it needs. Nothing on the page says why Quillnest is worth an account, and the form wants six fields where three would do.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Create your account", runs down a stack of six empty fields and stops on the navy button. It reads as a generic sign-up page for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and anonymous. Besides the logo, the only thing that names the product is "Start a free workspace for your notes."

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: this is the sign-up page for a note-taking product, and its job is to turn a first-time visitor into an account holder with as little effort as possible.

**Verdict.** The page is cleanly built, but it is a template that asks for too much. The biggest problem is that it never says why someone should sign up while asking for six fields, two of which do nothing for the visitor.

**What would move it most**

1. **The page asks for too much before giving anything back.** The six fields are first name, last name, email, password, workspace name and "How did you hear about us?". The referral question helps your marketing, not the person signing up. "Workspace name" asks them to name something they haven't seen yet. Every extra field is one more reason to leave before the button. This breaks Nielsen's "aesthetic and minimalist design" heuristic, because every element should support the user's main task. Nielsen's progressive disclosure points the same way. **Defect.** Cut to email and password, plus a name field if you truly need one. Ask for the workspace name inside the product, or prefill it ("Ben's notes") and let people change it later. Move the referral question to after sign-up, or drop it.

2. **Nothing on the page sells the product.** The left and right thirds of the screen are empty pale blue. The only pitch is "Start a free workspace for your notes." Any notes app could say that, so a visitor learns nothing about what makes this one different, and nothing pushes them to finish the form. "Create account" names what the button does but promises no result. **Taste, argued:** on a cold sign-up page, the copy is what carries someone through six fields. Use the empty side space or the subtitle for one concrete promise in the visitor's own terms. A comparison with the tool they already use ("your notes from X, searchable in seconds") works better than an adjective. Consider a button label that names the result, such as "Start my workspace".

3. **The form doesn't tell people its rules before they break them.** The Password field gives no requirements, so anyone whose password doesn't fit finds out only after they submit. No field is marked required or optional, although "How did you hear about us?" is almost certainly optional. People will either fill it in out of caution or wonder whether they can skip it. This breaks Nielsen's error prevention (heuristic 5) and help (heuristic 10). Budiu (NN/g) gives the convention for marking required fields. **Defect.** Put a one-line hint under the password field ("At least 8 characters"). Then put "(optional)" after each optional label, or mark the required ones with an asterisk explained once at the top.

**Smaller things.**
- "Sign in" in "Already have an account? Sign in" (bottom of the card) is the same grey as the rest of the sentence, with no underline, so a returning user can't tell it's a link. That breaks WCAG G183 and Norman's signifiers. Underline it, or set it in the button's blue and underline it.
- The input borders are a pale grey-blue on white and look below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 requires for component edges. Measure them. Below 3:1, low-vision users lose the outlines of the fields. Darken the border one step.
- The "How did you hear about us?" dropdown is blank by default. Use a "Select one" prompt so it doesn't look like it failed to load.
- "Workspace name" assumes the visitor knows what a workspace is in this product. Add a short hint, or move the field after sign-up (see finding 1).
- No quick route such as Google or Apple sign-in. For a free notes app, that is the fastest way past the form (common sense).
- The type scale has 14, 15 and 16 px sizes (labels and link, subtitle, button) sitting so close they don't read as separate levels (Bringhurst). Fold the subtitle into the 14 or 16 size and let colour do the separating.

**What's working.**
- Every field has a visible label above it, set bold and close to its own field, with clearly more space before the next label. You can scan the form instantly and it never relies on placeholder text. Keep this spacing rhythm when you cut fields.
- There is one clearly primary action. The full-width navy "Create account" button is the only saturated block on the screen, and the logo's blue is the only other use of that colour. Keep that restraint.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and the button: whether they are visible and at least 3:1.
- Error and validation states: where messages appear, whether they say how to fix the problem, whether they rely on colour alone, and whether typed input is kept.
- How the centred card behaves at narrow widths (reflow).
