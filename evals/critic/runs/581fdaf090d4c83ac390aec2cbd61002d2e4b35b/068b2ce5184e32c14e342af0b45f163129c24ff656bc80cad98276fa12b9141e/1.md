**First read.** The eye goes to the heading "Garden show gallery" and then drops straight to the big red Rose arch tile. From there it moves right down the column of three, then along the bottom row, and ends on a grey line saying "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size." It reads as a tidy, calm desktop gallery page in a featured-plus-grid layout. There are no gardens in it, only six coloured blocks with the same pale circle in each.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where visitors to a garden show browse photos of the gardens on display and pick one to look at.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound: one clear lead tile, a steady grid and quiet type. But as captured it can't do its job, because there are no pictures in a picture gallery and a stray internal note sits at the bottom of the page. Fix those two, then loosen the spacing between rows.

**What would move it most**
1. **The gallery has no images in it.** All six tiles are flat colour fields with the same clipped circle. A visitor sees "Rose arch: Climbing roses over the east gate" next to a red rectangle, so the heading promises gardens and the page shows none. If these are stand-ins, that's fine, but nothing about the gallery can be judged until real photos are in. Check one thing carefully when they arrive: the hero currently stands out partly because it's the only warm, saturated red. A real photo of roses on a green arch may not stand out the same way, so make sure its size alone (about four times the area of a small tile) still makes it the first thing seen. The six unrelated saturated hues are also a palette problem if any of them survive into the real design: they all shout equally, so nothing is marked as more important by colour. *Defect, unless these are known placeholders.*
2. **The line "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size" is left on the page.** It's the last thing the eye reaches. It reads as an internal note that got shipped, and it's wrong anyway: the Rose arch tile is plainly much larger than the others. A visitor gets a confusing message about a review they know nothing about, and it makes the page look unfinished. Remove it. Nothing else on the page needs it. *Defect.*
3. **The rows run into each other.** Each caption sits about 8–12 px below its own picture but only about 15 px above the next picture, which is the same as the 14 px gap between columns. The Rose arch caption is worst: "Climbing roses over the east gate." almost touches the Bean rows tile, so the hero's text looks as if it belongs to the row below. When the gap within a group is about the same as the gap between groups, the eye can't see where one ends (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on grouping by nearness). Make the gap between rows clearly bigger than the gap between picture and caption, roughly two to three times, and keep the caption tight to its own picture. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- The hero image's bottom edge (y≈437) misses the Pond edge image's bottom edge (y≈474) by about 37 px. That reads as an almost-alignment rather than a choice. Either end the hero image on the same line as the Pond image, or move it clearly away from it (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim on ambiguous placement).
- The hero caption and the Pond edge caption sit on slightly different baselines. They will line up once the hero image edge is fixed.
- The heading "Garden show gallery" could be on any show's site. Name the show and the year so visitors know what they're looking at. The tile captions ("Tomatoes in late July", "Plum and pear trees") are concrete and good.

**What's working.**
- **One lead tile and a calm grid around it.** A single large tile plus five equal tiles gives the page an obvious starting point and an easy order to follow. Keep that structure.
- **A clear three-step caption system.** The hero title is large and bold, the small titles are small and bold, and the descriptions are regular weight in grey. Each level is set apart by weight and colour, not just size, and the descriptions stay easy to read.
- **Consistent margins and gutters.** One left edge (x=60), one right edge (x=1220) and a steady ~14 px gap between columns make the page look deliberate.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles look clickable or open a larger view. Nothing on the tiles signals it, so check the hover and focus states.
- How the grid reflows on a narrow screen.
