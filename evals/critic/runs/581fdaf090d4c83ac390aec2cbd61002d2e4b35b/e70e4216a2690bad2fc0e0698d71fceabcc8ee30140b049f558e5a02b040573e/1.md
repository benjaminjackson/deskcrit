**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tilted blue SALE badge on the right, then back across to the headline "Linens and cookware for the weekend", then down to four grey cards that don't show any products. Then it runs out of things to look at: there's no button, no product photos, and about a quarter of the screen is empty at the bottom. It reads as the homepage of a calm, oat-and-linen homewares shop with a loud discount sticker slapped on it. It feels unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage of a small homewares shop during a weekend sale, and its job is to get shoppers into the sale and onto products before Sunday.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The page announces a sale and then gives the shopper no way into it: the hero has no button, and none of the products shows a sale price. Everything else on the page comes second to that.

**What would move it most**

1. **The sale has no way in, and the promise never reaches a product.** The hero says "Up to 40% off through Sunday" but has no button. The badge is the loudest thing on the page and is styled like one: a fill, rounded corners, a shadow. The only sure link to the sale is the small "Sale" in the top-right nav. Below that, the four "Popular this week" cards show single prices ($34, $89, $52, $68) with no old price, no percentage and no sale tag. A shopper who came for 40% off can't tell whether any of these items are in the sale. To them, "up to 40%" is a claim they have to take on trust. *Defect* (Material Design and Apple HIG: one high-emphasis primary action per view; Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: things that look pressable should be pressable). Fix: give the hero one filled primary button that names the destination, such as "Shop the sale", in the brand's accent colour. Then either make this row sale items with the old price struck through next to the new one, or add a sale row. Let the price difference make the case on numbers the shopper already understands.

2. **The SALE badge breaks the page in three ways.** Close up, it's saturated red letters on a saturated blue of about the same lightness, top right of the hero band.
   - **The letters shimmer and are hard to read.** In greyscale the red and blue nearly merge, and the text looks well under the 3:1 needed for large text (WCAG 1.4.3). Red on blue at equal lightness is the textbook vibrating pair (Albers, *Interaction of Color*). *Defect.*
   - **It comes from a different shop.** Its primary-colour clip-art style and wide display lettering don't match the linen tones and quiet sans used everywhere else. The page stops reading as one brand. *Taste, argued:* this shop sells calm, and the badge shouts.
   - **It adds no information.** The subhead already says there's a sale. It also spends the page's only strong colour on decoration instead of an action, which goes against Apple HIG and Material's advice to keep the accent for the main action.

   Fix: remove the badge, or turn it into the primary button from point 1 in one brand accent with dark-on-light or light-on-dark contrast. Keep the tilt only if the brand wants that energy everywhere.

3. **There's no product imagery anywhere.** All four card images are flat tinted rectangles with a pale rounded box in the middle, and the hero band has no picture at all. Homewares are bought on how they look: the weave of the linen, the finish on the stoneware. Here the shopper is asked to want a "Wool throw" from its name and a price. If these are placeholders in a mock-up, fine. Real photos would then fix this, and the hero needs one too: the headline names linens and cookware, so show them. *Defect* as shown.

4. **The header is missing what every shop header has.** There's no cart, search or account link in the top right. The nav is four category links. Shoppers expect the cart top right (Nielsen, *10 Usability Heuristics*, heuristic 4, consistency and standards). Without it they can't see whether they've added anything or how to check out, and with no search they have to browse to find a specific item. *Convention, and here it costs something.* Fix: add search, account and a cart with an item count on the right, and move the category links next to them or below.

**Smaller things.**
- Hero text starts about 8 px to the right of the logo, the "Popular this week" heading and the cards (x≈56 against x=48). It's close enough to look like a mistake. Put it on the same left edge.
- The headline breaks as "for / the weekend", so "for" hangs at the end of line one. Break it at a phrase boundary, or reword it.
- The headline copy can be read two ways: products to use at the weekend, or a sale that lasts the weekend. It could also sit on any shop's homepage. Say the offer in the headline itself, e.g. "Up to 40% off linens and cookware, until Sunday night".
- "Through Sunday" is a deadline shoppers will hold you to. Give the date and the cut-off time.
- About 220 px of blank space below the cards looks left over rather than planned. Pull the next section up, or add one.

**What's working.**
- The base palette (oat hero band, white page, soft tinted image wells) is calm and fits linens and stoneware. Keep it and build the accent from it.
- The type scale is tight: about four sizes (headline, section heading, product name and subhead, price/nav), each clearly its own level, with weight rather than size separating product name from price. Keep it.
- The card row is a clean, evenly spaced four-up grid with consistent internal spacing. It's a good frame once real photos and sale prices go in.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the SALE badge is clickable. If it is, finding 1 gets better but finding 2 stands.
- Whether the badge is live text or an image of text. Check in the browser.
- Focus states, and how the page reflows at narrow widths. With four cards and a right-aligned badge, check reflow at 320 px.
