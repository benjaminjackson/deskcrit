**First read.** I look first at the inbox tray illustration, then the bold "No open conversations", then the grey explanation, then the outlined button. It reads as a calm, tidy empty state in a customer-support tool. The two versions read the same, apart from a cooler or warmer tint on the tray.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an empty state for a support agent's inbox. It should tell them nothing needs answering and give them a way to start a conversation themselves.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job. The only change is the tray's fill, green or blue. Both are pastels of about the same lightness, so it doesn't change hierarchy, contrast or meaning. Neither version is better. The real gaps are the same in both: the one action looks optional, and the nav doesn't clearly show which view you're in.

**What would move it most**
1. **The one action is styled as a secondary button.** "Open customer list" is a thin-bordered white button under grey text. It's the only thing the agent can do next, but it has the lowest emphasis on the page, below the illustration and the headline. Material Design and Nielsen Norman Group both say a view's one main action should get high emphasis. This is a **convention** broken at a small cost: the eye stops at the headline, and the button reads as a footnote. Fix: give it the filled primary style.
2. **You can't tell which view you're in.** In the sidebar, "Inbox" is bold and the rest are regular weight. That could mean "selected", or it could mean "section title, with Assigned / Waiting / Closed under it". The headline says "No open conversations" without naming the view, so an agent who has clicked "Waiting" can't confirm they're empty there rather than in the inbox. This is a **defect** against Nielsen 1, Visibility of system status. Fix: mark the selected item with a tint or bar as well as weight, and name the view in the headline or a page title ("Inbox: no open conversations").

**Smaller things.**
- The second sentence ("You can also start a conversation from a customer's profile") points to profiles, but the button opens the customer list. Make the sentence say the button's step ("Open a customer to start a conversation").

**What's working.**
- The copy says why the screen is empty and what will fill it, in the agent's own words. Keep that.
- The scale is restrained: three type sizes with clear steps, and one quiet illustration sitting directly above the headline it supports.
- Either fill colour is fine. Both are soft and similar in lightness, neither competes with the headline, and neither carries a meaning the copy contradicts. Green hints at "all caught up", blue is neutral. That's a matter of taste and brand palette, not a reason to prefer one version.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus and hover states on the button and nav items.
