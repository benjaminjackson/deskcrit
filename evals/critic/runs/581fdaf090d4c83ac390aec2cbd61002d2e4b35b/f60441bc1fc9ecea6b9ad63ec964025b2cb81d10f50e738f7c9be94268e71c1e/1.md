**First read.** The eye lands on the three big KPI numbers (3.8%, €64.20, 12.4k), which sit level and equally weighted. It then drops into the orange bar chart, where Saturday's peak and Sunday's drop stand out at once. It reads as a calm, well-made back-office overview for the owner of a small bakery that delivers. It is quiet and trustworthy, and nothing on it is loud.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home screen of a small bakery's shop admin, where the owner checks how things are going and decides how to plan baking and staffing across the week.

**Verdict.** This is a strong, disciplined dashboard. The one real problem is that its chart gives a wrong answer to the question it exists to answer. Because it adds up raw totals for a month with an uneven number of each weekday, it misranks the early-week days. A clear headline on the chart would also do more than the current topic label.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart compares totals for weekdays that occur a different number of times.** *Defect.* The source line says "orders delivered in September 2026". September 2026 starts on a Tuesday, so it has five Tuesdays and five Wednesdays but only four of every other weekday. The bars are raw monthly totals, so Tuesday and Wednesday each get an extra day of orders.
   - Read by eye, the bars are roughly 640, 710, 760 and 820 for Monday to Thursday, which looks like a steady climb.
   - Per delivery day, that is about 160 on Monday, 142 on Tuesday, 152 on Wednesday and 205 on Thursday. Monday is actually busier than Tuesday or Wednesday, and the real jump comes on Thursday.
   - An owner planning bakes and drivers from this chart would put more effort on Tuesday than Monday, which is the wrong way round.

   This breaks Tufte's graphical integrity (*The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2): the size of the bars does not match the effect in the data. Fix it by plotting the average orders per delivery day, and say so in the axis label or the source line ("average per day, September 2026"). If totals are what you want, at least state how many of each weekday the month contained. Check the exact values in the data rather than trusting my estimates from the screenshot.

2. **The chart title names the topic, not the finding.** *Taste, argued on its own terms.* "Orders by delivery day" makes every visitor work out the pattern for themselves, and nowhere on the page is the point stated in words. That also leaves screen-reader users with no summary of the chart. The finding is obvious and useful, something like "Weekends drive deliveries: Saturday is about 3× Sunday" (recheck it once the bars are corrected). Put it in the title, or in one line under it, so the owner gets the answer in the first glance.

**Smaller things.**
- The KPI periods don't quite match: two cards say "Last 30 days", the subscriber card is "As of 30 September 2026", and the chart covers "September 2026". If these are the same window, say it the same way on every card and the chart, so nobody wonders whether they cover different spans.
- This is taste: a shop overview with no orders or revenue figure among its headline numbers is missing the first thing an owner checks. Consider whether one of the three cards should be that figure.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards are complete.** Each number has its unit, its period, and a comparison with an arrow, a sign and a coloured value, so the meaning never rests on green alone. "+0.4 pts" is exactly right for a rate. Keep this pattern.
- **The type is restrained.** Units (%, €, k) are set smaller and lighter than the figures, so the number reads first and the unit second. The hierarchy comes from weight and grey value as much as from size.
- **The chart is clean.** It uses one flat colour, pale gridlines, a zero baseline, days in natural order, and a source line. Almost all the ink is data.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether the nav links show focus, or how the three cards reflow at narrow widths. For an admin screen that will be opened on a phone, check the reflow especially.
