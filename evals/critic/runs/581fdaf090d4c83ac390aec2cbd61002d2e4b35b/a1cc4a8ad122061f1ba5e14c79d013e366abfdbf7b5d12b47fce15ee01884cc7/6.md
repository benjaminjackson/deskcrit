**First read.** My eye goes straight to "How can we help?" on the navy band, then down to the white search bar, then out across the eight topic cards. In about two seconds it reads as a customer help centre: calm, tidy and trustworthy. It also reads as generic. Nothing on the page says what Harbor is, and the topics don't settle the question either.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page of a help centre for an existing Harbor customer who has a problem and wants to find the right article fast, usually by searching.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The hierarchy is clear, the grid is disciplined and the topic copy is good. The biggest problem is that the search field, which is the page's main tool, doesn't look like a search box. The page also offers no shortcut to the questions most people come with.

**What would move it most**
1. **The search field doesn't say "search" (defect / convention).** The white box in the hero has no magnifier icon and no submit button. The only label is the grey placeholder "Search for articles", which disappears on the first keystroke. A visitor will probably still click into it. But it reads as a plain text input, there is no visible way to submit, and once they start typing nothing on screen says what the field is for. Familiar search has a magnifier (Jakob's law, Nielsen Norman Group), and NN/g's "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful" argues against placeholder-only labels. Fix: put a magnifier inside the left edge of the field and a Search button (or an arrow) at the right. Keep a label that stays visible: the headline can do this job if it's tied to the field, or add a visually hidden label for screen readers.
2. **No path to the common questions (defect, common sense).** The page offers two routes: type a query, or pick one of eight broad categories and dig. Most help-centre traffic is a handful of questions, such as resetting a password, finding an invoice or tracking an order. Here they all cost a search or two clicks. Fix: add a short "Popular articles" list of 5 to 8 specific question-shaped links, either under the search field or above the cards. It also tells a visitor in one glance that the help here is specific, which the stock headline doesn't.
3. **A stranger can't tell what Harbor is (defect, Norman's conceptual model).** "Set up your workspace and invite your team", "Connect calendars, chat tools, and storage" and "Sync problems" describe team software. "Orders and shipping: Track a delivery or start a return" describes a shop. Put together, a visitor can't tell what product this is help for, and a customer might wonder whether they've reached the right help centre. Fix: if Harbor really does both, say so with one line under the logo or in the subline (for example, "Help for Harbor workspaces and the Harbor store"). If it doesn't, the shipping card is wrong for this product.
4. **The hero copy could be anyone's (taste).** "How can we help?" over "Browse a topic or search the guides." is the default help-centre opener. The subline also just describes the UI underneath it. Together they cost about 40px of the most valuable space and say nothing a visitor can't already see. Fix: keep the headline if you like, but use the subline for something useful. Name the product, or point to the busiest routes in the reader's own words (for example, "Find answers about billing, sign-in, deliveries and more"). Or drop the subline and move the search up.

**Smaller things.**
- Card descriptions look about 14px, smaller than the 16px nav links and the hero subline. Set them at 15–16px so the text you read isn't the smallest on the page.
- The type scale has about five sizes (34, 20, 18, 16, 14). The logo (~20 bold) and "Browse by topic" (~18 bold) are too close to read as different levels. Make them the same size, or move one clearly up or down.
- The wordmark "Harbor Help Center" is plain bold text in a stock sans, with no mark and no brand colour beyond the navy. The page could belong to anyone. Use the real Harbor logo.
- Two card descriptions end with a single word on their second line ("data." under Privacy and security, "where." under Notifications). Tighten the copy so each fits on one line, or rewrite so the second line carries more than one word.
- The grey placeholder in the search field looks close to the 4.5:1 limit. Measure it against white (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- **The card copy.** Each description names concrete tasks in the reader's words ("Track a delivery or start a return", "Choose which alerts you get and where"), so people can pick a card without opening it. Keep this voice and use it in the hero too.
- **The grid and rhythm.** The cards share one interval horizontally and vertically, and the content runs edge to edge from 64px to 1216px, matching the header. This is why the page feels calm and trustworthy.
- **The icon set.** All the icons use one thin stroke at one size and the same navy, so they set a tone without pulling attention from the titles. The serif headline against a sans body adds a single clear note of character.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the cards and the search field (whether there's any feedback that a card is clickable), and whether the four-column grid reflows to one column on narrow screens.
