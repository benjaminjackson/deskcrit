**First read.** The eye goes straight to "How can we help?" in the navy band, drops to the white search field, then down to two rows of eight topic cards. It reads at once as a product's help center home: calm, tidy, trustworthy. It also reads as every other help center. Swap the name in the top left and nothing on the page would tell you whose product this is.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing page of a SaaS help center. The visitor comes with a specific problem and wants to get to the right article as fast as possible, mostly by searching and sometimes by browsing.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy, the grid and the spacing are sound, and nothing on the page misleads anyone. The biggest gap is in the words, not the layout: the search field, which is the page's main tool, gives the visitor no help working out what to type. The rest is fine-tuning.

**What would move it most**
1. **The search field gives the visitor nothing to go on.** This is the white field in the hero. "Search for articles" names a kind of content, not the visitor's problem. Someone who arrives thinking "my calendar stopped syncing" gets no sign of how to phrase that, or that the answer is in here. The subtitle, "Browse a topic or search the guides.", only describes the page they can already see. The field also calls the content "articles" while the subtitle and the top menu call it "guides", so a careful reader may wonder whether search covers the guides (convention: Nielsen 4, consistency). The field also has no magnifier icon and no submit button. Nielsen's Jakob's law names a search field with a magnifier as the pattern people already know, so the field reads as a generic input until someone clicks into it.
   - **Fix:** make the placeholder or the subtitle point to real cases from the visitor's own world, e.g. *Try "reset my password" or "refund an order"*. Put three or four of the most common questions as links right under the field, so the frequent cases need no typing. Pick one word, either "guides" or "articles", and use it everywhere. Add the magnifier.
   - **Kind:** the copy point is a defect, since it slows the person the page is for. The icon is a convention.

**Smaller things.**
- **The type scale is muddled.** I count six sizes: about 36 (headline), 20 (logo), 18 ("Browse by topic"), 16 (card titles, subtitle), 15 (menu) and 14 (card descriptions). The 20, 18 and 16 bold sizes collide. "Browse by topic" is barely bigger than the card titles below it, so it doesn't read as a separate level. Fix: either make the section heading a clear step larger, or drop it to a quiet small label (smaller, grey or spaced capitals) so it's set apart by more than two pixels (Refactoring UI; Bringhurst).
- **Card descriptions end on single words.** Six of the eight cards break onto a second line holding one word: "your team.", "recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages." This makes the grid look ragged. Fix: rewrite the lines to fit on one line or fill two, or use balanced or pretty wrapping (Bringhurst).
- **"Orders and shipping" doesn't fit with the other topics.** "Track a delivery or start a return" sits next to workspaces, team invites, calendar integrations and sync errors. A new visitor can't tell whether Harbor is a software tool or a shop. If Harbor really does ship physical goods, this is fine. If it doesn't, the card is leftover template copy and should go (Norman, conceptual model).

**What's working.**
- **The grid is real.** The search field spans exactly the middle two card columns. The logo and the menu line up with the outer card edges. The spacing between cards is the same across and down. Keep this when you add the common-question links: they should sit on the same columns.
- **One clear thing to look at first.** The serif headline on navy is the only large, high-contrast element, and the search field is the only white shape in that band. A visitor can't miss what to do first.
- **The icons hang together.** All eight are line icons of one weight, one detail level and one colour, so the grid reads as a single set and the titles carry the meaning.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards and the search field show clear hover and focus states.
- How the four-column grid collapses at narrow widths.
