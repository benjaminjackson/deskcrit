**First read.** The eye goes first to the serif "How can we help?" on the navy band, then down to the white search bar, then into the two rows of topic cards. It reads straight away as a software product's help center. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also familiar: it could be almost any SaaS company's support page.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the home page of a customer help center. Its job is to get someone who is stuck to the right article fast, by search or by topic.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The structure is clear and nothing on it confuses. The biggest thing in the way is the search bar. Search is the main tool on the page, yet the bar has no icon and no button, so it looks like a white strip. After that, the page offers eight broad topics and nothing else, so a person with a specific problem has to guess which one holds the answer.

**What would move it most**
1. **The search bar doesn't look like search.** The white field in the navy band has placeholder text and nothing else: no magnifier, no Search button. On a dark band, a plain white rectangle is about as easy to read as a banner or a blank slot as it is a text field. Mouse users have nothing to click to run a search, and nothing on the page says that pressing Enter will do it. This breaks a convention: Jakob's law (Nielsen) says people already know search by its magnifier icon. It also breaks Norman's signifiers (*The Design of Everyday Things*): nothing tells you where to act or how. The cost is real here because search is the page's main action. **Fix:** put a magnifier icon inside the left of the field, and either add a filled Search button on the right or give the field a clear focus ring. Then nobody has to guess how to submit.

2. **Nothing on the page meets a specific problem halfway.** Below the hero there are only eight broad buckets, and some of them overlap. Someone whose calendar has stopped syncing could reasonably try "Integrations" ("Connect calendars…") or "Troubleshooting" ("Fix sync problems…"). Below the grid the page is empty. There are no popular articles, no sign of whether the service is up (Status is only a nav link), and no "Still stuck? Contact us" at the bottom. The copy is generic too. "Browse a topic or search the guides" just describes the controls you can already see, and "Search for articles" says nothing about what you can type. This is common sense, not a cited rule. **Fix:** add a short list of the five or six most-read articles under the grid, and end the page with a contact route. Rewrite the placeholder as a real example from the user's world, such as *Try "reset two-step sign-in" or "track my order"*. That tells people they can type their problem in their own words. Also adjust the Integrations and Troubleshooting descriptions so sync problems clearly live in one of them.

**Smaller things.**
- **Lone last words:** card descriptions end on one word alone ("data.", "where.", "messages.", and "your team." is close). Rewrite or rebalance them so each one breaks into two even lines.
- **Too many nearby sizes:** I count about five text sizes between 14 and 20 px: logo ~20, "Browse by topic" ~18, card titles ~16, the hero subtitle ~16, nav ~15 and descriptions ~14. "Browse by topic" and the card titles are both bold and only a step or so apart. Use fewer sizes with bigger steps (Bringhurst), and tell the section label apart by weight or colour rather than a near-size.

**What's working.**
- **The hero is a clear entry point.** A serif headline on a solid navy band, set against a sans-serif body, gives one obvious place to land. The serif/sans split reads as a deliberate contrast, not a clash. Keep it.
- **The card grid follows one consistent set of rules.** All eight cards share the same width and gaps. The icons share one stroke weight and level of detail. Each card has the same title-plus-one-line structure, and the grid's left edge lines up with the logo's margin. That makes the grid quick to scan.
- **The colour is restrained.** One navy, a near-white background and grey secondary text. All text looks comfortably readable against its background.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't see the search field's focus state or the cards' hover state. Since I'm recommending a focus ring as part of fix 1, check it in a browser. I also couldn't check how the four-column grid reflows on a phone.
