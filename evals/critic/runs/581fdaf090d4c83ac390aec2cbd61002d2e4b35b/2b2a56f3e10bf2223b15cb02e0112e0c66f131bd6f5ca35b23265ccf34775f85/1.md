**What would move it most**
1. **What changed, and why version 2 wins: one element now leads** (defect in v1). In version 1 the headline is about 26 px and the card titles are about 28 px. The headline is the smallest of the four headings on the page, so the order is upside down, and the page's best line ("ready for a cold garden on the day they arrive") goes unread. Version 2 makes the headline clearly the first thing seen and sets everything else back by size (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). It also fixes a muddled type scale: 26 next to 28 reads as a mistake (Bringhurst). Version 2 runs roughly 64 / 28 / 18 / 16 / 14 px, and each step does a clear job. Keep this change.
2. **The page has no next step** (defect, both versions). After the headline and the cards there's no button and no link that looks like one, and the bottom fifth of the screen is empty. The card titles and nav look like labels, with no underline, arrow, or button shape (Material Design and Apple HIG on one high-emphasis action; NN/g on flat elements drawing less attention). A shopper who believes the claim has to guess where to click. The fix is one main action under the lede. Make it specific and in the gardener's terms, for example "Shop plants for cold gardens" or "Browse 300 perennials", not "Shop now". Give it the one accent fill on the page, and give the cards a visible cue such as "See perennials →".
3. **Flat colour blocks where the photos belong** (defect, unless these are images that didn't load). The headline promises plants grown on a real hillside, and the picture slots show three swatches of sage. A stranger sees no plants, no hillside, and no proof. That's common sense, not a cited rule. If these are placeholders, real photos of this nursery's stock and slope would do more for trust than any type change. If they're images that failed to load, check the live page.

**Smaller things.**
- **"pots." alone on a line** in the Perennials card (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Trim the copy or widen the text box so the line ends in more than one word.
- **"hillside" alone on line 2** of the version 2 headline. Break after "grown" so the lines read "Hardy plants grown / on our hillside", or let the headline run wider so it fits on one line.

**What's working.**
- **The copy.** "Raised outdoors in Fernhill, so they are ready for a cold garden on the day they arrive" says exactly what's different here and why it matters to the buyer. Most nursery pages don't manage that.
- **The structure.** One clean three-column grid with equal gutters, left-aligned throughout. The quiet green-on-cream palette fits a nursery without decorating it.
- **The large headline from version 2.** It's the reason version 2 wins, so don't shrink it back to fix the line break.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards and nav links have hover and focus states. Whether the nav links are big enough to tap or click (they look about 18 px tall; measure them). How the three columns reflow on a phone.
