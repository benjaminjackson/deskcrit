**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", drops to the orange "Start your free week" button, and then stops on a grey card that says "Keep your streak going". That card throws you: the page has been talking to a newcomer, and suddenly it seems to be talking to someone already halfway through the course. The chat-bubble panel on the right gets a glance and adds nothing. It reads as a calm, tidy, competent language-app landing page that looks a lot like many others.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the top of the landing page for a speaking-first language app, meant to get a first-time visitor to start the free trial.

**Verdict.** The core works: one clear promise, one strong button, and a plain three-step explanation of how it works. Three things get in the way. A retention message sits where the reason to start should be. The panel on the right takes up half the hero and shows nothing about the product. And the trial button doesn't say what the visitor is agreeing to.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "Keep your streak going" card is talking to the wrong person, and sits in the wrong place.** *Defect.* The card is about 28px below the button and about 50px above the steps, so it reads as part of the offer, right where a visitor decides whether to click. But "keep your streak going" assumes a streak the visitor doesn't have yet. The first thing they read after the button is advice for a habit they haven't started, and it reads like it was meant for someone else. It also has no job on this screen, so it slows the path from promise to click. Proximity is doing the damage here (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on grouping by nearness). Fix: take it out of the hero. If the statistic matters, rewrite it as a reason to start, such as "people who practise five days in a row are twice as likely to finish", and put it further down the page, apart from the button.

2. **The right half of the hero shows no product.** *Taste, argued.* The pale teal panel with two empty chat bubbles is the second-largest thing on the screen. It just repeats "conversation" from the headline as a picture, so it adds nothing. The page's real difference is "a tutor that listens" and "feedback on each word", and you never see either one. A visitor can't tell this from any other chat or language app. Fix: put the product in that space. A real screen showing a spoken phrase with feedback on each word would do the most, because the visitor sees the promise instead of taking it on trust.

3. **The trial button and the tutor line can each be read two ways.** *Defect, because these are the words the visitor acts on.* "Start your free week" could mean a week free and then nothing, or a week free and then an automatic paid plan. Someone who assumes the first and gets billed will feel tricked. Fix: add one line under the button that says what happens after day 7 and whether a card is needed. "Practice with a tutor that listens" could mean a human tutor or software. Price and expectations depend on which, so say which it is. Also, the ask only invites the visitor to try the product. Letting them pick their language right at the button (Spanish, French, Italian, German) would make the first click concrete.

4. **The illustration panel misses the grid.** *Convention.* The nav and the steps row both end at x≈1224, but the panel stops at x≈1076. That leaves a 150px strip of dead space on the right. The gap is too big to be a rounding error and too small to look deliberate, so the hero looks unfinished (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*; Arnheim on placements that are neither clearly here nor there). Fix: run the panel out to the 1224 edge, or bring it clearly inside to a column line, and line its top and bottom up with the headline block.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "conversation in / ten weeks", leaving "in" at the end of the first line. Break it as "Hold a real conversation / in ten weeks".
- The grey card's title (about 18px bold) and the step titles (about 17px bold) are nearly the same size and don't read as different levels. Pick one size.
- "finish the course twice as often" has no point of comparison: twice as often as whom? Name the comparison group or cut the claim.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** It is a specific, checkable promise (a result plus a time frame) written for the visitor. Keep the specificity.
- **One primary action.** The saturated orange button is the only warm colour on the page and the only filled control, so there is no question about what to do next.
- **The three steps.** "Listen / Speak / Use it" use short verbs, numbers, thin rules, and equal columns that run edge to edge on the grid. This is the clearest explanation of the product on the page, and it is plainer than the panel above it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Button hover and focus states, and how the two-column hero stacks on a phone.
