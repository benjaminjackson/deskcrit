# Crit: The Ashby Courier homepage, version 1 vs version 2

Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the layout: the footbridge story is made the lead and the other four sit in a 2×2 grid beside it. That one change fixes nearly everything wrong with version 1.

**First read.**
- **Version 1:** the eye goes to the masthead, then runs along a row of five coloured blocks with nowhere to stop. It reads like a list of equal items, not a front page, and the empty bottom half makes it feel unfinished.
- **Version 2:** the eye lands on the large blue block and the big "New footbridge over the Soar…" headline, then moves right through four smaller stories. It reads straight away as a local paper with a top story.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage of a small-town news site, where readers should see at a glance what the main story is today and then scan the rest.

**Verdict.** Version 1 fails at the main thing a front page does, which is ranking the news. Five equal cards make the reader do the editor's job, and the bottom half of the screen is wasted. Version 2 makes that call clearly and works. What's left is spacing: the right-hand column is stretched to match the lead's height, so loose gaps open up.

**What changed, and why version 2 wins**
1. **One story now leads (defect fixed).**
   - In version 1, five headlines of the same size, weight and image size compete equally. A reader can't tell whether the footbridge or the library hours matter more, so they scan all five.
   - In version 2, the lead image is about four times the area of the others and its headline is roughly twice the size. You see one thing first, then four, which gives the page clear levels (Müller-Brockmann; Refactoring UI on hierarchy).
2. **The screen is used.** Version 1 crams everything into a 300 px band and leaves about 400 px of white below. That space isn't composed; it's just left over, and it makes the page look like it's still loading. Version 2 fills the screen with a clear mix of large and small.
3. **Wider columns fixed the text.** Version 1's columns are about 210 px wide. That leaves the deks ending on lone words ("March.", "Farm.", "November.", "out.") and splits "shoot-/out." across a line. At version 2's widths, every dek fits on one line and every headline breaks cleanly into two.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The right-hand column has holes (defect, mild).**
   - **What's wrong:** the gap between the top pair of small stories and the bottom pair is about 90 px. The gap between columns is about 24 px. That makes the top-right pair look orphaned rather than part of one block of four. It happens because the two rows are spread out to match the lead's height.
   - **Fix:** use one spacing between all cards, across and down. Then set the lead image height so the lead block ends level with the bottom-right card, or let the small images grow taller to take up the space.
   - **Source:** Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one fixed interval between grid fields.
2. **The bottom edges don't line up (convention).**
   - The lead image ends at about y 534 and the bottom-row images at about 578. The lead's dek ends at about 643 and the right column's at about 653. Nothing lines up at the bottom, so the edge looks ragged instead of locked in.
   - **Fix:** pick one bottom line for the lead block and the bottom row, and make both the images and the last text lines end on it.

**Smaller things**
- The masthead (about 32 px) and the lead headline (about 36 px) are nearly the same size and the same bold serif, so they compete at the top left. Make the lead clearly bigger, or set the masthead smaller or in a different style, so the gap between them is obvious.
- The nav links look about 16 px tall. Check that each one gives at least a 24×24 px click area (WCAG 2.5.8).

**What's working (version 2)**
- **The lead-plus-four structure.** Keep it. It's the change that makes this a front page.
- **The two typefaces split the jobs cleanly.** Bold serif headlines and small grey sans deks give each story two clear levels without needing another size.
- **The copy is specific and local** ("Forty plots added at Glebe Farm", "Open from ten until four from November"). Each dek tells you something new rather than repeating the headline.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- The images are flat colour blocks. If real photos are meant to be there, check in a browser that they load, because whether the lead works also depends on the lead photo.
- Hover and focus states on the nav and the headlines.
- How the 2×2 grid reflows on narrow screens.
