**First read.** The eye lands on the six colour blocks, not on anything in particular. It sweeps across the top row and then the bottom row, and the red Rose arch tile and yellow Glasshouse tile pull slightly harder only because they are warm. "Garden show gallery" in the top left reads as a small label rather than a page title. It reads as a tidy but anonymous gallery template with placeholder art. It feels orderly and flat, and nothing says "come and look at this garden".

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page where visitors to a garden show browse the exhibits and choose which ones to look at.

**Verdict.** The grid is clean, but the page doesn't do its job yet. It has no focal point and no real pictures, so there is nothing to make a visitor want any one exhibit. The footnote at the bottom says "the large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point", and no such tile exists.

**What would move it most**
1. **Nothing leads, and the page claims something that isn't there** (defect). Six tiles of the same size, weight and saturation compete equally, so the eye scans the grid and lands nowhere. A visitor gets no signal about which exhibit is the highlight or where to start. The line under the grid ("The large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point") describes a design that isn't on the page. It looks like a leftover from an earlier version or a missing element, and either way it tells the visitor something untrue. Fix: make one exhibit clearly dominant, for example by having the feature tile span two columns and both rows, and set the rest back by size (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*, on much against little). If no feature is planned, delete the line.
2. **The pictures are placeholders, so the gallery shows no garden** (defect, if this is what visitors will see). Each tile is a flat saturated rectangle with a pale centred circle. On a gallery, the pictures are the content. Without them, the names ("Herb bed", "Pond edge") have nothing to back them up, and nothing makes one exhibit more tempting than another. Six unrelated primary hues at equal strength also read as a UI kit, not a garden in late July. This is fine only if these are image slots that will hold real photographs. Fix: show real photographs that share one treatment (light, crop and subject distance). If colour blocks have to stay for now, use muted tones taken from the planting rather than six full-strength hues.
3. **The page title is weaker than the tile titles** (convention). "Garden show gallery" looks about 20 px bold, and "Rose arch", "Herb bed" and the other tile titles look about 22 px bold. That is the same weight with a slightly larger size, so the hierarchy is inverted and the gap is too small to read as a choice. I count three sizes: about 20/22 for the two headings that collide, and about 15 for the descriptions and footnote. Fix: make the page title clearly the top level, a big step larger than the tile titles, so the scale reads as three levels: page title, tile title, description (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8; Bringhurst, 3.1.1).

**Smaller things.**
- The Rose arch caption block (title and description) sits 2 to 4 px higher, closer to its image, than the other five, so row 1's text doesn't share a baseline. It reads as a slip. Use the same caption spacing on every tile (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*).

**What's working.**
- The three-column grid is strict. The margins, the 14 px gutters and the caption-to-image spacing repeat across both rows, so the page has a solid frame to build the feature tile into.
- The two-level captions work: a bold name, then a grey one-line description. The descriptions are concrete and specific to this show ("Climbing roses over the east gate", "Tomatoes in late July"). Keep that voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are links, and if so, whether they show a visible focus state.
- How the three-column grid reflows at narrow widths.
