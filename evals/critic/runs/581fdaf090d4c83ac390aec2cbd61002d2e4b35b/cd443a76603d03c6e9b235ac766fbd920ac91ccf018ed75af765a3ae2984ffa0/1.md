**First read.** The eye goes to the big serif title "Leek and potato soup with brown butter", then down to two columns: Ingredients on the left, a numbered Method on the right. It reads as a calm, editorial recipe page from a small food blog. Version 1 feels warm and papery. Version 2 is the same page on white, and it feels cleaner but colder and more generic.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a single recipe page for a home cook, who scans the ingredients and then follows the steps at the stove.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job slightly better. Both versions have the same layout, type and copy. The only change is the background: cream in version 1, white in version 2. On white, the thin rules under the ingredients turn out to be in two different colours, so the list looks unfinished. On both versions, the biggest problem is the "Serves 4 · 45 minutes" line, which floats in empty space between the title and the content.

**What would move it most**
1. **The serving and time line belongs to nothing (defect).** "Serves 4 · 45 minutes" sits roughly 100 px below the title and roughly 100 px above the columns. With equal gaps on both sides, it doesn't attach to the title or the recipe. It looks like a stray caption, and the page has two dead bands of space near the top. That line is one a cook acts on: it tells them whether to make this tonight and whether to double it. Move it up under the title, so the title and its facts form one block. Then put the space you gain above the columns, where it marks a real break between sections (Ruder: gather white space rather than scattering it).
2. **Version 2's ingredient rules don't match (defect, version 2 only).** On white, the dividers under "3 large leeks", "1 litre vegetable stock" and "A small bunch of chives" are a warm yellowish cream. The others are neutral grey. The rules look as if they were picked for the cream ground. Once the ground changed, the difference showed, and it reads as a mistake (Albers: one colour looks different on a different ground). On cream, the difference is nearly invisible. If you keep white, use one neutral colour for every rule. If you keep cream, unify them anyway, because the mismatch is still in the code.

**Smaller things.**
- Method step 4 ends with "chives." alone on its last line. Rewrap it or tighten the wording so the last line has more than one word.

**What's working.**
- The contrast between the heavy serif title and the sans-serif labels and ingredients gives a clear order with very few sizes, roughly four. Keep that restraint.
- The two-column split mirrors how people cook. The ingredients are short, ruled lines that are easy to check off. The method is set in a comfortable serif at a good line length (about 70 characters), with generous space between numbered steps.
- The cream ground in version 1 is a taste call, but it fits a food site called "Second Helpings": warm and homely, without lowering contrast. On white, the same page could be anyone's template.

- Whether the nav shows which section you're in (Recipes).
- How the two columns reflow on a phone, which matters for a page people read in the kitchen.
