**First read.** The eye goes to the masthead, "The Wexley Gazette", then drops onto three headlines that all look the same, and settles on none of them. Within a second it reads as a quiet, well-set local news page. Everything happens in the top half. The bottom 45% of the frame is blank white, so the page looks unfinished more than calm.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the front page of a small-town news site or newsletter, where readers should see at a glance what the main story is and then scan the rest.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is clean and the copy is good, but it doesn't work yet as a front page. It has no lead story, and the three equal columns sit on top of a large empty area, so it reads as a list of notices rather than an edited page.

**What would move it most**
1. **No story leads (defect).** "Bridge to reopen in spring", "Market moves indoors" and "School wins garden prize" share the same size, weight, column width and length. The masthead is only about a third larger than they are. Nothing tells the reader which story matters most, so they start top-left by habit and the page does none of the editor's job. A bridge closed for eighteen months is plainly bigger news than a school prize, and the layout hides that. Make one element clearly the first thing seen and set the rest back by size, so the order of importance is plain (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). In practice, give the lead story a much larger headline and more width, for example two columns, and run the other two smaller beside or below it. Hofmann's point in *Graphic Design Manual* applies here too: one very large element set against small ones is what gives a page tension. Right now nothing on it is large.
2. **The bottom half is leftover space, not designed space (defect, unless the page goes on below).** Everything ends by y≈415 and the rest of the frame is empty. All the weight sits at the top, so the page looks top-heavy and half-loaded instead of open. Ruder's point (*Typography: A Manual of Design*) is that empty space has to be set against dense areas on purpose. This space is just what remained. Fill it with the content this page is missing: a lead image, more stories, briefs or an events list. If this is really a short page, make the white space a choice, for example by giving the lead story room. If the screenshot just stops where more content follows below, this finding doesn't apply. Check that in a live browser.
3. **The masthead has no date or issue (defect, copy).** A newspaper with no date leaves the reader unable to tell whether "next month", "in April" and "from March" are still in the future. Every story depends on relative dates, so this one missing line changes what the reader takes away. Add a dateline under the masthead with the date, issue and place, set small, between the title and the double rule.

**What's working.**
- The text is set well. It's a comfortable reading serif in near-black on white, the columns are about 40 characters wide (right for news), the leading is generous, the rag is gentle, the baselines line up across columns, and each headline sits tight to its own story. Keep all of this when you rework the hierarchy.
- The grid is exact: three equal columns, equal gutters, and the 100/1180 margins line up with the double rule. That gives you a solid base for a lead story spanning two columns.
- The headlines are plain, specific and active ("School wins garden prize"), and the opening sentences answer what, where and when right away. The copy is not generic.
