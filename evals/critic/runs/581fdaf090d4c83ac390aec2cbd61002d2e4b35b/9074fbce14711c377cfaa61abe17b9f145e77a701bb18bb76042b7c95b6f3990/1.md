**What would move it most**
1. **The headline falls apart into three lines.** The space between the headline's lines looks about the same, relative to its size, as in the body text, around 1.4–1.5 times the type size. At this size that leaves a wide white band between each line, so the eye reads "The farmers who are letting their", stops, and has to cross a gap to find the rest. The line breaks make it worse. The headline has two parts, the farmers and the birds, but the last line break comes after "and the", which strands the start of the second part at the end of line two. The payoff, "birds that came back", loses its lead-in. Fixing it takes two steps:
   - Close the line spacing until the lines read as one block, with descenders and ascenders still clear of each other. Bringhurst allows tight or even negative spacing for titles as long as letters don't collide.
   - Break at the phrase: "…flood on purpose, / and the birds that came back". You can do this by narrowing the headline's measure, which at the moment runs past the body column to about x 960, or with a manual break.

   This is **taste / common sense**, not a cited rule. The approved sources only back the "don't let lines collide" half, and they don't cover phrase breaks in headings. I'm arguing it because the headline is what makes someone start the story, and right now it reads in three halting steps.
