**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", drops into the first paragraph, and then catches on the blue links. It reads straight away as a help-centre article for whoever pays for a SaaS account. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy: good help-desk restraint, with no decoration in the way.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an account owner who wants to change the payment card lands here and should be able to find how to do it, and what happens to open invoices and to a card that gets declined, in seconds.

**Verdict.** The layout and type do their job well. The page fails at the one thing its headline promises: it never says how to update the card, and its most important date is worded so it can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The how-to is missing (defect).** The headline promises a task, but the first paragraph only says "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page". It gives no path, no link to that billing page and no steps. The links that are there go everywhere else: billing history, roles, paying by invoice, restoring a plan. A reader who came to do the job finishes the article still having to hunt for the screen, and many will give up and use "Contact support" in the header. The fix:
   - Put the billing page link in that first sentence.
   - Add a short numbered list right under the headline, three or four steps, saying where to click and what to press. For example: "Go to Settings → Billing, choose Payment method, and select Replace card."
   - Move the invoice-timing explanation below the steps.

   Krug's test is that a page should make plain within seconds what you can do. This one tells you what will happen but not what to do.
2. **"After the second attempt" can be read two ways, and it sits on a deadline (defect).** In "When the new card is declined", the page says "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days". Then: "If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan."
   - Reading 1: the second attempt is the day-3 retry, because the original charge was the first attempt. Then the downgrade comes on day 3.
   - Reading 2: the second attempt is the second retry, on day 7.
   - It's also unclear whether "seven days" counts from the first decline or from the day-3 retry.

   This is the line a reader will plan around and hold you to. A reader who expects day 7 and loses paid features on day 3 has a real complaint. Fix it with plain dates in the reader's terms: "If your card is declined, we retry it 3 days later and again 7 days after the first decline. If the day-7 retry fails, your workspace moves to the free plan."
3. **The sidebar doesn't show which page you're on (convention).** Under "In this section", the item "Update a payment card" is drawn exactly like the four around it. A reader who arrived from search can't see where this page sits among its siblings or jump to the next one with confidence. Mark the current item with one clear cue, such as a bolder weight, a left bar or a tint (Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", NN/g; Nielsen heuristic 1). The breadcrumb partly covers this, so the cost is moderate, but the fix is trivial.

**Smaller things.**
- **Inline links are shown by colour only (WCAG 1.4.1).** The blue links ("check your billing history", "roles and permissions", "paying by invoice", "restore your previous plan") have no underline. Against the near-black body text the blue looks to have well under 3:1 contrast, which fails G183. Measure it, and underline the links in body text, which also helps colour-blind readers.
- **The body text is slightly wide.** Lines in the main column look about 80–85 characters long, past the 45–75 range Bringhurst gives. The generous leading softens it. Narrowing the column by about 60–80 px, or setting the type a touch larger, would bring it into range.
- **The headline overstates the article.** "Update the card that pays for your subscription" is clear, but once the steps are added the body should deliver it. Right now the headline sells a task and the body delivers a policy.

**What's working.**
- **One clear type scale.** I count four levels: a 32 px headline, 18 px bold for the subheading, 18 px body, and about 15 and 13 px for the nav and breadcrumb. Levels are separated by weight as well as size, so nothing collides. Keep it.
- **One grid, used consistently.** The sidebar column, the content column on its own left edge, and the breadcrumb–headline–body stack all line up, and the space below headings is tighter than the space above them. The page needs no rules, boxes or cards to hold together.
- **Copy that speaks to the reader.** Most lines say "you" and end on what the fact means for the reader, such as "any invoice that is already open keeps the old card until it is paid" and "your workspace stays open during this period". That tone is right for billing anxiety. Keep it when you add the steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the links get an underline on hover and focus. Check this in a live browser.
- Focus visibility on the sidebar and header nav.
