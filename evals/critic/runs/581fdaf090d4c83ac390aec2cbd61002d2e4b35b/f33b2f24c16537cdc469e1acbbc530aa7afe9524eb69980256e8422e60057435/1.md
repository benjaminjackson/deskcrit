**First read.** The eye lands on "Garden show gallery", drops into the big red Rose arch tile, then hops to the yellow Glasshouse tile and around the coloured blocks. Within a second it reads as a tidy photo gallery with a featured item and five smaller ones. The feel is calm and orderly, but empty: there are no pictures of a garden yet.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a web page where visitors to a garden show browse the exhibits and pick one to look at.

**Verdict.** The layout works. The order of importance is clear, the grid is sound and the captions are good. But a gallery is judged by its pictures, and there are none here: every tile is a flat colour block with a pale circle. The second thing in the way is a headline that doesn't say whose show this is.

**What would move it most**
1. **The gallery has no pictures, so its main job can't be judged** (defect, unless these are placeholders on purpose). All six tiles are flat colour fields with a cut-off pale circle. A visitor gets labels ("Pond edge", "Glasshouse") but nothing to look at. The colours also do a job real photos would do differently: the saturated yellow Glasshouse tile pulls almost as hard as the red feature, so the order you see now will change once photos go in. This is fine only if these are placeholders or a capture taken before the images loaded. Put the real photos in, then check two things again: that the Rose arch photo still clearly wins over the five small ones, and that no small photo (a bright yellow glasshouse, say) outshines it. If one does, tone the small photos down to the same grade rather than make the feature bigger.
2. **The headline could be anyone's** (taste, but it costs something). "Garden show gallery" doesn't say which show, where or when. A visitor who arrives from a search or a shared link can't tell whether this is the show they went to, or this year's. The captions underneath are specific ("Tomatoes in late July", "Climbing roses over the east gate"), so the vagueness stands out more. Name the show and the year in the headline or right under it, e.g. "<Show name> 2026: the gardens", so the page is about one event.

**Smaller things.**
- The feature image ends at about y 437 while the Pond edge image beside it ends at about y 474. The caption baselines line up, but the two picture edges sit about 37 px apart and look slightly off rather than chosen (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: pictures take their top and bottom from the lines of text). Either end both images on one line or make the difference clearly larger.

**What's working.**
- **The grid.** Three equal columns with one gutter (about 14 px), the feature spanning two, and the same column lines from top to bottom. It looks planned, and it will hold up when you add more exhibits.
- **Contrast of scale.** The roughly 64 px title, then the much larger Rose arch tile with its 40 px name, against five small tiles gives the page a clear first, second and rest. Weight and grey, not another size, separate the small bold labels from their captions. Keep that.
- **The captions.** Short, concrete, in the visitor's own words, and each sits clearly nearer its own picture than the next one.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles link anywhere. Nothing marks them as clickable. If they open a detail view, check that the hover and focus states make that obvious.
- Whether the flat colour tiles are loading placeholders. Check in a live browser that real images load, and that the layout doesn't jump when they do.
- The page has no header or navigation. This may be a cropped screenshot, but if the page really has none, add a way back to the show's main site.
- How the two-column feature reflows on a narrow screen.
