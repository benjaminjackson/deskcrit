**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, drops to the subline and the "Create your link" button, then has nowhere to go. The right half of the hero is empty. Below it sit four blue-outlined boxes of equal weight, and a row of grey names nobody explains. It reads as a scheduling SaaS homepage for small service businesses. It feels clean and competent, but it also feels like a template, and everything on it is the same blue.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing homepage. Its job is to get owners of small appointment-based businesses (dentists, tutors, physios, vets) to create a booking link.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and readable, but it doesn't sell. It never shows the thing it's selling. The one colour is spent on everything, so the action doesn't stand out. And the copy could sit on any competitor's page.

**What would move it most**

1. **The product is never shown, and the empty right half proves it.** The hero is a headline, a sentence and a button, with roughly 550 px of blank space to the right. It's leftover space, not shaped space. The page is about a link that guests use to book, yet a viewer never sees what their client would see: a booking page, open slots, a confirmation. So they have to take "fill your calendar" on trust and imagine the product themselves. You do offer "See a live example", which is the strongest idea on the page because it lets the viewer judge for themselves. But it's a small link and easy to miss. Fix: put the live example into the hero's right side as a real booking page, ideally in a recognisable client's name (Harbor Dental, say), so the visual and the headline make one argument. That also balances the layout. *Defect.*

2. **One blue does every job, so the button doesn't stand out.** The headline, the nav links, all four card titles, the icons, the badges, the 2 px card borders, the divider rule and both buttons are the same saturated blue. Blue stops meaning "click here". The blue card titles look like links, the filled blue pills look like small buttons, and "Create your link" competes with about thirty other blue things instead of being the one strong mark on the screen. Apple's HIG and Material Design both say to keep the accent for the main action and interactive states. Fix: keep the blue for the main button and real links. Make the headline and card titles near-black, make the card borders a light grey (or a pale tint, or no border with more space between cards), and make the badges and icons neutral or a pale tint. *Convention, and here it costs the viewer something.*

3. **The headline is generic.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" is a claim every scheduling tool makes, and a booking link doesn't fill a calendar anyway; demand does. A viewer can't tell what's different here, so nothing gives them a reason to switch. The subline is better because it names concrete actions. The customer names suggest your edge is small service businesses: the team routing, reminders that cut no-shows, intake questions. Fix: say that in the viewer's terms ("Your clients book themselves, get reminded, and show up", or whatever the true difference is). *Taste, but it decides whether anyone acts.*

4. **The feature cards break their own pattern.** In card 1, the "New" badge is pushed to the right edge and forces "Shared / calendars" onto two lines. That puts its text and "Learn more" about 30 px lower than in the other three cards, so the row looks broken by accident. The badges also mix two kinds of label: "New" and "Beta" are statuses, while "Auto" and "Email" are descriptions that a stranger can't decode ("Auto" what?). Fix: give every card the same structure. Put badges in one fixed spot, use them only for status, and drop "Auto" and "Email" or turn them into words in the body text. *Defect (small, but it's the first thing a careful viewer notices).*

**Smaller things.**
- Customer strip at the bottom: five grey names with no label and no logos read as placeholder text, not social proof. Add a label ("Used by 2,000 clinics and tutors", or whatever is true) plus real logos or a quote.
- Four identical "Learn more" links: when read out of context (screen readers, link lists) they're indistinguishable. Name each one ("More on reminders").
- Icons: the calendar is an outline while the clock, mail and layout icons are solid. Pick one style. The "Intake forms" icon is a page-layout glyph, not a form; use a form or clipboard glyph.
- Header "Start free" and hero "Create your link" are probably the same action under two names. Use one label so the viewer knows it's one step.

**What's working.**
- The subline's verbs ("share one link, let people pick a time… keep every meeting in the right place") make the product's whole loop clear in one sentence. Keep that plain register.
- The card body copy is concrete and lands on the viewer: "so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows", "so the first minutes are useful". Each one ends on a benefit, not a feature, which is exactly right.
- Type is calm and well built: one family, a clear scale (headline, card title, body, small labels), comfortable measure and leading, and a strict left edge throughout.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the links and buttons. That matters here because blue currently can't tell links apart from decoration.
