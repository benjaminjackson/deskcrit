**First read.** My eye lands on the big blue headline, then goes to the "Create your link" button, then across the row of four blue-outlined cards. The cards pull hard because their borders, titles, icons and badges are all the same blue as the button. It reads as a scheduling or booking-link SaaS homepage: tidy and competent, but it could belong to any of a dozen products in the category. The right side of the hero is empty, so the page feels lopsided and unfinished, as if a picture is missing.

**What it's for.** You didn't give me a brief, so I'm assuming this is the homepage hero for a booking-link product. Judging by the customer names, it's aimed at small appointment businesses (dentists, tutors, physios, vets), and its job is to get them to create a link or look at an example.

**Verdict.** It's clean and gets the category across in a few seconds, but it doesn't make anyone want to choose it. Two things hold it back. Brand blue is spread over almost everything, so the main action doesn't stand out and non-clickable things look clickable. And the page never shows the product, so the visitor has to take the claim on trust.

**What would move it most**

1. **The accent blue is on everything, so it marks nothing.** The headline, the card titles, the 2px card borders, the icons, the filled badges, the nav links, both buttons and the "Learn more" links all use the same saturated blue. The visitor can't tell by colour what is clickable. The filled "New / Auto / Email / Beta" pills look like small buttons, and the blue card titles look like links. The four heavy outlined boxes fight the headline and the CTA for attention, and the page turns into one blue note. This is a convention: Apple HIG and Material reserve the accent colour for the main action and interactive states. It's also a defect, because it hides what's clickable (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on affordances). Fix: set the headline and card titles in a near-black neutral. Replace the card borders with a light grey hairline, a pale tint, or just space. Make the badges quiet grey tags. Keep full-strength blue for the buttons and links only, so "Create your link" is the one saturated object in the hero.

2. **The product never appears, and the right ~45% of the hero is empty.** Visitors want to know what their customers will see when they open the link, and nothing on the page answers that. The empty space doesn't read as a choice; it reads as an image that didn't load. That leaves the left side heavy and the right side dead (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on balance). This is a defect for the job, and if a picture was meant to load there, it's a capture problem instead. Fix: put a real booking page in that space, the same one "See a live example" opens, preferably one for a dentist or tutor. That would balance the hero and show what the product does without asking the visitor to take it on trust.

3. **The words could be on any competitor's page, and the best proof is unlabeled.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" names the category and makes a generic promise. Nothing in it tells a physio clinic why this tool rather than the one they already know. The strongest evidence is the row of customer names along the bottom (Harbor Dental, Kestrel Physio, Orchard Vets). Those names show exactly who this is for, but they're small grey text with no label, so they read as footer links (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should explain itself). The badges also make the visitor guess: "Auto" and "Email" say nothing, and they share one style with "New" and "Beta", which are statuses (Nielsen heuristic 4, consistency: one style should mean one thing). This is a defect. Fix: write the headline for the audience the customer list implies, for example letting clients or patients book themselves without phone tag. Label the customer strip ("Used by clinics, tutors and vets") and move it up near the CTA. Drop the badges or keep only the status ones. "See a live example" is the right kind of ask, because it lets visitors judge for themselves. Make it more specific ("See a dental clinic's booking page") so they recognise their own case.

**Smaller things.**
- "Shared calendars", card 1: the badge is pushed to the far right and the title wraps to two lines. That puts its body text and "Learn more" about 30px below the other three cards. Put the badge inline, or below the title as in a consistent card pattern, so all four cards line up.
- The four identical outlined cards with no accent are monotonous. Once the borders go quiet, consider which feature leads, or give the row one point of difference.
- The Intake forms icon is a layout/dashboard glyph, not a form. Swap it for one that reads as a form.

**What's working.**
- The headline really dominates. It's at least twice the size of anything else, bold, and alone at the top left, so the eye knows where to start. Keep that scale contrast when the colour changes.
- The CTA pair is set up correctly: one filled primary ("Create your link") and an underlined text link for the secondary. "See a live example" is the best line on the page.
- The card body copy is concrete and talks about the visitor's outcome ("so nobody has to do the math", "cut down on no-shows"). Write the headline in that voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right side of the hero is the design or an image that didn't load: check in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the cards and links: once the blue is taken off the non-clickable elements, check that focus rings still show at 3:1.
