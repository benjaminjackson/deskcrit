I've finished the critique below. The page is clear and orderly, but almost everything is the same blue, so the main button doesn't stand out. The other big gaps are the missing product visual and the generic copy.

---

**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, drops to the subhead and the "Create your link" button, then slides straight into four blue-outlined cards that are about as loud as the button. It reads as a scheduling-tool homepage, like a Calendly alternative, for small service businesses. It feels tidy, calm and competent, but it also looks like a template: there's no picture, no product and nothing I'd remember a minute later. The right half of the opening section (the hero) is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage of a booking-link tool, meant to get owners of small appointment-based businesses (dentists, tutors, physios, vets) to create a link.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound and nothing is broken, but it doesn't sell. One blue is doing every job on the page, so the button has no special pull. And the page never shows what a booking link looks like, which is the one thing that would make a visitor want one.

**What would move it most**
1. **The accent blue is on everything, so the main button doesn't stand out.** The same saturated blue is used for the headline, all four nav links, the card titles, icons, borders and badges, the four "Learn more" links, the line above the customer names, and both buttons. "Create your link" has no colour of its own, so it's only one blue shape among about 25. The eye reads the headline and then drifts to the cards instead of the button. The accent should mark the main action and almost nothing else (Apple HIG, Color; Material Design 3, colour roles). Fix: set the headline in near-black, set the card titles, icons and borders in neutrals, and keep the full blue for the two "Create your link" / "Start free" buttons and maybe the links. The button will then stand out without being made any bigger. *Convention, and it causes a real defect: the main action is weaker than it should be.*
2. **There's no product visual, and the hero is half empty.** The right half of the hero (roughly x 700–1216, y 100–380) is blank. It isn't a deliberate open area set against something dense; it looks like a gap where something is missing. It also leaves all the weight in the left column (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I). For a booking tool, the strongest argument is the thing the visitor's own customers would see: a booking page with a week of open time slots. "See a live example" is the right instinct, but it's hidden as a small underlined link. Fix: put a real screenshot of a booking page in that space, ideally one for a business like those in the customer strip, so the headline's promise is shown. That screenshot also becomes the counterweight the layout needs. If an image is meant to be there and didn't load, this finding becomes a check on the live page (see below). *Defect.*
3. **The copy could be on any scheduling tool's homepage.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "keep every meeting in the right place" are true of Calendly, Cal.com and a dozen others. A visitor can't tell what's different here, so nothing pushes them to act. "Fill your calendar" can also be read two ways: as "get more bookings" (good news for a vet) or as "you'll be busy" (not obviously good news). The customer strip at the bottom shows who this is really for: clinics, tutors and coaches, with clients who book appointments rather than colleagues who book meetings. Fix: write the headline for that visitor and that result, with a verb that says who does what, for example the clients booking themselves so the owner stops handling calls and texts. Make "See a live example" show a booking page for a business like theirs, so they can compare it with how bookings work for them now. *Taste, argued: this is what decides whether the page persuades anyone.*
4. **The card row looks heavy and isn't consistent.** Each card has a 2px saturated blue border, and a blue rule runs under the row. Together they turn the middle of the page into four boxes that are louder than the hero. Space or a pale tint would group the cards just as well (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*). Inside the row, the "New" badge forces "Shared / calendars" onto two lines, so card 1's text and link sit about 30px lower than in the other three. The baselines don't line up across the row, and cards 2–4 end with empty space at the bottom. The badges also mix two kinds of label: "New" and "Beta" are status, while "Auto" and "Email" describe the feature and tell the reader nothing the title doesn't. Fix: drop or soften the borders. Keep badges only for status ("New", "Beta"), and keep them short enough, or place them so, that every title fits on one line. That way all four cards follow one pattern (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- Customer names (y ≈ 770): plain grey text with no label, sitting under a rule, so they read like footer links rather than proof. Add a short label such as "Used by", or use their logos.
- Icons: the calendar is an outline, while the clock, envelope and form icons are filled. Use one style for all four.
- The header says "Start free" and the hero says "Create your link" for what looks like the same action. Use one label, or make clear that they differ.

**What's working.**
- The structure is clean: a 64px margin, one column edge shared from the logo down to the customer strip, and even gaps between the cards. Keep this grid when you add a visual.
- The hero order is right: headline, a one-sentence explanation, then one filled button with a quieter text link next to it. Once the colours are fixed, that pairing is the correct way to separate the main action from the secondary one.
- The card body copy is concrete and talks about the user's problem: "so nobody has to do the math" and "to cut down on no-shows" say what the feature does for the reader. The headline should be written that way too.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half of the hero is meant to hold an image that didn't load. Check the live page; if an image is there, re-judge finding 2 against it.
- Hover and focus states for the buttons and links, and how the four-card row reflows on narrow screens.
