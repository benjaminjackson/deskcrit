**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers in the KPI row, left to right, then drops to the blue Search line. It reads as an analytics overview for a marketing or growth team. It's calm, tidy and orderly, and it looks like a standard dashboard template.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal overview where a marketer checks, in a few seconds, whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy over the last 12 weeks and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The layout is competent. But the numbers contradict each other, and on a dashboard that is the one failure that matters: a reader who checks them will stop trusting the page. After that, the chart shows trends without saying what they mean or what it's measuring.

**What would move it most**

1. **The conversion card says the opposite of what its own numbers imply.** *Defect.*
   - Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups up only 2.4%, so the conversion rate must have fallen. By the cards' own numbers it was about 2.20% before and is 2.12% now, a change of roughly −0.08 pts.
   - The card instead says "+0.1 pts" and shows it in success green.
   - A reader who skims comes away thinking conversion improved. A reader who does the maths decides the dashboard is wrong and starts doubting every other figure on it.
   - Fix: recompute the delta from the same data as the other two cards. Colour each delta by whether it's good or bad, not by default. A falling conversion rate should read as a decline, with a minus sign and not in green.

2. **The chart doesn't add up to the Visits headline.** *Defect.*
   - By eye, the three lines in "Visits by channel" total about 138k visits over the 12 weeks. The Visits card says 184,220.
   - Either about a quarter of the traffic sits in channels that aren't shown (direct, referral), or the two use different data.
   - Because the title says "by channel", a reader assumes these are all the channels. The gap then looks like an error, or hides the channel that may be your second biggest.
   - What would make it fine: an "Other" or "Direct" series, or a subtitle such as "Top 3 channels — 75% of visits" (common sense; Tufte's graphical-integrity principle is the nearest rule).

3. **The chart names a topic but doesn't say what the reader should take from it, or what one point means.** *Convention and copy.*
   - "Visits by channel" leaves the reader to work out the story.
   - Nothing says each point is one week, and the y-axis "8k" has no label. A first-time viewer may read the values as daily.
   - The real story is easy to state: Search is up about 36% and drives most of the growth, Email is small but steady, and Social is noisy.
   - Fix: make the title or a one-line subtitle the takeaway, aimed at the reader. For example: "Search drove most of the growth — up from 5k to 6.8k visits a week." Add "Visits per week" to the axis or subtitle.
   - That one sentence also gives screen-reader users the point of the chart, which they currently don't get.

4. **The legend sits away from the lines.** *Convention (Tufte, Wilke).*
   - To match a line to its channel, the eye has to jump up to the legend and back to the line.
   - The three lines never cross and end at clear, separate heights, so this is an easy case for labels at the line ends.
   - Fix: put "Search", "Social" and "Email" at the right-hand end of each line, in the line's colour, and drop the legend.

**Smaller things**
- **"Last 12 weeks" (top right):** if it's a date-range picker, it gives no sign of it: no chevron, border or button shape. If it's only a label, it's fine as it is. If it's a control, add a chevron and a subtle border so people know they can change the range.
- **Type scale:** I count about seven sizes. The brand name (~18px) and chart title (~17px) are nearly the same, and the deltas, card labels, legend and axis labels sit at roughly 12, 13 and 14px. Steps that small read as accidents (Bringhurst). Fold them into about four levels and make finer distinctions with weight or grey instead (Refactoring UI).
- **Chart card:** the empty band below the x-axis labels (~45px) is wasted space. Trim it, or use it for a source or "updated" line.

**What's working**
- **Each KPI card is complete:** a label, a big number and a comparison to the previous period. A reader can judge each number without hunting for context. Keep this pattern, just fix the delta logic.
- **The page is quiet:** faint gridlines, no chart borders or fills, and one consistent card style on a single grid with even gutters. Almost all of the ink is data, which leaves the eye free for the numbers.
- **The order is right:** summary numbers first, then the breakdown underneath, all on one screen without scrolling.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its hover and focus states.
- Whether the chart has tooltips for exact weekly values.
- How the three-card row reflows on narrow screens.
