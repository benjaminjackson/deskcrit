Version 2 does its job worse than version 1. The only change is that version 2 centres everything below the header, and that makes the main selling text harder to read.

**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the serif headline "Throwing on the wheel for beginners", drops through two short paragraphs, and stops at the tinted card with $320 and a rust "Book a place" button. It reads as a calm, warm course page for a small pottery studio, aimed at adults with no experience. In version 1 the eye runs straight down one left edge. In version 2 each line of the paragraphs starts somewhere different, so reading them takes more effort, and the page feels more like an announcement than a description.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a booking page for an 8-week beginners' wheel course. It needs to tell a would-be student what they'll learn, what it costs and when, and get them to book.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job well: one clear headline, readable copy, and a booking card that holds every fact you need next to one button. Version 2 only changes the alignment, and that costs readability without adding anything. The biggest flaw shared by both is the line "Eight places", which can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2 centres paragraphs of four lines** (convention, and it causes a real defect). The opening paragraph runs four lines and the second runs two. Both are centred, so the start of each line moves and the eye has to hunt for the next one. That falls on the sentences that sell the course. The ragged left edge also leaves "at your own wheel." floating mid-page as a short last line. Tschichold rejects setting text on a central axis as rigid, and the approved fix is to set paragraphs of several lines flush left and keep centring for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, 2.1.3). Version 1 already does this.
2. **Version 2 drops the page's one shared edge** (convention). In version 1 the logo, kicker, headline, body, card and button all hang from the same left edge, about 88 px in, so the header and content read as one page. In version 2 the logo stays at the left while everything else sits on the centre axis, and nothing below the logo lines up with it. The page now has two layout systems, and the centring looks like a default rather than a choice: there's no formality or ceremony here that symmetry would serve (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). Fix: keep version 1's single left edge.
3. **"Eight places" can be read two ways** (defect, both versions). It's a number the reader will act on, inside the booking card. It could mean the class holds eight people or that eight places are left. If a reader takes it as places left and finds the course full, or takes it as class size and assumes there's no hurry, the page has misled them on the thing they act on. Fix: say which, for example "Eight students per class" or "3 of 8 places left". Similarly, "$320" doesn't say what it covers. Adding "for all 8 weeks, clay and firings included" settles that next to the button.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Throwing on the wheel for / beginners", which leaves "for" hanging at the end of the first line and a single word on the second. Break it at the phrase: "Throwing on the wheel / for beginners".

**What's working** (version 1).
- One left edge runs through the whole page, from logo to button, so the eye never has to find its place. Keep it.
- The booking card works well. Price, day and time, start date, size and the one filled button sit together on a pale tint, so everything you need to decide is in one place. The rust colour is saved almost entirely for the button, which is why it's the clear next step.
- The copy is specific and says "you": centre clay, open it, pull up walls, make mugs and bowls; clay, glazes and two firings included; you take home what you make. A beginner can picture each evening, and the cost has no hidden extras.
