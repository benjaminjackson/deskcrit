**First read.** My eye lands on a big slab of saturated indigo, two-thirds of the grid, and only then finds the warm blob in the weekday late morning. It reads as a support-team report on ticket arrival by hour and day, for whoever plans shifts. It feels clean and competent. But it's loud in the wrong places: the hours when nothing happens shout as much as the hours that matter.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal reports screen. It's there so a support lead can see when tickets pile up and staff for it.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but the chart's main job isn't done, and one number on the page contradicts it. The rainbow colour scale hides the peak's shape and gives empty night hours the most visual weight. The "Busiest hour" figure points at a different column from the one the chart shows as busiest.

**What would move it most**

1. **The rainbow scale puts the weight in the wrong place.** In the heatmap, 0–4 tickets is a fully saturated indigo that, in greyscale, comes out darker than the 25–29 orange. Yellow (20–24) is the lightest step. So lightness goes up, then down, with no relation to volume. The quiet nights and weekends form the heaviest mass on the page. The peak can't be read as "more" at a glance, and the viewer has to decode seven hues against the legend cell by cell. The middle steps (green, yellow, orange, red) also collapse for red-green colour-blind viewers, which is exactly where the staffing decision sits.
   - **Kind:** convention (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*: ordered values take one hue going light to dark; WCAG 1.4.1).
   - **Fix:** use a single-hue ramp from near-white for 0–4 to dark for 30+, keeping the same seven steps. The empty hours then drop back into the page and the late-morning block becomes the obvious shape. This also lets the legend work as a simple light-to-dark strip.

2. **"Busiest hour, weekdays: 10:00" disagrees with the chart.** The hour labels sit over the start of each cell, so the 10:00 column is all orange (25–29). The 11:00 column holds the only red (30+) cells on the page, Monday and Friday, and orange on the other weekdays. Anyone who checks the number against the chart will conclude 11:00 is busier, and then trust neither.
   - **Kind:** defect.
   - Averaging within the buckets could, in theory, make 10:00 the winner. If so, the page needs to show it: label the KPI with its count ("10:00–11:00, avg 28 tickets") or make the scale fine enough to show it. If the data says 11:00, the KPI is wrong. Also make it unambiguous whether "10:00" means the hour starting or ending at 10.

3. **The title names a topic, not what the viewer should take from it.** "When new tickets arrive" makes the reader find the answer themselves. That's the answer the page exists to give, something like "Tickets peak 10:00–12:00 on weekdays; weekends stay under 10 an hour." Putting the finding in the title or subtitle also gives a text version of the chart's message for anyone who can't read the colours.
   - **Kind:** taste/common sense. No approved source covers this; it just saves every reader the decoding step.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type sizes collide.** The page title (about 28 px) and the KPI figures (about 26 px) are a step apart that reads as a mistake. The 12 px axis/legend labels and 13 px KPI labels also sit nearly together. Pick one size for each pair, or separate them by weight instead.
- **Periods don't match.** The subtitle says "1 to 28 September" but the KPI says "New tickets, September". State whether 3,412 covers 28 or 30 days, or the reader can't relate it to the chart.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI column.** Every number carries its period and a comparison ("Up 4% on August", "Down 6 min on August"), so good and bad can be told apart without a colour code. Keep that pattern.
- **The source line.** It says what "hour" means: when the ticket was opened, in the team's local time. That's exactly the ambiguity a staffing reader would trip on.
- **The structure.** Hour × weekday heatmap, a calm grid, a quiet nav with the current section in bold. This is the right form for the question. Only the colour encoding needs changing, not the chart type.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether cells give exact counts on hover. With bucketed colour that's the only way to check a claim like the busiest hour.
