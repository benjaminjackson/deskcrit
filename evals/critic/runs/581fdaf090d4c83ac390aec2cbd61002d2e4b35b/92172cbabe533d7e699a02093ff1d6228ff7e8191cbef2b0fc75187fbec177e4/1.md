**First read.** The eye lands on the run of six bright bars, then gets pulled to the orange and red ones in the middle. They are the warmest, loudest colours on the page, and they belong to channels the page says nothing about. The title reads clearly and the chart reads as a tidy "sign-ups by channel" report. The point of the page, "What changed this quarter", sits off to the right in small type and you only find it on a second pass.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal growth dashboard panel that tells a marketing team or its leads how Q3 sign-ups split by channel and what will change because of it.

**Verdict.** It's clean and honest but it doesn't make its point. The chart shows the topic, the story is only in the sidebar text, and the colours point at the wrong bars. One even says the opposite of the text.

**What would move it most**

1. **The colours encode nothing and point the wrong way.** This is one series of six bars, already named on the axis, drawn in six saturated hues. The colour adds no information, so it can only steer attention, and it steers it badly:
   - Orange (Referral) and red (Paid social) are the warmest and most forward colours, so they pull the eye first.
   - The bar the story is about, Organic search, is a cool blue that sits back.
   - Red and green read as bad and good. Podcast ads is green, but the note says it underperformed and is losing budget.

   The fix: draw every bar in one neutral or brand colour, and keep a single accent for Organic search. If you want to show the second point too, give Podcast ads and Events a muted shared tone. This is a **defect**: it sends the reader to the wrong bars and suggests the wrong verdict (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design", pitfall 12; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 19.1).

2. **The chart states the topic, not the finding, and the comparison the finding rests on isn't drawn.** "New sign-ups by channel" could head any quarter's report. Both points in the sidebar are about share and change: "a third, up from a quarter in Q2" and "together fewer than 700". The chart shows only Q3 counts, so a reader has to work out 1,840 ÷ 5,400 and take the Q2 figure on trust.
   - Put the comparison where the number is. Add a Q2 marker or ghost bar per channel, or label the Organic search bar "34% (Q2: 25%)" (Few, pitfall 2: show the change from the last period next to the number).
   - Bracket Podcast ads and Events with their combined 670.
   - Rewrite the title as the finding, e.g. "Organic search now brings a third of new accounts". That is **taste**, argued on common sense: the title is the one line everyone reads, so it should carry the point. This also links the chart to the sidebar, which right now is a separate place the eye has to find on its own.

3. **"Up from a quarter in Q2" can be read two ways.** On a page full of Q2, Q3 and Q4, "a quarter" can mean 25% or a three-month period. The reader has to stop and work out which, and that phrase carries the main claim of the page. Use numbers: "34% of new accounts, up from 25% in Q2". In the same way, "fewer than 700" hides the real figure, 670. Since this line justifies moving a budget, give the exact number and its share (about 12%). This is a **defect**: these are the figures people will quote back.

**Smaller things.**
- The teal Email bar looks under 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Check it with a contrast tool. Moving to one bar colour fixes it anyway.
- The small type uses about four sizes one pixel apart: axis ticks around 12 px, value labels around 13 px bold, category labels around 14 px, body around 15 px. They read as accidents rather than levels. Fold them into two sizes and separate the value labels by weight alone.

**What's working.**
- The chart basics are right: bars sorted by value, axis from zero, faint gridlines, and values labelled directly on the bars, so nobody has to read the axis.
- The source line gives the period and a total, and the total reconciles: the six bars sum exactly to 5,400. That builds trust; keep it.
- The sidebar ends on a decision ("The events budget moves to referrals in Q4"), which most dashboard panels never get to. Keep it, and make the chart lead to it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover, focus and selected states on the nav links, and whether the page lets you switch quarters.
