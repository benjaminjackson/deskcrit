**First read.** The eye goes straight to the purple bars and the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. The chart looks like it's saying Growth converts much better than Starter, with Growth's bar about two and a half times as tall. Only after that do you notice the 2.5% at the bottom of the axis. It looks like a clean, calm, competent internal analytics page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal product analytics dashboard. Its job is to help a product or growth team compare trial-to-paid conversion across plans and decide where to act.

**Verdict.** The page is tidy and well built, but the chart misleads its own readers. Because the value axis starts at 2.5%, a gap of under one percentage point looks like a several-fold difference, and none of the differences comes with any sense of how far it can be trusted. Someone deciding where to invest would draw the wrong conclusion from the first glance.

**What would move it most**

1. **The bars start at 2.5%, so the gaps look about five times bigger than they are.** *(Defect; convention.)*
   - **Where:** the value axis of the main chart.
   - **What the viewer sees:** Growth's bar is about 2.5× the height of Starter's, but 4.0% is only about 29% more than 3.1%. That gives a lie factor of roughly 5 (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2, graphical integrity).
   - **Why it matters:** bar length *is* the value, and there's no break marker to warn anyone. Most readers will take away "Growth converts 2–3× better than Starter," which is false.
   - **Fix:** start the bar axis at zero so bar lengths keep the real proportions (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, 17.1; Few, "Common Pitfalls," pitfall 8). If the small differences are the point, switch to dots on a non-zero scale, which is allowed for position-based charts.
   - **Expect the honest version to look flat:** four bars of nearly the same height. That flatness is the real finding.

2. **Nothing shows how much the gaps can be trusted, and the smallest plan is thin.** *(Defect.)*
   - **The numbers:** Enterprise's 3.4% comes from 38 paid accounts out of 1,120 trials. By rough arithmetic its plausible range runs from about 2.5% to 4.5%, which overlaps Team and Growth completely. Growth (118 of 2,950) also overlaps Team.
   - **What it costs:** the page presents four precise one-decimal figures as if their ranking meant something. Only Starter vs Growth looks like a real gap.
   - **Fix:** add interval marks or a short sample-size note on the chart (Wilke, ch. 16), so nobody reorders the roadmap around 0.2 points of noise.
   - The table already has the counts, but nobody does that maths in their head.

3. **The subtitle and the definition describe different rules.** *(Defect: this is the metric definition people will hold the number to.)*
   - **The two rules:** the subtitle says trials "became paid accounts **within 30 days**." The definition says trials "still open on **30 October** are counted as not converted." The first is a 30-day window per trial. The second is one cutoff date for everyone.
   - **Reading one:** a trial started 1 July that paid on 15 September counts as converted under the definition but not under the subtitle.
   - **Reading two:** under the subtitle, a trial started 30 September has until 30 October, so the two only agree for the last cohort.
   - **Why it matters:** an analyst re-running the number will get a different answer depending on which line they believe.
   - **Fix:** state one rule in both places, in full. For example: "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice was paid within 30 days of the trial starting."

**Smaller things**
- The title only names the topic, "Trial to paid conversion by plan." After the axis fix, consider stating the finding, such as "Conversion is 3–4% on every plan; Growth leads Starter by about 1 point." That's taste, not a published rule: a dashboard reader comes for the answer, and the honest chart will look flat enough that a caption needs to say what it means.

**What's working**
- **Direct labelling and restraint.** Each bar carries its own value, the gridlines are pale, there's one accent colour and no legend. The data is the darkest ink in the chart. Keep this when you rescale.
- **The supporting panel shows its working.** The raw counts sit beside the rates, the definition is on the page, and the source line gives exact dates. That's rare, and it's what lets a reader check the chart. The counts match the rates exactly (286 / 9,210 = 3.1%, and so on).
- **The plans are in tier order,** not sorted by value. That's right here, because readers look up plans by name and size.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Hover tooltips on the bars, keyboard focus on the nav tabs, and how the two-column layout reflows on narrow screens.
