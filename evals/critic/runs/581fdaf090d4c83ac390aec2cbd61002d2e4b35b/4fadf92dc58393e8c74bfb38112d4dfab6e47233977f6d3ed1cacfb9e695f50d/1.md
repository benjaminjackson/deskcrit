**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1: the description is right-aligned, so it has no starting edge (defect, plus a broken convention).** The two paragraphs (about 4 and 3 lines) are flush right under a flush-left label, headline, price and button. Your eye has to find a new starting point on every line. That slows down the shopper on exactly the text that explains tare, the units and the battery life. It also breaks the shared left edge that holds the column together, so the text looks like it slid out of place. Fix: set paragraphs of several lines flush left, and keep centring or right alignment for a few short lines (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3). Version 2 does exactly this, and that's the whole reason it wins.
2. **Both versions: the image panel shows no product (defect, unless the photo just hadn't loaded).** The right half of the page, its largest element, is an empty beige square with a pale circle. A shopper can't see what the scale looks like, how big it is, or what the display shows. The headline promises a "flat glass scale" and the picture doesn't back it up. If this is a placeholder or a photo that failed to load, this goes away. If not, put in a real photo of the scale, ideally with its display lit, so the picture shows what the text describes. This is common sense rather than a cited rule.
