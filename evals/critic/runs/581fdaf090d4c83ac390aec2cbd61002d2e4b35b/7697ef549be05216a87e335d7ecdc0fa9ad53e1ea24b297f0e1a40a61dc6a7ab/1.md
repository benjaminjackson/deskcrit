**First read.** My eye goes to "Planning a vegetable bed", then down a column of four short, evenly spaced sections. It reads as a calm, plain how-to page for a beginner gardener. It feels tidy and quiet. At the bottom, "Design review: approved, a model of clear heading styles" is set exactly like the gardening advice, and it reads as a stray line that's part of the article.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short web article teaching a first-time grower how to set up a vegetable bed, meant to be read top to bottom and skimmed later.

**Verdict.** The page mostly does its job. The structure is clear, and there are few enough sizes that each one has a role. The biggest problem is the copy: a review note sits in the article's body style and reads as content. Next come body lines that run too long and headings that differ from the text only in size.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Design review" line is set as part of the article.** It sits at the bottom at y≈613, in the same size, weight and colour as the planting advice. A reader can't tell that it's a note about the page rather than advice, and it means nothing to someone growing radishes. It also looks like a leftover note from the team that shipped by mistake. *Defect.* Remove it from the published page. If it has to stay, style it as metadata that's clearly outside the article (smaller, greyer, set apart).
2. **Body lines are too long.** Each paragraph runs about 790 px. "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" is about 90 characters, well past Bringhurst's 45–75 (ideal about 66). The paragraphs are short, so the cost is modest, but each return to the next line is a longer jump than it needs to be. *Convention.* Narrow the text column to about 65 characters at this size. The headings can keep the full width.
3. **Headings differ from the text only in size.** The 48 px title, the 32 px section heads and the 18 px body are all the same regular weight and the same near-black. To make the levels clear, the section heads have to be large, so "Watering" (32 px) outweighs its own one-line paragraph and the page reads as headings with captions. Dair calls this the mark of average work: hierarchy built from size alone. *Convention.* Use a smaller step in size plus a change of weight: for example, section heads at about 22–24 px in semibold. The H1 could gain weight too, so it keeps its lead without growing.

**Smaller things.**
- "Choose a spot that gets six hours of sun" has no time unit. Write "six hours of sun a day" so a beginner doesn't have to guess.
- "Compost from last year's heap" assumes the reader already has a compost heap, which a first-timer (the reader the radish-and-lettuce advice is aimed at) probably doesn't. Say "compost (bought, or from your own heap)".
- The apostrophe in "year's" is straight. Use a curly one. This is common sense rather than a checked rule.

**What's working.**
- Space puts each heading with its own paragraph. There's about 30 px above each section head and only a few px below, so every section reads as one unit (Bringhurst allows exactly this: more space above a subhead than below). Keep that when you change the heading styles.
- The type scale is short and well stepped. There are three sizes with big gaps between them and no near-duplicates, so each size has one job.
- The instructions are concrete. "Depth of a spade", "twice a week" and naming radishes, lettuce and bush beans give the reader things they can actually do. Keep that voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Reflow on narrow screens. Check that the column narrows and the 48 px title wraps cleanly at 320 px.
