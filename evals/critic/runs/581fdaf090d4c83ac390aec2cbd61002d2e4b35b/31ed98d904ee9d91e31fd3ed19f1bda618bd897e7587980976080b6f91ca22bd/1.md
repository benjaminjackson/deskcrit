**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title, takes in the subtitle, and then meets a heavy black cage. All 28 cells are boxed with the same dark rule, so the borders show up before the times do. This reads as a plain, competent spreadsheet export of a ferry schedule. It feels clerical: everything is legible, but nothing helps you find your boat.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a passenger timetable for the north-landing-to-market-quay ferry, read on a screen or a posted sheet by someone asking "when is the next boat I can catch today?"

**Verdict.** It's readable but doesn't do its job well. The table answers "what boats exist" when the reader is asking "which boat can I catch today." Heavy cell borders and a Period column that adds nothing push the useful data apart.

**What would move it most**
1. **The day rule is hidden in the last column, so every row needs a second check.** Daily, Weekdays and Weekends sit at the far right in the same weight as everything else. A Saturday rider has to read each departure, track across about 800 px to Runs, and mentally strike out 12:40 and 17:10. A weekday rider has to notice that the 19:30 boat doesn't run for them. Nothing in the layout separates the boats that always run from the exceptions. That's a **defect**: it slows every lookup, and someone who reads the time but misses the Runs column turns up for a boat that isn't coming (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should be self-evident). Fix: make the day pattern something the reader sees, not something they cross-reference. The usual ways are separate Weekday and Weekend lists or columns, or marking the exceptions next to the departure time and leaving "Daily" as the unmarked default.
2. **Every cell is boxed in a near-black 2 px rule.** The grid has as much visual weight as the data. It breaks rows into separate compartments, so your eye has to jump over a heavy line between departure and arrival. This is a **convention** broken at a cost. Tufte calls ink that carries no data, such as the frame and cell borders, something to remove. Lupton warns that table rules should "illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell" (*Thinking with Type*, p. 157). Fix: drop the vertical rules and the frame, and keep thin pale horizontal rules or a pale tone on alternate rows, so each row reads as one trip.
3. **The Period column takes a quarter of the width and says nothing the times don't.** "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday…" repeats on every row. It's the widest content after the headers, and it stretches the table so that 06:40 and 06:52 sit about 300 px apart with empty cell space between them. This is a **defect** of grouping: the periods are meant to be groups, but they show up as repeated labels instead of through space. Fix: either cut the column and narrow the table so departure, arrival and day pattern sit close together, or turn the periods into group subheads with extra space between groups.

**Smaller things.**
- Subtitle, "spring season": the reader can't tell whether today falls inside it. Give the start and end dates, because people act on them.
- Arrivals are always 12 minutes after departure: you could state that once ("crossing takes 12 min") and give that column's space back. This is taste, and keeping the arrival column is defensible for scanning.

**What's working.**
- One clean sans in two weights, with a big step from title to body. The hierarchy above the table is plain and quiet; keep it.
- Rows run in time order, and the times use 24-hour HH:MM consistently, so the left column of times scans well once the boxes are gone.
- The subtitle states direction and route right under the title, which is exactly the first question a rider has.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the table reflows on a phone at 320 px wide, which matters if riders check this at the landing.
