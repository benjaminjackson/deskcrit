**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place" at the upper left, drops to the grey subhead, then to the filled "Get started" button, and stops there. The right half and the bottom third are empty, so the eye has nowhere to go after the buttons. It reads as a clean but anonymous SaaS hero, and the empty right side looks as if an image failed to load.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the top of a home page for a group trip planner, and its job is to make a first-time visitor understand the product and sign up.

**Verdict.** The type and the button order are competent, but the hero never shows the product. The whole case rests on a headline that could belong to any travel app. The biggest problem is the empty right half: it is where the proof should be, and right now it makes the page look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half is empty, so the claim is never shown.** *(Defect.)* The area from x≈700 to the right edge and everything below the buttons is blank. The page's main claim is that flights, stays, and day plans live in one place, but the visitor never sees that place, so they have to take it on trust. All the visual weight sits upper left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"). The empty area is leftover space, not space that gives the headline room. This is a common-sense point, not a published rule: a headline and a picture should be two halves of one idea. Here there is only one half.
   - **Fix:** put one real view of the product on the right, such as a trip itinerary with a flight, a stay, and a day plan. Ideally show a group's votes or comments on it, so it shows "agree without a dozen chat threads" in one picture. If an image is supposed to be there and didn't load in this capture, check it in a live browser. The finding stands until it appears.
2. **The headline could be on anyone's page, and the real idea is hidden in the subhead.** *(Defect, copy.)* Plenty of travel apps say "Plan the whole trip in one place." What makes this one different, a *group* agreeing without the chat-thread chaos, only shows up in the grey 20 px subhead. A visitor scanning only the big type learns nothing that sets this product apart.
   - **Fix:** move the group-agreement idea into the headline and say it to the visitor, with a verb and a "you". Something like getting your group to agree on the trip without the chat-thread chaos. Then the subhead can list what's included.
3. **"Get started" doesn't say what happens next.** *(Defect, copy.)* The button gives the visitor no picture of their first step. Start a trip? Make an account? Import something? It also misses the comparison the subhead already sets up: the dozen chat threads the visitor already lives with.
   - **Fix:** name one exact first action from the visitor's own situation. For example, "Start a trip and invite your group." Better still, set it against what they already use, such as pulling in the plans from their group chat. That way the visitor can see the gain on their own terms.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place", which strands "in" and splits the phrase "in one place". Break before "in" or rework the line so the phrase stays whole.

**What's working.**
- **The order of actions is right.** The filled teal "Get started" next to the outlined "See pricing" gives the page one clear main action and a quieter second one. Keep that pairing when you change the labels.
- **The headline dominates.** At about 60 px, heavy, and slightly tightly spaced, it is the obvious first read and holds together as one block. Its left edge lines up with the logo, the subhead, and the buttons.
- **The subhead is the best copy on the page.** "Without a dozen chat threads" names a real pain the visitor recognises. Use that sentence as the seed for the headline and the button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the two buttons and the nav links, and how the layout reflows on a phone. Check in a browser that the hero image (once it's added) stacks under the copy instead of pushing the buttons off-screen.
