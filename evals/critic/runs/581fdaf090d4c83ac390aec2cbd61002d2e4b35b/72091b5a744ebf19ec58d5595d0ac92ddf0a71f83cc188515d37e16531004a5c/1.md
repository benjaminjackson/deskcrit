**First read.** The eye slams straight into "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY", a heavy black block in the upper left. Then it drops to the two-line paragraph, then off into a large empty white field with nowhere to go. It reads as a hero or poster for a harbour open-day ferry. It feels loud and compressed, closer to a headline shouting a warning than to an invitation for a gentle morning boat ride.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web hero or poster announcing free ferry rides at a city harbour open day, aimed at local families and day-trippers, meant to get them to turn up.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy is clear and the type scale is disciplined, but the piece fails at its job. It never says when the open day is, and the headline is tracked so tight that its letters crash into each other. Fix the missing date and time first, then open up the headline.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no date, time, or next step.** *Defect.* Someone who sees this learns that free rides run "every half hour from the north landing", but not which day, what time the first and last sailings are, or where the north landing is. The eye leaves the paragraph and drops into roughly 300 px of empty white with nothing to act on. For an event announcement the date is the one fact people remember and plan around, so without it they can't come. Add the day and date, first and last sailing times, and the place. Set them as their own small group at a level between the headline and the paragraph, ideally in that empty lower band. If this is a web page, finish with one specific ask, like "See sailing times" or "Find the north landing". Avoid a generic "Learn more".
2. **The headline's letters collide.** *Defect and convention.* The display type is tracked so tight that L–L in "ALL", R–D in "ABOARD", N–I–N–G in "MORNING" and R–R–Y in "FERRY" touch or overlap. The word spaces have shrunk to slivers, so "ALLABOARD" and "MORNINGFERRY" read as single clumps. Display type does want tighter spacing than text (Bringhurst; Butterick). But here it has passed tight and become damaged: the letter shapes merge into a dark mass, and the reader has to decode words instead of reading them. Loosen the tracking until no pair touches and each word space reads clearly, then check the RD, NI and RR pairs by eye.
3. **The mood fights the message.** *Taste.* A crushed, ultra-heavy black grotesque says urgency, like a protest poster or a breaking-news banner. The copy says "bring a hat and a thermos": a relaxed, free family morning out. Easing the tracking (point 2) helps a lot. Beyond that, consider a little air or a touch of harbour colour so the page invites rather than shouts. Keep the scale. That's what makes it work.

**Smaller things.**
- Eyebrow "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" (top left): small capitals set tight, so the words run together at ~13 px. Capitals need slightly *opened* tracking (Bringhurst), so add a little letterspacing.
- Paragraph line break: "Bring a / hat and a thermos." splits the phrase. Rebreak so "Bring a hat and a thermos." sits on one line, or narrow the measure slightly.
- The lower ~40% and right half are empty, and with no date block they read as leftover rather than shaped. Once the facts from point 1 land there, this resolves itself.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is three clean sizes: eyebrow, headline at ~110 px, body at ~20 px. The big steps mean nobody wonders what to read first. Keep it.
- One strong left edge at x=90 aligns every element, which gives the page a firm spine.
- The body copy is concrete and human ("every half hour", "north landing", "hat and a thermos"). It's the right voice for the event, so keep it when you add the missing facts.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the headline is live text or an image (it should be live text so screen readers and search can read it), and how the tight headline wraps on a narrow phone screen. Check that "MORNING FERRY" doesn't break mid-word.
