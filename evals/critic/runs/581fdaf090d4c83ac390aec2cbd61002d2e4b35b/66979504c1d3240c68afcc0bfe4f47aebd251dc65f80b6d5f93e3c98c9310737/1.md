**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big black headline, "Send an invoice in the time it takes to pour coffee.", then down to the grey subline and the dark green button. From there it jumps right to a small invoice card sitting by itself, then drops to three white cards that are mostly empty. It reads as a landing page for invoicing software aimed at freelancers and small studios. The top half feels calm, warm and confident. The bottom half looks unfinished.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing homepage, and its job is to get a freelancer to start the free trial.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job: one clear focal point, a specific promise, one action, and type that is well handled. Two things hold it back. The three feature cards are about 60% empty, so the bottom of the screen looks broken. And the only picture on the page doesn't prove the speed the headline promises.

**What would move it most**
1. **The feature cards are mostly blank (defect).** Each of the three cards along the bottom is about 220 px tall, but its title and one or two lines of text fill only the top 80 px. The rest is empty white, outlined by a border, and the row ends right at the fold. A viewer reads empty framed boxes as images that failed to load or a page that isn't finished. That makes the product look less trustworthy at the exact moment it is trying to earn trust. This empty space isn't doing anything: it is left over in three boxes, not gathered into one open area that gives the headline room (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"; Tschichold and Müller-Brockmann on leftover space). If a picture or demo was meant to go there and didn't load in the capture, this is fine once it loads. Check that in a live browser. If nothing was meant to go there, shrink the cards to fit their content. Better still, give each card a small, real piece of proof: the reminder email itself, the two-tap payment screen, the exported file.

2. **The invoice card shows a finished invoice, not a fast one (taste, argued).** The headline promises speed, and the subline promises that clients, rates and tax are filled in for you. The card on the right is just a static, completed invoice. It could come from any invoicing tool, so it adds nothing the viewer didn't already assume. It is also small, about 340 × 200 px, floating in the right half of the green panel, with a dead gap of about 270 px between it and the copy. Its top and bottom don't line up with the headline or the button, so it looks placed by eye. Make the picture prove the claim. For example, show the line items visibly filled in automatically, a "Sent in 0:41" time, or the "Paid" state that follows a reminder. Make it big enough, and align it with the copy column, so it balances the headline as an equal partner rather than a sticker.

3. **The button asks the viewer to trust the claim, not test it (taste).** "Start free for 14 days" is clear, and it's better than "Get started". But it only offers a trial period. The headline sets up a test the viewer could run on their own work: the next invoice they have to send. A button such as "Send your next invoice free" puts the product next to the viewer's current routine, so they judge the speed for themselves. Keep the 14-day detail as a small line under the button.

**Smaller things.**
- Grid: the invoice card's left edge (about x=892) lines up with nothing; the third feature card starts at about x=861. Put the card on the same column lines as the row below it.
- Copy: the subline says Tallyfold "chases late payments", but the first card says the reminders come "before an invoice is overdue". Those are different promises, so pick one, or say both happen.
- Copy: "Export a clean year of income in one file" doesn't say what kind of file or who it's for. Something like "one file your accountant can open" makes the point land on the viewer.

**What's working.**
- The headline. It is specific, makes a concrete comparison anyone can picture, and is clearly the most important thing on the page. It is set tight and heavy, so its three lines read as one block. Keep its size and weight.
- The subline says exactly who does what ("fills in your clients, rates, and tax, then chases late payments for you"), with real verbs. It is the strongest piece of copy on the page.
- The palette is restrained: warm off-white, pale sage, near-black, and one deep green used only for the button. Nothing competes with the call to action. The invoice figures add up correctly ($640 + $45 + 8% tax = $739.80), which is a quiet sign of care.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty card bottoms are images that didn't load (check in a live browser). Hover and focus states on the button and the navigation links. How the two-column top section reflows on narrow screens.
