**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", drops to the grey subtitle, then runs down the times. It reads straight away as a clean, quiet timetable for one ferry route, set in a neutral web-app style. The trouble starts when you try to use it. To find out whether a boat runs on a Saturday, you have to read along to the far right of every row, and nothing there stands out.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a public web page where riders check when the next boat leaves north landing, often on a phone and in a hurry.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and legible, but it answers "when do boats leave?" and not the rider's real question, "when do boats leave *today*?" The day each boat runs is buried in the last column and set exactly like everything else. Missing context (season dates, the return trip) makes this worse.

**What would move it most**
1. **The days of service are hidden where they matter most (defect).** In the "Runs" column on the right, the three exceptions look exactly like the four "Daily" rows: "Weekdays" at 12:40 and 17:10, and "Weekends" at 19:30. A weekday commuter planning the evening has to read every row to the far edge to learn that the 19:30 boat won't come. A Sunday rider has to do the same to learn the 12:40 and 17:10 boats don't run. Nothing in the table breaks its even rhythm where the service changes, so the one fact that could strand someone has the least visual weight. Fix: organise by the day the reader is travelling. Either use two columns or tables, Weekdays and Weekends, each listing only the boats that run, or keep one list and mark the exceptions clearly (with weight, a short tag, or a footnote mark beside the time), so that "Daily" is the quiet default.
2. **The copy leaves out the dates and the trip back (defect).** "Spring season" is a date range the reader acts on, with no dates given. A rider in late May can't tell whether this table still applies, or what replaces it. The page also covers only north landing to market quay. Anyone going to the market needs the return times, and nothing says where they are. This is fine only if they're elsewhere on the page and you can see a link to them. Fix: write the dates into the subtitle ("North landing to market quay, 1 March to 31 May") and add the return timetable or a visible link to it. A line naming the last boat back would also stop riders getting stranded.
3. **The first column is the least useful thing in the table (defect, mild).** "Period" takes the leftmost, first-read position, yet it only restates what the time already says, and it repeats ("Morning, Morning", "Midday, Midday") because the space doesn't do the grouping. Every row sits at the same interval, so the groups exist only in words. Fix: lead with the departure time. If you keep the periods, make each one a small label over a group of boats, with extra space between groups, not a value repeated on every row (proximity: Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II).

**Smaller things.**
- The times look like they use proportional figures ("11:00" sets narrower than "06:40"), so the digits don't line up perfectly down the column. Check that `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` is on (Bringhurst 4.4.3).
- In my view, the table runs about 1,080 px wide for four short values, so the eye travels a long way from a time to its "Runs" value. That's taste, not a rule. A narrower table, or moving "Runs" next to "Leaves north", would shorten the trip.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is plain: one bold title, a quieter subtitle, then a table with a bold header row and very light row rules. Keep that restraint. The rules separate the rows without competing with the times.
- One typeface in a few weights, near-black on white, and a single text size in the table. It's calm and easy to read, which suits a page people glance at.
- A table, not a chart, is the right form for looking up exact times.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Reflow on a phone: check that the four-column table stays readable at 320 px without scrolling sideways. Many riders will open this on a phone at the landing.
