**First read.** The eye goes to the bold question "Save changes to the shared calendar?", then straight down to the filled blue **Save changes** button at the bottom right. It reads as a standard confirmation dialog in a calendar or community app, and it feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. Then the eye has to slow down, because the button row offers four choices for what is really a yes-or-no question, and the one that throws work away looks just like the harmless one next to it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a confirmation dialog in a web app. Someone editing a shared calendar used by a choir decides whether to publish their changes to everyone who follows it, and the dialog has to make that choice quick and hard to get wrong.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The question, the explanation and the main action are clear and in the right order. The problem is the button row: **Discard changes** throws work away but looks like a harmless link, and **Export as file** has nothing to do with the question. Together they turn a two-second decision into a four-way one and make losing your edits a little too easy.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" doesn't look destructive (defect).** It's the second of four actions in the bottom row. It uses the same blue text-link style as "Export as file" and sits next to "Keep editing", so the action that throws away your edits looks no different from the one that just downloads a file. Someone moving quickly toward "Keep editing", or reading the blue links as "the minor options", can lose their rehearsal-time edits with one click. They may get no warning if that click isn't followed by a confirmation or undo. Apple HIG ("Alerts") and Material Design ("Dialogs") both say destructive actions should be set apart from safe ones, styled as destructive, and never the default. Fix: move Discard to the far left, away from Keep editing and Save, style it as destructive (red text is the usual cue), and either keep it out of default focus or offer an undo after it.
2. **"Export as file" is in the wrong place (defect).** It's the first action at the left of the bottom row. The question asks "save or not?", and exporting answers neither. It's an extra decision the viewer has to read and rule out before they can act, and it pulls the row's visual centre toward the left, where the least relevant action sits. Nielsen's heuristic 8 (aesthetic and minimalist design) and progressive disclosure (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure") both say rare options shouldn't sit next to the main choice. Fix: take Export out of this dialog and put it in the calendar's menu or toolbar. That leaves three actions: Discard, set apart on the left, with Keep editing and Save changes together on the right.

**Smaller things.**
- The body line says the new times "will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar", but not whether those followers get a notification. If they do, say so ("…and they'll get a notification"), because that's what makes a hesitant editor stop and check the times first.

**What's working.**
- **The copy fits the decision.** The heading is the actual question, with a specific object ("the shared calendar"), and the body line says exactly what will happen and to whom ("everyone who follows this calendar"). Keep that pairing of question and consequence.
- **The button emphasis is right for Save and Keep editing.** One filled primary button, one outlined secondary and plain text for the rest is the textbook order. Keep Save as the only filled button.
- **The type is restrained.** One family in about three sizes: a bold heading, then body and button text at the same size. It reads quickly with no wasted levels.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Which button has keyboard focus when the dialog opens. It should be Save or Keep editing, never Discard.
- The dialog floats on a blank white page with no dimmed backdrop. That could just be how the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser that the app behind it is dimmed, so it's obvious the dialog blocks the page.
