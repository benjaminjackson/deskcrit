**First read.** The eye goes to "Bin collection days", then to the tinted box: "Next collection: Thursday". The five blue links are the next layer. It reads as a plain, calm, mobile council page. In version 1 the link underlines look heavy, sit low and stand apart from the words. In version 2 they sit close under the words in the same blue.

**What it's for.** My assumption: residents check which bins go out and when, then follow a link to see the calendar, report a missed bin or order a replacement.

**Verdict.** The two versions differ only in the link underline, and version 2 handles it better. The page as a whole does its job. The biggest problem, in both versions, is that it never says which address "Thursday" is for or which date it means.

**Which is better and why**
Version 2. In version 1 each link has a dark rule set well below the baseline. On the two-line links ("full / calendar for the year", "report it within two / working days", "replaced / for a fee of $25") that rule fills the gap between lines and nearly touches the ascenders of the line below. That makes the paragraphs look ruled and busier, and the links shout louder than the instructions around them. Version 2 uses the standard underline: thin, in the link colour, close to the baseline, and broken around descenders. You can still clearly tell the links apart from the text by more than colour, so it meets the same need (WCAG 1.4.1 use of colour). The text keeps its even texture and the links look like what people expect a link to look like (Nielsen, consistency and standards). The difference is small but real. Version 2 loses nothing and reads more cleanly.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **"Next collection: Thursday" doesn't say for whom or which Thursday.** Defect. This is the line people act on: they put the bin out because of it. The page asks for a postcode further down, but here it has already given an answer without one. A resident can't tell whether it applies to their street, or whether "Thursday" means this week or next week. If they read it wrongly, the bin is missed. Fix: add the date and the address it applies to ("Thursday 26 November, for 14 Mill Lane"). If the page doesn't know the address, ask for the postcode first and show the answer after.
2. **"$25" on a UK council page.** Defect. The words "bins", "postcode" and "council" all say UK, so a dollar sign makes a reader wonder whether the fee is real or a template error, and this is the one price on the page. Fix: £25. If the page really is non-UK, make the rest of the wording match.

**Smaller things.**
- "Put your bins out by 7 am on the day" is a deadline, but it's buried in body text. Consider moving it into the tinted box next to the day.

**What's working.**
- The tinted "Next collection" box makes the answer the first thing you see after the title. Keep it as the only emphasised block.
- The link text describes what each link does ("report it within two working days", "yearly permit"), so each one makes sense on its own.
- Comfortable text size, line spacing and line length for a phone.
