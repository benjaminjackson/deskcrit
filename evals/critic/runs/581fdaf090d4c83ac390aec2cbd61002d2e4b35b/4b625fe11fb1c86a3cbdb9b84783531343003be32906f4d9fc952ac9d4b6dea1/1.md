**First read.** The eye lands on "Low-light houseplants", drops to the one-line promise under it, then runs across four identical cards. It reads at once as a small online plant shop's category page. It is calm, tidy and competent, but the four plant pictures are near copies: two or three green leaves in the same terracotta pot. After the first second nothing tells the plants apart except the names.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a category page where someone with a dim room picks one plant and adds it to the cart.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and works as a shop page, but it doesn't help anyone choose. The pictures don't show the plants being sold, and the cards give no reason to pick one over another. The biggest problem is the pictures: on a plant shop, how the plant looks is most of the sale.

**What would move it most**

1. **The pictures don't show the products.** *Defect.* In all four image tiles, the snake plant has two round leaves, but a real one has tall, upright, sword-shaped leaves. The pothos stands upright, but the copy right under it says "trails to 6 ft". The cast iron plant's pot is listed as 8 in, yet it is drawn the same size as the three 6 in pots. A shopper who knows these plants sees a mismatch. A shopper who doesn't learns nothing about what will arrive, and the row reads as one plant shown four times. The pictures also do nothing for the headline: nothing in them says "happy in low light". Direction: use real photos of each plant, all shot the same way (same backdrop, same angle, same distance). If you keep illustrations, each one must show that plant's real shape: the snake plant's upright blades, the pothos spilling over the pot, and a visibly bigger pot for the 8 in plant.

2. **The price doesn't say what's in the box.** *Defect.* Each card pairs "6 in pot" with a price, and the picture shows a terracotta pot, but "Pots" is a separate section in the top nav. "$24" can be read two ways: the plant in the terracotta pot shown, or the plant in a plastic nursery pot, with the pot sold separately. Someone who reads it the first way and then gets a plastic pot will feel misled, and that's the line they'll hold you to. Direction: say it on the card, for example "In a 6 in plastic grower pot" or "Includes 6 in terracotta pot". And if the drawn pot isn't included, don't draw it.

3. **The cards give no reason to choose.** *Defect, from the copy.* The subhead, "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window", is good: specific and in the buyer's own terms. But then each card offers only pot size, grown size, and price. The questions a low-light buyer actually has are how dark the room can be, how often to water, and whether the plant is safe for pets. Those answers would separate these four plants, and none of them is on the page, so choosing means opening four product pages (Nielsen: recognition rather than recall). Direction: add one line per card that tells this plant apart from the other three, in the buyer's words. For example: "Fine in a hallway with no window", "Water once a month", or "Safe around cats and dogs".

**Smaller things.**
- Top nav: "Houseplants" looks the same as the other links even though this is the Houseplants page. Mark the current section with one cue, such as a bolder weight or an underline (Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", NN/g).
- On each card, the plant name (about 18 px bold) and the price (about 20 px bold) are the same colour and almost the same size, so they read as one level picked twice. I count about seven text sizes on the page, with 18/20 and 14/15 colliding. Put the name and the price at one size, and tell them apart by weight or colour instead (Bringhurst on scales; Dair on contrast).

**What's working.**
- The grid is disciplined. The logo, headline, and first card share one left edge at about 48 px, the cart and last card share the right edge, and the gaps between cards are equal. Keep this when you add the extra line to each card.
- The colour is restrained: teal is used only for "Add to cart", so the action is unmistakable without any shouting. White on teal looks to pass 4.5:1.
- The subhead is the best copy on the page: a concrete distance anyone can check against their own room. The card lines should be written to that standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the buttons and nav links, and how the four-up row reflows on a phone.
