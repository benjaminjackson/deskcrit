## Which version is better

**Version 2 is better.** The only change between them is how the table is ruled, and that change matters.

**First read.** In both versions the eye goes to the bold heading "Departures from Corn Exchange", then down the bold times, then across to the two red statuses. In version 1 the dark grid of boxes hits the eye at the same moment as the data, so the page reads as a spreadsheet. In version 2 the type itself is what you see first, and the page reads as a calm, finished departures board.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a live departures screen for one bus stand, read quickly by someone trying to catch a bus.

**Verdict.** Both versions work. Version 2 does the job better because it removes the heavy cell borders, so the times, destinations and the two problem rows read faster. Nothing else is different. The weakness both versions share is that the late and cancelled rows tell you what went wrong but not what to do about it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cell borders (version 1 only, already fixed in version 2). Convention.** In version 1 every cell sits inside a near-black 1 px box. The vertical rules are as dark as the text and carry no information. They cut each departure into five separate pieces when your eye wants to follow one row from time to status. They also compete with the red statuses, which are the only things on the page that should stand out. Lupton writes that table rules "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell" (*Thinking with Type*, p. 157), and this is Tufte's non-data ink. Version 2 does what the approved fix says: no vertical rules, and pale horizontal rules that keep each row together. Keep it.
2. **The late and cancelled rows leave the rider to work out what to do (both versions). Defect.** "4 min late" next to 14:11 means the rider has to calculate 14:15 themselves, and the time they act on is the one shown least directly. Saying "Expected 14:15" puts the number in front of them. "Cancelled" on the 14:40 to Northgate is a dead end. Right now the rider has to scan the list and find that no other Northgate bus appears, which is common sense rather than a published rule. Point them to the next option ("Next 19: 15:10") or say plainly that there isn't one. You could also strike through or grey the cancelled time so it doesn't look like a bus you can still catch (my taste).

**Smaller things.**
- The Via column is very wide, which pushes Status far from Time. On a wide screen the eye has a long jump to find out whether its bus is running. Narrowing Via or moving Status nearer the time would shorten it (my taste).

**What's working.**
- The bold times down the left edge give the eye a clear spine to scan in order. They look like tabular figures and line up cleanly.
- Status is written in words as well as red, so the meaning survives for colour-blind readers and in greyscale.
- "Stand C · Updated 14:02" answers the two questions a rider has before they trust the board: am I at the right stand, and is this current?

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the board refreshes, and how it looks on a small phone. If it's shown on a display at the stop, check that the text is big enough to read from a few metres away.
