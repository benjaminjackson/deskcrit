**First read.** The eye lands on "Project notes" top left, then runs down five identical dark-outlined boxes. On the way down it catches on the third box, "Sketches", which sits a little to the right of the others. It reads as a plain internal page listing the stages of a design project, in order. It feels tidy but heavy. The thick outlines are the loudest thing on the page, louder than the words inside them.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal page or doc where a team checks the stages of a project and what each one involves. It needs to be read and scanned quickly.

**Verdict.** The words and the type work, but the layout gives a false message. The Sketches box is pushed in just enough to look either nested under Research or misplaced. On top of that, every stage is fenced in a near-black frame that outweighs its own text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The Sketches box is out of line, and the viewer can't tell why** (defect). Its left edge sits about 14 px to the right of the other four boxes, and its title and text move over with it. That is too small to read as a choice and too large to miss. A reader takes it one of two ways. Either Sketches is a sub-step of Research, which the copy doesn't support because it's a separate stage with its own output, or the page is broken. Either reading costs trust in a page whose job is to say what happens in what order. An element that leaves the grid has to do it by a clear amount, or it reads as an error (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance"). Repeated elements of one kind also need to share one set of rules (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). Fix: put Sketches back on the shared left edge at x≈160. If it really is a sub-step, make the nesting obvious: a clear indent of a full step, a smaller box, or a label such as "Research → Sketches". Don't leave it at a near miss.
2. **The heavy outlines on every box outweigh the content** (convention). Each stage sits in a dark frame about 3 px thick, so the page reads as five boxed compartments before it reads as five steps. The frames are the darkest, heaviest marks on the page, darker in effect than the bold stage names. The 20 px gaps already separate the stages, so the frames carry no information. They also make static text look like buttons or input fields you could click. Lupton (*Thinking with Type*) says rules and boxes should show relationships, "not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell". Fix: remove the borders and let space do the grouping. If the stages need a container, use a pale tint or a 1 px light-grey rule.

**Smaller things.**
- Title "Project notes": it names a category, not this content. A stranger can't tell which project it is, or that this is an ordered plan. Say what it is, for example "<Project name>: stages, kickoff to handover".
- The stages are a sequence, but nothing shows the order or which stage is current. Numbering them (1–5) would show that order matters without extra chrome. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight and clear: about three sizes (title, stage name, description), with weight doing the work between levels. The bold stage name sitting directly above its one-line description is easy to scan. Keep it.
- The copy is good. Every description starts with a verb and names a concrete output ("Visit three sites and write up what the teams there need", "Draw two rough directions and share them for comment"). The reader knows who does what and when it's done.
- Spacing between the boxes is even, and the column is left-aligned at a sensible width with plenty of open space to its right.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the boxes are clickable. If they are, they need visible hover and focus states. If they aren't, that's one more reason to drop the button-like outlines.
