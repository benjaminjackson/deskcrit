**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, then gets pulled straight into a field of hundreds of jittering green ovals that fight it for attention. It reads as a local plant nursery's homepage. It feels loud, sunny, and busy, the opposite of shade, and nothing shows what they actually sell.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the homepage hero for a small independent nursery that specialises in ferns and shade plants. Its job is to show local gardeners what they sell and get them browsing or visiting this weekend.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The headline promises ferns and shade, but the picture is a generic confetti of bright green ovals that shows no fern, no shade, and no product, and that same pattern undermines the legibility of everything placed on top of it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The picture shows nothing for sale, and the wrong mood (defect).** The full-bleed background is abstract ovals in saturated lime and grass greens. A fern is a frond and a shade garden is dim, cool, and layered. This reads as a sunny lawn or spring confetti. Visitors to a nursery want to see the plants: the image is the product. Right now the page could belong to any green brand, and "Ferns and shade plants" rests on trust alone. Fix: use a real photograph of their stock, ideally fern fronds in low light. It will look cooler and darker, so it matches "shade" and gives the white type a calm ground. If this is a placeholder for a photo that hasn't been commissioned yet, that photo is the most important thing on the page.
2. **The text and button fight the background (defect, WCAG 1.4.3 / 1.4.11).** White text sits on leaves that range from near-black to pale lime. The headline survives on sheer size. The subhead ("Grown on the hill… this weekend.") and the button label at around 16–18px lose letters wherever a light leaf passes behind them, for example under "weekend." and around "Browse". Contrast has to hold at the worst point behind the text, not on average. Squint and the white type merges with the palest leaves. Fix: a darker, quieter area behind the text block (scrim, gradient, or a photo with a shadowed region on the left), then check the subhead against the lightest pixel behind it for 4.5:1.
3. **The one call to action is the weakest thing on the screen (defect).** "Browse the greenhouse" is a ghost button: a thin white outline with no fill, laid over a pattern full of edges. Its border shares the visual language of the ovals around it, so it reads as one more shape rather than the thing to press. This is the page's only action and it should be the high-emphasis one (Material, Refactoring UI). Fix: a solid fill in a colour the background doesn't use, such as a warm cream, or white with dark green text. The button then wins on value and hue, not just on its outline.
4. **The ask is vague where the subhead is good (copy).** "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" is the best line on the page. It is specific, local, and has a deadline. The button then asks the viewer to "browse the greenhouse", which is a category, not a thing. Someone with a dark corner wants to know what will grow there. Fix: point the ask at the viewer's own garden, such as "Find plants for your shady spot" or "See what's ready this weekend". "This weekend" also begs for opening hours or a location near the button, and those are currently only behind "Visit".

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks so that "corner" sits alone on line 3. Rebreak as "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner" or tighten the width.
- The hero text block starts at about x=96 while the logo starts at about x=64, so the two left edges miss by roughly 32px. Put them on one margin.
- The headline's word spacing looks slightly open at this size. Tighten the tracking a touch.
- Saturation sits in the wrong place: the background is the most saturated element, so the content can't get louder than it. Mute the image, or give the CTA the only distinct hue.
- Bright lime against mid green buzzes slightly at the oval edges (Albers). It goes away with a photo.

**What's working.**
- The headline. One clear dominant element at a big size jump over everything else, short enough to take in at a glance. Keep the scale and the bold sans.
- The type system is lean: about four sizes with clear jobs (headline, subhead, logo, nav/button) and one family. Keep it that way when you add hours and location.
- The header: a dark-green wordmark on white, four plain nav labels in the visitor's own words ("Plants, Pots, Care guides, Visit"). It is calm and conventional, and it is the only part of the screen that is easy to read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Button hover and focus states. With a bare outline on a busy ground, check that the focus ring stays visible against the pattern.
- How the hero crops on mobile, where the text would sit over even more of the pattern.
