**First read.** The eye goes to "Changing your payment card", then down the left column through three bold subheads: add, remove, if a payment fails. It reads at once as a help-centre article for someone paying for a SaaS product. It feels calm and trustworthy, and it is easy to scan. The look holds up. The words have gaps in exactly the places a worried reader will go.

**What it's for.** Assumed: a customer who needs to change or remove their card, or whose payment has just failed, should be able to do it right without contacting support.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job. The article is let down by its copy. The failed-payment section, the part read under the most stress, can be read two ways about when the account locks. It also never answers whether a new card pays the bill that just failed. Fix the words before you touch the type.

**What would move it most**
1. **The failed-payment deadline can be read two ways** (defect). "We try again after three days and email the account owner each time. After a third failed try the account moves to read-only…" One reader counts the original charge as the first try, so the lock comes after two retries, about day 6. Another counts three retries, so the lock comes about day 9. "Each time" also suggests several retries, but "after three days" describes only one. This is the one date the reader will plan around and hold you to. Getting it wrong means they lose access a few days earlier than they expected. Fix: give the schedule as dates from the failed charge ("We retry 3 and 6 days later. If the retry on day 6 fails, your account becomes read-only that day"). Say who gets the email by role, and say what read-only stops them doing.
2. **The article doesn't answer the question that brought most people here** (defect, common sense). Someone reading "If a payment fails" wants to add a new card and pay what they owe. The intro says the new card "is used from your next invoice, and nothing already paid is moved". That suggests the overdue invoice stays on the failed card, but it never says so. "Nothing already paid is moved" is also unclear to a stranger: moved where? Refunded? Charged again? Fix: in the failed-payment section, say in one line what happens to the unpaid invoice when they add a card, and how to pay it now if they can. Rewrite the intro line as what the reader gets: "Invoices you've already paid stay on the old card."
3. **The steps make people remember a path and leave a step out** (convention: Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). "Open Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card" is set in plain body text. Nothing marks the interface labels, so the reader has to keep the path in their head while they switch screens. "Remove an old card" depends on "another card is set as the default", and the article never says how to make a card the default. Someone who has just added a card will press Remove, get blocked, and come back confused. Fix: set the interface labels in bold, add a direct link to Billing settings, and add the step for setting a default.

**Smaller things.**
- Subheads float. Each sits about 50 px below the paragraph before it and about 50 px above its own text, so "Remove an old card" belongs to neither. Cut the space below each subhead to about half the space above (Bringhurst 2.2.2: space subheads unevenly, with more above than below).
- The measure runs long. The widest lines ("A card can be removed once another card is set as the default. Select the card, then") are about 84 characters. Narrow the column to about 66 characters (Bringhurst 2.1.2).
- Runt: "days." sits alone on the last line of the hold paragraph. This will change when the measure does. Check it again then.
- There's nothing at the end for a case the article doesn't cover, such as a hold that hasn't been released or a card that keeps failing. Add a contact or next-step line under the last section.
- "After a third failed try the account moves" needs a comma after "try". The run-on makes the deadline sentence harder to read.

**What's working.**
- The subheads name tasks ("Add a new card", "Remove an old card", "If a payment fails"). A reader finds their case in one scan. Keep that wording pattern.
- The type scale is tight. About three sizes (17 / 21 bold / 38 bold), with weight doing part of the work, give a plain hierarchy with nothing competing.
- The £1 hold line is exactly right: an amount, a reason, and a time limit, which heads off a "why was I charged" ticket. Write the failed-payment section to the same standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the column reflows on narrow screens, and whether the breadcrumb links show a focus state.
