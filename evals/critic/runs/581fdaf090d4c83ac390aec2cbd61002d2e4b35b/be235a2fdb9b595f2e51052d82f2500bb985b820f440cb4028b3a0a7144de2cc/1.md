**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the bold black "Before you sign a tenancy", then steps down through three bold subheads: deposit, photos, notice. You get the shape of the page before reading a word. In version 2 the eye still finds the headline, but it lands more weakly. The bold black "Tenant Guide" in the nav competes with it, and the three subheads blend into the grey body text. The page reads as one even grey block rather than three tips. Both feel calm and trustworthy, like a gov.uk-style guidance page.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a short guide page for UK renters about to sign a tenancy. They should skim it, catch the three things to check, and read whichever one applies to them.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The two share layout, copy, sizes and spacing. The only change is that version 2 makes the headings regular weight and the same grey as the body, and that takes away the skim path a checklist page depends on. Version 1 works with little left to fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **The subheads differ from the body only in size (version 2).** "Check the deposit", "Take photos on day one" and "Know your notice period" are set in the same weight and the same slate grey as the paragraphs under them. They are only larger (about 24px against about 17px). If you squint or skim, they don't read as stops, so a renter scanning for "deposit" has to read the lines rather than spot the headings. The H1 has the same problem: as regular-weight grey, it is now less dark than the bold "Tenant Guide" wordmark above it, so the page's own title is outweighed by the nav. This is a convention break (Dair, *Design with Type*, chapters 8a–8g and 9: build hierarchy from more than size), and here it costs the viewer something. Fix: put back a second contrast, either weight or the darker near-black, which is exactly what version 1 does. That is the version to keep. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- Both versions: the body lines run about 80–85 characters ("Your deposit must go … and the landlord"), a bit over Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column to about 640px, or about 66 characters.

**What's working** (version 1).
- Bold near-black headings over regular slate body give a clear three-level hierarchy (title, tip, explanation) without adding any more sizes. Keep that pairing.
- Spacing groups each tip correctly. Each subhead sits about 12px above its paragraph and about 36px below the one before, so every heading clearly belongs to its own text.
- The copy is plain, speaks to the reader as "you", and ends every section on something they can do: ask for the certificate, email the photos, check before you move.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the column reflows on a phone.
