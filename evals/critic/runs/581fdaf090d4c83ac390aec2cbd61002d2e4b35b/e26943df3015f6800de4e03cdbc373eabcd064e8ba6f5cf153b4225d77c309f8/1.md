**First read.** The eye lands on the bold two-line headline, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then drops into the paragraphs. It reads at once as a calm, competent SaaS help article: one column, plain sans serif, blue underlined links, a quiet sidebar. It feels trustworthy and a little bare. When I finished reading, I still didn't know where to click to change my card.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article for an account owner who has come to swap the card that pays for their Ledgerly subscription, often because the old card expired or was declined.

**Verdict.** The visual design is clean and mostly correct. The article fails at its job because it explains the rules around changing a card but never shows how to change it, and its two time-sensitive lines (what happens to the open invoice, and when the account is downgraded) can each be read two ways. The problem is the content, not the styling.

**What would move it most**

1. **The task itself is missing.** The first paragraph says "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page", but "the billing page" isn't linked and there are no steps. Every link on the page goes to a side topic: billing history, roles, paying by invoice, restoring a plan. The reader came to do one thing and leaves without a path to it. It would be a different page if it opened with a short numbered list (go to Billing, choose Payment method, add the card, set it as default) and a direct link to the billing page in that first step. Then the conditions could follow as notes. **Defect.** This page doesn't pass Krug's test from *Don't Make Me Think*: the user can't tell where to click within seconds. Putting the next action in front of the user is also plain common sense for a how-to page.

2. **The open-invoice line leaves the likeliest reader stuck.** "Any invoice that is already open keeps the old card until it is paid." People usually change cards because the old one expired or failed. Read as a stranger, this says the unpaid invoice will keep going to a dead card, and it never says how that invoice gets paid. They will either open a support ticket or assume the new card covers it and get a surprise. The line needs its consequence for the reader: does the open invoice retry on the new card, can they pay it by hand, and where? **Defect** (copy about money the viewer will act on).

3. **The decline schedule and downgrade date read two ways.** "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days" could mean day 3 and day 7 after the first decline, or day 3 and then seven more days (day 10). "If the card is still declined after the second attempt" is worse. If the first charge counts, the second attempt is the day-3 retry. If only retries count, it's the day-7 (or day-10) one. So the reader can't work out when their workspace drops to the free plan, which is the one date they'll hold you to. "Moves to the free plan" also doesn't say what they lose there. State it as dates from the first decline ("on day 3 and day 7; if the day-7 retry fails, your workspace moves to the free plan and you lose X"). **Defect.**

**Smaller things.**
- Left sidebar: "Update a payment card" looks exactly like its four neighbours, so nothing marks the current page. Mark it with one cue, such as a bolder weight or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The same page has three names: "Update a payment card" (sidebar), "Payment methods" (breadcrumb) and "Update the card that pays for your subscription" (H1). Pick one term so readers trust they're in the right place (Nielsen, heuristic 4).
- There's no search box in the header of a help site. Readers who arrive without the right category have no fast way in (Nielsen, heuristic 7).
- The body lines look like they run about 80–85 characters, above Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrowing the text column by roughly 10% would bring it to about 70.
- The H1 breaks as "…for your / subscription", leaving one word on line two. A slightly narrower or wider heading box, or a shorter headline ("Update your payment card", which also fixes the naming), would make it break as a phrase.

**What's working.**
- The type hierarchy is clear and cheap to read. The H1 is far larger than the body, and the H2 "When the new card is declined" differs mainly by weight rather than size, with more space above it than below, so it clearly belongs to its paragraph. Keep that system.
- Inline links are blue and underlined, so they're obvious without relying on colour, and the link text describes where each one goes.
- The layout is restrained: one content column, a quiet sidebar, generous margins, no ornament. Nothing competes with the text, which is right for a help page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus and hover states on the nav, sidebar and inline links, and how the sidebar reflows at narrow widths or 400% zoom.
