**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers along the top and reads them easily: visits, sign-ups and conversion, each up on the previous 12 weeks. Then it drops to the chart, which fills two thirds of the screen, and stops there. Three coloured lines climb gently, but nothing says which channel each line is, what values they reach, or which weeks they cover. It feels clean and trustworthy until you try to use the biggest thing on the page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing or growth analytics overview where someone checks traffic and conversion over the last 12 weeks and sees which channels drive the visits.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job and the bottom half doesn't. The chart that takes up most of the screen can't be read: there is no channel key, no value scale and no dates. "Visits by channel" shows three nameless shapes, so the viewer can't answer the one question the chart exists to answer.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no labels at all** (defect). Nothing in the "Visits by channel" panel says which line is which channel. There is no legend and no names on the lines, so blue, orange and green mean nothing. There are no y-axis values, so you can't tell whether the blue line is 3,000 or 30,000 visits a week, or how far apart the lines are. There are no x-axis dates, so you can't tie the dip around week 8 to anything. The viewer sees that "something went up" and nothing else. That is less than the Visits card above already says in one number. Every chart should have labelled axes with units and a readable scale (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2). Series identified only by colour also fail WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color), and here the colour isn't even explained. The empty strip on the left of the plot and the band under the baseline look like the space where labels were meant to go. If they were dropped by the capture or a failed render, check it in a live browser. If it's the design, it's the main defect on the page. **Fix:** put the channel name at the right end of each line, next to its last point. That beats a legend because the eye doesn't have to look back and forth. Add a few y-axis values with a unit ("visits/week") and week dates along the x-axis. With three lines that never cross, direct labels fit easily and also give colour-blind viewers a second cue beyond hue.

**Smaller things.**
- **Chart title:** "Visits by channel" only names the topic. A title that states the finding, such as "Organic drove most of the +6.1% growth", would tie the chart to the KPI above it (taste: right now the cards and the chart don't connect).
- **Type scale:** I count about six sizes: roughly 13, 14, 15, 17, 24 and 26 px. "Overview" (about 24) and the KPI numbers (about 26) are too close to read as separate levels. The 13/14/15 sizes for the deltas, the card labels and "Last 12 weeks" blur together too. Keep four sizes and use weight or colour for the finer differences (Refactoring UI).
- **"Last 12 weeks", top right:** it reads as plain text, but it is probably the period picker. If you can change it, it needs a signifier: a chevron, a border or a button shape (Norman). If it's fixed, it's fine as a label.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards:** each number has its period and a comparison next to it ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). That is what turns a big number into a judgement (Few). "+0.1 pts" for the conversion rate is exactly right: it avoids the classic confusion between a percentage change and a percentage-point change. Keep that wording.
- **Restraint:** gridlines are pale, the data lines are the strongest marks in the panel, and there's no decoration. Once the labels are back, the chart will read cleanly without other changes.
- **Layout:** one grid and one gutter run through the page. The three cards and the chart share edges, so the screen reads as a single, calm unit.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Controls:** whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its hover and focus states.
- **Hover values:** whether the chart shows values on hover. That would not replace visible labels, but it would soften the problem.
