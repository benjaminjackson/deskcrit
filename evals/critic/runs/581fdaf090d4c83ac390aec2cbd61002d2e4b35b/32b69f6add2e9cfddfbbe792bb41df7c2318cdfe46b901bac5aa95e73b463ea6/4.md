**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers in the top row, then drops to the chart, the largest thing on the page. It reads as a clean, generic SaaS analytics overview for someone checking traffic. The KPI row answers its question in a second. The chart doesn't: you see three lines going up gently and then look for something that tells you what they are, and there is nothing.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the landing view of a web analytics dashboard. A marketer or founder checks it to see how traffic and sign-ups are trending and which channels drive them.

**Verdict.** The top row works. The chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, tells the viewer nothing they can act on. "Visits by channel" has no channel names, no legend, no values and no dates, so the biggest element on the page is decoration.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart can't be read at all** (defect). The "Visits by channel" panel, rows 283–758.
   - **Series:** The three lines are told apart only by colour, and nothing maps the colours to channels. A viewer can see that "blue is biggest" but can't find out whether blue is organic search, paid or referral, which is the whole question the title asks. This breaks WCAG 1.4.1 twice over: colour alone carries meaning, and here it isn't even explained.
   - **Axes:** There is no y-axis scale and no x-axis dates. You can't read how many visits any channel had, whether the gap between orange and green is 500 or 50,000, which week dipped, or whether the bottom rule is zero. A rising line with no scale could be +2% or +200%. Tufte and Few both require labelled axes and units so the reader never has to guess what a mark measures.
   - **Fix:** Name each line at its right end, in its own colour (direct labels, per Tufte and Wilke). Add a light y-scale with a few round values and week or date ticks along the bottom.
   - **Check this first:** There is an empty strip about 70 px wide left of the plot and about 70 px tall below it, the exact space axis labels would take. They may have failed to render, or the screenshot caught the page before they loaded. If so, check it in a live browser. The missing series names are a defect either way.

2. **The chart doesn't answer the question the KPI row raises** (taste). The top row says visits are up 6.1%. The obvious next question is "from where?", and the chart is in the right place to answer it, but its title only names the topic.
   - **Fix:** Have the chart lead with the finding, either in the title or as one annotation, e.g. "Paid search drove most of the +6.1%". Or give each channel its own delta next to its label, matching the KPI cards.
   - **Why it matters:** That one line turns the panel from a picture into an answer, and it's the reason someone opens this page.

3. **No text version of the chart** (defect, WCAG). Nothing outside the image says what the chart shows: no summary, no values, no table. A screen-reader user, or anyone who can't tell the lines apart, gets nothing from the biggest block on the page. Fixing item 1 with direct labels and stating the takeaway (item 2) covers most of this. A "View as table" option covers the rest.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type sizes:** I count about seven: roughly 13, 14, 15, 17, 17, 24 and 26 px. The 13/14/15 group (deltas, card labels, "Last 12 weeks") and the 24/26 pair ("Overview" vs the KPI numbers) are too close to read as different levels. Fold them into one small size, one label size, the heading size and the number size, and use weight or grey for anything finer (Refactoring UI).
- **"Last 12 weeks", top right:** It sits where a date-range control normally goes, but it is styled as plain text. If it's a picker, give it a border and a chevron. If it's only a label, the viewer has no visible way to change the range, which is something a dashboard user will want.
- **Green means two things:** It means "up/good" in the KPI deltas and is also one channel's colour in the chart. That invites a false link between the green line and the positive numbers. Use a hue for that series that isn't one of your status colours.
- **Repeated text:** "vs previous 12 weeks" appears three times. Say it once, next to "Last 12 weeks" or under "Overview", and leave each delta as "+6.1%". The cards get quieter and the numbers stand out more.
- **"Overview" heading:** It tells the viewer nothing the page doesn't already show. Either drop it, or use the space for the date range so the period sits next to the numbers it applies to.

**What's working.**
- **KPI cards:** Each has a label, a big number and a delta against a stated period, exactly what Few asks for. The conversion card correctly uses "pts" instead of "%" for the change in a rate, a detail most dashboards get wrong.
- **Grid and spacing:** Cards and chart panel share one set of edges, there is one even gap between blocks, and the type is quiet and neutral. The page is calm and easy to scan. Keep that restraint when you add labels.
- **Chart drawing:** Gridlines are pale and the lines are the darkest marks, so once the chart is labelled, the data will carry it.
