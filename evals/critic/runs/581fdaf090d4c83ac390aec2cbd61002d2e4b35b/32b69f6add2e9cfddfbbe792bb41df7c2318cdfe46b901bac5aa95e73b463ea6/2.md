**First read.** The eye lands first on the three big numbers across the top and reads them left to right as a quick summary. Then it drops to the large chart and stops there, because three coloured lines climb across it with no names, no values and no dates. It reads as a tidy, generic SaaS analytics overview for a marketing or growth team. It feels calm and competent until you try to use the chart.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a dashboard a growth or marketing lead opens to see how traffic and sign-ups are trending, and which acquisition channel is driving it.

**Verdict.** The top strip works, but it contains a number that contradicts the two beside it. The chart, which takes up about two-thirds of the screen, can't be read: you can't tell which line is which channel, how big any of them is, or which week is which. So the page fails at the one thing its title promises: showing visits by channel.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart can't be decoded.** In the "Visits by channel" panel the blue, orange and green lines have no legend, no direct labels, no y-axis values and no week labels. A viewer can't say which channel is growing, by how much, or when the dip in week 8 happened. The pale gridlines and the empty ~50 px left indent show where the axis labels should be. Without a scale you also can't tell whether the gaps between the lines are honest: green might be a tenth of blue or nine-tenths of it. **Defect**, and also a broken convention: Tufte (*Visual Display*, ch. 2) on clear labelling and graphical integrity, and WCAG 1.4.1, since colour is the only thing separating the series, and here even that colour has no key. Fix: put the channel name at the right-hand end of each line (direct labels, so nobody has to look back and forth to a legend), add a y-axis with visit counts and a few week or date ticks along the bottom, and say whether the axis starts at zero. If anything here is a capture artefact (labels that hadn't rendered yet), check in a live browser. As shown, it's broken.

2. **The conversion-rate delta contradicts its own inputs.** Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups only 2.4%, so conversion must have gone down. Working back, the previous period was about 173,600 visits and 3,820 sign-ups, a rate of about 2.20%, against 2.12% now. That's down about 0.08 points, yet the card says "+0.1 pts" in success green. A growth lead who trusts that card will report an improvement that didn't happen, and anyone who spots the error will stop trusting the rest of the dashboard. **Defect.** Fix: check the calculation. If the rate really is defined some other way (a different denominator or an attribution window), say so in the card label. Show the change to the precision of the figure ("−0.08 pts"), and colour it by direction.

3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** "Visits by channel" tells you what the chart is, not what happened. With labels added, a title like "[Channel] drove most of the 6.1% rise" would give the viewer the answer and let the chart confirm it. Right now the only takeaway on the page is the KPI strip, and one of its three figures is wrong. **Taste**, argued on its own terms: a dashboard viewer has seconds, and a title that states the finding saves them from working it out themselves.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count about seven sizes (roughly 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 24 and 26 px). "Overview" (~24 bold) and the KPI figures (~26 bold) are nearly the same size and weight, so they compete. "Visits by channel" (~17) and the brand name (~18) are too close to read as different levels. Pick four levels and make the KPI numbers clearly the biggest thing on the page.
- Bottom of the chart card: a dead band of ~70 px under the baseline, plus the unlabelled left indent. Space was left for axis labels that never arrived. Either fill it with the labels or tighten the card.
- The plot's left edge (~x 121) doesn't sit on the card's text edge (~x 71). Once y-axis labels are there, align them to the title's left edge.
- "Last 12 weeks" at the top right: if it's a range picker, it looks like plain text. Give it a control shape and a chevron. If it's only a label, fine.
- There's no text summary of the chart for screen-reader or skim users. The takeaway title from item 3 would cover this.

**What's working.**
- Each KPI has the metric name, the figure, and a comparison to the previous period stated in words ("vs previous 12 weeks"), which is exactly what Few asks of a headline number. Keep that pattern, once the conversion figure is right.
- The grid is clean and consistent: equal 20 px gutters, the three cards together match the chart's width, and everything fits on one screen with the key numbers at the top left.
- The chart is restrained: pale gridlines, no frame, no effects. The data lines are the strongest marks on the plot, so once it has labels it will read well.

- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its hover and focus states.
