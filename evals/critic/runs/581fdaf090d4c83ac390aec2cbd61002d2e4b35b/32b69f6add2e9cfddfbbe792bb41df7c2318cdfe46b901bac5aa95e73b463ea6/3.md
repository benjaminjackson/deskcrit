**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top: 184,220, 3,912 and 2.12%. They're clear and calm, and the green deltas say "up". Then it drops into the large chart and stops. Three coloured lines go up and to the right, but nothing says what any of them is, what the values are, or what weeks they cover. It reads as a tidy, generic SaaS analytics overview. It looks finished, but it isn't.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the overview screen of a web analytics product for a marketer or founder. It should show at a glance how traffic and sign-ups are doing over the last 12 weeks, and which channels are driving them.

**Verdict.** The KPI row does its job. The chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, doesn't. It has no series labels, no legend, no y-axis values and no dates, so the only question its title asks ("visits by channel") can't be answered. Until that's fixed, the biggest thing on the page is decoration.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart doesn't say which line is which channel.** (Defect; convention: Tufte, *Visual Display*, on labelling the graphic itself; WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color.) In the "Visits by channel" panel, the blue, orange and green lines have no names, no legend and no key at all. A viewer can see that blue is biggest and orange is choppiest, but not whether blue is organic search, paid or referral, so they can't act on it. Colour is the only thing telling the series apart, and even that leads nowhere. Orange and green are also close in lightness, which makes them harder to separate for someone with red-green colour blindness. Fix: put the channel name at the right-hand end of each line, in the line's colour. There's room, because the lines stop about 15 px short of the panel edge. Direct labels also fix the colour-only problem, since position and name now carry the meaning.

2. **The chart has no scale and no time axis, so you can't read sizes or dates.** (Defect; convention: Tufte, ch. 2 on clear labelling and graphical integrity; Wilke and Few: a line chart may skip zero only if it's labelled.) There are four faint gridlines and a baseline with no numbers, and no week labels along the bottom. The viewer can't tell whether the blue line is 10,000 or 100,000 visits a week. They can't tell whether the baseline is zero, so the gap between lines could be 3× or 10%, and the slopes could be inflated. And they can't tell which week the dip at point 8 happened, so they can't connect it to anything they did. You can see where the labels were meant to go: about 70 px of empty gutter to the left of the plot and an empty band under the baseline. Fix: add visit counts on the y-axis gridlines, start the axis at zero or mark it clearly if it doesn't, and add week-start dates on the x-axis (every second or third week is enough). If those labels exist in the product and just failed to render in this capture, check that in a live browser. As shown, though, the chart can't be read.

3. **The chart is disconnected from the numbers above it.** (Taste, argued.) The headline is 184,220 visits, up 6.1%, but the chart doesn't show the total. Sign-ups and conversion rate, which are arguably what this business cares about, get no trend at all. So the big panel doesn't explain the KPIs: the viewer sees "+6.1%" and can't find which channel produced it. Fix direction: either label the channels with their share of the 184,220 and their own change, or make the chart answer the KPI row (a sign-ups trend, or a selector to switch metrics). A title that states the finding, like "Paid search drove most of the growth", would turn the panel from a topic into an answer.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks" (header, top right) is plain grey text. If it's the date-range control, it needs a chevron or a button shape so it looks clickable. If it's only a label, it sits oddly far from the content it describes.
- "+0.1 pts vs previous 12 weeks" is in the same green as a 6.1% rise. A tenth of a point on 2.12% is about a 5% relative change, which is fine to show, but check whether green "up" is applied automatically even when the change is within noise.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards: a quiet grey label, a big bold number, and a small green comparison that names its period. That's three clear levels from weight and size together, and every number has its context (Few's "bare big number" pitfall avoided). Keep this pattern.
- The restraint in the chart itself: pale gridlines, no frame inside the panel, flat 2 px lines. The data is the darkest ink in the panel. Add labels without adding weight to the scaffolding.
- One consistent grid: the cards and the chart panel share edges and a fixed ~20 px gutter, and the type scale has about five sizes with clear steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether hovering the chart shows a tooltip with the channel, week and value. Even if it does, the static view still needs labels, because a glance shouldn't need a hover.
- Whether the missing axis and series labels are a rendering failure in this capture. Check in a live browser.
