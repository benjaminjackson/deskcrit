**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top, scans them left to right, then drops into the large chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen. That's where reading stops: three coloured lines rise gently, with no channel names, no values and no dates. It reads as a calm, competent SaaS analytics overview, and everything looks fine until you try to use the chart.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an analytics dashboard where a marketer or founder checks traffic and sign-up performance over the last 12 weeks and decides which channels to back.

**Verdict.** This doesn't do its job yet. The chart takes most of the screen and can't be read: it has no legend, no axis values and no dates. And the one KPI that combines the other two seems to contradict them. The layout is clean, but the information isn't there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The conversion card says the opposite of what the other two cards imply.** Visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups rose only 2.4%, so conversion must have fallen. Working back from the cards gives about 2.20% before and 2.12% now, a drop of about 0.08 pts. The card says "+0.1 pts" in success green. A viewer who checks the numbers stops trusting the whole dashboard. One who doesn't comes away thinking conversion improved when it got worse, and that's the number this audience acts on. *Defect.* Check the calculation or the comparison window behind that delta. If it really is a fall, show it as a fall, in a colour that doesn't say "good."
2. **The chart can't be read.** In "Visits by channel" the three lines have no legend or direct labels, so you can't tell which colour is which channel. The y-axis has no values and the x-axis has no weeks. The empty gutter on the left and below looks like room kept for labels that never appeared. Without a scale you can't tell whether blue beats green by 2× or by 5%, or whether the axis starts at zero, so the gap between the lines may be exaggerated. The chart covers roughly two-thirds of the screen and tells the viewer only "three things went up a bit." *Defect* (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2 on clear labelling and graphical integrity; Few). The fix: name each line at its right-hand end in the line's own colour. That also covers colour-blind viewers, so the lines aren't told apart by hue alone (WCAG 1.4.1). Add a few y-axis values with units, and week or date ticks along the x-axis. If the labels exist but didn't render in this capture, check that in a live browser. Either way, this capture is what a slow load looks like to a user.
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** "Visits by channel" makes the viewer work out what the chart means for them. A title like "Organic drove most of the 6.1% rise" (whatever the data says) connects the chart to the KPI above it and tells the viewer where to look. *Taste*, but it's the difference between a report and a dashboard someone acts on.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is plain grey text. If it's the date-range control, give it a chevron or a button outline so it looks like something you can change. If it isn't a control, ignore this.
- The chart has no text alternative, such as a one-line summary or a data table on demand, so screen-reader users and anyone who wants exact numbers get nothing.

**What's working.**
- Each KPI card is built the same way: grey label, big bold number, small delta with an explicit comparison ("vs previous 12 weeks"). The type scale is tight, about five sizes each with a clear job. Keep this pattern.
- Restraint: one grid, equal gutters, light card borders, faint gridlines. Nothing competes with the data, so once the chart has labels it can carry the page.
- The three series colours are distinct, and the lines don't cross, so they stay separable for colour-blind viewers too. With direct labels added, this palette works.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart's axis labels and legend failed to load in this capture or were never designed. Check in a live browser and on a slow connection.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its hover and focus states.
