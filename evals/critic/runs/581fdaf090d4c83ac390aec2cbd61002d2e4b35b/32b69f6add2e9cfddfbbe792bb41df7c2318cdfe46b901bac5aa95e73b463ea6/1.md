**First read.** The eye goes to the three bold numbers along the top, reads them left to right, then drops into the big chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen. There it gets stuck: three coloured lines all rising, with nothing to say what any of them is, what the values are, or which weeks they cover. It reads as a clean, calm, generic SaaS analytics overview for a marketing or growth team. It looks finished, but the main panel tells you nothing beyond "things went up".

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly-glance dashboard where a growth or marketing lead checks traffic, sign-ups and conversion for the last 12 weeks and sees which acquisition channels are driving it.

**Verdict.** The KPI row works. The chart, which is most of the page, does not: it has no channel names, no values and no dates, so it can't answer the one question its title asks. As it stands, the page's main panel is decoration.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't say which line is which channel.** *(Defect.)* The panel is titled "Visits by channel", but the blue, orange and green lines have no labels and no legend. The viewer can see that one channel is biggest and one is the most volatile, but can't tell whether that's Organic, Paid, Referral or anything else. So the chart can't support any decision. Colour is the only thing separating the series, and here it doesn't even map to a name (WCAG 1.4.1). Fix: put each channel's name at the right-hand end of its line, in the line's colour, ideally with the latest value (Tufte: put labels on the graphic itself). Don't add a legend the viewer has to look back and forth to decode.
2. **The chart has no scale and no time axis.** *(Defect.)* There are no y-axis values, no x-axis dates and no units. The viewer can't tell whether blue is 2,000 or 120,000 visits a week, or whether the gap between orange and green is large or small. The space for these labels is clearly reserved, with roughly 50 px empty left of the plot and about 70 px empty below the baseline, but it's blank. Without a scale you can't tell whether the y-axis starts at zero, so the steepness of the lines can't be trusted either (Tufte, graphical integrity: the size of the effect shown should match the data). Fix: add value labels on the y-axis (e.g. 0 / 5k / 10k…), say "visits per week", and add week or date labels along the bottom. If these labels exist in the build and just failed to render, check in a live browser. The design is only fine if they really show up.
3. **The panel title names a topic but says nothing about the data.** *(Taste.)* "Visits by channel" tells you what the chart contains, not what happened. The KPI cards already give the overall result (+6.1% visits), so the chart's job is to explain which channel drove that. A title like "Organic drove most of the 6.1% rise" (whatever the real finding is) gives the viewer the answer in one read, and the chart becomes the evidence for it. Right now the viewer has to do that analysis, and even with labels added, many won't.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Last 12 weeks", top right:** it's plain grey text, so it reads as a label, not a control. If users can change the range, give it a dropdown or button shape with a chevron. If it's fixed, this is fine.
- **Chart plot area alignment:** the plot starts at about x=120, which doesn't line up with the panel title (x≈71) or the card text above. Once the axis labels are in, the labels should sit on the 71 px line, so the indent has a reason.
- **No text summary of the chart:** a screen-reader user, or anyone skimming, gets nothing from this panel. The takeaway title from point 3 would also fix this.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards are done right:** a quiet label, a big number, and below it a small green delta with its comparison period spelled out ("vs previous 12 weeks"). That's exactly the context a bare number lacks (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Keep the pattern. Also keep "+0.1 pts" for conversion, which correctly avoids a misleading relative percentage on a rate.
- **The type scale is restrained:** about five sizes, each with a clear job, and bold weight doing the hierarchy work rather than more sizes.
- **Spacing and the grid are consistent:** equal gutters between cards, one left edge for "Overview", the cards and the chart panel, and pale gridlines that stay behind the data.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover tooltips on the chart (they don't replace visible labels).
- Focus states for the date range control.
- How the three-card row reflows on narrow screens.
