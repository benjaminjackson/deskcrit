**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", runs down through the paragraph and three loose lines, and stops at the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as the top of a small ferry operator's landing page. It feels calm, tidy and plain. Then the empty right half of the frame takes over, and the page starts to look as if its picture failed to load.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the landing hero for a local passenger ferry, meant to tell someone what the crossing is and get them to the timetable.

**Verdict.** It's clean and the headline is good, but it doesn't yet do its job. A would-be rider can't learn where the boat goes, when it next leaves or what it costs without clicking away. Meanwhile, half the screen sits empty and says nothing about the crossing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy holds back what a rider needs to decide.** *Defect.* Neither shore is named anywhere: "the bay" and "the north landing" mean something to the company but not to a visitor. The sailing times are "every forty minutes from early morning until late evening", which is vague: does the first boat leave at 6 or at 8? There is no fare and no next departure. So the reader can't answer "can I make this, and is it worth it?" from the page, and "See the timetable" becomes a click they have to make just to learn the basics, not a next step. Name both landings, state the first and last sailings ("6:40 am to 10:20 pm, every 40 minutes") and the fare. If the page can, show the next departure next to the button. The headline's "twelve minutes" also has nothing to compare against. If the alternative is a long drive around the bay, say so ("twelve minutes across, or forty by road"), so the reader weighs the ferry against a trip they already know. (Putting what the user needs next in front of them is common sense; no approved source covers it.)
2. **The right half of the screen is empty, so the page tips left and feels unfinished.** *Defect, unless an image was meant to load there.* All the content is in one column from about x=160 to x=790. Nothing on the right answers its weight, and the bottom third is empty as well. This space isn't shaped or set against anything; it's what was left over. Arnheim: a heavy side needs a counterweight. Ruder: empty space works only when it stands in tension with something. The page sells a feeling ("calm", "watch the harbour slide past"), yet shows nothing of the harbour, the boat or the deck. If a photo belongs there, make sure it loads, and that's most of the fix. If text-only is the intent, scale the type up so the column fills more of the frame, and make the empty space look like a choice.

**Smaller things.**
- The three-line feature list repeats the paragraph ("bicycle", "open deck"), so it adds reading but no facts. Cut it or replace it with new information, such as fare, journey time or accessibility.
- The type has five sizes. Three of them (paragraph ~20px, list ~18px, button ~17px) are so close that the step looks like a mistake, not a level, and the list doesn't separate from the paragraph (Bringhurst on scales; Dair on weak contrast). A tighter scale keeps three: the headline, body (the list joins it), and one small size for the brand line and button.
- "Coastal Ferry Co." is set in the button's action blue, so the one colour that means "click here" also marks plain text. Set the brand line in a neutral, or give it a real logo.
- This frame looks like the top of the page, but there's no header, logo or navigation, so a visitor has no way to the rest of the site. If a header exists above this capture, ignore this.

**What's working.**
- The headline is concrete and does the selling: "twelve calm minutes" turns a number into a benefit and sets the tone. Keep it as the dominant element.
- There's one filled button, and its label says exactly what happens ("See the timetable"), not "Learn more". Nothing competes with it.
- The paragraph is comfortable to read, at about 70 characters per line with generous line spacing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is a picture that didn't load. Check it in a live browser.
- The button's hover and focus states.
