**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, "Ferns and shade plants for every corner", then gets pulled right into a wall of bright lime ellipses and stays there, because nothing in that half rewards the look. It reads as a home page for a small plant nursery. It feels fresh and competent but generic, like a template with a leaf pattern dropped in where the photograph should be.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home-page hero for a local nursery. It should convince nearby gardeners that this is the place for shade plants and get them browsing or visiting this weekend.

**Verdict.** The type and layout on the left are clean and do their job. The hero fails at the one thing a nursery's hero has to do, which is show the plants. Half the screen is a loud field of random leaf shapes that shows no fern and no shade, and pulls attention away from the headline and the button.

**What would move it most**
1. **The image shows no plants, and it argues against the headline.** This is a defect. The right half, and the background behind everything, is flat confetti made of generated ellipses. People buy plants by looking at them. A gardener comes for fronds, leaf texture, and the shape of a hosta, and gets nothing they could want or recognise. The pattern is also the brightest, sunniest, most saturated green on the page, while the headline promises *shade* plants, so the picture contradicts the words it sits next to. It also hints at the site's quality: with no real plant anywhere, the nursery looks like it has nothing to show. Fix: replace the pattern with a real photograph of the stock. Ferns unfurling in a dim, damp corner would make the headline and the picture one idea. Keep the left third dark enough for the white type, as it is now.
2. **The pattern competes with the content instead of backing it.** This is taste, argued: the leaf field is high in contrast and saturation exactly where there is no content. After the headline, the eye runs into the right half and dead-ends; then it has to come back left to find the button. The brightest colour on the page sits on decoration, and nothing marks the action. Whatever goes in that space should get quieter and lower in contrast toward the edges, or hold a real subject that the eye can rest on.
3. **The one action on the page is the weakest-looking element.** This is a convention: Material Design and Apple HIG both give the main action the most emphasis. "Browse the greenhouse" is a thin white outline on a busy background, styled like a secondary button, and it is lighter in visual weight than the subline above it. It is the only thing to click. Give it a solid fill, for example white with dark green text, so it is the second thing the eye finds after the headline.
4. **The headline could sit on any garden centre's site.** This is about the copy. "For every corner" is filler: it says nothing about what makes Fernhill different, and the reader has to guess at "corner". The subline is the good copy: "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" is specific, local, and timely. Move that idea into the headline, or make the headline name a real corner from the reader's own garden, such as "the damp north side", "under the oak", or "the bit nothing grows in". A gardener who recognises their own problem in the headline will click.

**Smaller things.**
- The logo starts at about x=64 and the hero text at about x=96. A 32 px offset is too small to look intended, so it reads as an error. Put both on one left margin.
- The headline breaks as "for every / corner", leaving "corner" alone on its last line and splitting the phrase. Rebreak it as "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner", or rewrite it per finding 4.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight and clear: headline, subline, button, and nav each have an obvious job. The headline's tight leading holds its three lines together as one block.
- The left third is dark enough that the white text reads easily over the pattern. Keep that dark zone when you swap in a photograph.
- The header is plain and conventional: logo top left, four short nav labels ("Plants, Pots, Care guides, Visit") in the visitor's own words.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button and the nav; how the hero reflows on a phone, where the text would sit directly over the bright part of the pattern.
