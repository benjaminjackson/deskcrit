**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, then to the three prices, then to the blue "Start free trial" on the middle card. This is a calm, standard SaaS pricing table for a small-business accounting or invoicing tool. In version 1 the feature lists look washed out next to the dark prices. In version 2 they read as real content.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a pricing section that helps small-business owners pick a plan and start a trial, with the middle plan as the one to push.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is darker body text, and it fixes a real readability failure in exactly the part buyers compare: what each plan includes. Both versions still leave buyers to guess whether the tiers include each other, and they don't say why the middle plan is the one to pick.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1's descriptions and feature lists are too faint. Version 2 fixes this (defect).** In v1, the description and the four feature lines in every card are a mid grey that looks to be around 3.5–4:1 on white, below the 4.5:1 WCAG 1.4.3 asks for at this roughly 15 px size. These lines are what a buyer reads to choose a plan, and in v1 they sit close to the hairline dividers, so the lists look like supporting detail. In v2 the darker grey looks to clear 4.5:1 and the lists read properly, while the bold, dark prices and titles still rank above them. To confirm, measure the actual hex values of both greys.
2. **Buyers can't tell whether the plans build on each other (defect, both versions).** Only "Growing firm" starts with "Everything in Small team". Small team's list doesn't mention receipt capture or the year-end tax summary, so a sole trader moving up could fairly read it as losing those features. Pricing is where people hold you to your words. Either start Small team with "Everything in Sole trader" or show features in a comparison table.
3. **Nothing says why Small team is the one to pick (taste, both versions).** The middle card is set apart only by its blue button. Its border, size and position match the others, so the page leans toward a choice without stating it. Add a plain label that gives a reason in the buyer's terms, such as "Most popular with businesses that run payroll", or give the card a stronger edge, so the push reads as advice.

**Smaller things.**
- Each card has an empty band about 60 px deep under its button, deeper than the padding above the title. Tighten it to match the top padding.
- The "Talk to us" button on Growing firm doesn't say what happens. Name the action, for example "Book a 15-minute call" or "Get a quote for your locations".

**What's working.**
- The prices are large and bold, and "a month" sits small beside them, so you can compare the three tiers at a glance.
- There is one filled primary button, and the other two are quiet grey. The action hierarchy is clear.
- The subtitle ("Pay monthly, cancel when you like. Prices exclude sales tax.") answers the two questions that stop people before they commit.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Button focus and hover states, and how the three cards stack on a narrow screen.
