**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "Library opening hours" at top left, then moves down a single column of evenly spaced lines. It reads at once as a plain, calm notice for a small public library, maybe a council website. After the title, every line gets the same space, so the page reads as one list of nine lines rather than three branches. And because the times are spelled out in words, you have to read each line in full to find a number.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page where local residents check when, and for the mobile library where, they can visit a branch.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, but it doesn't do its job well as a quick reference. The times are written as words and some are missing, and the even spacing hides where each branch begins. A reader has to read the whole thing to answer "is it open now?" The copy matters more here than any visual point.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hours are hard to scan, and some are missing.** Lines such as "Monday to Friday, nine to eight" and "Saturday, ten to four" spell the times out, so there are no numbers for the eye to catch. They also leave am/pm to the reader. "Nine to eight" is safe in context, but these are times people plan a trip around, and you should not make them work it out. Worse, the mobile library lines ("Wednesday at the market square", "Thursday at the school gates") give a place but no time. A reader can't use them without looking somewhere else, and the two branches give times while the mobile library doesn't. Closed days are never stated: Harbour on Monday, and Sunday everywhere, can only be worked out from what's missing. **Defect** (Nielsen: match the real world, consistency; Krug: the page should explain itself). Fix: write times as numerals with am/pm ("9am–8pm"), give the mobile library its times ("Wed 9:30am–12pm, Market Square"), and say "Closed" on the closed days. A day-by-day table per branch, with days in one column and times in the next, would make all of this obvious at a glance.
2. **The spacing doesn't show the three groups.** Every line, label or not, sits the same distance (about 45 px, by eye) from the one above it. So "Harbour branch" is as close to "Saturday, ten to four" above it as to its own hours below, and each label floats between two groups. Only the bold weight tells you where a branch starts. On a page you scan for one branch, that slows you down. **Convention:** equal gaps everywhere erase the groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on grouping by proximity). For more space above a subhead than below it, see Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, §2.2.2. Fix: tighten the gap between a branch's own lines to roughly the line spacing, and roughly double the space above each branch name. The same gap is spread over the whole page; gather it into three blocks.

**What's working.**
- The type is restrained: one sans serif in three clear levels. The title is large and bold, the branch names are bold, and the hours are regular. The branch names differ from the hours by weight, not by a near-miss size. Keep that when you add spacing.
- High contrast dark grey on white, a comfortable text size, and one firm left edge make it easy to read. Don't decorate it. For a utility page, plain is right.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How it reflows on a phone, which matters because most people check hours on mobile. Check that a table version stays readable at 320 px wide.
