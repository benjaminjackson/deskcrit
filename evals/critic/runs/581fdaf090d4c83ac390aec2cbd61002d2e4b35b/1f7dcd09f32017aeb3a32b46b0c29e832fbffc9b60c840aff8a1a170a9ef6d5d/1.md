**First read.** The eye goes straight to the June bar, which jumps well above the steady climb before it. Then it moves to the serif headline "Savings deposits rose every month" and across to the three big numbers on the right. It reads as a calm, trustworthy credit union report for members: clean, quiet and competent. The first question it raises is "what happened in June?", and the page answers it only in grey 12 px type under the chart.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public half-year report telling members, in a few seconds, how savings deposits went and whether that is good news.

**Verdict.** It is well built. The chart is honest, the numbers add up and the hierarchy is clear. But it tells the wrong story. The loudest thing on the page, June, is mostly a one-off bonus payroll, and the explanation is hidden in the footnote. The headline and all three cards lean on that same month, so a member leaves believing growth was stronger than it was.

**What would move it most**
1. **The June spike is explained only in the footnote.** *Defect.* The June bar ($1,150k) climbs by $282k, about four times any other month's step of $43–79k. It is the focal point of the whole page. "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll" sits in the smallest, palest text at the very bottom, where most readers never get. So the viewer reads the jump as members suddenly saving more. That is a false conclusion, coming from a financial institution, about money. Fix: annotate the June bar itself, for example a short label at its top: "includes mid-year bonus payroll". Better still, if the ledger allows, split June into regular deposits plus a lighter bonus segment, so the true trend is visible. This is common sense rather than a rule from the approved sources: put the key point where the eye already is.

2. **All three cards measure June, the bonus month.** *Defect.* "Average deposit, Jun 2026", "Accounts that made a deposit, Jun 2026" and much of the $4.82M total all depend on the bonus month. Showing one month alone, and the strongest one, looks cherry-picked even if it wasn't. It would be fine only if June 2025 also had a bonus payroll so the comparison is like for like, and the page doesn't say so. Fix: show the half-year figure on every card, or state on the cards that June 2025 also included the bonus. This is my judgment, not a cited rule: the comparison has to be fair before the green "Up" means anything.

3. **The headline makes a claim the chart can't judge.** *Defect.* "Rose every month" is true. But without last year's January to June, the viewer can't tell whether that rise is unusual or just the normal seasonal climb towards a June bonus. The more meaningful fact, up 6.1% on the same half of 2025, is in a card label, not the headline. Fix: let the headline carry the comparison the member actually cares about (for example, deposits up 6.1% on the first half of last year). Or add a pale grey 2025 bar beside each month, so "rose every month" comes with a benchmark.

4. **"Average deposit" names the wrong thing.** *Defect.* $214 × 5,374 accounts ≈ $1.15M, the June total. So $214 is the average deposited per depositing account in June, not the average size of a single deposit, which is what "Average deposit" says to a stranger. A member comparing their own deposits will compare against the wrong number. Fix: say what it is, for example "Average paid in per account, Jun 2026".

**Smaller things.**
- The value labels say "$1150k" and the axis says "$1200k", while the card says "$4.82M". Use one unit style ($1.15M, or $1,150k with a comma).
- The type sizes crowd between about 12 and 16 px: axis, bar labels, card labels, nav, chart title and subtitle. I count five sizes that barely differ. Settle on two small sizes and separate the rest by weight and colour.
- The card stack ends about 75 px above the bottom of the chart panel, leaving a stray hole on the right. Either stretch the cards to match the chart's height, or end the stack on purpose with something that fills it, such as the source line.
- "Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025" reads clumsily. "Up 6.1% on the same months of 2025" or "vs Jan–Jun 2025" reads at once.

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest and quiet. The bars start at zero, there is one blue, values are labelled directly, the gridlines are faint and the source has a cut-off date. Keep all of that.
- Every number comes with a period and a comparison, and "Up" is written in words, not just shown in green. The cards pass the "is this good?" test, so keep that pattern once the periods are fair.
- The serif headline against sans-serif data gives the page one clear entry point and a trustworthy, institutional voice that suits a credit union.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the chart and the card column reflow at narrow widths. With six labelled bars plus three cards, check at 320 px wide that the value labels don't collide.
