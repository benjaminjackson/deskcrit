**First read.** The eye lands on "Account settings," then goes down the stack of four white cards. On the way it catches on the third card, Billing, which sits about 28 px to the right of the others and runs past their right edge. It reads as a calm, standard SaaS settings overview for a workspace admin. It feels tidy apart from that one card, which looks like a layout bug.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a settings overview where a workspace member finds a setting (profile, notifications, billing, sessions) and goes to change it.

**Verdict.** The base is clean and readable, but it doesn't yet show the user where they are. The sidebar says they're on Billing, the page says "Account settings" and lists everything, and the Billing card is shoved off the grid. Together these read as broken rather than finished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The Billing card is out of line.** It's the third card, roughly 28 px right of the stack, with its right edge past the cards above and below. The shift is too small to look intended, so the viewer reads it as a rendering error. That costs trust on the one card that's about money. If it's meant as emphasis, the eye can't tell that from a mistake. A break that small doesn't read as a choice (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I, on ambiguous placement). It also breaks the single column every other block uses (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*) and the one card style the others share (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Fix: put it back on the column. If Billing really needs attention (a renewal coming up), mark it inside the card with a status line or badge, not by moving the box. *Defect.*
2. **The sidebar and the page tell two different stories about where you are.** "Billing" is highlighted in the left nav, but the page title is "Account settings" and shows Profile, Notifications, Billing and Sessions together. The nav also lists Members, Integrations and Security, which have no cards, and the page has a "Sessions" card the nav doesn't name (it's presumably Security). A user can't tell whether they're on the Billing page, an overview, or whether clicking "Security" goes to the same place as "Review" on Sessions. That's a Nielsen 1 failure (the current location is marked but wrong) and a Nielsen 4 failure (two names for one thing), and it gives the user no clear picture of how settings are organised (Norman, *Design of Everyday Things*). Fix: pick one structure. Either this is an overview, so the nav highlights nothing (or an "Overview" item) and the cards cover every nav section in the same order with the same names. Or this is the Billing page, so the heading says Billing and the page shows billing. *Defect.*
3. **The Billing copy stops short of what the user will act on.** "Your plan renews on 1 March. The invoice goes to the address on file." doesn't say which plan, how much, or which address. Those are exactly the things someone checks before a renewal, and later holds you to. "Address on file" makes them remember an address shown somewhere else (Nielsen 6). The button, "View invoices," points backwards at past bills, while the text is about the next one. Fix: say the plan, the amount and the billing email in the line ("Team plan, £240, renews 1 March. We'll email the invoice to accounts@…"), and make the button match what the line raises (manage plan or billing details), with invoices as a secondary link. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Type sizes drift: logo ~18 px bold, card titles ~17 px bold, nav ~15 px, body and buttons ~14 px. Logo and card titles, and nav and body, are a step apart that reads as an accident. Merge to three sizes plus heading, and get the remaining differences from weight and colour (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).

**What's working.**
- Each card is a heading, one plain sentence, and one outlined button on the right. The pattern is simple to scan, and it's right not to promote any one action to a filled primary on an index page like this.
- The descriptions say what the setting does for you ("Your name and photo appear on comments and in the member list"; "You are signed in on three now"). The Sessions line in particular gives a concrete fact that makes "Review" worth clicking. Keep that voice.
- The selected nav state uses a tint block, bold weight and colour together, so it doesn't rely on colour alone. Keep the treatment once it points at the right item.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus visibility and focus order on the four card buttons and the nav. Whether the Billing offset is a hover or animation state caught mid-transition: check in a live browser before treating it as static layout (the fix stands either way if it's static).
