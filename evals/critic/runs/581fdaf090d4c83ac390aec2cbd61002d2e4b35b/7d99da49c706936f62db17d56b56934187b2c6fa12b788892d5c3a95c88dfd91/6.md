**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming this is the FAQ or help section of a small bakery's online pre-order site. Customers scan it for the one question they have, then act on the answer.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The spacing groups each question with its answer correctly, but the questions look exactly like the answers, so a customer can't scan for their question. They have to read everything. Several answers also leave the reader without the exact fact or the link they came for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The questions are indistinguishable from the answers.** *Defect.* "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients" and "Cancelling an order" have the same weight and colour as the answers below them. They are even about a pixel *smaller*. In a squint or in greyscale the section is one uniform grey texture. A customer looking for "cancel" has to read line by line instead of running down the left edge. A heading only a hair different from its text looks like an accident rather than a level (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). When the only difference is a tiny step in size, there is effectively no hierarchy. Make each question obviously different at a glance. A heavier weight (semibold) at the same size or one clear step up is enough for this quiet page. You don't need large display type. Never make the question smaller than the answer.

2. **The answers stop short of what the customer needs, in the places they'll hold you to.** *Defect (copy).*
   - **"Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead."** This can be read as 24 hours before the pickup slot or as any time the day before. Someone who cancels at 9pm for a 10am pickup gets a different result depending on which reading is right. It also never says *how* to cancel. Give the exact cutoff ("until 24 hours before your pickup time") and the action ("Open your order and choose Cancel").
   - **"…show it at the counter along with a name."** Whose name? Yours or the collector's? If the counter expects the orderer's name and the friend gives their own, they leave without the bread. Say which one.
   - **"Every loaf has a card by the till."** That only helps someone already standing in the shop. A customer ordering online who has an allergy can't check before they buy. Either point to the ingredients on the website or say so plainly ("Ask us before you order").
   - **"Open your order"** names a place with no route to it. Link it, and do the same for cancelling (Nielsen heuristic 10, help and documentation: help should take you to the task).

3. **The lines are too long.** *Convention.* The answers run about 820 px wide, which is roughly 95–100 characters ("Give the person your order number. They will need to show it at the counter along with a name."). Bringhurst gives 45–75 characters as comfortable. Over this width the eye loses its place on the return, which matters on a page people skim. Cap the text column at about 65 characters (roughly 600 px at this size). That will also fix most of the awkward wraps.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the second line of the Allergies answer, a one-word runt. Narrowing the measure or rewording will fix it (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- **The spacing.** Each question sits about 10 px above its answer and about 35 px below the previous answer, so the pairs group correctly. Keep this ratio when you add weight to the questions.
- **The restraint.** It uses one sans serif, one text colour, a flush-left rag and plain readable sizes, with nothing decorative. For a help page that's the right register. The fix is to add one level of contrast, not to add style.
- **The voice.** Most answers are short, direct and in the second person, and "the bread has already been baked for you" gives the reason behind the rule in human terms. Keep that tone when you make the answers more exact.
