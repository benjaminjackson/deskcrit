**First read.** The eye lands on the top-left line and then moves down, but nothing stops it. The question lines ("Changing your pickup time") are the same weight and the same near-black as the answers. They also look a pixel or two *smaller*. So the block reads as eight lines of even grey text, not four questions with answers. It's clearly a bakery's order/pickup FAQ, and it feels calm and honest, but flat.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the help/FAQ section of a bakery's online-ordering site, where customers scan for their question and need a firm answer about times, pickup and cancellation.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. You can't scan it because the questions don't stand out from the answers. And the two answers people will hold the bakery to (when they can cancel, and what a collector must show) can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The questions don't look like questions.** All four headings (`Changing your pickup time`, `Collecting for someone else`, `Allergies and ingredients`, `Cancelling an order`) share the answers' face, weight and colour, and are if anything slightly smaller. Someone looking for "can I cancel?" has to read every line to find the one about cancelling. No heading stands out first, and nothing on the page outranks anything else. That's a defect for a page whose whole job is scanning. A difference this small looks like an accident, not a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8, "weak contrast is worse than none"). Fix: make the headings obviously different at a glance. The easiest way here is a clearly heavier weight (semibold) at the body size or one clear step larger, with the answers in a slightly softer grey. Don't rely on size alone (Dair, chs. 8a–8g and 9). The page also has no title, so nothing tells you what it is. A plain "Ordering and pickup" above the list would anchor it.

2. **The cancellation answer and the collection answer can each be read two ways.** These are commitments, so they're defects, not style notes.
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead." Is that 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time the day before? A customer who reads it as "the day before" tries to cancel at 9 pm for a 10 am pickup and gets refused, or charged, for bread they thought they'd cancelled. State the cutoff as a time: "You can cancel until [time] the day before your pickup."
   - "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name." Whose name, the collector's or yours? A friend who gives their own name may be turned away. Say "along with your name" (or theirs), whichever the bakery means.

3. **The answers send people to places the page doesn't show them.**
   - "Open your order and choose a new slot from the list" gives no link and no hint where orders live. The reader has to remember the way to their order themselves (Nielsen, recognition rather than recall). Link "your order" directly.
   - Separately, "Every loaf has a card by the till" only helps someone already standing in the shop. A customer with an allergy who is ordering online is told nothing at the moment they're deciding what to buy. Say where ingredients appear on the order page, or how to ask before ordering.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the answers run about 95–100 characters, e.g. "Orders can be cancelled… baked for you." That's past the comfortable 45–75 (Bringhurst, 2.1.2). Narrow the column to about 640 px at this size.
- Widow: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Narrowing the measure or rewording will likely fix it.

**What's working.**
- The spacing does the grouping well. Each heading sits tight to its answer, with clearly more space before the next pair. Keep that rhythm when you strengthen the headings.
- The voice is plain and human. "After that the bread has already been baked for you" explains the rule in the customer's terms, not as policy. Keep that ending even after you add the exact cutoff.
- One face, one colour, one left edge, nothing decorative. That restraint suits a help page. The fix is contrast inside the system, not more stuff.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The lower ~45% of the frame is empty. It could be the end of the page or a cropped capture. Check in a browser whether anything (contact details, an order link) is meant to sit there.
