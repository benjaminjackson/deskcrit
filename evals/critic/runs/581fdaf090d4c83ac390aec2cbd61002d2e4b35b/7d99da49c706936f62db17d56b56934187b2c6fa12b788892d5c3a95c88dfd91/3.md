**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land, so it starts at "Changing your pickup time" in the top-left and reads down line by line. It reads as an FAQ or help page for a bakery's order-and-collect service. It feels calm and plain, but flat: the headings and the answers look the same, so the page reads as one block of paragraphs rather than four questions with answers. The bottom 45% and the right quarter of the page are empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help section on a bakery's ordering site, for customers who have ordered or are about to order bread for pickup, to answer a specific question quickly.

**Verdict.** The content is close to right, but the page doesn't work as a help page yet. You can't pick out the questions at a glance, because the headings are set almost exactly like the body text. Several answers also leave you unsure about a deadline or about what to do next.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings are set like the body text, so you can't scan for your question.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two headings look like regular grey-black text at about 18 px, with the answers at about 19 px. So the headings are a touch smaller than the text they introduce, in the same weight and colour. At a glance the page is eight similar lines of text, and someone looking for "can I cancel?" has to read the whole page to find it. That defeats the point of a help page. *Defect*, backed by a convention: a difference this small looks like an accident, not a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). The heading also differs from the text in nothing except size, and there it goes the wrong way. Fix: make each heading clearly different from its answer in a way you see at a glance. The usual way is a heavier weight (semibold), with the size the same or one clear step up. Then you can run your eye down the four headings and skip straight to yours.

2. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways.** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead": a day ahead of what? Is it 24 hours before your pickup time, or any time on the day before? Someone with a 9 am pickup who tries to cancel the evening before is right under one reading and wrong under the other. They will hold the bakery to their own reading. That's a *defect* because it's a deadline the customer acts on. The answer also never says *how* to cancel. Fix: state an exact cutoff ("until 6 pm the day before your pickup") and the action ("Open your order and choose Cancel"). The follow-up line, "the bread has already been baked for you", is good: it tells you *why*, in terms that matter to you. Keep it.

3. **The answers name actions but give no way to do them.** "Open your order and choose a new slot from the list": open it where? The answers to "Changing your pickup time" and "Cancelling an order" each describe a task you could do right now, but there's no link to your orders and no button. You have to remember the instruction, leave the page and find the order yourself. *Convention*: show the next step where it's needed and don't make people remember it (Nielsen, heuristics 6 and 10). Fix: link "your order" (or add a "Go to my orders" link) inside those two answers.

4. **The copy leaves gaps a stranger has to fill.**
   - "Along with a name": whose name, yours or theirs? Your friend at the counter can't guess, and if the staff expect the other name they may not hand the order over. Say it outright: "your order number and your name".
   - "Holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" doesn't say what happens after that. Is the bread kept, sold, or donated? Are you refunded? Add the consequence.
   - The allergies answer only helps in the shop. Someone ordering online can't see "a card by the till" before they pay. If people order online, the fix is ingredients on each product, or a phone number or address to ask.

   *Defect* where it touches money or getting your order; taste elsewhere.

5. **The lines are too long to read comfortably.** The answers run about 95–100 characters per line ("Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead. After that…"). That's well past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.2), and the page has room to spare. *Convention*, and it costs something even in short answers because it makes skimming harder. Fix: cap the text column at about 600–640 px at this size.

**Smaller things.**
- There's no page title and no bakery name or logo anywhere in view, so nothing anchors the page or tells you whose help page this is. Add a title such as "Collecting your order" above the first question. It would also give the page the one dominant element it lacks.
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer. Narrowing the column (finding 5) will change the line break; check it afterwards.
- The empty bottom and right areas are just leftover space because the content is short. That's fine for a help page, but if this is a standalone view, bring the column and the space around it into proportion with the page.

**What's working.**
- The spacing between questions is clearly bigger than the gap between each heading and its answer. The four groups separate cleanly. Keep that rhythm when you strengthen the headings.
- The voice is plain and human: "Ask the baker", "the bread has already been baked for you". Short sentences, no jargon, and the "why" comes alongside the rule. Keep that tone while you add the missing details.
- The headings are written as tasks ("Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else") rather than vague labels. People can match them to the question they came with.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the headings are marked up as real headings, so screen-reader users can jump between them.
- Whether the questions are meant to expand and collapse. Nothing on the page suggests they do.
- How the column behaves on a phone.
