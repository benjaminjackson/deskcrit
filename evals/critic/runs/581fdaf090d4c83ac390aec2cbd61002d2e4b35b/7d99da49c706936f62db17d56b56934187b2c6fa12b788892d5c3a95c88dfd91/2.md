**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It enters at "Changing your pickup time" only because that line is top-left, then runs down eight lines of grey text that all look the same. It reads as a bakery's pickup FAQ, apparently for customers who ordered online. It feels calm and plain, and also unfinished: the type fills the top half of the frame, the bottom half is empty, and there's no title saying what the page is.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the help section of a bakery's online ordering flow. A customer with a specific worry (changing the time, sending someone else, allergies, cancelling) should find their question in seconds and leave knowing exactly what to do.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The questions look the same as the answers, so you can't skim the page, and the answers that commit the customer to something (a deadline, what to show at the counter) can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The questions don't look like questions.** In all four blocks, the heading ("Changing your pickup time", "Allergies and ingredients", and so on) has the same weight, colour and face as the answer under it, and looks slightly *smaller* (about 18 px against about 19 px). A reader scanning for their question sees eight similar lines instead of four labels, so they end up reading every answer to find the right one. That's the one thing an FAQ has to avoid. This is a **convention** break with a real cost: a difference this small reads as an accident, not a choice (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8). The type scale has effectively one level. **Fix:** make the headings obviously different at a glance. The cheapest way is a heavier weight (semibold) at the same or one clear step larger size, keeping the answers as they are. Don't fix it with size alone (Dair, ch. 8a–8g, 9; Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).

2. **The lines that commit the customer can be read two ways.** This is a **defect**, because these are the lines people act on and later hold the bakery to.
   - "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name." Whose name? The collector's own, or the name on the order? If the collector gives the wrong one, they could be turned away with your bread behind the counter. **Fix:** say it: "along with your name, as it appears on the order."
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead." Ahead of what, and how far? 24 hours before the pickup slot, or by a set time the day before? A customer who reads it the generous way misses the deadline and pays. It also never says *how* to cancel. **Fix:** give the exact cutoff against the pickup time and name the action ("Cancel from your order page until 24 hours before your pickup time").

3. **The allergy answer sends people to the till after they've already ordered.** "Every loaf has a card by the till" helps someone standing in the shop. The reader of this page ordered online and wants to know *before* they pay. For an allergy question, "find out when you collect" is the wrong answer, and someone with a serious allergy will either not order or will order and hope. This is a **defect**. **Fix:** point to the ingredients and allergens on each product online ("Each loaf's page lists its ingredients and allergens"), and keep the card and the baker as the backup.

4. **The page tells people to do things but gives them nothing to click.** "Open your order and choose a new slot" doesn't say where the order is, and cancelling has no route at all. Someone who came here to act has to go and find the order themselves. This is a **defect** (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should be self-evident). **Fix:** make "your order" a link in the first and last answers, or put a single "View your order" link at the top. A short title ("Collecting your order") would also tell a newcomer what page they're on.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: "Collecting for someone else" and "Cancelling an order" set their answers as single lines of about 95 characters, past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrow the column to about 640 px; at that width the answers wrap to two lines.
- Runt: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer. Rewording or narrowing the column will fix it.
- Empty space: the lower ~45% of the frame and a ~300 px strip on the right are leftover space, not composed space. Once there's a title and the column is narrower, the block will sit as a deliberate shape. If this is just a short page in a tall viewport, ignore this.
- Value: headings and answers are the same dark grey, so a squint or greyscale view shows no levels at all. The weight change in finding 1 fixes this too.

**What's working.**
- The answers are written in short plain sentences that speak to the reader ("baked for you"). The bakery's voice is warm without being cute. Keep it when you rewrite the ambiguous lines.
- The spacing is already doing grouping work: each heading sits closer to its own answer (about 35 px) than to the block above (about 50 px), so the four question-and-answer pairs hold together. Keep these intervals when you change the heading weight.
- The type is calm and readable: one sans serif, a dark grey with good contrast on white, comfortable line spacing. Nothing needs replacing, only differentiating.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the page reflows to one readable column on a phone, which is where most pickup questions will be asked. Check it at 320 px wide.
