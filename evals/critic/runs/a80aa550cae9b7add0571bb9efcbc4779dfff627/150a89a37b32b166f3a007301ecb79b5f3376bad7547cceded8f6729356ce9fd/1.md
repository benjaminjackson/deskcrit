**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the big serif headline, "Hold a real conversation in Italian by summer", then drops to the orange buttons. The chat panel on the right comes third. It reads as a warm, calm landing page for an Italian course aimed at adult beginners. In version 1 the eye stalls at the three identical orange buttons and has to read all three to choose one. In version 2 it goes straight to "Start your first lesson".

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero section of a landing page for Parla, an Italian course. Its job is to get adults who want to speak Italian to start a first lesson.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the buttons. Version 1 shows three equal orange buttons. Version 2 keeps "Start your first lesson" as the one filled button and turns "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" into underlined text links. Now the page asks for one thing. In both versions the biggest remaining gap is the chat panel. It is the page's proof, but it doesn't show the teacher correcting anything, which is the thing that makes this course different.

**What would move it most (for version 2)**
1. **The demo doesn't show the promise** (defect). The body copy promises "a teacher in Bologna who corrects what you say as you say it". The panel on the right shows three chat bubbles where the learner's sentence ("Sono andato al cinema con mia sorella") is already correct. Nothing gets corrected, and nothing says who is speaking. A visitor sees an ordinary chat and has no reason to think this beats any other app. Fix: show one learner mistake and the teacher's inline correction, and label the teacher (a name and a small face). Then the panel proves the claim instead of decorating it.
2. **Dead space inside the panel** (defect). The bottom 40% or so of the beige panel is empty. It looks like content failed to load, or like the conversation stopped. Fix: fill it with the correction exchange from point 1, or shrink the panel to fit what's in it.
3. **The ask doesn't say what it costs or commits you to** (defect, because it is the button the visitor acts on). "Start your first lesson" can be read as "free, starts now, no card" or as "pick a plan and pay". Someone who expected free and lands on a checkout will leave. Fix: add one line under or next to the button, such as "Free, 15 minutes, no card needed", or whatever is actually true.

**Smaller things.**
- "By summer" has no reference point. From November it means seven months; from May it is a stretch. Tie it to a duration ("in six months") or keep it but make sure the date is true when the page runs.
- The nav is a single run of text joined by middle dots ("Courses · Teachers · Prices · Log in"), so "Log in" looks like just another section link. Set it apart.
- The bottom third of the screen below the hero is empty cream. Bring up the next section, or some proof such as the teachers or a price, so visitors have a reason to scroll.

**What's working.**
- **One filled button and two text links** (version 2). Keep this. It gives the visitor one clear next step, and the other options stay available without competing for it (Refactoring UI on giving the primary action the most visual weight).
- **The headline.** It promises a specific result in the visitor's own terms ("hold a real conversation") with a deadline, and the bold serif at this size gives the page one clear place to land.
- **The body copy** is concrete: fifteen minutes a day, a weekly video call, a teacher in Bologna. It says who does what and when.

Better: version 2
