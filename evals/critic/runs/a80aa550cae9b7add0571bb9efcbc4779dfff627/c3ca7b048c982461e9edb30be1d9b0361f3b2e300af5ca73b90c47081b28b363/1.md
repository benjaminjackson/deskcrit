**First read.** My eye goes straight to the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", and then down a single centred column to the blue button. Within two seconds I know it's a ferry, and I know what the button does. It feels calm, tidy and quite empty. There is no boat, no water, no place and no price, so it reads as a template with ferry words in it.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the landing page of a local passenger ferry. Its job is to get people who might cross the bay, such as commuters, cyclists and day-trippers, to plan a trip, starting with the timetable.

**Verdict.** It does the basic job. It's clear what this is and what to click. But it leaves out the facts a person needs to decide to take the ferry: when it runs, what it costs, and where it goes. It also looks like it could belong to any service at all. Missing facts are the bigger problem.

**What would move it most**

1. **The copy points at facts but doesn't give them (defect).** In the paragraph, "From early morning until late evening" makes the reader guess. Is the first boat at 6:00 or 8:00? Is the last one at 19:00 or 23:00? A commuter can't tell if it works for them, and a day-tripper can't tell if they'll get back. "Every forty minutes" is concrete, but "the north landing" names one end and never says where the boat goes. "Cross the bay" doesn't say where you land. There's no fare anywhere, and price is the first thing people compare against driving round or taking the bus.
   - **Fix:** put the real first and last sailings, both landings and the fare in the paragraph or the list. For example: "First boat 6:20, last boat 22:40, from North Landing to Harbour Quay. £3 one way." The button is then a step to more detail, not the only way to find anything out.

2. **The page doesn't show the reason to take the trip (taste).** The headline sells calm, and the paragraph sells "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck". Neither shows up anywhere visually. The page is about 40% blank white below the button, and nothing looks like it belongs to a ferry or to this bay. The cost is that the one feeling that would make someone pick the boat over the bridge is described but never seen, so it could be anyone's product.
   - **Fix:** give that lower area to one strong photo of this boat on this water, or a simple map of the crossing. The map would also answer "from where to where" at a glance.

3. **The three benefits look like a stray second paragraph (defect).** "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" are set centred, unmarked, and almost the same size and colour as the paragraph. They read as more body text, not as a list to scan.
   - They also repeat the paragraph: bicycles and the open deck are both mentioned twice.
   - "Hot drinks on board" is fine. But the strongest point, "Bicycles travel free", is buried among weaker ones.
   - **Fix:** make the list look different from the paragraph. Use icons or markers, a short row of three, or a weight or colour change, and set it apart with more space (Dair: weak contrast is worse than none). Then cut the repeated items from the paragraph, which also makes the paragraph shorter.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count roughly five sizes: the 14 px label, a headline around 52 px, a body around 20 px, the list around 18 px and the button around 18 px. Body and list are only a couple of pixels apart, so they look like a slip rather than two levels. Keep three sizes and separate the list by weight or colour.
- The three-line paragraph is centred, which gives it a ragged left edge, so each line starts somewhere new. Three lines is short enough to get away with, but set it left-aligned or cut it to two lines (Tschichold; Bringhurst 2.1.3).
- "Coastal Ferry Co." is small blue bold text in the same blue as the button. It reads like a link or a category label, not the operator's name. Make it the logo or wordmark, top left, and keep the blue for the button.
- Each headline line is centred on its own, which leaves "calm minutes" as a short second line. That's acceptable, but "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes" would break at a more natural point.
- The space above the list (about 40 px) is close to the space between the headline and the paragraph. Groups need bigger gaps between them than inside them.

**What's working.**
- The headline gives a specific, checkable promise: a number ("twelve") and a feeling ("calm"). It isn't a slogan. Keep it.
- "See the timetable" names exactly what happens when you click. There's one primary action, and the button is clearly a button, with good contrast and a decent size.
- The restraint is right for something sold on calm: one blue accent, one typeface and lots of space. Fill the space with the place, not with more interface.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How it looks on a phone, which is where most people will check ferry times. Confirm the headline wraps cleanly there and the button stays above the fold.
