**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It starts at the top left and reads straight down a single grey block of text. The headings are about the same size and weight as the answers, and a little lighter, so the page doesn't split into questions and answers until you start reading. It reads as a FAQ or help section for a bakery's pickup-ordering service. It feels plain and unfinished, like body text that was never styled.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help section where a customer who already has an order scans for their one question (changing a time, sending someone else, allergies, cancelling) and gets the answer quickly.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. A page like this exists to be scanned, and the headings can't be told apart from the answers. The line at the bottom says "Each heading here stands out clearly." It's wrong, and I'm treating it as content, not evidence. Two answers that customers will act on (the cancellation deadline and what a collector must show) can also be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings don't work as headings.** This is a defect. "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two headings look about 1–2 px smaller than the answers below them, in the same regular weight and nearly the same dark grey. Headings that are slightly smaller than body text read as a mistake, not as a level (Bringhurst on type scales). The result is that a customer looking for "cancel" has to read every line instead of jumping to it. The fix is to separate the levels with weight first, not size (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*: bolder, not bigger). Set the headings semibold or bold at the body size or slightly larger, and keep the answers regular. Two levels are all this page needs.

2. **The spacing doesn't group questions with their answers.** This is a convention. The gap between a heading and its answer (about 10 px) is not much smaller than the gap between one Q&A pair and the next (about 25–30 px). So the four pairs don't read as four separate units, and that adds to problem 1. Make the gap between pairs clearly larger, roughly 2–3× the gap between heading and answer, so each heading sits tight to the answer it introduces.

3. **"Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" can be read two ways, and customers act on it.** This is a defect. "A day ahead" could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot or by the end of the previous day. A customer who reads it the wrong way finds they can't cancel and pays for bread they don't want. State the exact cutoff, for example "You can cancel until 6 pm the day before your pickup," and say what happens after it: is there no refund, or does the button just go away?

4. **"Collecting for someone else" leaves the collector guessing.** This is a defect. "Show it at the counter along with a name": whose name, theirs or yours? Does the order number need to be on a phone screen, or is saying it enough? Getting it wrong means a person stuck at the counter without the bread. Spell it out: "They'll need your order number and your name."

5. **The allergy answer sends people to the shop for information they need before ordering.** This is a defect, and here the copy itself is the problem. "Every loaf has a card by the till" only helps someone who is already standing in the shop. Someone ordering online for pickup can't check ingredients before committing. If the information exists, link to it from each product. If it doesn't, say plainly that the customer should ask before ordering, and how to ask.

**Smaller things**
- Line length: the longer answers ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") run to about 85–95 characters, past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrow the text column to about 600–640 px.
- The text block is pinned to the left at x≈160 with a lot of empty space on the right and below. That's fine for a help column, but the page has no title ("Pickup help" or similar), so nothing tells you where you are.
- "Choose a new slot from the list" assumes the customer knows where the list is. Name the button or link ("Open your order and tap *Change time*").
- The self-describing line at the bottom ("Each heading here stands out clearly…") should go. It's placeholder copy, and it isn't true.

**What's working**
- The headings are written as the task the customer has in mind ("Changing your pickup time", "Cancelling an order"), not as category labels. Keep that wording when you restyle them.
- The answers are short, in the second person, and mostly plain. "The bread has already been baked for you" gives the reason behind the rule in a friendly way. Keep that voice.
- The typeface is clean and comfortable at body size. The problem is how it's set, not the face.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether this is a static list or an accordion with open/close states. If it's an accordion, the headings also need to look tappable, with a chevron or similar marker.
