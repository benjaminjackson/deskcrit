**First read.** The eye goes to "Crossing the river" at top left, then down the left column through "Early boats" and "The market quay". Then it jumps to the right column, where the text starts noticeably higher than the left column's text. It reads as a short, calm piece of local writing about a ferry, set in two columns like a booklet page. It feels quiet and orderly, but the right column looks slightly out of place.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page of a printed or on-screen booklet or magazine, meant to be read straight through, top to bottom and left to right.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The text is easy to read and the structure is clear. The one real flaw is that the two columns don't share a grid: the right column starts at a different height and its lines fall between the left column's lines, so the page looks put together by eye rather than built.

**What would move it most**
1. **The columns don't line up across the gap (convention, with a small real cost).**
   - **Where it starts:** The right column's first line ("Stallholders arrive…", around y≈127) is level with the "Early boats" heading, not with the first line of text under it (around y≈167).
   - **What follows:** Every line in the right column then sits about one and a half lines higher than its neighbour on the left. Across the gap, each line on one side is level with the space between two lines on the other, so no row reads straight across. "Going home" also floats free of any line on the left.
   - **What the reader experiences:** The text in the right column continues "The market quay" from the bottom of the left column. When the reader finishes the left column and looks up, they land on text that sits at a different height from where the left column's text started. It looks like a separate block, not the next paragraph.
   - **Source:** Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, "Construction of the grid": each column holds a set number of lines, with the first line at the top of the field.
   - **Fix:**
     - Start both columns on the same first line, below the space the subhead takes on the left.
     - Put the column text, the headings, and the space around the headings on one shared line spacing, so every subhead takes up a whole number of lines.

**Smaller things.**
- **Uneven column ends:** The left column ends near y≈508 and the right near y≈441, with about a third of the page empty below them. On a printed page this looks unfinished. Either even the columns out or make the empty space look intended, for example with a picture or a running footer.
- **Title vs. subheads:** The title is only about 1.6 times the size of the subheads. It's enough, but a bigger step would make the page title clearly a level above the section names.

**What's working.**
- **Type sizes:** There are three clear sizes (title, subheads, body), and the subheads are bold rather than another size. Keep that discipline.
- **Paragraphs:** New paragraphs get a first-line indent with no space between them, and the first paragraph after each heading isn't indented. That's correct and makes the text read as one continuous piece.
- **Line length and spacing:** The lines look about 60–65 characters long with generous line spacing, close to the ideal (Bringhurst). The space between the columns is wide enough that the eye never jumps across by mistake.
