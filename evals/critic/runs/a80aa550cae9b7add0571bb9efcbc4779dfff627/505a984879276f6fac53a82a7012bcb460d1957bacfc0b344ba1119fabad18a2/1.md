**First read.** My eye goes to the bold headline, then down a plain column of three short blocks, and stops at the grey box, the only shape on the page. It reads as a page of service information for a local ferry, for people who need to know when to turn up. It feels calm and tidy but bare, a bit like a default template. Most of the screen below the box is empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page telling riders when the winter ferry runs, when it might be held, and what vehicles pay, read on the way to the landing or before setting off.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, but it fails the job of a timetable. The times a rider acts on are buried in long sentences and spelled out in words. The one bit of emphasis on the page goes to fares, not to the times or the ice warning.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule is prose when it should be data (defect).** In the first paragraph, "every forty minutes", "seven in the morning" and "six in the evening" are written out in words inside a 100-plus-character line. Someone checking at the landing has to read the whole sentence to pull out three numbers, and numerals are what people scan for. The copy also leaves gaps a stranger can't fill. It gives departures from the north landing only, so what about the return from the south? And is "the last at six" the last departure or the last arrival? Get that wrong and someone misses the last boat in the dark. Fix: pull the times into a short block of numerals: first boat 07:00, last boat 18:00, every 40 minutes, with the side of the river named for each. Then add the return-side times, or say plainly that they match. Make that block the first thing under the headline (Krug: make the page scannable; Nielsen Norman Group: people scan the web, they don't read it).
2. **The box highlights the wrong thing (defect).** The tinted box is the only emphasis on the page, and it holds fares. That's the least urgent content here. The paragraph that can actually change someone's trip, "the crew may hold the boat… Check the board before you queue", is plain text that looks the same as everything around it. Fix: move the emphasis to the ice-delay notice, or to the schedule block if you build one, and let fares sit as ordinary text under a small heading.
3. **The lines are too long (convention).** Body text runs about 100 to 105 characters per line across a column about 880 px wide, well over the 45 to 75 range (Bringhurst). On text this short the cost is small, but the eye has to travel far back to start each line. Fix: narrow the column to about 640 to 680 px at this size, or split the content into headed chunks, which point 1 already does.

**Smaller things.**
- The grey box has uneven padding: about 28 px above the text and about 48 px below it. The text sits high, so the box looks unfinished. Make top and bottom equal.
- The fares copy leaves questions a rider would act on. "Standard fare" has no amount. "Free on weekdays" leaves weekend cyclists guessing. "Vans over two metres high" should be written "over 2 m". Give the fare and say what bikes pay at weekends.
- "Check the board at the ticket hut" sends the reader somewhere else for live status. If the page can show the current status or link to it, it should.
- The page has three type levels with no subheads, so there's no way to jump to times, delays or fares. Add two or three short subheads.

**What's working.**
- The headline is plain and specific, and it has a clear step in size and weight over the body text. Keep that one dominant level.
- The body text is dark on white, set flush left in a sensible sans at a comfortable size with good line spacing. It's easy to read.
- The ice paragraph tells the reader what to do ("check the board before you queue"), not just what might happen. Keep that wording when you move the emphasis onto it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the column reflows on a phone, which is likely where riders read this.
- Whether the empty lower half of the screen is the end of the page or content that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
