**First read.** My eye goes to the headline, then to the blue "Save changes" button at bottom right. The question is clear and the safe answer is easy to find. Then the eye drifts left over a row of four choices. Two of them are identical blue text links, and one of those throws your work away. It reads as a calm, tidy confirmation dialog in a calendar or community app.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a confirmation dialog in a shared-calendar app. The person editing has to decide whether to publish their changes to followers, keep working, or drop them, without doing the wrong thing by accident.

**Verdict.** The question and the main action are well made. The weak spot is the one action that can't be undone. "Discard changes" looks exactly like a harmless export link and sits right next to "Keep editing", so nothing tells you that this choice deletes your work.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" is styled like the harmless option next to it.** (Defect; also a convention: Apple HIG "Alerts", Material Design "Dialogs".) In the button row, "Export as file" and "Discard changes" share the same blue link style, size and weight. Nothing marks one as safe and the other as destructive. The viewer has to read each label to tell them apart. Someone who wants out of the dialog quickly might click "Discard" when they meant "Keep editing". "Discard" sits only about 40 px from that button, and both are kinds of "not now". A wrong click silently loses the choir schedule edits. The convention: a destructive action should be set apart from safe ones and styled as destructive, often in red. **Fix:** give "Discard changes" a destructive treatment, such as red text or a red outline. Move it away from the safe group, usually to the far left of the row on its own, with clear space between it and "Keep editing". Keep it less prominent than "Save". Where you can, replace the confirm-before-discard pattern with an undo after discarding (Raskin, *Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo*).

2. **Four choices where the question needs three.** (Defect.) "Export as file" doesn't answer "Save changes?". It's a different task placed among the answers. It adds a fourth item to read before the viewer can act. It's also the first thing on the left, so anyone scanning left to right meets it first. It makes the confusion in point 1 worse by giving "Discard" a lookalike. **Fix:** remove "Export" from this dialog and put it in the editor's menu. If it has to stay, move it out of the action row, for example to a quiet link below the body text.

**Smaller things.**
- "Export as file" and "Discard changes" have no underline or shape, so they show they're clickable only through their blue colour (convention: links need more than colour alone). This mostly goes away once point 2 is fixed. Otherwise give them a shape like "Keep editing" has.
- The "Keep editing" border is mid-grey on white. Check that it reaches 3:1 non-text contrast (WCAG 1.4.11). It looks close to the limit.
- The body text could say what happens on the other side: "…will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar" is clear. Adding "and they'll be notified", if that's true, would let the viewer weigh the decision properly. It's only worth adding if notifications are actually sent.

**What's working.**
- The headline asks a real question and names what's at stake: "the shared calendar". The body text then says exactly who will see the change. That's the stranger-proof copy a confirmation needs. Keep it.
- "Save changes" is the one filled button, bold and on the right, and its label repeats the verb from the headline. The main path needs no thought.
- The type scale is tight: one headline size, one body size, one button size. All the weight differences come from bold and fill, not extra sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Which button has default focus and what Enter or Escape do. Focus must not start on "Discard changes", and Escape should mean "Keep editing", not discard.
