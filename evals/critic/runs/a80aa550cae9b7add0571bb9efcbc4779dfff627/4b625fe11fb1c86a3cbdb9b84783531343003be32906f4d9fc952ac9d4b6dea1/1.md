It reads at once as a small, tidy plant shop showing a curated category page to someone furnishing a dim room. It feels calm, clean and trustworthy, and also a little blank.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an e-commerce category page that should help a shopper with a dim room pick one of four plants and add it to their cart.

**Verdict.** The page is competent and easy to use, but it doesn't help anyone choose. It promises plants for low light, then gives the shopper nothing that tells the four apart on that point, and pictures that don't show what each plant looks like. So the choice falls to price or guesswork.

**What would move it most**

1. **The cards don't answer the question the page raises.** *Defect.* The subtitle sets up one specific need: "do well more than six feet from a window". Every card then gives only pot size and height ("6 in pot, grows to 2 ft"). There's nothing about how much darkness each plant tolerates, how often it needs water, or whether it's safe for pets, which is what someone choosing a plant for a dim corner is weighing. A shopper comparing Snake plant ($24) with Cast iron plant ($38) can't see what the extra $14 buys, so the cheapest one wins by default or they leave to research elsewhere. The fix is to give each card one line that sets that plant apart, said in the shopper's terms ("Fine in a north-facing room; water every 3 weeks"), and keep it in the same spot on every card so the four can be compared at a glance (Krug: don't make the viewer do the work).

2. **The pictures don't show the plants.** *Defect.* All four illustrations use the same clay pot and two or three similar rounded leaves. A real snake plant has stiff upright blades, but here it's drawn with soft round leaves. Pothos is a trailing vine ("trails to 6 ft"), but here it's drawn as two upright leaves. Swap the names and nobody would notice. In a shop the picture is how the shopper recognises a plant and pictures it in their room, so here they're buying blind. The fix is photos, or illustrations that show each plant's real shape (blades, vine, broad leaves). Keep the shared tinted tile and framing, which do hold the set together.

3. **"6 in pot" can be read two ways, right above the price.** *Defect.* "In" reads as the preposition first, so "6 in pot" can mean six plants in a pot. Read that way, $18 looks like a different deal. This line sits next to a price the shopper commits to, so the ambiguity matters. Write "6-inch pot" or "6″ pot".

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count about six sizes. The plant name (bold, about 18 px) and the price (bold, about 20 px) are nearly the same size and weight, so neither clearly leads on the card. Make the name and price differ by weight or colour rather than a couple of pixels. The subtitle and the grey spec line are also only about a point apart.
- The bottom quarter of the screen is empty after the cards, with no way forward for someone not ready to buy. A link to the matching care guide, or to "Pots that fit a 6″ plant", would use that space.
- The grey spec text and subtitle look light at that small size. Measure them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- The subtitle is concrete: "more than six feet from a window" is a test the shopper can apply to their own room. Keep this voice and carry it onto the cards.
- The card layout is strict and the same every time: equal gutters, the same order inside each card, the image tile matching the card width. That makes the four easy to scan, and it means the comparison line from finding 1 will slot in cleanly.
- The palette is restrained: one teal used only on buttons, with the green and terracotta kept to the pictures. Action and content never get confused, so keep teal reserved for actions.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Button hover and focus states. What "Add to cart" does after a click (whether "Cart (0)" updates visibly). How the four cards reflow on a phone.
