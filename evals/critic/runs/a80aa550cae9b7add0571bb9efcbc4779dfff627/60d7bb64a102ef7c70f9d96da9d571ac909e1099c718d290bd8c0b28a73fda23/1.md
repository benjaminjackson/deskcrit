**First read.** The eye goes to "Library opening hours" at top left, then runs down a column of nine lines that are all the same distance apart. It reads as a plain web page or CMS block, calm and sober. The bold branch names help a little. But the even rhythm turns the page into one list, not three branches. To find "is Harbour open Monday?" I have to read every line, and the times are written in words, so nothing jumps out.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a library web page where a local resident checks quickly when, and which, branch is open.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well. The content is nearly all there, but the times are spelled out as words, some of them can be read two ways, and the even spacing hides the three groups. A lookup that should take one glance takes a full read.

**What would move it most**

1. **Times written as words are slow to read and can be misread.** *Defect.* Lines like "Monday to Friday, nine to eight" and "Tuesday to Friday, ten to six" are the facts a reader acts on, so they are the part that has to be exact. "Ten to six" is also the everyday way to say 5:50. "Nine to eight" can be read the same way, and it gives no am or pm. Someone who reads "ten to six" as a closing time of 5:50, or wonders whether the main branch closes at 8 am or 8 pm, either comes early or doesn't come. Numerals are also what people scan for: in a column of words nothing stands out, while "9:00–20:00" can be spotted from across the page. Fix: write the times as numbers with an en dash, in one format used everywhere (for example "9 am – 8 pm"). Better still, put the day ranges and the times in two aligned columns so the hours line up down the page.

2. **The even spacing hides the three groups.** *Convention (Arnheim, Art and Visual Perception, ch. II, proximity).* Every line, including each branch heading, sits the same distance from the line above and the line below. "Harbour branch" is as close to "Saturday, ten to four" (the main branch) as it is to its own hours. The only thing showing the groups is the bold heading, so the reader has to work out where one branch ends and the next begins. Fix: tighten the space inside each group, so a heading sits close to its own lines and the lines sit close together. Then open a clearly larger gap between branches, about twice the space inside a group. That alone will make the page read as three blocks.

3. **It leaves out what the reader most needs to know.** *Defect.* Sunday isn't mentioned for any branch, and Monday isn't mentioned for Harbour. The reader has to guess that missing days mean closed. The mobile library gives a day and a place but no time, so nobody can actually get there for it. Fix: say "Closed" on the days a branch is closed, give the mobile library its times, and if the hours change on public holidays, say so in one line.

**Smaller things.**
- I count three sizes and weights here: the heading, the bold branch names, and the regular lines. That's a sound scale, so keep it. The branch names sit close in size to the lines below them, though, so with the spacing fixed they could stand out more.
- Branch names carry no address. A visitor can't tell where "Harbour branch" is. Add a short street line, or a map link.
- The block takes up the left third and the right two-thirds are empty. That's fine on a web page, but if this is a sign or a printout, the content should be set for that format.

**What's working.**
- The type is plain and steady: one clean sans-serif with dark text on white, and good contrast throughout. Keep it.
- The heading says exactly what the page is, and the branch-first structure matches how people search for hours. Keep that order.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How it reflows on a phone. Most people will check hours on mobile, so look at it at a 320 px width once the times are in columns.
