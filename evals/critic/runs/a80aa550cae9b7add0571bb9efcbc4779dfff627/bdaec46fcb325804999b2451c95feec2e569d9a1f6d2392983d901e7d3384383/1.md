**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the big "Recent work" heading, drops to the wide library tile, then moves right across the two narrower tiles. It reads as a calm, confident architecture practice homepage with a lead project. In version 2 the eye has nowhere obvious to land. It slides along three identical tiles, and "Recent work" is so small it reads as one more caption.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the portfolio landing page of a small architecture studio. Its job is to show prospective clients the studio's best recent buildings and get them to open a project.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. Version 2 took out the two things that gave the page a hierarchy: a heading that clearly outranks everything else, and one project that leads. What's left is an even grid where nothing is more important than anything else.

**What changed, and what it costs**
1. **The heading lost its rank (defect).** In version 1, "Recent work" is set very large and light, roughly 50 px, against project titles of about 22 px. It's clearly the page title. In version 2 it shrank to about the same size as the project titles and sits right next to them. That leaves the page with two text levels that look the same size but do different jobs, so the heading reads like a stray caption rather than a section title. Sizes that close read as a mistake (Bringhurst). Version 1 has a clean four-step scale: heading, title, nav/wordmark, metadata. Version 2 has three steps, and the top two collide.
2. **No lead project (defect against the job).** Version 1 gives the library about twice the width of the other two tiles. It's also the largest and newest project, the one with an area figure. That gives the viewer a focal point and says "this is our flagship." Version 2's three equal squares make the 2,400 m² public library look as important as a canopy. A practice showing its portfolio should be able to say which building it's proudest of, and version 2 gives that up.
3. **Smaller side effect, in version 2's favour.** Equal columns give "Two houses on Weir Road" room. In version 1 that title fills almost the whole narrow column, with no spare width. This is minor and doesn't make up for the lost hierarchy.

**Remaining findings for version 1**
1. **Empty bottom of the screen (taste, borderline defect).** Below the metadata, about the bottom quarter of the viewport is blank. Nothing tells the viewer there are more projects, and nothing links onward ("All projects"). If these three are the whole list, the page feels unfinished. If they aren't, visitors may think they are. Either let the grid continue past the fold or add a clear link to the full projects list under the row.

**Smaller things.**
- Narrow column titles: "Two houses on Weir Road" fills the full tile width. Check that longer titles wrap cleanly, or give the two narrow tiles a little more width.
- Nav: "Projects · Studio · Contact" are small, run together with middots, and look like a tight row of targets. Check that each is at least 24×24 px to click (WCAG 2.5.8).

**What's working.**
- Version 1's very large, light heading over a small, heavy wordmark. The contrast in size and weight is confident and fits an architecture studio. Keep it.
- The two-line captions: a plain-language title, then type, area, and year in a quieter grey. A client gets what kind of building it is and when it was finished at a glance.
- A restrained palette and margins that align on one left edge, from the wordmark through the heading to the tiles.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The tiles are flat colour fills, which look like placeholders or images that hadn't loaded. Check in a live browser how real photography changes the balance, especially whether the wide lead image still dominates in version 1.

Better: version 1
