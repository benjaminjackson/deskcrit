**First read.** The eye goes to the big blue headline, then straight down to the four blue-outlined cards, which have about as much pull as the headline. It reads as a scheduling SaaS landing page, clean and competent. It also feels like a template: the whole right half of the hero is empty, and almost everything is the same blue, so nothing beyond the headline stands out.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the hero of a marketing homepage for a booking-link product, meant to get small appointment-based businesses (the dentists, tutors, physios and vets named at the bottom) to create a link or try the live example.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and readable, but it doesn't sell. The hero has no picture of the product and uses words any scheduling tool could use, so a visitor can't see what they'd get or why this tool beats the one they already have. Using one blue for everything also flattens the page and blurs which things you can click.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero shows nothing and says nothing only Slotwise could say.** The right half of the hero, beside the headline (roughly x 680–1216, y 100–380), is empty space. Nothing on the page shows what a booking link looks like to the person booking. "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "keep every meeting in the right place" could sit on any competitor's page. A stranger reading "in the right place" has to guess which place: your calendar, the right staff member, the right room? The customer row shows who the real audience is: clinics, tutors and vets, who think in appointments and no-shows. The hero talks about "meetings" instead. *Defect:* visitors can't tell what's different here, so nothing gives them a reason to switch. **Fix:** use the empty right half to show the booking page as a client sees it, ideally a real one from a named customer, so visitors can compare it with how their own patients book now. Rewrite the subhead in the audience's words and end it on their gain, e.g. who books, into whose diary, and what they stop having to do. "See a live example" is already your strongest ask because it lets visitors judge for themselves. Give it a picture to point at.

2. **One blue does every job, so it stops meaning "you can click this."** The headline, nav links, card titles, card borders, icons, badges and both buttons are all the same saturated blue. Meanwhile the real links in the cards ("Learn more") are dark grey with an underline. The blue card titles look like links and the actual links don't. The four heavy blue 2px card borders also compete with the headline, which is why the eye jumps to the cards so quickly. *Convention:* keep the accent colour for actions and keep surrounding content neutral (Refactoring UI; Nielsen heuristic 4, consistency). **Fix:** make the headline and card titles dark ink, give the card borders a light grey hairline or a pale tint (or drop them), and keep blue for buttons, links and maybe the icons. The headline then wins on size and weight, and blue means "act."

3. **The proof row doesn't look like proof.** Below the thin blue rule (y≈740), "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" are plain small grey words with no label. They read as footer links or a sitemap, not as customers who rely on you. This is the one place the page speaks to the real audience, and it's the quietest element on the screen. *Taste, with a conversion cost:* people looking for reassurance skim past it. **Fix:** label it (e.g. "Clinics and tutors booking with Slotwise"), use their logos if you have them, and add one concrete outcome or quote, such as a drop in no-shows, if it's true.

**Smaller things.**
- Card badges mean different things in the same pill style. "New" and "Beta" are status labels, "Auto" and "Email" describe the feature. Keep pills for status and fold the descriptions into the copy.
- In the first card, the "New" badge is pushed to the right edge, so "Shared calendars" wraps onto two lines. That card's body and its "Learn more" sit about one line lower than the other three, which breaks the row. Put the badge inline as in the other cards, or let it sit under the title.
- The four identical "Learn more" links don't say where they go (WCAG link purpose). Use "How team routing works", "How reminders work" and so on.
- The icons mix styles: the calendar is an outline and the clock, envelope and layout icons are filled. Pick one.
- "Start free" in the nav and "Create your link" in the hero are probably the same action with two different names. Use one label.
- The headline breaks as "fill your / calendar", leaving one word on line two. A slightly wider measure or a different break would hold it together better.

**What's working.**
- One strong left edge (x≈64) runs through the logo, headline, subhead, buttons, cards and rule. The page feels ordered without trying. Keep it.
- The actions are ranked correctly: one filled primary button and a quieter underlined secondary link.
- The card copy is concrete and lands on the reader: "so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows", "so the first minutes are useful". The hero copy should be held to the same standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the whole card is clickable or only "Learn more" (this changes how much the blue borders mislead), and what sits below the fold under the customer row.
