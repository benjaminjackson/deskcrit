**First read.** The eye goes to the big blue headline and reads it easily. Then it drops straight to the row of four blue-outlined cards, which pull harder than the "Create your link" button above them. It reads as a clean scheduling SaaS landing page, tidy and calm. It also feels like it could be any scheduling tool. The right half of the hero is empty, so the page never shows the product.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a marketing homepage for a booking-link tool, meant to get small appointment-based businesses to create a link or try the live example. The customer strip (dental, tutoring, physio, coaching, vets) points to that audience.

**Verdict.** It's competent and readable, but it doesn't stand out. Almost everything is the same saturated blue, so after the headline nothing leads. The copy never says who this is for or what makes it different from the scheduling tool they've already heard of.

**What would move it most**

1. **One blue does every job, so the main action doesn't stand out** (defect). The headline, nav links, icons, card titles, badges, all four "Learn more" links, the divider rule, both buttons and the 2px card borders are all the same strong blue. The four outlined cards are the biggest blue shapes on the screen, so together they outweigh the one filled "Create your link" button. Blue therefore can't tell the viewer "click here". The headline is in link colour, and a card border means the same as a button. Saturated colour should mark what matters, with everything else neutral (Refactoring UI). Keep blue for the things you can act on: the two buttons and the links. Set the headline and card titles in the dark ink colour. Give the cards a light grey border or tint, or no border at all. The primary button will then be the only solid blue block, and the eye will go headline, button, cards.

2. **The copy could sit on any scheduling product's page** (defect, copy). "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "keep every meeting in the right place" describe the whole category. A stranger also has to guess what "the right place" means: their calendar, the right staff member, the right room? The customer list says the buyer is a clinic, tutor or vet. The hero talks to nobody in particular, so those buyers can't see why this one is for them. Name the viewer and the thing they do today: phone tag, email back-and-forth, a front desk taking calls. Then land the claim on them: what they stop doing, or what they get back. "See a live example" is the strongest ask on the page because it lets the viewer judge for themselves. Make it more concrete, for example "See a clinic's booking page". Show the guest-side booking page in that empty right half, so the value is visible on terms the viewer already knows.

3. **The card row breaks on its first card, and the badges mean different things** (defect, craft). On "Shared calendars", the "New" badge is pushed to the far right. That forces the title onto two lines and drops its body text and link about 30px below the other three, so the row's lines no longer match across the cards. The four badges also mix two kinds of label in one style: "New" and "Beta" are a feature's status, while "Auto" and "Email" describe how it works. The viewer can't learn what a badge means. Place the badge inline after the title on every card, as the other three do. Use badges only for status (New, Beta), and move "Auto" and "Email" into the body text.

**Smaller things.**
- The customer names strip has no label and is set in small grey text under a blue rule, so it reads as a footer, not social proof. Add a short lead-in ("Booked through Slotwise by…") or use real logos, and drop the rule or make it grey.
- "Start free" (nav) and "Create your link" (hero) look identical but have different labels. If they do the same thing, give them the same words. If they don't, the nav one shouldn't look identical.
- Four "Learn more" links tell the viewer nothing. Name where each one goes ("How team routing works").
- The icon set is inconsistent: the calendar is outlined while the clock and envelope are filled, and the intake-forms glyph looks like a page-layout icon rather than a form. Use one style and draw a form for intake forms.
- The headline breaks after "fill your", which leaves "calendar" alone on the second line. Widen the text box or break after "links" so each line holds a whole phrase.

**What's working.**
- There is one clear left edge at 64px, shared by the logo, headline, subhead, buttons, cards and customer strip, and the four cards have equal gaps. Keep that grid.
- The card body copy ends on the viewer ("so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows", "so the first minutes are useful"). That is the voice the hero is missing. Use it there.
- Primary and secondary actions are styled correctly: a filled button for "Create your link", and an underlined text link for "See a live example".

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The right half of the hero (about x 680–1216, y 100–380) is completely empty. Check in a live browser whether an image or demo failed to load. If it's empty on purpose, see point 2: that's where the product should be.
- Hover and focus states for the buttons, the links and the four cards (whether each whole card is clickable).
