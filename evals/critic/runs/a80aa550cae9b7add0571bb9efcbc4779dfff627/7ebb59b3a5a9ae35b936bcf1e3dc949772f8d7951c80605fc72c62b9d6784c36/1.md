**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big white headline, "Ferns and shade plants for every corner", and then gets pulled back out into the leaf pattern, which fills the whole screen and is loud and bright. The subhead and the outlined button sit low in that noise and are hard to pick out. It reads as a local plant nursery's home page. **What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the hero section of a small nursery's website. Its job is to get local gardeners to browse the plants and come in this weekend.

**Verdict.** The words do their job, but the background works against them twice. It makes the text hard to read, and it shows bright, sunny confetti on a page selling ferns and shade plants. Replacing that background is the one change that matters most.

**What would move it most**

1. **The text sits straight on a busy, high-contrast pattern** (defect). The pale lime leaves cross behind the letters everywhere. The headline survives because it's large and bold, though the bottoms of "plants" and "corner" still break up where light leaves pass behind them. The subhead ("Grown on the hill…") is regular weight at about 20 px, and the button label is smaller still. Both lose letters against the light leaves, so the viewer has to work to read the two lines that carry the offer and the action. Contrast has to hold at the worst point behind the text, not on average (W3C, Techniques for WCAG 2.2, F83). The fix: put a dark scrim, a see-through overlay or a solid panel behind the text block, or calm the background on the left where the text sits, so every letter stands out from what's directly behind it.

2. **The image shows nothing they sell and the wrong mood** (taste, argued). The background is scattered flat green ovals. There's no frond, no fern shape, no plant, no shade. A shade nursery's selling point is cool, deep, layered green under cover. This pattern is saturated, sunlit and scattered, the opposite feeling. It looks like any eco or wellness template, so the viewer gets no sense of this nursery or its plants. The fix: use a real photo of ferns in shade, darker in value with the detail away from the text. That also solves most of finding 1.

3. **The only action on the page is styled as the weakest element** (convention). "Browse the greenhouse" is a ghost button: a thin white outline with no fill, sitting on the busiest part of the screen. It's lighter than the subhead above it and blends into the leaves. A view's one main action should be the strongest-styled control, a filled, high-contrast button (Material Design; Refactoring UI). Nothing else on this screen competes with it, so it can afford to be bold. The fix: give it a solid fill in a colour that stands apart from the greens, such as cream or a deep near-black green on a scrim, with clear contrast.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "…shade / plants for every / corner", leaving "corner" alone on the last line. Break at the phrase boundary instead: "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner".
- "For every corner" could sit on anyone's page. Naming the viewer's actual problem would earn more, e.g. "for the dark corner where nothing grows". The subhead already does this well.
- The hero text's left edge (about x 96) doesn't line up with the logo above it (about x 64). Put them on one edge so the header and hero read as one page.
- "Visit" in the nav is vague. "Visit us" or "Opening hours" tells people what they'll find.

**What's working.**
- The subhead "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" is specific and local, and gives a reason to act now. Keep it word for word and just make it readable.
- The headline says plainly what they sell, and its size and weight give the page a clear first stop.
- The header is calm and well balanced: a dark green wordmark, four short nav links, plenty of space. It's a good anchor to build the hero's calmer palette from.
