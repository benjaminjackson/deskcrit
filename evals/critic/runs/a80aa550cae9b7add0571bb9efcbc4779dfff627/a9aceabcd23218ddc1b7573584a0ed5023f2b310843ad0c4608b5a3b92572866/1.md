**First read.** The eye goes to "Changing your pickup time" and then runs down four bold headings of the same weight. Within two seconds it reads as a help or FAQ section for a bakery's click-and-collect service. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. Nothing tells you which bakery this is or what page you're on.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a help section on a bakery's online ordering site that answers customers' questions about picking up, allergies and cancelling. They arrive with one question and want a clear answer they can act on.

**Verdict.** The layout mostly works: you can find the question you have and it's quick to scan. The answers let it down. Two of them are vague on exactly the points a customer will act on and hold the bakery to: the cancellation deadline and who has to give what name at the counter. One answer, on allergies, sends the customer to the shop to find out.

**What would move it most**

1. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways** (bottom section, "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead"). "A day ahead" of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time on the day before. Someone collecting at 9am who tries to cancel the evening before reads it the second way, finds out it meant the first, and pays for bread they didn't want. A deadline is the line people hold the shop to later, so this is a **defect**, not a style note. Fix: give the cut-off as an exact time ("You can cancel until 24 hours before your pickup time" or "until 6pm the day before"). Then say what happens to the viewer, not to the bread: "After that we've already baked it, so you'll still be charged" (or whatever the real policy is).

2. **"Along with a name" leaves the person collecting guessing** (second section). Whose name: the customer's or the person collecting? The friend who turns up giving their own name when the counter expects the customer's, or the other way round, gets held up or turned away. **Defect.** Fix: say who does what: "Give them your order number and tell them to give your name at the counter."

3. **The allergy answer doesn't answer the question for someone ordering online** (third section). An allergy check matters before you order, and the card "by the till" can only be read once you're standing in the shop. As written, the page tells a customer with an allergy to order blind and check when they collect. **Defect** for this group of readers, and the costliest one if it goes wrong. Fix: link to the ingredients list on each product page, or say where online to find it. Keep "ask the baker" as the backup, and tell people how to ask before they order (a phone number or email).

4. **The text lines run too long** (all four answers, roughly 160–980 px). The single-line answers ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") look like they hold about 95–100 characters, well past the 45–75 range with about 66 as the ideal (Bringhurst). They're only one or two lines, so the cost is small now. It grows quickly if any answer gets longer. **Convention.** Fix: narrow the text column to about 640–680 px at this size. The headings can stay as they are.

**Smaller things.**
- No page title or bakery name appears in the capture, so the viewer can't tell whose help page this is or that it's a list of FAQs. Add a title such as "Collecting your order". If it sits above this crop, ignore this point.
- "Open your order" (first section) doesn't say where. Name the place: "Go to My orders and open the order."
- "The bakery holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" stops at the fact. Add what it means for the reader: what happens to their order, and whether they still pay, if they come later than that.
- The four headings are bold and about twice the body size, so each one carries more weight than its answer. This is taste: for a quiet help page, dropping the headings one step, or keeping the size and making them semibold, would make the answers read as the main content. It's optional.
- The content block sits left with about 300 px of empty space to its right and a wide empty band below. That's fine for a help page and not a finding unless the whole page looks like this.

**What's working.**
- Each heading sits tight to its answer, about 10 px, with roughly 45 px between sections. The question-and-answer pairs read as units without any rules or boxes. Keep this spacing ratio.
- The type scale has two sizes with a big step between them, one face, dark text on white with strong contrast, and everything aligned flush left on one edge. It's clean and there's nothing to tidy.
- The headings name the situation the customer is in ("Collecting for someone else"), not internal category labels, so people find their question by recognising it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the four sections are links, accordions or static text, and how the column reflows on a phone. Most pickup questions will be asked on a phone, so check the line length and heading wrap at 375 px.
