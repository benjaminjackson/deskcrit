**Verdict.** The content is clear, and most of the page does its job. The one real problem is the indented "Sketches" box. It's the only irregular thing on the page, so it gets read as meaning something, and the meaning it suggests is probably wrong.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Sketches" box is indented (defect).** Its left edge sits roughly 14 px inside the other four. Every other box shares one left edge, so this one shift stands out more than any heading on the page. A reader will take it one of two ways. Either Sketches is a sub-step of Research, which the copy doesn't support because sketching is its own stage, or the page looks broken. Either way it costs trust and a second read. Alignment only works if every element shares an edge with the others (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*), and a near-miss reads worse than a deliberate offset. Fix: put it back on the shared left edge. If you really do want to show nesting, indent by a clear amount and add something else that marks it as a child, like a connector or a smaller box.
2. **Heavy boxes split a sequence into five separate things (taste, backed by convention).** Each stage sits in a dark border about 3 px thick with an 18 px gap between boxes. The borders carry more ink than the text inside them, and they cut the stages apart when the content is a single sequence: kickoff → research → sketches → review → handover. Space alone could do this job (Refactoring UI: use fewer borders, separate with space or a background). Fix: drop the borders or make them much lighter, such as a hairline or a pale tint. If order matters, number the stages so the sequence is shown, not just implied by position.

**Smaller things.**
- The title "Project notes" names a category, not what the page is. Something like "Project stages, kickoff to handover" tells a stranger what they're about to read.
- Nothing shows timing or ownership, even though the Kickoff line promises "the dates, and who signs off each stage." If the page is meant to be used as a plan, each stage needs a date or owner slot.
- The type scale is fine: three sizes (title, stage name, description), each with a clear job.
