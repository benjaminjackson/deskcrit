**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline on navy, "See where every dollar goes this month". It's big, bold, and holds together as one block. Then it drops to the subhead and the two buttons, and then to the right half of the navy band, which is empty. The bottom of the frame is a thin row of three small features and then white space. It reads as a clean, competent budgeting-app landing page that shows you nothing of the app.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the marketing home page for a consumer budgeting app. Its job is to get a stranger to sign up and link their bank accounts.

**Verdict.** The words are good and the hierarchy is clear, but the page asks people to hand over their bank logins without showing them the product and without answering their biggest worry where they decide. The empty right half of the hero and the buried "Read-only access" point are what hold it back.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero promises a view and never shows it** (defect). The headline says "See where every dollar goes", and the right half of the navy band, about 500 px wide, is plain navy. A visitor deciding whether to link their accounts has to picture the product from three lines of text. Nothing here tells this app apart from any other budgeting app, so the page could be anyone's (navy band, sans serif, three feature columns). The empty side also leaves the hero heavy on the left with nothing to balance it. Fix: put the product's answer to the headline in that space. A real-looking month view would work: categories, the limits, and one category flagged as about to go over. Then the visual makes good on the headline's promise. If an image was supposed to be there and didn't load, check in a live browser (see the end), but the point stands.

2. **The biggest objection is answered in the smallest type, far from the button** (defect). "Connect your accounts" is the scary step for a money app. The answer, "The app can see balances and history but can never move money", is the strongest line on the page. Right now it's ~15 px grey text, third in a row of three, below the fold of attention. Someone hesitating over "Try it free" never sees it. Fix: move a short version of that line right next to or under the buttons ("Read-only: Penny Path can never move your money"), and keep the feature column for the detail.

3. **"Try it free" can be read two ways** (defect, copy). It could mean a free plan or a trial that starts billing. On a money app, the reader will hold you to whichever reading they took, and the wrong one costs trust at exactly the wrong moment. Fix: name the terms in the button or a line beside it ("Free for 30 days, no card needed", or "Free plan, upgrade anytime"), whichever is true. "Watch the tour" is fine as a secondary action, and its outline style ranks it correctly below the filled primary button.

4. **The feature row looks like an afterthought** (taste). It starts only ~40 px below the hero, so it hugs the hero's edge and gets less breathing room than the hero's own padding (~65 px). Below it the frame is empty (~220 px). The row's tight spacing makes it look like a footnote to the hero instead of its own section. Fix: give the row clearly more space above than the hero has inside it, so the white space marks a new section and isn't left over at the bottom.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count about six sizes (headline ~56, subhead ~20, logo ~19, feature heads ~18, buttons ~17, body/nav ~15). The 17–20 group sits too close together to read as separate levels. Settle on headline / subhead / small heading / body, and use weight instead of size for the in-between ones (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- "Sign in" looks the same as the nav links. Returning users have to hunt for it among Features/Pricing/Security. Separate it a little, with a divider or extra space, or give it a light button style.
- Feature headings "Automatic categories" and "Shared budgets" name categories. The body text under them does the real work ("you can fix any of them in one tap"). Consider headings that say what happens for the user.

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and speaks to the reader: one promise, a real time frame ("this month"), and a tight two-line break with good leading. Keep the words.
- The subhead walks through the product in three plain verbs (connect, set a limit, get a heads-up) at a comfortable line length. That's how every line on the page should read.
- One left edge at x≈64 runs from the logo through the hero to the first feature column, and the three columns share an even grid. The page is orderly, so the fixes above are about content and spacing, not structure.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right side of the hero and the white space at the bottom are intended or a capture taken mid-load. Check in a live browser for a hero image or a further section that didn't render.
