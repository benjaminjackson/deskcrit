**First read.** The eye goes to "Crossing the river", then down the left column. It looks like a short feature or local guide page, set as two columns of serif text. It feels calm, plain and easy to settle into. Nothing grabs, and nothing gets in the way.

**What it's for.** You didn't say, so I'm assuming this is a short editorial reading page, on screen or in print, meant to be read start to finish without effort.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The type is comfortable, the hierarchy is clear and the copy reads well. What's in the way is finishing work on the grid: the two columns don't share a line rhythm, and the paragraphs under the headings are indented when they should start flush. Both make a careful setting look slightly unsure of itself.

**What would move it most**

1. **The lines of the two columns don't line up across the gap.** *Convention: Müller-Brockmann, Grid Systems in Graphic Design, "Construction of the grid".*
   - **Where:** The right column's first line sits level with the "Early boats" heading, not with the body text below it. From there every right-hand line sits about half a line off its partner on the left. The left column's first body line is at about y 167, and the nearest right-hand line is at about y 178.
   - **What the viewer gets:** Looking across the gutter, the rows don't run straight. The page looks a little jumbled even though each column is fine by itself.
   - **Fix:** Put everything on one line interval. The space above and below each heading should add up to whole body lines, so text below a heading goes back onto the shared rows. Start the right column on the same row as the left column's first body line.

2. **The first paragraph after each heading is indented.** *Convention: Bringhurst, The Elements of Typographic Style, 2.3.1, "Set opening paragraphs flush left".*
   - **Where:** Under "Early boats", "The market quay" and "Going home".
   - **What the viewer gets:** The heading already marks the start, so the indent adds a gap right under the heading's left edge. That gap breaks the strong left line the headings set up.
   - **Fix:** Set the opening paragraph after a heading flush left. Keep the indent for the paragraphs that follow.

**Smaller things**
- **Indent depth:** The indents look about 30 px, nearly two ems at this size. One em would mark the paragraph without the deep notch on every third line. This is taste.
- **Empty lower third:** About a third of the canvas is blank below the text, and the right column stops roughly four lines short of the left. On a fixed page, either balance the columns or make the empty space look intended, for example by setting a bottom margin for the text block. A bigger fix isn't needed. This is taste.

**What's working**
- **Measure and leading:** Lines run about 55–65 characters, inside Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Leading looks about 1.45. Together they make the text genuinely easy to read, so keep both.
- **Type scale:** There are three clear sizes (title, section head, body), and all the emphasis comes from one bold serif. Each level has an obvious job and none collide.
- **Heading spacing:** Each section head sits closer to its own text than to the text above it, so the sections read as distinct groups.
