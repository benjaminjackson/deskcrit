**First read.** The eye goes to "Crossing the river" first, then across to the blue "See the timetable" button, which is the only colour on the page. Then it drops down the left column. It reads as a calm, plain information page for a small local ferry, aimed at people about to travel. It feels trustworthy and quiet, if a bit generic.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's for a passenger planning a crossing on the north landing to market quay route: they need to know when they can travel, and what they can bring.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and does no harm, but the page fails at its main job. It never says when the boats actually run, and the one firm rule it gives ("before dusk") is one a passenger can misread and miss the last boat. A muddled type scale also lets the sidebar outrank the main content.

**What would move it most**

1. **The timetable section gives no times** (defect). "When the boats run" says "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" and "the last crossing leaves before dusk". A passenger can't plan with any of this. When does the first boat leave? When does winter start? Dusk changes every day, so "before dusk" can mean 16:00 or 17:30 depending on who's reading. Someone who guesses late gets stranded on the wrong bank. These are exactly the facts people act on and later hold the operator to, so a vague line here is a defect, not a style choice. The fix: give the first and last departures in clock time, the winter dates, and the actual last winter crossing (or "the last boat leaves at 16:20 from November to February"). Put that inline, above the button, so the page answers the question without a click.

2. **The type scale has too many sizes, and the sidebar is louder than the article** (convention: Bringhurst on a deliberate scale; Refactoring UI, "bolder, not bigger"). I count about seven sizes: the headline, "Good to know", the section heads, the grey subtitle, the card body, the main body, and the nav and button text. Two pairs nearly collide:
   - The card body (around 20 px) is set noticeably larger than the main body (around 18 px). The aside reads as more important than the guide itself, and the mismatch looks like an accident.
   - "Good to know" is bigger than "When the boats run" and "Bicycles and luggage". A side note now outranks the sections that carry the content.

   The subtitle and section heads (roughly 24 and 28 px) are also close in size and are told apart mostly by colour. Cut to about four levels: headline, section head, body, small. Set the card on the same section-head and body sizes as the main column, and let its border and button do the work of setting it apart.

**Smaller things.**
- The card's right edge (around x=1150) stops short of the nav's right edge (around x=1184). The left margin is shared, but the right one isn't. Pin the card to the same right margin as the header.
- "Good to know" is a label that could go on any site. Name what's in the card, for example "Timetables and changes".
- The card tells people that this week's changes are posted on a noticeboard at the landing. A web visitor can't see that board, so they only find out about changes when they arrive. Show changes on this page, or say where to find them online.

**What's working.**
- Restraint with colour. Blue appears once, on the one action, so the button needs no other help to be found. Keep it that way.
- Spacing groups the content correctly. Each section head sits close to its paragraph, with clearly more space before the next section, so the two topics read as separate at a glance.
- The bicycle paragraph is good copy: specific, it says who does what and when ("Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded"), and it puts the cost up front ("at no extra charge"). Write the timetable section to the same standard.
