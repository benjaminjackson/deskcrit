**First read.** My eye goes to the headline, then slides across four price numbers of equal weight: $0, $12, $39, $99. Nothing tells me where to stop. It's clearly a SaaS pricing section for a collaborative tool. It feels clean and calm, and also generic: four identical cards with four identical blue buttons. I compare the rows one by one and get caught at "editors", because Free gives 3 and Solo gives 1.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a pricing section for a design or creative collaboration tool, aimed at freelancers and small studios, meant to get each visitor to pick the right plan quickly and click.

**Verdict.** It looks tidy, but it doesn't help anyone choose. All four plans have equal weight, and the content has a contradiction and an unclear price unit that a careful buyer will catch. Those are what most get in the way of a confident click.

**What would move it most**

1. **The paid Solo plan looks worse than Free.** In the "editors" row (second feature line), Free says "3 editors" and Solo says "1 editor". Someone reading across sees that paying $12 removes two seats. That makes the whole ladder look arbitrary, and a free user who adds two collaborators has a reason never to upgrade to Solo. *Defect.* Fix direction: make every row get better from left to right. If Solo really is single-seat on purpose, say why it's worth paying for in that row ("Just you, 20 projects"), or drop the editors line from Solo.

2. **"/ month" can be read two ways, and it's the line people will hold you to.** "$39 / month" next to "15 editors" can mean $39 for the team or $39 per editor. That's either $39 or up to $585 a month. Then Company shows a fixed "$99 / month" but its button says "Talk to us", which suggests the price isn't real or needs a sales call. Someone who reads it as per-seat will leave over the cost. Someone who reads it as flat and then gets billed per seat will feel tricked. *Defect.* Fix direction: name the unit outright ("$39 / month for the whole team" or "per editor"). Also make Company's price and its button agree: either "From $99" with "Talk to us", or $99 with "Choose Company".

3. **No plan is marked as the one to pick.** The four cards and four filled blue buttons are identical. This breaks the convention of one high-emphasis action per view (Material Design; Refactoring UI). The visitor has to do all the comparing alone, and with four equal choices many will put it off. The headline aims at "teams", yet Team gets no more emphasis than Free. *Convention, and it costs something here.* Fix direction: pick the plan most visitors should buy (probably Team) and make it look different, with a label, a stronger border or tint, or a slight lift. Make the other buttons secondary (outline). Keep the filled style for one button, or for the recommended plan plus "Start free".

**Smaller things.**
- Each card has a band of about 70 px of white below its button, and the page has a large empty area below the cards. It looks like leftover space. Close the cards up under the buttons.
- The headline "Plans for every size of team" could go on any SaaS pricing page. Use the line to say what's different, or move the strongest fact (unlimited viewers) up into it.
- "Named contact" is internal shorthand. Say what the buyer gets, e.g. "A named person to call".
- The grey taglines ("For trying it out", etc.) look small, around 13 px, and light. Check that they reach 4.5:1 against white.
- "Community help", "Email help", "Priority help": "Priority" doesn't say how fast. A response time would make Team's row easier to judge.

**What's working.**
- Every card lists its features in the same order (projects, editors, history, support), with thin dividers between rows. That makes comparing across cards fast, and it's why the editors problem is so easy to spot. Keep that structure when you fix it.
- The type scale is disciplined: one large bold price, a plan name, body text, and a small muted size, with clear steps between them. The prices are easy to scan.
- "Change or cancel at any time. Every plan includes unlimited viewers." is the most useful copy on the page. It removes a risk and answers a real question in one line. Consider giving it more prominence.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether there's a monthly/annual toggle, or a currency setting, somewhere off-screen. Either one would change how "/ month" reads.
- How the four cards stack on narrow screens. Check that the recommended plan doesn't end up last on mobile.
