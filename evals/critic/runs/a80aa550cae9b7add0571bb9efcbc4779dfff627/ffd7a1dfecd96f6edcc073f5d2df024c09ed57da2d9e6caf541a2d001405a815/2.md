**First read.** The eye lands on the bold headline at top left, then drops into three even grey-black paragraphs. It reads as a calm, tidy help or info page, competently set. Nothing in the body stands out, so a passenger looking for "when is the next boat?" has to read every sentence to find out.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web notice that tells ferry passengers how the service works (times, duration, bikes, winter changes), read on the move by people who want one or two facts fast.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is good, but the piece doesn't do its job. It holds no actual times, and the facts a passenger acts on are buried in running prose, so they can't find them or rely on them.

**What would move it most**

1. **The schedule has no times in it.** In the first and third paragraphs, "early morning", "late evening" and "before dusk" are the facts a passenger plans around, and none of them is a time. A reader can't tell whether 6:50 am is too early or whether 9 pm is too late, and "before dusk" moves by hours across a winter. Anyone who guesses wrong misses the last boat. "Every forty minutes" also gives no starting time, so you can't work out the departures. *Defect.* Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing as clock times, and give the winter dates and last-boat time the same way.

2. **"The last crossing leaves the quay" can be read two ways.** In paragraph 3 there are two landings, the north landing and the market quay. "The quay" most likely means the market quay, which leaves the last boat from the north landing unstated. A rider on the north side may assume the same time applies to them. "In winter" and "one boat each hour" have the same problem: they don't say which months, or at what minute past the hour. *Defect*, because these are the times people act on and later hold the operator to. Fix: name both landings and give both last departures.

3. **Facts people scan for are set as prose.** Route, frequency, crossing time, bike rules and winter changes are separate facts, but they're spread through sentences of the same weight. Someone checking a detail on a phone at the landing has to read the whole page. *Convention* (text people skim should be broken up with headings, short blocks and lists; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; Nielsen Norman Group on scanning). Fix: put a short timetable block first (first boat, last boat, how often, how long), then small subheads such as "Bicycles" and "Winter" over the existing paragraphs. Show the numbers as figures (40 min, 12 min) so the eye can catch them.

4. **The headline names a category, not what the reader gets.** "Notes for ferry passengers" could head any page and says nothing about the river, the route or the times. *Taste*, but it matters here. Something like "North landing ⇄ Market quay: times, bikes and winter service" tells the reader at once that they're in the right place and what's on the page.

**Smaller things.**
- The bike paragraph says what riders do but not what happens if the lower deck is full. Say whether bikes can be turned away at busy times, if they can.
- "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter" sends readers to paper the page itself should have. If the page holds the timetable, this can shrink to "check the noticeboard for changes this week."
- The right half of the frame is empty. That's fine for a reading column, but a timetable block could sit there and be the first thing anyone sees.

**What's working.**
- The measure: lines run about 65 to 70 characters, and leading is generous at roughly 1.5. The text is comfortable to read (Bringhurst's 45 to 75 range). Keep this column width when you restructure.
- The type scale is clean: one bold display size and one text size, a clear step apart, in one sans family, with near-black on white for strong contrast. Don't add in-between sizes when you add subheads. Mark subheads with weight at body size or slightly larger.
- The plain, calm tone ("a little longer when the tide turns against the boat") sounds like a person. Keep that voice in the restructured version, with the hard facts pulled out.
