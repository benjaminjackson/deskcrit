**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board" first. The heading is the only bold, dark, large thing on the page. Then it drops into the body and hits a wall: three paragraphs set entirely in capitals, which read like a block of shouting. It reads as a passenger information page for a small river ferry, and it feels calm and tidy right up until you try to read the body.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web or print notice that gives passengers the practical facts they need before a crossing, read quickly and often on a phone or at the landing.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job well. The frame is clean, but the text you came for is set in all capitals, so it reads slowly. The copy also leaves out the times and conditions a passenger would actually plan around.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body is set in all capitals** (all three paragraphs). This is a defect. Capitals give every word the same rectangular outline, so the reader loses the word shapes they use to skim. Here that's about 80 words per paragraph, and the reader has to work through them letter by letter. It also flattens the page: the eyebrow label is in capitals, so the body now looks like the label, and the only contrast left is the heading. This is my own argument, not a rule from the reading list, but it's hard to dispute at this length. Fix: set the body in sentence case at the current size and weight. Keep capitals only for the short "PASSENGER NOTES" label, where they work.
2. **The copy is vague about exactly the facts a passenger acts on** (paragraphs 1 and 3). This is a defect. "Between early morning and late evening" doesn't tell anyone whether they can catch a 6:30 or a 22:00 crossing. "In winter" doesn't say which months, and "one boat each hour" doesn't say at what minute past. "Every forty minutes" without a first departure means you can't work out any actual sailing. A visitor reading this still has to find the printed timetable at the shelter, so the page fails the job its heading promises. Fix: give the first and last sailings, the winter date range, and the minute past the hour each boat leaves (or link to the timetable). The bicycle paragraph also says "no extra charge" but never gives the base fare. Add it, or say where to find it.

**Smaller things.**
- Body measure: the lines run to roughly 75–85 characters in capitals, which take more width per character than lower case, so they're at the long end. In sentence case at this width they'd land closer to the 45–75 range Bringhurst gives. Check after changing the case.
- Paragraph 2 ("Riders wheel them on… and lock them to the rail") is the one instruction on the page, but it's buried mid-paragraph. A short lead-in such as "If you have a bicycle:" would let a cyclist find it at a glance.
- The eyebrow label (grey, small capitals) looks only lightly letterspaced. That's fine, but it looks pale. Check that it reaches 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- The scale has three clear levels (small label, bold heading, regular body) with big steps between them. Keep it.
- The heading "Before you board" says when to read the page, in the passenger's own terms. That's better than a generic "Information".
- The generous left margin and paragraph spacing make the three topics (schedule, bicycles, winter) separate at a glance. Sentence case will let that structure show.
