**First read.** My eye goes to the headline first, then straight to the purple box, which is the heaviest block on the left and pulls harder than the orange button above it. Next it goes to the big teal panel on the right, which holds nothing but two empty speech bubbles, and only then back to "Start your free week." It reads as a clean, friendly landing page for a language-learning app. It feels calm and competent, but the colours are crowded.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page for a language app. It should turn first-time visitors, adults who want to speak a language, into free-trial sign-ups.

**Verdict.** Most of it works. The headline is specific and the three steps explain the product. But the purple "Keep your streak going" box takes attention away from the one action the page exists for, and it talks to existing users rather than visitors. Removing or demoting that box is the biggest fix.

**What would move it most**

1. **The purple box is beating the call to action** (defect). The box at left, below the button, is wider than the orange "Start your free week" button (about 400 px against 250 px). It's a solid, saturated colour and it has a bold heading, so it has as much weight as the button or more. It also has rounded corners and a button-like fill, so it looks clickable. That makes two competing "buttons" stacked together: the visitor has to work out which one is the action, and a click on the purple one leads nowhere. This breaks "one high-emphasis action per view" (Refactoring UI; Material Design's primary vs. secondary action). The copy makes it worse. "Keep your streak going" speaks to someone who already has a streak, and a first-time visitor has none, so the line is aimed at the wrong person. Fix: cut it from the hero. If the "twice as often" stat matters, make it plain supporting text below the steps, in the page's neutral grey or ink with no fill. Rewrite it for a newcomer: "Practise five days in a row and you're twice as likely to finish."

2. **The teal panel is the biggest coloured shape on the page and says nothing** (taste, with a real cost). At about 400×300 px of solid teal, it's the largest block of saturated colour. What it shows is two speech bubbles with placeholder lines, the most generic picture "conversation" could have. It gives the visitor no sign of what a Parlo lesson looks like or that a tutor listens to them, so prime space carries no information and the page looks like a template. It also uses up the attention the orange button needs: with orange, purple and teal all at full strength, no single colour marks "act here" (Refactoring UI: keep saturated colour for what matters). Fix: put the actual product there, such as a lesson screen showing the per-word feedback from step 2, or a short phrase with the tutor's reply. Let orange be the only strong colour above the fold.

3. **The hero image breaks the page's own grid** (convention). The nav and the three-step row both run from 56 px to 1224 px. The teal panel stops at about 1076 px, so a strip about 150 px wide on the right edge is left empty and the hero looks off-balance and unfinished next to the full-width steps. This breaks Müller-Brockmann's rule that pictures take their edges from the grid. Fix: make the image span to the right margin, or set the whole page to the narrower width.

**Smaller things.**
- Orange button: white on that orange looks close to 4:1. At about 18 px bold it may just miss WCAG's large-text cutoff (18.66 px bold), where it would need 4.5:1. Measure it, and darken the orange slightly if it fails.
- Type scale: I count about six sizes (logo ~20, headline ~40, intro ~18, button/card/step titles ~17–18, body ~15, nav ~15). The intro and the bold titles sit a point or two apart, so they read as mistakes rather than levels (Bringhurst). Use one size for the 17/18 group and tell them apart by weight.
- Headline break: "in / ten weeks" splits the phrase. "Hold a real conversation / in ten weeks" breaks where you'd pause when saying it.
- Intro copy: "Short daily lessons" has no number. "Ten weeks" is the promise people will hold you to, so say what it costs them: "ten minutes a day."
- The button doesn't say what happens after the free week (is a card needed, what does it cost). That's what makes people hesitate at a trial button, so add one line under it, e.g. "No card needed" or the price after the free week.
- The three steps end at about 620 px of an 800 px frame, and nothing below hints that there's more. If this is the full first screen, pull the steps up or show the top of the next section.

**What's working.**
- The headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks," promises an outcome the visitor wants and a deadline they can judge. Keep that framing.
- The three steps (Listen / Speak / Use it) each name a concrete thing you do, and the hairline above each one sets them apart without boxes. That restraint is right, and it's the tone the purple box should follow.
- The nav is quiet and conventional, with "For schools" kept separate, so it doesn't compete with the hero.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the purple box is clickable, and how the two-column hero stacks on a phone, where the teal panel could push the button below the fold.
