**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers across the top, $4.82M first, then drops into the large blue bar chart, which takes most of the page. It reads straight away as a SaaS finance dashboard, a year-end revenue summary for a founder, finance lead or board. It feels calm, competent and trustworthy, until you start adding the numbers up.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a revenue overview for someone running or funding a subscription business, who needs to see in a few seconds how 2025 went and believe the figures enough to act on them.

**Verdict.** It is well built and mostly does its job, but the revenue figures on the page don't agree with each other. On a finance screen, that undoes everything else. The second problem is that the biggest element on the page says only "it went up."

**What would move it most**

1. **The revenue figures don't reconcile (defect).** The table on the right is titled "Revenue by plan, 2025" and totals **$4.82M**. That is exactly the ARR card's figure, not 2025 revenue. The monthly bars, read against the gridlines, add up to roughly **$4.49M** of recognised revenue for 2025. December's bar, about $447K, works out to about $5.4M a year, which doesn't match the $4.82M ARR either. A finance reader who notices any one of these gaps stops trusting all of them, and there's no way to tell which figure is wrong. This matters more than anything else here because these are the numbers people quote in board decks and hold the product to.
   - Each figure should state its metric and period, and figures that share a basis should add up.
   - If the table is ARR by plan at December, title it that way ("ARR by plan, Dec 2025").
   - If it really is 2025 revenue, its total must match the chart.
   - If recognised revenue and ARR are meant to differ (one-off fees, mid-year churn), say so in one line so the gap reads as intended, not as an error.

2. **The chart takes about 60% of the page and only shows a topic (taste, argued).** "Monthly revenue, 2025" names the subject, but the page never states 2025 revenue as a number. There's no total, no December value on the chart, and no 2024 to compare against. The reader can see the trend but has to estimate every value from the gridlines, and the bars climb gently with no annotation saying whether that's good. The small dips in April and July pass without comment. The KPI cards show what works: each number has a comparison underneath.
   - Give the chart the same treatment: a title or caption that states the finding with the number (for example "Revenue grew X% to $4.49M"), the December value labelled on the last bar, and ideally 2024 as a muted grey series behind.
   - Then the biggest element on the page also says the most.

**Smaller things**
- **Navigation:** the page is called "Revenue overview" but the highlighted item is "Overview", with a separate "Revenue" item right below it. It's unclear where you are and what "Revenue" would show. Rename one of them so the page title matches the active item.
- **Date range** ("Jan to Dec 2025", top right): there's no chevron or border, so it's unclear whether it's a filter or a label. If it's a control, add a signifier (Norman). Its year also repeats in every card and chart label, so if it changes, those labels must change with it.
- **"Total" row** in the plan table: it has the same weight and the same rule as the plan rows, so it reads as a fourth plan. Set it apart with a heavier rule above it or a different weight.
- **"Revenue by plan" heading** sits a few pixels lower than "Monthly revenue, 2025" and has no subtitle line, so the two headings don't share a baseline. Align them.
- **Right column:** the table ends about a third of the way down, leaving a tall empty strip beside the chart. Either fill it with something useful (plan mix as a share, or per-plan change vs 2024) or narrow the column and widen the chart.
- **Type scale:** I count about seven sizes (KPI figures, page title, section heads, nav and table text, card labels, deltas, axis and source). The 13/14/15 px group in particular doesn't read as separate levels. Merge those into one or two sizes and separate them by weight or colour instead (Refactoring UI).
- **Green deltas** are small text on a pale grey card, so check they reach 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). The +/− sign already gives a second cue besides colour, which is good.

**What's working**
- **The chart is honest.** The bars start at zero, there's one restrained blue, the gridlines are faint, the units are stated ("Thousands of US dollars, recognised revenue") and the source line says "closed invoices only." Keep all of it. Few and Tufte would approve.
- **Every KPI has a comparison and a period**, including "+3 pts" for NRR instead of a misleading percentage-of-a-percentage. That's exactly how KPIs should be shown.
- **The hierarchy is clear:** the page title, the three KPIs as a group, then the detail. The eye's path is obvious and nothing competes with it.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the date range is interactive, and whether changing it updates the cards and table.
- What hovering a bar reveals. A tooltip with exact values would partly answer finding 2.
