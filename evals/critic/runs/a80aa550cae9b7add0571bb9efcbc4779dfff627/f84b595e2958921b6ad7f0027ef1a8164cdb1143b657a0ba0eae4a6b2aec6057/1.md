**First read.** My eye goes straight to the dark middle card. It's the only dark shape on a white page, so it pulls attention before the heading does. Then I notice it doesn't look like the other two at all. It has a serif heading, the text sits almost against its left edge, the corners are square, and the button is a yellow outlined pill where the others are solid blue rectangles. The page reads as a set of three walking routes, but the middle one looks like it came from another site.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page that lists local walks so a visitor can compare them and open one. Each card should be easy to scan against the others.

**Verdict.** It doesn't work yet. The two outer cards are clean and fine. The middle card breaks every rule they set, and nothing on the page says why. Visitors get loud emphasis with no meaning, and the three routes become harder to compare.

**What would move it most**

1. **The Woodland loop card breaks the card pattern in about six ways at once, without explaining itself.** It differs from the outer cards in:
   - typeface: a serif heading where the others use sans
   - padding: the heading and text start about 15 px from the card's left edge, against about 34 px in the outer cards
   - corners: square instead of rounded
   - fill: dark instead of white
   - button: shape, colour and style all change, from a solid blue rectangle to a yellow outlined pill
   - vertical position: its heading sits higher than the other two

   Visitors will see it as either "the recommended walk" or "a glitch", and nothing tells them which. If it's meant as the featured route, the emphasis promises something the copy never says. If it isn't, the card is simply wrong. Repeated elements of one kind should follow one set of rules and only vary where those rules call for it (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). **Defect.**

   Fix: decide whether this card is featured. If it isn't, rebuild it from the same component as the others. If it is, keep the card's structure identical (same padding, type, corners, button shape) and change one thing, such as the dark fill. Then add a visible reason, like a "Recommended" or "Easiest walk" label.

2. **Every card is about half empty.** All three cards stop at the same fixed height of about 440 px. The content ends around 395 px, leaving roughly 200 px of empty box under each button. The borders outline empty space, so the row looks unfinished, as if a photo or a block of details failed to load. **Defect** (wasted space, and the page looks broken).

   Fix: either shrink the cards to fit their content or fill the space with something a walker needs. A photo or a small map would do, and so would the details in point 3.

3. **The routes can't be compared at a glance.** Each card describes its walk in a different way:
   - Cliff path: distance, climbs, a cafe
   - Woodland loop: distance, terrain, benches
   - Canal towpath: distance, surface, bicycles

   To choose a walk, a visitor has to read all three sentences and line the facts up in their head. Only distance appears on every card, and even that is spelled out in words inside the sentence ("Six kilometres") instead of shown as a number. **Defect** for a page whose job is choosing between routes.

   Fix: give every card the same short row of facts in the same order, such as distance, time, difficulty or climb, and surface. Show distance as a number ("6 km"). Keep the sentence underneath for character.

**Smaller things.**
- Button labels: all three read "View route". In context that's fine, but naming the route ("View Cliff path") would help screen-reader users and the page's link list.
- Card borders: the pale grey outlines carry no information. Once the cards are sized to their content, a pale tint or plain space would separate them just as well (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*).
- Page title: "Walking routes" is generic. A place name or a line about who these walks suit would tell visitors where they are.

**What's working.**
- The outer cards are a sound component. The heading, body text and button sizes step clearly from one to the next, the grey body text looks readable against white, and the solid blue buttons are obviously clickable. Keep this as the template for all three cards.
- The descriptions are concrete and pleasant ("a cafe at the far end", "benches every few hundred metres"). They name things a walker actually cares about. Keep that voice when you add the structured facts.

- Hover and focus states for the three different button styles.
