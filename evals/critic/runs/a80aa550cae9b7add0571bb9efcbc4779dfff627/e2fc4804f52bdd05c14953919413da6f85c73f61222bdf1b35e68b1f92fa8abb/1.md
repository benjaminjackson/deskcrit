**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the event page for an author reading at an independent bookshop. Its job is to get local readers to book a free seat.

**Verdict.** It half works. The words and the reading order are good, but the biggest visual area is spent on a placeholder, and half the screen is left blank. The page looks like a template with the content missing rather than an evening worth leaving the house for. The date line also needs checking, because it's the line people will act on.

**What would move it most**

1. **The picture is a stand-in, and it's the biggest thing on the page.** About 600×330 px at top left is a dark gradient with a grey head-and-shoulders silhouette. It's the first thing the eye reaches, and it tells the viewer nothing: not who Ines Varga is, not what the book looks like, not the island where it's set. A first-time visitor's first impression is "nothing here", and that drags on the headline underneath. Replace it with a real photo of the author, the book cover, or a picture of the island the talk is about. The island would tie the picture to the talk and is the strongest of the three (my taste). If you can't get a real image, a smaller picture or none is better than a large empty one. *Defect.* I can't rule out a photo that failed to load during the screenshot; see the last section.

2. **Everything sits in the left half; the right half is empty.** The nav runs the full width, out to about x 1232, but the content stops at about x 648. Roughly 580 px to the right of the picture and text is blank, along with the bottom of the screen. That space isn't framing anything. It looks left over, so the page reads as half-built rather than spacious. Ruder's point is that empty space works when it stands in deliberate contrast to the filled part, and here it's just the rest of the page. Pick one of two fixes:
   - Fill the right side with the details people need before booking: venue and address, how long the evening runs, how many seats are left, and the book itself.
   - Or centre a narrower column under the nav so the empty space is the same on both sides and looks chosen.

   *Defect* (it costs credibility), and a *convention*: one grid should govern the whole page (Müller-Brockmann).

3. **Check the date: "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year, and the weekday may be wrong.** 13 November was a Thursday in 2025, but in 2026 it falls on a Friday. If this is for 2026, people will come on the wrong night, or not know which night is right. If it's 2025, the event is already past and the page is still offering a booking. Either way, a date is what people put in their diary and hold you to. Make the weekday match the date, and add the year if the page could stay up past the event. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Venue: the page never says where the evening is. Add a line under the date, e.g. "At the shop, 12 Example Street" (your real address), and don't make people go to "Find us".
- Book title: "The Salt Year" is set like the rest of the headline. Italicise it so it reads as a title (standard practice for book titles).
- Nav: "Events" isn't marked as the current section. Give it a weight change or an underline so people know where they are (Nielsen 1).
- Image to date gap: about 30 px, against about 10 px between the date, headline and paragraph. The text block is grouped correctly, but the picture and its text drift apart slightly. Tighten the gap a little so the card reads as one piece.

**What's working.**
- **The copy.** "A reading, a short talk about the island where the book is set, and questions from the floor. Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm" tells a stranger exactly what happens and when. Keep it word for word.
- **The ask.** "Book a free seat" names the exact action and removes the cost worry in two words, so it's much stronger than a plain "RSVP". It's the only filled, high-contrast button, so nothing competes with it.
- **Clean type with clear levels.** One sans-serif family carries everything: the large bold headline, the small brown bold date, body text at a comfortable size and well within a sensible line length, and a quiet nav. The brown on the date is the only colour and points straight at the "when". Keep that restraint.
