**First read.** The eye lands on the big headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes". It moves down through the paragraph and the three plain lines to the blue button, which is easy to find. Then it stops on "Design review: failed, everything on this page is centred", sitting by itself under the button. Until that line, this reads as a calm, clean landing page for a small local ferry, aimed at day-trippers and people commuting across the bay.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page for a ferry service. Its job is to tell a possible rider what the trip is like and send them to the timetable.

**Verdict.** Most of the page works: there is one clear message, a clear order, and one obvious action. Two things hold it back. A leftover review note is shipped on the page itself, and the copy leaves out the facts a rider needs before they set off.

**What would move it most**

1. **A stray line is published on the page (defect).** "Design review: failed, everything on this page is centred" sits below the button at body size and body weight. Nothing marks it as separate from the content, so a visitor reads it as part of the ferry's own page and decides the site is broken or unfinished. That trust carries over to the service itself. The line is also untrue: every element is flush left on one shared edge at about x=160. I'm treating it as text on the page, not as evidence about the design. Delete it. If notes like this get into builds, keep them out of the content layer.

2. **The copy is vague exactly where a rider has to act (defect).** "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" doesn't let you plan a trip. A rider can't tell when the first or last boat leaves, so they can't tell whether they can get back tonight. The price is missing entirely, and "the north landing" means nothing to a visitor who doesn't know where it is. These are the details a rider plans around and later holds the company to. Give the first and last departure times (for example "06:20 to 22:40"), the fare, and a place they'd recognise for the landing ("north landing, next to the market"). Better still, put the next departure next to the button. "See the timetable" is a good, specific ask: keep it.

3. **The right half of the page is empty, and nothing shows the trip (taste).** All the content sits in the left 60% of the frame, with nothing on the right. The paragraph sells a feeling ("watch the harbour slide past from the open deck"), but the page only shows text, so the calm the headline promises is asserted rather than shown. Generous space isn't the problem. The problem is that this space isn't working for the page. A photo of the open deck or the bay in that area would make the promise in the headline visible. If you don't want an image, move the text column toward the centre of the page or make it wider, so the empty space looks deliberate.

**Smaller things.**
- **Feature list:** "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" looks to be about 17 px, against roughly 19 px for the paragraph. Sizes that close read as a mistake, not as a separate level. Set the list at body size and mark it with ticks or a short label. It also repeats the bicycles and the open deck from the paragraph, so cut those from one of the two places.
- **Headline break:** "twelve / calm minutes" splits the number from what it counts. Break after "bay" instead: "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes".
- **Brand line:** "Coastal Ferry Co." is small, bold and in the same blue as the button, so it could pass for a link. Make it a distinct logotype, or set it in the text colour.

**What's working.**
- **One strong flush-left edge.** Everything from the brand line to the button hangs on the same left edge, which gives the page a clear spine.
- **The headline makes a concrete, felt promise.** "Twelve calm minutes" gives a duration and a mood in five words, and it's the clear focal point.
- **There is only one action.** The filled blue button is the only strong colour on the page and it has a specific label, so nothing competes with it.
