**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", drops into three paragraphs of grey-black prose and finds no times. I came for a departure time and got an essay. It feels calm, clean and generic, like a default documentation template. About 40% of the frame on the right is empty.

**What it's for.** Assumed: a web page that tells a ferry rider (commuter, cyclist, visitor) when the next boat leaves and how to board. I was given no context, so correct me if it's something else.

**Verdict.** It fails its job. The page is called a timetable but has no timetable. The one thing a rider came for, a departure time, isn't on it, and the page sends them to a printed sheet at the landing instead.

**What would move it most**

1. **There are no times** (all three paragraphs, defect). "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" can't be acted on. The rider can't work out the next departure without knowing the first one, and "early morning" isn't a time. The winter line, "one boat each hour… before dusk", has the same problem. Then paragraph 3 says the real timetable is "kept in the shelter at each landing", so the page admits it's a pointer to the actual information. Fix: put the departures first, as a real table: one column per direction, the times listed or at least first and last boat plus the interval, and a separate summer/winter block. This matters more than anything else here. "Show the information the user needs to act" is basic Nielsen (recognition over recall) and Krug (don't make me think). Here the information simply isn't there.

2. **The lines a rider acts on can each be read two ways** (paragraphs 1 and 3, defect). Each one could make someone miss the last boat home.
   - "Runs from the north landing to the market quay": is that one-way, or a round trip? Does the 40-minute cycle count from both ends?
   - "The last crossing leaves the quay before dusk": which quay? Is there a last boat back from the north landing, and when? "Before dusk" moves by more than an hour across winter.
   - "In winter": which dates?
   
   Fix: give each claim a time, a place and a date range. For example: "Nov–Feb: hourly; last boat from Market Quay 15:40, from North Landing 16:00."

3. **The page is prose when it should be scannable** (whole text block, convention: Krug; Nielsen on scannable text). Three kinds of information share one undifferentiated body style with no subheads: the schedule, bicycle rules, and seasonal changes plus where to find updates. A cyclist hunting for the bike rule, or a winter rider looking for the change, has to read everything. Fix: once the times are in a table, give "Bicycles" and "Winter service" short headings, each with one or two lines underneath.

**Smaller things**
- "Each crossing takes about twelve minutes… a little longer when the tide turns against the boat": either say how much longer ("up to 20 min") or cut it. As written it doesn't help anyone plan.
- "The noticeboard there lists any changes for the coming week": if changes exist, the web page is where they should appear first. Add a dated "Changes this week" line, or say where changes are posted online.
- The right ~500 px is dead space. That's acceptable for a reading page. If a table is added, though, it can sit beside the prose there instead of pushing it down (taste).

**What's working**
- The type is clean and the scale is disciplined: two sizes, a bold heading clearly separate from the body, and a heading that sits close to its text. Keep that restraint when you add the table.
- The measure (about 70 characters) and leading are comfortable, and the text contrast looks solidly dark on white. The body is easy to read; it just says the wrong things.
- The bicycle paragraph is the best copy on the page. It says who does what, in what order, and where ("wheel them on after foot passengers… lock them to the rail"). Write the timetable lines to that standard.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether a real timetable, such as a table or an embed, failed to load below the fold or to the right. That would explain the empty area. Check the live page. If a table is there, findings 1 and 3 mostly go away, and finding 2 still applies to the prose.
