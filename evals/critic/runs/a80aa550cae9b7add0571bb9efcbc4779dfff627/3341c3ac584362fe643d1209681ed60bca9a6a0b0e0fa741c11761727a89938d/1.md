**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes," then goes straight down the left column to the blue button. In about two seconds it reads as the hero section of a small local ferry's website: calm, plain and trustworthy. Then the eye runs out of things to look at. The right half of the frame and the bottom third are empty, so the page feels unfinished, as if something failed to load.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the top of a ferry operator's home page. It's for locals and visitors deciding whether to take the boat, and it should get them to the timetable or straight to the landing.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy and the headline are sound. But the page leaves out the facts someone needs before deciding to take a ferry: where it goes, when it runs and what it costs. The empty right half makes a confident page look half-built.

**What would move it most**

1. **The copy leaves out the go/no-go facts.** This is a defect. "Cross the bay" never says which side of the bay you arrive on; the north landing is the only place named. "From early morning until late evening" can't be planned around: a viewer who needs a 6:15 boat or a 22:00 return still can't tell whether this ferry works for them. There's no fare anywhere, and "Bicycles travel free" makes the missing passenger fare more noticeable. These are exactly the details a person acts on and later holds the operator to, so vagueness here costs more than anywhere else on the page. The button only works if the viewer is curious enough to click. The fix is to name both landings ("North landing to [X]"), give the first and last sailings as times, and state the adult fare. If the page can show it, the next departure time is the strongest possible ask, because it checks the ferry against the viewer's own clock. "See the timetable" is a good, specific label, so keep it.

2. **The three feature lines read as stray text, not a list.** This is a defect, partly taste. "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" have no bullets, ticks or change of weight or colour. Their line spacing looks about the same as the paragraph's leading, so at a glance they look like the paragraph continuing in short lines. Two of the three also repeat the paragraph ("Bring a bicycle", "from the open deck"), which weakens both. The fix is to drop the repeats from the paragraph or from the list. Then either give the list a visible marker and a clearly bigger gap above it than between its items, or replace it with the fare and times from point 1, which are the facts worth listing.

3. **The empty right half reads as leftover, not as chosen space.** This is taste. All the content sits in a column from about x=160 to x=790 at the top left. Nothing balances it, so the frame tips left and the blank area feels like a missing image rather than calm (Ruder: white space works when it stands in tension with the printed area, not as leftover). The paragraph practically asks for a picture: "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck". The fix is a photo or illustration of the boat or the crossing on the right. Failing that, a simple route sketch with the two landings and the twelve minutes would fill the space and answer "to where?" at the same time.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count five sizes. The body (about 20 px) and the list (about 18 px) are too close to read as different levels. Set the list at body size and separate it with a marker or weight instead (Refactoring UI).
- The headline breaks as "twelve / calm minutes", which separates the number from its unit. A break after "bay" keeps "in twelve calm minutes" together.
- The brand exists only as small blue text ("Coastal Ferry Co."). There's no logo or navigation in view, so it's hard to tell whether this is the home page or a landing page.

**What's working.**
- The headline is concrete and benefit-led: "twelve calm minutes" gives a number and a feeling in four words. Keep that voice.
- The hierarchy is clean. It has one dominant headline, readable body text at a comfortable measure (about 60–70 characters), and one filled primary action. Nothing else competes with it.
- The restrained blue-on-white palette fits a calm, practical service, and the blue is saved for the brand line and the button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty right half is the design or an image that didn't load in the capture. Check this in a live browser before acting on point 3.
