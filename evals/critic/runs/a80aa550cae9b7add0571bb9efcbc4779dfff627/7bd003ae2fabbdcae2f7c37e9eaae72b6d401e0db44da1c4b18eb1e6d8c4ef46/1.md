**First read.** In version 1 the eye goes straight to **$184,320**. It is about twice the size of every other number and sits in a double-width card, so the page reads at once as "revenue this month, and it's up." In version 2 the eye has nowhere to land. Seven cards of the same size and weight compete equally, and the eye ends up on the empty eighth slot at the right of row two. Both versions feel calm, clean and generic.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal month-to-date sales dashboard for a small retailer's owner or manager, who checks it quickly to see how the month is going.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. It answers the main question, "how much have we sold," before the viewer starts reading. Version 2 removed the hierarchy and gained nothing in return. Both share a weak chart that gives no numbers and no time axis.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **Version 1 has a focal point; version 2 does not.** In v1 Revenue gets a double-width card and a number at roughly 2× the size of the others, so the main measure wins before any reading happens. In v2 Revenue is set like Items per order, so the viewer has to scan all seven labels to find what matters. A secondary measure like Returns ends up with the same weight as revenue. This is a defect for a dashboard you glance at: the glance no longer gives you the answer. Keep v1's approach (Refactoring UI: show importance through size and weight).
2. **Version 2 leaves a gap in the grid.** The empty fourth slot in row two looks like a card that failed to load. It pulls attention to the one place with nothing in it. Version 1's 1+2 / 4 layout fills both rows cleanly. This is a defect, though a minor one.

**Remaining findings for version 1**
1. **The chart has no numbers or time axis** (in the "Revenue by week" panel). Eight bars sit with no week labels, no y-axis or values, and no baseline. Eight weeks also doesn't match "October so far," so the viewer can't tell which bar is this week or how big the differences are. The bars also fill only the left half of a full-width panel, so the right half reads as missing data. Defect. Fix: label each week, give either a light axis or a value on each bar, mark the current or partial week, and either fill the width or shrink the panel (Tufte; Few; Wilke: a bar chart starts at zero, so make the baseline visible).
2. **Most of the changes have no comparison point.** Only Revenue says "on September." The other "+5%" and "+9%" figures leave the viewer to guess: September as a whole, or September to the same date? A month-to-date figure compared against a full month would be misleading. Defect. Fix: state the comparison once, for example "vs. Sept 1–N" under the heading, or on each card.

**Smaller things.**
- Coloring "-0.4 points" on Returns green is correct, since fewer returns is good. But a viewer scanning for minus signs will read it as bad. Consider a small "(better)" or a down arrow paired with the green.
- The card labels are small grey text that looks close to the contrast limit. Check them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.** Version 1's single dominant revenue figure. Plain labels ("Repeat customers", "Items per order") that a stranger can read without help. A restrained palette that saves color for the change figures.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The chart is cut off at the bottom of the capture, so a baseline or axis labels may exist below the fold. Check the full page.
