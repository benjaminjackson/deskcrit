**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers across the top, reads them left to right, and then drops into the large chart, which takes up two thirds of the screen. It's clearly a marketing or growth analytics overview for a small team. It feels calm and tidy, and everything looks green and rising. Then I reach the chart and can't answer anything about it: which line is which channel, what the values are, or which weeks these are.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly overview so someone on the growth or marketing team can see at a glance whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy over the last 12 weeks, and which channel is behind it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The chart takes most of the screen and tells the viewer almost nothing, and one of the three headline numbers contradicts the other two. The layout is fine. The problem is that the information isn't trustworthy or readable.

**What would move it most**

1. **The conversion card contradicts the two cards next to it.** *Defect.* In the top-right card, the rate reads "2.12%, +0.1 pts vs previous 12 weeks" in green. But visits rose 6.1% and sign-ups only 2.4%, so the conversion rate must have **fallen**. Working back from the two other cards, the previous period was about 3,820 / 173,600 ≈ 2.20%, so the change is roughly −0.08 pts, not +0.1. A viewer who does the sum stops trusting the whole dashboard. A viewer who doesn't takes away the wrong conclusion, that conversion is improving, from the one metric the business probably cares about most. This is a number people will act on, so it can't be read two ways. Fix the calculation or the label (it may be comparing something else, such as the last week against the week before). If it really is down, show it as down, in a non-success colour with a down cue that isn't only colour.

2. **The chart has no legend, no axis labels and no values.** *Defect.* The "Visits by channel" panel shows three lines (blue, orange, green) with no channel names, no y-axis scale, no week labels on the x-axis and no units. The viewer can see that three somethings went up, but not which channel is which, how big they are, or when the dip in week 8 happened. The title promises a breakdown by channel and the chart doesn't deliver it. The empty gutter of about 70 px to the left of the plot looks like space reserved for y-axis labels that never rendered. Every chart needs labelled axes with units and a readable scale (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). The fix is to label each line at its right-hand end with the channel name and latest value, which also removes the need for a legend (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). Add a light y-scale with 3 to 4 ticks and week or date labels along the bottom. Also say whether the lines add up to the 184,220 total.

3. **Colour is the only thing telling the series apart, and nothing is emphasised.** *Convention, made worse by finding 2.* Hue alone separates the three lines (WCAG 1.4.1). Orange and green also lie close together in value, which is fragile for red-green colour-blind viewers. Direct labels fix most of this. Beyond that, decide what the chart is meant to show. If the point is which channel is driving growth, make that line the accent and put the other two in grey. A title that states the finding, such as "Paid search drove most of the growth", would do more than the topic label "Visits by channel". That last point is my own view (taste), not a published rule.

**Smaller things.**
- Delta lines in the cards: the green on white is fine for contrast, but colour is the only up/down signal. Add an arrow or ▲/▼ so a drop can't pass for a rise.
- "Last 12 weeks", top right: it's styled like plain text, so it's unclear whether it's a control. If the viewer can change the range, give it the look of a control (border and chevron). If they can't, say the actual dates ("1 Sep – 23 Nov").
- Chart panel: there's a band of about 70 px of empty white below the baseline, and the lines sit in the middle 40% of the plot. Use that space for x-axis labels, or tighten the panel.
- Y-axis baseline: on a line chart a non-zero start is acceptable, but it has to be labelled (Wilke, Few). At the moment the viewer can't tell how big the gaps between lines are.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards: one label, one big number and one comparison against the previous period is the right structure, and showing a bare number with no context would be worse. Keep the size and weight contrast between label and value.
- The restraint: one neutral grey background, white panels, hairline borders and faint gridlines. Nothing competes with the data, so once the chart has labels it will be easy to read.
- Everything fits on one screen. The headline metrics sit top left and the trend comes below them, which is the right order for something people glance at.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the missing chart axes and legend are a design decision or labels that failed to render or load. The empty left gutter suggests the latter, so check in a live browser. Either way, the screen as shown can't be read.
- Hover tooltips on the chart. Even if they exist, they don't replace visible labels on an overview screen.
