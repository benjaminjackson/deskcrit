**First read.** The eye lands first on the three big numbers across the top, reading left to right. Then it drops to the large chart, which takes up 60% of the screen, and finds three coloured lines with nothing to say what they are. This reads as a calm, tidy weekly marketing dashboard for a small SaaS team. It feels clean and capable for about two seconds, and then confusing once you try to read the chart.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal analytics overview for a growth or marketing lead who needs to see quickly how the last 12 weeks went, overall and by channel.

**Verdict.** The KPI row works. The chart, which is the largest thing on the page, tells the viewer almost nothing. It has no legend, no series labels, no y-axis values and no x-axis dates, so "Visits by channel" can't be read by channel or by visits. Until that's fixed, the main element of the dashboard is decoration.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart can't be decoded (defect).** The blue, orange and green lines in "Visits by channel" have no names, so the viewer can't tell which channel is which. The y-axis has four gridlines but no numbers, so they can't tell whether blue is 2× orange or 20× orange. The x-axis has no week labels, so the dips (blue at about week 8, orange at weeks 3 and 5) can't be matched to anything that happened. The only thing a viewer gets is "three lines, roughly going up", and the KPI card already says that. Tufte and Few both require labelled axes with units and a readable scale; Tufte and Wilke say to label series at the data. The fix: put the channel name (and ideally the latest value) at the right end of each line, where the open space already is. Add 3–4 y-axis values with a unit and week or date ticks along the bottom. Colour alone also fails WCAG 1.4.1, so the direct labels solve both problems at once.

2. **The chart answers the least important question (defect, judgment).** The KPI row puts Sign-ups and Conversion rate beside Visits, but the only chart breaks down Visits. If a growth lead opens this dashboard, the question is usually "which channel drives sign-ups, and is conversion moving?", and nothing on the page answers it. The 2.12% conversion card in particular has no trend and no channel split. The fix: either let the chart switch between the three metrics (selecting a card drives the chart), or make it the metric that matters, sign-ups by channel. If the chart stays on visits, at least title it with the finding (for example "Paid search drove most of the growth") rather than only naming the topic.

3. **The deltas have no frame of reference (defect, copy).** "+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks" is clear. But "Last 12 weeks" top right is plain text, not a control the viewer can see they can change, and nothing gives actual dates. The viewer can't tell whether this is calendar weeks or rolling weeks, or whether the current partial week is included. That matters when they quote these numbers to someone else. The fix: show the date range ("Aug 31 – Nov 22") under or in the period selector, and make the selector look clickable (a chevron and a border) if it is one.

**Smaller things**
- The chart panel has about 70 px of empty space below the x-axis baseline and a top gridline with no data near it. Once labels are in, tighten the padding so the plot fills its panel.
- The green delta lines on the cards (around 12–13 px) are the smallest text on the page. They look legible, but check the contrast of that green on white against 4.5:1, since it's the one line that says good or bad.
- The type scale is tight and sensible: page title, KPI numbers, card labels and title, deltas. "Overview" (about 24 px bold) and "Visits by channel" (about 16 px semibold) are fine. No collisions.
- "+0.1 pts" is correct, but some readers will stumble on it. "+0.1 percentage points" fits on the card.

**What's working**
- The KPI cards are disciplined: one label, one large number, one comparison each. They're the same size on an even gutter, which follows Few's rule of always giving a metric context. Keep this structure.
- The restrained neutral palette means the only saturated colour is data (lines and deltas). That's the right instinct; keep chrome grey so colour keeps meaning something.
- One grid governs the page. Card edges, the chart panel and the header all share the same 48 px left and right margins.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- The plot lines and gridlines rendered, but the labels, legend and ticks are absent. Check in a live browser that these aren't failing to load or appearing only on hover. If they appear only on hover, finding 1 still stands, because the still view should read on its own.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" and the cards are interactive.
