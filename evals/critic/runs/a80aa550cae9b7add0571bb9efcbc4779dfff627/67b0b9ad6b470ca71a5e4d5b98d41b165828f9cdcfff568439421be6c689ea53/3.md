**First read.** My eye goes to the headline first, then to the purple bars, then straight to the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. I read the table on the right afterwards, as backup. It looks like a calm, clean analytics page for whoever owns pricing or growth. The first impression is "Growth converts best, and the plans are all close."

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal analytics view that helps a product or revenue team compare trial-to-paid conversion across plans and decide where to act.

**Verdict.** This is a competent, honest chart. The bars start at zero, the labels are direct, the source is given, the definition is stated, and the numbers check out against the table (286 ÷ 9,210 = 3.1%, 38 ÷ 1,120 = 3.4%). The biggest problem is that all four rates are drawn as equally certain. They aren't. Enterprise rests on 38 conversions, so the viewer will rank plans on gaps the data may not support.

**What would move it most**

1. **Every bar looks equally certain, but the sample sizes differ by eight times** (the chart's four bars against the table's Trials column). Starter's 3.1% comes from 9,210 trials. Enterprise's 3.4% comes from 1,120 trials and 38 paid accounts. By a rough calculation, Enterprise's rate could be about a point higher or lower by chance, which covers the whole range on the chart. Growth's lead over Starter is probably real. Enterprise sitting "between Team and Starter" probably isn't. Someone deciding on pricing or onboarding will compare the bar heights and act on the ranking. The trial counts that would warn them sit in a separate table they have to cross-reference themselves. *Defect*: it can lead to a wrong decision. Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16, says estimates from samples should be shown with their uncertainty. **Fix:** add interval whiskers to the bars, or at least put "n = 1,120 trials" under each plan name on the x-axis. That way the reader sees how much data is behind each bar while comparing heights, not afterwards.

2. **The headline names the topic, not the finding.** "Trial to paid conversion by plan" tells the viewer what the chart measures. They still have to work out the answer themselves, and as point 1 shows, they may get it wrong. The finding that holds up here is roughly "Growth trials convert best; the other plans are within a point of each other." Putting that in the headline, or in a one-line caption under the chart, tells the team what they can and can't conclude. *Taste* (common sense; no approved source). On a dashboard that refreshes, the fix may be a short generated summary line instead of a fixed headline.

**Smaller things.**
- Table, right column: it has Trials and Paid but no conversion rate, so it never connects to the chart. Add a "Rate" column, or replace the table with n-labels on the chart as in point 1.
- Table header "Paid": this could mean paid invoices, paid accounts, or revenue. "Converted" or "Paid accounts" matches the chart's subtitle.
- Y-axis: the band from 4% to 5% is empty, and the axis labels repeat what the direct labels on the bars already say. Lighten the axis or drop it, and end the scale near 4.5% (Tufte: data-ink).
- Definition, "Trials still open on 30 October": "open" could mean the trial period or an unpaid invoice. Say which, e.g. "Trials with no paid invoice by 30 October are counted as not converted."
- Right column: the definition block ends at about 400 px and leaves a large empty area beside the lower half of the chart. That's fine for a dashboard, but the right column could make better use of the space, for example with the sample-size note.

**What's working.**
- The data is honest: bars start at zero, there's one colour with no meaningless variation, and labels sit directly on the bars. Keep all of that when you add uncertainty.
- The subtitle and definition spell out the measure: the cohort (Q3 2025 starts), the 30-day window, and what counts as conversion. A stranger reading it won't misread the metric. Keep this pattern for the other tabs.
- The plans are in tier order, which is the natural order here. Don't re-sort them by value.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the bars or table rows show exact counts or intervals on hover, and how the two-column layout reflows on narrow screens.
