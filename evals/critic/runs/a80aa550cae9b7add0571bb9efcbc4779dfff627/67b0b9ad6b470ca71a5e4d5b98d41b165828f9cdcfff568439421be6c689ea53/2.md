**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: an internal analytics view that helps a product or pricing team compare how well each plan's trials turn into paid accounts, and decide where to act.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid and the chart is honest. Its weakness is the analysis it hands the reader. It shows four rates within one point of each other and never says whether the gaps are real or what to take from them. The Definition can also be read two ways, which changes what the rates measure.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart doesn't show whether the differences are real.** *Defect.* The bars run from 3.1% to 4.0%, and the Enterprise rate rests on 38 paid accounts. By my rough calculation, the 95% ranges for Growth (118 of 2,950) and Enterprise (38 of 1,120) overlap almost completely: about 3.3–4.7% and 2.4–4.5%. The chart draws them as clean, exact heights. A reader will conclude "Growth converts best, Enterprise lags" and may act on a gap that could be noise.
   - The checklist rule "Show uncertainty and sample context" asks for exactly this.
   - Fix: add interval whiskers to each bar, or at least the paid/trials count under each label (e.g. "38 of 1,120"). If the gaps aren't significant, say so.

2. **The headline names the topic, not the finding.** *Convention.* "Trial to paid conversion by plan" tells the reader what the chart is, not what it shows, so each viewer draws their own conclusion from the bars, likely the over-confident one from point 1. The rule that titles should state the takeaway comes from data-visualisation practice, but the source in the reference entry isn't on the approved list, so treat this as common sense.
   - Fix: write the finding in the headline or the subtitle. Choose the one the data supports, e.g. "All four plans convert 3–4% of trials; only Growth stands clearly above Starter".

3. **The Definition can be read two ways.** *Defect.* The subtitle says trials count if they became paid "within 30 days". The Definition says trials "still open on 30 October are counted as not converted". The two readings:
   - **Each trial gets its own 30-day window.** Then 30 October only matters for trials started on 30 September, and the sentence confuses more than it helps.
   - **Everything paid by 30 October counts.** Then July trials get about three months to convert and September trials get one. The rates would then mean something different from what the subtitle promises.

   This is the line people quote when they challenge the numbers. Fix: say the rule once, in one way, e.g. "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice is paid within 30 days of the trial starting."

**Smaller things**
- Table on the right: the reader has to divide Paid by Trials to link it to the chart. Add a "Rate" column, or rename "Paid" to "Converted" so it matches the chart's word.
- The right column is empty below the Definition, roughly the lower half of that column. The page feels left-heavy and unfinished. Either let the chart and table share one width, or move the source and definition into that space.
- Table column headers ("Plan", "Trials", "Paid") are small, grey and bold. They look close to the contrast floor, so check them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- The 5.0% gridline adds headroom but no data. Ending the axis at 5% is fine; just keep the lines this faint.

**What's working**
- The bars start at zero, use one colour, and carry direct value labels with no legend. The chart doesn't exaggerate small gaps, and that honesty is worth keeping in any revision.
- Plans are in tier order (Starter → Enterprise), not sorted by value. That's the right choice, because readers think in tiers.
- The source line, date range and definition are all on the page. The reader can check the numbers, and the counts in the table do match the percentages.
