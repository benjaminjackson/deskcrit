**First read.** My eye goes first to the headline, then to the purple bars, and settles on the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. The page reads as a clean internal analytics view for a growth or product team, part of a funnel dashboard. It feels calm, competent and neutral. After that first look, there is no clear message to take away.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal report page meant to help a product, growth or pricing team decide which plans' trials need work. The audience reads data well but is short on time.

**Verdict.** The chart is honest and well made. As a decision tool it is weak: it shows four rates that differ by less than one percentage point and never says whether that gap is real or what to do about it. The biggest problem is that the page leaves the conclusion to the reader. Its sample sizes also suggest at least one comparison (Enterprise) may be noise.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headline names a topic, not a finding.** "Trial to paid conversion by plan" tells the reader what the chart is about. They then have to work out for themselves that Growth leads, Starter trails, and that all four sit between 3% and 4%. In a funnel dashboard, the person who opens this tab wants the answer. Make the headline the claim, e.g. "Growth trials convert best at 4.0%; Starter lags at 3.1%". Then let the bars back it up. A light accent on the bar the claim is about, with the others muted, would show the reader where to look. Right now all four bars are the same purple, so none stands out. **Taste** (the usual source for this rule isn't on the approved list), but on an analytics page it decides whether anyone acts on the chart.

2. **The chart presents tiny differences as solid, and Enterprise rests on 38 conversions.** I worked out rough ranges from the table. Enterprise's 3.4% comes from 38 of 1,120 trials, so the plausible range is about ±1 point, roughly 2.3% to 4.5%. That overlaps every other bar. Growth's 4.0% is about ±0.7 points. Bars of equal confidence with bold labels at one decimal suggest more precision than the data has. A reader could cut Enterprise trial investment, or praise the Growth flow, over a gap that might be noise. Add error bars or interval ranges, or at least a note on sample size near the bars. Avoid stating the Enterprise comparison as a finding (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16, on showing uncertainty). **Defect.** It can mislead the decision this page exists to support.

3. **The subtitle and the definition describe two different conversion rules.** The subtitle says "became paid accounts **within 30 days**", which is a window per trial. The definition says "Trials still open on **30 October** are counted as not converted", which is one fixed cutoff for everyone. Take a trial that started on 1 July and paid on day 45. Under the subtitle it doesn't count; under the definition it seems to. A reader who checks these numbers against their own data will get different results depending on which line they believe. Use one rule and state it the same way in both places. "Within 30 days of starting the trial" is the clearest. **Defect.** This is the line people will hold the numbers to.

**Smaller things**
- The table on the right gives Trials and Paid but no rate. Readers have to match it with the chart by eye, or do the division themselves. Add a "Conversion" column, or a rough interval column, so the table is the full reference.
- The right column ends at about y≈420, leaving the lower half of the page empty. This space is left over, not designed. Uncertainty notes or a short "what this means" line could go there.
- Every bar has a direct label, so gridlines and the y-axis add very little. Either drop them or fade them further (Tufte, data-ink).
- The y-axis runs to 5.0% with no bar near it. That's fine for honesty, but the extra headroom makes the differences look even smaller next to the large labels. This is a minor point once uncertainty is shown.

**What's working**
- The bars start at zero, the plans are in natural tier order, and there are direct labels and a dated source line. The chart is honest and easy to read, so keep all of that.
- The table numbers match the chart exactly (286/9,210 = 3.1%, 185/5,140 = 3.6%, 118/2,950 = 4.0%, 38/1,120 = 3.4%), and the table is set in right-aligned tabular figures. It's a reliable reference.
- The type scale is restrained: a headline, a subhead, section labels, body and small detail, using weight and grey rather than extra sizes. The page looks clean without looking empty.
