**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. Eight lines of near-identical grey-black text sit in the top half of a white page, so I read it straight down like one paragraph. Only on the second pass do I see that the shorter lines are questions and the longer ones are answers. It reads as a help or FAQ panel for a bakery's order pickup. It feels tidy but flat, like a draft that hasn't been styled yet.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help section on a bakery's online ordering page or app. It answers common questions for customers who have ordered or are about to. Its job is to let someone find their question fast and leave knowing exactly what to do.

**Verdict.** It doesn't yet do its job. The headings are set no larger or bolder than the answers (in a close crop they look slightly smaller), so the page can't be scanned. Several answers also leave out the one detail the customer will act on.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings don't read as headings.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two are regular weight and the same colour as the answers. In a close crop they also look a point or so smaller than the body. The eye can't jump from question to question, so someone looking for "cancel" has to read the whole page. Two elements meant to differ have to differ clearly in at least one dimension (Dair, *Design with Type*), and a difference that's too small reads as an accident. Make the headings semibold or bold in the same face. Then either keep them at body size or step up clearly, and stop them sitting below the answers. *Refactoring UI*'s "bolder, not bigger" is the right tool here. (Defect: the page can't be scanned.)

2. **The answers leave out what the customer acts on.** These are deadlines, conditions and safety information, the lines people hold a shop to:
   - **"Cancelled up to a day ahead."** This can mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time before the day of pickup. A customer who reads it the second way and cancels at 9 pm the night before for a 10 am pickup will think they're within the rule when they may not be. The answer also never says *how* to cancel, though the first answer does say how to change a time.
   - **"Show it at the counter along with a name."** Whose name? The person collecting will be unsure whether to give yours or theirs. Say which.
   - **"Holds orders for two hours after the chosen time."** It doesn't say what happens after that: is the order released, or charged and not refunded? That consequence is why the line matters to the reader.
   - **Allergies.** The answer points to a card by the till. Someone ordering online with an allergy can't see that card before they pay. Either show ingredients where people order, or say how to ask before ordering.

   (Defect: each gap can lead to a missed cancellation, a refused collection, or an allergy risk.)

3. **The lines are too long for comfortable reading.** The answers run about 820 px wide. "Give the person your order number… along with a name." is roughly 95 characters on one line, well past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*). With answers this short the cost is small, but the wide measure also makes the column feel loose and unanchored on the page. Cap the text column at about 600–640 px at this size. (Convention, with a mild cost here.)

**Smaller things.**
- The section has no title. Add something like "Pickup and orders: common questions" so it's clear what this is before reading the first question.
- "anything." is left alone on its own line under the allergies answer. A shorter measure will probably fix it. Otherwise rebalance the line.
- The headings are topic labels ("Allergies and ingredients"), while the answers are written as instructions. Phrasing the headings as the customer's own question ("Can I change my pickup time?") matches how people look for help. This is taste.
- The block sits high and left, with the bottom half of the frame empty. That's fine for a help panel, but if this is the whole screen, a way to contact the bakery would fill the obvious gap: what do I do if my question isn't here?

**What's working.**
- The spacing is right. Each heading sits about 10 px above its answer and about 30 px below the previous one, so once the headings get weight, the groups will read correctly with no rules or boxes needed. Keep those intervals.
- The voice is plain, warm and concrete: "After that the bread has already been baked for you" explains the rule in the customer's terms instead of just stating a policy. Keep that tone while adding the missing details.
- The text contrast looks solid: near-black on white, at a comfortable body size.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell whether the headings are meant to expand and collapse like an accordion. If they are, they need a visible cue such as a chevron, and that changes how much weight they need.
