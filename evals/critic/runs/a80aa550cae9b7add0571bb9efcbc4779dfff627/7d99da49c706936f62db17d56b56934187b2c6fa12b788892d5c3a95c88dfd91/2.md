**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It starts at the top-left line, "Changing your pickup time", because that line comes first, not because it stands out. It then reads down a grey-black block of eight lines that all look alike. Only on reading does it become clear that this is a bakery's help or FAQ section, made of four question-and-answer pairs. It feels calm and plain but undifferentiated, like a text file rather than a page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the FAQ or help section on a bakery's online ordering site. Customers scan it to find the one question they came with and act on the answer.

**Verdict.** The copy is mostly clear and human, but the layout fails at its main job, which is letting someone scan for their question. The headings can't be told apart from the answers. Two answers that commit the customer to something (cancelling, and collecting for someone else) can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The headings don't read as headings.** This is a defect. "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two have the same weight and colour as the answers, and look slightly *smaller* than them. Size is meant to rank them, but here it ranks them backwards. Someone scanning for "cancel" has to read every line, because nothing marks where one topic ends and the next starts except the spacing. This is Dair's weak-contrast problem: a difference too small to see is worse than none, and here it runs the wrong way. Make the headings clearly different in one way and leave the rest alone. Semibold at the same size is usually enough (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). Alternatively, set the answers in a lighter grey so the headings stand forward. The type scale currently shows two sizes a pixel or two apart, which reads as a mistake. Use one size and let weight do the job.

2. **The cancellation answer can be read two ways, and it's the line people will hold you to.** This is a defect in the copy. "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot or by the end of the day before. A customer with a 9 am pickup who cancels at 6 pm the evening before will think they're in time under one reading and late under the other. That leads to a dispute at the counter or a lost refund. The answer also never says *how* to cancel, while the pickup-time answer does ("Open your order and…"). State the cutoff exactly ("until 24 hours before your pickup time" or "until 6 pm the day before"), say how to do it, and say what happens after the cutoff for the customer ("you'll still be charged" or whatever the rule is), not just for the bread.

3. **"Along with a name" leaves a gap.** This is a defect in the copy. Whose name: the collector's or the person who ordered? If the collector gives their own name and staff expect the account holder's, someone leaves without their bread. Say it outright: "They'll need your order number and your name."

4. **The allergy answer only helps someone already in the shop.** This is a defect against the page's job. People read this page before or while ordering online, and the answer sends them to "a card by the till". A customer with an allergy can't check before buying, which is when it matters most. Point to where the ingredients are listed online (or say there's no online list), and give a way to ask the baker before visiting.

5. **The lines are too long for comfortable reading.** This is a convention. The text column runs about 820 px, and the longest answers come to roughly 95 characters per line, well past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). With only one or two lines per answer it doesn't hurt much, but it adds to the sense of a flat wall of text. Narrow the column to about 600–650 px. That will also make the empty right side look intended rather than left over.

**Smaller things.**
- There's no title above the list: nothing says "Questions about your order" or similar, so the four items begin without context. Add a page heading. It also gives the eye a place to land first.
- Runts: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergy answer, and "after the chosen time." nearly does in the first answer. Narrowing the column will probably fix both. Check them afterwards.
- "The bakery holds orders for two hours after the chosen time" stops at the fact. Add what it means for the reader: what happens if they're later than that.
- Headings are topic labels; the visitor arrives with a question. Phrasing them as questions ("Can someone else collect my order?") makes them easier to match by eye. That's my taste, not a rule.

**What's working.**
- Spacing groups the content correctly. Each heading sits close to its answer, with a clearly bigger gap before the next pair, so the four topics hold together even without weight contrast. Keep these spacings when you add the weight change.
- The voice is plain, short and specific ("After that the bread has already been baked for you"). It explains the rule in the reader's terms instead of quoting policy. Keep that tone when you tighten the wording.
- Text is dark on white and looks comfortably above 4.5:1. It's a readable sans at a generous size.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the headings are meant to expand and collapse like an accordion. If they are, they need a visible cue such as a chevron. Check this in the live page.
