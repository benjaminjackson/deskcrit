**First read.** There's nowhere for the eye to land. It goes to the top-left line and reads down, because every line looks about the same. It takes a sentence or two to work out that this is an FAQ or help panel for a bakery's order-and-collect service. It feels calm, plain and quiet, but more like a draft than a finished page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help or FAQ section on a bakery's online ordering site. Customers who already have an order, or are about to place one, scan it for the one question they have.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The questions look the same as the answers, so a reader scanning for their question has to read everything. The two lines that set deadlines can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The question headings look like body text, and actually look slightly smaller.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else" and the other two headings have the same weight and colour as the answers. They also look a touch smaller than the answers. The page is meant to be scanned, but the four questions don't stand out from the answers, so the reader can't skip to theirs. They read it from the top like an essay. Dair: when two elements are meant to differ, the difference has to be obvious at a glance. A heading within a pixel or two of its text reads as an accident. *Convention, and a defect here.* Fix: make each heading clearly different from its answer in a way you can see at a glance. Bold or semibold at the same size or one step up would do it, or keep the heading dark and drop the answer to a softer grey. Don't make the headings smaller than the answers.

2. **The deadlines can be read two ways.** These are the lines customers will hold the bakery to. *Defect.*
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead." Ahead of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot or by the end of the day before. Someone with a 9 am Saturday pickup who reads "the day before" might try to cancel at 10 pm Friday, get refused, and feel cheated. Fix: state the cutoff as a clock time relative to the pickup, e.g. "You can cancel until 24 hours before your pickup time".
   - "The bakery holds orders for two hours after the chosen time." It stops at the fact. It doesn't say what happens to the customer at 2 h 01 m: is the bread resold, refunded, or gone? Fix: end on the consequence for them, e.g. "…after that we can't guarantee your bread."

3. **"Along with a name" doesn't say whose name.** The person collecting could read it as their own name or the buyer's name, and if they give the wrong one they get turned away at the counter. *Defect.* Fix: "…along with your name (the name on the order)."

**Smaller things**
- Line length: the single-line answers ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") run about 95 characters, beyond Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column to about 600–650 px at this size.
- There's no page title. Nothing says "Help" or "Ordering questions", so the viewer has to work out what this is from the content. Add one heading that clearly outranks the questions.
- The text sits left of centre with a wide empty band to the right and a large empty area below. The white space looks left over rather than shaped. That's fine for a help panel if it sits inside a real page; it's worth checking in context.
- Allergies: "a card by the till" helps only once the customer is in the shop. Someone ordering online with an allergy can't check before they buy. Say where they can see ingredients before ordering, or how to ask. This is content, but it's the one answer with a real safety cost.
- "Ask the baker if you are unsure about anything" has a one-word runt ("anything.") on its second line. It'll go away once the measure is narrowed.

**What's working**
- The spacing already shows the groups. Each heading sits tight to its answer, with a clearly bigger gap before the next pair, so once the headings get weight the structure will read straight away. Keep these gaps.
- The voice is plain, short and human ("the bread has already been baked for you"). Most lines say who does what. Keep that tone when you tighten the deadline lines.
- One clean sans and dark text on white with good contrast. Nothing needs taking away.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether these are expandable items (an accordion) or static text. If they expand, the headings need a visible cue such as a chevron, and that cue is missing here.
