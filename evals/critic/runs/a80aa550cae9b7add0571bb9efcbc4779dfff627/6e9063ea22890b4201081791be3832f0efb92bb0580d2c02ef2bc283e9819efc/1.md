**First read.** The eye goes to the bold headline "Get the weekly show list", then down to the purple Subscribe button, with the station name above in small purple type. It reads straight away as an email sign-up for a local radio station's schedule. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. The two versions look the same at a glance.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a sign-up page that gets a listener of Riverside FM to subscribe to the Friday email of next week's shows, with a fallback that lets them see a sample first.

**Verdict.** Version 1 is slightly better. The only change is the corner radius on the two buttons. In version 1 the Subscribe and "Read last week's list" buttons have rounded corners (about 6 px) that match the email field and the card. In version 2 the buttons have nearly square corners (about 2 px), but the email field above them still has the rounder corners. Both versions work, and the layout, copy and hierarchy are otherwise identical.

**What would move it most**
1. **The button corners in version 2 don't match the rest of the form** *(convention, a small cost).* The email field, Subscribe and "Read last week's list" sit in a tight stack 12–18 px apart, so you compare their shapes without meaning to. In version 2 the field is rounded and the buttons below it are square, so the stack looks put together from two different sets of parts. That doesn't stop anyone subscribing, but it takes a little polish off a form that is otherwise very tidy. Visual consistency is basic advice in Refactoring UI and Material Design: give each type of control one shape. Version 1 already does this, so keep its radius. If you want square buttons, make the field and card corners square too.

**Smaller things** (these apply to both versions)
- The placeholder "you@example.com" looks fairly pale grey. Check that it reaches 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). It doesn't block anything, because the "Email address" label carries the meaning.
- The station name "Riverside FM 97.4" is small purple text on white. It looks readable, but measure the contrast.
- Below the button there's no line about how often the email comes or how to leave, such as "One email a week. Unsubscribe anytime." The body text already says "every Friday", so this is a minor point.

**What's working**
- The copy is specific. "Every Friday we send the next week's programmes, guest names, and any changes to the schedule" tells you exactly what you'll get and when. It doesn't fall back on generic newsletter wording.
- "Read last week's list" is a strong second option. You can judge the email by looking at a real issue instead of taking the page's word for it. It's correctly styled as an outline button, so it doesn't compete with Subscribe.
- The type scale is clean: a headline, body text, and one small bold size for the label and station name. There are no sizes that nearly collide.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- What happens on submit: whether there's a confirmation, and what an error looks like when the email is invalid.

Better: version 1
