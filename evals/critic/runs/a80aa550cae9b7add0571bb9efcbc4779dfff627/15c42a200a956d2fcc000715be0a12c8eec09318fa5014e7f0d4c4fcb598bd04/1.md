**First read.** The eye goes to the bold "River ferry timetable" heading, then down the time columns. It reads straight away as a calm, clean timetable for local riders, with light grey rules and lots of air. Then it reaches the sentence under the table, which says there are "heavy black lines around every cell". There aren't any. That makes the reader stop and wonder whether they're looking at the right thing.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a public timetable that lets someone quickly find the next ferry from North landing that runs on the day they're travelling.

**Verdict.** The table itself mostly works: it's quiet, readable and well spaced. The biggest problems are the false sentence under it and a layout where the reader has to cross wide gaps and check every row to answer "which boats run on my day?"

**What would move it most**
1. **The sentence under the table is wrong and should go.** "The heavy black lines around every cell make this timetable a mess" describes a different design. The rules here are pale hairlines, and only between rows. As the last thing on the page it is all copy and no information. A rider reading it as a notice may wonder if there's a newer or different timetable. Remove it, or replace it with something a rider needs, such as the crossing time or the season dates. *Defect.*
2. **Riders have to check every row to see which boats run on their day.** The "Runs" column sits about 600 px right of the departure time. A weekend rider has to read all seven rows and track each one across the page to find that 12:40 and 17:10 don't run for them and 19:30 runs only for them. The day you're travelling is the first filter for this job, but it's the last column, and "Daily" is repeated five times so the exceptions blend in. Fix: make the exceptions stand out. Either split into Weekday and Weekend timetables, or move "Runs" next to "Leaves north" and show only the exceptions, marked in the time cell. *Defect.*
3. **The columns are much wider than their contents.** Four short columns are stretched across about 1080 px, so each value floats about 250–300 px from the next. Reading along a row works only because of the rules. Narrow the table to fit its content, or group the columns closer together, so each row reads as one line ("07:20 → 07:32, daily"). *Taste, but it affects how fast people can look things up* (Tufte: data–ink; Refactoring UI on letting tables size to their content).
4. **"Period" takes the most prominent column and adds almost nothing.** "Morning / Midday / Afternoon / Evening" is just a vaguer version of the time next to it, and each label repeats on consecutive rows. Readers have to skip it to reach the departure time, which is the thing they came for. Drop it, or turn it into small group headings with extra space between groups, and make "Leaves north" the first and strongest column. *Taste.*

**Smaller things.**
- Subtitle, "spring season": give the actual dates (e.g. "1 Mar – 31 May"). Validity dates are something riders act on, and "spring" can mean different date ranges to different people.
- Every crossing takes 12 minutes. Saying so once ("Crossing takes 12 min") would let you drop or lighten the arrival column.
- The return trip (quay to north) isn't shown. If it's on another page, say so in a line under the table.
- Times: check that they use tabular figures (in Inter, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) so 11:00 and 17:10 line up exactly.
- Header row: "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are clipped. "Leaves North landing" and "Arrives Market quay" match the subtitle and read as place names.

**What's working.**
- Light hairline rules only between rows, with no vertical lines or boxes. This is the right restraint (Tufte). Keep it.
- The type scale is tight: one large bold heading, one grey subtitle, and the body set at a single size with bold column headers. Every level has an obvious job.
- 24-hour HH:MM times in their own column are clear and can't be misread.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the table behaves on a narrow phone screen. Four wide columns will need to reflow, and many ferry riders will check this on a phone at the landing.
