**First read.** Three solid cards in orange, green and purple, all the same size and weight. My eye goes to the orange one first, but only because warm colours come forward and it sits top-left, not because the layout points there. It then moves across the three headlines and lands in the empty lower halves of the cards. It reads as a section of a swimming pool's website listing what's on. It feels bright and friendly but blank, like a template waiting for its content.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a section of a local pool's homepage that should get adults and families to choose a session and book or turn up.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do that job. Three equal offers compete, with no ask, no prices and no way to act, and over half of each card is empty colour. The line under the cards says "one offer clearly leads the page", but that's just text in the image, and it's false: nothing leads.

**What would move it most**

1. **No offer leads, and no action is offered (defect).** The three cards have the same size, headline weight, text length and position. Each also has a fully saturated colour of about the same lightness. So the viewer has to give all three equal attention and decide alone. There's no button, link or "Book" on any card, so after reading the viewer has nowhere to go. The eye stops at the empty bottom of the card. The page needs one clear order: one element seen first, the rest after (Müller-Brockmann; Dair: weak contrast is worse than none). If the pool's priority is the paid adult classes, make "Learn to swim" dominant through size, position or being the only saturated card, and calm the other two. Whatever leads, every card needs one specific ask that names what happens, e.g. "Book a Tuesday class". A vague "Learn more" won't do it.

2. **Over half of each card is dead space (defect).** The text fills roughly the top 45% of each card. Below that is about 300 px of flat colour that holds nothing. That's not space that gives the content room. It's leftover space, and it makes the cards look unfinished or half-loaded (Ruder: white space should be gathered and in tension with the content, not left over). It also pushes anything below the cards down the page. Either put the decision-making content there (time, price, who it's for, the ask) or shorten the cards to fit what they hold.

3. **The copy leaves out what a person needs in order to come (defect).** Each description gives a nice detail ("a coach in the water beside you", "floats and toys") but none gives the facts people act on: no times except "from six", no price, no age range, no booking or drop-in rule, no length of course. "Tuesday evenings": what time, and for how many weeks? "Sunday afternoons": which hours? "Three speeds to choose from" means something to regular swimmers but nothing to a newcomer. "Join the lanes" is regulars' language too. These are exactly the details people remember and hold the pool to, so they belong on the cards, worded so they can only be read one way (e.g. "Tuesdays 7–8pm, 6 weeks, £X").

4. **Three loud colours with no job (taste, edging into defect).** Orange, green and purple are each fully saturated and fill equal areas. The colours don't mean anything (they aren't programmes, ages or levels), and together they drown each other out, so colour can't do its usual work of marking what matters (Refactoring UI; Itten on saturation). The orange and green are also close in lightness, so viewers with red-green colour blindness will see two similar muddy cards. That costs little here because the cards are labelled, but it confirms the colour carries nothing. Pick a colour system: one brand colour for the lead offer and quieter tints for the rest, or colours tied to a real meaning such as adults, everyone, and families.

**Smaller things.**
- The margins are lopsided: about 56 px on the left and about 96 px on the right of the row of cards. Centre the row or run it to a shared right edge.
- There's no section heading. The viewer isn't told what these three cards belong to (e.g. "What's on at the pool this week").
- "from six": "every morning" implies 6am, but say "6am" and give a closing time.

**What's working.**
- The type is clean and well judged. There are two clear levels: a heavy, tightly leaded display headline and an easy-to-read body size. Headline breaks fall at natural phrase points ("Learn to / swim", "Family / hour"). Keep this scale and add weight or colour for any new levels, not more sizes.
- White text has enough contrast on all three backgrounds; by eye each looks well above 4.5:1. Confirm with a contrast checker.
- The descriptions have a warm, specific voice ("a coach in the water beside you") that sounds like a real place. Keep that tone when adding the facts.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable, and whether they have hover or focus states. If the whole card is the link, it needs a visible cue that it's clickable, because nothing on it looks interactive now.
