**Verdict.** As an editorial piece it works: clear headline, readable text, a picture that matches the words. As a page meant to get people to visit, it stops short. It makes you want to go but doesn't tell you when, how much, or what to do next. That gap matters most. Second, the picture floats against the text instead of lining up with it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The visit has no details you could act on (defect, if the job is getting visitors).** The second and third paragraphs say the tower is "open to visitors on summer weekends" and that "tickets are sold at the cottage by the car park". A reader who wants to go still has to look up which months, what hours, the ticket price, and where Gull Point is. The only concrete thing in the closing line is scones. The person who wrote it knows those answers; the reader doesn't. They'll either search elsewhere or forget about it. Fix: keep the story in the first two paragraphs. Then make the last part a short, scannable block that sets the dates, opening hours, price and location apart from the story (as a short list or with a small label). Add one specific next step if there is one, such as "Plan your visit" linking to directions or ticket times. Avoid a vague "Find out more". If the page is only a story with no visit to promote, ignore this and the piece is basically done.
2. **The picture lines up with nothing in the text (convention: pictures take their edges from the text, Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).** The square runs from about y=230 to 573. The headline starts near y=145 and the text ends near y=500. So its top sits 85 px below the headline, its bottom hangs about 70 px below the last line, and it's centred on neither. To the eye, the two halves look placed one at a time rather than built together. Fix: align the picture's top with the headline's cap height (or the first line of body text). Or size it so its top and bottom match the text block. Either way the two columns would read as one unit.

**Smaller things.**
- Paragraph 1 ends with "room." alone on its own line, a runt (Bringhurst). Adjust the measure slightly or rewrite a few words so at least two words go to the last line.
- The gap between paragraphs (about 14 px extra on a roughly 30 px line pitch) is only just enough to show a break. Use about half a line to a full line so the three parts separate at a glance.
- The illustration is a generic flat-vector lighthouse: a tower standing straight in the water, with no headland and no gulls. Anyone's lighthouse page could use it. Something specific to Gull Point (the point itself, the bay, the ferry the text mentions) would make the picture as particular as the words (taste).
