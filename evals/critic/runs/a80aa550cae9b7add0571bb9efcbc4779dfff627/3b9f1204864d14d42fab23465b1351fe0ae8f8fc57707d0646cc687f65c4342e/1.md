**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, then moves along four large prices: $0, $12, $39, $99. Nothing stops it. The four cards look identical, so this reads as a tidy, neutral SaaS pricing table. It feels calm and competent, and also anonymous. Then you read the features, and one plan doesn't make sense: Free gives you 3 editors and the paid Solo plan gives you 1.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the pricing section of a SaaS site for a collaborative design or document tool. It needs to help a visitor pick the right plan fast and get them to click.

**Verdict.** The craft is clean, but the page doesn't do its job well. It shows four plans as equals and makes no recommendation. On top of that, its feature list contradicts itself, so the step up from Free to Solo looks like a downgrade.

**What would move it most**

1. **Free beats Solo on editors** (defect). Look at the second feature row: Free has "3 editors" and Solo has "1 editor". Anyone comparing the two cheapest plans sees that paying $12 takes away two seats. They'll suspect a typo or a trap, and either way they stop trusting the table. The intended reading is probably "Solo is for one person". If so, say it in a way that doesn't look like a loss, for example "Just you" or "1 editor (you)". Or change Free to 1 editor. The numbers should only go up from left to right.

2. **No plan is recommended, and all four buttons are identical** (convention, with a real cost here). Every card has the same border, the same weight and the same filled blue button, so the viewer has to work out the choice alone. Material Design 3 ("Buttons") says to give a view one high-emphasis action and to style the others lower, for example as outlined buttons. Pick the plan you want most people on, probably Team for "small studios". Give it the filled button, a stronger border or tint, and a label such as "Most popular". Make the other three buttons outlined. Then the eye has somewhere to land, and the row reads as a recommendation rather than a menu.

3. **The Company card mixes two messages** (defect). It shows a firm "$99 / month" but the button says "Talk to us". So can I buy it at $99 or not? A visitor who is ready to pay is sent to a sales call, and a visitor who expects custom pricing has already been told a number. Choose one. If it's self-serve, use "Choose Company". If it needs sales, use "From $99 / month" or "Custom", and make the button a specific ask such as "Book a 15-minute call". "Named contact" has the same problem: name what the buyer gets, e.g. "A named account manager".

4. **The headline could sit on anyone's page** (taste/copy). "Plans for every size of team" describes the table, not the product, so the visitor can't tell what is different here. The subhead holds the real selling point, "unlimited viewers", and it's set in grey at body size. Land it on the viewer: "Share with as many people as you like. You only pay for editors." That also explains why the plans are priced by editors.

**Smaller things**
- The gap between the Team and Company cards looks wider than the other two (about 37 px against about 24 px). The row stops looking evenly spaced and Company looks detached. Use one fixed gap (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- Every card has about 70 px of empty white below its button. The cards look unfinished. Trim the bottom padding, or put a "See all features" link there.
- "Community help", "Email help" and "Priority help" are categories, not outcomes. Say what the buyer gets, e.g. "Replies within 1 business day".
- "Full history" next to "7 days / 90 days / 1 year" probably means unlimited. Say "Unlimited history" so it matches "Unlimited projects".
- The "For trying it out" taglines and "/ month" look like a fairly light grey at small size. Check them against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working**
- The type scale is tight and clear: headline, large price, plan name, small text. Price is the dominant level in each card, which is right for this page.
- The feature rows are in the same order on every card, so the eye can read straight across a row (projects, editors, history, help). Keep that consistent order.
- One accent colour, used only for actions, keeps the page quiet. Once one plan is recommended, that blue will tell the viewer where to click.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether there is a monthly/annual toggle or a per-seat note elsewhere on the page. It affects how "$99 / month for 100 editors" reads.
