**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a visitor information page or panel for a reservoir site. It's meant to show recent rainfall and tell people how to get there.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The chart is the focal point but it says nothing: no categories, no values, no units, no time period. A visitor can see that the middle bar is tallest and nothing else. The second problem is that the page title covers two unrelated blocks, so neither one is clearly introduced.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read** *(defect)*. The three bars in the left panel have no category labels under them, no value axis or numbers, no unit (mm? inches?) and no period (which months or years?). The viewer sees "middle is more" and has to guess what is being measured, so the main element on the page tells them nothing. Every chart needs labelled categories, values with units, and a date or source (Tufte, *Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). Fix: put the category under each bar, add the value with its unit at the top of each bar (with only three bars, direct labels work better than an axis), and say what period it covers.
2. **The title states a topic, not a finding** *(convention; the approved source for this is uncertain, so treat it as my judgment)*. "Rainfall at the reservoir" names a subject. A visitor wants the answer: is it wet, is the water high, does it matter for their visit? Fix: make the headline the takeaway, e.g. "March was the wettest month this spring: 84 mm." The figure here is a placeholder. Then the chart backs up a claim instead of being decoration.
3. **The title spans two unrelated blocks** *(defect)*. "Rainfall at the reservoir" sits over both columns, so "Getting there" reads as part of the rainfall story and the viewer stops to work out how they connect. They don't. Fix: either give the chart its own heading and make the page title the site name (so rainfall and directions are both sections under it), or split them into separate panels with equal-rank headings.

**Smaller things.**
- Chart frame: the dark 3 px border and the pale blue fill add ink but carry no data (Tufte, data-ink). Drop the border, and keep the tint only if it's needed to group the chart. The baseline alone is enough.
- Layout: the content stops about 60% of the way down and leaves an unplanned empty band at the bottom. Either centre the block vertically or let the content set the page height.
- "Getting there": the heading sits close to its paragraph, which is good. The tops line up, but the paragraph's first baseline doesn't match anything in the chart. That's minor and only worth fixing if a grid is being set up.
- The copy says "number twelve bus" and "every twenty minutes" in words. People scan for a route number and a frequency, so "Bus 12" and "every 20 minutes" are found faster. The same goes for "after 4 pm."
- Bar colour: the blue on the pale tint looks well above 3:1, so no issue there.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three sizes (page title, section heading, body) with clear steps between them, all in one sans family. Keep it.
- The "Getting there" text is set well. The line length looks like about 45 to 50 characters, the leading is comfortable, the left edge is flush with a decent rag, and the copy is specific (gate, interval, time, location). That's the most useful content on the page.
- The bars sit on a zero baseline with even widths and gaps, so once values are added, the lengths will be honest.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart has a tooltip, alt text, or a data table somewhere else. If it doesn't, the chart's meaning isn't available to screen-reader users either, and it needs a text summary.
