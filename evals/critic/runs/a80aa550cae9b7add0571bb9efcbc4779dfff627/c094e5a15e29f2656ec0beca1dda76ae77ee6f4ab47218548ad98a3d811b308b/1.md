**First read.** The eye goes to the bold green headline "What we do each week", then down into two paragraphs of serif body text, then to the three green numbers (412, 96, 4). It reads as the "about us" section of a local food bank's site: calm, plain and trustworthy. The two versions are identical except for one thing. In version 1 the body paragraphs are centred, while the headline and stats above and below them sit flush left. In version 2 everything shares one left edge.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this tells donors, volunteers and people who need help how the food bank works each week, and builds trust with concrete facts.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. Version 1's centred paragraphs break the page's one left edge and make eight lines of prose slow and tiring to read. Version 2 fixes that and is a clean, readable section. What still holds it back is that the section ends on numbers with nothing to do next.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **Centred body text in version 1 (defect).** Both paragraphs are centred under a left-aligned headline and left-aligned stats.
   - **Harder to read:** with centred text, every line starts at a different place, so the eye has to hunt for the start of each new line. Across four-line paragraphs of long sentences, that makes reading slow and choppy. Centring works for a few short lines, not running prose (Butterick, *Practical Typography*; Bringhurst).
   - **Muddled layout:** the headline and stats sit on the x≈96 edge, but the paragraphs float between them. The block looks like it belongs to another layout, and the column reads as three pieces, not one.
   - **The fix is version 2:** set everything flush left on the same edge as the headline and stats. Keep it.

**Remaining findings for version 2**
1. **The section ends with no next step (defect for the job).** The copy is concrete and good. It even tells a reader who can't travel that "a driver will bring the parcel to your door". But after the stats there is only empty space. Someone who has just learned they need a referral, or that the food bank needs drivers, has nowhere to go except back up to the nav. Fix: under the stats, add one specific link for each audience, such as "How to get a referral" and "Drop food at Carver Road". Use the page's own facts rather than a generic "Get involved".
2. **The heading and the numbers use different time periods (defect, copy).** The headline promises "each week", but the lead stat is "412 parcels last month". A reader has to convert to work out the weekly figure, about 100, which is the number the heading sets them up for. Fix: give the weekly figure, or change the heading so it doesn't promise a weekly view.

**Smaller things.**
- Body lines look about 80–85 characters long, a little above the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrowing the column slightly, or setting the text a touch larger, would make it more comfortable to read.
- "96 volunteers" and "4 collection points" have no reference point. "Volunteers" could mean active each week or registered in total. Say which.
- The whole right half of the page is empty. That's acceptable in a narrow reading column, but a photo of the depot or a map of the four centres would make the "east of the city" claim concrete.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific: the days, the depot on Carver Road, three days of meals, how referrals work and home delivery. It reads like this food bank and could not be anyone else's.
- The type pairing is clear. A bold sans is used for the heading, stats and labels, and a readable serif for prose. There are only about four sizes, each with a clear job.
- The stats are in the brand green, which gives the eye a second place to land without competing with the headline.

Better: version 2
