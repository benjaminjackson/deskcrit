**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Booking links that fill your calendar", then to the blue "Create your link" button, then drops to the row of four cards. Nothing holds it on the right: the top right half of the page is empty. It reads as a clean, competent SaaS landing page for a scheduling tool. It feels calm and trustworthy, but you could swap the name for any competitor's and nothing would change.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero and first screen of a marketing page for a scheduling product. Judging by the customer row, it's aimed at small appointment-based businesses (dentists, tutors, physios, coaches, vets), and its job is to get them to create a booking link.

**Verdict.** The layout is tidy and well made, but the page never shows the product and never speaks to its real audience. A visitor is asked to sign up for something they haven't seen, sold with a line every scheduling tool uses.

**What would move it most**

1. **The product is missing, and the empty right half shows it.** Right of the headline, from the nav down to the cards, about 550 × 330 px is blank. It isn't space giving something room. It's what's left over where a visual should be. This product is something the visitor's own clients will see: a page of open time slots. Showing it is the fastest way to explain it. Right now the viewer has to imagine it from the sentence "let people pick a time that works". The only way to see it is the small underlined "See a live example" link. *Defect*: it slows understanding and leaves the main ask resting on trust. Fix: put the booking page itself in that space, ideally the live example, showing a real customer's slots in the guest's time zone. The headline then explains a picture instead of standing in for one.

2. **The copy could sit on any scheduling tool's page, and the stronger ask is the weaker button.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "keep every meeting in the right place" describe the whole category. "Fill your calendar" can also read two ways: more clients, or no free time left. The customer row shows who this is really for: practices whose clients book appointments. The cards hold the lines those people care about, like "cut down on no-shows" and "nobody has to do the math", but they're buried in 15 px body text. *Defect* (copy). Fix direction: say in the headline what the viewer gets, in their terms ("Your clients book themselves, and fewer forget to turn up" is the kind of line). Then make the comparison ask concrete and visible. "See a live example" is the better of the two asks, because it lets the visitor judge the product for themselves instead of taking the claim on trust. Name it ("See Harbor Dental's booking page"), and keep it from reading as an afterthought.

**Smaller things.**
- **"Shared calendars" card (first card):** the "New" badge forces the title onto two lines. That pushes its body text and "Learn more" about 30 px below the other three cards, so the row's baselines don't line up. Shorten the title, or put the badge somewhere that can't cause a wrap.
- **Badges:** "New" and "Beta" are status labels, but "Auto" and "Email" just describe the feature, so they're noise next to the titles. Only "New" uses the brand blue, which elsewhere means "click me". Keep the status badges, drop the rest, and don't use the action colour on a label you can't click.
- **Primary action labels:** "Start free" in the nav and "Create your link" in the hero look like the same action under two names. Use one label, or make the difference clear.
- **Customer row:** five grey names at the bottom edge, with no heading and no logos. They read like footer links, not proof. Label them (for example "Used by 2,000 practices like…"), or use their logos, or a quote.
- **Four identical "Learn more" links:** they are fine for screen readers because each sits inside its card, but they're pure repetition when skimming. "How time zones work" and the like would carry information.
- **Card icons:** they're grey, small, and in slightly mixed styles. The intake-forms glyph is a layout or template icon, not a form. Either make them one consistent set that means something, or cut them.
- **Cards:** about 60 px of empty padding sits at the bottom of each card. Tighten the cards so the customer row moves up into view.

**What's working.**
- **One clear primary action.** The filled blue button is plainly the main action and the text link plainly the secondary one (Refactoring UI; NN/g primary vs secondary). Keep that order when you rewrite the ask.
- **A single grid.** The left edge at 64 px runs from logo to headline to cards to the customer row, and the card gutters are equal. The page feels orderly because of this. Keep it when you add the product visual.
- **Card body copy.** "So nobody has to do the math" and "to cut down on no-shows" say who benefits and how, in plain words. That's the voice the headline should have.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't see whether "See a live example" opens a real, working booking page. If it does, that page is your best asset and should be the hero.
