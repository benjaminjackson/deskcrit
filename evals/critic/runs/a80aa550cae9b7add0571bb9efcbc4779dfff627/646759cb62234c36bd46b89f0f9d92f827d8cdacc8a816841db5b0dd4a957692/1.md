**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline, then the green "Get started" button, and that's the whole trip. The left half reads as a hero. The right half and the bottom third are empty cream, so the page looks unfinished or like an image failed to load. It feels calm, tidy and anonymous. I can tell it's a trip-planning app, but nothing I can see shows me the app.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing-page hero for a group trip-planning web app. Its job is to get someone who organises trips for friends or family to understand the product and click "Get started".

**Verdict.** The type and buttons are clean and competent, but the page doesn't do its job yet. It describes a product it never shows, and its sharpest idea (ending the chat-thread chaos) is buried in the subhead while a generic headline takes the top spot.

**What would move it most**

1. **Half the screen is empty where the product should be.** The whole right half (roughly x 720–1200) and everything below the buttons (y 500–800) is bare background. This isn't composed white space. Nothing pushes against it, so it reads as leftover or as a picture that didn't load (Ruder: the empty areas only work when they're set in tension against the printed ones). The cost is that the viewer has to take "the whole trip in one place" on trust. They never see flights, stays and day plans sitting together, which is the one thing that would make the claim believable and the product feel real. **Defect.** Fix: put something on the right that shows the promise. The obvious choice is a shared itinerary with a flight, a stay and a day plan, plus a sign that several people are in it (avatars, a vote, a comment). Size it so the hero fills the screen as one block instead of floating in the top-left corner. If that's a loading image and not the design, check in a live browser. Either way, the page needs it.

2. **The headline could be on anyone's page. The real pitch is in the subhead.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" fits almost every travel or planning tool, so a viewer can't tell what's different about this one. The specific part is "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads". It names who it's for (a group), the actual job (agreeing) and something the viewer already knows and hates (the chat threads). That's the strongest line on the page, and it's set in grey at body size. **Defect** (generic copy). Fix: build the headline from the group and the thread pain, and speak to the viewer. For example, something that says your group can agree on the trip without the chat threads. Then let the subhead list what's covered (flights, stays, day plans).

3. **"Get started" doesn't say what happens.** The viewer can't tell whether they're making an account, starting a trip or inviting friends. The best ask here would set the product against the group chat they already use, for example "Start a trip and invite your group", so they can picture the result. Right now it only asks them to trust the claim. **Defect** (weak ask). Fix: name the first concrete action, in the viewer's own terms.

**Smaller things**
- Headline line break: "in" hangs at the end of line 1 and splits the phrase "in one place". Break it as "Plan the whole trip / in one place".
- "See pricing" repeats the "Pricing" link in the nav about 400 px above it. A second button that shows how it works, or a sample trip, would earn the spot better.
- "Destinations" in the nav suggests a travel-content or booking site, which blurs whether this is a coordination tool. Check that the label matches what's behind it.

**What's working**
- The button order is right. The filled dark-green "Get started" is clearly primary and the outlined "See pricing" is clearly secondary, so there's one obvious action. Keep that.
- The type scale is tight: headline, subhead, nav and button sizes have clear steps between them. The bold, tightly set headline holds together as one block, and the subhead's line length looks comfortable (about 70 characters).
- The palette is restrained: cream, near-black and one green. The green is used only for the logo and the actions, so it marks what to click. Keep it that way when you add the product image.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the empty right side is a hero image or animation that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
