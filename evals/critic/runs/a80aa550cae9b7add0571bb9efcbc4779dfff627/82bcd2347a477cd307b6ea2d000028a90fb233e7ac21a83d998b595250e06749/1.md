**First read.** The eye lands on the bold H1, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then drops into an even grey block of body text with a few blue links in it. It reads straight away as a SaaS help-centre article: calm, plain and trustworthy, with no noise. After the H1 the page gives the eye only one more stop, the "When the new card is declined" subhead, and nothing on the page says "do this now".

**What it's for.** My assumption: an account owner who needs to swap the card on their subscription comes here to get that done quickly and to learn what happens to their money and access.

**Verdict.** The typography and layout are sound and better than most help centres. As task help it falls short: it explains rules around changing the card but never shows how to change it, and its one deadline, the retry schedule, can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **There are no steps and no way to get to the billing page.** *Defect.* The only instruction is "from the billing page" in the first sentence. It isn't a link, and the article never gives the clicks (where the billing page is, which button, what to enter). Meanwhile five other links pull the reader away: billing history, roles, paying by invoice, restore plan, and the sidebar. Someone who came to do one thing has to go and find it themselves, which is exactly the case where Krug says a page should be self-evident. Fix: put the action first. Link "billing page" directly, or add a short numbered list of 3–4 steps right under the H1, and move the policy paragraphs below it.

2. **The retry schedule can be read two ways, and it decides when the reader loses their plan.** *Defect (copy).*
   - "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days" could mean day 3 and day 7 after the decline, or day 3 and then 7 days after that (day 10).
   - "After the second attempt" is ambiguous too. If the original charge counts as attempt one, the downgrade comes at the day-3 retry. If it doesn't, it comes at day 7 (or day 10).
   - A reader planning around a payday will hold you to whichever reading they picked.
   - Fix: give the dates relative to one event, e.g. "If a payment fails, we retry 3 days and 7 days later. If the day-7 retry also fails, your workspace moves to the free plan."
   - Say what "moves to the free plan" costs them: which features or data they lose access to. That's what makes them act in time.
   - A related gap in paragraph one: "any invoice that is already open keeps the old card until it is paid". The most common reason to change a card is that the old one expired or was cancelled. As written, the reader has to work out alone that this open invoice will then fail. Say what happens and what they should do about it.

3. **The sidebar doesn't show where you are.** *Defect (Nielsen 1, visibility of system status).* In the left column, "Update a payment card" is styled exactly like its four siblings: no weight change, no colour, no marker. Its wording also differs from the H1 ("Update the card that pays for your subscription"). So a reader glancing left can't confirm they're on the page they clicked. Fix: mark the current item with weight or colour plus a left rule, and make the nav label and the H1 match closely enough to recognise.

4. **Links are told apart by colour alone.** *Defect / convention (WCAG 1.4.1).* Inline links like "roles and permissions" and "paying by invoice" are mid-blue against near-black text, with no underline. By eye the blue looks under 3:1 against the body text colour, so for some readers the links simply vanish. Measure the link colour against the body colour. The simple fix is a persistent underline.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line length:** the body column runs about 80–85 characters a line (e.g. "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page. The new card is"). Narrow it by about 10% to sit under 75 (Bringhurst).
- **No search in the header:** a help centre with no search field forces people to browse a tree. Add one to the top bar (Jakob's law; Nielsen 7).
- **Sidebar and breadcrumb contrast:** "In this section" and "Billing / Payment methods" are small (~13 px) and grey. They look like they pass but are close; check them against 4.5:1.
- **Dead space at the bottom:** the page ends at "working card is added." with no "Was this helpful?" and no "Still stuck? Contact support" next step. The only escape is "Contact support" in the top bar. Add one closing route next to the content.

**What's working.**
- **The type is disciplined:** one sans-serif family and roughly four clear levels (H1, H2, body, small UI). The body is set larger than the nav, so the reading text clearly comes first. Keep that scale.
- **The spacing groups correctly:** the subhead sits closer to its paragraph than to the one above, and the sidebar's top aligns with the breadcrumb. The page holds to one simple two-column grid.
- **The voice is right:** it speaks to the reader ("you can", "your workspace stays open") and names who is allowed to act, which answers "why can't I see the button?" before it's asked.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether links underline on hover or focus, and what the focus indicator looks like.
- Whether the sidebar shows a current-page state on hover or at other widths, and how the two columns reflow on mobile.
