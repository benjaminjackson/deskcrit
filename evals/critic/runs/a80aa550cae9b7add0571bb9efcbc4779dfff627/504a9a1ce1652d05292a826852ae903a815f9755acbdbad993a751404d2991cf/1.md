**First read.** The eye goes to the three blue "View route" buttons first. They are the only colour and the heaviest marks on the page, so they beat the route names to it. Then it moves up to the names and across the three cards. It reads at once as a plain, tidy chooser for three walks. Then the eye drops into the empty bottom half of each card, and the page starts to feel unfinished, like content that hasn't loaded.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page where someone picks one of three local walks and opens its details. Tell me if it's something else.

**Verdict.** It's clean and legible, but it doesn't help anyone choose. What separates the routes (distance, difficulty, surface) is buried in prose. Nearly half of each card is empty frame, which makes the page look broken rather than calm.

**What would move it most**

1. **The facts people choose by are hidden in sentences.** "Six kilometres… two steep climbs", "Four kilometres… flat and shaded" and "Ten kilometres… paved" are exactly what a walker compares. But they're spelled out as words in the middle of grey paragraphs. To compare the three, the viewer has to read every card and keep the numbers in their head. Nothing lines up across the cards, so a glance tells you nothing. *Defect:* it slows the main task. Pull distance, effort (steep, flat, paved) and, if you have it, time into a short line of facts in the same place on every card, with numbers as numerals: "6 km · 2 steep climbs · café at the end". Keep a sentence for character if you want one. A rough walking time ("about 2 hours") would land the distance on the reader: it says what the walk costs them, where "6 km" leaves them to work it out.

2. **Each card is about 440 px tall, but its content ends about 200 px down.** The bottom 45% of each box is empty white inside a border. Without the border it would just be margin. With it, it looks like a picture or a map failed to load, or the cards were given a fixed height. *Defect:* it makes the page look broken and pushes the bottom edge of the cards down for no reason. Ruder's point applies here: open space works when it's gathered and set against dense areas on purpose, not left over inside a frame. Either shrink the cards to fit their content, or fill that area with what a walker would actually want, such as a route map thumbnail or a photo. For walking routes, I think a picture of the place would help people choose more than any copy could (*taste*).

3. **Nothing on the page sets these walks apart, and nothing leads.** The heading "Walking routes" and three equal cards could sit on any council or tourism site. There's no place name, no hint of who the walks are for, and no route highlighted. Every card has the same weight, so the viewer gets no starting point. *Taste:* three equal choices is a fair design, but the heading should at least say where these walks are ("Walks from Porthleven", or whatever applies). If one route suits most visitors (the flat woodland loop for families, say), you could mark it so the page answers "which should I do?"

**Smaller things.**
- **Buttons:** the three identical filled "View route" buttons are fine here, because they're the same action on three peer items, not competing actions. But they outshout the route names. Consider making the whole card clickable, or toning the button down so the name leads. Screen-reader users will also hear "View route" three times. Make each one name its route in accessible text, such as "View Cliff path".
- **Body text colour:** the grey paragraph text looks close to the 4.5:1 limit. Measure it against the white (WCAG 1.4.3).
- **Card borders:** the pale borders do the grouping well. But if the cards shrink to fit their content, try them without the frame. Space alone may group them well enough.
- **Copy:** "cafe" should probably be "café". In the woodland card, "benches every few hundred metres" is nice detail and worth keeping.
- **Bottom of the page:** below the cards, the page just stops, with no footer and nothing after the choice.

**What's working.**
- **Type scale:** three clear levels (page heading, card title, body), separated by size and weight with no near-duplicates. Keep it.
- **Grid:** the margins are consistent, the gutters are equal, and the card contents start at the same left edge in each card. The page reads as one system.
- **Descriptions:** each one manages to include distance, terrain and a reward in a single sentence. The content is right; it just needs restructuring as described in point 1.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- I can't tell whether the empty bottom halves of the cards are where images should load. Check in a live browser. If they are, point 2 becomes a loading and fallback problem rather than a layout one.
- I couldn't see the hover and focus states on the buttons, or how the three columns reflow on a phone.
