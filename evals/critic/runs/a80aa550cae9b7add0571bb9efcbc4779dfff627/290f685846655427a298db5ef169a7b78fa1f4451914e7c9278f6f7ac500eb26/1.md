**First read.** Both versions look the same at first. The eye lands on the big serif headline, "A restored hay barn for weddings of up to 120 guests", then moves to the large tan panel on the right, then to the brown "Book a visit" button. It reads as a warm, small-venue wedding site. In version 1 the second look hits a wall: two full paragraphs set in a brush script, which you have to decode word by word. In version 2 they read straight through.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page hero of a wedding barn's site, for couples shortlisting venues, and its job is to get them to book a site visit.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the body text, from a script face to a readable serif, and that change decides the page. Version 1 hides the most persuasive copy on the page, the bring-your-own-supplier terms, inside text that's hard to read. With that fixed, the biggest remaining problem in version 2 is the empty picture panel. If that's what the live page shows, it isn't selling the venue.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Body text, script vs serif (defect in v1).** Version 1 sets about 90 words of running text in the same brush script as the logo. Script letterforms are made for a few words at display size. Across two paragraphs, readers slow down, skip ahead, and mostly read only the headline. Version 1's paragraphs also look heavier on the page than the headline's supporting role calls for, so they compete with the headline. Bringhurst and Butterick both keep decorative faces out of running text. Version 2 keeps the script for the logo only, where it gives the brand some character, and sets the body in a serif that pairs with the headline. The line length looks to be about 55–60 characters, inside the comfortable 45–75 range. Version 2 is also taller, so the button sits about 30 px lower, but that costs nothing.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The picture panel on the right is a blank tan gradient (defect, if it's the design).** It takes up half the hero. Couples choose a venue largely on how it looks. Without a photo, the headline's "restored hay barn", the oak floor and the glass doors are only claims, and the page could belong to any venue. Put a real photo of the barn interior, set up for a wedding, in that frame. If the gradient is a placeholder or an image that didn't load, check the live page (see below).
2. **"Book a visit" is a good ask, but it leaves something out (taste).** It names one specific thing to do, which is better than "Get in touch". But nothing says what a visit involves or that it's free and carries no obligation. The second paragraph already promises "as many times as you need", so a short line under the button such as "Free, about an hour, weekdays or Saturdays" would back up that promise right where the person decides. Use the venue's real details.

**Smaller things.**
- "guests" sits alone on the third line of the headline. Narrow the measure slightly or rebalance the break so the headline breaks over two even lines or three even lines.
- The hero content ends around 690 px of an 800 px screen with nothing after it. Adding a cue that there's more (gallery strip, dates, price band) would draw people down the page.
- The page gives no price or "from" figure, and no availability. Couples screening venues look for these first, so a hint near the call to action would save them a click.

**What's working.**
- The headline is concrete and answers what a couple screens on: what the venue is, and the 120-guest capacity. Keep that specific.
- The second paragraph speaks directly to the couple with real terms: the barn, the lawn and the cottage for two nights, and bring your own caterer, band and florist. That's what sets this venue apart, and version 2 finally makes it readable.
- The type system is clear: a script logo, a serif headline and body, and sans nav and button, in about four distinct sizes with clear steps between them. The muted brown palette fits a rustic venue.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the gradient panel is the intended design or a photo that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Load the page in a browser and check.

Better: version 2
