**First read.** The eye goes straight to the H1, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then down the body text, which reads well. You can tell at once that this is a calm, plain help article for a SaaS billing customer. It feels trustworthy and quiet. The trouble shows up only when you try to do the task: the page never tells you how.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a help-centre article. A customer arrives, often from search or from a "card declined" email, and wants to swap their payment card and know what happens to their money and their workspace.

**Verdict.** The visual design is sound and doesn't need much. As a help article, though, it fails at its main job. It's titled as a task, but it explains policy and gives no steps and no link to where the change is made. It also leaves the decline-and-downgrade timeline open to two readings.

**What would move it most**

1. **The page has no instructions and no way to the place where you change the card.** *Defect.* The H1 promises a task. The first sentence says "from the billing page" and then moves on. There are no steps (where the billing page is, which button, what to enter), and "billing page" isn't a link. The only link in that paragraph goes to *billing history*, which is a side errand. Someone who came here to change a card has to go and hunt for it. That's exactly what a help page is meant to save them (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; Nielsen heuristic 10: help should be task-focused and list concrete steps).
   **Fix:** put a short numbered list straight under the H1, using the product's real labels, e.g. "1. Go to **Settings → Billing**. 2. Under Payment method, choose **Replace card**…". Make "billing page" a direct link. Then move the rules about who can do it and which invoice gets charged below the steps. The current order (policy first, action never) should be reversed.

2. **The decline timeline can be read two ways, and the reader acts on it.** *Defect.* "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days" doesn't say what the days count from. It could be day 3 and day 7 after the first failure, or day 3 and then seven more days (day 10). "If the card is still declined after the second attempt" adds another gap: is the second attempt the original charge plus one retry (day 3), or the second retry (day 7 or 10)? This is the date someone uses to work out when they get downgraded, so the wrong reading costs them their plan. "Moves to the free plan" also doesn't say what they lose there, which is what they actually need to know. In the first section there's a related gap: "any invoice that is already open keeps the old card until it is paid". If the old card is the one failing, the reader can't tell how that open invoice ever gets paid.
   **Fix:** count everything from one named moment: "If a payment fails, we retry 3 days later and again 7 days after the first failure. If the card fails then, on day 7 your workspace moves to the free plan, and you lose X." Add one line on how to pay an open invoice with the new card.

**Smaller things**
- In the left sidebar and top nav, neither "Update a payment card" nor "Billing" is marked as the current page. Readers who arrive from search can't see where they are (Nielsen heuristic 1). Give the current item a weight change or a left bar.
- The breadcrumb says "Billing / Payment methods", but the sidebar has no "Payment methods" item, so the structure of the help centre reads inconsistently. Make the two match.
- The sidebar label for this page ("Update a payment card") and the H1 use different wording for the same thing. Match them so people can tell they've landed on the item they clicked.
- Body lines look like about 80–85 characters ("You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page. The new card is"), a little past Bringhurst's 45–75 range. Narrow the column by about 10%.
- Small sizes cluster: top nav about 15 px, sidebar about 14, "In this section" and the breadcrumb about 13. They read as near-misses rather than separate levels. Merge them into one small size and separate the levels by weight or colour.
- The article just stops at the end, with no "Still stuck? Contact support" or link to the related "Paying by invoice" article. Add one so readers whose case wasn't covered aren't left at a dead end.

**What's working**
- The hierarchy is clean and restrained. One dominant H1, one bold H2, regular body text, and a muted sidebar give an obvious reading order without boxes or decoration. Keep this level of restraint.
- The H1 is written as the user's task in their own words ("the card that pays for your subscription"), not as an internal category name. Keep that voice, and have the body follow through on it.
- Inline links are blue *and* underlined, and the link text says where each one goes ("roles and permissions", "paying by invoice"). That meets WCAG's rules on colour use and link purpose without extra effort.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the page reflows to one column on narrow screens, and what happens to the sidebar. That matters here because a lot of declined-card emails get opened on a phone.
