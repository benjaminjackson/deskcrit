**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The page is clean and the total and button are clear in both versions. Version 2's capitals make the one paragraph the buyer most needs to read, the one with a condition they must act on, harder to read.

**What would move it most**
1. **The all-caps delivery notice in version 2** (yellow box, lower left). This is a defect. Four lines of running text in capitals lose the word shapes readers rely on, so they have to read letter by letter. Readers skim long passages of caps, or skip them as boilerplate. This paragraph holds the commitment that matters most after the price: measure your doorways and stairs, because the drivers won't take doors off. A buyer who skims past it gets a table that won't fit through the door and a failed delivery. The caps also add urgency the box doesn't need, because the yellow fill and amber rule already flag it. Bringhurst and Butterick both limit capitals to short runs such as labels and headings, not paragraphs. The fix is version 1: sentence case. If you want one part to stand out, set only "Please measure doorways and stairs first" in bold.

**Smaller things** (these apply to both versions)
- The notice sits in the left card, away from the button. The measuring condition is part of what the buyer agrees to when they pay, so think about repeating a short version of it just above "Continue to payment".
- "Two-person delivery to room of choice" appears among the bought items, and then "Items $1,320.00" in the Summary leaves delivery out. The left list's three rows don't add up to the "Items" line. Move delivery out of the item list, or label it as a service.
- The notice says "a weekday" but gives no date or expected week. When you're spending $1,385, "when does it arrive?" is a question this page leaves open.
- In version 2 the Summary card stretches to match the taller left card, so the empty space under the button grows. That's a small thing, and it goes away once the caps go.

**What's working**
- The total is the heaviest piece of type in the Summary, and the button is the only filled dark shape on the page, so the eye ends where the action is.
- The type scale is disciplined. I count about four levels (heading, card title, body, total), each with a clear job.
- Version 1's notice speaks to the buyer in plain words and says why: "because our drivers cannot take doors off their hinges." Keep that clause.
