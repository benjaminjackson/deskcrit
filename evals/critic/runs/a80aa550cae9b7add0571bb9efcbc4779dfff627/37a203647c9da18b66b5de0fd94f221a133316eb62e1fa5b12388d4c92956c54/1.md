**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The type is clean, but the schedule people need is written out in words and can be read two ways, and the one highlighted box is used on fares instead of the ice warning that could actually leave someone stranded.

**What would move it most**

1. **The timetable is written as prose, and "the last at six" can be read two ways (defect).** In the first paragraph, "every forty minutes", "seven in the morning" and "six in the evening" are the facts a passenger acts on. Spelled out in words, the eye can't scan for them. Worse, the sentence only says the ferry leaves the *north* landing. Read as a stranger would, "the last at six" could mean the last boat leaves the north landing at six, or that the last crossing of the day is at six. Someone on the south bank can't tell when their last boat home goes. If they get it wrong, they're stranded overnight in winter. Fix: write the times as numerals (07:00, 18:00, every 40 min), and state the first and last departure from *each* landing. A small two-row table or a short list beats a sentence here.

2. **The emphasis is on the wrong paragraph (defect).** The tinted box is the only thing on the page that's set apart. It highlights bike, car and van rules, which matter less than the ice delay in paragraph two. "The crew may hold the boat… Check the board at the ticket hut before you queue" is the one line that changes what a passenger does today, and it's set as ordinary body text. Fix: give the callout to the ice and delay notice. Better still, make it a clear instruction ("In icy weather, crossings may be delayed. Check the board at the ticket hut before you queue."). Move the fare rules into plain body text or a short list.

3. **The column sits on the page edge, with no margin (defect / convention).** The headline, both paragraphs and the box's left edge all start at x = 0, while about 400 px of white space sits unused on the right. The text looks cut off, and readers will trust it less, as if it were a broken page. The empty space is just leftover, not a deliberate choice (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: content sits inside a type area with margins). Fix: put the column in a type area with a real left margin, or centre a narrower column. Check in a live browser whether the container's padding is actually missing or the screenshot was taken from inside a frame.

4. **The lines are far too long (convention).** Body lines run about 100 characters ("From November to March … The first crossing is at"). That is well past Bringhurst's 45–75, so the eye has to work to find the start of the next line. On a page people scan for one fact, that slows them down. Fix: narrow the column to about 600–680 px at this size. That also takes care of most of the empty right-hand space in point 3.

**Smaller things**
- **Callout padding is uneven:** about 26 px above the text and about 45 px below, probably a paragraph margin left inside the box. Make the top and bottom padding equal.
- **"Cars pay the standard fare" gives no amount:** a passenger can't tell what they'll pay. Give the figure or link to the fares.
- **"Bicycles ride free on weekdays" leaves weekends unsaid:** say what bikes pay on Saturday and Sunday.
- **The fare box isn't marked as a different kind of content:** it has no heading. If it stays, label it ("Fares and vehicles") so the tint has a meaning.

**What's working**
- **A tight type scale.** There are two sizes, a bold headline at about 40 px and body at about 18 px, with a big, obvious step between them. Keep it. Don't add in-between sizes when you restructure.
- **The headline wording is plain and specific.** "How the river ferry runs in winter" tells the reader exactly what the page covers.
- **Body type is a readable sans in dark ink on white, with generous leading.** The contrast looks comfortably above 4.5:1, including the text on the tinted box.
