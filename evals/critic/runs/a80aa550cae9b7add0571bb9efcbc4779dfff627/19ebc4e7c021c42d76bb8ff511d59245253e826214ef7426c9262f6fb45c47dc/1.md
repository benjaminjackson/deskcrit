**First read.** The eye goes to the saturated tiles: the yellow Glasshouse and red Rose arch first, then it moves around the grid with nowhere to stop. The page title is small enough that I took it in after the tile names. It reads as a tidy, generic six-up gallery whose pictures are flat colour blocks with a pale circle in each. It feels calm, but it's a placeholder.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that shows visitors the areas of a garden show and invites them to look at, or click into, each one.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. Nothing here shows a garden, and nothing gets priority. The page's own closing line promises a "large feature tile" that isn't there. The biggest problem is that six equal tiles of flat colour give the viewer no reason to look at any one of them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures don't show anything.** In all six tiles the image area is a flat colour field with a pale circle. A gallery exists to show the thing, and here every tile says only "a colour". Visitors can't tell the rose arch from the bean rows without reading the captions, so the images do no work. **Defect.** This could be stand-in art or images that failed to load (see the last section). Either way, the fix is real photographs, cropped so each one shows its subject clearly at this size.
2. **There's no focal point, and the page claims there is one.** The six tiles are identical in size and weight. The only thing ranking them is colour saturation, so the Glasshouse yellow wins by accident, not by intent. The line at the bottom, "The large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point", describes a layout that doesn't exist. A visitor who reads it will look for the feature tile and conclude the page is broken. **Defect.** Decide which area leads, perhaps the show's headline garden, and make it a double-width or double-height tile above or beside the others. Then either rewrite that line or cut it. Commentary on the layout doesn't belong in the page's copy.
3. **The page title is weaker than the tile titles.** "Garden show gallery" looks about 20 px, while "Rose arch", "Herb bed" and the other tile names look slightly bigger, about 22 px, in the same bold. The page's own name loses to its parts, and the two sizes are too close to read as separate levels. They look like a mistake (Bringhurst: sizes only a few points apart read as errors). **Convention.** Make the page title clearly bigger (one full step up), and give it more space above the grid than the grid has between rows. The scale would then be three sizes: page title, tile title, caption.

**Smaller things.**
- Rose arch caption: the title and description sit a few pixels higher than the other five. Align every caption block to the same offset below its image.
- Spacing: the gap between rows (about 60 px) is roughly four times the gap between columns (about 14 px). The grid reads as two horizontal strips rather than one gallery. Bring the two gaps closer together, while keeping each caption visibly attached to its own image.
- Generic copy: "Gallery" and descriptions like "Plum and pear trees" could belong to any garden. One concrete detail per tile (when it's in bloom, where it is on the site) would give visitors a reason to go there. "Tomatoes in late July" is the only caption that does this.
- Ask: nothing on the page tells visitors what to do next (open a tile, find the area on a map, book). If the tiles are links, nothing in the still image says so.

**What's working.**
- Caption structure: a bold name over a short grey line, tight under the image, scans quickly and groups clearly. Keep it.
- Each tile title is short and concrete ("Pond edge", "Bean rows"), the right size of label for a grid.
- The grey caption text looks dark enough to read comfortably on white. Keep it at least this dark.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the coloured blocks are intended stand-ins or images that didn't load. Check in a live browser with a cold cache.
- Whether the tiles are clickable, and how they respond to hover and focus.
