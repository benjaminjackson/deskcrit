**First read.** The eye lands on "Planning a vegetable bed", then runs down the left edge through three smaller headings. Within two seconds it reads as a short, plain how-to for a beginner gardener. It feels calm, clear and unfussy, a little like a document more than a page someone designed.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a beginner's guide on the web, read top to bottom once and then skimmed again later while working in the garden.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order is clear, the type is readable and nothing gets in the way. The one thing worth fixing is the body text: its lines run too long for comfortable reading. Everything else is small.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body lines are too long** (convention). In the "Preparing the soil" paragraph, the first line ("Dig to the depth… Rake the surface level") looks like about 90 characters, and the other paragraphs are set to the same width. Bringhurst gives 45 to 75 as comfortable and about 66 as ideal (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2). On this page the two-line paragraphs keep the cost low, but each return to the start of a line is a longer trip than it needs to be. That gets worse once a section grows past two lines, which a guide like this will. Narrow the text column to about 65 characters. The empty right side of the page shows there's room to give up. To check, count the characters on the longest line.

**Smaller things.**
- **The first step has no heading.** "Choose a spot… mark the corners" is an instruction just like the three headed sections, but without a heading it reads as an intro. A reader skimming the headings later never sees "Choosing a spot". Give it a heading like the others.
- **The steps aren't numbered.** The sections are a sequence (site, soil, plants, water), but nothing says so. Numbering them, or saying "Step 1" and so on, would tell the reader that order matters (taste).
- **"Choosing what to plant" leaves out when.** It says what to sow but not when, and timing is what a beginner most often gets wrong. Add the sowing window, or say "after the last frost".
- **The headings differ from the body only in size.** They're set in the same light-to-regular weight and the same colour as the text. The size step is large enough that this works, but a little more weight on the section headings would let them carry with a smaller size step (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9).
- **The headings sit right on their text.** The gap below each heading is only a few pixels, while the gap above is about 40. The grouping is right, but the heading looks pressed onto its paragraph. A few more pixels below would help (taste).
- **Check the apostrophe in "last year's".** At this size it may be a straight quote. Use a curly apostrophe.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale.** I count three sizes (title, section heading, body), with steps big enough that each size has an obvious job. Keep it at three.
- **The copy.** Every line is a concrete instruction from the reader's own world: "the depth of a spade", "six hours of sun", "twice a week, rather than a little every day". Nothing is vague and every sentence has a verb. Keep that voice when the sections grow.
- **The spacing between sections.** The gap between sections is clearly larger than the line spacing within a paragraph, so the four groups read at a glance.
