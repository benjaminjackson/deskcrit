**First read.** My eye goes to "Book a workshop seat", then down a column of bold labels and empty boxes. Before I've read any label, I see each label belonging to the box above it. "Preferred date" sits right under the first box and looks like its caption. Then I reach the bottom and find nothing to press. It feels plain and neat, but it's unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web form where a member of the public books one or more seats at a workshop, and it should take seconds to fill in correctly.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The spacing ties every label to the wrong field, and there's no visible way to send the booking. Those two problems decide whether people can use this form at all. Everything else is secondary.

**What would move it most**

1. **Each label sits next to the wrong field. (Defect.)** "Workshop name" sits about 40 px above its box, but "Preferred date" sits only about 10 px below the first box. The same pattern runs all the way down. Items that sit close together are seen as one group (Wertheimer's proximity rule, Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II "Shape"). So the eye pairs "Preferred date" with the workshop-name box, "Number of seats" with the date box, and so on. Only the first and last labels are unambiguous, and that's only because nothing competes with them. People will type the date into the name field, or hesitate at every row to work it out. To fix it, flip the spacing: put each label about 4–8 px above its own box, and leave a clearly larger gap of about 24–32 px between one field group and the next.

2. **There's no submit button. (Defect.)** The form ends at the "Notes for the host" box, and the space below is empty. A booking form with no visible button doesn't finish, so the viewer can't book. Add one primary button directly under the last field, aligned to the column's left edge, labelled with what happens: "Book 1 seat" (updating with the count) or "Request this booking", not "Submit". If a button exists and just failed to render in this capture, see the last section.

3. **Every field is the same empty text box, whatever it asks for. (Defect.)** The name, date, seat count and notes fields all look identical, with no placeholder, hint or format. This raises three problems:
   - **Workshop name:** a free-text box makes people recall and spell a name that the host already knows. It should be a list of the workshops on offer, or the workshop should already be chosen if they came from its page.
   - **Preferred date:** this gives no format and no sign of which dates actually run. "Preferred" also makes it unclear whether they're booking a date or asking for one. Viewers will remember the date and hold the host to it, so that ambiguity matters. Show the scheduled dates to choose from, or say plainly that this is a request the host will confirm.
   - **Number of seats:** there's no minimum, maximum or default. Default it to 1 and show any limit.

   The fix is to make each control fit what it asks for, so people can't enter something the host can't use.

**Smaller things.**
- "Notes for the host" is a single-line box, so it suggests a one-word answer. Make it a 3–4 line text area and mark it "(optional)".
- Nothing shows which fields are required. Mark the optional one instead of starring the rest.
- The page has no context at all: no workshop details, no price, no capacity. If someone could land here directly, add a line under the heading saying what they're booking and whether it's confirmed straight away.
- The heading's copy is generic. "Book a workshop seat" could sit on any site. If the workshop is known, use its name, e.g. "Book a seat at [workshop]".
- The field borders are a mid grey that looks to be around the 3:1 minimum for component edges (WCAG 1.4.11). Check it with a contrast tool.

**What's working.**
- There's a single left-aligned column of 480 px boxes with labels on the same left edge. It's the fastest layout to scan from top to bottom, so keep it.
- The type scale is simple: one bold heading and one bold label size. It's clear and needs no extra sizes.
- The fields are few and short. Only ask for more if the host truly needs it.
