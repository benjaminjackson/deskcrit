**First read.** The eye goes to the six colour blocks, starting with the red one at top left, and then moves along the rows. It reads as a plain three-by-two gallery of garden areas for a show, and it feels tidy, calm and a little generic. The page title "Garden show gallery" hardly registers. You only find it after you've looked at the cards.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web gallery page where visitors browse the areas of a garden show and pick one to look at. Each card has a picture, a name and a one-line description.

**Verdict.** The grid is sound and five of the six cards are well made. But the first card, the one everyone reads first, is set differently from the rest, and the page title is weaker than the card titles under it. Both are quick to fix. Until they're fixed the page looks unfinished rather than deliberate.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Rose arch" caption doesn't match the other five.** *(Defect.)* Its title sits about 2 px higher than "Herb bed" and "Pond edge" beside it. Its description sits tighter under the title, and the description text looks a little smaller. So the baselines across the top row don't line up. It's the first card the eye reaches, so the first thing a visitor sees is the one mistake. That makes the whole page look less carefully made, and it suggests the card was built or styled separately from the rest. Repeated items should follow one set of rules (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*), and text in neighbouring columns should share baselines (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Fix: make card 1 use the same caption component as the others, with the same gap between image and title, the same gap between title and description, and the same size. Then check that all three captions in each row line up.
2. **The page title is smaller than the card titles.** *(Defect against the type scale.)* "Garden show gallery" looks about 20 px bold, and "Rose arch", "Herb bed" and the other card titles look about 22 px bold. I count four sizes: page title, card title, description, and the off-size description in card 1. The two largest are only a few pixels apart, and the bigger one belongs to the less important text. So the page's own name reads as a weaker label above its contents, and nothing on the page says what this is or which show it belongs to. Bringhurst says sizes should be clearly different steps. Refactoring UI says a difference you mean should be obvious. Fix: make the page title clearly the top level, a full step or two above the card titles (roughly 1.5–2× their size). Or, if the page should stay quiet, drop the card titles to body weight and size and let the title lead.

**Smaller things.**
- Copy: "Garden show gallery" could be the title of any show. Name the show and year so visitors know whose garden they're looking at.
- In every card the circle sits exactly on the bottom edge of the image (a tangent), so it looks stuck to the edge. If this art is final, either crop the circle clearly or lift it clear of the edge.
- The six hues are all equally saturated, so no card stands out and the colour doesn't mean anything. That's fine for a browse gallery, but if the colours are meant to code something (like a zone on a map), the page doesn't say so.

**What's working.**
- The grid: three equal columns, one gutter of about 14 px between all of them, and image widths taken from the columns. Keep it.
- Grouping by spacing: each caption sits close under its own image, with a much bigger gap before the next row. So each card reads as one unit without needing borders or boxes. Keep it that way and don't add card outlines.
- The descriptions are short, concrete and specific ("Tomatoes in late July", "Climbing roses over the east gate"). They tell a visitor what they'll actually see, which is the right voice for this page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The images look like placeholders (flat colour with a pale circle). If these are what ships, the gallery shows visitors nothing of the gardens and that becomes the main problem. Check whether real photos load in a live browser.
- I can't tell whether the cards are links, or how they look on hover and keyboard focus. If they open a detail page, check that they have a visible focus state and that something tells visitors they can click them.
