**First read.** The eye goes to "Create your account", then down a tidy stack of empty boxes to the dark blue button. It reads straight away as the sign-up page of a small note-taking SaaS. It feels calm, competent and generic. Nothing is wrong at a glance, and nothing tells you why you'd want this product.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a first-time visitor, probably coming from a marketing page, should get from "interested" to "inside the product" with as little friction as possible. A returning user who lands here by mistake should get to sign-in.

**Verdict.** The craft is clean and the hierarchy is right. The form asks for too much and looks unfinished in one spot. Six inputs, none marked optional, stand between someone who wants to try a notes app and the app, and the biggest drag is the questions that serve you rather than them.

**What would move it most**

1. **Too many fields before the user gets anything (defect).** The card asks for first name, last name, email, password, workspace name and "How did you hear about us?" Nothing says which are optional, so the user has to assume all six are required. Two of them don't help the user start at all:
   - The workspace name is a decision they can't make well before they've seen what a workspace is.
   - The attribution question is your marketing data, charged to them at the moment they're most likely to drop off.

   Every extra required-looking field is one more reason to quit a free trial. The form convention (ux.md: mark required and optional fields, keep optional ones few) and Nielsen's minimalist-design heuristic both point the same way. Fix: cut the form to what account creation truly needs (email and password, plus a name if the product uses it). Default the workspace name (for example "Ben's notes") so it can be renamed later, and move "How did you hear about us?" to a one-click question after sign-up. Anything that stays should be marked "(optional)".

2. **The empty dropdown looks broken (defect).** The "How did you hear about us?" select in the lower third is a blank box with a browser-default chevron that doesn't match the rest of the styling. It gives no hint that it holds options, and it looks like a field that failed to load. Users hesitate here or wonder whether it's required. Fix: give it a prompt such as "Choose one (optional)" and style the chevron to match the inputs. Better still, take it off this screen, as in point 1.

3. **"Sign in" can't be told apart from the text around it (defect, WCAG 1.4.1).** In "Already have an account? Sign in" under the button, the link looks the same grey as the question, with no underline and no weight change. A returning user who landed here is exactly the person who needs to find it quickly. Right now they have to guess that the last two words are clickable. Fix: give "Sign in" the brand blue and an underline, or at least medium weight.

**Smaller things.**
- **Input borders:** the grey-blue outlines on white look light, possibly under 3:1. Sample the border colour and check it against WCAG 1.4.11, and darken it if it fails.
- **Password field:** it gives no requirements up front, so users only find the rules by failing them. Add a one-line hint under the field.
- **Subtitle:** "Start a free workspace for your notes" could sit on any notes app's sign-up page. It says "free", which is good, but one concrete line on what's different (taste) would give a hesitant visitor a reason to finish.
- **Terms and privacy:** there's no line near the button saying what the user agrees to by creating an account. Add one if your legal setup requires it.

**What's working.**
- **One primary action:** the full-width, high-contrast "Create account" button is unmistakably the thing to do. Keep it.
- **Labels and spacing:** persistent bold labels sit above each field, and the label-to-field gap is clearly tighter than the field-to-next-label gap, so every pair reads as a unit. Don't switch to placeholder-only labels when you trim the form.
- **Name pair:** first and last name share a row, which is the one place two fields side by side make sense. The rest stays in a single column.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Error and validation states:** where the messages appear, whether they're in plain words, and whether the input is kept after an error.
- **Focus states:** whether they're visible on the inputs and the button.
- **Show-password toggle:** whether the password field has one.
