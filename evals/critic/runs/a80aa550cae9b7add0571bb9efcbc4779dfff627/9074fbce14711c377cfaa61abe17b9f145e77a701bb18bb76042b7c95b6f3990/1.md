**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's line spacing is too open** (taste, with a common-sense basis). The three lines of "The farmers who are letting their fields flood…" look to sit at about 1.4–1.5 times the type size, which is roughly the same spacing as the body text. At this size that leaves wide white bands between the lines, so the headline feels slightly weak and loose instead of tight and heavy, and it takes up more height than it needs. Bringhurst's p. 37 supports setting display type tight as long as ascenders and descenders don't touch. No approved source states the other half of the rule, that headings must be tighter than body text, so this part is my judgement. Fix: close the spacing to about 1.1–1.2 times the type size. The lines will read as one unit, and the byline and first paragraph will move up into view.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line breaks:** "…and the / birds that came back" splits "the birds" across two lines. A break after "on purpose," would follow the meaning: line 1 is the farmers, line 2 is the birds. This can be done with a manual break or with `text-wrap: balance`.
- **Byline spacing:** the gap from the headline to the byline looks about the same as the gap from the byline to the first paragraph. The byline should sit a little closer to the headline so it clearly belongs to the header group.
- **Paragraph spacing:** the space between the two paragraphs is only a little more than the line spacing, so the break is weak. Either make the gap clearly larger (about half a line or more) or use a first-line indent instead.
- **Straight apostrophe:** "hand's" uses a straight apostrophe. Use a curly one (’).
- **Byline contrast:** the grey "By Tom Harrow · 9 min read" looks close to the limit for small text. Measure it against 4.5:1.
