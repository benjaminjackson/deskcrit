**First read.** Both versions look the same at first. The eye goes to the "Notifications" heading, then down the column of bold setting names, then over to the two blue toggles that are on. It reads as the email-preferences page in a team task tool's account settings. It feels calm and well ordered. The only difference between the two versions is the colour of the grey description text under each setting.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a settings screen where a user decides which emails to get. To decide, they need to read each description, because the description says what the email contains and when it arrives.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. In version 2 the descriptions are set in a pale grey that looks well below readable contrast. Those descriptions are the only place that says what each toggle actually sends, so making them harder to read works against the purpose of the page.

**What would move it most**
1. **Description text contrast (version 2), a defect.** The lines under each setting ("A short email every Monday…", "sent at 8 am in your time zone", "no more than one a month") are a light grey, around #8E–#9A, on white, at about 14 px. By eye that is roughly 3:1. That passes neither the 4.5:1 that WCAG 1.4.3 requires for normal-size text nor a comfortable read for anyone on a dim or glare-washed screen. These lines hold the facts the user decides on: what day the email comes, what time, how often. When they fade, people start choosing from the bold labels alone, and "Mentions" or "Due dates" on their own don't say when or why an email arrives. Version 1 uses a dark slate grey, around #4B–#55, which looks about 7:1. It still sits clearly below the black labels, so you keep the hierarchy without losing legibility. **Fix:** keep version 1's colour. To check, measure the description colour against #FFFFFF and confirm it is at least 4.5:1.

Version 2 changed nothing else: layout, type sizes, toggles and copy are identical. That contrast loss is the whole difference between them.

**Smaller things (version 1).**
- **Toggles have no visible state label.** The off toggles are a pale grey that looks close to the 3:1 minimum for meaningful component edges (WCAG 1.4.11). Check the off-track colour, or add a darker outline.
- **Toggles sit a long way from their labels.** At about 1070 px they are roughly 700 px from the names at 312 px. On a wide screen the eye has to track across the row to connect them. The divider rules help, but a narrower content column would tighten the connection. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is tight.** I count three sizes (page title, setting name, body) and one weight step. Each size has an obvious job.
- **The copy is specific.** "Sent at 8 am in your time zone" and "no more than one a month" tell the user exactly what they're agreeing to. The intro line also settles the obvious worry up front: account and security emails are always sent.
- **The settings are easy to scan.** Dividers, consistent row rhythm and the blue "on" state let you see at a glance which emails are on.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states and keyboard operation of the toggles, and whether a change saves straight away or needs confirming.

Better: version 1
