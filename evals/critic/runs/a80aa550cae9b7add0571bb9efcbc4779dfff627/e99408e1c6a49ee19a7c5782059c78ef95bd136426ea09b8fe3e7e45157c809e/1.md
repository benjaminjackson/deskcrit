**First read.** The bold title "River ferry timetable" lands first, which is right. Then the eye goes to the heavy black grid, before any of the numbers in it. It reads as a plain, competent schedule for one route, but it feels more like a spreadsheet than something a rider can scan on the way to the landing.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a public timetable for riders going from North landing to market quay, so that someone can answer "when is the next boat I can actually catch today?"

**Verdict.** It's tidy and legible, but it doesn't yet answer the rider's real question at a glance. The fact that decides whether a boat runs on your day sits in the last column, set exactly like everything else. On top of that, the season has no dates, so a rider can't tell whether the table applies to them at all.

**What would move it most**

1. **Which boats run on which days is buried (defect).** The "Runs" column is far right, in the same weight as every other cell. Three of the seven sailings don't run every day: 12:40 and 17:10 are weekdays only, and 19:30 is weekends only. They look exactly like the daily rows. A Saturday rider who reads down the time column sees a 17:10 boat that won't come. Someone on a weekday sees 19:30 as their evening option, and it isn't. The rider has to cross-read every row to the far edge to rule boats out. The fix is to make the day pattern part of the structure instead of a trailing attribute. The usual way is two short tables, "Weekdays" and "Weekends", each listing only the boats that run. The other option is to keep one table and mark the exceptions so they stand out (a symbol or tint plus a key), with "Daily" as the unmarked default.

2. **"Spring season" has no dates (defect).** The subtitle is the only thing that tells a rider whether this table is valid. Dates are exactly the kind of fact people act on and later hold the operator to. "Spring season" can be read as the calendar spring or as an operator's season with its own start and end. It also doesn't say whether public holidays count as weekdays or weekends. If a rider guesses wrong, they turn up for a boat that isn't running. State the range ("Valid 1 March – 31 May 2026"), and say how holidays are treated if they differ.

3. **The heavy box grid competes with the times (convention: Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, data-ink; also "No rules, boxes, or ornament where space would do").** Every cell is caged in a black rule about 2 px thick, as dark as the text. So the strongest thing on the page after the title is the lines, not the departure times. The vertical rules in particular add nothing, because the columns already separate by their wide gaps. Drop the vertical rules. Make the horizontal ones light grey hairlines, or use a faint alternating row tint for tracking. Then the numbers become the darkest thing in the table.

4. **The Period column takes the lead position and adds little (taste).** "Morning / Midday / Afternoon / Evening" repeats what the times already say, and it sits in the first column where the eye starts each row. That pushes the departure time, the one thing every rider looks for, into second place. I'd move departure time to the first column and set it a step bolder. Then either drop Period or turn it into quiet group breaks between rows. If the day-split in point 1 happens, this mostly resolves on its own.

**Smaller things.**
- Column widths look arbitrary and wide. Each time sits about 300 px from its neighbour across mostly empty cell. Size the columns to their content so departure and arrival read as a pair.
- "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are clipped headers. "Departs North landing" and "Arrives market quay" match the subtitle's place names, so a stranger doesn't have to work out which quay is meant.
- The crossing always takes 12 minutes. If space is tight, one line saying "Crossing takes 12 minutes" could replace the arrival column. That's a judgment call, since some riders want the arrival time spelled out.
- There's no return direction and no pointer to it. If this is one half of a pair, say where the quay-to-north times are.

**What's working.**
- The title and subtitle hierarchy is clean: one bold size step, a muted subtitle, and the route named plainly. Keep it.
- The times are consistent 24-hour HH:MM in strict chronological order, set in one sans family with figures that line up. Scanning down a column is easy. Keep that discipline when you restructure.
- It's restrained, with no decoration or colour noise. Once the grid is lightened, that restraint will let the times carry the page.
