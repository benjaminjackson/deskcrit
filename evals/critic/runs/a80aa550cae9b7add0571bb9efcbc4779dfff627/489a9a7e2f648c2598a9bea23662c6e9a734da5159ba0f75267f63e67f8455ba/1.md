**First read.** Both versions put the eye on the title "Departures from Corn Exchange" first. It then drops to the bold times and the two red status words. Each one reads straight away as a stop-side or web departure board for bus riders. The only difference between them is the table's rules. Version 1 draws a full black grid around every cell, so the page reads as a spreadsheet. Version 2 keeps only thin, light lines between rows, so the page reads as a list of buses.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming it's for a rider at Stand C who needs to find their bus quickly and see whether it's running.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. In version 1 the dark vertical and horizontal lines are as heavy as the text. That makes every cell equally loud, so it takes longer to read along a row. Version 2 removes that noise without losing any information. Both versions share the same remaining weaknesses: a cancelled bus looks almost the same as one that's running, and "4 min late" makes the rider work out the new time.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **The grid (convention, and it costs something here).** In version 1 the black cell borders are as dark as the body text. The eye has to cross a vertical line between every pair of columns. That breaks up the one action riders repeat: reading across from a time to the destination and status. The boxes also make the bold times and red statuses stand out less, because every cell is boxed the same way. Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, calls these lines non-data ink and argues for erasing them. Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*, recommend spacing and faint row dividers instead of borders for tables. Version 2 does exactly that. Column alignment plus generous gutters keep the columns clear, and the pale horizontal rules help the eye stay on one row.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **A cancelled bus looks like one that's running (defect).** The 14:40 route 19 row has the same weight and colour as every other row. Only the word "Cancelled" is red, and it sits at the far right edge. A rider looking for Northgate reads "14:40 … Northgate" from the left and may stop reading before the status. They could then wait for a bus that isn't coming. Mark the whole row: dim or strike through the time and destination, or put the status next to the time. Cancelled should also look more severe than late, because right now both use the same red.
2. **"4 min late" leaves the rider to do the sum (defect, copy).** The rider wants to know when the bus will come. "4 min late" is a number with a reference point the rider has to supply. It could mean 4 minutes after 14:11, or 4 minutes from now. Show the expected time, for example "Expected 14:15", and keep "late" as the label.
3. **Status is far from the time (taste).** The Via column is very wide, which pushes Status about 870 px away from Time. Riders read a departure board as time, destination, status. Narrow the Via column, or move Status next to Destination, so the answer to "is my bus coming?" is close to where the rider starts reading.

**Smaller things.**
- The column headers are small grey semibold. They're fine for a table this simple, but check their contrast against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- The route numbers (12, 7A, 31) are regular weight, but riders often look for a route by its number. Consider bolding them like the times.
- "Ring road" is in sentence case while the other stops are title case. If it's a proper name, write "Ring Road".

**What's working.**
- The bold times in the first column give the eye a clear place to scan down. Keep them.
- Colour is used only for problems, so the two red cells stand out right away. Keep "On time" neutral.
- The type scale is tight: title, subtitle, header and body, each with a clear job.
- "Updated 14:02" under the title tells riders how fresh the data is.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell whether the board refreshes live, or how it looks on a phone. With five columns, a narrow screen will probably need Via dropped or wrapped.

Better: version 2
