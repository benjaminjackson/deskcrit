**First read.** The eye goes to "Library opening hours" at top left, then down three bold branch names in an even, quiet column. In a second or two it reads as a plain, clear notice for local library users: calm and civic. The trouble starts when you try to read an actual time.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or panel where residents check when each branch, and the mobile library, is open before they set off.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and well grouped, but it fails at its one job, because the hours are written in a way that can be misread. "Ten to four" is a common way of saying 3:50, and every time on the page has this problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times can be read two ways (defect).** Every hours line: "nine to eight", "ten to four", "ten to six", "ten to two". These are meant as ranges, 9:00–20:00 and 10:00–16:00. But in everyday British and Irish speech, "ten to four" means 3:50, and "ten to two" means 1:50. A reader can come away thinking Saturday at Harbour is one moment in time, 1:50, rather than a four-hour opening. Even if they read it as a range, nothing says am or pm, so "nine to eight" has to be worked out. These lines are exactly what people act on, and they will hold the library to them. A misreading means someone turns up to a locked door. Fix: write the times as figures with an en dash, in the format your readers use, for example "9:00–20:00" or "9am–8pm". Set them in tabular figures so they line up down the column (Bringhurst on figures; Butterick on dashes).
2. **The mobile library has no times (defect).** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" tell you where it will be but not when. A van that stops for an hour or two is the one service where the time matters most. Fix: add the time window to each line, for example "Wednesday, 10:00–12:00, market square".
3. **Closed days are left for the reader to work out (defect, smaller).** The reader has to notice that Sunday is missing at both branches, and that Monday is missing at Harbour. Someone scanning for "is it open today?" has to rebuild the whole week in their head. Fix: say it outright with a line such as "Closed Sunday" (and "Closed Sunday and Monday" for Harbour). Better still, show the days as a short two-column list, day then hours, so the eye can scan down to today (Krug: don't make me think).

**Smaller things.**
- The heading is a topic, not a promise. If these hours can change around holidays, add "Updated [date]" or a note about holiday closures under it.
- If this sits on a wider page, the empty right two-thirds is fine for a web page, but it would also fit a day/hours table well if you take up finding 3.

**What's working.**
- The grouping is right. The space between branches is clearly bigger than the line spacing inside each one, so the three blocks read as three places at a glance. Keep that ratio when you add closed-day lines.
- The type scale is tight: one heading size, and bold versus regular at body size for the branch name versus the hours. That gives three clear levels with no near-miss sizes (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- The text is dark on white and comfortably large, and every line sits flush left on one edge, so nothing fights the content.
