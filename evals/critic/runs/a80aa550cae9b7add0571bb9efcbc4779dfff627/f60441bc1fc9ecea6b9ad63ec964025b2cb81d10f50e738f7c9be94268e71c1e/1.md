**First read.** The eye lands on "3.8%" top left, runs across the three big numbers, then drops into the orange bars and stops on Saturday, the tallest. It reads straight away as a small bakery's monthly health check: calm, tidy and trustworthy. Nothing fights for attention, and nothing is decorative.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal dashboard for the owner or ops lead at a bakery that sells subscriptions, used to check the month and plan baking and delivery capacity by day.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The layout, type and KPI cards are good work. The one serious problem is in the chart. September 2026 has five Tuesdays and five Wednesdays but only four of every other weekday, so summing orders by weekday makes Tuesday and Wednesday look about 25% busier than they are. The gentle Monday-to-Thursday rise the chart shows is partly not real.

**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday chart compares unequal numbers of days.** *Defect.* The source line says "orders delivered in September 2026". That month starts on a Tuesday, so Tuesday and Wednesday each get one extra delivery day. Reading the bars by eye (about 640, 710, 760, 820, 1,040, 1,130 and 380 orders) and dividing by the number of days gives roughly 160 orders per Monday, 142 per Tuesday and 152 per Wednesday. Monday is actually busier than either, yet the chart shows a steady climb through the week. The chart exists to support decisions about baking, staffing and delivery slots, so an owner would plan against a pattern that isn't there. This is a lie factor above 1 on the very comparison the chart is for (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, graphical integrity). Fix: plot average orders per delivery day, or use a window with an equal count of each weekday (four or eight full weeks). Say which in the source line, e.g. "Average orders per delivery day, 1–30 Sep 2026".
2. **The chart title names the topic, not the finding.** *Taste, though the approved checklist line agrees; its source is off the reading list, so I argue it on its own terms.* "Orders by delivery day" makes the viewer work out the pattern every time they visit. With corrected numbers the story is roughly "Friday and Saturday carry about 40% of orders; Sunday is the quiet day". Put that in the title. A darker or more saturated tint on the peak bars would also stop seven equal orange blocks from carrying equal weight.

**Smaller things.**
- Time windows: the KPI cards say "Last 30 days", the chart says "September 2026" and the subscriber card says "As of 30 September 2026". If these are the same window, use one label (e.g. "1–30 Sep") so the viewer doesn't have to wonder whether the cards and the chart cover the same orders.
- The subscriber delta is "+6.3%" while the other two are absolute ("+0.4 pts", "+€2.10"). Adding the count (e.g. "+730") would tell the owner how many people actually joined. "pts" for a rate is the right call; keep it.
- All three deltas are green with an up arrow, which shows direction but not whether the change is good enough. If there are targets, a "vs target" line would make the green mean something.
- The bars have no value labels, so reading Friday against Saturday means estimating from gridlines 300 orders apart. Direct labels on top of the bars would remove the guesswork and let you drop the gridlines.
- The 12 px grey axis labels and source line look close to the floor for contrast and size. Check them against 4.5:1 for normal text (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- The units are set smaller and lighter ("%", "€", "k"), so the figures carry the weight while each number still says what it measures. Bolder, not bigger, applied well (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*).
- Every KPI comes with a comparison period and a direction cue that isn't only colour (arrow and sign as well as green). The card titles name exactly what is measured.
- The page is restrained: one accent hue shared by the brand name and the data, a bar axis that starts at zero, a stated source, and a type scale of about five clear levels. Keep this restraint when you add the takeaway title and labels.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the nav's active state (only bolder and darker on "Overview") stays clear on hover and focus, and how the three cards reflow on narrow screens.
