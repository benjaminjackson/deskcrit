**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers across the top, left to right, and then drops to the blue Search line because it's the highest and most saturated thing in the chart. It reads as a clean, calm marketing-analytics overview for a small team. All three deltas are green, so the whole page says "everything is up, nothing to worry about."

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a weekly-check dashboard for someone who owns acquisition. They need to see at a glance whether traffic, sign-ups and conversion are healthy, and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The layout is competent and easy to read, but the numbers don't add up. The conversion card contradicts the two cards next to it, and the channel chart doesn't sum to the visits total. A dashboard whose figures disagree loses the trust it exists to earn. Fix the data story before touching anything visual.

**What would move it most**

1. **The conversion delta contradicts the other two cards (defect).** Visits are +6.1% and sign-ups only +2.4%, so conversion must have *fallen*. The previous period works out to about 3,820 / 173,600 ≈ 2.20%, against 2.12% now, a drop of roughly 0.08 pts. The card says "+0.1 pts" in green. Any viewer who does that sum in their head will stop trusting every number on the screen. Worse, anyone who doesn't will read a decline as good news and act on it.
   - Check the calculation or the copy.
   - If conversion fell, show "−0.08 pts" in the decline colour, with a second cue such as an arrow or sign so it doesn't rely on colour alone.
   - This is the one metric that really answers "is the funnel working?", so it's the one that most has to be right.

2. **"Visits by channel" doesn't add up to "Visits" (defect).** By eye, the three lines average about 6k, 3.6k and 2k a week. Over the 12 weekly points that's roughly 140k, against 184,220 in the card. Either about a quarter of traffic (direct, referral, other) is missing, or the chart and the card measure different things. Both ways, the viewer can't explain where a quarter of their traffic came from, and the chart's title implies it's the whole picture.
   - Add the missing channels, or an "Other" line in neutral grey, so the parts add up to the total.
   - If the chart is deliberately partial, say so in the title ("Top 3 channels").
   - My estimate is from a still, so check it against the real data.

3. **The chart doesn't say what a point measures (convention: Tufte, ch. 2, clear labelling).**
   - **Unit:** The y-axis runs 0–8k with no unit. The viewer has to work out that each point is *visits per week*, and they could just as easily read it as cumulative or daily. Label the axis or title "Weekly visits".
   - **Last week:** The x-axis labels every second week and leaves the final point (about Sep 22) unlabelled. That's the week the viewer cares about most. Label the end of the range.

4. **The page shows the data but says nothing about it (taste/common sense).**
   - "Overview" and "Visits by channel" name topics; they don't state findings.
   - Search grows steadily (about +36%) while Social is jagged. That is the actual story, and the viewer has to dig it out themselves.
   - A chart title that states the takeaway would make this a briefing rather than a readout, e.g. "Search drove most of the growth; Social is volatile week to week".
   - A dashboard can get away without this, which is why it ranks last. It's still the cheapest gain once the numbers are right.

**Smaller things.**
- **Legend:** It sits above the chart, away from the lines it explains. The line ends on the right have free space, so label Search, Social and Email there and drop the legend.
- **Type sizes:** I count about seven sizes, and the small ones crowd together. The brand (~17px), chart title (~16), card labels (~14), deltas (~13) and axis ticks (~12) sit only a point or two apart (Bringhurst). Collapse them to three or four steps and use weight and grey for the finer differences (Refactoring UI).
- **"Last 12 weeks" in the top right:** It's styled as plain text. If it's the date-range picker, it needs a visible signifier like a chevron or button outline. If it isn't, it's fine.
- **Small green delta text:** It looks dark enough, but measure it against white; it needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- **"+0.1 pts":** "pts" is fine for analysts. For a wider audience, write "percentage points" once, or a hover note.

**What's working.**
- **The card row:** Every KPI carries a period and a comparison (Few). The numbers are big, dark and unboxed by decoration. Keep this structure.
- **The chart's restraint:** The y-axis starts at zero, gridlines are faint, there's no chartjunk, and the three hues are easy to tell apart. The page fits on one screen with nothing competing.
- **The grid:** Cards and chart share the same left and right edges and gutters, so the page reads as one calm piece.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and whether the chart has hover values for exact weekly figures.
