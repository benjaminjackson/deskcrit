**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big cream italic "Harbour Lights Festival", then back up to the amber date, then down to the two-line description. It reads as the hero of a small-town event page or a poster: warm, calm, a little old-fashioned in a good way, like evening on the water. After the paragraph the eye drops into about 200 px of empty navy with nothing to land on.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top of a web page or a poster for a free local festival, meant to get people to turn up.

**Verdict.** It looks very good, but it can't do its job yet: it tells you what and when, but never where, and there's no next step. A stranger who wants to go has nowhere to go.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no location anywhere on the piece** (defect). The date line says "Saturday 14 June, from noon" and the paragraph mentions "the quay" and "the old net shed". Those only help someone who already knows which harbour this is. Anyone seeing it shared online or out of context can't tell which town it's in, so they can't decide whether to come or plan how to get there. For an event, the place matters as much as the date. Add the town or harbour by name. The easiest way is to extend the amber line ("Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour") so when and where stay in one group above the title.
2. **The page stops with no ask, and the empty band below the paragraph is where the ask should be** (defect, if this is a web page). The viewer learns that the festival is free, then hits about 200 px of blank navy and gets no next action: no map, no programme, no "add to calendar". Krug's point applies: don't make the viewer work out what to do next. Put one specific action in that space, tied to something the viewer would actually do, such as "See the day's programme" or "Get directions to the quay". A vague "Find out more" isn't enough. If this is a poster rather than a page, the empty band is fine as breathing room, and finding 1 matters even more.

**Smaller things.**
- The date line gives a start time but no finish ("from noon"). The lantern boats are "at dusk", so people will want to know whether things go on into the evening. Add an end time or "until late".
- No year on the date. That's fine for a live page, but add it if this will be archived or printed.
- The paragraph text looks pure white while the headline is a warm cream. Next to each other, the white looks slightly cold. Use the same cream for both, or a slightly dimmed cream for the body (this is taste).

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy from only three type sizes:** a small tracked-caps date in amber, a huge italic serif title and a mid-size sans paragraph. Each level differs in size, face and colour at once, so nothing competes and the title is clearly in charge (Dair's contrast of form and colour, not just size). Keep this scale.
- **The colour choice fits the mood.** Deep harbour navy with cream and one amber accent reads as lanterns at dusk without any picture. Using amber only on the date line makes the date the second thing you read, which is right for an event.
- **The copy is concrete and appealing.** "Lantern boats on the water at dusk, music on the quay, and a fish supper at the old net shed" names three real things to look forward to. "Free entry for everyone" answers the cost question straight away. The measure looks to be about 60 characters, comfortable to read. The two-line break in the title falls neatly between "Harbour" and "Lights Festival".

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether there's a navigation bar, a map or more content further down the page that already gives the location and an action. If there is, finding 1 still applies, because the hero is what gets shared and screenshotted. Finding 2 would then matter less.
