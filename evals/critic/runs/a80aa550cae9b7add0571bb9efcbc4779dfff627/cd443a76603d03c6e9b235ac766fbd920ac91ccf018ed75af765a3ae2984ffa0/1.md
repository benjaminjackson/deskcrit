**First read.** The eye goes to the large serif title "Leek and potato soup with brown butter", then drops to the two columns, with the ingredient list on the left and the numbered method on the right. It reads as a calm, editorial recipe page from a small food site. It feels quiet and confident. The two versions are identical except for the page colour: version 1 is a warm cream and version 2 is plain white.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a recipe page that someone reads before cooking and glances back at while cooking, so it has to be easy to scan for quantities and steps.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job, and almost equally well. Version 1 is slightly better. The cream page fits the warm, cookbook voice of the serif title and the name "Second Helpings". It also absorbs the pale divider rules under the ingredients. On white in version 2, some of those rules show a faint yellow-green tint that looks like a mismatch rather than a choice. This is a craft and taste difference, not a change in how usable the page is. Text contrast is high in both.

**What changed and why it matters**
1. **Page colour (taste).** The cream in version 1 makes the page feel like paper and gives the brand some character at no cost. Black text on cream still has strong contrast. Version 2's white is cleaner but more generic: it could be any blog template.
2. **Divider rules in the ingredient list (detail/craft defect in version 2).** In version 2, under "3 large leeks" and "1 litre vegetable stock" for example, the rules look slightly yellowish while the others look grey. On cream the difference disappears. On white it reads as inconsistent. If white is kept, make every rule one neutral grey.

**Remaining findings for version 1 (the better one)**
1. **The serves/time line is cut off from the title (defect, minor to moderate).** "Serves 4 · 45 minutes" floats in a large empty band, roughly 100 px from the title above and from the columns below. Small grey text alone in that space reads as an afterthought, yet it's the information a cook checks first. Tighten the gap to the title so the two read as one header block, and consider giving it a little more weight.
2. **Large empty band above the columns (taste).** Between the title area and the "Ingredients"/"Method" headings, the page spends close to a quarter of the first screen on empty space. This pushes the method lower than it needs to be. Closing up the header would bring the whole recipe into view sooner.

**Smaller things.**
- The ingredient rules are very faint (a deliberate light touch). Check that they're still visible on low-quality screens, or drop them and rely on spacing.
- There are three sizes for the title, headings and body text, plus the small sans. That's a clean scale, so no change needed.

**What's working.**
- Splitting ingredients and method into two columns means a cook can check quantities without losing their place in the steps.
- The method text is in a readable serif with a comfortable line length (looks about 70 characters) and generous spacing between steps.
- The copy is specific and sensory ("until it smells of hazelnuts", "until the potatoes break when pressed"). It tells the cook exactly when a step is done.

Better: version 1
