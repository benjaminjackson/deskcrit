**First read.** The eye lands on "Help for ferry passengers," then drops to the two bold headings and splits left and right. Within a second it reads as a plain help page for people on a small local ferry. It feels calm, orderly and sensible. Nothing pulls at you, and nothing stands out either.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or printed notice that gets occasional ferry users through boarding and tells them what to do after a problem. If it's a sign posted at the landing, reading distance changes the type sizes. See the last section.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The type is readable, the structure is clear, and the instructions are written well. What holds it back is the words, not the layout. The one fact that can strand someone is buried and vague. The "If something goes wrong" column covers filing a complaint afterwards, not getting help while something is going wrong.

**What would move it most**

1. **The last-boat time is buried and vague.** It sits at the end of the first bullet, top left ("…and the last boat leaves before dusk"). This is the only line here where misreading it means you're stuck on the wrong shore. It's a subordinate clause after "because," and "before dusk" isn't a time. Dusk moves through the season, and a visitor can't tell if it means 4:00 or 4:45. The line also never says what missing the boat costs the reader. Deadlines are what people act on, so a line that can be read two ways is a defect here. Fix: pull it out as its own first item and say it to the reader in plain terms, e.g. "The last boat leaves before dusk, around [time] in winter. If you miss it, you can't cross until morning." Then let the timetable bullet follow. *Defect.*

2. **The "If something goes wrong" heading promises more than the column gives.** The heading reads as "help when things go wrong." All three steps are about writing a complaint after the crossing. Someone who's been left behind, is hurt, or has lost a bag on board reads the column and finds no one to ask now: no crew instruction, no phone number. Fix: either rename the heading to what it is (e.g. "To report a problem after your crossing"), or add one first line for help on the spot (ask a crew member, or a number to call). *Defect.*

3. **Step 3 sends the reader to another sign.** It says to send the note "to the address on the noticeboard in the shelter." A reader at home or on the boat has to find a second board to finish the task. The page also never says what happens after they report: whether anyone replies, or how long it takes. They're left trusting that the box is read. Fix: print the address here and add one line on what they can expect back. *Defect* (it slows and partly blocks the task).

**Smaller things.**
- The bottom third of the page and the space under the right column look like leftover space, not planned space. If this is a fixed format like a printed sheet or a panel, tighten the frame or place the content on purpose. Taste.
- "Winter sailings run once an hour" with no summer schedule leaves summer readers guessing. Either say it's a winter notice or give both schedules.

**What's working.**
- **The copy itself.** Every item starts with what to do ("Check," "Bring," "Wheel," "Ask," "Write down") and gives the reason in the same sentence ("so that the gangway is kept clear"). It sounds like a person explaining, not a rulebook. Keep that voice when you revise.
- **The structure fits the content.** The before-boarding tips are a bulleted list because order doesn't matter. The complaint steps are numbered because it does. The bullet markers hang outside the text, so each column has a clean left edge.
- **It reads easily.** There are three clear type levels (title, bold section headings, body), and weight does the work between headings and body. Lines in each column run about 55 to 60 characters with generous line spacing, and the dark grey on white has plenty of contrast. Don't add decoration; it has no need for any.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The medium.** If this is posted at the landing and read from a metre or more away, the body text looks too small and should be measured at its real printed size. As a web page, check that the two columns stack into one on a phone, with "Before you board" first.
