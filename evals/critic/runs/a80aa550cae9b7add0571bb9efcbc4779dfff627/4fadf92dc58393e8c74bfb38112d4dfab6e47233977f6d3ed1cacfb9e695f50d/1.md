**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **Body copy alignment (version 1's defect, fixed in version 2).** Version 1 sets the two paragraphs flush right and ragged left. Each line starts at a different point, so after every line break the eye has to hunt for where the next one begins. In two paragraphs of real product detail (capacity, tare, units, battery life) that slows reading and makes people skim past the selling points. It also breaks the column's structure. "Kitchen", the headline, the price and the button all hang from one left edge, and the paragraphs pull away to the right, so the column looks like two blocks that don't belong together. Bringhurst and Butterick both advise flush-left, ragged-right for running text, and right-aligned setting only for short lines like captions or labels. Version 2 uses one left edge for everything, so the column reads top to bottom as one unit. *Defect, and a convention whose breaking buys nothing here.*

**Remaining findings for version 2**
1. **The product image shows no product.** The right half of the page, its largest element, is a beige square holding a pale circle. On a product page the photo is what tells the viewer what they're buying and builds trust, and here it tells them nothing. If this is a placeholder or an image that didn't load, see below. If it's the design, it's the biggest problem on the page: replace it with a real photo of the scale, ideally with a bowl on it to show the tare feature. *Defect, if intended.*
2. **The button and the copy stay generic.** "Add to basket" is clear and right for a shop, so leave it. But the copy never turns its best facts into something the shopper cares about. "In steps of one gram" stops at the spec. One clause on what that means for the cook, such as "precise enough for yeast and spices", would make the point for them. *Taste.*

**Smaller things.**
- The "Kitchen" category label looks very small and is set in a mid-brown. Check its contrast against white; it needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- The left column ends at the button, about two-thirds of the way down the image panel, leaving dead space below. Consider aligning the column vertically with the image, or putting delivery or returns details there.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight: headline, price, body and label, four clear levels with big steps between them, and the price is bold enough to find instantly.
- The copy is concrete and written to a person: "without doing any sums", "stays put on a wet counter". Keep that voice.
- The single dark button is the only filled shape in the column, so there's no doubt about what to do next.
