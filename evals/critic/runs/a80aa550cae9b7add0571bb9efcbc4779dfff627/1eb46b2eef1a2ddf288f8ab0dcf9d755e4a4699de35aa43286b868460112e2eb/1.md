**First read.** The eye goes straight to "About the trust", then drops into a single dense block of about twelve lines. It reads as the About section of a small local conservation charity's website: plain, calm and trustworthy, with nothing to look at but text. On a skim, nothing stands out from the paragraph. The one thing a reader could actually do, join a work day, is hidden in the middle of it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an About section on the trust's website for local people, which should tell them who the trust is and get some of them to come to a work day.

**Verdict.** The typography is clean and readable. The page doesn't do its job, though, because the invitation to volunteer is buried as sentence five of one unbroken paragraph and leaves out the details someone needs to turn up. The ambiguous "every second Saturday" makes that worse.

**What would move it most**

1. **The invitation is buried inside the history** (defect). The block puts two different jobs in one paragraph. Lines 1–6 cover who the trust is: footpaths, woods, the quarry, 1987, staff and members. Lines 6–12 are a recruitment pitch: work days, tools provided, no experience needed. Someone skimming for "can I help?" gets no heading, break or change of weight to show them where that starts. They have to read the whole block to find it, and most won't (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: people scan, they don't read). The fix is to split it into two paragraphs at "Work days run…". Better still, give the volunteering part its own short subhead and pull the practical facts out where they can be scanned.

2. **"Every second Saturday" can be read two ways, and it's the date people act on** (defect). It could mean every other Saturday (fortnightly) or the second Saturday of each month. A reader who picks the wrong one turns up on a day with no work party and probably doesn't try again. Say which one you mean, for example "on the second Saturday of every month", or give the next date.

3. **The ask is missing what someone needs in order to come** (defect). "Anyone is welcome to join for the morning" doesn't say what time, where to meet, or whether to book or just turn up. It also gives no contact or link. A reader who is convinced still can't act on this page. Add the meeting point, the start time and one concrete next step ("Meet at the quarry car park at 9:30, no need to book" or "Email … to join"). That makes "join for the morning" something a person can actually do.

**Smaller things.**
- The voice switches from "it" ("It was set up… Today it has") to "We provide" halfway through. Pick one; "we" suits a volunteer pitch better.
- "About the trust" could head any organisation's page. Something specific to this place, or the trust's name, would tell a stranger more.
- The text block covers only the left half of a wide frame, with the right half empty. That's fine if a sidebar or image belongs there. If not, the empty space looks left over rather than intended.
- The last line, "across the path.", is a short runt. It's minor at this size but easy to fix in the edit.

**What's working.**
- The type is set well for reading. The line length looks to be about 60–70 characters, within Bringhurst's 45–75. The leading is generous, the body is a comfortable size and dark on white, and the text is flush left with an even rag. Keep all of that when you split the text up.
- The type scale is clear: there are only two sizes, and the heading is set apart by both size and weight. The heading also sits close to the text it introduces.
- The copy is concrete and sounds human ("clearing drains, mending stiles, and cutting back gorse", "a hot drink at the end"). Those details make the invitation feel real, so keep them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** What surrounds this block on the page (navigation, a sidebar, a sign-up form or contact details elsewhere). If a clear volunteer sign-up sits right next to it, finding 3 matters less.
