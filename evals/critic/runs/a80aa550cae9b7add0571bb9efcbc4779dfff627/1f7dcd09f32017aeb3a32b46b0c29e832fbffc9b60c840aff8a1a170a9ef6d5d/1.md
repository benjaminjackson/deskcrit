**First read.** The eye goes to the serif headline, "Savings deposits rose every month", then to the June bar, which is far taller than the rest. Next it goes to "$4.82M" at the top of the right column. It reads as a calm, trustworthy credit union report to members. It feels orderly and plain, maybe a bit too sure of itself given what the June bar is hiding.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a page in a member report showing ordinary members, who are not analysts, that the credit union's savings grew in the first half of 2026.

**Verdict.** The page mostly does its job. The chart is honest and clearly labelled, and the numbers add up (the six bars sum to the $4.82M total). What's in the way is that the most striking thing on the page, June's jump, is caused by a one-off bonus payroll. That is explained only in the footnote, and two of the three side cards measure only June. So a member comes away with a stronger growth story than the data supports.

**What would move it most**

1. **The June bar tells a story the page only explains in the footnote.** From January to May, deposits grow by about 7–12% a month. June jumps about 32%, to $1,150k, and on its own makes up roughly a quarter of the half-year total. The only explanation is the last clause of the grey source line under the chart: "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll." Most readers won't get that far, so they will believe growth took off in June. **Defect**: it misleads the intended viewer about the trend, on the one page meant to report it truthfully. Put the explanation on the June bar itself as a short note next to it, such as "Includes mid-year bonus payroll". If you have the number, split June into ordinary deposits and bonus deposits, so the trend runs cleanly through all six months. The headline holds up either way, since May to June still rose without the bonus. But "rose every month" is less interesting than the real pattern: steady growth, plus a one-off in June.

2. **Two of the three cards measure the bonus month, and one label says something different from what it measures.** "Average deposit, Jun 2026: $214" is exactly $1,150k ÷ 5,374 accounts. So it is the average amount *deposited per account that made a deposit* in June. "Average deposit" reads as the size of a typical single deposit, which is a different number. A member who compares it with their own deposits will reach the wrong conclusion. **Defect** in the copy. Rename it to say what it is, e.g. "Average paid in per depositing account, Jun 2026". Also, both June cards compare with June 2025 ("Up $11", "Up 402"). The reader can't tell whether June 2025 also had a bonus payroll, so they can't tell if these are like-for-like comparisons. Say so in a word ("both Junes include the bonus payroll"), or base these two cards on the half year like the first one. As it stands, the three cards look identical but cover different periods (half year, June, June), which invites the reader to compare figures that don't belong together.

**Smaller things.**
- Card deltas: "Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025" makes the reader work it out. "Up 6.1% from Jan–Jun 2025" or "vs. the same months of 2025" reads more easily for a US audience, and the same goes for "Up $11 on Jun 2025".
- Units vary across the page: the axis and bars use "$1200k" and "$1150k", while the card uses "$4.82M". Use $M throughout ($1.15M, $1.2M), so June's value reads in the same terms as the total.
- Every bar has a value label, so the y-axis labels and gridlines repeat the same information. You could drop the axis labels, or keep only the baseline and a faint gridline or two (Tufte, data-ink).
- Type scale: I count roughly 13, 14, 15 and 16 px all used for secondary text (axis labels, card labels, chart title, standfirst). They don't read as distinct levels. Merge them into two sizes and separate the levels by weight and colour (Refactoring UI).
- The card numbers (~32 px bold black) are as heavy as the headline, so "$4.82M" competes with it for the first look. Making them a step smaller or lighter would let the headline lead clearly.
- The card column ends about 75 px above the bottom of the chart panel, which leaves a loose gap bottom right. Either line the bottoms up or let the gap read clearly as space.
- The grey source line on the light grey page background looks faint at its small size. Check it reaches 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- The headline states the finding rather than just naming the topic. The standfirst then says exactly what is being counted, over what period, and in what units. Keep that pattern.
- The chart is honest: the bars start at $0, there is one colour, values sit on the bars, and there is a dated source line. Nothing in it is decoration.
- Every card gives the number with a comparison to last year, so a member can tell good from bad without having to work it out.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the page reflows on a phone, and whether the two-column layout stacks chart first and cards after.
