**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline. "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY" is huge, near-black, wide-tracked capitals, and nothing else on the page competes with it. Next it drops to the two-line paragraph, then back up to the small grey "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" label. It reads as a calm, confident announcement for a public harbour event. Then the eye goes looking for when this happens and finds a blank lower half and an empty right side.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a poster or landing page that gets local people to turn up for free ferry rides on the harbour open day.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy is clean, but the piece can't do its job yet: it never says which day or what hours. A viewer who wants to go can't, and the empty space where that would sit makes it look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts people act on are missing.** *Defect.* The page says "Free rides every half hour from the north landing" but gives no date, no first or last sailing, and no time for the open day at all. A date and a start time are what people put in their diary and later hold you to. Without them the viewer has to look elsewhere, and most won't. "Every half hour" with no hours is also open to two readings: all day, or only in the morning, which the headline suggests. Fix: add the date and the sailing window as their own line, for example "Saturday 14 June · sailings 8:00–11:30". Make it the second thing the eye reads: clearly bigger or bolder than the paragraph, still far below the headline. The empty lower left, on the same left edge, has plenty of room for it. "The north landing" is fine for locals; if strangers are the audience, say where it is.

2. **The empty lower half reads as a gap, not a choice.** *Taste, tied to point 1.* Everything sits in the top 55% of the frame. Under it is about 340 px of white with nothing pushing back, so it looks like content that didn't load rather than space you shaped. Ruder's point is that empty space works when it stands in tension with what's printed. Here it has nothing to work against. When you add the date and time, put them low, near the bottom margin, rather than right under the paragraph. That turns the white into a deliberate pause between "what" and "when", and the page gets a top and a bottom.

3. **The headline's tracking is at the loose end for its size.** *Convention, borderline.* Capitals should be letterspaced; Bringhurst gives 5–10% of the type size. But the spacing between letters already looks wider as type gets bigger (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80), and at roughly 110 px this looks like it sits near the top of that range. The result is that letters like the A and L in "ALL" read as separate letters more than as one word. The word spaces stay clear, so it's readable. It just reads as eight-plus separate letters per line rather than four words. Try pulling the tracking back by a few percent and check that "ABOARD THE" still holds as two words. This is a refinement, not a rescue.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks after "THE", leaving the article hanging at the end of line one. "ALL ABOARD / THE MORNING FERRY" breaks at the phrase and gives two more even lines.

**What's working.**
- One clear focal point and three distinct type levels (label, headline, body) in one family. Weight, size and grey carry the hierarchy without fuss. Keep the scale this tight when you add the date line: make it a weight or size step that's obviously different, not a fourth size a few points off the body.
- The copy has a voice. "Bring a hat and a thermos" tells the viewer what the morning will feel like and talks to them directly. Keep that tone in the new detail line.
- One strong left edge for every element and a comfortable measure on the paragraph, at roughly 60 characters.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I can't tell whether this is the whole page or only the top of a scrolling page that has the details further down. If there's more below, the date and time still need to be in this first screen.
