**First read.** The eye lands on the "Bin collection days" heading, drops to the grey "Next collection: Thursday" box, then scans the blue links in the body. It reads as a plain, calm council page for residents on a phone. Both versions are the same pixel for pixel except for how the links are underlined.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a resident checks which bins go out next and when, and finds the links for the calendar, missed bins, replacements and permits.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job slightly better. The page is sound in both versions. The only change is the link underline, and version 2's is the clearer one.

**What changed and why it matters**
1. **Link underlines in the three body paragraphs** (defect, but a small one). Version 1 draws each link's underline as a rule set well below the baseline, in a darker colour than the link text. Because the line spacing is tight, the rule under "full" in line two nearly touches "calendar" on the line below. The same happens under "replaced" and "for a fee of $25". As you read, each rule looks like it could belong to either line, so the body text looks busier and the links read less cleanly as single phrases. Version 2 uses a normal underline in the link's own blue, sitting just under the letters. Each link reads as one unit, and it looks like what people expect a link to look like (Nielsen Norman Group on making links look clickable). The cost is that version 2's underline runs through descenders, as in the "y" and "p" of "yearly permit". That is a much smaller problem.

**Remaining findings for version 2**
1. **"Next collection: Thursday" has no date** (defect). This is the line people act on. "Thursday" could mean this Thursday or next, depending on when someone checks, for example late on a Wednesday or on Thursday morning itself. If they guess wrong, the bins miss the collection. Add the date, e.g. "Thursday 26 November".
2. **The full calendar link hides a postcode step** (defect). "Find your full calendar for the year by entering your postcode" sits mid-paragraph as an inline link. For many residents this is the main task. Make it a clear button or a postcode field right under the next-collection box.

**Smaller things.**
- The page doesn't say which address "Next collection" is for. If it's personalised, show the address. If it isn't, it can't be accurate for everyone.
- "$25" on a page that looks like a UK council (bins, blue box, postcode): check the currency.
- The lower half of the screen is empty. Moving the actions up into clearer blocks would use it.

**What's working.**
- The next-collection box answers the main question before anything else, and it says which bins go out, not just the day.
- There are only three type sizes (heading, box title, body), with clear steps between them. The copy is plain and has verbs: "report it within two working days and we will come back for it."
- The link text describes what happens ("report it within two working days"), not "click here".

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus and hover states on the links, and whether "Thursday" is worked out from the resident's address.

Better: version 2
