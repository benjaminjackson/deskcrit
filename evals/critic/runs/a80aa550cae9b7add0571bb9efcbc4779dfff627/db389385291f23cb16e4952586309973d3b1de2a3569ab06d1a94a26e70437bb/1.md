**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline. It is huge and dark, and it is the only weight on the page. Then it drops to the grey subhead and the blue button, and stops there. It reads as a SaaS landing page for team planning. It feels clean and confident, but empty: the right half and bottom third are blank, and nothing shows what the product is.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero of a marketing site for a team-planning tool. Its job is to get team leads or managers to understand what Weekplan does and start a trial.

**Verdict.** The hierarchy and the headline are solid. But the page promises "one honest picture of the next five days" and never shows that picture, so the viewer is asked to start a trial on trust. The missing proof is what holds it back, not the type or the layout.

**What would move it most**

1. **The promised "picture" is nowhere on the page.** *(Defect.)* The subhead says Weekplan turns tickets, meetings, and leave into "one honest picture of the next five days". Then the right half of the screen (roughly x 750–1160) and everything below the button are empty white. The viewer has to imagine the product from one sentence, and the trial button is the only way to find out what it looks like. The empty space isn't being used for anything; it reads as leftover, not as calm. Put a real view of a team's week in that space: five days, with tickets, meetings, and someone's leave visibly combined. Then the headline, the subhead, and the image become one argument (headline says it, image proves it). That one change does more than everything else here combined.

2. **Nothing sets Weekplan against what the viewer already uses.** *(Defect, copy.)* "Actually works" and "honest" both imply that the viewer's current plan is fiction. That's the right instinct, but the page never says what's wrong with today's setup, so you have to fill in the gap yourself. The viewer already trusts their ticket tracker, their calendar, and the leave spreadsheet, and the page could name them and show the gap between them. For example, a line that ends on the viewer: "Your board says 40 points; your calendar says Tuesday is all meetings and Priya is out. Weekplan shows you both." The visual from finding 1 could make the same comparison: planned work against the hours people really have. Then the viewer judges the value on terms they already accept, instead of taking "honest" on trust.

3. **"Start a free trial" is generic, and it leaves out the terms.** *(Defect.)* It's the one button on the page, and it commits the viewer to something, yet it says nothing about length, card, or setup. Viewers hesitate at exactly that point. Add the terms beside or under it ("14 days, no card"), and consider an ask that names the first concrete step ("Connect your tracker and see this week"). A quieter second action, like "See an example week", would also help viewers who aren't ready to sign up. Right now they have nowhere to go.

**Smaller things**
- Headline line break: "Plan the week your / team actually works" splits "your team". Break after "week" so each line is a phrase: "Plan the week / your team actually works".
- Headline letter spacing looks like text spacing at about 88 px. Tighten it a little (display sizes want slightly negative tracking) so the two lines hold together as one shape.
- The "Product" nav item is a category label, not a destination. "How it works" or "Tour" tells the viewer what they'll get.

**What's working**
- **One clear focal point.** The headline is about four times the body size and is the only heavy element, so there's no doubt where to start (one element dominates; strong contrast of scale).
- **The subhead does real work.** "Tickets, meetings, and leave" names the inputs and "the next five days" names the scope. Few heroes are that specific. Keep it.
- **Restrained colour.** Neutral type and one blue used only on the action, so the button is unmistakably the thing to do (accent colour restraint).

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether there's content below the fold that already shows the product. If there is, pull some of it up into this first screen anyway, because the empty bottom third gives the viewer no reason to scroll.
