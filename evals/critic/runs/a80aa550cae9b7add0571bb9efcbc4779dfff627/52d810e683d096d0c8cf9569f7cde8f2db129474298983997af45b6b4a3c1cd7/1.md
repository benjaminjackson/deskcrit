**First read.** My eye goes first to the purple "Sign in" button, then up to the red-outlined email field. It reads straight away as a sign-in card for a product called Quillpost. It feels calm and competent. Then I stop on the red field and look for the reason it's red, and there isn't one.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web sign-in screen caught just after a validation error, and its job is to get a returning user signed in quickly, or tell them exactly what to fix.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job. The error state doesn't. A red border with no message tells the user something is wrong without saying what. At the one moment this screen has to talk to them, it says nothing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The email error is shown by colour alone, with no words.** This is a defect. The email field (top of the form) has a red border and nothing else: no message, no icon. The user knows something is wrong, but not what. The actual problem is hard to see: "dana.ortiz@exmaple" has a typo ("exmaple") and no ending like ".com". People reread their own email as correct, so they'll likely press Sign in again or give up. Anyone who can't tell red from the grey border on the password field gets no signal at all. This breaks WCAG 3.3.1 (an error must be named in text) and 3.3.3 (say how to fix it when you know how). NN/g's error-message guidelines ask for plain words next to the field, with a fix and a non-colour cue. Fix: put one line of text under the field with an icon, saying what's wrong and how to fix it, e.g. "This email is missing its ending, like .com. Did you mean example.com?" Keep what they typed, as the screen already does.

**Smaller things.**
- "Create an account" at the bottom is grey body text with no link colour or underline, so it doesn't look clickable, while "Forgot your password?" above it is purple. Make it the same purple link style. If it sits next to plain text, underline it too (Norman, signifiers; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The "Forgot your password?" link sits close under the password field and close above the button. It's small, so check its clickable area is at least 24×24 px (WCAG 2.5.8).
- The "New here?" line looks like light grey on white. Check it reaches 4.5:1 contrast.

**What's working.**
- One column with labels above the fields, and only one filled button, so the next step is never in doubt. Keep that.
- The type scale is tight: wordmark, heading, labels/inputs, and small links. That's about four sizes with clear jumps between them and no near-duplicates.
- The spacing groups things correctly: each label sits nearer its own field than the field above it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether screen readers hear the error (e.g. `aria-invalid` plus a linked message), and whether focus moves to the field.
- The focus ring and the hover state of the button and links.
