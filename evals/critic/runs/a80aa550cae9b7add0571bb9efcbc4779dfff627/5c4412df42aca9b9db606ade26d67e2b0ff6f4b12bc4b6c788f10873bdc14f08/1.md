**First read.** The eye lands on "Pricing", drops to the three prices, and then has nowhere to go. The three cards are identical in weight, so no plan stands out and the eye slides across them as one row. It reads as a clean pricing page for Tidewell, which looks like a calendar or booking tool for small teams. It feels tidy, calm and competent. It also feels like a default template, and it stops before it asks you to do anything.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a SaaS pricing page. Its job is to help a small-business or team buyer pick a plan and start a signup or trial for that plan.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page shows prices but gives no way to act on them: no card has a button. It also doesn't say what "$29 / month" buys or how the plans relate, so the visitor can compare the numbers but can't choose with confidence or move to the next step.

**What would move it most**

1. **No plan has an action (defect).** The three cards end after their feature lists. The only action on the page is "Start free" in the header, top right, far from the prices, and it doesn't say which plan it starts. A visitor who has just picked Team has to go back up the page and hope the button means Team. That gap between deciding and acting is where pricing pages lose people (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the next step should be self-evident). Fix: give each card its own button at the same spot at the bottom, named for the plan and what happens next, e.g. "Start Team free for 14 days". If there is a trial, say how long it lasts and whether a card is needed, right under the button.

2. **The price is ambiguous (defect, in the copy).** The subtitle says "teams of every size", yet each price is just "/ month". A buyer can read "$29 / month" two ways: $29 for the whole team, or $29 per person. If it's per person, a 10-person team that budgeted $29 gets a bill for $290 and feels misled. If it's flat, you're hiding your best selling point. The same problem runs through the feature lists. Does Team include Solo's booking page and email reminders, or only the three things it lists? As written, Business looks like it drops the booking page. Fix: say "per month, whole team" or "per user / month" in words. Start the higher tiers with "Everything in Solo, plus:" so the lists add up instead of looking like three separate products.

3. **No plan is made the obvious pick, and nothing helps the visitor choose (convention + taste).** All three cards have the same border, weight and size, so nothing tells a hesitant buyer where to start (Refactoring UI: one high-emphasis element per view). The plan names are categories ("Team", "Business"), not situations the buyer recognises. Fix: make the plan you most want sold (probably Team) clearly dominant with the accent colour, a heavier border or the only filled button. Give each plan one line in the buyer's terms, e.g. "For a front desk booking for up to 10 people". That lets visitors place themselves by their own situation rather than read three lists side by side.

4. **The copy could be on anyone's page (defect).** "Pricing" as the headline and "Simple plans for teams of every size" as the subtitle are templated pricing-page boilerplate. They tell the visitor nothing about Tidewell or why it costs what it does. The one useful promise, "Change or cancel whenever you like", is buried at the end of a centred second sentence. Fix: use the headline to state the offer in the buyer's terms, e.g. what a booking calendar replaces for them at this price. Then move the cancel-anytime reassurance next to the buttons, where the visitor is deciding whether to commit.

**Smaller things.**
- Subtitle: the two centred lines break as a long line plus an orphaned "you like." Widen the measure so it fits on one line, or rebreak it at the sentence boundary (Bringhurst: break at natural phrase boundaries).
- Type scale: the headline (~48 px) and the prices (~40 px) look close in size. So do the plan names (~20 px) and the subtitle (~18 px). Those pairs read as near-misses rather than separate levels. Separate them by weight or colour, or use a wider size step.
- Plan names and prices are both dark and bold, so the name adds little in the hierarchy. Making the name lighter or a muted colour would let the price lead.
- Lower third: a large empty band sits under the cards. On a pricing page that is where an FAQ, a compare-all-features link, or reassurance (trial terms, refunds) usually goes, and those are the things that answer a buyer's last objections.

**What's working.**
- Restraint: one blue accent used only for the logo, the active nav underline and the header button. Keep that discipline. Once each card has a button, the blue will clearly mean "act here".
- Clear structure inside each card: name, price, then features, with consistent spacing and the same left edge in all three cards. The prices are the largest thing inside the cards, which is right.
- The active "Pricing" tab is clearly marked with an underline, so you know where you are in the site.
