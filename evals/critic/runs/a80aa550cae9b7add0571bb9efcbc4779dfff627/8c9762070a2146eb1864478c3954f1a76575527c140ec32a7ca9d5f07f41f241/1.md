**First read.** The eye goes straight to the headline. It is big, black and heavy on a warm cream ground, and nothing competes with it. Then it drops to the subhead, the dark green button, and across to the invoice card on the right. In about three seconds you know this is invoicing software for freelancers and small studios. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, a little quiet. The feature cards below come last, as they should.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero of a marketing homepage. It needs to get a freelancer or small-business owner to start a free trial by convincing them that invoicing and chasing payments will stop eating their time.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the promise is concrete, and there is one obvious action. The biggest weakness is that the invoice card, the only picture on the page, shows a finished invoice. It doesn't show the speed or the automation the headline and subhead promise, so the claim has to be taken on trust.

**What would move it most**

1. **The invoice card shows the result, not the product doing the work** (taste, but it carries the pitch). The card on the right is a neat, correct invoice, and the sums check out. But it is something any invoicing tool, or a spreadsheet, could make. The headline promises speed ("the time it takes to pour coffee") and the subhead promises that the product fills in clients, rates and tax and chases late payments. The card shows none of that. The viewer sees a static document and has to imagine the time it saved. The card is also small and light: a white box with thin type, sitting in a lot of cream. It weighs far less than the headline, so the right half of the hero feels empty, not balanced. Direction: make the visual prove the claim. Mark the fields Tallyfold filled in (client pulled from contacts, rate from last job, tax applied), or add a timestamp such as "Created 9:02 · Sent 9:03". You could also add the reminder schedule under it. Give it enough size and darkness to hold the right half against the headline.

2. **The subhead and the first feature card describe chasing differently** (defect, because it is a promise people will hold you to). The subhead says Tallyfold "chases late payments for you". The "Reminders that sound human" card says "Three gentle nudges before an invoice is overdue." A reader can take this two ways: it only reminds before the due date, or it also chases after the invoice is late. Someone whose real pain is clients who are already 30 days late will either doubt the subhead or sign up expecting something they won't get. Direction: make both lines say the same thing, and spell out the schedule if there is one, for example "Nudges before the due date, then follow-ups until it's paid".

**Smaller things.**
- **Hero spacing:** headline, subhead and button sit close together (about 20 px gaps) under a large empty band above. The block reads slightly cramped. Open the gap before the button a little so the ask gets air.
- **Feature cards:** the text fills only the top half of each card, leaving a big empty white area in the lower half. Shorten the cards to fit their content, or drop the borders and let space group them (Refactoring UI: fewer borders).
- **CTA copy:** "Start free for 14 days" is clear but generic. Something like "Send your first invoice free" names the exact thing the viewer will do and ties back to the headline. If no card is required, say so in small text under the button.
- **"Clients pay from the email with two taps":** "the email" assumes the reader knows which email. "From the invoice email" closes the gap.
- **Type scale:** the subhead (about 19 px) and the card titles (about 18 px) are almost the same size. Weight separates them, so it's fine. Just don't add any more sizes in between.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** it is the one dominant element, set tight and heavy, and it measures speed against something every reader already knows, pouring coffee. Keep both the copy and the weight.
- **Restraint with colour:** the dark green appears only on the button, so the single action is unmistakable. Keep it reserved for actions.
- **A shared left edge and grid:** logo, headline, subhead, button and the first card all line up on the same left edge, and the cards span the same width as the hero. The page feels ordered without any extra rules or lines.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Exact contrast of the grey subhead and nav text on the cream background. It looks above 4.5:1, but measure it to be sure.
