**First read.** The eye goes straight to the huge black headline, and it reads fine as a shape. Then it trips: the letters are packed so tightly they touch, and "MORNING" turns into a dark bar with no gaps. Next the eye drops to the one line of body text and then stops, because there is nothing else on the page. It feels bold and confident at first, then unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web hero or poster for a public harbour open day, meant to get local people to turn up and ride the free ferry. Correct me if it's something else.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline is crushed until it stops reading as letters, and the page never says when the event is or what to do next, so a viewer can't act on it even if they want to.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline's letters collide (defect).** In both lines of the display type, the letters run into each other. L–L, R–D, N–I–N, E–R and R–R all touch, and in "MORNING" the I and N strokes merge, so the word reads as a block of verticals ("MORNNNG"). The viewer has to decode the most important line on the page. All-caps text should be opened up, not tightened: Bringhurst (2.1.6) suggests about 5–10% of the type size, and Hochuli says letters should not touch. At this size it doesn't need that much, but it needs to go from heavily negative to roughly zero or slightly positive. Then check the gaps between words, which are almost gone ("ALL ABOARD", "MORNING FERRY").
2. **The viewer can't tell when it is, and the page asks nothing of them (defect).** "Free rides every half hour from the north landing" has no date, no start or end time, and nothing to tell a stranger where the north landing is. "Morning" in the headline is the only hint of timing. Those details are what people act on and remember. Without them the page is a mood, not an invitation. Add the date and hours (for example "Saturday 14 June, 8–11am"), a pointer to the landing, and one exact next step, such as a map link or "Add to calendar". "Bring a hat and a thermos" is good: it's specific and human, so keep it.
3. **The bottom half and right side of the frame are empty, with no reason given (taste).** Everything sits in the top-left 60%. Below the body text, about 340px of white space does nothing. As a full composition it reads as content that never loaded, not as calm. Either use that space for the practical details from point 2 (they belong here and would anchor the bottom), or add an image of the harbour or ferry. Right now nothing on the page says "water" or "boat" except the words.

**Smaller things.**
- The "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" eyebrow has the same crammed tracking as the headline, at a small size and in grey, so it's harder to read than it should be. Letterspace it positively.
- The type scale has three sizes (eyebrow, display, body) with clear jumps between them. That's fine, but the eyebrow is only slightly smaller than the body text and is also grey and bold. Pick either size or colour to set it apart.

**What's working.**
- There's one clear focal point. Nothing competes with the headline, so once the spacing is fixed the hierarchy works with no further changes.
- The body copy uses plain, specific language ("every half hour", "hat and a thermos"). It sounds like a person speaking and gives a real picture of the morning.
- The left alignment is strong: eyebrow, headline and body all share one left edge at about x=90, which gives the page a clean spine to build on.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** This may be a hero section cropped from a longer page. If the date and hours appear further down, point 3 becomes less serious, but point 2 still stands, because they need to be visible without scrolling.
