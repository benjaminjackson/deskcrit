**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline at top left, reads "Travel lighter with the Wayfarer pack", drops to the grey line about 22 litres and a kilo, and stops on the black "Shop the pack" button. That path is clean and quick. Then the eye goes right and down and finds nothing: about half the frame is blank white. I can tell it's a bag, but I never see it. It feels calm and competent, but it could be any brand's page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero at the top of a product page, meant to make someone who travels a lot want this bag and click through to buy it.

**Verdict.** The type and the copy are well made. As captured, though, it asks people to buy a bag they cannot see, and I can't tell whether that's the design or a capture fault. If the right side really is meant to be empty, that is the main thing stopping this from selling.

**What would move it most**
1. **Show the bag (only if the empty right half is the design).** *Defect.* The text column stops around x≈700, and roughly 580 px of width plus the bottom 300 px are empty. The design's best claims are all physical: it fits under a seat, opens flat like a suitcase, weighs under a kilo. A viewer can't check any of them without a picture. A shopper deciding on a bag wants to judge its size, shape and material, and here they have to click through just to find out what it looks like. Put a large product image in that right-hand space, ideally showing one of the claims: the bag opened flat, or the bag under a seat. If the layout already has an image slot there, see "Couldn't judge" below.
2. **The headline could sit on anyone's page.** *Taste, and a copy point.* "Travel lighter with the…" is a category promise every carry-on brand makes. Your sharpest point is buried in the grey subline: a bag that opens like a suitcase but fits under the seat in front of you. That is what makes this bag different. Move that into the headline and let the grey line carry the specs.
3. **The ask makes people commit before they can judge the bag.** *Taste.* "Shop the pack" gives no price, so the one number a shopper acts on is missing. The best line on the page is "fits under an airline seat", because it measures the bag against something the viewer already knows, and the ask doesn't build on it. Show the price near the button. Point the secondary link at the comparison people actually make ("See it next to a standard carry-on" or "Will it fit your airline?") rather than "Compare sizes", which names a category, not an action.

**Smaller things.**
- No logo or navigation is visible. If that's the real page and not a crop, people can't tell whose store this is. Check the full page.
- The text block sits high, with about 120 px above it and about 300 px below. Once there's an image, line the block up with the image's vertical middle or its top edge so the white space looks planned, not left over.
- Bodies like the CAA or FAA publish weight in kg, and "less than a kilo" is approximate. Giving the exact weight (for example "890 g") makes the claim easy to check and easy to trust.

**What's working.**
- **Type scale.** I count four clear levels: the bold headline at about 72 px, the body at about 20 px, and the eyebrow and button labels at about 15 px, with weight and colour (blue eyebrow, grey body) separating the small sizes instead of extra sizes. That is the "bolder, not bigger" approach from *Refactoring UI*. Keep it.
- **Headline set tight.** The two lines have tight leading and tight tracking, so they read as one block (Bringhurst).
- **Specific subline and clear action order.** "22 litre", "under an airline seat", "opens flat" and "under a kilo" are concrete facts a traveller can check. The subline runs about 60 characters a line, in the comfortable range. The filled primary button with an underlined text link beside it makes the order of the two actions obvious.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Possible missing image.** The empty right half looks like a two-column hero whose image didn't load. Check it in a live browser. If an image does load there, finding 1 doesn't apply, and the verdict moves to findings 2 and 3.
- Button hover and focus states.
