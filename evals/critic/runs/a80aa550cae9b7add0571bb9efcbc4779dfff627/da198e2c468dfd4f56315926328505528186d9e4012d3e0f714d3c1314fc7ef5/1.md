**Comparison.** The only change between the two versions is the colour of the small grey text: the plan descriptions ("For one person who sends a few invoices a month") and the four feature lines in each card. In version 1 that text is a light mid-grey and looks weak against white. My guess is about 3.5–4:1, which is under the WCAG 1.4.3 minimum of 4.5:1 for text this small. Check it with a contrast picker to be sure. In version 2 it's a darker grey that looks comfortably over the minimum. These lines are what the reader compares to choose a plan, so in version 1 the part they need most is the part that's hardest to read. Version 2 fixes that without losing the hierarchy, because the plan names and prices are still darker and bolder. Version 2 is better.

The rest of this critique is about version 2.

**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, drops to the three prices, then goes to the blue "Start free trial" button in the middle card. It reads as a tidy, ordinary pricing page for a small-business accounting or invoicing tool. It's calm and clear, but it doesn't stand out.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a pricing page that helps a small-business owner pick a plan and start a trial.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job: the prices, tiers and main action are clear. What's in the way now is that the feature lists can't be compared across plans, so choosing between them takes more work than it should.

**What would move it most**
1. **The feature rows don't line up across cards (defect).** Row 1 is invoices in "Sole trader", invoices and bills in "Small team", and "Everything in Small team" in "Growing firm". Payroll shows up in two cards but not the first. Bank feeds are in the first two but not the third. To see what an upgrade gets you, you have to read each list on its own and do the comparison in your head. Fix: give every card the same rows in the same order, with the same dimension on each row (invoices, bank feeds, payroll, support). Then the differences line up and can be read straight across.
2. **The blue button on the middle card isn't explained (defect, minor).** "Small team" gets the only filled button, but nothing tells the reader why. A visitor who fits "Sole trader" may wonder whether they're being pushed toward the dearer plan. Fix: add a short label to that card saying why it's highlighted ("Most popular" or similar).
3. **The headline is generic (taste).** "Plans for every size of business" could be on anyone's pricing page and tells the reader nothing about the product. Fix: say what the product does for them, for example "Invoicing, bank feeds and payroll from $9 a month."

**Smaller things.**
- Each card has about 60 px of empty space under its button. Trim it so the button ends the card.
- "Talk to us" doesn't say what happens next (a call? an email? a quote?). Name the actual step.
- "Prices exclude sales tax" is a fact people will hold you to, and it sits in the subhead at body size. That's fine, but keep it next to the prices if the layout changes.

**What's working.**
- The prices are big and bold, with "a month" set small next to them, so all three amounts can be compared at a glance.
- Each plan name has a one-line "For…" description that tells readers which plan fits them before they reach the features.
- The type scale is tight: headline, price, plan name, body and button, each with a clear job.

Better: version 2
