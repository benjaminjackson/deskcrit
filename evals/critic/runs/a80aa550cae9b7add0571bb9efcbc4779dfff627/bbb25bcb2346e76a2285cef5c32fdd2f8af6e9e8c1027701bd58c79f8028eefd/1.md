**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the title "Maps of the inland sea", then moves to the large tan panel on the right, then down the left column. It reads as a museum exhibition page: calm, scholarly and quiet. The only change between the versions is the headings. Version 1 sets them in the same regular-weight serif as the body text. Version 2 sets the title and the two subheads in a bold sans that matches the "Calder Museum" wordmark.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an exhibition page for museum visitors. They need to learn what the show is, what's in it, and when and how they can go.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The bold sans headings give the column a hierarchy you can scan, so "Opening times", which is what a visitor actually acts on, can be found at a glance. In version 1 the subheads are a regular serif only a few sizes up from the body, so they blend into the paragraphs. In both versions the biggest remaining problem is the image panel, which shows no map.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **The image panel on the right shows no map.** It is a plain tan gradient that takes up nearly half the page. On a page about forty hand-coloured charts, the one thing that would make someone want to come is a picture of a chart, and it isn't there. This is a defect if it's the design, but it may be an image that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken (see the last section). The fix is a real detail of one chart, cropped tight enough that you can see the hand colouring.
2. **There is no next step.** The page gives dates, times and "Entry is free", then stops, leaving the lower third empty. A visitor who has decided to go has nothing to act on next to the information that made them decide. "Tickets" exists only as a small item in the nav, and it is ambiguous because entry is free: does this show need a timed booking or not? This is a defect. The fix is to say it plainly under Opening times ("No booking needed — just come in" or "Book a free timed slot"), plus directions or a link to the address.
3. **The subheads sit almost equally between the text above and below them** (version 1 has the same problem). There is only slightly more space above "What you will see" and "Opening times" than below, so each heading doesn't clearly belong to the paragraph it introduces. This is a convention: proximity is what groups a heading with its text. The fix is to clearly increase the space above each subhead and tighten the space below.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav ends about 36 px short of the image's right edge, so the header and content don't share a right margin. Align them to the same edge.
- "Until 2 March" has no year. Add it, because a visitor plans around that date.
- The intro paragraph (18 px or so) is the same size as the body. It could step up slightly or take a muted colour so it reads as the standfirst (the short intro under the title).

**What's working.**
- In version 2, the bold sans headings over a serif body give a clear two-voice system. The wordmark already uses that sans, so the headings feel like part of the brand rather than an add-on.
- The copy is concrete and specific: "a brass sounding lead found in the harbour mud in 1958" makes the show feel worth the trip. "Entry is free, and the gallery is on the ground floor" answers cost and access in one line. Keep that voice.
- The body measure looks like about 70 characters per line, which reads comfortably.
