**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big white headline on the left, then drops to the subline and the outlined button. Then it slides right into a bright, busy field of green ovals and stays there with nothing to find. It reads as a small plant nursery's homepage. It feels fresh and friendly, but generic. It could be any green business, because nothing on the screen is a real plant.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage hero for a local nursery. It should make nearby gardeners want shade plants from this shop and get them either browsing stock or coming to visit.

**Verdict.** The type and the hierarchy work. The piece fails at its main job because a plant nursery shows no plants. The hero image is abstract confetti, so the right half of the screen grabs attention and gives nothing back. The one action is also styled too weakly to pull the eye.

**What would move it most**
1. **The background isn't ferns.** The full-bleed backdrop, and most of all the right half, is randomly scattered flat ovals. None of them has the shape of a fern frond or a hosta leaf. The headline promises "Ferns and shade plants" and the image doesn't show one. Plant buyers choose with their eyes, so the most persuasive thing this page could show is missing. The pattern also looks like a placeholder or generated texture, which costs trust for a business whose selling point is "grown on the hill." **Defect.** The visual and the headline should work as two halves of one idea; here the visual says only "green." Fix: use one real photograph of their own stock, such as fern fronds in shade on the nursery's benches, cropped so the left third is quiet and dark behind the type.

2. **The brightest area on the screen is empty.** On the right, saturated lime and light-green shapes at full contrast carry more visual weight than anything except the headline. They pull the eye away from the button and the button has to win it back. Saturated colour should mark what matters (Refactoring UI). Here it marks the decoration. **Defect.** Fix: if the texture stays at all, calm it down to a narrow, darker, lower-contrast range. Better, let the photograph in point 1 do the work and keep its brightest area out of the corner the eye has no reason to visit.

3. **The only action is a ghost button.** "Browse the greenhouse" is a thin white outline with no fill, sitting on a textured background. Next to the bold headline it reads as secondary, even though it's the one high-emphasis action on the page (Material Design; Refactoring UI on primary vs. secondary actions). **Convention, with a cost here:** the eye goes from headline to texture rather than headline to button. Fix: make it a solid button in a colour the background doesn't contain, such as a warm cream or terracotta, so it is the second thing seen.

4. **The copy could belong to any nursery.** "Ferns and shade plants for every corner" is a category plus a cliché. "Grown on the hill" assumes the reader already knows which hill. The ask, "Browse the greenhouse," leaves the viewer to work out what they'll find. The words don't say what's different about this nursery or give the visitor a case from their own garden. **Defect.** Fix: point to the viewer's actual problem, e.g. a dark north-facing bed or the space under a tree, and make the ask that specific ("Find plants for a shady bed"). If the hill and "this weekend" are the real selling points, name the place and say what "ready to plant this weekend" means: in stock, potted up, collectable on Saturday.

**Smaller things.**
- **Subline over texture:** the 20 px white subline sits on mid-green ovals. Check contrast at the brightest leaf behind it, not the average (WCAG 1.4.3), or deepen the dark scrim under the left column.
- **Headline break:** "corner" sits alone on line three. Breaking after "plants" or widening the measure slightly would give a less ragged block. This is taste.
- **Missing visit details:** "Visit" is in the nav, but the hero has no address, opening hours, or town. For a local nursery that's what many visitors came for. A small line under the button would cover it.
- **Type scale:** I count four sizes: the headline (~64 px), the subline (~20), the wordmark (~22), and nav and button text (~16). The wordmark and subline are close in size, but colour and weight separate them, so it holds.

**What's working.**
- **The type hierarchy:** the headline is clearly dominant, it's set tight so the lines hold together as one block, and the subline and button step down cleanly. The left column shares one edge, so the reading order is never in doubt.
- **The plain header:** a white bar, the wordmark on the left, four plainly named links on the right. It's conventional in the right way, and "Care guides" and "Visit" use the customer's own words.
- **The left-side darkening:** fading the background to deep green behind the type is the right move. Keep it when the real photo goes in.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states, which matter more on a ghost button than on a filled one, and how the hero reflows on a phone, where the texture could end up directly behind the headline.
