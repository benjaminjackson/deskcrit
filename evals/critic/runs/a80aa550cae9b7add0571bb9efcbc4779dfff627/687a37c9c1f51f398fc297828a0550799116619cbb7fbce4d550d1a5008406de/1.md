**First read.** The eye goes to the bold title, "Notes for ferry passengers", and then straight down a single left-hand column of plain text. It reads as a quiet, sober information page for people about to use a small river ferry. It feels calm and trustworthy. It also feels like one even stream of text: the subheads are easy to see, but the page doesn't break into sections.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page a passenger reads, or comes back to and skims, to learn when the ferry runs, how to board, and how to pay.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The type is clean, the column is a readable width, and the copy is plain. The biggest thing in the way is spacing. The gap above each subhead is about the same as the gap between ordinary paragraphs, so sections don't separate. Next is the timetable line: it gives no actual times, and times are the thing a passenger acts on.

**What would move it most**

1. **Sections don't separate (defect).** By eye, the paragraphs are about 51 px apart, measured from the last line of one to the first line of the next. "Boarding" starts about 54 px below the line before it, and "Tickets" about 52 px below the last bullet. A section break is therefore spaced like a paragraph break. Someone skimming for "how do I pay?" sees an even run of blocks and has to read the bold lines one by one instead of seeing three chunks.

   Each heading does sit nearer its own text (about 36 px) than the text above it, so it isn't floating. The problem is that the space between groups isn't clearly bigger than the space inside them, and equal gaps erase the groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on grouping by proximity). Bringhurst §2.2.2 sets subheads with more space above than below.

   Fix: either roughly double the space above each subhead (to about 80–90 px), or cut the space between paragraphs to about half a line. Then the paragraph gap and the section gap become two clearly different steps.

2. **The service hours aren't given (defect, copy).** "Every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" is the line a passenger plans around, and it can't be acted on. One person reads "early morning" as 6:00 and another as 8:00, and the one who guesses wrong misses the last boat home. The same goes for "the last…" in the winter section, if it ends the same way.

   Fix: give the first and last departure from each landing ("First boat 6:20 from the north landing, last 22:40 from the market quay"), and link to the full timetable. Put these times above the twelve-minute crossing note, because they matter more.

**Smaller things.**
- Opening paragraph: "every forty minutes" spelled out in a timetable line is harder to scan than "every 40 minutes". Use figures for times and intervals.
- "Tickets": no price, and no word on whether the kiosk or on-board sales take cards. A passenger needs both before they reach the ramp.
- Bulleted list: the bullets are indented from the text edge. Hanging them in the margin would keep the column's left edge clean. This is taste, not a fix that matters.

**What's working.**
- The measure: lines run about 60–70 characters, which is right for reading (Bringhurst). The empty right side is fine for a single reading column, so don't stretch the column to fill it.
- The type scale is three clear levels: title, bold subhead, body. Subheads are set apart by weight as well as size, so nothing collides.
- The voice: short, concrete sentences in the order a passenger needs them ("Foot passengers board first, from the ramp on the upstream side"). Keep it.
