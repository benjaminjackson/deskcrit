**First read.** The eye goes first to the headline, then drops to the orange button, which is the only warm, saturated thing on the page. Then it hits the grey "Keep your streak going" box right under the button and stalls, because it reads like a second offer. The teal chat-bubble panel on the right takes about half the hero but gives the eye nothing to look at. The page reads as a calm, clean, somewhat templated landing page for a language-learning app.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage of a speaking-first language app, meant to get a new visitor to start the free trial.

**Verdict.** The top half nearly works. The headline makes a concrete promise and there is one clear button. What gets in the way most is the streak box: it's a message for people already using the app, sitting where a newcomer needs a reason to click. The second problem is the illustration, which takes up half the hero and shows nothing.

**What would move it most**

1. **The streak box talks to the wrong person, in the wrong place.** *Defect.*
   - The grey card under the button says "Keep your streak going," but a first-time visitor has no streak. They have to stop and work out whether they already have an account.
   - Its bold heading is the same weight as the button label and sits 30 px below it. That creates a second stop in the hero: the eye path goes headline, button, then a dead end. The tinted card also makes it look like a second action (Material Design / Refactoring UI: one high-emphasis element per view).
   - The claim doesn't land either. "Finish the course twice as often" leaves out the comparison: twice as often as whom? It also never says what that means for this visitor.
   - Fix: move it out of the hero, or rewrite it for a newcomer and the moment of deciding. For example: "Five minutes a day is enough: learners who practise five days in a row are twice as likely to finish as those who don't." Set it as plain text, not a box, so the button is the only thing below the headline with any weight.

2. **The illustration takes half the hero and shows nothing about the product.** *Defect (wasted prime space), with a taste judgement on the fix.*
   - Two empty speech bubbles in a pale teal box could belong to any chat, support or messaging product. The page's one real difference is "a tutor that listens" and "feedback on each word." The best spot on the page fails to show it, so the visitor has to take that claim on trust.
   - The panel also ignores the page's grid: it stops at about x=1076, while the nav and the three-step row run to about 1224. That leaves an unexplained strip of white on the right of the hero (Müller-Brockmann: one grid governs the page).
   - Fix: replace it with the product doing its job, such as a spoken phrase with per-word feedback marks, sized to the right-hand column edge. That lets visitors judge the value for themselves instead of reading about it.

3. **The promise and the ask leave gaps the visitor has to fill.** *Defect: these are lines people will hold you to.*
   - "Start your free week" doesn't say what happens on day eight: paid automatically, card required or not? Visitors who suspect a trap hesitate.
   - "A tutor that listens" can be read as a human tutor or as software, and those are different products at different prices. If the visitor guesses wrong, they'll feel misled after signing up.
   - Fix: say who the tutor is in a few words, and add one line under the button that answers the day-eight question (e.g. "No card needed").
   - Optional: make the ask more specific, so the visitor can picture their first five minutes: "Say your first Spanish sentence today."

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale:** I count the headline (~44 px), then the subhead, card heading, step headings and body/nav all within about 15–18 px. The card heading, step headings and subhead are only a point or two apart, so they read as near-misses, not separate levels. Keep three sizes (display, lead, body) and separate the headings by weight alone.
- **Three-step row:** it's set small and quiet below a 50 px gap, and the screen ends with about 180 px of empty white under it. Either give the steps more presence (they're the clearest explanation on the page) or pull them up.
- **Step 3, "Finish with a short chat about your day":** chat with whom? Name the tutor again so the three steps form one loop.
- **Nav:** "For schools" sits beside the consumer links with equal weight. That's fine, but check it doesn't pull school buyers into the consumer trial.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks" is concrete, has a time frame, and promises an outcome rather than a feature. Keep that specificity.
- **One warm accent:** the orange button is the only saturated warm colour on the page, so it's unmistakably the action. Don't spend that orange anywhere else.
- **Listen / Speak / Use it:** the steps are short, use verbs, and explain the method in one glance.

- Whether the page continues below 800 px (social proof, pricing). If nothing follows, the bare white bottom band matters more.
