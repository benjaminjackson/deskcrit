**First read.** My eye lands on "Account settings", then drops down four identical white cards, each with a bold label, one grey line and an outlined button on the right. In the sidebar it catches the blue "Billing" pill, which is the only coloured block on the screen. It reads as a calm, tidy SaaS settings hub, and a slightly generic one. The second look raises a question: if I'm on Billing, why am I looking at an overview of everything?

**What it's for.** My assumption: the overview screen of a team product's account settings, for a workspace member who wants to find and change one setting quickly.

**Verdict.** It's clean, readable and well built, but the navigation contradicts the content. The sidebar says "you are on Billing", the page shows a general overview, and the cards don't match the sidebar's list. On a screen whose whole job is helping people find the right setting, that mismatch is the main problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **The selected state is wrong.** The sidebar highlights "Billing", but the title says "Account settings" and the page shows Profile, Notifications, Billing and Sessions as equal cards. People are told they're in one place while looking at another, so they can't tell whether clicking "Billing" again would show something new or whether this is the Billing page. This is a **defect** against Nielsen heuristic 1 (visibility of system status) and heuristic 4 (consistency). Fix: if this is an overview, either highlight nothing or add an "Overview" item and highlight that. If it really is the Billing page, show billing content and title it "Billing".
2. **The cards and the sidebar describe two different structures.** The sidebar lists six areas: Profile, Notifications, Billing, Members, Integrations, Security. The cards show four, and one of them, "Sessions", isn't in the sidebar at all, while Members and Integrations have no card. People have to guess that Sessions lives under Security, and they get no overview of half the areas. This is a **defect** against Norman's idea of the conceptual model. Fix: use one list of names for both. Either give every sidebar area a card, or make the overview cover only what the sidebar covers and rename "Sessions" to match "Security" (or place it clearly under it).
3. **The Billing card describes something the button doesn't let you act on.** "Your plan renews on 1 March. The invoice goes to the address on file." It tells people a renewal is coming but not which plan, how much, which year, or which address. Then the only button, "View invoices", looks backwards at past bills. Someone who reads this and wants to change their plan or fix the address before 1 March has no clear next step. Dates and money are what people hold you to, so this is a **defect**, not a style note. Fix: say the plan, amount and date in full (for example "Team plan, £96, renews 1 March 2027"), name the billing email or address, and make the button match what people will want to do here, such as "Change plan or billing address", with invoices as a secondary link.

**Smaller things.**
- The cards are about 125 px tall for two lines of text, so the page feels padded and needs scrolling sooner than it should. This is **taste**: about two-thirds of that vertical padding would still breathe.
- "Tallis Cloud" (about 18 px bold) and the card headings (about 17 px bold) are nearly the same size and weight, so they read as one level by accident. Make the wordmark clearly bigger or clearly different.
- The logo lines up with the edge of the selected pill (x≈64), but the sidebar text and title start at x≈78 and x≈304. Choose one left edge for the logo and the nav text.
- All four buttons are outlined and equally strong. That's right for a hub with no single main action, so keep it. Just don't add a filled button later without deciding which action matters most.
- "Review" (Sessions) and "Manage" (Notifications) are vague next to "View invoices". "Review devices" says what you'll get.

**What's working.**
- Each card has one plain sentence that says what the setting does for you. "Your name and photo appear on comments and in the member list" and "You are signed in on three now" are specific and speak to the reader. Keep that voice.
- The restraint: one neutral grey background, white cards, one blue used only for things you can click and the selected state. Hierarchy comes from weight and value, not decoration, and the description text looks comfortably above 4.5:1 contrast.
- Strong alignment. The page title sits level with the first nav item, the card edges match the header's right margin at x≈1215, and the buttons share one right edge.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and keyboard-focus states for the outlined buttons and nav items. With a thin border and no fill, the focus ring needs to be clearly visible (at least 3:1 contrast).
- Whether clicking a card button opens a new page or the matching sidebar section. That decides which fix to finding 1 is right.
