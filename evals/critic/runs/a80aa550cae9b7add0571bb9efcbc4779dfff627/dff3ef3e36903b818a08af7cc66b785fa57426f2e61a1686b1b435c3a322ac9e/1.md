**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming this is a help section for customers who have ordered bread for pickup. They want to find their question fast and get a clear rule they can act on.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job. You can scan it, the groups are clear, and nothing competes. What holds it back is the words. The two answers people will act on and later hold the bakery to, cancelling and collecting for someone else, can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways** (defect; fourth block, "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead"). "A day ahead" of what, and how much of a day? One customer reads it as 24 hours before their pickup slot. Another reads it as any time the day before. Someone picking up at 9am Saturday who cancels at 8pm Friday is fine under the second reading and too late under the first. They lose the bread and the money, and they feel misled. This is a line people commit to, so it can't be fuzzy. State the exact cutoff in their terms, such as "You can cancel until 24 hours before your pickup time." Then say what happens after it: do they pay, or are they refunded? "The bread has already been baked for you" explains the rule but doesn't tell them what it costs.

2. **"Along with a name": whose name?** (defect; second block). The person collecting can't tell whether to give the counter your name or their own. Guessing wrong means an awkward exchange at the till, or being turned away. Say it outright: "They'll need your order number and your name." If that's the rule, add "the name on the order".

3. **The allergy answer only helps someone already standing in the shop** (defect; third block). Every route in it is physical: a card by the till, ask the baker. But this page is for people who order ahead. Someone with an allergy can't check ingredients before they commit to an order. That's the moment it matters, and it's the risky gap on the page. Point to where ingredients appear when they order, or give a way to ask before ordering (a phone number or email). Keep the in-shop card as the second option.

**Smaller things.**
- "Open your order" in the first answer assumes the viewer knows where that is. Link it, or say where to find it ("in your confirmation email" or "under My orders").
- The stray "Design review: failed…" line sits in the content column at body size with no heading, so it reads as a fifth answer. Remove it before this ships.
- The answers run about 95 characters a line at their longest ("Orders can be cancelled…"), well past the 45 to 75 range (Bringhurst). With one- or two-line answers it costs little. Still, narrowing the column to about 640 px would make them easier to read and stop the one-word runt "anything." in the allergy answer.

**What's working.**
- **Grouping by space.** Each heading sits much closer to its own answer than to the one above it, so every question-and-answer reads as one unit without rules or boxes. Keep that ratio.
- **A two-level type scale.** There's one bold heading size and one regular body size, with a big, obvious step between them and nothing in between. For an FAQ that's exactly enough. Don't add a third size when you revise.
- **Headings phrased as the task in the customer's words** ("Collecting for someone else"), not as system labels. That's why someone scanning finds their question on the first pass.
