**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the masthead, then slides along five identical cards and has nowhere to stop. It reads as a row of tiles, not a front page. In version 2 the eye goes straight to the big blue block and the large headline under it, "New footbridge over the Soar opens after two years of work". From there it moves to the four smaller stories on the right. Version 2 feels like a local paper's front page. Version 1 feels like a list.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page of a small local news site. It should tell a reader at a glance what the main story is today and let them scan the rest.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. It makes one decision version 1 doesn't: the footbridge story leads. That gives the page a focal point and a reading order. Version 1 gives every story equal weight, so the reader has to read all five headlines to learn what matters.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **One story now leads (defect fixed).** In version 1 all five cards share the same image size, headline size and position. The reader can't tell the lead story from a library opening-hours update. In version 2 the footbridge image takes about half the width, and its headline looks about twice the size of the others. The page now answers "what's the news today?" before anyone reads a word. This is the main reason version 2 wins.
2. **The type scale now has real steps (convention).** Version 1 uses three sizes: masthead, one card-headline size and one body size. With every story at one level, the headline size has nothing to rank. Version 2 adds a clearly larger lead-headline size, so it runs masthead, lead headline, secondary headline, dek (the one-line summary under each headline). Those are four levels with large gaps between them, and each size has an obvious job (Bringhurst).
3. **Headlines stop breaking into stacks (taste, but noticeable).** Version 1's narrow columns, about 210 px wide, break "New footbridge over the Soar opens after two years of work" across three lines. The deks wrap after two or three words. Version 2's wider secondary columns hold most headlines on two lines and every dek on one, so each story reads in one glance.

**Remaining findings for version 2**
1. **The lead headline and the bottom-row headlines sit at different heights (taste).** The footbridge headline starts at about y 548, the bottom-right headlines at about y 590. The lead dek ends at about 643, the small deks at about 653. Because the edges almost line up, it looks like a mistake rather than a choice. Either make the lead image end level with the bottom-row images, so the headlines share a top line, or offset them clearly.
2. **The gap between the rows on the right looks unintended (taste).** Under "Traders pay half rent until March." and "Forty plots added at Glebe Farm." there are about 90 px of blank space before the next images. The gap exists only because the right-hand grid is stretched to the lead image's height. Tighten the rows or give the gap a job, such as a timestamp or section label. Otherwise the grid reads as loosely held.

**Smaller things.**
- The lead dek runs about 85 to 90 characters on one line, which is past the 75-character upper limit (Bringhurst). It's fine as a single line, but set a narrower measure if it ever wraps.
- The nav ("News · Sport · Business · Weather") is small, grey and separated by dots, so it reads like a caption. Check the contrast against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3), and consider spacing the items more so they read as links.
- About 140 px of empty space sits below the stories with nothing to end the page. A section rule or a "More news" heading would show the reader there's more.

**What's working.**
- The serif headlines with a sans-serif dek are a classic newspaper pairing. Keep them: they make the page feel like a paper rather than an app.
- The heavy rule under the masthead clearly anchors the page.
- The headlines are specific and local ("Allotment waiting list falls below 100", "Keeper saves two in the shoot-out"). Each says who did what, and none could sit on any other site.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The images are flat colour placeholders. Whether the lead works depends on the real photo carrying that much space. Check it with an actual image.
- I can't tell how the layout behaves on narrower screens.

Better: version 2
