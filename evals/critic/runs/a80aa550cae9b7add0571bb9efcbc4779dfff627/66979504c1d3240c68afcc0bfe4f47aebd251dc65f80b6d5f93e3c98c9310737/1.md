**First read.** Your eye lands on the big black headline, "Send an invoice in the time it takes to pour coffee.", then drops to the dark green button. Only after that does it drift right to the small white invoice card. Then it falls to three tall white cards that are mostly empty. It reads as invoicing software for freelancers and small studios. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, but very quiet, and the bottom third looks unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a landing page for invoicing software aimed at freelancers and small businesses, and its job is to get them to start the free trial.

**Verdict.** The headline and button work. The page states its two real claims (it fills in the invoice for you, and it chases late payments for you) but never shows either. The one piece of product on screen is a small, static invoice pushed to the far edge, and the cards below are half-empty boxes.

**What would move it most**

1. **The product proof is too small, too far right, and shows the wrong thing.** *(defect)*
   - **Where:** the invoice card in the hero (about 340 × 200 px).
   - **What the viewer gets:** it sits about 270 px from the end of the text column with nothing between. The eye has no path from headline to evidence, and it reads as decoration.
   - **What it shows:** a finished invoice, which any invoicing tool can show. Nothing shows the two claims in the subhead, that Tallyfold fills in clients, rates and tax, and that it chases late payments. So the viewer is asked to take the coffee claim on trust.
   - **Fix:** make the product the second thing the eye reaches. Enlarge it and pull it toward the text so the hero reads as headline, then proof. Make it show the behaviour, not the result. For example, mark the client, rate and tax fields as filled in automatically, or add a status line under the total such as "Reminder 1 sent Tue · paid Thu".
   - A good comparison to aim for: set the viewer's current routine (a spreadsheet or a Word template) beside a Tallyfold invoice. Then they can judge the saving on terms they already know.

2. **The feature cards are mostly empty white boxes.** *(defect)*
   - **Where:** the three cards across the bottom, each roughly 395 × 220 px.
   - **What the viewer gets:** one bold line and one or two grey lines at the top, then about 120 px of blank white. At a glance it looks like images failed to load or the page is half-built. That undercuts the "trustworthy money software" feel the rest of the page earns. The space is leftover, not chosen, and the borders mark out emptiness.
   - **Fix:** either shrink the cards to fit their text, or fill them with evidence. The obvious one is a real three-line reminder email under "Reminders that sound human", which is your most distinctive claim and currently has no proof.

3. **The copy contradicts itself on when reminders go out.** *(defect)*
   - **The clash:** the subhead says Tallyfold "chases late payments for you". The first card says "Three gentle nudges before an invoice is overdue."
   - **Two readings:**
     - It only nudges before the due date, so nothing happens once a payment is actually late.
     - It chases after the due date too.
   - **Why it matters:** this is the feature people buy, and they will hold you to it the first time a client pays late. A viewer who takes the first reading may decide it doesn't solve their real problem.
   - **Fix:** state the schedule in one sentence, for example "A nudge before it's due, and two more if it's late."

4. **The button names the trial, not the thing you'll do.** *(taste, close to a convention)*
   - "Start free for 14 days" tells the viewer the terms, not the first step. It also leaves out what people act on: do they need a card?
   - **Fix:** make the button name the action, such as "Send your first invoice free". Put "14 days free · no card needed" (if true) in small text under the button.

**Smaller things**
- **Headline to subhead:** the subhead sits very close under the three-line headline, about as close as the headline's own line spacing, so it looks wedged in. Give it a little more space, but keep it tighter than the gap to the button.
- **Too many boxes:** the nav bar, hero panel, invoice card and three feature cards are all boxed, rounded containers. Six frames on one screen flatten the hierarchy. Drop the white box around the nav and let space do the separating. *(taste)*
- **Nav text:** the links look small and pale grey next to the 16–17 px subhead. Check that they reach 4.5:1 contrast.
- **Type scale:** I count about five sizes (headline, subhead, card titles, invoice and card body, nav). The invoice line items and the card body look only slightly different in size. Use one size for both.

**What's working**
- **The headline:** big, heavy, tightly set and short. It's the one clear focal point, and the coffee line is concrete and easy to picture. Keep its scale and weight.
- **The invoice details:** the figures are right-aligned with a light rule above a bold total, and the numbers add up ($640 + $45 + 8% tax = $739.80). That kind of care builds trust in money software. Keep it when you enlarge the card.
- **The palette:** the warm off-white, pale sage and deep green button are calm and coherent. The green is used only on the action, so the button reads as the one thing to press.
