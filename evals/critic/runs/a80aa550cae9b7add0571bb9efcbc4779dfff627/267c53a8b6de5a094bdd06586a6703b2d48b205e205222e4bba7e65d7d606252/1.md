**First read.** The eye goes to the bold "Landing stage rebuild" headline, then to the paragraph and the flat grey-and-blue illustration, then right to the boxed form with the blue "Sign up" button. It reads as a local ferry operator's news or info page with a sign-up for timetable alerts in the sidebar. It feels calm, plain and civic. Nothing is loud, and nothing is especially memorable.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it tells ferry passengers what changed at the rebuilt landing stage and gets regular riders to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and readable, but the sign-up form can't do its job. It promises to "send one short note" but never asks where to send it. The article also hides its one point that matters to passengers, step-free boarding at every tide, in the last line.

**What would move it most**

1. **The form has no way to reach anyone.** In the right-hand card, the fields are "Your name" and "Home landing", and the helper text says "We send one short note when the timetable changes." There's no email or phone field. A careful reader stops and wonders whether this sends texts, emails, or nothing at all. A careless one signs up and never hears back. Either way the form can't deliver what it promises. This is a **defect** (Norman's conceptual model: the screen should show how the system works). Fix: add the contact field the notes go to, and say which channel in the heading or button, e.g. "Email me when my timetable changes". "Sign up" is generic and doesn't say what the viewer is agreeing to. "Name" is the field you could cut, because nothing on the page needs it.

2. **"Home landing" is a blank text box for what's really a fixed list.** People will type "North", "north landing", "N. stage" or a misspelling, and an alert keyed to a landing will quietly miss them. The label also doesn't say why it's asked. This is a **defect** (Nielsen 5, error prevention; Norman, constraints). Fix: make it a picker of the actual landings, labelled so the reason is clear, e.g. "Which landing do you leave from?"

3. **The one line that matters to passengers comes last.** The headline, "Landing stage rebuild", is a label with no verb. The first paragraph lists amenities (benches, kiosk, tea and bread rolls). The real news, "The ramps now meet the deck at every tide, so prams and wheelchairs roll straight on", sits under the figure, where many readers won't get to it. This is **taste**, argued on the job: someone with a pram or wheelchair needs to see that first. Fix: lead with it, in the headline or the first sentence, and say it to the viewer, e.g. "Step-free boarding at every tide". The kiosk and benches can follow.

**Smaller things**
- Form labels ("Your name", "Home landing"), the helper text, the figure caption and the footer note are set in a light weight at roughly 12–13 px and look spindly. Use the regular weight at that size (convention: light weights are for large sizes).
- The blue rounded bar in the illustration is the same blue as the "Sign up" button, so a non-interactive shape looks like a control. Keep that blue for the action, or make the boat a different colour.
- The illustration is a schematic of boxes. It doesn't show the ramp or the tide the caption and text are about, so it adds no evidence. Show the ramp meeting the deck, or use a photo.
- The footer line ("Ferry timetables are a sample…") floats far below the content with a large empty band above it. Either pull it up or let it sit as a real footer with a divider.

**What's working**
- The body text is easy to read: about 18 px, comfortable line spacing, a measure of roughly 65–70 characters, ragged right. Keep all of that.
- The form follows good conventions: one column, labels above the fields, dark input borders, and one clear filled primary button. Fix the fields, not the structure.
- The two-column split is clear: article left, action right, with the card's top lining up near the headline. Nobody will confuse which part is which.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Focus and error states for the form fields and button, and how the card stacks on a narrow screen (it should come after the ramp news, not before it).
