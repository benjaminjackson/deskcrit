**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big dark box at the top, then finds a grey silhouette in it. It skips down to the bold headline, which does the real work: it's an author evening at a bookshop. It feels calm and tidy, but the top half reads as unfinished. The first thing you see is a stand-in for content, not content.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an independent bookshop's event page for regular customers and local readers, and its job is to get them to book a free seat.

**Verdict.** The text half works: a clear headline, a plain description and a single specific button. Two things let it down. The largest thing on the page is a generic avatar placeholder, and the details someone acts on (year, place, how many seats) are thin or missing.

**What would move it most**

1. **The placeholder takes the best spot on the page (defect).** The 600 × 330 px dark panel with a head-and-shoulders silhouette fills about 40% of the screen. It's the first thing the eye lands on, and it tells the reader nothing: not who Ines Varga is or what *The Salt Year* looks like. It also makes the page look unfinished, which costs trust just before you ask someone to commit an evening. The image and headline should be two halves of one idea; right now the image adds nothing to the headline.
   - **Fix:** use the book cover, the author's photo, or both. The cover is the stronger choice for a bookshop, because it's what readers will recognise on the shelf and on the night. If there's no image yet, drop the panel and let the headline lead. A blank page beats a stand-in.

2. **The date and logistics are incomplete (defect, and the one people act on).**
   - **No year.** "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" gives no year. In 2026, 13 November is a Friday; the pairing only works for 2025. Either this page is stale (the event has passed and the button still invites bookings) or the weekday is wrong. Either way, a reader may turn up on the wrong night or book a seat for an event that's over. Check the date, add the year, and if the event is past, swap the button for a "this event has passed" state.
   - **No venue.** There's no address or branch. "Find us" in the nav suggests location matters, but the reader has to leave the page to learn where to go.
   - **No end time or capacity.** "Book a free seat" implies limited seats but doesn't say how many.
   - **Fix:** add one short line under the description, or a compact details block next to the button: date with year, start and end times, the venue address, and "free, limited seats" if that's true.

**Smaller things**
- **Description measure:** the line runs about 77 characters, a little past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrow the text block slightly or set the description a step larger.
- **Headline break:** "on her / new novel" splits the phrase. Breaking after "Varga" reads more cleanly: "An evening with Ines Varga / on her new novel, *The Salt Year*".
- **Book title styling:** the title "The Salt Year" isn't set off from the rest of the headline. Italicise it so it reads as a title, not as words in the sentence.
- **Generic description:** "a short talk about the island where the book is set" could be on any author's page. Name the island; that one word gives a reader a reason to care.
- **Header alignment:** the "Margin Books" wordmark looks slightly higher than the nav links. Align them on one baseline or centre line.
- **Nav:** "Events" isn't marked as the current section, so the reader gets no "you are here" cue.

**What's working**
- **The headline:** it says who, what and which book in one line, at a size that clearly dominates the text.
- **The button:** "Book a free seat" names the exact action and removes the cost question in three words. It's the only filled element below the image, so there's no doubt what to do. Keep that wording.
- **Type and colour restraint:** there are four clear levels (small rust date, large bold headline, regular body, bold button label) in one family. The single warm accent on the date fits a bookshop and marks the date as the key fact.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- **Below the fold:** whether the page continues with the author, the book or the venue. If it ends at the button, the missing logistics above matter even more.
