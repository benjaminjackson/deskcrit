**First read.** The eye lands on the serif "Good morning, Dana", then drops to the paragraph, the two class rows and the dark "Book a class" button. It reads as a yoga studio's member home screen on a phone, and it feels calm and warm. The two versions differ in one way only: the paragraph under the greeting. Version 1 sets it in all capitals and version 2 in normal sentence case. In version 1 that capitalised block is the loudest thing below the headline, and it reads like a warning notice.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a member opens the app, checks what's left on their pass, and books today's class quickly.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The paragraph holds the rules a member acts on (six sessions left, valid until 30 November, book two hours ahead, cancel by 8 pm), and version 2 makes it easy to read. Version 1 makes it hard to read and gives it the tone of a warning. Neither version shows which class "Book a class" books, and that is the biggest thing left in the way.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **The paragraph in all capitals (version 1) vs sentence case (version 2). Defect in version 1.** Five lines of capitals in a regular weight lose the shapes of the words that readers rely on, so the dates, times and limits take longer to pick out. Bringhurst and Butterick both say to keep capitals for short labels and never set running text in them. All capitals also reads as shouting, which fights the calm, welcoming feel of the rest of the page. And the capitals take more room, pushing the class list and button about 25 px further down. Version 2 fixes all three.

**What would move it most (version 2)**
1. **The button doesn't say what it books. Defect.** "Book a class" sits under two specific classes but names neither, and the rows have no chevron or other sign that you can tap them. The member has to guess whether the button opens another list or books one of these two. Fix: put the action on each row, such as a "Book" button per class or rows that clearly look tappable. Or, if the button stays, have it name the next class ("Book Slow flow, 7:00 am").
2. **The cancellation rule can be read two ways, and it governs credits the member pays for. Defect.** "Cancel by 8 pm the night before to keep the session" could mean the member keeps their credit, or keeps their place in the class. Fix: say what happens to them: "Cancel by 8 pm the night before and the session goes back on your pass."

**Smaller things.**
- "4 spaces" / "9 spaces": write "4 spaces left" so it doesn't read as the class size.
- The class rows give no day. If these are today's classes, add "Today" above the list.
- The image block takes about a third of the first screen and carries no information. Shrink it or drop it on this screen so the classes sit higher.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: a serif headline, sans text, and bold class names over regular time and room details. That's about four sizes, each with a clear job.
- The greeting by name and the summary of the pass up front answer the member's first question before they ask it.
- One dark button on a pale background makes the main action obvious.
