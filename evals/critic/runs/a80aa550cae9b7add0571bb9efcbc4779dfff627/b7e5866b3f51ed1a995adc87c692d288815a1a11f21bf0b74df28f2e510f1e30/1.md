**What would move it most**
1. **The line spacing in all three body paragraphs is collapsed (defect).** The baselines look about 15 px apart for type that looks about 18–19 px, so line-height is under 1.0. In the crop, the descenders of one line touch or cross the letters below them: "landing" runs into "minutes", "lock" into "deck", "week" sits on top of "landing". The reader can't track from the end of one line to the start of the next, and every word takes extra effort to make out. For a passenger checking a departure time on their phone at the quay, that is the whole page failing. Bringhurst and Butterick both put comfortable body leading at roughly 120–145% of the type size. Set the body to about 1.4–1.5 line-height, then check in the live CSS whether a `line-height` value (a unitless 0.8, or a fixed px value smaller than the font size) is causing this.
2. **The facts a passenger acts on are buried in running prose (defect).** These are: every 40 minutes, early morning to late evening, about 12 minutes per crossing, hourly in winter, last boat before dusk, bikes free. Someone checking "when's the next boat?" has to read all three paragraphs to find the answer, and "before dusk" and "early morning to late evening" give no times they can plan around. If the real times vary, say where to find them ("see the timetable in the shelter").
