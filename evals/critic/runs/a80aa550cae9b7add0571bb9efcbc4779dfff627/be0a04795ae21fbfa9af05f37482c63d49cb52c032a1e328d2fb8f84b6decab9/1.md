**First read.** The eye goes to the big headline "Bring your bike in for a service", then across to the filled "Book drop-off" button, which is the only strong colour on the page. It reads at once as a local bike shop's booking page. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy. Both versions give the same first read.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this page gets a local cyclist to book a service drop-off on a specific day, and that the price list helps them decide.

**Verdict.** The two versions are the same in every way that matters. The only change is the button colour: teal in version 1, a stock bright blue in version 2. Both give white text strong contrast, and both make the button the clear end point of the page. So neither one books more bikes than the other. The teal looks a little less like a template, but that's taste and doesn't change how well the page works. The real problem is shared by both: the booking panel never asks which service you want.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form books a day but not a job.** On the right, the panel only asks for a drop-off day. The three services and prices on the left aren't connected to it. A customer who presses "Book drop-off" hasn't told the shop whether they want a $25 safety check or a $160 rebuild, and can't be sure what they've signed up for. That's a defect, because the price is the thing they'll later hold the shop to. Fix: make the three price rows selectable, or add a service choice inside the panel, so one booking covers both the day and the job.
2. **The day options don't look like choices.** The three date boxes look like plain text fields, and none is selected by default. Nothing shows that you pick one, or which one is picked, until you interact. That's a convention issue (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*: the control should show what it does). Fix: make them look like radio buttons or selectable tiles with a clear selected state, and preselect the earliest day or show a visible "pick one" state.
3. **"The quote" is never defined.** The line "We call you before doing any work that costs more than the quote" points to a quote the viewer hasn't received. They might take the listed price as the quote, or expect a separate estimate at the counter. That's a defect, because it's a promise about money. Fix: say which it is, for example "…costs more than the price you booked."

**Smaller things.**
- Thursday 30 October is missing from the dates with no explanation. Add a short note such as "Closed Thursdays" so nobody wonders if it's a mistake.
- The panel heading says "Choose a drop-off day" but repeats nothing from the left column. Once a service is chosen, show a one-line summary (service, price, day) above the button.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is tight: headline, subhead, body and labels are clearly separate levels with nothing colliding.
- The copy under the headline is concrete and speaks to the customer: before 10 am, same evening, and "we call you". Keep it.
- The page has exactly one filled button, so there's no question about what to do next. Keep that, whichever of the two colours you choose.
