**First read.** The bold sans headline lands first, then the eye drops into three narrow serif columns and gets caught on the holes between words: "minutes    between    early", "weather,    and    a    little", "bicycle        during        the". The texture reads as broken grey before it reads as text. It looks like a short explainer for ferry passengers, calm and plain, but the setting makes it look unfinished.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short informational article or web page telling ferry passengers how the service works: route, frequency, bikes, winter timetable.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound. The text setting lets it down, and that is the part the reader actually spends time with. The biggest problem is full justification on a measure far too short to justify, which tears every paragraph full of holes.

**What would move it most**
1. **Justified text on a very short measure (defect, also a convention).** All three columns are justified at roughly 28–32 characters per line, so the word spaces stretch to fill each line. The worst lines are "minutes between early", "weather, and a little", "longer when the tide" and "sandbank near the old" in column 1; "Riders wheel them on", "bicycle during the" and "stays clear for people" in column 2; and "In winter the timetable" and "each landing, and the" in column 3. The reader's eye jumps the gaps, the even grey of the column breaks up, and stacked gaps start to form short rivers (column 2, lines 8–11). Bringhurst warns that short justified lines produce "white acne". Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 34) says that below about 50 characters per line even word spacing becomes "difficult, if not impossible". To fix it, set the text ragged-right, which is the honest choice at this width. If justification has to stay, widen the measure to at least about 50 characters and turn hyphenation on.
2. **The measure is half what body text wants, and the page gives the space away (taste, backed by convention).** Three columns of about 30 characters sit at the top left of the frame. A strip about 260 px wide on the right and the whole lower 40% are empty. Bringhurst puts comfortable body text at 45–75 characters, so these short lines break every sentence into fragments. Three short paragraphs don't need three columns. Two wider columns, or one column at about 60–66 characters, would fix the line length, give the justification problem room to go away, and stop the block looking pushed into a corner.
3. **The headline promises more than the text delivers (defect in the copy).** "Through the seasons" sets the reader up for spring, summer, autumn and winter. Only winter appears, in the third column. The first two columns are about the route and bicycles, which have nothing to do with seasons. A reader looking for summer times or tide effects won't find them and will feel misled. Either retitle it to what's there (something like "How the river ferry works") or write the seasons in.

**Smaller things.**
- Column 1: "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening" gives no clock times. That's the line passengers will plan around and hold the operator to, so give the first and last departures.
- Column 3: "before dusk" is ambiguous in winter, because dusk moves by about an hour across the season. Give a time, or say it varies and point to the posted timetable.
- Column 1: the last line, "harbour wall.", sits alone with a visibly loose space. It's a minor runt.
- Columns end at three different depths. That's fine for running text, but with this much empty space below it makes the block look unfinished. Most of this goes away once the measure is fixed.

**What's working.**
- **Two-level type scale.** A bold sans headline over a serif text face, with no in-between sizes, gives each size one obvious job. Keep it.
- **Leading and grid.** The line spacing is comfortable, and the baselines appear to line up across all three columns, so the grid is doing its job. Keep that alignment when you change the column count.
- **Plain copy.** The text is concrete and passenger-centred ("lock them to the rail", "the noticeboard there lists any changes"). It says who does what.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the columns reflow at narrower widths. On a phone, a single justified column would get even worse word spacing, so check that ragged-right setting carries through there.
