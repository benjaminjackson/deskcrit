**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Changing your payment card", then runs down the three bold subheads like a table of contents. Within two seconds it reads as a help-centre article for someone who pays for a product. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy: there is nothing to fight through.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help article for an account owner who needs to swap the card they pay with, and to know what happens to their money and their account while they do it.

**Verdict.** The layout does its job. The weak point is the words. The article contradicts itself on the one question the reader came with (will my new card actually be charged?), and the payment-failure section leaves out the facts the reader has to act on. It reads as finished, but the reader can't be sure what to do.

**What would move it most**

1. **The article contradicts itself on which card gets charged** (defect). The intro says "The new card is used from your next invoice", which reads as automatic. Then "Remove an old card" says a card can only be removed "once another card is set as the default". That tells the reader adding a card doesn't make it the default, and nothing on the page says how to set the default. Someone who follows "Add a new card" and stops may still be charged on the old card, which could be expired, and that leads straight into the "If a payment fails" section. Fix: say whether a newly added card becomes the default. If it doesn't, add the step that makes it the default to "Add a new card" ("…then select Make default"), and change the intro to match.

2. **"If a payment fails" leaves the reader to work out the timeline and the consequences** (defect: this is a deadline and a penalty, so the reader will act on it and hold you to it).
   - "We try again after three days" can mean one retry after three days, or a retry every three days. Under the first reading the account locks after about six days; under the second, after about nine. Name the number of days.
   - "After a third failed try the account moves to read-only" has no comma after "try", so on first pass it reads "failed try the account". Put the comma back.
   - "Read-only" is a category, not a consequence. Say what the owner can no longer do, in their own terms (for example "you and your team can view projects but can't edit them").
   - The section ends on the penalty and gives no way out. Finish with what to do: "To get out of read-only, add a working card and we'll charge it straight away." Say whether that charge happens straight away.

3. **The subheads sit halfway between sections instead of with their own text** (convention). Above "Add a new card", "Remove an old card" and "If a payment fails", the gap to the previous paragraph looks about the same as the gap below to the paragraph the heading introduces, roughly 30 px each way. So each heading floats instead of starting its section. That costs a skimming reader a little, and this page is built for skimming. Bringhurst (*Elements*, 2.2.2) sets subheads with more space above than below. Fix: roughly double the space above each subhead and halve the space below it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Steps written as prose:** "Open Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card" and "Select the card, then Remove, and confirm" are click paths written as sentences. Set the UI labels in bold, or as a short numbered list, so the reader can follow along with the app open (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web").
- **Intro wording:** "Nothing already paid is moved" makes the reader reconstruct the meaning. Say it to them: "Invoices you've already paid stay on the old card."
- **The hold amount:** "a hold of one pound" is clearer as "a £1 hold". Readers look for amounts as figures.
- **Runt line:** "days." sits alone at the end of the hold paragraph. Trim a word or rebreak the line.
- **Line length:** the measure looks to be around 78–80 characters, slightly above the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrowing the column by about 40 px would fix it, and there's plenty of room on the right.
- **No way out at the end:** the page stops after the last paragraph. Add a "Still stuck? Contact billing support" line, or a link straight to the billing settings.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: a heavy title, bold subheads, regular body text and a grey breadcrumb. That's four clearly different levels with no sizes that nearly collide. Keep it.
- The subheads are written as tasks ("Add a new card", "Remove an old card", "If a payment fails"), so the reader finds their case without reading anything else. Keep that pattern for any new sections.
- The copy is short and talks to the reader ("your bank", "your next invoice"). The fixes above are about missing facts, not about tone.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Settings", "Billing" and the breadcrumb items are links, and whether they look like links on hover or focus. If the article points to a screen, linking it directly would remove the first step.
