**First read.** The eye goes straight to the hot red and orange patch in the middle of the grid, then to the large solid slab of blue-purple around it. Within a second this reads as "tickets bunch up in weekday working hours", which is correct. Then the eye has to keep going back to the legend, because the colours don't say which is more and which is less. The page feels clean and calm, but the chart looks loud.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal support-ops report. A team lead uses it to decide when to staff up, so what they act on is the busiest hours and how busy they get.

**Verdict.** The layout and labelling are better than most dashboards, but the colour scale works against the reader. Quiet hours get the heaviest ink, the order of the colours has to be decoded, and the chart seems to disagree with the "Busiest hour" figure beside it.

**What would move it most**

1. **The rainbow scale hides the order of the values (convention, and a defect here).** The seven colours across the grid and legend go blue-purple → sky → teal → green → yellow → orange → red. That order only makes sense after you read the legend. Nothing about the colours says sky blue is more than blue-purple, or that green is less than yellow. Lightness doesn't track the values either. Yellow (20–24) is the lightest colour, so the mid-busy band glows. The 0–4 blue-purple is dark and saturated and fills about two-thirds of the grid, so the quietest hours carry the most visual weight. Someone scanning for "when are we slammed" has to look past a wall of the least important data. Wilke's rule is that ordered values use one hue running light to dark. The fix: use one ramp from near-white for 0–4 to the darkest shade for 30+. The quiet night hours then fade back, and the busy shape stands out on its own.

2. **The chart and the "Busiest hour" figure seem to disagree (defect).** The hour labels sit centred over their cells, so the column under 09:00 is 9–10 and the 30+ red cells (Mon and Fri) are in the 11:00 column. The 10:00 column is orange (25–29) every weekday. A team lead sees red at 11 and reads "10:00" next to it. They'll either distrust one of the two or staff the wrong hour. The weekday average might really peak at 10, since the buckets are too coarse to show it. If so, the page has to show that. Mark the busiest column on the grid (an outline, or a tick that says "10:00 peak"), or put the actual value in the stat ("10:00, avg 28 tickets"). If the axis is off by an hour, fix the labels.

3. **Red and green carry meaning (convention, WCAG 1.4.1).** The 15–19 green sits right next to 20–24 yellow and 25–29 orange, and the 30+ red sits among the oranges. For readers with red-green colour blindness, green, orange and red lose most of their difference, so the peak cells stop standing out. The single ramp from point 1 fixes this too, because every step then differs in lightness, not just hue.

4. **"Up 4% on August" can be read two ways (defect, on a figure people quote).** The subtitle says the data covers 1 to 28 September, and the stat is labelled "New tickets, September". Is 3,412 four weeks compared with all 31 days of August, or the same 28 days? If it's the partial month against the full one, the real per-day rise is much larger. Say what's being compared, for example "Up 4% on 1–28 August".

**Smaller things.**
- The title "When new tickets arrive" names the topic. A takeaway title would do the reading for the viewer, e.g. "Tickets peak late morning on weekdays".
- Type scale: the H1 (~28 px) and the stat figures (~26 px) look nearly the same size, so the three numbers compete with the title. Set the figures clearly smaller, or keep the size and lighten the weight.
- "Median first reply" is about response, not arrival. It's a good number but off-topic beside an arrival chart. Separate it, or say why it's there.
- The active nav item "Reports" is marked by bold alone, which is faint at this size. Add an underline or a background.
- The 21:00–23:00 columns have only one label. That's fine, but end-of-day reading would be easier with a tick at 23:00 or 24:00.

**What's working.**
- The subtitle and source line spell out scope, time zone and what "hour" means ("opened, not answered"). That removes the questions a careful reader would ask, so keep this copy exactly.
- The plain grid with white gutters, no borders and no chartjunk, plus hour labels every 3 hours, gives a high data-ink ratio. Changing the colours will be enough; the structure doesn't need touching.
- Each KPI has a comparison ("Same as August", "Down 6 min"), so none of the figures is a bare number.
