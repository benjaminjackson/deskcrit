**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's a web page for riders who want to know when the next ferry leaves North landing and whether it runs on the day they're travelling.

**Verdict.** The craft is good, but the information isn't ordered for the question a rider brings to it. The day-of-week exceptions, which are what strand people, look exactly like "Daily". The first column repeats a label nobody looks things up by.

**What would move it most**

1. **The exceptions are hidden in plain sight.** Three of seven sailings don't run every day: 12:40 and 17:10 are weekdays only, and 19:30 is weekends only. In the Runs column, "Weekdays" and "Weekends" have the same weight, colour and position as "Daily". The two words also differ only in their last few letters, and they sit at the far right, about 850 px from the departure time. A Saturday rider scanning for "a ferry around 5" finds 17:10 and stops reading before reaching the last column. That's a **defect**: it misleads the very person the page exists for. Fix: make "every day" the quiet default and make the exceptions loud. Drop the word "Daily" and mark only the exceptions with a distinct, non-colour-only cue placed next to the time, such as a bold "Weekdays only". Or split the table into weekday and weekend timetables so each rider reads only the sailings that apply to them. The split is the stronger fix.

2. **The first column is the wrong key.** Period sits where the eye lands first, repeats in pairs, and tells the rider nothing the time doesn't already say. 17:10 labelled "Afternoon" is debatable anyway. People look up a timetable by departure time, but here the time is the second thing they read on each row. This is a **convention**: a table should lead with the column people search by, so put the key column first (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*, on scannable pages). Fix: lead with "Leaves North landing". If the periods earn their place, use them as small group headings between bands of rows rather than as a repeated cell. Otherwise drop them.

3. **"Spring season" has no dates.** It's the only scope this timetable has, and it can be read two ways: the calendar spring or the operator's season. A rider in late May or early April can't tell whether these times apply to them. A date range is something people act on and later hold the operator to, so this is a **defect**. Fix: state the dates, e.g. "North landing to Market quay · 21 March – 20 June".

**Smaller things.**
- The table is stretched to the full 1080 px content width, so each row has 250–300 px gaps between columns. The eye has a long way to travel from time to "Runs", and only the hairline rules keep it on the right row. Tighten the table to its content, roughly half the width (taste, but it serves item 1).
- "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are clipped labels. "north" in lower case reads as a direction, not a place. Use the place names as given in the subtitle: "Leaves North landing", "Arrives Market quay".
- Every crossing takes 12 minutes. One line saying so ("Crossing takes 12 minutes") would let you drop or lighten the Arrives column and make the table narrower.
- The figures look proportional: "11:00" and "11:12" sit slightly narrower than "06:40". Turn on tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) so the times line up in a true column (Bringhurst).
- There's no return direction. If it lives on another page, link to it from here. A rider at Market quay is the next person to open this page.

**What's working.**
- The restraint. There's one typeface, a clear three-level scale (bold title, grey subtitle, bold column headers over regular cells), no fills and no boxes. The header rule is only slightly heavier than the row rules, which is the right amount. Keep all of this when you add emphasis for the exceptions; one strong mark will stand out against this quiet base.
- 24-hour times with a leading zero (06:40) can't be misread as a.m./p.m. Keep them.
- The subtitle's grey looks like it clears 4.5:1 while still reading as secondary.
