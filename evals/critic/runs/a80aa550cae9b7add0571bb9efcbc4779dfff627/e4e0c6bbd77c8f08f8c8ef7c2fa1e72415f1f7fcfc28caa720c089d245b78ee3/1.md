**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the first screen of a local bakery's website. Its job is to make nearby people want the bread and tell them how to get it: where the shop is, when it's open, or how to order.

**Verdict.** The typography is good, but the screen doesn't do its job. It makes a claim and then gives the visitor nothing to do. The biggest problem is that there's no next step: no address, no hours, no order link.

**What would move it most**

1. **There's no ask.** Below the subhead (around y≈500) the screen just ends. A hungry visitor learns the bakery exists and is on Mill Street, but not when it's open, where on Mill Street it is, or whether they can order ahead. Then they have to scroll or leave to find out. *Defect:* it slows or loses the exact person the page is for (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the page should make the next step obvious). The fix is to put one specific action in the empty space under the subhead, based on what the visitor actually does: "Open today 7–2 · 14 Mill Street", or a button like "Order tomorrow's loaves". Then make it the one clear thing to do after reading the headline.

2. **The headline could be on any bakery's site, and the best line is buried under it.** "The finest bread in the valley" is a claim every bakery makes, and the visitor has to take it on trust. The subhead is where the real reasons are: baked every morning, a wood-fired oven, a named street, and 1962 in the eyebrow line. Those are concrete and believable. *Defect (copy):* the headline gives the visitor no reason to pick this bakery over the one they already use. The fix is to lead with the concrete thing a local can check for themselves (fresh every morning, wood-fired, since 1962) and let the claim of being the best rest on that.

3. **The empty lower half reads as missing, not as a choice.** The space under the subhead is just what's left over. Nothing sits in it, it pushes against nothing, and the text block hangs in the upper left. There is also no picture of bread on a bakery page, where the product sells itself by sight. *Taste, argued:* white space works when it sets something off (Ruder's tension between printed and unprinted areas). Here it makes the page look like it hasn't finished loading. The fix is to fill it with the ask from point 1 and, ideally, one strong photo of the loaves. Or, if you want the stark look, move the text block down so the space reads as intended.

**Smaller things.**
- "valley" sits alone on the second line of the headline, a one-word runt. Rebreak it ("The finest bread / in the valley") or tighten the wording so the two lines are closer in length.
- The eyebrow uses a straight apostrophe in "HARTLEY'S". Use a curly one (’).
- At display size the headline's word spacing looks a little loose. Try slightly tighter word spacing.

**What's working.**
- **A clear, short type scale.** I count three sizes: a small tracked-caps eyebrow, body at about 20 px, and a very large headline. The steps between them are big, so each size has an obvious job, and the brown eyebrow sets itself apart by colour rather than by adding another size. Keep this.
