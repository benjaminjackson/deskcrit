**First read.** My eye lands on the bold question, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", which reads well. Then it hits a row of four identical solid-blue buttons and stalls. Nothing tells me which one is the answer, so I have to read all four labels left to right, and "Save changes" comes last. The dialog itself looks clean and calm, but the decision row feels flat and a bit risky.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a confirmation dialog in a calendar app. It asks someone who has edited a shared choir calendar to commit the edits, which will reach everyone who follows it, and it offers ways out.

**Verdict.** The question and the explanation do their job. The button row does not. Four buttons with the same weight make a quick yes/no into a reading task, and the irreversible "Discard changes" looks exactly like the safe options. That row is the one thing to fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **All four actions have the same weight (defect).** The row under the body text has four buttons with the same fill, size and label weight. The person can't see which one answers the question, so they have to read every label. The likely answer, "Save changes", sits last, and the eye reaches it only after scanning three other options. One primary action per view should be filled and high-contrast. Secondary actions should be outlined or text, and cancel or tertiary actions should be the weakest (Material Design 3, "Buttons"; Apple HIG, "Buttons"; NN/g). The fix: keep "Save changes" as the only filled button. Make "Keep editing" an outlined or text button, since it's the cancel. Push "Export as file" down to a text link, or move it out of the row entirely.
2. **"Discard changes" looks as safe as "Save changes" (defect).** It is second from the left and styled the same as the buttons next to it. One mis-click, or a guess about which button is the default, throws away the person's edits to the rehearsal times. Destructive actions should be set apart from safe ones and styled as destructive (often red). They should not be the default focus, and they should come with a confirmation or undo (Apple HIG, "Alerts"; Material Design, "Dialogs"; Raskin, "Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo"). The fix: give "Discard changes" a destructive style, either red text or a red outline but not a filled button, and separate it from the safe actions. The usual layout puts it on the far left, with "Keep editing" and "Save changes" grouped on the right.
3. **"Export as file" doesn't belong in this decision (taste, with a convention behind it).** The dialog asks a question with three possible answers: save, discard, or go back. Export doesn't answer it. Does exporting save? Does it close the dialog? Putting it here adds a fourth choice at the moment someone is trying to commit. Progressive disclosure points the same way: rare options should come after the main decision. The fix: take it out of this dialog and put it in the editor's menu. If it has to stay, make it a quiet link that is visibly separate from the answer buttons.

**Smaller things.**
- Button order: the buttons have no grouping or spacing that separates destructive, cancel and confirm. Fixing items 1 and 2 solves this.
- Body copy: "will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar" is good, because it tells the person the consequence. A more concrete version would help more, such as saying how many followers there are or whether they get a notification. Even so, it's the strongest line in the dialog.
- The button row starts slightly left of the heading and body text. The buttons sit at about x=276 while the text starts at about x=280. Align their left edges.

**What's working.**
- The heading is a real question, and the labels answer it with verbs plus objects ("Save changes", "Discard changes"), not "OK" and "Cancel". Keep that.
- The body line shows the person what the save means for them: other people will see the new rehearsal times. It explains why the choice matters in one sentence.
- The type scale is tight, with three sizes, each doing a clear job. White space and the soft card edge make the dialog easy to read at a glance.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Which button gets the default keyboard focus, and what Enter and Escape do. With "Discard changes" in the row, the default focus must not be on it. Check this in the live build.
