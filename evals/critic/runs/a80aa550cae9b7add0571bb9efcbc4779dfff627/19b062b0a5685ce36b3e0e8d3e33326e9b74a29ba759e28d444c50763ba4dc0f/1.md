**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif headline, "Winter timetable at the harbour", then drops into two even grey blocks of prose that look the same. Nothing in either column stops the eye: there are no times, figures or labels to catch on. Then it reaches "Fares and cards", a heading with nothing under it, and a matching rule on the right with no heading at all. It reads as a calm, well-set information page for a small ferry, more like an essay than a timetable.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page for ferry passengers who want to know when boats leave, what to expect and what they can bring. Most will be checking it on the way, not reading it at leisure.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is careful, but the page doesn't do its job: it's called a timetable and contains no times. The facts a passenger needs are spelled out in words and buried in running prose, so the one question everyone arrives with, "when is the next boat?", can't be answered.

**What would move it most**

1. **The timetable has no departure times** (whole page, mainly the second paragraph on the left). The page says boats leave "every ninety minutes" and that "the first crossing is usually the quietest", but never says when the first boat leaves or when the last one does. A passenger can't plan a trip from this. The opening hour of the office (seven) is the only clock time on the page, and it isn't a sailing. "An hour later than in summer" only helps someone who knows the summer hours. **Defect.** Fix: put a real departure list at the top, straight under the headline: first sailing, last sailing and each time between, in both directions. Make that block the thing the eye lands on. Everything else on the page supports it.

2. **Facts people scan for are set as continuous prose in two equal columns** (both columns). Opening hour, how often boats run, crossing time (20 minutes, or about 35 when rough), bicycles, prams and wheelchairs, dogs: these are separate facts people look up one at a time. Here they sit inside sentences, with no headings in either column, so a reader looking for "can I bring my bike?" has to read down the left column and then the right. The split between columns is also arbitrary: the left has the office, boats and waiting room, the right has crossings, bicycles and dogs, and neither column is labelled. Text people skim should be broken up by headings, short paragraphs and lists (checklist: basics; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). **Defect.** Fix: give each topic a short bold label ("Departures", "Crossing time", "Bikes, prams and wheelchairs", "Dogs", "Waiting room"), keep each to one or two lines, and group the travel facts ahead of the comfort details.

3. **Numbers are spelled out** ("seven in the morning", "ninety minutes", "twenty minutes", "thirty-five"). In a document whose whole job is times and durations, numbers written as words don't stand out from the text around them, so the eye can't find them by scanning. Spelling numbers out suits literary prose. Here it hides exactly what the reader is looking for. **Convention / defect.** Fix: use numerals throughout ("07:00", "every 90 min", "20 min, about 35 in rough weather"), ideally in tabular figures in the departure list.

4. **Cancellations are only posted at the gate** (left column, second paragraph). "Notices are pinned to the board beside the gate" means a passenger finds out a sailing is cancelled only once they've already travelled to the harbour. On a page that already sits online, this is the most costly piece of information to leave out. **Defect (content).** Fix: say where to check before leaving home (this page, a phone number, a text alert), and put it next to the departure times.

**Smaller things.**
- Single-word last lines: "bags." and "thirty-five." each sit alone on the last line of their paragraph. Rewrap or edit slightly.
- The headline doesn't say which harbour or which island. Name the route ("Winter ferry times: [Harbour] – [Island]") so the page can't be mistaken for another one in search results or a shared link.
- "Dogs on a lead are welcome on every sailing." stands alone as a one-line paragraph, so it gets the same weight as the timing paragraphs. This will sort itself out once there are labels.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three sizes (headline about 40 px, section head about 24 px, body about 19 px) with clear steps and bold weight doing the work. Keep this scale when you add the departure list and labels.
- The line length (about 55–60 characters) and generous leading make the body comfortable to read. Lines and paragraph gaps in the two columns sit on the same baselines, which shows care. Keep both.
- The tone is warm and specific (the stove, the paperbacks, the crew helping with bags). That suits a small local ferry and is worth keeping as the secondary layer once the facts lead.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The "Fares and cards" section is empty under its rule, and the right-hand rule has no heading. This may be the capture cutting off or content that hadn't loaded, not the design. Check in a live browser. If it really is empty, that's a defect: fares are something passengers act on.
