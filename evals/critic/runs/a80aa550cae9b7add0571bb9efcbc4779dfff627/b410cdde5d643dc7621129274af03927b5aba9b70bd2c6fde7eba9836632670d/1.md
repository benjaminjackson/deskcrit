**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the big dark headline "Plan a week of dinners in ten minutes", drops through two short paragraphs and three plain lines, and stops on the green "Plan my first week" button. It reads as a meal-planning product for busy home cooks, and it feels calm and plain. The only change is alignment: version 1 sets the whole block flush left, and version 2 centres it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page for a weekly meal-planning service, meant to get a home cook to start a first plan.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The copy is the same in both, and it carries the page. Centring the 2–3 line paragraphs and the three feature lines in version 2 makes them slower to read and makes the features look less like a list. Version 1's main weakness is the empty right half of the screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **Centred running text (version 2): defect, small but real.** The two body paragraphs are three and two lines long. When they are centred, every line starts at a different point, so the eye has to hunt for the start of each one. Ragged-left setting slows reading of anything longer than a line or two (Bringhurst; Butterick). The three feature lines ("Recipes tested in a home kitchen…") are also hurt. Left-aligned, they share one edge and read as a list. Centred, they look like three loose captions. Version 1 keeps one strong left edge from the logo down to the button, so the page scans in one straight line. Fix: keep the left alignment. If you want a centred hero, centre only the headline and the button, and cut the body copy to one line.
2. **Empty right half (version 1): taste.** The text column stops at about x=770, and the right side of the screen holds nothing. This is the one thing version 2's centring was fixing. The page doesn't fail because of it, but the empty half makes the page look unfinished, and it wastes the best place to show what the product makes. Fix: put a picture of the output there, such as a sample week of dinners with its shopping list sorted by aisle. That shows the promise instead of only stating it.
3. **The headline breaks onto a one-word second line (both): convention.** "minutes" sits alone on line two, which gives the headline a lopsided shape and weakens the payoff. Fix: break it as "Plan a week of dinners / in ten minutes", or set the measure so that it breaks there.

**Smaller things.**
- The three feature lines look identical to the body text and have no bullets or marks. In version 1 they still read as a list, but a little weight or a small mark would help.
- The nav is a single run of text with middle dots ("How it works · Recipes · …"), so the links don't look like separate things to click. Space them apart, and make "Sign in" look different from the rest.
- About 200 px of empty space sits below the button with no hint that the page continues.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and puts the viewer first: "who you cook for, what you will not eat", "less food goes to waste by Friday", "sorted by the aisles of your usual shop". Each line says what the viewer gets, not just what the product does. Keep it.
- "Plan my first week" names one exact thing to do and is the only strongly coloured element, so the next step is never in doubt.
- The type has three clear levels (headline, body, button) with big jumps between them. Nothing is crowded.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the layouts reflow on mobile. On a narrow screen both versions would probably end up stacked, so the alignment difference matters mostly on desktop.
