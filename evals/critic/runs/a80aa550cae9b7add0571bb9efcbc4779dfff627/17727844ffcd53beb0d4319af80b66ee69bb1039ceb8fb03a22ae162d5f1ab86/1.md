**First read.** The eye goes first to the serif "How can we help?" on the navy band, drops to the search field, and then jumps straight to two cards: Billing and Privacy. Their icons are the only coloured objects on a white-and-navy page, and the shield is green on top of that. It reads as a calm, competent SaaS help centre, until those two icons make the grid look as if it was put together from two different icon packs.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the help-centre home page for a workspace product. It should get a customer who is stuck to the right topic or article fast, mainly through search or one of the eight topic cards.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The structure is clear, the search comes first, and the topic labels are good. The one thing that clearly hurts it is the mismatched icons. They add emphasis nobody chose: Billing and Privacy look more important than the other six topics, and the page looks less finished than the rest of the craft deserves.

**What would move it most**
1. **Two icons break the set: Billing and invoices (row 1, card 3) and Privacy and security (row 2, card 1).** Six cards use thin, one-colour outline icons in the navy ink. These two are filled, multi-coloured illustrations: a blue card with an orange chip and a green shaded shield. Because the rest of the page is so restrained, they are the brightest things below the hero. The eye goes to them first and reads them as featured or urgent topics, which they aren't, and the grid stops reading as one family. Apple's HIG ("Icons") asks for one consistent size, level of detail and stroke weight across interface icons, so this breaks a stated convention. Here it costs something: it creates a false hierarchy among topics that should rank equally. *Convention, with a defect's consequence.* Fix: redraw or replace both as plain outline glyphs, with the same stroke weight and navy colour as the other six.

**Smaller things.**
- Search field (hero): there's no magnifier icon or submit button, so it looks a bit like a plain text box. Add the standard search icon inside the field (Jakob's law / Nielsen's "Consistency and standards").
- Topic mix: "Orders and shipping" (track a delivery, start a return) sits oddly next to workspace, integrations and notifications. If the product really does both, fine. If not, it reads like leftover template content and will confuse people looking for it.
- "Privacy and security: How we protect and store your data" is the only description written from the company's side. The others say what *you* can do. Something like "Control who sees your data and how it's stored" would match them.

**What's working.**
- The hero hierarchy is right. One large serif question, one short line, then the search field as the obvious first action. Nothing competes with it except the two icons above.
- The card copy is specific and uses the customer's words ("Track a delivery or start a return", "Fix sync problems and error messages"), so people can match their problem to a card without guessing. Keep that voice.
- The type scale is tight: about four sizes (hero heading, section label/card title, body, nav) with clear jumps between them. The 4×2 grid keeps even gutters and lines up with the header margins.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards look clickable on hover and focus, and whether search shows suggestions as you type. On a page like this, both matter. Check them live.
