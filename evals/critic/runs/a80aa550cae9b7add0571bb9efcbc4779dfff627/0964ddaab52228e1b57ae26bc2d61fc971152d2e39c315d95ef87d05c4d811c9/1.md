**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", a heavy black headline in the top left. It drops to a small grey smudge below it and then has nowhere to go, because roughly 85% of the frame is empty white. It reads as a page that hasn't finished loading or was never built. It feels empty, and it doesn't look trustworthy.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a public information page for ferry passengers who want to know when the next boat leaves and how the service runs.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The body text is far too small to read, and the page promises a timetable but shows no times. A passenger leaves knowing nothing they didn't know when they arrived.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text can't be read.** The three short paragraphs under the headline (about x 120–220, y 140–195) are set at roughly 4–5 px, about 100 px wide, in pale grey. Even at full resolution I could only pick out fragments: the ferry runs from the north landing to the market quay about every forty minutes, a crossing takes about twelve minutes, bikes go on the lower deck, and winter service drops to hourly with the last boat before dusk. That is the content a passenger needs, and they can't get at it. **Defect.** Readable body text on screen sits around 16 px, and the paragraphs need room for a line of 45–75 characters (Bringhurst); each line here has about 40 characters squeezed into 100 px. The grey also looks likely to fall below WCAG 1.4.3's 4.5:1 at this size; check it with a contrast tool. Fix: set the body at normal reading size and dark enough to pass, in a column about 60–70 characters wide.

2. **The headline promises a timetable that isn't there.** "Timetable" tells the viewer to expect departure times, but there's no table, list, or "next boat" anywhere on the page. The only schedule information is buried in prose ("every forty minutes", "one boat each hour" in winter), so the viewer has to work out the times themselves. **Defect.** It misleads the reader and slows down the one task they came for. Fix: make the departure times the main thing on the page. Show a table of times for each landing, with summer and winter as separate columns or tabs, or at least first boat, frequency and last boat for each season. Move the prose under it as supporting notes.

3. **The type scale has two levels, and they're far too far apart.** The headline looks to be about 40 px bold, the body about 5 px, and there's nothing in between. The page needs a middle size, such as section labels ("Summer", "Winter", "Bicycles"), and a body size readers can actually read. **Defect** here, not just taste, because the gap is the reason the hierarchy has collapsed. Fix: use three or four sizes with clear steps between them: headline, section heading, body, and one small size for notes (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).

4. **The layout uses none of the space.** Everything sits in the top-left eighth of the frame, and the rest is blank. This isn't generous white space. It's unused space, so the page looks broken or still loading. **Taste/common sense:** once items 1 and 2 are fixed, the timetable will fill the frame. Plan the grid around the table rather than around the headline.

**Smaller things.**
- Some phrases in the copy are vague. If I've read them right, "a little longer when the tide turns" and "before dusk" are guesses for a passenger planning a trip. Give clock times, and say how long a tide delay usually is.
- Nothing tells the reader that the times or service could change, or where to check for that. The prose seems to mention printed timetables at each landing, which doesn't help someone reading online. Add a "last updated" date and a place to find disruptions.
- If I've read it right, the bicycle rule (lower deck, wheel bikes on after foot passengers) is a rule passengers are expected to follow. It should be a short labelled note, not a line hidden in the second paragraph.

**What's working.**
- The headline is clear, plain and well weighted. It tells the reader what the page is in three words, and the left margin of about 120 px gives a sensible edge to line everything else up on.
- The prose seems to cover the right things (route, frequency, crossing time, bikes, winter service). The information is there; it just needs structure and a readable size.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The tiny text and blank page might be a capture problem, such as a page grabbed mid-load or zoomed out, rather than the design. Check in a live browser at 100% zoom whether the body really renders at this size and whether a timetable loads below it. If the table loads, item 2 changes, but items 1 and 3 still stand.
