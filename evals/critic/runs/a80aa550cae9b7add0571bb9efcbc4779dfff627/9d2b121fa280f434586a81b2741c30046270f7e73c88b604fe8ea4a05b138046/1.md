**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the homepage of a local garden centre. Its job is to get a nearby visitor to come in, book a workshop or order plants for delivery.

**Verdict.** The page is clear and calm, but it doesn't do its job yet. It makes a specific offer, shade plants ready this weekend, and then gives the visitor no way to act on it. There's no button and no link in the content, so interest has nowhere to go.

**What would move it most**

1. **There is no next step anywhere on the page.** The hero, the line below it and the three cards are all plain text. Only the small grey nav links (Plants, Tools, Workshops, Visit) at top right can be clicked. A visitor who wants shade plants has to guess that "Plants" leads to them, and the headline's promise goes nowhere. *Defect*: it slows the people most likely to buy (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; Material and HIG on having one primary action per view). Add one filled button under the "Ferns, hostas…" line that names the exact thing, such as "See shade plants in stock". Give each card a plain text link with its own verb: "Book a workshop", "Get directions", "Order for delivery". Keep the button as the only strong one.

2. **The workshop card states a fact and stops.** "Repotting, pruning and balcony herb beds, ten places in each class." This is the one line that should get someone to sign up, but it gives no date, time or price and no way to book. The "ten places" detail only matters to the reader if it tells them something, for example that classes fill up. *Defect* in the copy. Say when the classes are, what they cost and how to get a place, and end on what it means for the visitor ("ten places, so book ahead"). The opening-hours and delivery cards already do this well, so this card stands out as the weak one.

3. **The headline breaks the left edge by a few pixels.** The logo, the "Ferns, hostas…" line and the cards all line up at about x=56. The headline starts at about x=72. The gap is too small to look intended, so it looks like a mistake right where the eye lands first (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*: a placement that is almost aligned makes the eye waver). *Convention*, and here it costs something because the headline is the first thing anyone sees. Put the headline on the same left edge as everything else.

4. **The headline and its supporting line are split up, and the hero is mostly empty space.** The leaf band is about 400 px tall and holds only the headline. The line that makes the offer real ("grown at our nursery and ready to pot this weekend") sits below the band in grey on white, so it reads as part of the card area rather than finishing the headline. Meanwhile the bottom ~120 px of the screen is empty. *Taste*, but it matters: the strongest selling line has been demoted. Either put the supporting line (and the new button) inside the hero under the headline, or make the band shorter so the cards and the action move up. Also consider putting real plants in the hero. The abstract leaf pattern says "green" but doesn't show any fern or hosta the visitor could buy.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline over the pattern: white bold on dark green holds up, but the lighter olive leaves and grey dots behind "small" and "spaces" lower the contrast in places. Check the lightest point behind the text, or darken the pattern behind the text area.
- The headline wraps to leave "spaces" alone on the second line. Widen the text box or break after "shade" so the two lines are better balanced.
- The type scale has one level too many: the supporting line (~20 px) and the card titles (~17 px) are too close in size to read as different levels. Make the supporting line clearly larger, or set it the same size as the body and let weight do the work.
- The nav links look faint and small (grey, ~15 px) for the site's only clickable items. Darken them.
- The card borders look very pale (light grey-blue on white). That's fine for decoration, but once the cards become clickable, give them a clearer edge or put the action in a visible link.
- The opening-hours card breaks "until 8 / pm" across lines. Keep "8 pm" together with a non-breaking space.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and local: "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, grown at our nursery", "Free within ten miles on orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box". This is far better than generic garden-centre copy, so keep this voice.
- The three cards answer the visitor's real questions (when are you open, what can I learn, will you deliver) in one clear sentence each, with consistent styling and spacing.
- The serif logo against the sans-serif text is a clear contrast, not a clash. Together with the restrained green, it gives the brand a quiet, trustworthy feel that suits a nursery.
