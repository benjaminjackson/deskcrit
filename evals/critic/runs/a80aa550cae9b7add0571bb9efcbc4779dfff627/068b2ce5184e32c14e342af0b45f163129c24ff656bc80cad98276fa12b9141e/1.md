**First read.** The eye goes to the big title and then straight down to the red Rose arch tile, which is the largest and warmest thing on the page. From there it moves right and down through five smaller tiles of equal size. It reads as a photo gallery from a garden show, and it feels tidy and cheerful but impersonal. Then the eye reaches the grey line at the bottom, "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size", and stops, because it reads like a note someone forgot to delete.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web page where visitors browse highlights from a garden show, with one featured exhibit and the rest as supporting tiles.

**Verdict.** The structure works: the page has one clear lead and an orderly grid. Two things hold it back: a stray internal note printed on the page, and a heading that doesn't say which show this is.

**What would move it most**
1. **The review line at the bottom has to go.** *Defect.* The line at the bottom left is aimed at the design team, not visitors, and it isn't even true: Rose arch is twice as wide and about twice as tall as the other tiles. A visitor who reads it learns nothing about the garden and wonders whether the page is finished. Delete it. If the page needs a closing line, use it for something a visitor can act on, such as show dates or a link to the full programme.
2. **The heading could be anyone's page.** *Defect, about the words.* "Garden show gallery" doesn't say which show, where, or what year. A visitor arriving from search or a shared link can't tell if this is the show they went to or are thinking of going to. Name it, for example "[Show name] 2026: highlights from the east garden", or add a one-line subtitle with the place and dates. The tile captions already show how: "Climbing roses over the east gate" and "Tomatoes in late July" are concrete and specific to this place. Bring the heading up to that level.

**Smaller things.**
- **Captions are closer to the tile below than they should be.** Each small caption sits about 10 px under its own image, and the next tile starts only about 15 px below the caption (for example, Herb bed's caption and the Pond edge image). Widen the gap between rows so each caption clearly belongs to the image above it.
- **The feature image's bottom edge lines up with nothing.** The Rose arch image ends at about y=437, partway along the Pond edge image (which ends at about y=474). Make the feature image end on the same line as a right-column image, or have the feature caption fill exactly the space down to the next row.
- **The small caption text looks small and grey.** "Thyme, sage and chives." and the other descriptions look like about 13 px in mid-grey. Check that they reach 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.3), and consider one step larger.
- **Six full-strength colours with no system.** Each tile has its own saturated colour, so the set reads as a rainbow, and nothing ties the colours to anything. This is taste, and it only matters if the colour fields stay in the final version (see below).

**What's working.**
- **The feature-plus-grid layout.** Rose arch spans two of the three equal columns, and its title is roughly three times the size of the tile titles. The page has one obvious entry point and a clear order after it. Keep that contrast in scale.
- **One grid throughout.** Three equal columns, a single gutter of about 14 px, and 60 px margins on both sides. Every edge comes from the same lines, which is why the page looks calm.
- **The caption copy.** Each caption is a short, concrete line about this particular garden, never generic filler. Keep writing them that way.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The images look like placeholders.** Each is a flat colour field with a pale circle cut off by the bottom edge. I haven't judged their cropping or colour as final artwork. Once real photos go in, check that they share one crop style and colour treatment, and that the Rose arch photo is strong enough to earn its size.
- **Whether tiles open or enlarge.** Nothing on the page shows that the tiles can be clicked. If they can, check the hover and focus states and the size of the click target in a live browser.
