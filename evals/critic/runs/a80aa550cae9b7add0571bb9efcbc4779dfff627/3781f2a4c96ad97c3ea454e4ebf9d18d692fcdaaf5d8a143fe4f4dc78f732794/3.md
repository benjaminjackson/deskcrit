Within two seconds it reads as a contact page for a small design or dev studio. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also a little plain: it could be any studio's contact page.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the contact or enquiry page of a small studio's site, meant to get prospective clients to send a useful first brief.

**Verdict.** It works. The hierarchy is clear, the form is easy to fill in, and there's one obvious action. The biggest thing holding it back is that the copy asks for a budget and a timeline, but the form gives people nowhere to put them. Most messages will arrive without them, and each of those costs you an extra round of emails.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form doesn't collect what the copy asks for.** The paragraph on the left says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The form on the right has only one short Message box, about five lines tall, with no prompt. People read that paragraph once and then look at the form, which says nothing about budget or timeline, so many will forget. The ones who remember are cramming a whole brief into a box that looks like it wants a sentence or two. This is a **defect**: it slows down the exact outcome the page is for, a brief you can reply to. Fix it by putting the ask where people type. Either add a line of helper text under the "Message" label (not a placeholder, which disappears as soon as they type), or add two short optional fields, Budget (a select with ranges) and Timeline, marked "optional". Also make the Message box taller so it invites a real description.
2. **The form card sits off the page grid.** The nav runs from the left margin at about 56 px to the right margin at about 1224 px. The card stops at about 1068 px, which leaves a dead strip about 160 px wide on the right that nothing explains. The left column lines up with the logo, but nothing on the right lines up with "Contact". The page ends up weighted to the left, and the card looks parked rather than placed. This is a **convention** (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one grid sets every edge on the page), and here breaking it costs you, because it's the only thing that makes this otherwise clean page look unfinished. Fix: put both columns on the same column lines as the header. Either run the card out to the right margin, or set the two columns as a deliberate ratio such as 5/7 across the full width.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks exactly like the other links, so nothing shows you're on that page (Nielsen heuristic 1). Mark it with weight, colour, or no underline.
- No fields are marked required or optional. If Name and Email are both required, say so, or mark only the optional ones.
- Input borders: the grey on white looks close to the 3:1 minimum for component edges (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure it, and darken the border if it falls short.
- Some people won't use a form. Add a plain email address under the paragraph on the left.
- "Tell us about your project" could sit on any agency's site. This is **taste**, but a line that names the kind of work you take on would tell a stranger they're in the right place.
- Type sizes: the field labels (about 14 px bold), nav links (about 15 px) and body text (about 16 px) are only a pixel or two apart. They separate fine by weight and colour, but you could drop one size.

**What's working.**
- The promise "We read every message and reply within two working days" is specific and something you can be held to. It's the best line on the page, so keep it.
- The form is done right: labels sit above the fields and stay visible, it's one column, and the single filled button is clearly the primary action with no competing buttons.
- The restraint suits the job. Grey page, one white card and one blue accent used only for links and the button, so the eye has exactly one place to act.
