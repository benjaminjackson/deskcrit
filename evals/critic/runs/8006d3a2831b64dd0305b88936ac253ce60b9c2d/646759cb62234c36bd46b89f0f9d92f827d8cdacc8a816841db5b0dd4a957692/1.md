**First read.** You see the big headline at top left first, then the subline, then the filled "Get started" button. After that there's nothing else to look at. The right half of the frame and the bottom third are blank cream. It reads as a group trip planner. It feels calm and tidy, but sparse enough to look unfinished, like a template whose hero image never got added.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero of a marketing homepage for a consumer group-trip planner. Its job is to get a first-time visitor, likely the friend organising a group trip, to understand the product and start a trip.

**Verdict.** The type and button hierarchy are clean and correct, but the screen doesn't sell anything. It says what Wayfold does without showing it, and the copy could belong to any trip planner, so the visitor has nothing to believe and no clear reason to click. The biggest problem is the empty right half where proof of the product should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no picture of the product, and the right half is blank.** About 560 px of the right side and everything below the buttons is empty. Nothing shows flights, stays and day plans sitting together, or a group agreeing on them. The visitor has to take the headline on trust. Because all the weight sits top-left with nothing to balance it, the page looks unfinished rather than calm (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). The space is left over, not shaped to work against the text (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). *Defect* (the visitor can't see the value) *and convention* (balance). **Fix:** put one concrete product view in the right column, aligned to the headline's top. A good choice is a real-looking shared itinerary for a named trip, such as "Lisbon, 4 friends, 12–16 May", showing a flight, a stay and a vote on a day plan. If an image was meant to be here and didn't load, check it in a live browser. Even then, the bottom third still needs work, or the hero should be shorter.
2. **The words could be anyone's, and the ask is vague.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" is the default line for every travel planner, and "in one place" is a claim, not a benefit. The subline holds the real idea: "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads". But it describes a group in the third person and buries that idea in grey 20 px text. "Get started" doesn't say what happens: create an account? Start a trip? Invite friends? *Defect* (the visitor can't tell what's different here or what clicking commits them to). **Fix:** promote the group pain into the headline and say it to the visitor, for example "Stop planning your group trip in five chat threads". Make the button name the action, such as "Start a trip and invite your group". The strongest version compares Wayfold with what they already use, the group chat, so they can judge the gain themselves.
3. **There's no way to sign in.** The nav offers Destinations, How it works, Pricing and Help, but no "Log in". Returning users expect it at top right, and this is exactly the product people come back to mid-trip. Without it they have to guess, and they may click "Get started" and end up signing up again (Nielsen, Jakob's law: use the patterns users already know). *Convention, and it costs something here.* **Fix:** add "Log in" as the last nav item, styled lower than the primary button.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place", which splits the phrase "in one place". Rebreak it after "trip", or let the new headline find a natural break.

**What's working.**
- There's one clear entry point and a clean button ranking. The 64 px bold headline dominates, and there's exactly one filled primary button with an outlined secondary, "See pricing", that does a different job. Keep this ranking when you add the image.
- The left edge is strict and the scale is tight. The logo, headline, subline and buttons all hang from the same x = 80 line. The scale has about four sizes with big steps between them: about 64 px headline, 20 px subline, 17 px bold buttons and 15 px nav.
- The colours are restrained. One deep teal on warm cream with dark grey text gives a calm, trustworthy tone that fits "without the chaos".

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank right column is a hero image or animation that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check it in a live browser.
- Focus states for the nav and buttons, and how the two-column layout reflows on a phone.

What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (white space gathered; weight balances)
Text: 30 lines, 29 yes, 1 no (headings break at phrase boundaries)
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 12 yes
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
