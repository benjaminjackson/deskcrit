**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big headline, "Plan the whole trip in one place", then drops to the teal "Get started" button, and stops there. The right half of the screen and the bottom third are empty cream. It reads as a clean, calm, very standard SaaS hero for a trip planner. It feels tidy and competent, but it could belong to any travel app, and the empty side makes it feel half-built.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home-page hero of a group trip-planning web app. Its job is to make a first-time visitor (someone organising a trip with friends or family) understand the product and start using it.

**Verdict.** The craft is fine and the order of importance is clear, but the hero doesn't make its case. The one thing that's different about Wayfold (getting a group to agree without a dozen chat threads) is buried in the grey subhead, while the headline and both buttons are generic, and two of those buttons ask for the same thing. Above the fold, the visitor gets a slogan, a void, and two ways to say "start".

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline sells the category, not the product.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" could be the headline of any trip planner. A visitor can't tell what's different here, so nothing pulls them in. The real pitch is in the subhead: "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads". It names the viewer's actual pain in their own terms (the group chat), and it's set small and grey. *Defect (copy).* Fix: put the group-agreement idea into the headline, aimed at the viewer, so the first big line names the problem they already have, e.g. something about getting everyone to agree on one plan. The subhead then explains how (flights, stays, day plans together).

2. **"Get started" and "Start now" are the same button twice** (the two buttons under the subhead). The visitor reads two choices, looks for the difference, and finds none. The outlined button weakens the filled one instead of backing it up, and it looks like a placeholder nobody filled in. *Defect.* (Nielsen heuristic 8, aesthetic and minimalist design; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*.) Fix: keep one control for the action and remove its twin, or give the second button a different job, such as "See how it works" or "See pricing". While you're there, make the main ask name one concrete thing. "Get started" leaves the visitor to work out what starting means. An ask like "Start a trip and invite your group" points to their own situation, and it sets Wayfold against the tool they use today, the group chat, which lets them judge the value for themselves.

3. **All the weight sits in the top-left, and the right half is empty.** The headline, subhead, and buttons fill roughly the left 55% of the top 500 px. Nothing on the right or below balances them, so the screen tips left and the empty space reads as left over, not chosen. *Convention* (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"; Ruder, *Typography*, on gathered white space). Fix: give the right side a counterweight that also does work. The obvious one is a picture of the product: a shared trip plan with flights, stays, and days, ideally showing several people's input. That would show "one place" instead of just claiming it. Or, if the page stays text-only, make the empty area clearly deliberate, for example by scaling the headline up and letting it take the width.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "trip in / one place", which splits the phrase "in one place". Break after "trip" so the second line reads as one unit (Bringhurst, on breaking display lines by sense). If you rewrite the headline, check the break again.

**What's working.**
- The order of importance is clear, with three levels: a heavy, dark, tightly set headline, then a regular grey subhead, then the buttons. The scale has big steps and no near-miss sizes. Keep it.
- The palette is restrained: a warm cream ground, near-black text, and one deep teal used only for the logo and the actions. The teal points at what's clickable. Keep the accent that scarce.
- The subhead copy is concrete and speaks the viewer's language ("a dozen chat threads"). It's the best line on the page. Promote it rather than cut it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The empty right half may be where a hero image or product screenshot failed to load in the capture. Check in a live browser. If an image belongs there, finding 3 mostly goes away, but check that the image shows group planning rather than a stock travel photo.
