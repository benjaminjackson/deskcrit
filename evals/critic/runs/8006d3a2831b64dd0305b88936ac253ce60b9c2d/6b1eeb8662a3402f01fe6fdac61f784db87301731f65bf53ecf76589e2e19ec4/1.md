**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that introduces the Gull Point lighthouse and gets people to visit it. Correct me if it's an editorial piece.

**Verdict.** It's well made and it reads easily. Layout, type and colour are all under control. The weak point is the words: the part a visitor acts on (when it's open, where to go, what it costs) is a passing mention at the end of the prose. So the page charms the reader but doesn't give them enough to plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The visit details are too vague to act on (defect, for a visitor page).** The second and third paragraphs say the tower is "open to visitors on summer weekends" and "tickets are sold at the cottage by the car park." A stranger can't tell which months count as summer, what hours, what a ticket costs, or where the car park is. They have to search somewhere else, and many won't bother. "Summer weekends" can be read two ways: June to August, or Easter to September. A visitor who gets it wrong turns up to a locked door. Keep the story in the first paragraph. Then give the visit its own short block: the months, the hours, the price and how to get there, with one clear next step such as a map or directions link.
2. **The best reason to climb is buried mid-paragraph (taste).** "Climb to the gallery on a clear morning and you can follow the ferry all the way across the bay to the harbour wall" is the line that sells the visit. It speaks to the reader and gives them something they can picture. Right now it sits between a fact about a timer and a sentence about tickets. Move it up, or end the copy on it, so the reader finishes on the reason to come rather than on scones.

**Smaller things.**
- **Lone word in paragraph one:** "room." sits alone on the last line. Rewrap or trim a word so the line carries at least two words (Bringhurst, *Elements of Typographic Style* 2.4.2).
- **The picture repeats the headline (taste):** the illustration shows a lighthouse by day with its lamp dark, so it only labels what the headline already says. If the picture showed the beam at dusk, or the view from the gallery, it would add something the words don't.

**What's working.**
- **Clean structure:** the illustration and the text block line up top and bottom, and there are only two type sizes (bold serif headline, regular serif body). The page needs nothing more. Keep that restraint when you add the visit details.
- **Readable body text:** the paragraphs are short, the lines run about 65 to 72 characters, and the line spacing is generous. It reads with no effort.
- **A palette that matches the mood:** pale sky, two blues for the sea, and the red and yellow kept to the tower. The warm colours bring the subject forward and the cool ones sit back.
