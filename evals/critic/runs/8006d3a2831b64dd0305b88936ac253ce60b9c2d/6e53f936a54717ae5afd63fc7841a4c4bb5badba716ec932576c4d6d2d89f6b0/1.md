**First read.** The serif headline lands first, then the eye drops straight to the first card, because it is the only photo in a row of flat illustrations. It is dark, ornate and busy: a wood-panelled lounge with leather sofas and leaded windows. It reads as a stately-home hotel, not window cleaning. Then the eye runs across the three calm illustrated cards and stops. The page is a tidy, friendly home-services site with a warm, trustworthy feel. Apart from the photo it is close to a template.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page of a local home-help booking service. Its job is to get a homeowner to pick a service and book a visit.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound and mostly well made, but the page doesn't yet do its job cleanly. The window-cleaning photo is the loudest thing on the page and the wrong one. The service cards, which the subhead tells you to pick, give you no way to pick them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The window-cleaning photo breaks the set and sends the wrong message.** It's in the top-left card. The other three cards are flat illustrations on a pale mint tile, and this is a dark, high-detail photo of a grand interior. Because it is the odd one out and the darkest block on the page, it pulls the first look after the headline. What it says is "period mansion", not "streak-free glass". The windows don't stand out as the subject; they are lost among sofas, panelling and a coffered ceiling. The row looks pieced together from different sources, and the first service makes the business look upmarket and unrelated. *Defect* (it mis-signals the service), and it breaks the consistent-rendering principle, which no approved source confirms, so I'm arguing it as common sense. Fix: use one rendering style for the set. The simplest route is a matching flat illustration of a window or squeegee on the same mint tile. If you want photos, use them on all four cards, with the same light and crop, each clearly showing the service.
2. **The cards are the first step, but they don't look clickable and don't lead anywhere.** The subhead says "Pick a service, choose a day…", so the obvious move is to pick a card. Nothing on the cards says you can: no button, no arrow, no "Book" link. The only action is "Book a visit" in the far top-right corner, away from the services. A visitor who has just decided "laundry, $24" has to guess whether to click the card or go back up to the header. *Defect* (it slows the main task and makes the visitor unsure). Krug's point in *Don't Make Me Think* applies: make it obvious what's clickable. Fix: give each card a clear action at the foot, such as "Book window cleaning →" next to the price, or a whole-card link with a visible cue. Then picking a card leads straight into choosing a day.
3. **The prices don't say what you get for them.** "From $89", "From $189", "From $24" and "From $65" have no unit. The visitor can't tell whether $24 laundry is per bag, per load or per week, or whether $65 garden care is per visit or per month. These are the numbers people remember and later hold you to. "From $24" can be read as "$24 for my weekly washing" or "$24 for one small bag". If the real answer is the second, the first reading turns into a complaint at checkout. *Defect.* Fix: add the unit and what it covers, for example "From $24 per bag (up to 15 lb)" or "From $89 per visit, up to 10 windows".

**Smaller things**
- **Headline line break.** "Help around the house, on your / schedule" splits the phrase and leaves "schedule" alone. Break after the comma ("Help around the house, / on your schedule"), or widen the measure so it fits on one line.
- **Generic headline.** "Help around the house, on your schedule" could sit on any competitor's page. The subhead is the stronger and more specific line ("vetted crew arrives with their own supplies"). Pull one of those facts into the headline so the reason to choose Fernway shows up first.
- **Copy contradiction.** The subhead promises "choose a day", but the laundry card says "We collect on Monday and bring it back folded on Wednesday", which is a fixed day. Either soften the subhead or say laundry runs on a set schedule.

**What's working**
- **The type pairing and scale.** The serif headline and logo against a plain sans for everything else give a clear, warm voice. The steps are distinct: about 40 px headline, ~19 px card titles, ~15 px body, and the price set apart by weight and green rather than another size. Keep this.
- **The illustration system on cards 2–4.** Each has one simple object, a shared teal/coral/mustard palette, the same mint tile and a few scattered dots. They are consistent, legible and friendly. This is the standard the first card should meet.
- **The subhead.** "Pick a service, choose a day, and a vetted crew arrives with their own supplies" explains the whole process in one sentence, with verbs and a concrete promise.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cards are already clickable whole, and whether they show a hover state. If they are, a hover state alone doesn't fix finding 2, because touch users never see it.
