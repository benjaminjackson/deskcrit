**First read.** The eye lands on the big headline "Plan the week with your whole team", jumps right to the blue "Create account" button, then works up through the five pale boxes. It reads straight away as the sign-up page for a team planning tool, and it feels calm and tidy. Within a second or two you notice that the boxes don't say what they are. The text inside them is all you get, and it will vanish as soon as you start typing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the sign-up screen for a team planning web app, and its job is to get a work visitor to create a free workspace in one go.

**Verdict.** The page mostly works. The order of importance is clear, the layout is calm and the main action is plain. But the form has no field labels: it only works while the fields are empty. And the one line about price can be read two ways. Fix those two and this is a solid sign-up screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The fields have no labels, only grey placeholder text.** Defect (convention). The text inside each of the five fields is the only thing that says what goes there: "Enter first name", "name@company.com", "At least 10 characters", "Company name". When someone types, that text disappears. The password rule goes first, and that's the one people need while they type. Someone who tabs back to check a field, or whose browser autofills a few fields, sees values with no names. The fourth field never even says it's a password; you only guess from "At least 10 characters". The NN/g article "Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful" and Nielsen's heuristic 6 (recognition rather than recall) both say this. Fix: put a short label above each field ("First name", "Work email", "Password", "Company name"), and move "At least 10 characters" to a short note under the password field that stays visible while typing.
2. **The price line can be read two ways.** Defect (copy). "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." Reading 1: free covers five people and the rest cost money. Reading 2: start with five and add the others later, still free. The headline says "your whole team", which pushes people toward reading 2. If the truth is reading 1, a team of eight signs up expecting it to be free and runs into a bill. That's how you lose trust, or get a support ticket. Fix: say what happens at person six, in the viewer's terms. For example: "Free for teams of up to five. Bigger team? It's $X per extra person, and you can add them any time." Or, if it really is free: "Start with five people now and add the rest whenever you like, still free."
3. **The left side could be any planning tool's page.** Taste. "Plan the week with your whole team" fits a dozen products, and under it there's nothing: no glimpse of the product, no example week, no reason to pick this one. A visitor who came here to decide sees nothing that tells Tallyboard apart, and the empty lower-left half wastes space that could make the case. Fix: let the headline say what is different about how this tool plans a week. Put one concrete thing in the empty space: a small, real screenshot of a planned week, or a line comparing it with what teams use now (the shared spreadsheet, the Monday stand-up). That way the visitor judges the value against something they already know.

**Smaller things.**
- The input borders are pale grey-blue on white and look about 2:1, below the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 asks for field edges. Darken them so the fields hold their shape for low-vision users. Measure the border colour against #fff to confirm.
- "Sign in" in "Already have an account? Sign in" is the same grey, weight and size as the rest of the sentence, so nothing marks it as a link. Underline it or give it the brand blue (with 3:1 against the surrounding text), per WCAG G183.
- Nothing shows which fields are required. "Company name" in particular reads as optional for a small team. Add "(optional)" after any optional label, or put asterisks on the required ones with a one-line key (Budiu, NN/g).
- The "Tallyboard" wordmark and the "Create your account" heading sit at almost the same height in the two columns but not quite on one line; they look about 2–3 px apart. Line them up exactly, or move one clearly off.
- Field order: "Company name" comes after the password, where people don't expect it. Taste: move it up next to the name and email fields, so the password is the last thing typed before the button.

**What's working.**
- The order of importance is right. One big dark headline, one solid blue button, and everything else quieter. Keep that two-step contrast when adding labels; labels should be small and medium weight, not bold.
- The palette is restrained. Grey-white ground, near-black text, and a single blue used only for the wordmark and the main button, so the button is the only strong colour in the form. Don't spend that blue on anything else.
- Spacing groups the form well. The name pair sits close together, the gaps between fields are even, the button has a bit more space above it, and the sign-in line sits close to the button it relates to.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the password field hides what you type, and whether it has a show/hide toggle.
- Focus rings on the fields and the button: they need to be visible, at least 2 px with 3:1 contrast, given how pale the borders are.
- Where errors appear, and what they say, when a field fails.
