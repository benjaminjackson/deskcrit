**First read.** The eye lands on "Planning a vegetable bed", the largest text by far, then drops through three subheads down the left edge. Within a few seconds it reads as a short how-to article for a beginner gardener. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, close to a well-set default document.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a beginner's how-to page on the web, read once from top to bottom and maybe skimmed again later by its headings.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The order is clear, the copy is concrete and the page is easy to scan. The one thing holding back the reading is that the paragraph lines are too long for the type size.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraph lines are too long.** "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" runs about 90 characters on one line, and the other paragraphs are similar. Bringhurst puts the comfortable range at 45 to 75 characters, with about 66 as the ideal. Here each line's end is far from where the next one starts, so the eye has to search for the start of the next line, and the short two-line paragraphs make the long lines more obvious. There is plenty of room to the right, so this was a width setting, not something forced by the layout. Narrow the text column to about 65 characters at this size; the headings can stay where they are. (Convention.)

**Smaller things.**
- **Subheads:** "Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant" and "Watering" use the same regular weight and the same colour as the body text, so they differ only in size. The size step is big enough to read, but the page looks like unstyled defaults. Use a smaller step and add a change of weight (medium or semibold), as Dair suggests in *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9.
- **Apostrophe:** "year's" uses a straight typewriter apostrophe. Use the curly one (’). No source I checked confirms this rule, so treat it as common sense: a straight mark is a typing artefact.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing that groups the content:** each subhead sits about 4 px above its own paragraph and about 30 px below the paragraph before it, so the four sections read as separate blocks. Keep these gaps when you narrow the column.
- **A clear type scale:** I count three sizes (about 56, 32 and 18 px) with big steps between them, and no extra size in between. Each size has a single job.
- **Copy that tells you exactly what to do:** "six hours of sun", "the depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans", "twice a week, rather than a little every day". Every line names a specific action and gives a number or an example. Keep that standard if the page gets longer.
