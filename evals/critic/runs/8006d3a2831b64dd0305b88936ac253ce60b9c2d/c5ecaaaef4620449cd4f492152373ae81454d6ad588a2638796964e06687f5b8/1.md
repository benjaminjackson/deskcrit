**First read.** The eye lands on the big cream italic "Harbour Lights Festival", goes up to the amber date, then down to the two-line description. Then it stops. Nothing comes after it, and the empty bottom third reads as a space where something should be. It feels calm, warm, a bit grand and well made. It reads as a free local festival, the hero of an event page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a web page (1280×800) for a free one-day town festival. Its job is to get locals and visitors to put the date in their diary and turn up.

**Verdict.** It looks good but doesn't do its job. A reader learns what the event is and that it's on a Saturday, but not where it is, what year it is, when the main event happens, or what to do next. That missing information is the headline problem, not the styling.

**What would move it most**
1. **It never says where the festival is** (defect). No town, harbour or address appears anywhere. "The old net shed" only helps people who already know the place, and they're the ones who need the page least. A visitor can't tell if this is near them, and nobody can plan a trip. The fix: put the place next to the date in the amber line, e.g. "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour". It should be as easy to see as the date.
2. **There's no next step** (defect). Nothing after the description invites the reader to act: no map, no programme, no "add to calendar". The empty band from about y 610 to 800 is the obvious place for one. Since entry is free, "buy tickets" is the wrong ask. Give one specific action, such as "See the evening programme" or "Get directions to the quay", so the interest from the headline turns into a plan.
3. **The timing doesn't hold together** (defect). The top line says "from noon", but the main attraction, "lantern boats on the water at dusk", is hours later. Someone deciding when to come can't tell whether to arrive at 12 or at 9pm, and there's no end time. "14 June" has no year, which matters once the page is shared or found later. Fix: give the year, and name the time of the lights event (e.g. "Lantern boats at 9.30pm"). The reader needs that time to plan, and they'll hold you to it.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Harbour / Lights Festival", which separates "Harbour Lights", the actual name, and reads as "Harbour" plus "Lights Festival". Break after "Lights" so the name stays on one line ("Harbour Lights / Festival"). It fits the width. This is common sense; no source confirms the rule for headings.
- This is my taste: a festival named for lights is shown with no light at all. The navy page is all type and could be any event. The amber date is the only warm spot. A glow, a lantern image or a warm highlight would give it something of its own. This is lower priority than points 1 to 3.

**What's working.**
- The headline clearly dominates. The large italic serif against the plain sans body and the small amber spaced capitals makes three distinct levels, with no sizes that nearly collide. Keep this scale.
- The colours are few and suit the subject: deep navy for the harbour at night, cream for the text, amber used only on the date line. Every colour combination reads with plenty of contrast.
- The description has good copy in concrete detail: lantern boats, music on the quay, a fish supper, free entry. It's specific enough to make people want to go, and "Free entry for everyone" answers the cost question straight away.
