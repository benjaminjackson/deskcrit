**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold "River ferry timetable" at top left, then down into three paragraphs of prose. I expected a table of times and kept scanning for one, but there are no numbers on the page. It feels calm, plain and competent, like a well-set article, and the rest of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** You didn't say, so I'm assuming this is a public web page for ferry riders: they want to know when the next boat leaves and whether they can bring a bike.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The page calls itself a timetable but gives no departure times. The typesetting is clean, but a rider who comes here to catch a boat leaves without the one fact they came for.

**What would move it most**
1. **No times on a page titled "timetable" (defect).** The heading promises a schedule, but the body gives only an interval ("every forty minutes"), a vague span ("between early morning and late evening") and a vaguer winter cut-off ("before dusk"). The third paragraph even sends people to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing", so the page sends you elsewhere for the very thing it is named after. A rider can't work out the first or last boat, or when the next one leaves. Headings should describe what their section actually holds (WCAG 2.4.6). Fix: put the times on the page, as a table of departures from each landing, summer and winter, with first and last boats named, so the title is true. If that really can't be done, rename the page to "About the river ferry" and link to the timetable.
2. **The deciding facts are buried in prose (defect, in the copy).** Things a rider acts on and will hold the operator to are written as soft sentences: "about twelve minutes", "a little longer when the tide turns", "one boat each hour", "before dusk". "Before dusk" moves by more than an hour across the winter, so two riders reading it could plan around different last boats, and one of them gets stranded. Fix: state each commitment as a figure. Give the last winter departure as a clock time (or "30 minutes before sunset, see table"), give the dates winter service runs, and pull the bicycle rule ("Bikes free, lower deck, board after foot passengers") onto its own scannable line under a small subhead.

**What's working.**
- The type is set with care. A bold title clearly above regular body text gives two levels that need nothing more, the body is near-black on white, lines run about 70 characters with comfortable leading, and paragraphs are separated by space only. Keep this setting when you add the table.
- The bicycle paragraph says who does what and when ("Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"). That's the plain, specific voice the rest of the page should use for times.
