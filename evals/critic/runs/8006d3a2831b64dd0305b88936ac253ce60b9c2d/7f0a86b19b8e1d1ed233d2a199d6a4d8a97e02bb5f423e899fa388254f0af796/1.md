**First read.** The eye lands on the big black "Learn to swim", drops to the grey line under it, then crosses the two small offers in the middle band. Then it runs out of page: the lower half and the right third are empty, and there is no button, link, price, or venue to land on. It reads as the top of a website for a local pool or swim school. It feels calm and clean, but also unfinished: it looks like a wireframe someone forgot to finish.

**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming this is the landing screen for a local pool, and its job is to get adults who can't swim to sign up for the Tuesday classes, with lanes and family hour as secondary offers.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The type is well set and the order of importance is clear, but the page never asks for anything. Someone who is persuaded has no next step, no venue, no time, and no price, so the page ends in blank space just as interest peaks.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no ask, and the facts needed to act are missing** (defect). Below the cards there is about 350 px of nothing. There is no "Book a Tuesday class" button, no start time, no price, no place, and no class size, even though "small" is the selling point. A nervous adult beginner who has read "a coach in the water beside you" and been won over has no way to act, and nothing to weigh the decision against. Fix: put one primary action directly under the subtitle that names one exact thing ("Book your first Tuesday class, 7pm"). Next to it, give the facts people commit to: time, price, how many per class, and where. The strongest version lets them judge for themselves, for example "First class free" or "Most people swim a length by week 6". That turns the claim into something they can check, rather than something they must trust.
2. **The page sends three different audiences in three directions under one headline** (defect). "Learn to swim" sets up a page for beginners. Then the two equal cards offer lane swimming to people who already swim well and a family hour for children. A beginner wonders whether those are for them. A lane swimmer who arrives here reads "Learn to swim" and assumes the pool isn't for them. Fix: decide what this page is. If it's the lessons page, cut the two cards or move them down as "Also at the pool". If it's the pool's home page, the headline should name the pool and the lessons become one of three offers, each with its own link.
3. **The most persuasive line is set second, and the headline is generic** (taste). "Learn to swim" could sit on any pool's page. What sets this one apart is "with a coach in the water beside you", which speaks to the adult beginner's real fear. Right now it sits in mid-grey at roughly a quarter of the headline's height. Consider bringing that promise into the headline, or giving the subtitle more weight, so the reason to choose this pool is read first rather than second.
4. **The composition stops halfway and tips to the upper left** (convention; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). All the weight is in the top-left 60% of the frame. Nothing to the lower right answers it, and the eye path ends in empty space instead of on an action. Fixing finding 1 mostly fixes this. A strong button, and if possible a picture of the water or of a coach beside a learner, would give the page somewhere to end and something that looks like swimming. At the moment the page is all grey type on white.

**Smaller things.**
- The subtitle (about 22 px, grey) and the card headings (about 20 px, black) are nearly the same size, so they almost read as one level. Pull them apart: make the subtitle clearly larger, or make the card headings smaller and set them in a heavier weight (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).

**What's working.**
- The scale contrast in the headline. Its heavy, tightly spaced type is about four times the size of everything else, so there is no doubt where to start. Keep that dominance when you add the button.
- The subtitle copy is concrete: it says who (small adult classes), when (Tuesday evenings), and how (a coach beside you in the water). The card copy is just as specific ("every morning from six", "floats and toys"). Write the new ask and facts to that standard.
- The setting is clean: one typeface family, a calm grey for secondary text that still looks easily readable, comfortable line lengths, and consistent left edges at one margin.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether this is the whole page or only the first screen of a longer scroll. If the ask and the details are further down, that still fails: the first screen ends empty, so they need to move up.
