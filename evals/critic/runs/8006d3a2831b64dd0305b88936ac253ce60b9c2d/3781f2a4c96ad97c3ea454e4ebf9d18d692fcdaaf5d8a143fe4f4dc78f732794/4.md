**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", then drops to the white form card and the blue "Send message" button. Within a second it reads as a design studio's contact page for prospective clients. It feels calm, competent and tidy, and also anonymous: apart from the wordmark, nothing on it belongs to Fernwick in particular.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the contact page of a small design studio, where a prospective client should send a useful first enquiry.

**Verdict.** It does its basic job. Anyone can tell what this is and how to send a message. What holds it back is that it asks for a budget and timeline, then gives one blank box, so the studio will get thinner enquiries than it wants. The layout also stops short on the right, which makes the page look unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form doesn't ask for what the copy asks for** (defect). The left column says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them", but the form only has Name, Email and one Message box. The visitor has to remember that advice while typing in a box 450 px away, and most won't, so the studio will have to send a follow-up asking for budget and timing. This breaks Nielsen's "recognition rather than recall" and "help and documentation": put guidance next to the step it concerns. Fix: add a Budget field (a short list of ranges) and a Timeline field, both marked optional, or at least put that hint under the Message label. This also makes the request feel specific instead of a generic "get in touch."
2. **The layout stops 150 px short of its own frame** (convention/taste). The header runs from x≈56 to x≈1224, but the content ends at the card's right edge, x≈1068. That leaves an empty strip on the right that lines up with nothing, so the page tips left and looks like a column is missing. The card's top (y≈121) also sits a few pixels off the headline's cap height. These are near-misses where an exact alignment was clearly intended. Following Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems*, take the content columns from the same edges as the header. Either stretch the card to the 1224 line, or set both columns on a centred measure. Then align the card's top exactly with the headline's cap height.

**Smaller things.**
- Navigation: "Contact" looks identical to Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the current page. Mark it with one cue, such as bold, a heavier underline or dark text (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Required fields: none of the three fields says whether it's required. Put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or write "optional" after the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- Input borders: the borders on the three fields look like pale grey on white, probably below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for. Measure them, and darken them a step if they fall short.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean, with about four clear levels: a bold headline around 36 px, 16 px body text, 14 px bold labels and a bold button. Each size has an obvious job, so keep it.
- The supporting line, "We read every message and reply within two working days", makes a specific promise the visitor can hold the studio to. That builds more trust than a generic "we'd love to hear from you."
- There's one primary action, labelled with exactly what it does ("Send message"). Labels sit above fields, the form is a single column, and the links are underlined. These are all standard, readable choices.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button.
- Whether the email field uses an email input type and validates when the visitor leaves it.
- What the error and success states look like after sending.
