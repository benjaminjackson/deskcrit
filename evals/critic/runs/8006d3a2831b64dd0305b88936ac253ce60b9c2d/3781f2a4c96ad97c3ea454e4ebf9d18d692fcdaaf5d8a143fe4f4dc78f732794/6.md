**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves right to the white card, and drops straight down the three fields to the blue "Send message" button. Within two seconds it reads as a small design studio's contact page. It feels calm, competent and neutral, close to a default web template.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a studio's contact page, meant to get prospective clients to send a useful project enquiry.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The page is clear, the order of importance is obvious and the form is easy to fill in. The one thing that affects the quality of the enquiries is that the advice on what to write sits apart from the box you write it in.

**What would move it most**
1. **The guidance about what to write is in the wrong column.** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline" is in the left column, about 450 px from the Message box it refers to. People read it, move across to the form, start typing in an empty box and forget it. The studio then gets "Hi, can we chat?" messages and has to reply to ask for the budget and timeline anyway. Help belongs at the step it's for and controls should sit beside what they affect (Nielsen heuristic 10; Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). **Defect.** Fix: put the budget and timeline prompt as hint text directly under the "Message" label, or as two short optional fields ("Budget range", "When do you need it?"). Keep the "reply within two working days" promise on the left, where it reassures the reader.

**Smaller things**
- **Navigation doesn't show the current page:** "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing confirms where you are. Mark the current item with one cue, such as a bolder weight or no underline in a dark colour (Nielsen heuristic 1; Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Required fields aren't marked:** none of the three labels says which fields are required. Put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once, or add "(optional)" to the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- **Input borders look pale:** the grey field outlines on white look like roughly 2.3:1, below the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 asks for. Measure the border colour against #FFF and darken it if it fails.
- **The right edge isn't on the page grid:** the card ends around x = 1068, which lines up with nothing. The nav ends at 1224, so there's a dead strip about 155 px wide on the right that reads as leftover space. Either run the card's right edge out to the nav's right margin, or make the two columns clearly proportional (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). This is a convention break; the cost is that the page looks unfinished rather than anything functional.

**What's working**
- **The intro copy makes a specific promise:** "We read every message and reply within two working days" tells the visitor exactly what happens after they click Send. Keep it near the headline.
- **The hierarchy is clean:** one bold headline, bold labels above every field, and a single filled primary button. There's never any doubt about where to start or what to press.
- **The type scale is tight:** about four sizes (headline, logo, body, labels and nav), each with a clear job and no near-duplicate sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Focus styling on the fields and the button: check that each shows an indicator at least 2 px thick at 3:1.
- Whether the email field uses an email input type and what validation errors look like.
