**Verdict.** It does the basic job: what this is and what to do next are obvious, and nothing gets in the way of sending a message. The biggest problem is that the one instruction that would make enquiries useful (send a budget and a timeline) sits in the left column, far from the box where people actually type. Second, the page says nothing about a studio whose job is design.

**What would move it most**

1. **The brief prompt is away from the field it's about.** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them" sits in the left column at about y 258. The Message box it refers to is about 450 px to the right and 100 px lower. Many visitors go straight to the form, and when they reach Message they see an empty box with only the word "Message" above it. So the studio gets "Hi, we'd love a quote" instead of a brief it can answer within the promised two days. Help belongs next to the step it concerns (Nielsen 10, Help and documentation). **Defect.** Fix: put the prompt under the Message label as hint text, or give budget and timeline their own short fields (a budget range picker and a "when do you need it" field) so the answer arrives in a form you can act on. Keep the reply promise in the left column, because it reassures and doesn't instruct.

2. **For a design studio, the page shows no design.** The type is Inter, the background is a cool grey, the form is a white card with rounded corners and pale borders, the button is the stock blue, and the wordmark is plain bold text. Every part is a framework default. A visitor deciding whether to hire this studio takes the contact page as a sample of its work, and right now it says nothing about Fernwick's taste or skill. The headline is just as generic: "Tell us about your project" could be on any agency's site. **Taste**, but it costs more here than on any other kind of business. Direction: give the page one deliberate choice of its own, such as a typeface, a colour or a piece of recent work beside the form, and a headline that says something only this studio would say.

3. **The content doesn't sit on the page's grid.** The header runs from x 56 to x 1224, but the card stops at about x 1068. That leaves a 156 px empty strip on the right that matches nothing else. The page looks pushed to the left, as if a column is missing. Every block should take its edges from one grid (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*; Samara, *Making and Breaking the Grid*), and a heavy side needs a counterweight (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). **Convention.** Fix: carry the card to the header's right edge at x 1224, or narrow the whole layout, header included, to one centred width.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing shows which page you're on. Give the current item one cue, such as a bolder weight or no underline in a dark colour (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Form: no field is marked required or optional. Add an asterisk to the required labels and explain it once, or write "optional" after the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- Input borders: the field outlines look like a light grey on white, roughly 2.5:1 by eye. Measure them, and darken them to at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
- Headline: "Tell us about your / project" leaves "project" alone on the second line and splits the noun phrase. Break it as "Tell us about / your project", or widen the column so it fits on one line (common sense; the catalog's source for heading breaks is unverified).

**What's working.**
- The paragraph under the headline makes a concrete promise ("We read every message and reply within two working days"). That is a real number the visitor can hold you to, and it's the best line on the page. Keep it.
- The form is right for the task: one column, three fields, labels in bold above each field and close to it, and one filled primary button. Nothing competes with sending.
- The type scale is clean: about four sizes (headline, logo, body/nav, labels), and the steps between them are plainly different.
