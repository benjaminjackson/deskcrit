**First read.** The eye goes to the big white headline, "Ferns and shade plants for every corner". Then it gets pulled into the background: a full-bleed spray of lime, emerald and olive ovals that moves everywhere at once. The subtitle and the outlined button sink into it. It reads as a plant nursery's homepage and feels lively, but busy, like wrapping paper rather than a place to buy plants.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the homepage hero for a small local nursery. Its job is to make a nearby gardener want ferns and shade plants from this place and click through to browse them.

**Verdict.** It's legible at the top and doesn't do its job. The background is the biggest problem: it's a generic pattern that shows none of the plants on sale, and it's loud enough to bury the subtitle and the only button.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero picture sells nothing** (defect). The whole area behind the headline is abstract green confetti. A gardener looking for shade plants sees no fern, no foliage they recognise, no greenhouse, no hillside. The headline promises "ferns and shade plants" and the picture can't back it up. A nursery's best argument is its stock, and the page never shows any, so the viewer has no reason to pick Fernhill over a garden centre. The picture also has no subject at all, so nothing in it pulls focus. The fix is to replace the pattern with a real photograph of what's on sale: fern fronds in shade, or the greenhouse benches. Choose or crop it so the image and the headline make one point ("this is the corner you could have"). Leave a calm, darker area on the left for the type.

2. **The text sits straight on a busy texture** (defect, WCAG Techniques F83). White text has to hold its contrast against the lightest thing behind it, not the average. Behind "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend." and the "Browse the greenhouse" label, pale lime ovals pass right under the letters, and the thin white button outline nearly disappears against them. The headline survives because it's huge and bold. The smaller lines are where people actually decide whether to click, and they flicker in and out. The fix is to put a dark scrim or solid panel behind the text block, or to use a picture with a calm, dark area where the text sits, so every letter stands clear of what's behind it.

3. **The colour is one loud mid-green with no light-and-dark structure** (convention: Itten, *The Art of Color*, on saturation contrast; Albers, *Interaction of Color*, on value). The whole hero is filled with saturated greens in roughly equal amounts of light and dark, spread evenly. In grayscale it would be an even mid-grey speckle, with nothing near the type gathered into a dark area and no open space for the eye to rest (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, on gathered versus scattered space). Saturation is supposed to mark what matters. Here it covers everything, so it marks nothing. The fix is to fill the big area with a deep, muted green or a dark photo, and keep full-strength colour for one small thing, which should be the button.

4. **The only action is the weakest kind of button** (convention: Material Design, Apple HIG, NN/g on button emphasis). "Browse the greenhouse" is a hairline white outline with no fill. Outline buttons are meant for secondary actions. As the one thing the page wants you to do, it has the lowest emphasis it could have, and the texture flattens it further. The fix is to make it a solid filled button with a strong contrast against the background. Separately, the wording should be more concrete. "Browse the greenhouse" is pleasant but vague. A line that points at what the viewer already has would work better, for example "Find ferns for a shady spot" or a choice like "Deep shade / Part shade". That gives them a choice from their own garden.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks so "corner" sits alone on the third line (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Rebreak it or widen the measure so the last line carries more than one word ("plants for / every corner").
- The headline and subtitle start at about x=96, while the "Fernhill Nursery" logo starts at about x=64. The 32 px gap looks like a near-miss rather than a choice. Put them on the same left edge.
- Taste: the subtitle is the one line with a real reason to buy ("ready to plant this weekend"), and it gets the least emphasis. Once it's readable, consider making it carry more of the pitch.

**What's working.**
- The headline is set well: one family, bold, tight leading, and clearly dominant. The size jump from headline to subtitle is decisive, so the type has an obvious order. Keep the scale.
- The copy speaks like a person and is specific: "Grown on the hill and ready to plant this weekend" tells you what's local and when. Keep that voice.
- The white nav bar is plain and standard (Plants, Pots, Care guides, Visit), and the labels are in the customer's own words.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus states on the button and nav links. With a white outline over this texture, check that a focus ring would be visible at all.
