**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question, "Save changes to the shared calendar?", drops to the one-line explanation, then goes straight to the filled blue "Save changes" button at bottom right. It reads at once as a save confirmation in a calendar app. It feels calm, clean and competent. The trouble is in the four-item row of actions on the left, which the eye skims as "other stuff".

**What it's for.** Assumption: a desktop web dialog that asks someone editing a shared calendar (a choir organiser) to confirm a change that everyone who follows the calendar will see.

**Verdict.** The dialog mostly works: the question is clear, the consequence is stated, and the safe main action is obvious. The one real risk is "Discard changes". It throws away the user's edits, yet it is styled exactly like the harmless "Export as file" and sits right next to "Keep editing", so it is the easiest action to hit by mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" is styled like a harmless link and sits among the safe actions.** *Defect.* In the button row, "Discard changes" is plain blue text, the same as "Export as file". Its edge is about 40 px from "Keep editing". Someone who wanted to keep working, or who reaches for the nearest secondary action, can lose all their rehearsal edits with one click, and nothing in the styling warns them. Destructive actions should be set apart from safe ones, given their own destructive style, and labelled with the exact outcome (Apple HIG, "Alerts"; Material Design, "Dialogs"). Fix: move Discard away from the Keep editing / Save pair, for example alone at the far left. Give it a destructive treatment, such as red text. Consider a label that names the loss ("Discard my edits"), or offer undo after discarding.
2. **Four actions where the question needs two or three.** *Convention.* The question is "save or not?" "Export as file" answers a different question, yet it sits first in the row with the same weight as Discard. That makes four choices to read before acting, and it creates the false pair described above. Rare options belong behind their own control, with the main choice shown first (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). Fix: take Export out of this dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu. The row then becomes Discard … Keep editing / Save changes.

**Smaller things.** None.

**What's working.**
- **The body line states the consequence in the viewer's terms.** "will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar" tells the user exactly what clicking Save does to other people. It names the specific content ("the new choir rehearsal times"), not "your changes". Keep that.
- **Clear order of emphasis among the safe actions.** One filled primary button, one outlined secondary and text for the rest. The answer you'd expect is the obvious one, and both buttons say what they do ("Keep editing", not "Cancel").
- **Tight type.** A bold question over a regular body line, with nothing in between. Two levels are all this needs.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover, focus and pressed states.
- Which button gets focus by default. It must not be Discard.
- Whether Escape or a click outside the dialog acts as "Keep editing".
- The page behind the dialog is plain white with no dimming. That may be the capture and not the design. Check in a live browser that the calendar behind is dimmed, so the dialog reads as blocking.
