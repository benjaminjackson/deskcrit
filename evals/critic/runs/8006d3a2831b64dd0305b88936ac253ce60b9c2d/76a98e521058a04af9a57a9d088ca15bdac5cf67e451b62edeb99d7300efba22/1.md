**First read.** The eye lands on "Landing stage rebuild" first, then moves to the blue "Sign up" button and the blue shape in the illustration, which look like a matching pair. It reads as a ferry operator's news page with a timetable-alerts sign-up in the sidebar. It's calm, clean and orderly. Then you try to fill in the form and find you can't read what it asks for.

**What it's for.** Assumed: tell passengers about the rebuilt landing stages and get them to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The sign-up is the only thing on the page that asks the viewer to act, and its field labels, its privacy note, the figure caption and the footer are all set at about 4 px, too small to read. The illustration also shows none of what the article describes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The small text can't be read (defect).** The two field labels above the inputs, the line under "Sign up", the caption under the figure and the footer line are all around 4 px tall. Even at full resolution the letters run together. The labels seem to say "Your name" and "Home landing", and the note seems to say "We send one short note when the timetable changes. You can stop the notes at any time." A viewer can't tell what goes in either field, and can't see the reassurance that would make them sign up. Apple's HIG puts the smallest readable text at 11 pt. Set labels and notes at about body size or one step below (about 14 px), in a darker grey. The type scale is fine at the top: 32 / 22 / 18 are clear steps. It just has no workable bottom step.
2. **The form doesn't say how the alerts reach you (defect, if the labels read as they seem to).** It asks for a name and a home landing but no email or phone number. If those are really the only fields, the viewer has no idea how the "short note" will arrive, and the operator has no way to send it. Add the contact field, and make the button say what happens, e.g. "Email me when my landing's times change". "Sign up" could be on any site.
3. **The illustration doesn't show the rebuild, and it looks like a button (defect).** It's grey and blue blocks: no ramps, benches, kiosk or tide. It reads as a wireframe placeholder, so it adds nothing the headline doesn't already say. Worse, the boat uses the same saturated blue and rounded corners as "Sign up", so it looks like a second button. Use a real photo or a drawing of the new ramps at low tide, which is the point of the second paragraph. Either way, keep the button's blue off the picture.

**Smaller things.**
- "Home landing" is a free-text field. The operator has a fixed list of landings, so use a picker and avoid typos that would quietly miss alerts.
- Neither field says whether it's required. Mark the optional one.
- The card's top edge (56 px) nearly lines up with the headline's cap height (~62 px), and the card heading doesn't line up with the headline either. Align the card to the H1 cap height, or offset it clearly.

**What's working.**
- The type is clear: one sans-serif face, a bold headline, and body text at a comfortable size with good line spacing. The first paragraph runs about 70 characters a line.
- "The ramps now meet the deck at every tide, so prams and wheelchairs roll straight on" says what the rebuild means for the reader. Keep that kind of line.
- The colours are restrained: neutrals plus one blue, kept for the action. That's exactly why the blue boat is a problem.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus, error and success states for the form.
- How the two columns stack on a phone.
