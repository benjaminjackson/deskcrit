I finished the checklist walk and the reference reads. Here's the critique.

**First read.** I look at the big serif headline first. Then I drop to the eyebrow and the two-line description, and then there's nowhere to go: the lower 40% of the screen is empty cream. It reads as the top of a small, traditional, independent bakery's website. It feels calm, warm and well-made, but also unfinished, as though the rest of the page hasn't loaded.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the hero of a local bakery's homepage, and its job is to get people nearby to visit the shop or order bread.

**Verdict.** It looks good, but it doesn't do its job. Nothing tells the viewer where Mill Street is, when the shop is open, or what to do next, and the headline is a claim any bakery could make. The type is careful work. The page around it does nothing yet.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no next step.** Below "a wood-fired oven on Mill Street." there's no button, no link, no opening hours and no address beyond a street name. A hungry neighbour who has been persuaded has to go looking for how to act on it, and most won't. Add one specific ask for this audience, such as "Today's loaves are out at 7am. Find us at 14 Mill Street" or "Order for Saturday pickup", with the hours next to it. *Defect* (common sense; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: the next action should be self-evident). The ask also gives the eye somewhere to end. Right now the path stops after the description and leaves the viewer at a dead end (Arnheim on a clear path through the composition).
2. **The headline is a generic claim.** "The finest bread in the valley" could sit on any bakery's site in any valley. The viewer can't check it, so they either take it on trust or ignore it. The concrete, believable facts are buried in the 20 px line underneath: since 1962, wood-fired, baked every morning, Mill Street. Move one of them up into the headline, for example "Baked in a wood fire on Mill Street every morning since 1962". The viewer then gets a fact they can weigh, which a superlative doesn't give them. *Taste*, argued: specific beats superlative because it can be checked.
3. **The empty lower half makes the page look broken.** All the weight sits in a block at the top left. The bottom ~300 px and the right ~40% of the screen are empty, so the page tips up and left. That emptiness doesn't frame anything, so it reads as a page that didn't load rather than as calm. Fill the space with something the viewer needs: a photo of the loaves or the oven, which would also give the headline an image to work with, and the hours and address. *Defect*: unplanned empty space and imbalance (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). Note that I don't know whether a hero image failed to load here; see below.

**Smaller things.**
- "valley" sits alone on line two of the headline, which splits the phrase "in the valley" and leaves a single-word runt. Rebreak it as "The finest bread / in the valley", or let the rewrite above set the break (Bringhurst on breaks in display lines).
- The right edges of the headline (~1085 px) and the description (~720 px) don't line up with each other or with a visible column, so the block has no clear right-hand structure. Set the description to a grid column that the headline also respects.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is three sizes with large steps between them: a ~110 px serif headline, a small tracked eyebrow in bold capitals, and ~20 px sans for the description. Each level does a distinct job, and the serif/sans pairing is plainly different rather than nearly matched. Keep it.
- The palette is cream, near-black and one bakery brown, used only on the eyebrow. It feels warm and traditional without getting twee, and the brown is held back enough to keep its meaning.
- The headline is well set. The "fi" is a real ligature, the leading holds the two lines together, and the description runs about 60 characters a line, which reads comfortably.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether an image, navigation or call to action is supposed to appear in the empty areas but didn't load or hadn't animated in yet. Check the live page. If something should be there, findings 1 and 3 become a loading problem.
- How the headline reflows on a phone.
