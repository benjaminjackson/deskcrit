**First read.** I look at the purple "Sign in" button first, then up to the email field because of its red border. It's a clean, plain sign-in card for a product called Quillpost, and it feels calm and generic. After that first glance I'm left asking: something is wrong with my email, but what?

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the sign-in screen for a web app (Quillpost), shown after a failed or invalid email entry. It should let a returning user get in quickly, fix a mistake, or go to sign-up.

**Verdict.** The layout is tidy and the hierarchy works, but the one state this screenshot shows, an error, fails. A red border with no words leaves the user to guess what's wrong. And the actual mistake ("exmaple") is easy to catch, but the screen says nothing about it.

**What would move it most**

1. **The email error has no message.** (Defect.) The email field (top of the form) gets a red border and nothing else. A user who sees it has to work out alone that "exmaple" is a typo and that the address is missing ".com". Anyone who can't tell red from the grey border on the password field gets no signal at all. That breaks WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of color), 3.3.1 (Error identification), and 3.3.3 (Error suggestion), and Nielsen's heuristic 9 (help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors). Fix: put a short line in words right under the field, plus an icon, that names the problem and the fix. For example: "That email looks incomplete. Did you mean dana.ortiz@example.com?" Best of all, offer the corrected address as a one-tap fix. A misspelled domain is the most common email typo, so suggesting the likely address saves the user a re-type.

2. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link.** (Defect.) In the bottom line, "New here? Create an account" is set entirely in the same mid-grey, with no colour, underline, or weight to set the link apart. A new user, who is exactly the person looking for it, can't tell which words to click, or that anything is clickable. "Forgot your password?" is purple, so the two links on the card also follow different rules (Nielsen 4, consistency). Fix: style "Create an account" exactly like the password link, and give both links a second cue besides colour, such as an underline, so they pass WCAG 1.4.1.

**Smaller things.**
- Password field border: the pale grey-blue edge looks close to 2:1 against white, below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for. Darken it so the field reads as a field. Measure it.
- Wordmark vs. heading: "Quillpost" (~20px bold) and "Sign in" (~24px bold) are only a few pixels apart, so they read as two near-equal headings. Make the wordmark clearly smaller or lighter (or use a logo), so "Sign in" is the obvious title (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- No show-password toggle on the password field. Most sign-in forms now have one, and it cuts failed attempts from typos (taste and convention).

**What's working.**
- A single primary action: the solid purple "Sign in" button is the only heavy element, so there's no doubt what to do. Keep everything else this quiet.
- Visible labels above each field, set in bold, sitting closer to their own field than to the one above. That's the right form pattern, so don't swap the labels for placeholders.
- The spacing inside the card is consistent, all left edges line up, and the empty space around the card is gathered rather than scattered. The page feels calm, which suits a login.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the red border means a validation error or is just the focus style. If it's focus, it uses error red and will read as an error every time. Check the focus style separately.
- Whether the error message appears after submit or on blur, and whether screen readers announce it.
