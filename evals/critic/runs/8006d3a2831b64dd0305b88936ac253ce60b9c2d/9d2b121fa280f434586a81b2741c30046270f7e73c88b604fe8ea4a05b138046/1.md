**First read.** The eye lands on the white headline in the dark leafy band, "Plants for shade and small spaces", then drops to the grey lead line, then reads across three identical cards, and then stops. Nothing asks you to do anything. It reads as a small local garden centre's home page: calm, decent and competent, but forgettable. The leaf pattern could be wallpaper on any plant shop's site.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page for a local nursery. Its job is to get nearby people, probably with shady yards or balconies, to browse the shade plants or come in this weekend.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, but it doesn't do its job. It has a good, specific offer ("ready to pot this weekend") and gives the visitor nothing to do about it. There's no button, no link from the offer and no next step. The hero picture is a generic pattern, not the plants being sold.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no ask (defect).** No element on the page is a call to action. The headline names a range and the lead says "ready to pot this weekend", but the visitor can't see the plants, find the address or book a workshop from here. Their only route is guessing which nav word ("Plants"? "Visit"?) holds the offer. Add one primary button right under the lead, with an exact ask from the visitor's world, like "See the shade plants in stock" or "Find what grows on a north-facing balcony". Make it the only strongly styled control (Material Design / Apple HIG on primary vs secondary actions; Krug on making the next step obvious). The Saturday workshops card needs a smaller ask of its own, like "Book a place". "Ten places in each class" sets up scarcity and then gives no way to grab one.
2. **The hero image says "leaves", not "these plants" (defect).** The band is a flat repeating pattern of olive and green leaves with red and grey dots. It doesn't show a fern, a hosta or a shady corner, so the headline and image just repeat each other ("green, plants") instead of adding up to one idea. The visitor also gets no proof the stock is good. On top of that, the busy texture flickers behind the white headline letters. It stays readable, but it competes. Use a real photo of the shade stock, ideally potted up in a small shady space, with a calm area or a darkening overlay where the headline sits. This is taste plus common sense: for a nursery, the product shot is the sales pitch.
3. **The headline is cut off from its own message (defect / convention).** The headline sits inside the hero band, and the lead line that explains it ("Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs…") sits outside it. The lead is closer to the cards than to the headline. The headline is also indented about 16 px from the left line shared by the logo, the lead and the cards. So the visitor's eye has to jump the band edge and shift left to keep reading one thought. Put the headline, lead and button in one group on one left edge, either all inside the band or with the headline moved down to join the lead (Müller-Brockmann on one grid; proximity per Lupton). While you're there, rebreak the headline so "small spaces" stays together. Right now "spaces" hangs alone on line two.

**Smaller things.**
- Type scale: I count four sizes within a few pixels (lead about 20, card titles about 17 bold, nav about 16, card body about 15). Drop the card titles to body size and keep them bold, or open the gaps between sizes (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- Opening hours card: "until 8 / pm" splits the number from its unit. Rewrite or rebreak it ("8am–6pm daily, till 8pm Thursdays").
- Opening hours card: it gives hours but no address or map link, though "come this weekend" is the implied ask.
- Card titles are bold black and look like they might be links, but nothing marks them as clickable. If they link, style them as links. If they don't, make them link.
- The large headline looks set at default letter spacing. Tighten it slightly at this size (Bringhurst).

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and local: "grown at our nursery", "ready to pot this weekend", "Free within ten miles on orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box". These are real, checkable promises that a chain couldn't make. Keep this voice.
- The restrained palette (one deep green, neutrals and the serif logo in the same green) gives a calm, trustworthy, garden-shop feel without any decoration fighting for attention.
- The three cards answer the three questions a local visitor actually has (when, what's on, can you deliver) in one line each.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the card titles or the nav show hover or focus states, and how the hero headline and band reflow on a phone.
