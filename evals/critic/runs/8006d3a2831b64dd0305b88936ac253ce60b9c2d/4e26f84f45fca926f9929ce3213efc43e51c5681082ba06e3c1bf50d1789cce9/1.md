Checklist and deep reads are done. Here's the critique.

**First read.** My eye goes to "Create your account", then straight down the stack of fields to the dark blue button. Within a second I know it's a signup form for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and safe, but also anonymous: take the wordmark away and it could be the signup page for any SaaS product.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the signup screen for a free notes product. Its job is to get a stranger from interested to signed in with as little friction as possible.

**Verdict.** The visual side works: the hierarchy is clear, the spacing is even, and there's only one primary action. The form asks too much, though. A promise of a "free workspace for your notes" leads into six fields, and two of them, the workspace name and "How did you hear about us?", don't help the user get in. Nothing says which fields are required or what the password must be, so the first time they'll find out is an error.

**What would move it most**
1. **The gate asks for more than getting in needs.** This is a defect. "Workspace name" and "How did you hear about us?" make up the bottom third of the card. Here they are a cost the visitor pays before seeing any value, and every extra field is another point where they might give up. The attribution question helps you, not them. The workspace name asks them to name something they haven't seen yet. Move both after signup (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"): ask for the name in onboarding, or default it to "Ben's notes" from the first name, and ask the survey question once they're inside. That cuts the form to name, email and password, which matches the "Start a free…" promise.
2. **Nothing says which fields are required.** This is a convention break. None of the six labels has an asterisk or "(optional)", and the empty "How did you hear about us?" select is exactly the kind of field people skip and then get bounced back for. Mark the required fields with an asterisk and one line explaining it, or put "(optional)" after the optional labels (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). If you take point 1, this mostly disappears.
3. **The password field doesn't state its rules.** This is a defect. "Password" is just a label over an empty box. If you enforce a minimum length or a character mix, people will find out by failing. Put one line of hint text under the field, such as "At least 8 characters", so they get it right the first time (Nielsen 10, help in context).
4. **"Sign in" doesn't look clickable.** This is a defect. In the line "Already have an account? Sign in", the link is the same grey, weight and size as the question before it, with no underline. A returning user who landed here by mistake has to hunt for their way out. Underline it or set it in the brand blue, ideally both (Norman, signifiers; WCAG 1.4.1).

**Smaller things.**
- **Field borders:** the pale grey-blue input borders look like they fall under 3:1 against the white card (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure them, and darken them if they fail, so the field edges hold up on dim screens.
- **Empty select:** the "How did you hear about us?" select shows nothing at all, not even "Select one". Until someone clicks it, it looks broken or unfinished. Add a prompt option.
- **No prefill:** the workspace name could be filled in from the first name, so the user confirms it instead of inventing one. This is taste, argued as common sense.
- **Generic copy:** "Start a free workspace for your notes" could be on anyone's page. One concrete line about what Quillnest does differently would give the visitor a reason to finish the form. This is taste.

**What's working.**
- Each label sits tight above its own field, and the gap to the next label is clearly bigger, so every pair reads as one unit at a glance. Keep that spacing rhythm.
- There's a single filled primary button the full width of the form, with nothing competing with it. The page has exactly one clear thing to do.
- First and last name share a row, and that's the only paired row. It's the right exception to a single-column form.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus:** whether a focused field shows a ring at least 2 px thick with 3:1 contrast, which matters on a keyboard-driven form.
- **Input types:** whether the email field is `type=email` and the password field hides its input and offers a show toggle.
