**First read.** My eye goes to the bold heading, "Notes for ferry passengers", and then drops into three grey blocks that look smudged. Up close, each line of text runs into the one below it. "Forty minutes" sits on top of "takes about", and "lock them to the rail" sits on top of "the side of the deck". It reads as a passenger information page that broke while it rendered. It feels careless, and that undercuts the reassurance a timetable notice is supposed to give.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or posted notice that tells ferry passengers how the service runs: how often, how long a crossing takes, bicycles and bags, and winter hours. The reader wants facts they can plan around.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The body text is set with less space between lines than the height of the letters, so every paragraph is hard work to read and some words are hard to make out. Underneath that, the copy holds back the times a passenger would need to plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The lines of body text overlap.** This affects all three paragraphs from y≈146 to y≈360. The type looks about 18 px, but each line starts only about 15 px below the one above. That leaves less space between lines than the letters are tall, so descenders run into the ascenders below ("forty minutes" / "takes about"; "on after foot passengers" / "the side of the deck"; "crossing leaves the quay" / "the shelter at each landing"). The reader has to untangle every line, and on a 68-character line the eye can't easily find the start of the next one. Bringhurst (*Elements*, 2.2.1, p. 37) and Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 47) both say a longer line needs more space between lines, not less. This is a **defect**, and nothing else on the page matters until it's fixed. Set the body line spacing to roughly 1.4–1.5 times the type size (about 25–27 px here). Check whether this is a CSS line-height bug, for example a fixed pixel value or an inherited one. Then keep the space between paragraphs clearly larger than the new line spacing, so the three topics still read as separate blocks.
2. **The copy leaves out the facts a passenger acts on.** This is the first and third paragraphs. "Between early morning and late evening", "a little longer when the tide turns" and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" all make the reader guess. "Before dusk" is the worst, because it's a cut-off and the reader can take it two ways. They might think it means just before dark and miss the boat, or play safe and leave far earlier than they need to. "One boat each hour" also doesn't say at what minute past the hour. This is a **defect**, because these are the lines people will hold the service to. Give real times for the first and last boats in each season, a minute past the hour for the winter service, and the dates winter starts and ends. If times change, say where the current ones are: the noticeboard is mentioned, but only at the very end.

**What's working.**
- **The heading.** It's bold, plainly larger than the body, and says exactly what the page is. That's a clean two-level hierarchy, and it doesn't need anything more.
- **Short paragraphs, one topic each.** Schedule, bicycles and winter hours each get their own short block in a plain sans serif at about 68 characters a line. Once the line spacing is fixed, this will be easy to scan.
- **Restraint.** There's no decoration, one dark neutral colour, and a consistent left edge and margin. Keep it that way.
