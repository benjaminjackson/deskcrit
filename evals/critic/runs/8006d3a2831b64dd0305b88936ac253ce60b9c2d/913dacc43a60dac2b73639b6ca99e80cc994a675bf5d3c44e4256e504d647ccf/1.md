**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The chart shows no information, so the title's promise of rainfall is never kept, and the travel directions pressed against it make the page look like two sections that collided. Before anything else, the chart has to say something.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no labels, so it says nothing (defect).** The three bars in the tinted box have no category names, no value axis, no units and no numbers. A viewer can see that the middle bar is tallest but not whether it stands for a month, a year or a site, or whether it means 5 mm or 500 mm. With no axis, they can't even check that the bars start at zero. Every chart needs labelled axes or direct labels with units, so nobody has to guess what a number measures (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few, *Show Me the Numbers*). Fix: name each bar under it (for example "Sept", "Oct", "Nov"), put the value and unit on or above each bar ("84 mm"), and show the time period.
2. **The title names a topic, not a finding, and the chart doesn't back it up (my judgment, not a published rule).** "Rainfall at the reservoir" makes the visitor work out the point themselves, and with no labels they can't. Rewrite the title to say what the bars show and what it means for the visitor, such as "October was the wettest month this autumn: the lower paths may be muddy." That turns three blue shapes into a reason to read on.
3. **"Getting there" doesn't belong under this title, and it touches the chart (defect).** The heading and paragraph sit flush against the chart's right border with no gap. I checked this at full size. That makes them read as the chart's caption, so a visitor tries to connect bus times with rainfall. The block also falls under a title that doesn't describe it: either the title is wrong or the directions belong in their own section. Fix: give "Getting there" its own section under its own heading, separated by clearly more space than the space inside each block. If it must stay beside the chart, open a real gutter of 32 to 48 px and give the pair a title that covers both.
4. **The layout is crowded into the top-left corner (convention).** Everything sits in the top 60% and left 80% of the page. The bottom 300 px and the right edge are leftover space, not space anyone chose, so the page looks top-heavy and abandoned. Fix: once the content is sorted out, put the blocks on one grid with a consistent gutter and let the remaining space collect in one deliberate place.

**Smaller things.**
- The 2–3 px dark grey frame and tinted panel around the chart are as heavy as the data. Remove the frame and the tint so the bars are the darkest thing in the chart (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*).
