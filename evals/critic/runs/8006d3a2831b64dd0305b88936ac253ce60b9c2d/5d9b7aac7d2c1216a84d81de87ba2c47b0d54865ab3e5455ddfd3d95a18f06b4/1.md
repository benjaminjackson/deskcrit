**First read.** The eye goes straight to the red-bordered email field, then down its error line to the purple **Sign in** button. It reads at once as a sign-in screen for a product called Quillpost, caught mid-mistake. It feels calm, competent, and very familiar: a clean, standard SaaS login.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the email-validation error state of a desktop web sign-in, shown to returning users who mistyped their address.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is right, the error is visible and in words, and there's one clear action. What stands between it and good is the copy in the error message: it states a rule instead of pointing at what's wrong with *this* address. Two craft slips also need fixing: the sign-up link you can't see and a pale field border.

**What would move it most**
1. **The error message doesn't say what's wrong with what the user typed** (copy; defect). "Enter an email address in the format name@example.com" sits under "dana.ortiz@exmaple". The user has to compare the two strings and work out the difference themselves. Most people will see "example" next to "exmaple" and fix the typo, but the real failure is the missing ending (".com"). They may fix one, resubmit, and fail again. The message also reads like a form rule rather than help. The fix is to name the specific problem in the user's terms: "This address is missing its ending, like .com". Even better, when the domain looks like a typo, suggest the correction ("Did you mean …@example.com?"). This follows Nielsen heuristic 9: help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors.
2. **"Create an account" doesn't look like a link** (defect). In the bottom line, "New here? Create an account" is one grey run of text. Nothing separates the action from the question: no colour, no underline, no weight. A new visitor who lands here has to hover to find their way in. "Forgot your password?" sits two lines above in the brand purple, so the screen uses two different styles for the same kind of element (Nielsen 4: consistency and standards). Style "Create an account" like the other link, and underline both, so they don't depend on colour alone.

**Smaller things.**
- Password field border (and the email field border in its normal state): the pale grey looks to be around 2:1 on white, below WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 for component edges. Measure it, then darken it to at least 3:1.
- There's no show-password control on the password field. NN/g recommends offering one, because it cuts retype errors on sign-in, especially on the masked 12-character string seen here.

**What's working.**
- **The error state has the right weight.** A red border plus text directly under the field, with no icon clutter and no banner at the top, means the error is found in one glance and tied to its field. The meaning doesn't depend on colour alone. Keep this pattern.
- **Spacing does the grouping.** Each label sits tight to its field, the gaps between fields are larger, and the button has clear air above it. The form reads as label/field pairs without any dividers.
- **One unmistakable action.** The full-width purple button is the only saturated block on the page. With the logo as the only other use of that purple, the brand colour doubles as the "act here" signal.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus ring on the fields and button: check that it's visible and at least 2 px with 3:1 contrast, especially on the red-bordered field, where focus and error styling can collide.
- Whether the error is announced to screen readers (aria-describedby / live region) and whether the field uses `type="email"`.
