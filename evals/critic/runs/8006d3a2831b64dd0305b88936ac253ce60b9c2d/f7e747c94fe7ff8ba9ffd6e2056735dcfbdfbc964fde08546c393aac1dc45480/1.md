**First read.** The eye goes to "Account settings" and then straight to the Billing card, which is pushed about 28 px right of the other three cards and sticks out past the page's right margin. It reads as a calm, standard SaaS settings hub: neutral, tidy and trustworthy. The one off-line card spoils that, and so does a sidebar that says you're on Billing when the page is the overview of everything.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the overview page of account settings for a team workspace. Users come here to find one setting and jump to it quickly.

**Verdict.** The basics are sound: clear title, consistent cards, readable copy, sensible secondary buttons. But two problems make it look broken rather than finished. The Billing card is out of line, and the navigation disagrees with the page about where you are. Fix those two and this is a solid screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The Billing card sits off the column.** Its left edge is at about x 333 and its right edge at about x 1243. The other three cards run from about x 305 to x 1215, which is also where the header's "Workspace: Harbour Studio" ends. The vertical gaps between all four cards are even, so only this one card moved. A 28 px shift is too big to miss and too small to read as intentional. The viewer sees a layout bug, and on the card that deals with money that costs trust. It also makes Billing stand out without any reason. *Defect and convention:* every block should take its edges from one grid (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*), and a break should be clear enough to read as a choice (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The fix: put the card back on the same left and right edges as the others. If you do want Billing to stand out, say why with content, such as a renewal notice or a status line, not with an offset.
2. **The navigation and the page disagree.** The sidebar highlights "Billing", but the page is titled "Account settings" and shows four summary cards. Those cards don't match the sidebar either. "Sessions" has a card but no sidebar item, perhaps because it lives under "Security". "Members", "Integrations" and "Security" have sidebar items but no card. A first-time user can't tell whether they're on the Billing page, an overview page, or something that half-loaded. They also can't tell where "Sessions" lives, so the overview doesn't show them how settings are organised. *Defect:* Nielsen heuristic 1, visibility of system status (the current item and the page heading should name the same place), and Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on the conceptual model. The fix has two parts:
   - **Pick one place.** Either this is an overview page, with no sidebar item selected or with an "Overview" item that is selected. Or it is the Billing page, titled "Billing" and showing billing content.
   - **Match the names.** Make the cards and the sidebar list the same sections under the same names. Either add cards for Members, Integrations and Security, or rename "Sessions" to "Security", or show it inside the Security section.

**Smaller things.**
- **The Billing summary leaves out what matters most.** "Your plan renews on 1 March" gives no plan name and no amount, and "the address on file" doesn't say which address. Someone checking billing wants exactly those facts. Show the plan, the amount and the billing email right in the card so most visits end there. This is taste, argued as common sense: the overview should answer the likely question instead of sending the user to find it.

**What's working.**
- **Each card is built well.** A bold heading sits over one plain sentence, with a single outlined button on the right. The sentences speak to the user ("You are signed in on three now"), so each card explains why you'd open it. Keep that pattern.
- **The buttons are all equal secondaries.** On a hub where no action matters more than the others, that's the right call. Nothing pulls focus away from finding your section.
- **The page is quiet.** The palette is restrained: grey ground, white cards, one blue for actions and the selected state. The type steps are clear (title, card heading, body) and the text has good contrast. Nothing competes with the content.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Focus indicators** on the buttons and sidebar items. Check them in a live browser, because keyboard users depend on them on this kind of page.

What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 14 lines, 12 yes, 2 no (one grid; aligned exactly or clearly off)
