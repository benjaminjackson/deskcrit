**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", drops to the grey subtitle, then runs down the rows. It reads at once as a schedule for one route. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also plain: it could be any transit page.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a public timetable (web or print) for riders who need the next ferry from North landing on the day they travel.

**Verdict.** It's clean and easy to read, but it's laid out for the person who wrote the schedule, not the person catching the boat. The question every rider brings is "which boats run today?", and the table makes them work that out row by row. On top of that, nothing tells them whether the timetable is still valid.

**What would move it most**
1. **The day filter is in the last column, and the first column repeats what the times already say.** *Defect.* "Runs" is the column that decides whether a row applies to the rider. It sits at the far right, about 570 px from "Leaves north", in the same weight as everything else. A Saturday rider has to read across all seven rows and drop 12:40 and 17:10 in their head. A Tuesday rider has to notice that 19:30 doesn't exist for them. Meanwhile "Period" takes the most prominent column to say that 06:40 is morning. Every element should support the main task (Nielsen 8: aesthetic and minimalist design), and this one takes the best position while telling the rider nothing new.
   - **Fix:** organise the table by the day the rider is choosing. Either split it into Weekdays and Weekends tables, or give each departure a tick under Mon–Fri / Sat–Sun columns. Then drop Period, or turn it into a light group label. "Daily" is the normal case; only the exceptions need marking.
2. **"Spring season" doesn't say when it starts or ends.** *Defect (common sense; no catalogue entry).* Dates are exactly what a rider acts on. Read "spring season" as a stranger would: it could mean March to May, the operator's own season dates, or "until we print the summer one". If the rider guesses wrong, they stand on a landing for a boat that no longer runs at that time.
   - **Fix:** state the exact date range in the subtitle, e.g. "Valid 1 March – 31 May 2026".

**Smaller things.** None beyond the two above. Some copy is clipped: "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" drop the place names the subtitle uses ("North landing", "market quay"). "Leaves North landing" / "Arrives market quay" would cost almost nothing in width.

**What's working.**
- **Restrained ruling.** The pale hairlines between rows, with a slightly heavier rule under the header, do what Lupton asks of tables (*Thinking with Type*): they guide the eye along each row without boxing the times in. Keep them that pale.
- **A plain hierarchy.** There are three sizes, each with an obvious job: the bold title, bold column headers at body size, and a smaller grey subtitle. Headers differ by weight, not size, which is the right call. Don't add sizes when you restructure.
- **Times in 24-hour format.** Consistent zero-padded times (06:40, not 6:40 am) read cleanly down a column.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the times use tabular figures: 11:00 looks a little narrower than 06:40, so check the font's tabular-numbers setting. Also how the four widely spaced columns reflow on a phone, where most riders will check this.
