**First read.** The eye goes to the heading "Garden show gallery", then down to the big terracotta block and "Rose arch". From there it moves along the smaller tiles. It reads as a tidy, well-built gallery page, but every picture is a flat colour field with the same pale circle cut off at the bottom. It looks like a wireframe with the photos missing, and nothing in it is a garden.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page showing the highlights of a garden show to visitors or would-be visitors, with the photos as the content.

**Verdict.** The layout is good, but the page doesn't do its job: a gallery with no pictures shows nothing. The placeholder images are the one thing in the way. The generic title comes a distant second.

**What would move it most**
1. **The images are placeholders, so the gallery shows nothing.** All six tiles, the Rose arch feature included, are a solid colour with the same centred circle clipped by the bottom edge. Viewers come to a gallery to see roses on an arch and beans on canes. Here they get six coloured blocks and have to imagine the garden from the captions. This is a defect, and the checklist has no reference entry for it, so it's my common-sense call. It's fine only if real photos are already waiting to go in. In that case, judge the page again with them in place, because the saturated blocks now drive the page's colour and mood, and photos will change both. Choose the Rose arch photo to earn the big slot: it needs one clear subject that reads at that size.
2. **The title names a type of page, not this show.** "Garden show gallery" could head any garden show's page. A visitor can't tell which show, where, or which year. The captions are the opposite: "over the east gate" and "Tomatoes in late July" are specific and believable. Bring the title up to that level by naming the show and the year, for example "[Show name] 2026: what's growing". That also gives the large heading something to say. This is about the copy, so it's my judgment, not a rule.

**Smaller things.**
- Captions sit about 8–10 px below their image and only about 15 px above the next row. "Climbing roses over the east gate." floats about halfway between the rose image and the bean row. Make the gap between rows about twice the gap between an image and its caption, so each caption clearly belongs to its own picture (Arnheim on grouping by nearness).
- Every gap on the page is about the same 14–15 px, so the grid reads as packed tight, while about 85 px sits unused below the last row. Gather the space: open up the gaps between rows and let the page breathe at the bottom on purpose (Ruder, *Typography*, "Contrasts"). This one is taste.

**What's working.**
- The grid is solid. There are three equal columns with matching 14 px gutters, the feature image spans exactly two of them, and the 60 px side margins match. Keep this when the photos go in.
- The type has clear levels: roughly 60 px for the page title, 40 for the feature title, 18 for the feature caption, then 14 px bold names over 13 px grey descriptions. The two small sizes are close, but bold against grey keeps them apart. Each level has an obvious job.
- The captions are concrete and short, and they place each scene in the garden or the season. Keep that voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles open a larger view, and how the 2+1 layout reflows on a phone.
