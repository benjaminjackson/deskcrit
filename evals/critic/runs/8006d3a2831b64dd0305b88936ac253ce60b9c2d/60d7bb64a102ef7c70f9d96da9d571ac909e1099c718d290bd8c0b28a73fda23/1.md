**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours", then runs down one column of nine evenly spaced lines. The bold branch names are the only break in that rhythm. It reads at once as a plain, calm notice for a small public library. But it reads as one long list, not as three branches each with its own hours.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web page where local residents check when a given branch is open, or where and when to find the mobile library. Most will scan for one branch and one day.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The page is clean, readable and honest. Two things stop it working at a glance: the spacing doesn't show which lines belong to which branch, and the mobile library's entry leaves out the one thing a visitor needs, which is what time to turn up.

**What would move it most**
1. **The mobile library has no hours.** *(Defect.)* "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" tell you where and which day, but not when. Someone who wants to catch the van has to guess whether that means 9 in the morning or 3:30 when school lets out, and a wrong guess means a missed visit. This is the information people act on, so a gap here costs more than any layout problem. Add a time range to each line, in the same form as the branch hours.
2. **Even spacing hides the three groups.** *(Defect.)* Every line looks about 45 px from the next, headings included. So "Harbour branch" sits as close to "Saturday, ten to four" above it, which belongs to the main branch, as to its own Tuesday line below. "Mobile library" floats the same way. Only the bold weight tells you where a branch starts, and a reader scanning for "Saturday" will find two Saturday lines with nothing nearby to show which branch each one is. Spacing that shows grouping is the proximity rule (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II; Wertheimer). Bringhurst's examples of headings put more space above than below, which is permitted rather than required (*Elements of Typographic Style* 2.2.2). The fix: tighten the gap between a branch's lines to roughly normal line spacing, keep about the current gap or more above each branch name, and pull each name tight to its first line.
3. **Times written in words are slow to scan and don't say morning or evening.** *(Taste, plus common sense.)* "nine to eight", "ten to six" and "ten to two" read like speech. People checking hours scan for numbers, and "ten to two" can also be read as 1:50. Write them as figures, for example "9am–8pm". Also consider stating the days each branch is closed, such as Sunday, and Monday at Harbour, so nobody has to work it out from what's missing. Setting days and times in two aligned columns would make Saturdays easy to compare across branches. That part is taste, but it suits a timetable.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing beyond the above.

**What's working.**
- There's one clear title and the branch names are set apart by weight, not by a third size. That gives a tight scale of three sizes with an obvious job each (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger). Keep it.
- High-contrast dark text on white, set at a comfortable body size in a plain sans. It's easy to read for any visitor, including older ones with weaker sight.
- No decoration. Nothing competes with the information, which is right for a notice like this.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column behaves on a phone. It should hold up, but check that the heading spacing from point 2 stays tighter than the gaps between groups at narrow widths.
