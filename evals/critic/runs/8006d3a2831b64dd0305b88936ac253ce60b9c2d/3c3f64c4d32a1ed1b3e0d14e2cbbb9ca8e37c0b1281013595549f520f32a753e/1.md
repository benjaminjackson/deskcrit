**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "Notifications" title, then goes down the four bold row labels, with the two blue "on" switches pulling it right. It reads straight away as an email-notification settings page in a SaaS work tool: calm and orderly. The two versions are identical except for one thing. In version 2 the description under each setting is a much paler grey, and you have to squint to read the sentences.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a signed-in user scans the four email types, understands what each one sends and when, and switches them on or off.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. In version 2 the descriptions are faint, and they hold the information the user needs to make each choice. Version 1 keeps them readable and still clearly secondary, so it gives up nothing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The descriptions in version 2 look too faint (defect).** The grey lines under each label, such as "A short email every Monday…" and "…sent at 8 am in your time zone", look to be around 3:1 against white. That is below the 4.5:1 WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 asks for at this small size. These lines are not decoration. They carry the timing and frequency facts ("every Monday", "within an hour", "8 am", "no more than one a month") that decide whether a user turns a setting on. Making them faint pushes exactly that content toward unreadable for low-vision users and on poor screens. The step down in hierarchy was already done well in version 1: the descriptions are smaller, regular weight and mid-grey under a bold, dark label. Version 2 adds a second, unnecessary step by lightening them further. Fix: darken the text until it reaches at least 4.5:1, which is roughly where version 1 already is. Measure version 1's grey to confirm it passes.

**Smaller things** (both versions)
- The "off" switches (Due dates, Product news) are a pale grey track on white. That looks well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for control edges, so the control itself almost disappears when off. Darken the off track or give it a visible outline.

**What's working** (both versions)
- The intro line "Account and security emails are always sent" answers the obvious worry before anyone has to ask it.
- Each description says what arrives and when, in the user's own words. A reader can decide without guessing.
- The hierarchy is clean: large bold title, then bold row labels, then smaller descriptions, with dividing lines that keep each switch with its row.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The switches' focus and hover states, and whether the page reflows on narrow screens.
