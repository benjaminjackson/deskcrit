**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "About the trust" and drops into a single grey-black slab of text. It reads as the About section of a small local charity's website: calm, plain and trustworthy. Nothing tells you the paragraph is also asking you to do something.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an About section on a local footpath trust's website. It tells neighbours what the trust is, and it should also get some of them to turn up for a work day.

**Verdict.** It's clean, well set and very readable. But it hides its only call to action in the second half of one undivided paragraph, under a heading that promises history. On top of that, the invitation is missing what someone needs before they can come: which Saturdays, what time, and where.

**What would move it most**
1. **The volunteer invitation is buried under the wrong heading.** It starts with "Work days run every second Saturday…" on line 6. A reader who skims "About the trust" sees founding dates and member counts and stops before the one part that asks them to take part. Split the block in two. Keep the trust's story under "About the trust", then give the work days their own heading that says what's on offer (e.g. "Join a work day"). *Defect* (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web": people scan headings and the first lines of paragraphs; one long block gets skipped). The heading problem is the same issue: once the text is split, each heading names what's under it.
2. **"Every second Saturday" can be read two ways, and it's the line people act on.** It could mean every other Saturday, or the second Saturday of each month. Someone who picks the wrong one turns up to an empty car park. The invitation also gives no start time, no meeting point and no way to say you're coming. "Join for the morning" doesn't say when the morning starts. State the schedule without ambiguity ("the second Saturday of every month, 9am to 12"), name the meeting place, and add one specific next step, such as "Next work day: Saturday 12 December, meet at the quarry car park" with a link to sign up or a contact. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.** None worth raising. The setting is careful: lines run about 60 to 65 characters, the leading suits the measure, the rag is even, and the contrast is strong.
