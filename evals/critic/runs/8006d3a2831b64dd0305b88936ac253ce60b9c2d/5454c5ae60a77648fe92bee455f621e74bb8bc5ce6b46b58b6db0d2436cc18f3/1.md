**First read.** The eye lands on the leaf pattern, which is loud, bright and busy. The white headline is somewhere inside it, and I had to look again to read "small" and "spaces". Next the eye drops to the three tidy cards, then stops because there's nowhere to go. It reads as a friendly local garden centre's home page, pleasant but generic.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page of a local garden centre. Its job is to tell nearby people with shady or small gardens that the centre has plants for them, and to get them to visit, book a workshop or order.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do the job yet. The headline is the best thing on the page, but it's lost in a pattern that's busier and brighter than it is. Once someone has read it, the page gives them nothing to do.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline can't hold its own against the pattern (defect).** The white bold sans sits on a field of pale yellow and light green leaves. Over "small" and "spaces", much of the background is nearly as light as the letters, so the words break up and need a second read. Behind the type, the contrast is probably well under 3:1, which is the floor for large text (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure it against the palest leaves under "small". The pattern also has more value contrast and more edges than anything else on the screen, so it becomes the focal point and the message comes second. Fix: give the headline a calm field to sit on. Use a dark, flat area or a strong dark scrim behind the type, or quiet the pattern a lot (fewer colours, much less contrast) so it reads as texture, not subject.
2. **The picture doesn't say what the headline says (defect/taste).** The headline promises "shade and small spaces", but the image shows sunny yellow-green foliage with no sense of shade, scale, pots or a balcony. It could sit on any garden page. Right now the image only decorates, so the viewer gets no proof that this centre knows their shady courtyard. Fix: show the promise. Use real ferns and hostas in deep shade, or a planted balcony corner, so image and headline make one idea.
3. **There's no next step (defect).** The page has no button or link in the body. "Ready to pot this weekend" builds intent and then drops it. The workshops card says "ten places in each class", a scarcity cue with no way to book. The hours card has no address or map even though "Visit" is in the nav. Fix: add one primary action that matches the headline, like "See shade plants in stock" or "Plan your visit this weekend". Make the workshop card link to booking, and put the address next to the hours.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline starts about 16 px to the right of the left edge shared by the logo, intro and cards. That's a near-miss. Align it to 56 or offset it clearly.
- The headline breaks as "…and small / spaces", which splits the key phrase and leaves one word on the second line. Break after "for" ("Plants for / shade and small spaces") or widen the box.
- The hours card breaks "8 / pm" across lines. Keep the number with its unit (non-breaking space), or write "8 am–6 pm".
- The saturated hero covers about half the first screen, so the calm, useful content below looks like an afterthought. Quieting the pattern (point 1) fixes this too.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and human. "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, grown at our nursery and ready to pot this weekend" tells a stranger exactly what, from where and when. The delivery card ("free within ten miles on orders over $60") has no ambiguity. Keep this voice.
- The three cards are clean and even. Each has a bold heading and two short lines with a comfortable measure, and they're easy to scan. The info belongs there.
- The green serif wordmark against the plain sans sets a calm, crafted tone the pattern then shouts over. Lean on that restraint.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are links and how they respond to hover and focus. How the hero and headline reflow on a phone, where the headline would sit over even more pattern.
