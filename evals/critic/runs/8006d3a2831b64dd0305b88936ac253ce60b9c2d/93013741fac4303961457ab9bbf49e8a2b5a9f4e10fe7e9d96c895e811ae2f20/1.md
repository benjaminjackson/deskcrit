**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. Version 2's only change is to centre the column, and that makes the two body paragraphs harder to read and leaves the page with two unrelated alignments. **What would move it most**
1. **Centring is the regression in version 2 (convention).** In version 2 the four-line paragraph ("Over eight Wednesday evenings…") and the two-line one ("Clay, glazes, and two firings…") are centred. Every line starts at a different place, so the eye has to hunt for each line start. The first paragraph also ends on a short orphaned line, "at your own wheel." Centring suits a few short lines, not running paragraphs (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3). The centred axis also clashes with the logo, which stays on the left edge at the top. Version 1 puts every line, the price box and the button on that same left edge, so the page reads as one column from the logo down to the button. Fix: keep version 1's flush-left setting. If you want a centred hero, centre only the label and headline and keep the paragraphs and box flush left.
2. **There's nothing to look at, in both versions (taste).** This course sells a hands-on, physical result: "turn those cylinders into mugs and bowls." A beginner deciding whether to spend $320 wants to see what they'll make and where they'll make it. The empty right half of version 1 is the natural place for a photo of student pots or a wheel in use. Without one the page could belong to any course, and nothing sets this studio apart. If an image sits further down the page or failed to load, check that. Otherwise add one.
3. **"Eight places" can be read two ways (defect).** It sits right above the booking button, so readers will act on it. It could mean the class holds eight people, or that eight places are left. A reader who takes the second meaning may expect more room than there is, or feel rushed when there's no reason to. Fix: say "Class of eight" or "8 of 8 places left", whichever is true.

**Smaller things.**
- In version 1 the price "$320" is the same size and weight as nothing else on the page but is only slightly bigger than the details under it. "$320 for all eight weeks" would answer "per session or total?" without making the reader rebuild it from the paragraph above.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean. There are four clear levels: a small orange label, a large serif headline, serif body text, and sans-serif details in the box. Each level differs in face, weight or colour as well as size, so nothing collides.
- The tinted box holds exactly the facts you need to decide (price, day and time, start date, size) and ends with the one action. It's a tidy decision point with one primary button.
- The copy is specific and written in "you": it says what you'll learn, what's included, and that you take home what you make. That's far better than generic course copy.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states, and how the layout reflows on a phone. In particular, check whether version 1's right-hand space gets used or the column just narrows.
