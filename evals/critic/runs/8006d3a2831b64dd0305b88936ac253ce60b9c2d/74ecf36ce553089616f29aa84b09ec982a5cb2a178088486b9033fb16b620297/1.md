**First read.** The eye goes first to the three big numbers along the top, especially 184,220. Then it drops to the blue line, which rises steadily, and the orange line, which zigzags. It reads straight away as a marketing analytics overview for a growth or marketing team. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. It also looks like a hundred other SaaS dashboards.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing screen of a web analytics tool. A marketer should be able to see in a few seconds how traffic and sign-ups are doing over the last 12 weeks, and which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** It's clean and well built, and the top half does its job. The chart doesn't connect to the numbers above it, though. It has no unit or period, the lines don't seem to add up to the Visits total, and it never says what it shows. So the half of the screen that should explain the KPIs leaves the viewer to work that out alone.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart and the Visits card don't reconcile.** The y-axis reads 0 to 8k, with no unit and no period. Each point looks like one week (12 points, Jul 7 to Sep 22), but nothing says so. If the points are weekly, adding the three lines by eye gives roughly 135–140k visits. The card says 184,220. A careful viewer notices the gap of about 45k and either stops trusting the screen or concludes that a channel (Direct? Referral?) is missing without being named. Either way, the chart now raises doubts about the headline number instead of explaining it. **Defect.** Tufte (ch. 2) and Few: never make the reader guess what a number measures. Fix: name the unit and period ("Weekly visits") and make the parts add up to the whole. Show every channel, or add a grey "Other" line, or say "Top 3 channels" in the title. If my weekly reading is wrong, the missing label is the reason it was possible to misread.
2. **The chart names a topic, not a finding, and it only covers visits.** "Visits by channel" leaves the viewer to spot for themselves that Search drives most of the growth (~5k to ~6.8k a week), that Social is noisy, and that Email is small but steady. Also, two of the three headline metrics (sign-ups and conversion) get no channel breakdown at all. Yet "which channel brings people who sign up" is usually the question this screen exists to answer. **Taste / common sense:** the screen reports numbers but doesn't help the viewer decide anything. Fix: write the takeaway in the title or a one-line caption ("Search drove most of the growth"). Consider letting the chart switch to sign-ups or conversion by channel, or add sign-ups per channel next to it.

**Smaller things.**
- Legend above the plot: the viewer has to look back and forth to match colours to lines. The lines never cross, so put "Search / Social / Email" at the right end of each line and drop the legend (Tufte; Wilke).
- "vs previous 12 weeks" is repeated under all three numbers, and it also repeats the header's "Last 12 weeks". Say it once, e.g. "vs previous period" beside the period at the top, and let each delta be just "+6.1%".
- The type sizes crowd each other. I count about seven: brand ~18, "Overview" ~24, KPI numbers ~28, chart title ~17, labels and legend ~14, deltas ~13, axis ~11. "Overview" and the KPI numbers are close, and so are the brand and the chart title. Keep about four sizes and separate the rest by weight and colour (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- Green means "up/good" in the deltas and also means "Email" in the chart. That's a small clash of meanings. Pick a non-green hue for Email (taste).
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) looks like plain text. If it's the period picker, it needs a visible control: a border, a chevron or a button style. If it's only a label, this is fine.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards: a quiet label, a big number and a comparison with a sign. They read in a second, and the comparison lets each number be judged instead of taken on trust. Keep that structure.
- The chart is restrained: a line chart for change over time, faint gridlines, no chartjunk, a sensible y-axis starting at zero, and lines that never tangle. Its looks aren't the problem.
- The grid holds: the three cards and the chart panel share edges and gutters, so the page reads as one ordered thing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and whether the chart has hover tooltips that give exact weekly values. If it does, that partly offsets the missing unit, but the label is still needed.
