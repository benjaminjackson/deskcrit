**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big white headline, then the yellow "Get tickets" button, then back up to the small yellow date and venue line. It reads as the landing page for a small, thoughtful design and engineering conference in Edinburgh. It feels calm and confident. In version 1 the headline's two lines run into each other, so for a moment it reads as one dense block. In version 2 the lines are apart and it reads cleanly.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the hero of a conference site. It should tell designers and engineers what Fieldwork 2027 is, when and where it happens, and get them to buy a ticket.

**Verdict.** Version 2 is better. It is the only one that does its job cleanly, and the one change is the headline's line spacing. In version 1, the line that sells the event is the one part of the page that looks broken.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1's headline lines touch (defect, common sense).**
   - **Where:** In "Three days on building / quieter, slower software", the descenders of "y" and "g" in line 1 meet the tops of "l", "t" and "f" in line 2.
   - **What the viewer gets:** The two lines read as one knotted block, so the viewer has to untangle the event's pitch. That is the opposite of the "quieter, slower" calm the copy promises, and it looks like a CSS bug, not a choice.
   - **Fix:** Version 2's spacing (roughly 1.1× the type size) is right: still tight enough to hold together as one heading (Bringhurst on display type), and the letters no longer touch.
2. **There's no price near the button (defect, applies to both versions).**
   - **What the viewer gets:** "Get tickets" is a clear ask, but the viewer can't tell what it will cost before clicking, and price decides whether they act.
   - **Fix:** Add the price, or "from £X", in the date line or right next to the button.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav items are separated only by middots in plain light text, so they read more like a sentence than four links. Give them more space and drop the dots.
- The bottom third of the screen is empty, which is fine if the next section starts just below. If not, the hero should sit lower on the screen or be taller.

**What's working.**
- There is one clear focal point. The headline is roughly three times the size of anything else, and the page uses only four type sizes, each with a distinct job.
- Yellow appears only on the date line and the button, so the two facts a buyer needs (when and where, and how to buy) are tied together by colour.
- The copy is specific. "Quieter, slower software" and "tools that others rely on every day" tell the viewer who this is for and how it differs, and generic event pages rarely manage that.
