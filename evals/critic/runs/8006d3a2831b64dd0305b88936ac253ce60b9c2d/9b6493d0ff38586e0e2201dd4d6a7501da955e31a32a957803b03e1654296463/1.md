**First read.** The eye lands on "Crossing the river", then jumps right to "Good to know", whose heading and body text are larger than the main column's. It reads as a plain, calm passenger-information page for a small local ferry. It's tidy and quiet, and it could be any template. Nothing about it says river or boat.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you didn't say: a guide page that tells a passenger on the north landing to market quay route when they can travel and what they can bring, so they turn up at the right time.

**Verdict.** It's clean and easy to read, but it fails at its main job. A passenger finishes the page without a single time they could plan around. The second problem is the sidebar card: it's set larger than the main text, so it pulls attention away from the guide it's supposed to support.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable section gives no times (defect).** "When the boats run" says "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", that winter has "one boat each hour", and that "the last crossing leaves before dusk." A passenger will act on these lines, and each one can be read more than one way. "Early morning" could mean 6:00 or 8:00. "Winter" gives no months. "Before dusk" moves by an hour or more across the season. Someone who reads "late evening" and arrives at 20:30 in November finds the landing empty. The fix is to give the first and last departures as clock times, name the winter months, and give the winter last boat as a time. If the times change too often to print here, say so in one line and link straight to the current timetable. Then the button in the card becomes the answer to a gap in the page, not a side route.
2. **The sidebar card outranks the main column (defect, against Dair's rule that contrast must be obvious and Bringhurst's call for a deliberate size scale).** "Good to know" (about 36 px) is larger than the section headings "When the boats run" and "Bicycles and luggage" (about 28 px). The card's body text (about 20 px) is also larger than the main body text (about 17–18 px). So the eye goes to the side note before the content, and the page has two near-equal body sizes and two near-equal heading sizes. I count about seven sizes: 64 / 36 / 28 / 24 / 20 / 18 / 16, and 36–28 and 20–18 collide. A tighter scale would keep four: the title, one heading size shared by the card and the sections, one body size, and the small nav/button size. Set the card at or below the main column's sizes, and let its border and position mark it as an aside.
3. **"Good to know" names nothing (defect, WCAG 2.4.6).** The card is about where to find printed timetables and weekly changes, but its heading could sit on any card on any site. Someone scanning headings won't stop there. A heading that names the content, such as "Printed timetables and changes", lets them find it.

**Smaller things.**
- Card column: at that size, its lines run about 27 characters, well under Bringhurst's 40–50 for side columns, so the paragraph breaks into choppy lines. This goes away once the card text drops to body size.
- Alignment: the card's top (about y 147) sits just under the H1's cap height (about y 137), and its right edge (about x 1150) stops short of the nav's right edge (about x 1184). Both look like near-misses, not choices (Arnheim). Align the card's top with the H1 and its right edge with "Landings".
- Baselines: the card's line spacing (about 30 px) doesn't match the main body's (about 27 px), so lines across the gutter don't sit level (Müller-Brockmann). This is minor on a web page, and it resolves once the card uses the body size and spacing.

**What's working.**
- The H1 is clearly in charge: it's large, tightly set and heavy, with a quieter grey subhead under it. The eye knows where to start.
- Each section heading sits close to its own paragraph, with clear space above it, so the two topics read as separate, scannable chunks.
- The palette is restrained: neutrals plus one blue, used only on the single primary button. That makes "See the timetable" the obvious action. Keep the blue rare.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states for the nav links and the button. How the card stacks on a narrow screen: if it ends up above the guide, the size problem in finding 2 gets worse.
