**First read.** The serif headline "Hold a real conversation in Italian by summer" lands first in both, then the chat panel on the right. In version 1 the eye then drops to three identical orange buttons and stops: they're equally loud, so it reads them one by one to choose. In version 2 it goes straight to "Start your first lesson" and the two text links read as side doors. Both feel warm, calm and trustworthy. The page is a language-course landing page for adult beginners.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a landing hero that gets adults who want conversational Italian to start a first lesson.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. It makes the one action that matters the only button, and that's the only change between the two. Both still waste the chat panel. It shows an ordinary exchange, not the thing that makes Parla different (a teacher correcting you mid-sentence), and its bottom third is empty.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1 gives three actions equal weight (defect, fixed in version 2).** "Start your first lesson", "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" have the same orange fill, size and weight. The visitor has to weigh three equal options before acting, and gifting a course, a side path, gets as much pull as starting. That breaks the primary-versus-secondary convention (Refactoring UI; Nielsen Norman Group). Version 2 fixes this the usual way: one filled button and the rest as underlined links. Keep that.
2. **The chat panel repeats the headline when it could prove it (taste, but it matters).** Three bubbles of polite small talk only say "you'll have a conversation", which the headline already says. The subhead's real promise, "a teacher… who corrects what you say as you say it", is the thing nobody else offers, and the panel never shows it. Put a correction in the exchange: the learner makes a common mistake and the teacher's reply fixes it inline. That would let visitors judge the value for themselves, against the textbook or app they've already tried, instead of taking it on trust. It would also fill the empty space, and the gap is the next point.
3. **The bottom third of the panel is empty (defect unless it's animation).** The bubbles end around y 408 and the panel runs to 556, so about 150 px of tinted box holds nothing, and the panel looks unfinished. If the chat types in, this may be a mid-animation capture (see below). If not, fill it with the corrected turn from point 2, or shorten the panel to fit its content.

**Smaller things.**
- White labels on the orange buttons look about 3.6:1. That's below the 4.5:1 WCAG 1.4.3 asks for at this size (about 16 px bold). Darken the orange or enlarge the label, and measure it.
- "By summer" is a promise with a deadline the visitor will remember. In November it could mean next summer or "within a few months". Name the timeframe, such as "in six months" or "by June", or tie it to the course length.
- In the chat, which speaker is the teacher and which is you is shown only by bubble tint and side. A small name or "Teacher" label would make the roles obvious. This is common sense, not a cited rule.

**What's working.**
- In version 2, the single filled button plus underlined text links. The secondary actions are still findable, and the links are marked by more than colour, but they no longer compete.
- The headline. It's specific, it's an outcome rather than a feature, and the heavy serif against the sans body gives a clear two-voice hierarchy with no muddled sizes (roughly four levels: headline, subhead, button and nav, logo).
- The subhead. It says who does what and when ("Fifteen minutes a day… then a weekly video call with a teacher in Bologna"), so the method is clear in one read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the chat panel animates in, which would explain the empty lower third. Check it in a live browser after the animation finishes.
