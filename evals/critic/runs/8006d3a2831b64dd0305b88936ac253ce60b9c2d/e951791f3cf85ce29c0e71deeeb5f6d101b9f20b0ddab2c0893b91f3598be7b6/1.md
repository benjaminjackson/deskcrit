**First read.** The eye lands on the bold sans headline, "The river ferry through the seasons", then drops into three narrow serif columns. Before you read a word you see the holes: the columns look speckled, with wide white gaps between words on line after line. It reads as an informational web page or leaflet about a local ferry service. It feels calm and orderly from a distance, and blotchy up close.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a short public information piece telling ferry passengers how the service runs (route, frequency, bikes, winter timetable), read on screen by ordinary passengers.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the text setting is not. Justified type in columns this narrow tears the word spacing apart, so the one thing the page exists for, reading three paragraphs, is harder than it should be. The headline also promises "the seasons" and the page only covers winter.

**What would move it most**
1. **Justified text in narrow columns opens holes between the words in all three columns (defect).** Each column holds only about 25–35 characters per line. That is below the 40–50 Bringhurst gives for multi-column text, and well below 45–75 for a single column. Justifying lines that short forces the spare space into the gaps between words: "minutes    between    early", "weather,    and    a    little", "Riders    wheel    them    on", "bicycle    during    the", "In    winter    the    timetable", "each    landing,    and    the". The reader's eye stops at each hole, the even grey of the column breaks up, and on some lines the gaps nearly stack into vertical streaks. Bringhurst calls this "white acne" on short justified lines. Hochuli says that below about 50 characters "even wordspacing becomes difficult, if not impossible." Fix: set the columns flush left, ragged right, which on its own removes the holes. Also widen the measure, because the page has room. The three columns use only about 890 of 1280 px, so either spread them across the full width or go to two columns of about 45–55 characters each. If justification is a must, it needs both a wider measure and hyphenation turned on.

2. **The headline promises more than the page gives (defect, WCAG 2.4.6 Headings and labels).** "Through the seasons" tells the reader to expect how the service changes over the year. Only the third column mentions a season (winter). The first two cover the route, crossing times, and bikes. A passenger looking for summer hours or the spring tides won't find them and will wonder whether something is missing. Fix: either write a heading for what is there ("How the river ferry runs", "Riding the river ferry") or add the missing seasons. If the three columns cover separate topics (route, bikes, winter), short column headings would let a passenger jump straight to the one they need.

**Smaller things.**
- The copy is plain and specific ("every forty minutes", "about twelve minutes", "no extra charge"), but the times with real consequences are vague. Saying "between early morning and late evening" and "before dusk" means passengers can't plan a last crossing. Give the actual first and last times, or point clearly to the printed timetable for them.

**What's working.**
- The two-face pairing: a bold sans headline over a readable book serif gives a clear contrast with only two sizes, so the hierarchy needs nothing more. Keep it.
- The three columns sit on one grid with equal gutters, the same top line, and shared baselines, so the page looks orderly from a distance. Keep that structure when you change the measure.
- The text is near-black on white at a comfortable body size, with generous line spacing for the measure.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the columns reflow on a phone. If this is a web page, check that they stack into one column rather than shrinking, which would make the justified-text holes worse.
