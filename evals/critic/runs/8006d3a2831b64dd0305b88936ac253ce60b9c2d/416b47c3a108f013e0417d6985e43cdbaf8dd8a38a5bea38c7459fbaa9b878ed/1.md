**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: this is the hero screen of a web page for a free one-day local festival. Its job is to get local people and visitors to come.

**Verdict.** The type and layout are well made and do their part: one clear focal point, a clean three-level scale, a calm palette. The piece still fails its job, because it leaves out the two things a person needs to turn up: where the festival is, and what to do next.

**What would move it most**
1. **There's no place. Defect.** "Harbour Lights" and "the old net shed" sound like a place, but no town, harbour or address appears anywhere on the screen. Someone who arrives from a shared link or a search can't tell if this is ten minutes away or three hundred miles. Locals might fill the gap; visitors, who most need it, can't. Put the place in the amber line, where the viewer already looks for the facts, for example "Saturday 14 June, from noon · [Town] Harbour". That line already does this job well; it just stops early.
2. **There's no ask. Defect.** The eye path ends in empty navy, and the bottom quarter of the screen is unused. Even a free event needs one specific next step: a map or directions link, the programme, or "add to calendar". Generic wording like "Find out more" or "Join us" doesn't count. The one that fits what the viewer is thinking after this copy is when the lantern boats go out and how to get to the quay. Place it under the body text on the same left edge, as the only button.
3. **The times don't answer the question the copy raises. Defect, in the facts the viewer acts on.** "From noon" is the only time given. But the copy sells the dusk moments: lantern boats, music, a fish supper. Someone planning their day will come for the lanterns and can't tell when that is or when the festival ends. On an event page, that's the time they hold you to. Add a time for the main event ("lantern boats from 9.30pm") or an end time. No year is shown either. That's fine on a page that only lives for one season, but it's a risk if the page stays up.
4. **The best thing about the event is described, not shown. Taste.** "Lantern boats on the water at dusk" is the image that would make someone come, and it only appears in small body text. A headline in serif italic over a navy background with an amber caps line is a common template, so with type alone this could be any heritage festival anywhere. One photo, or even a single warm light shape on the dark field, would make it this festival. The navy already stands in for the harbour at night, so the job is only to put the lights in. Keep the headline as the dominant element if you do.

- **The palette fits the subject.** Cream and amber on deep navy already suggest lamplight on dark water. The background is navy, not pure black, which keeps it soft.
