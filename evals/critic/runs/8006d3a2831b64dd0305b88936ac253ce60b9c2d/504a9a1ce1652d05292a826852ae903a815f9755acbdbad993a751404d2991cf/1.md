**First read.** The eye lands on "Walking routes" top left, drops to three equal bordered cards, and stops on the three blue "View route" buttons, which are the heaviest marks on the page. It reads as a list of local walks for someone choosing one. It feels clean but generic, and oddly unfinished: each card's bottom 40% is empty white inside its border, as if something failed to load.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a listing page that helps a walker pick one of three routes and open its details.

**Verdict.** It works as a basic list, but it does little to help anyone choose. The facts you'd choose a walk by are buried in prose, and the tall empty cards make the page look broken.

**What would move it most**
1. **The empty lower half of every card** (each box runs from y≈160 to 600, and the content ends at about y≈395). *Defect.* A viewer sees about 200 px of bordered nothing under each button, so the cards look like a map or photo is missing or the page hasn't finished loading. That is wasted space, and it is trapped inside the boxes. Ruder (*Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts") asks for empty space gathered into a few areas that do work. Here it is split into three dead pockets that give the eye no rest and no point. Fix: either shrink each card to fit its content, or fill the space with what it seems to be waiting for, such as a route map or a photo of the walk. If an image is meant to be there and didn't load, this finding goes away, so check the live page.
2. **You can't compare the routes at a glance** (body copy in all three cards). *Defect.* Distance, terrain, gradient and facilities are written as sentences with no common structure. Card 1 mentions climbs and a cafe, card 2 benches and shade, card 3 paving and bikes. To compare them, a reader has to read all three paragraphs and keep them in mind. The facts a walker decides by are distance, how long it takes, difficulty and surface. Pull those out into one short row of facts in the same order on every card (for example "6 km · about 2 hr · steep in places"), and keep one line of prose for the character of the walk. The lines also describe without speaking to the reader: "two steep climbs" doesn't say who it suits. Ending on the reader would help ("Two steep climbs, so not one for buggies").
3. **Nothing on the page feels like being outdoors.** *Taste.* Stock blue buttons, grey hairline borders and system sans make this look like any product's card grid. Nothing in the look tells you these are a cliff, a wood and a canal. One picture per card, or even a colour or icon that hints at each landscape, would make each route easy to recognise and the page worth stopping on. That same image is the natural thing to put in the empty space from finding 1.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1 body: "end." sits alone on the last line. Rewrap or reword so the last line holds more than one short word.
- The cards' split of the frame is arbitrary: they stop at about three quarters of the page height with no relation to their content. This is fixed along with finding 1.

**What's working.**
- The left edges line up. The title, the card titles, the body text and the buttons all hang from clear left edges, and the three cards sit on an even gutter, so the layout reads as one system.
- The type scale is short and clear: title, card title, body and button, each obviously different and done with size and weight, not extra faces.
- The single blue is kept for the actions only, so it's plain what you can click.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty card space is a picture or map that didn't load in the capture. Check in a live browser.
- How the three columns reflow on a phone.
