**First read.** The eye lands on "What we do each week", then drops into the serif body copy, then to the three green numbers. It reads as a local food bank's "about our work" page: calm, plain, trustworthy. In version 1 the two paragraphs are centred under a left-aligned headline and left-aligned stats, so the block looks pulled off its axis and each line starts in a different place. Version 2 reads as one clean column.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this tells donors, volunteers and people who might need help, in plain words, how the food bank works each week, so they trust it and pick one of the three paths in the nav.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is that the body paragraphs are now flush left instead of centred, and that fixes the one real problem version 1 had. The biggest thing still in version 2's way is a body line that runs a little long.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Centred paragraphs → flush left (defect in v1, fixed in v2).** In v1, two paragraphs of four lines each are centred. Every line starts at a new x-position, so after each line the reader has to search for where the next one begins. In paragraphs this long that adds up to real friction. The centred block also breaks the 96 px left edge that the headline and the stat row share. Its lines start at roughly 100 to 125 px, which looks like a near-miss rather than a choice, so the page has no single spine. Centring is for a few short lines. Paragraphs of several lines should be flush left (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3). V2 puts the headline, body and stats on one left edge, and the column reads in one pass.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **Body lines are long (convention).** Lines in both paragraphs look like about 80 to 85 characters, above the 45 to 75 range, ideally about 66 (Bringhurst). The generous leading helps, but in a column this wide the eye still has a long way back to the next line. Narrow the column to about 600 to 640 px or raise the body size slightly. The empty right third of the page can absorb either.

**Smaller things.**
- Stat row, "412 parcels last month": the number has no reference point (more or fewer than usual? how many households?). Add a comparison, for example "up from 350 in September" or "about 100 households a week", so the figure means something to a donor.

**What's working.**
- One left edge (in v2) shared by the headline, body and stats gives the page a clear spine and a calm, credible tone. Keep it.
- The copy is concrete: "Tuesday and Friday", "Carver Road", "three days of meals", "a driver will bring the parcel to your door". A stranger knows exactly who does what. The last sentence speaks straight to someone who can't get to a centre.
- The scale is clean: a bold sans headline, a serif body, then green stat numbers with small grey labels. Each level is easy to tell apart.
