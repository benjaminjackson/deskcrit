**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on "Get the weekly show list", drops through the one-line promise to the purple Subscribe button, and finds a second way in: read last week's list. Within two seconds it's clearly a newsletter sign-up for a local radio station. It feels calm and tidy. The two versions differ in only one thing: in version 2 the Subscribe and "Read last week's list" buttons have nearly square corners (about 2 px), while the email field and the card keep their rounder ~6 px corners. In version 1 all of them share the same radius.

**What it's for.** My assumption: get listeners of Riverside FM 97.4 to sign up for the Friday email of next week's schedule.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job well. Version 1 is slightly better because its corners follow one rule. Version 2 mixes radii in the form, so the field and the buttons below it look like they came from two different kits. The biggest problem in both is the same: the field border is too faint.

**What would move it most**
1. **The email field and secondary button outlines are too faint** (both versions). The field border and the "Read last week's list" outline look like a pale grey around #c8c8cc on white, which is well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for component edges. The field is the one thing a visitor must find and type into. Low-vision users and anyone on a washed-out phone screen will see a placeholder floating in white, not a box. Darken both borders until they reach 3:1 against white (something near #8a8a94), and check the value with a colour picker. *Defect / convention.*
2. **Mixed corner radii** (version 2 only). Square-cornered buttons sit straight under a rounded field inside a rounded card. Nothing is gained by the change. It just reads as an inconsistency, as if the buttons came from another component library (Nielsen 4, consistency and standards). Use one radius for every control, as version 1 does. *Convention, minor, but it's the whole difference between the two versions.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Subscribe" is the generic word. "Send me Friday's list" says exactly what the visitor gets and when, which the paragraph above already promises. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- "Read last week's list" is the best idea on the page. Visitors can judge the email from a real issue before they hand over an address, instead of taking the promise on trust. Keep it, and keep it secondary.
- The copy is concrete: every Friday, next week's programmes, guest names, schedule changes. It answers "what will I get, and how often" in one sentence.
- The hierarchy is clean. There's one heading, one paragraph, one labelled field, and one primary button. The visible "Email address" label above the field is correct, so the placeholder isn't doing the label's job.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The focus ring on the field and buttons, and whether the field uses an email input type. Check both in a browser.
