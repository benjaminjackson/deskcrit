**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then drops down the centre column to the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads as a landing page for a small local ferry. It feels calm, clean and trustworthy, and also generic. Then the last line, "The strong left alignment of this page gives it a calm, clear edge.", stops you, because it describes the page and it isn't true: everything is centred.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page of a local passenger ferry, meant to get residents and day-trippers to check sailing times and take the boat.

**Verdict.** The structure works: one clear headline, one clear action, nothing fighting for attention. It isn't ready to ship, though. A line that looks like a leftover design note sits at body size under the button, and the copy leaves out what a rider actually needs (times, fare, where to board).

**What would move it most**
1. **Remove the stray line at the bottom (defect, common sense).** "The strong left alignment of this page gives it a calm, clear edge" is set at the same size and colour as the body copy, so it reads as part of the pitch. To a rider it means nothing. Because it contradicts what they can see, it makes the page look unfinished and makes them doubt the rest. Delete it. If that slot is meant to close the page, use something a rider can act on, such as where the north landing is or how to pay.
2. **Swap the vague claims for the facts a rider will act on (defect).** "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" makes the viewer guess the first and last boats, and the page never shows a fare. Yet "Bicycles travel free" implies there is one. "The north landing" assumes the viewer already knows where that is. Times and prices are what people plan around and hold you to. Put the first and last sailings (or the next departure) and the fare in plain figures next to the button. The timetable link then confirms what the page has already told them, so they aren't sent off to dig.
3. **Make the page look like this ferry, not a template (taste).** Inter, one blue, and centred blocks on white could belong to any company. The headline's promise, a calm twelve-minute crossing with the harbour sliding past, is visual, and nothing on the page shows it. One photo of the boat or the bay would carry the "calm" more convincingly than the adjective does.

**Smaller things.**
- The three benefits (~18 px) are only a step smaller than the paragraph above them (~20 px), so they read like a second paragraph rather than a list. Either set them at the body size with a marker or icon, or make the difference clearly bigger.
- The headline breaks "twelve / calm minutes", splitting the number from its unit. Re-break it as "Cross the bay / in twelve calm minutes", or tighten the measure so the break falls at a phrase boundary (common sense).

**What's working.**
- One dominant headline in heavy weight, set tight. It's unmistakably the start, and its two lines hold together.
- One filled button with a concrete label. "See the timetable" says exactly what you'll get. Keep it the only primary action.
- The restrained palette: dark grey text, one blue shared by the wordmark and the button, all at comfortable contrast. It gives the calm the headline promises.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's focus and hover states, and how the centred column reflows on a phone.
