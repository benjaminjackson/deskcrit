**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif title, "Winter timetable at the harbour", then drops into two even columns of prose. I go looking for times and there aren't any, just paragraphs. Two thin grey rules sit under the columns with nothing after them. It feels calm and bookish, like a well-set leaflet, but it isn't a timetable.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a harbour or ferry information page. It should tell winter passengers when boats leave and what they need to know before they travel.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is good, but the page fails at its job. It's called a timetable and has no timetable. Its one section heading also names something the section doesn't contain. A passenger can't find a departure time or a fare here.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable is missing, and the times it does give are buried in sentences.** The only time facts are scattered through the left column: "seven in the morning", "an hour later than in summer", "every ninety minutes". The right column adds "twenty minutes" for a crossing. Nothing says when the first boat leaves, so "every ninety minutes" doesn't tell anyone when to turn up. "The first crossing" and "the first boat" both come up, and neither has a time. Someone who wants to know when they can sail has to read the whole column and still can't find out. *Defect.* Fix: give the departures their own block that a passenger can scan, ideally a table of sailing times, or at least the first sailing, the interval and the last sailing. Put the office hours on one short line as well. The two rules and the empty band under the columns look like a place where a table was meant to go (see "Couldn't judge").
2. **The heading doesn't match its section, and only one column has a heading.** "Fares and cards" sits over crossing times, bicycles, prams, wheelchairs and dogs. No fare appears anywhere, and the only mention of a card (season cards) is in the left column. Anyone looking for a price will go straight to that heading and come away with nothing, which wastes their time and misleads them (WCAG 2.4.6, Headings and labels). Because only the right column has a heading, its body text also starts about 37 px lower than the left column's, so the lines no longer line up across the gap (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). *Defect.* Fix: give each column a heading that says what it covers, such as "Opening hours and sailings" and "On board", set the same way so both columns start level. If "Fares and cards" stays, the section needs to list the fares.
3. **The lines that affect a trip don't tell the passenger what to do.** "An hour later than in summer" only helps people who know the summer hours. Better to just say the time. "The captain may skip a sailing, and notices are pinned to the board beside the gate" means you only find out about a cancellation once you're at the harbour. The sentence states a fact and stops short of what it means for the reader. Say where to check before leaving home, such as a phone line, a web page or a text alert, if one exists. *Defect* for a page people use to plan a journey.

**Smaller things.**
- The two grey rules at about two-thirds of the way down (y≈585) separate nothing from nothing. Remove them, or put the content they're meant to introduce beneath them.
- Some paragraphs end on a single word: "bags." in the right column's second paragraph and "thirty-five." in its first. Rebreak or edit so at least two words sit on each last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2).

**What's working.**
- **The text is easy to read.** The serif is sturdy, the body size is comfortable, lines run about 55–60 characters (inside Bringhurst's 45–75), and the line spacing is open. Keep all of this when you add the timetable.
- **The type scale is clear.** There are three levels: the title (about 40 px bold), section headings (about 24 px bold) and body text. They're far enough apart that each has an obvious job, and bold weight separates the headings from the text, not size alone.
- **The layout is calm and orderly.** Margins are generous, and both columns share one edge, one width and one gutter, so the page looks orderly at a glance.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The rules with empty space beneath them look like content that never rendered, possibly the timetable itself. Check the live page to see whether something should appear below the rules. If it does, finding 1 becomes a question of how prominent the timetable is: it should still sit above the prose, not under it.
