**First read.** The eye goes to the title "New sign-ups by channel", then the tall blue Organic search bar, then moves left to right through a row of six differently coloured bars. It reads as an internal growth report: clean, calm, competent. Only when the eye drifts right to the sidebar does it find the story. Organic search grew after a rewrite, and events money is moving to referrals. The chart doesn't show either point.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal analytics page that tells the growth team where Q3 sign-ups came from and backs a budget decision for Q4.

**Verdict.** It's a tidy, honest chart, but it doesn't make its own case. The two points that matter, the Q2-to-Q3 shift and the weak podcast and events channels, live only in sidebar prose. Meanwhile the chart spends six loud colours on bars that are already labelled.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't show the change the page is about.** The sidebar's main claim is that organic search went "up from a quarter in Q2" to a third. The chart shows Q3 alone, so the viewer has to take the growth on trust. They can't see whether Referral or Paid social fell to make room. Defect: Few names a bare figure with no earlier period as a pitfall ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Fix: put Q2 next to Q3 for each channel, either as a paler paired bar or a marker on each bar. Then the shift and the budget move can be seen, not just read.
2. **Six hues encode nothing.** Each bar is already named on the axis, yet each gets its own saturated colour: blue, orange, red, teal, green, purple. The rainbow doesn't help you read anything, and it pulls the eye evenly across all six bars instead of to the ones the page talks about. It also adds a false signal. Paid social in red and Podcast ads in green read as "bad" and "good", which the data doesn't say. Convention, and it costs something here (Few, pitfall 12; Wilke, ch. 19.1). Fix: draw the bars in one colour. Give an accent only to what the prose is about: Organic search, and perhaps Podcast ads and Events as a grey-red pair to show the cut.
3. **The title names the topic, not the finding.** "New sign-ups by channel" could sit above any quarter's chart. The point is in a 15 px sidebar the eye reaches last. Taste, argued on its own terms: on a page that exists to justify a decision, the heading is the only line everyone reads. Fix: make the title or subtitle the claim, something like "Organic search now brings a third of sign-ups". Keep the sidebar for the why and the Q4 budget move.

**Smaller things.**
- Sidebar: "The events budget moves to referrals in Q4" doesn't say who decided it or what it means for the reader. If this is a recommendation rather than a decision already made, say so.
- Sidebar: "fewer than 700 accounts" is vaguer than the chart. Say "670 accounts, 12% of the total" so the number matches the bars.

**What's working.**
- The bars are sorted by value, start at zero, and carry their exact value on top. The light gridlines stay out of the way. Keep all of that.
- The source line gives the period and the total (5,400), and the labels add up: 1,840 is a third of that total, and podcast plus events is 670. The numbers are trustworthy. That's rare, and worth keeping.
- The type scale is clean: three sizes plus a small detail size, with weight doing the finer work.
