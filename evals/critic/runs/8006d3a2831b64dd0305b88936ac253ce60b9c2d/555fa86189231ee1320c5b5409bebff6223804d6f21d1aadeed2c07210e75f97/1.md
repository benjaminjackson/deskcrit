**What would move it most (version 2 against version 1)**
1. **The three paragraphs are set in a brush script** (all three columns, about 5 lines each). This is a **defect**. In a script, letters run together and the shapes are irregular, so readers have to decode it word by word. Details like "eleven miles up the valley" and "fifty pence off your next scoop" are the points that sell this shop, and they get skimmed or skipped. Bringhurst (*Elements of Typographic Style*, 6.2.1) says to set paragraphs in a face made for reading and keep a script to a few words of display. Version 1 does exactly that, and it's the fix.
2. **Headings and body text are no longer told apart** (version 2, all three columns). This is a **defect**. "Our milk" and the paragraph under it are now the same face. The body is set darker and looks heavier than the heading, so the heading only stands out by being slightly bigger and pink. The levels blur, and the eye can't scan the three headings as a row of topics. In version 1 the change of face (script heading over a plain sans paragraph) does the work: heading and body differ in form, not only in size (Refactoring UI: contrast from weight and colour, not just size).
3. **The script is overused, so the brand accent turns into a novelty** (version 2). This is **taste**. In version 1 the script appears only in the logo and three two-word headings. Used that sparingly, it carries the handmade feel. Spread over every paragraph, it reads as a costume rather than a voice, and the plain sans hours bar now looks like it belongs to a different site.
