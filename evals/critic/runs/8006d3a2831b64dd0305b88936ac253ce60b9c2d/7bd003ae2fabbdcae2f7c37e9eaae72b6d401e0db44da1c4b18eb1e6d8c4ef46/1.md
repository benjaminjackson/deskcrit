**First read.** Version 1: the eye lands on "$184,320", then takes in the smaller tiles and the bars. It reads as an October sales summary for an online shop, calm and tidy. Version 2: the eye lands on the top-left tile only because it comes first. All seven figures have the same weight, so you scan them like a spreadsheet row, and the empty slot at the right of row 2 looks like a tile failed to load.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a monthly sales dashboard that lets the owner or a manager see in a few seconds how October is going compared with September.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The one real change, making revenue the dominant tile, tells the viewer where to start, and the 3-over-4 grid fills both rows with no hole. Both share the same weak chart: it has no labels or dates and sits in half its panel. That is now the biggest thing between version 1 and a good dashboard.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- In version 1, revenue gets a double-width card and a number about twice the size of the others. The headline figure is found before anything else is read (Refactoring UI; Müller-Brockmann on one element dominating). In version 2 all seven tiles are equal, so revenue competes with "Items per order 3.4" and the viewer has to read every tile to find the one that matters.
- Version 1's 3 + 4 split fills both rows. Version 2's 4 + 3 leaves an empty cell at the right of row 2. That reads as a missing metric or a broken load, and it makes the right side of the page light.

**What would move version 1 most**
1. **The weekly chart can't be read** (defect). It has no week labels, no values and no axis. Eight bars under a heading that says "October so far" leaves the viewer guessing what the eight weeks are, whether the last bar is a partial week, and how big the dip after week 7 is. Name the marks: put dates under the bars, add a value axis or a value on each bar, and mark the current partial week (Tufte; Wilke, label axes and units).
2. **The chart fills only the left half of its panel** (defect). The bars stop near x=680, leaving an empty white field across the right half, and the panel runs off the bottom of the first screen. The chart looks unfinished or truncated, and the page tips to the left. Either spread the bars across the full width or narrow the panel and put something useful beside it. Then fit the whole panel on the first screen, because a dashboard's key content should fit there (Few).
3. **Six of the seven changes have no reference point** (defect, on numbers people act on). Only revenue says "+12% on September". "+5%", "+9%" and "+0.2" could be against September, last year, or target. A viewer who assumes the wrong one draws the wrong conclusion. Put the comparison on each figure, or once under the "October so far" heading ("Changes are against the same days in September"). Also say whether a partial month is being compared with a full one: "October so far" compared with all of September would understate every growth figure.

**Smaller things.**
- The chart heading "Revenue by week" names the chart but not its finding. A heading like "Revenue up most weeks; this week below last" would give the takeaway in words.

**What's working.**
- Revenue as the clearly dominant figure, with all the other tiles on one quieter level. It gives a clear two-level order: keep it.
- Restrained colour: a neutral page, dark figures, and green used only for the deltas, consistently as "good" (including returns going down). The deltas also carry a sign, so the meaning doesn't depend on colour alone.
- A tight type scale: page title, one big number, KPI numbers, labels and deltas. Each size has a clear job.
