**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's a page that lists the stages of a design project in order, for a team to scan and follow.

**Verdict.** It mostly works because it is short, clear and well set. The one thing that undermines it is the misaligned "Sketches" card. It looks like a layout bug, or it suggests "Sketches" is a sub-step of Research, which the copy doesn't support. Either way the page looks unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Sketches" card is shifted off the column** (third card, roughly x=175 instead of x=160, with its right edge unchanged so it is also narrower). *Defect.* The offset is too small to read as a choice and too large to miss. The viewer either reads it as a careless error or as nesting under "Research" and wonders what the relationship is. Every other card shares one left edge, so this one breaks the pattern the page sets up and interrupts the steady downward read (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I: an in-between placement makes the eye waver; Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: repeated elements follow one program). Fix: put it back on the same left edge and width as the other four. If it really is meant as a sub-step, show that clearly: a much larger indent plus a cue such as a connector or a "Research →" label. A 14 px nudge won't do it.
2. **Five near-black 3 px borders do the work that space would do** (every card). *Convention, with a real cost.* Each item is already separated by its bold heading and an 18–20 px gap. The heavy outlines add nothing, they are the darkest thing on the page after the title, and they turn a simple sequence into a stack of compartments that compete with the text. Fix (approved direction, Lupton, *Thinking with Type*): remove the borders or replace them with space or a pale tone, so the lines stop competing with the content.
3. **The stages are in order, but nothing says so.** *Taste.* The copy reads as a process (agree scope → visit sites → sketch → pick one → hand over), yet the cards carry no numbers and the title "Project notes" suggests loose jottings. Someone scanning can't tell at a glance that order matters or where a given stage sits. Number the stages (1–5) and give the title the job, e.g. "Project stages". The words are already good. Each line has a verb and a concrete object ("Visit three sites", "Draw two rough directions"), so keep that.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific and active. Every description starts with a verb and names a countable output ("three sites", "two rough directions", "one direction"), so each stage tells you what "done" looks like.
- The type scale is clean: three clear levels (title about 32 px bold, card heading about 20 px bold, body about 16 px regular) in one family, with no sizes that nearly collide.
- The restraint suits the job: one neutral palette, strong contrast and short lines. Nothing distracts from the content.
