**First read.** The eye goes straight to the two-line headline, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then drops into the first paragraph. Within a second it reads as a help-centre article for a SaaS billing product, written for an account owner or admin. It feels calm, clean and trustworthy: one face, one accent colour, plenty of room. The trouble starts when you read it as someone who actually needs to change their card: the page explains what happens around the change but never tells you how to make it.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a self-serve help article that should get a billing admin from "my card needs replacing" to "done" without contacting support.

**Verdict.** As a piece of layout it is competent, but it fails at its main job. The article promised by the title, how to update the card, is missing. The page gives policy and edge cases where the steps should be. That gap matters far more than anything visual here.

**What would move it most**

1. **The how-to is missing.** The first paragraph says "You can replace the card on file at any time from the billing page" and moves straight on to invoices and permissions. There is no link to the billing page, no numbered steps (where the billing page is, which button to press, what to confirm) and no screenshot. A reader who arrived from the title "Update the card…" has to leave and hunt for the billing page themselves, which is exactly the trip a help article should save them. The text even links to "billing history", "roles and permissions" and "paying by invoice", but not to the one place the reader needs to go. *Defect (common sense; the anticipation rule has no approved source).* Fix: put the steps right under the headline, as a short numbered list with a direct link such as "Go to Settings → Billing → Payment method", and move the paragraphs about open invoices and permissions below them.

2. **The decline section can be read two ways, and the outcome is a downgrade.** "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days… If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan." That's three charge attempts: the first one, day 3 and day 7. "Second attempt" could mean the day-3 retry, so you're downgraded on day 3, or the second retry, so it happens on day 7. A reader planning around a deadline can't tell whether they have three days or seven. "After three days" also doesn't say three days after what. And the paragraph never says what the free plan takes away. Dates and plan changes are what readers remember and hold you to, so this is a defect, not a style note. *Defect.* Fix: count from a named event and give the deadline plainly, e.g. "If your card is declined, we try again 3 days and 7 days later. If the day-7 try fails, your workspace moves to the free plan, and you lose X until you add a working card."

3. **The opening misses the most common reason people come here.** Most people update a card because the old one expired or was replaced. The page says an open invoice "keeps the old card until it is paid". For a reader whose old card is dead, that sounds like the open invoice can never be paid. The sentence states how the system behaves without saying what it means for you. *Defect.* Fix: say what happens to an open invoice when the old card no longer works, and whether the reader can pay it with the new card, in "you" terms.

4. **The page doesn't show where you are, and a help centre has no search.** "Update a payment card" in the left sidebar looks exactly like its siblings, and "Billing" in the top nav isn't marked either. Someone who arrives from a search engine gets no "you are here" cue. The header also has no search box. Searching is how most people use a help centre, and here the only routes are four top-level categories and "Contact support". *Convention* (Nielsen heuristic 1, via NN/g's menu-design checklist; Nielsen heuristic 7 for search). Fix: mark the current page in the sidebar with one clear cue, such as a bolder weight, an accent bar or a tinted background, and put a search field in the header.

**Smaller things.**
- Inline links (e.g. "check your billing history", "roles and permissions") stand out by blue alone. Underline them, or check they reach 3:1 against the body-text colour and get an underline on hover and focus (WCAG technique G183; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The body lines look long, about 80–85 characters (e.g. "You can replace the card on file … The new card is"). Narrow the text column by roughly 10–15% to land nearer 66 characters (Bringhurst).
- The same topic has three names: the sidebar says "Update a payment card", the breadcrumb says "Billing / Payment methods" and the headline says "the card that pays for your subscription". Make the breadcrumb and sidebar agree so readers can tell how the sections relate (Norman, conceptual model).

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clear. The headline dominates, the H2 "When the new card is declined" is set apart by weight rather than by a nearly-equal size, and it sits close to its own paragraph and well clear of the paragraph before it. Keep those spacing ratios.
- The quiet palette (near-black text, grey secondary text, one blue for links) keeps attention on the words, which suits a help page.
- The headline is written in the reader's own words ("the card that pays for your subscription") rather than system terms like "payment method". Keep that voice when you add the steps.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Hover and focus styling on the inline links (affects whether blue-only links pass), and how the sidebar and nav reflow on a phone.
