**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on "Change the card you pay with", then reads the intro. In version 1 it then jumps down three bold stops ("Add a new card", "Make it the default", "If the card is declined") and you can see the page is three tasks before reading a word of them. In version 2 those stops are gone, so below the title it reads as six paragraphs of equal grey, three of them oddly short.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help-centre article for a customer who wants to swap their payment card, or whose card just failed, and needs to find their step fast.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change is the section headings. Version 1 sets them bold and a step larger. Version 2 sets them at body weight and a touch smaller than the text, so they stop working as headings. Version 1 itself is a sound, quiet help page, and only small things are left.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings are what changed, and version 2 loses them.** In version 2, "Add a new card", "Make it the default" and "If the card is declined" look like regular weight in the body colour and about a pixel smaller than the paragraphs under them. They read as one-line paragraphs, not signposts. The cost lands on the reader who matters most here: someone whose card was just declined scans for their case and finds no landmark, so they read from the top. Only the spacing still groups them. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8): a contrast must be obvious at a glance, or it looks like an accident. *Defect.* Fix: keep version 1's treatment, bold and a clear step above the body size, which is a clean three-level scale (title, section heading, body) plus the small breadcrumb.

**Smaller things** (these apply to version 1 and equally to version 2)
- The body lines look about 80 to 85 characters long ("You can change your card at any time. The new card is charged from your next bill,"), just past Bringhurst's 45 to 75. Narrow the column by roughly 10% or raise the body size slightly. *Convention.*
- Copy: the interface labels "Add card", "Save" and "Use for payments" sit in plain body text. Bold them so someone reading with the app open beside them can match words to buttons. "Open the menu" doesn't say which menu; name it, or show its icon. *Taste / common sense.*

**What's working** (version 1)
- The title is a plain task in the reader's own words, and the intro answers the two worries people have before they act ("can I do it now?" and "will I be charged?") before the steps start.
- The spacing is right: each heading sits tight to its text and well clear of the paragraph above, so the three sections group without any rules or boxes.
- The declined-card section ends with a concrete next step ("call the number on the back of the card") instead of "contact support".
