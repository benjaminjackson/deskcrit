**First read.** In both versions the eye goes straight to the white dialog, reads "Archive this project?" and the two lines below it, then drops to the buttons. In version 1 it lands on the blue "Archive project" and the choice is clear right away. In version 2 there are two identical blue buttons side by side, so the eye stops and has to read both labels before it knows which one does what.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a confirmation dialog in a project-management web app. It has to make sure a team member understands what archiving does and then let them confirm or back out without misclicking.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job and version 2 doesn't do it as well. The only change is that version 2 makes Cancel a filled primary button. That takes away the one cue that tells the user which button is the action, at the exact moment they have to choose.

**What would move it most**
1. **The buttons have no hierarchy in version 2** (defect). In the bottom-right of the dialog, "Cancel" and "Archive project" are both solid blue with white bold text. The only differences are the label and the width. The user can no longer use the familiar pattern of a filled action next to a quieter Cancel to tell the two apart. They have to read both, and a quick click is now a guess. If they hit the wrong one, they either archive by accident and have to find the restore option, or they cancel and have to start again. Material Design and Apple HIG both treat one filled primary button with lower-emphasis secondary actions as standard, and two equal filled buttons as a fault. Fix: put Cancel back to an outline or text style, as in version 1, and keep the fill for "Archive project" only.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing. Version 1 has no real failures on the checklist.

**What's working** (version 1, and keep it)
- The copy states the consequence in the user's own terms: "its 38 tasks", "Members can still read them", "no one can add tasks or comments until you restore it". The user knows exactly what changes, and that they can undo it.
- The confirm button says "Archive project" instead of "OK", so the button's label repeats the question.
- The buttons are clearly ordered. The outlined Cancel sits on the left and the filled action on the right, with a single blue used only for the action.
