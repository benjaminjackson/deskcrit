**First read.** In both, the eye lands on "Before you sign a tenancy" and then steps down through three subheads. It reads as a plain, calm consumer-advice page for renters. In version 1 that step-down is instant: the bold, dark heads catch the eye on a skim, and you can take in the whole page as four headed points. In version 2 the light grey heads sink into the body, so the page reads as one even grey block and you have to read to find the points.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a short guidance page that a renter about to sign a lease skims for the three things to check.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. Both pages are clean and well spaced. The change in version 2 takes away the one thing that makes a checklist page skimmable: headings that stand out from the text.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's headings differ from the body only in size** (defect / convention). "Check the deposit", "Take photos on day one" and "Know your notice period" are in the same regular weight and slate grey as the paragraphs under them, and so is the page title. A renter skimming for "what do I need to check?" gets no strong anchors, so the three points blend into the prose. The title also loses its authority: at regular weight in mid-grey it looks like a large paragraph more than the page's headline. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8a–8g and 9) says to pair a size step with a change of weight, form or colour. Version 1 does exactly that with bold, near-black heads over regular slate body, and it's what makes it work. Fix for v2: put back a heavier weight and the darker colour on the title and subheads. Version 1 already shows the answer.
