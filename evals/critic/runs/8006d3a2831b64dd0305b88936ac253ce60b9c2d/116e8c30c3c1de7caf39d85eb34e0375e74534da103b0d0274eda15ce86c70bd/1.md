**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif title "Crossing the river" at top left, drops to "Early boats", and settles straight into reading. It reads as a short magazine or guidebook feature about a ferry: calm, literate and unhurried. Nothing fights for attention, and nothing is meant to.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a short descriptive article, in print or on a reading page, meant to be read top to bottom in two columns by a general reader.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The type is quiet and well set, the hierarchy is clear, and the paragraphs are handled correctly. The one thing that falls short of a considered page is that the two columns don't share a baseline grid, so the lines step out of register across the gutter.

**What would move it most**
1. **The two columns are out of register** (convention). In the top half, the right column's lines (from "Stallholders arrive…") fall between the left column's lines. After the "Going home" heading they shift again: "In the evening…" sits a few pixels off "By seven the boat…". Each heading's space above and below is not a whole number of body lines, so every heading moves the column below it off the grid. A reader won't name this, but a page whose rows run straight across the gutter looks settled. This one looks slightly loose next to otherwise careful typesetting. Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* ("Construction of the grid"), asks that lines in neighbouring columns sit on the same baselines. To fix it, make each heading block (space above, heading, space below) add up to a whole number of body lines, for example two. Then both columns keep one rhythm from top to bottom. To check, draw horizontal guides at the body line spacing across both columns.

**What's working.**
- **The paragraph marks are textbook.** The first paragraph after each heading is flush left. Later paragraphs get a clear indent and no extra space between them. The continuation at the top of the right column is correctly indented because it is not a new section (Bringhurst 2.3.1–2.3.2). Keep this when revising.
- **The hierarchy has three clear levels.** There is a large bold title, bold headings about one step up from the text, and regular body text. Each heading differs from the text in weight as well as size, so it reads at a glance without needing to be big (Dair, contrast of weight).
- **The line length is right for columns:** about 60–65 characters, with comfortable line spacing and a gentle rag. It reads easily.
