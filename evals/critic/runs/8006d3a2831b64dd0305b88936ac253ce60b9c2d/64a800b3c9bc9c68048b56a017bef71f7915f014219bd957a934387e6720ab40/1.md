**First read.** The eye goes to "The Wexley Gazette", drops through the double rule, then stalls: three bold headlines of the same size sit in a row and none of them goes first. It reads as a calm, trustworthy local paper. The page ends at mid-height and the lower 45% of the frame is blank, so it looks unfinished rather than quiet.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the front page or home screen of a small-town news site, for local residents scanning what has changed in town this week.

**Verdict.** The type is careful and easy to read, but the page doesn't work as a front page yet. It doesn't say which story matters most, and it stops halfway down the frame. The biggest problem is that the three stories are given equal weight, so the reader has to choose a way in.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no lead story (defect).** "Bridge to reopen in spring", "Market moves indoors" and "School wins garden prize" have the same size, weight, column width and length. The reader reads across all three headlines before choosing one, and the page never says what this week's news is. The bridge story affects nearly everyone in Wexley, while the garden prize is a nice extra, and the layout hides that difference. Tschichold asks for one element that is plainly seen first, with the rest set back by differences of size. Hofmann's contrast of much against little is what gives a page tension. Fix: make one story the lead. Give it a larger headline and a wider measure, or two columns, and set the other two back as secondary items.
2. **The page runs out at mid-height (defect).** All the content ends around y≈415 of 800, and everything below is unused white. The space isn't shaped against anything, so it reads as a short page or a failed load, not as calm (Ruder: white space works only against something dense). It also makes the frame top-heavy, with nothing low down to balance the masthead and columns (Arnheim, on balance). Fix: the lead story from finding 1 will fill some of the height. Add an image, more stories, or a deliberate footer so the composition covers the frame. If the page really is this short, close it with a rule or footer so the end looks intended.
3. **The time references have nothing to anchor them (defect, copy).** "From next month", "in April", "from March" and "this summer term" are the details residents will act on: when to drive over the bridge, where to shop on Saturday. But the page shows no issue date or story dates. A reader who arrives late, or finds it through search, can't tell which "next month" is meant and may go to the wrong place on the wrong Saturday. Fix: put the issue date under the masthead, and name the month in the market story ("from 7 March").

**What's working.**
- The type is one serif family with three clear jobs: masthead, bold headings, regular text. The headings differ from the body by weight as well as size, so you can tell each level apart at a glance. Keep this scale when you add a lead.
- The three columns are about 38–42 characters wide. That sits within Bringhurst's 40–50 for multi-column text, has comfortable leading, an even rag and no widows. It reads like a proper newspaper column.
- The copy is plain and specific: "sixty stalls, a small cafe, and a stage", "two of the five arches". A local reader trusts that kind of detail.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the headlines link to full stories. Nothing marks them as links, so check that they are clickable and look it. Also check how the three columns reflow on a phone.
