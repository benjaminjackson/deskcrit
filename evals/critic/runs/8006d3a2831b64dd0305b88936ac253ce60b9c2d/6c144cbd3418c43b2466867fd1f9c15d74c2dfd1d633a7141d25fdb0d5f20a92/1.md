**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Crossing the river", the big brush-script title, then over to the second script heading, "Good to know", in the box on the right. The blue button pulls it a moment later. Only after that do you reach the plain serif headings and the sans body text. It reads as a friendly information page for a small local ferry. But the brush script feels like a café menu or a holiday flyer, and it sits uneasily next to the plain sans body text and the bookish serif, as if three different people set the page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a practical guide for people about to take the north landing to market quay ferry: they want to know when boats run, whether they can bring a bike, and where to find the timetable.

**Verdict.** The layout is clear, but the page doesn't do its main job, and the type undermines it. The section on when the boats run gives no times, and the mix of three typefaces makes a calm public-service page look like a collage.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy never gives a time.** The paragraph under "When the boats run" says "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" in winter, and "the last crossing leaves before dusk." A passenger reads that and still can't plan a trip. They don't know if early morning means 6:00 or 8:00, or when dusk is in January. A last departure is something people hold the operator to, so "before dusk" can be read two ways (16:00 or 17:30), and someone who guesses wrong gets stranded on the wrong bank. *Defect.* Fix: give the first and last departures for each season as clock times. For example: "From 6:40, then every 40 minutes. Last boat 21:20 (summer) / 16:00 (winter)." Leave "See the timetable" for the full list. The box on the right has the same gap: it says where a printed timetable is but not what's on it.

2. **Three typefaces with no plan, and the same element styled two ways.** There's a brush script (title and "Good to know"), a book serif (subtitle, section headings, box text) and a plain sans (nav, body text, button). Dair's rule is that faces should either agree or contrast clearly. Here three unrelated faces share the page, and two of them swap jobs:
   - "When the boats run" is a heading in bold serif, while "Good to know", a heading of the same rank, is in script.
   - The main paragraphs are in the sans, while the paragraph in the box is in the serif.
   
   So the viewer can't learn the page's rules, and the box reads like it was pasted in from somewhere else. *Convention* (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 7–8), plus *taste* on the script itself. A casual brush script suits a party invite, not a schedule people rely on. Fix: pick two faces, one for headings and one for text, and give each kind of element one style everywhere. If the script stays, keep it to the page title only, and set "Good to know" like the other section headings.

3. **The type scale is muddled.** I count about eight sizes, roughly 64, 42, 28, 24, 20, 18, 16 and 15 px. Some pairs are too close to read as different levels:
   - The subtitle (~24) sits right next to the section headings (~28).
   - The box text (~20) is a little bigger than the main body text (~18), so two kinds of ordinary paragraph don't match.
   
   *Convention* (Bringhurst, §3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Fix: keep four levels (title, section heading, body, small UI text), set the box text at body size, and use weight or colour rather than an extra size to separate the subtitle from the headings.

**Smaller things.**
- The box text runs only about 25–28 characters per line (Bringhurst suggests 45–75), so it breaks into six choppy lines. Setting it at body size in the body face fixes most of this. Widening the box would also help.
- "week." sits alone on the last line of the box text. Rewrite the sentence or adjust the width so the last line holds more than one word.
- The box's right edge (~1150 px) misses the nav's right edge (~1184 px) by about 34 px, so it looks placed by eye. Align it to the same content edge as the header.
- "Good to know" doesn't tell you what's in the box. Something like "Paper timetables and changes" would say what the reader gets.

**What's working.**
- The structure is right for the job. There's one obvious title, then short sections whose headings name their content ("Bicycles and luggage"), with the single action set apart in a box. Keep the short, chunked sections.
- There's only one strong colour, the blue button. Because nothing else competes, the one action is easy to find. Keep it the only filled button.
- The space works: each heading sits closer to its own paragraph than to the one above it, and the open space is collected into one calm area below the content rather than scattered.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the button and nav links have a visible keyboard focus style, and whether the box stacks sensibly below the main text on a phone.
