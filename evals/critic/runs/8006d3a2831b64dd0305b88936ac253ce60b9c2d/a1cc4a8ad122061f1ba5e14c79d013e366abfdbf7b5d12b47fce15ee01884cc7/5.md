I've finished the checklist and the deep reads, and I'm writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye lands on "How can we help?" in white serif on the navy band, then drops to the white search field, then moves across the two rows of topic cards. Within a second it reads as the support home page for a product called Harbor, for existing customers with a problem. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and also familiar: it's the standard help-centre layout, done neatly.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a help-centre home page where customers either search for an answer or browse to the right topic, as fast as possible.

**Verdict.** It does its job. A stuck customer can tell at once what this is and has two clear routes in. The biggest gap is that the search field, the main tool on the page, undersells itself. Everything else is small polish.

**What would move it most**
1. **The search field doesn't look like search and doesn't prompt a query.** The white field in the hero has no magnifier icon and no submit button. Its only prompt is the placeholder "Search for articles", which names a content type rather than the problem a visitor arrives with. Most people get to a help centre with a specific problem ("I can't log in", "my refund hasn't arrived"). They have to guess whether to type a question or a keyword, and the only clue that this is search is a placeholder that vanishes on the first keystroke. A magnifier icon is the convention people scan for (Jakob's law, Nielsen). *Convention, plus a defect in the copy.* Direction: add the magnifier, and optionally a "Search" button on the right of the field. Rewrite the placeholder in the visitor's words with real examples, such as "Search guides, e.g. 'reset password' or 'start a return'". Examples show people what kind of query works and send them to the most common answers.

**Smaller things.**
- **Near-miss alignment.** The search field runs from about x 360 to 920, but the card columns below start at 357 and end at 923. It's about 3 px off on each side, so it looks like a slip rather than a choice (Arnheim; break the grid decisively or not at all). Make it exactly 357–923, or clearly narrower.
- **The section heading is too close to the card titles.** "Browse by topic" (about 18 px bold, dark) is barely bigger than the card titles (about 16 px bold, same colour), so it reads as a ninth label rather than a section heading (Dair: weak contrast is worse than none). Make it clearly larger, or set it apart by colour or style.
- **One-word last lines in the card descriptions.** "data." under Privacy and security and "where." under Notifications sit alone on their lines (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Shorten or reword those two, or set the description width so they wrap cleanly.
- **The topics mix two kinds of product.** "Set up your workspace and invite your team", "Integrations" and "Fix sync problems" describe software for teams, but "Orders and shipping: Track a delivery or start a return" describes a shop. If Harbor really ships physical goods, this is fine. If not, it's a leftover from a template and will confuse people about what Harbor is (Norman, conceptual model).
- **One description breaks the pattern.** Seven card descriptions name something the visitor does ("Set up…", "Track…", "Choose…", "Fix…"). Only "How we protect and store your data" talks about the company. Rewrite it in the visitor's terms, e.g. "Control who sees your data and how it's stored".

**What's working.**
- **The hero does one thing.** A short question, one line of guidance and one field, on a dark band that separates "ask" from "browse" without any extra boxes or rules. Keep that restraint.
- **The topic grid is a clean, consistent set.** Eight equal cards, one line-icon family with matching strokes, the same icon/title/description order in each, and even gaps. The descriptions name real tasks ("Track a delivery or start a return", "Passwords, two-step sign-in, and recovery"), so people can pick a card without opening it.
- **Type and colour are disciplined.** A serif headline against an Inter-style sans for everything else, one navy plus neutrals, and dark-on-light text with comfortable contrast throughout.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the page continues below the fold, e.g. with popular articles or a "still stuck? contact us" route. If it stops after the cards, people who can't find a match have only "Contact us" in the top nav, and that matters for a help centre.
- Hover and focus states on the cards and the search field.
