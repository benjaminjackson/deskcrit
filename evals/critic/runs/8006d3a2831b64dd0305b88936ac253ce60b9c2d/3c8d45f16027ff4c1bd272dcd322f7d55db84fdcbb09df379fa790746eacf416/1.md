**First read.** My eye lands on "Book a workshop seat", runs down four identical empty boxes, and drops into blank white space. It reads as a plain, tidy booking form. It feels unfinished, as if the bottom of the form didn't render.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a public web form where someone reserves a place at a workshop. Their job is to choose the workshop, a date and a number of seats, then confirm.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job: the form has no way to submit, so nobody can book a seat. Beyond that, it's four identical blank text boxes that make the user recall and type everything. A booking form should let them pick.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no submit button.** After "Notes for the host" there's about 280 px of empty white and no action. A user fills in four fields and then has nothing to press. Every booking is lost at the last step, and the page gives no signal of what happens next. This is a defect. Material Design and Apple HIG both expect one dominant primary action per view, and Krug says the user should be able to tell where to click within seconds. Fix: add one filled primary button directly under the last field, labelled with the outcome, e.g. "Reserve my seat" (or "Reserve 2 seats" if it can echo the seat count). If the button exists but didn't render in this capture, check that in a live browser. As shown, the form cannot be completed.
2. **Every input is a blank text box, so nothing guides or limits what people type.** This is a defect. Each field invites a mistake:
   - **"Workshop name":** users must remember and type the exact name. Expect typos and names that don't match a real event (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall).
   - **"Preferred date":** looks like free text, so users will type "next Tues", "3/4" (March or April?) or a date the workshop isn't running.
   - **"Number of seats":** looks like it would accept "a couple".

   Nielsen 5 (error prevention) and Norman (constraints) both call for inputs that accept only valid values. Fix: make the workshop a picker of real workshops, or prefill it when the user arrives from a workshop page. Make the date a date picker or a list of the sessions actually scheduled. Make seats a number stepper that starts at 1.
3. **The page doesn't say what you're booking or what it costs.** There's no workshop title, date, price, seats left, or confirmation of what happens after you submit. The viewer is committing to something with no terms in view. That's a defect under Nielsen 1 (visibility of system status). Fix: once a workshop is chosen, show a short summary near the button with the name, the session, the price per seat or "free", and seats remaining. A user who knows the price and availability can book with confidence. Without them, a careful user leaves to check.

**Smaller things.**
- **No required or optional markers:** "Notes for the host" is surely optional, but it looks as mandatory as the rest. Add "(optional)" after that label (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- **"Notes for the host" is a one-line box:** it signals a single short line. A small multi-line text area tells people they can write a sentence or two. This is common sense.
- **The heading misstates the task:** "Book a workshop seat" is singular, but the form asks for a number of seats. "Book seats at a workshop" or "Book your place" reads as a person speaking and matches what the form asks.

**What's working.**
- **Labels:** every field has a persistent bold label sitting tight above its box, with clearly more space between pairs than within them. The grouping is unambiguous (Norman, natural mapping). Keep this spacing.
- **Type scale:** two sizes with a big step (heading vs labels) and one face. Calm, nothing competing. Don't add sizes when you add the button and summary; use weight and colour instead.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the fields (a 2 px, 3:1 focus ring matters on a form people tab through), validation behaviour, and what happens after submission.
