**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title, "Notes for ferry passengers", then drops into three grey paragraphs that look like a stack of evenly spaced lines more than three separate blocks. It reads as a plain information page from a ferry operator or a town site. It feels calm and tidy, and a little thin: a narrow column on the left with most of the screen empty.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web page that tells people who plan to use the river ferry when it runs, how long it takes, and what to do with a bicycle, so they can catch the boat they need.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are clean and easy to read, but the page doesn't do its main job. A passenger can't find out when a boat leaves, because every time on the page is vague and buried in a sentence. The very loose line spacing also makes the three paragraphs run together, so even the facts that are there take more reading than they should.

**What would move it most**
1. **The times are too vague for anyone to act on** (defect, copy). The only things a passenger will act on are in the first and third paragraphs: "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" in winter, and "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk". None of them gives a clock time. Someone reading this still can't tell when the first or last boat goes, whether "every forty minutes" means from each landing or a round trip, when "winter" starts, or which quay "the quay" is. The text names both the north landing and the market quay, so a reader at the north landing could read "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" as a departure from their side, turn up at dusk, and miss the boat. Those are the lines people will hold the operator to, so each one that can be read two ways is a real failure. Fix: give real first and last departure times from each landing, the months the winter timetable applies, and the actual last departure from each side. Pull them out of the prose into something a passenger can scan in two seconds, such as a short times block under the title, and keep the sentences for the bicycle rules.
2. **The line spacing is so loose that the paragraphs disappear** (convention). In the 19 px body text, lines sit about 45 px apart, roughly 2.4 times the type size. The gap between paragraphs is only about 64 px from one baseline to the next, just a third more than the gap between lines. Each paragraph falls apart into separate strips, and the three paragraphs don't read as three groups. The eye can't tell where the timetable ends and the bicycle rules begin without reading. Bringhurst and Hochuli tie line spacing to line length. At about 70 characters per line, this column needs moderate spacing, nowhere near double. The grouping rule (Arnheim, on proximity) asks for the space inside a group to be clearly smaller than the space between groups, and here the two are nearly equal. Fix: bring line spacing down to about 1.4–1.6 times the type size, and keep a clear paragraph gap of about one full line, so each paragraph reads as one block and the breaks show at a glance.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: two sizes with a big step between them, and the title is bold as well as bigger. Nobody has to guess what is title and what is body. Keep it that way when you add a times block: give it weight or a layout of its own, not a third size close to these two.
- The column width is right. Lines run about 70 characters, flush left with a gentle ragged edge, in a plain sans at a size that's easy to read with strong dark-on-white contrast.
- The bicycle paragraph is good copy. It says who does what and when ("Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"), so a cyclist knows exactly what to do.
