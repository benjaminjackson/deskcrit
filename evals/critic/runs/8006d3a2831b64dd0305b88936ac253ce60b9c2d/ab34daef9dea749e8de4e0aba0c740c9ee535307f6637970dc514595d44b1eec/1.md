**First read.** The bold sans headline lands first, then the eye drops into the left column and works across three narrow columns of serif text. It reads as an information page for a local river ferry, probably for passengers or visitors. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, a little like a printed leaflet. Nothing in it feels wrong at first glance. The trouble starts when you try to find a specific fact.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or panel that tells ferry passengers how the service runs: how often, how long a crossing takes, bicycles, and the winter changes. The reader wants to find one answer quickly.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, but it's set like an essay when it's really a timetable notice. The columns are too narrow to read comfortably, and the facts a passenger acts on are buried in prose and stated vaguely. The heading also promises something the text mostly doesn't deliver.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts a passenger acts on are hidden and vague.** *Defect.* "Every forty minutes," "about twelve minutes," "one boat each hour," "free for bicycles" and "last crossing before dusk" are all buried mid-sentence. A rider who only wants to know whether the bike can come has to read all three columns to find out. "Between early morning and late evening" and "before dusk" are times people plan around, and they can be read more than one way. Dusk moves by hours across the winter, and "early morning" could mean 5 or 8. Someone who guesses wrong misses the last boat. The fix is to give each column a short subhead that names its topic (Frequency, Bicycles, Winter), pull the numbers forward, and replace the vague times with real ones ("first boat 6:20, last 21:40"; "last winter crossing 16:00"). The prose can stay as the explanation underneath.

2. **The columns are too narrow, and they leave a third of the frame empty.** *Convention.* Each column runs about 22 to 30 characters a line. Bringhurst gives 40 to 50 for text set in several columns, and 45 to 75 for a single column. The result is choppy reading: the eye returns to the left edge every three or four words, and phrases get split ("twelve minutes in calm / weather"). At the same time, the three columns stop around x≈1010 in a 1280-wide frame, leaving a 270 px empty strip on the right against a 120 px left margin. Widen the columns to use that width, so they end level with the right margin. That gets the lines close to 40 characters and fixes the lopsided frame in the same move. If the width is a fixed container, use two columns, or one column of about 66 characters.

3. **The headline says something the page doesn't.** *Defect.* "The river ferry through the seasons" promises a change across the year, but only the third column is about winter. The first two cover the everyday service and bicycles. A reader scanning for timetable or bike rules gets no signal that they're here. Name what the page holds instead ("Crossing times, bicycles and winter service", or "Taking the river ferry"). Or, if seasons really are the story, add the summer, spring and autumn content the headline implies.

**Smaller things.**
- Middle column, last line: "stairs." sits alone as a runt. Rewrap or edit so at least two words end the paragraph (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- Weight sits in the upper left, with nothing set against the empty right strip and lower half. Widening the columns (finding 2) mostly solves this.

**What's working.**
- Contrast between the bold sans headline and the serif body comes from face, weight and size together. There are only two sizes, with a big step between them, so the hierarchy is clear at a glance (Dair: contrast from more than size). Keep that, and add the subheads as a third level, in the sans at body size and bold.
- The columns sit on one grid with equal gutters (x = 120, 436, 752), and their baselines line up across the gaps. That's why the page feels orderly even though it's plain.
- Dark text on white at a generous body size: it's comfortable to read, and nothing competes with the content.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the three columns reflow on a phone (they need to stack into one column there), and whether this is the whole page or just the top of a longer one. The bottom 300 px is empty.
