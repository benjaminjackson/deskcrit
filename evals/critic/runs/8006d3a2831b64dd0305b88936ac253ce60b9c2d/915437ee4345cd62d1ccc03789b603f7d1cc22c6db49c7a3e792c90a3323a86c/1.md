**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a web info page or a printed notice. Its job is to tell riders when the ferry runs, how long a crossing takes, and what to do with a bike.

**Verdict.** The layout and the type are clean and mostly right. The page falls short on the facts riders act on: it never gives a time anyone can plan around, and the winter change comes last. The visible flaw in the type is a broken rag that looks like hard-coded line breaks.

**What would move it most**
1. **No usable times (defect).** The first paragraph says "between early morning and late evening", and the last says the final winter crossing leaves "before dusk". Someone planning a trip home can't tell whether "late evening" means 8 pm or 11 pm, and "before dusk" changes by more than an hour across the winter. These are the lines people remember and later hold the operator to, so a vague one is a defect, not a style note. A rider who guesses wrong gets stranded on the far bank. The fix is first and last departure times from each landing. If the times really do change, give the rule (for example "last boat 30 min before sunset") and say where to find the current timetable. Only the printed copies in the shelters are named now, which doesn't help someone who hasn't left home yet.
2. **The most consequential fact comes last (defect).** The winter timetable cuts service from every 40 minutes to every hour and moves the last boat earlier. It sits in the final paragraph, below details about the sandbank and bike locking. A winter reader stops after the first paragraph believing a schedule that is wrong for them. Put the season split up front: summer and winter frequencies and last boats side by side, ideally as a small two-row table. Move the tide and sandbank explanation after it as background. The heading would also do more work if it named what's below it ("Crossing times and bicycles") instead of the generic "Notes for…" (taste).
3. **The rag looks hard-broken (convention: Bringhurst 2.1.3; Hochuli, "The line").** In paragraph 1, "market" ends line 1 well short of the margin, and the last line ("sandbank … low tide.") runs about 70 px past every other line. Paragraph 2 goes long, short ("boarded,"), long. The right edge reads as accidents rather than a gentle ragged edge, and on any other width these breaks will land in the middle of lines. Remove the manual `<br>`s or fixed breaks and let the column wrap at one set width.

**Smaller things.** The first paragraph is one 60-word sentence about tides and the sandbank, so split it: crossing time first, then the delay at low tide as a separate sentence.

**What's working.**
- The bold heading over regular text uses only two sizes, so it's clear where to start without shouting.
- The measure is comfortable (roughly 50–60 characters) and the leading is generous, which suits short notices read on the go.
- Paragraphs are separated by space only, with no indents, and there's a single neutral palette. Keep this restraint when you add times or a table.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the line breaks are hard-coded or come from the container width: resize the page in a browser and see whether they move.
