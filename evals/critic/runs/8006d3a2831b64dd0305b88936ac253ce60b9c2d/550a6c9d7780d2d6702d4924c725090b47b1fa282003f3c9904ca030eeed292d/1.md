**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on "Release notes" and then goes to the first entry. In version 1 it then steps down through three bold "Version 4.x, date" lines. You can scan the page in a second. In version 2 the eye leaves the title and meets one even grey block. The version lines don't stand out from the descriptions, so you have to read the page to find where one release ends and the next begins. Both look calm, editorial and well made.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a release-notes page for a desktop photo editor. A returning user scans it to find their version, or the latest one, and see what changed.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only real change is the weight and size of the version headings. Version 2 makes them regular weight and almost body size, which removes the one cue a scanning reader needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version headings that look like body text (version 2 only). Defect.** The three lines "Version 4.2, 20 October", "Version 4.1, 2 September" and "Version 4.0, 15 July" look about one size step above the 18 px body. They use the same regular weight of the same serif and nearly the same dark grey. Two sizes that close don't read as two levels; they read as an accident (Bringhurst on scales; Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
   - What the reader experiences: on a page they scan for a version number, nothing marks the start of each entry, so they have to read each line.
   - Version 2's tighter vertical spacing makes this worse, because the space between entries now does less of the separating work.
   - Fix: give the headings a clear step by weight, as version 1 does with bold at about 24 px. Bold alone at body size would also do it. Don't add a size only a point or two larger.
