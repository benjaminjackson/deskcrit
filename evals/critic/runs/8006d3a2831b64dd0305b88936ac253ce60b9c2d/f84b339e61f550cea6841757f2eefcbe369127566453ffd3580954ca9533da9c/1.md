**First read.** Your eye goes straight to the four-line black headline, the only thing on the page with any weight. Then it drops to a two-sentence paragraph and a stray line at the bottom. It reads as the top of a ferry timetable page. It feels calm and competent, then empty: you finish it still not knowing when a boat leaves.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page telling passengers when the river ferry between the north landing and the market quay runs this spring, so they can catch one.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job. The page is labelled "Spring timetable" and has no times on it: no first sailing, no last sailing, no dates. The space is spent on a headline that says less than the schedule would.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times** (defect). The label "Spring timetable", the headline, and the paragraph all promise a schedule. What a passenger actually needs is a departure time, and the page doesn't give one. "Every forty minutes" with no starting time can't be used: forty minutes from when? "By daylight" moves with the season, so the passenger can't tell whether a 7 pm boat exists in April. "Spring through early summer" gives no dates for when this service starts or ends. These are the lines people act on and hold the operator to, so each vague one is a missed boat waiting to happen. Fix: put the facts at the top, near the headline: first and last departure from each landing, the exact dates the timetable applies, and either the full list of departure times or "every 40 minutes from 7:10 to 19:50". If the last boat really does depend on sunset, give the date-by-date last sailing.
2. **The headline takes up the space and carries none of the facts** (taste, argued on use). It runs to four lines at display size and fills the upper half of the screen. Its only concrete word is "Quayside". Make the headline short ("Quayside ferry: spring timetable") and let the times be the large, scannable thing. On a timetable, the schedule should be what dominates.
3. **A stray line that isn't copy** (defect). "The cramped, overlapping heading makes this page hard to read." sits as the last paragraph. It looks like a review note or test string left in the build. It also isn't true: in a close crop the heading lines are tight but don't touch. A passenger reading it will doubt the page is finished, and with it the times. Remove it.

**Smaller things.**
- The body paragraph ends with "weather." alone on a line. Rewrap it or tighten the measure slightly so the last line carries more than one word.
- The headline breaks "early / summer" across lines, splitting one phrase. If you keep a long headline, break it at phrase boundaries.

**What's working.**
- The type setting is sound. The headline is tightly tracked and leaded so its four lines hold together, the body runs about 68 characters a line at a comfortable size, and the small bold grey label clearly reads as a level above the headline. Keep this scale when you add the times.
- Everything hangs on one flush-left edge with generous margins, so whatever you add has a clear column to sit in.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the four-line headline reflows on a phone, where at this size it would likely push any times below the first screen.
