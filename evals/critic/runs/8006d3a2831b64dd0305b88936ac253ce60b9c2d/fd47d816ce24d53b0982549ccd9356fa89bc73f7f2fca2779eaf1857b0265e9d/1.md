**First read.** The eye lands on "Your account", then moves through four cards in a 2×2 grid with a left menu. It reads straight away as a shop's account overview for a returning customer: calm and plain. In version 1, the five black outlines are the darkest marks on the screen, so the frames register before the content inside them. In version 2 the frames fade back and the eye goes straight to the card headings and details.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a customer's account home: a quick check that their details, last order, address and card are correct, with a link to change each one.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only real change is the borders. Version 1 boxes every card and the menu in a near-black ~2 px outline, which competes with the text. Version 2 uses pale hairlines, so the cards still hold together and the content wins. What's still holding version 2 back is the space: each card and the menu panel has a large empty strip at the bottom.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1's heavy outlines (the change between versions).** All four cards and the sidebar sit inside dark outlines that look about 2 px thick. They're the strongest dark marks on the page, darker and longer than any line of text. The page reads as a grid of locked compartments, and the bold card headings ("Personal details", "Recent order") have to compete with the frames around them. The cards don't need the weight: the white fill on the off-white page and the gutters already separate them. This is a convention breach with a real cost (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 157: rules "should illuminate the relationships… not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell"; Tufte, non-data ink). Version 2 makes the approved fix: it replaces the borders with a pale tone and a hairline. Keep that.
2. **Empty bottom thirds in the cards and sidebar (both versions; the main remaining issue in v2).** Every card is about 280 px tall, but its content ends after about 160 px. Below each link sits roughly 100–120 px of nothing, and the sidebar has the same dead strip under "Sign out". The leftover space is spread across five boxes instead of gathered in one place, so the cards look unfinished or half-loaded rather than calm (Ruder, *Typography*, "Contrasts"). Fix: let the cards size to their content, with rows matched to the taller card in each row, and let the menu panel end after "Sign out". Then the open space collects below the grid, where it reads as deliberate. This is a defect (the page looks unfinished), though a mild one.

**Smaller things.**
- Sidebar current page: "Account" is marked by bold alone, and in a menu of five short words that's easy to miss. Add a tint bar or an accent marker behind the current item so the user can see where they are (Nielsen 1).

**What's working.**
- The copy is concrete and reads like a real customer's record: "Order 48213, placed 12 October / Two linen pillow cases / Delivered 15 October". The user can confirm what happened without clicking.
- Each card ends in one specific action named for what it does ("Change card", "Manage addresses", "View all orders"), all styled the same, so no card claims to be more urgent than it is.
- The type scale is tight and clear: three sizes (page title, card heading, body), with headings also set apart by weight.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The hover and focus states of the links and menu items, and whether the text links' click targets reach 24 px tall.
