**First read.** My eye goes first to the big black headline at top left, then the blue "Create your link" button, then along the four feature cards, and last to a faint grey row of names at the bottom. Within two seconds it reads as a scheduling tool for small businesses that take appointments. It looks tidy, calm and competent. It also looks like a lot of other SaaS homepages, and the right half of the first screen is blank.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage hero of a booking-link product. Its job is to get a small service business owner (a clinic, tutor or coach) to make a link or look at a live example.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, and nothing is broken, but it doesn't sell. Nothing on the first screen shows what the product looks like or how it's different. A visitor gets an empty right half and copy that any competitor could use word for word.

**What would move it most**

1. **The right half of the hero is empty, and the product never appears.** From x≈660 to 1216 and y≈100 to 400 there's nothing. The headline, subhead and buttons all sit in the left half, so the page tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). The space reads as leftover, not as an open area chosen to set off the content (Ruder: empty space should work in contrast with the dense areas, not be what's left over). The bigger cost: someone choosing a booking tool wants to see the page their clients will see, and here they have to click away to find it. *Defect.* Fix: put the booking page itself in that space, ideally the same one "See a live example" opens. Then the headline's promise and the proof sit side by side, and the right half balances the left.

2. **The copy could sit on any competitor's page.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "Share one link, let people pick a time that works…" say what every tool in this category does. The visitor can't tell why to pick this one. There's also a mismatch with the audience. The customer row names Harbor Dental, Kestrel Physio and Orchard Vets, businesses with appointments and patients, but the subhead talks about "every meeting". The best idea on the page is hidden in the Reminders card: "cut down on no-shows". For a dental practice, that's money they lose today. *Defect (copy).* Fix: lead with the result for that audience, in their words ("fewer no-shows", "patients book themselves after hours"). Make the ask compare to what they do now, for example "See what your booking page would look like" with their business name, rather than a generic "Create your link".

3. **The customer row doesn't read as proof.** At y≈770, five names sit in small grey text with no heading, logos or numbers. They look like footer links or a sub-nav, not customers. So the only evidence of real use on the page is easy to miss and doesn't persuade. *Defect.* Fix: add a label ("Used by 2,000 clinics and tutors", if that's true), use real logos or a short quote, and give the row enough weight to register as social proof.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1: the "New" badge is pushed to the far right, so "Shared calendars" wraps to two lines. Its body text and "Learn more" end up about 30 px below the other three cards. Put the badge beside the title as in the other cards, and the four cards will line up again.
- Badges "Auto" and "Email" on the Time zones and Reminders cards: they look like status tags but don't say anything the body text doesn't already. "Auto" means nothing on its own. Cut them, and keep only "New" and "Beta", which tell the visitor something.
- Headline break "fill your / calendar" leaves one word alone on line 2 and splits "your calendar". Rebreak it as "Booking links that / fill your calendar", or adjust the width.
- The header "Start free" and hero "Create your link" look like the same action under two names. Pick one wording so the visitor knows they lead to the same place (taste).

**What's working.**
- The type is disciplined. There's one family and a short scale: a heavy ~48 px headline, ~20 px subhead and card titles, ~15 px body. Each level is clear at a glance, and the headline is tight enough to read as one block.
- There's one strong left edge at x≈64 from logo to footer, and the cards end exactly at the right edge of the "Start free" button. The page feels ordered, and that's worth keeping when you add the product visual.
- The card body copy is concrete and talks to the reader ("so nobody has to do the math", "so the first minutes are useful"). Bring that voice up into the hero.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the two buttons and the "Learn more" links; how the four cards reflow on a phone.

The whole piece: 8 lines, 4 yes, 4 no (headline and image make one idea; every element does a job; repeated elements consistent; headings name their section)
