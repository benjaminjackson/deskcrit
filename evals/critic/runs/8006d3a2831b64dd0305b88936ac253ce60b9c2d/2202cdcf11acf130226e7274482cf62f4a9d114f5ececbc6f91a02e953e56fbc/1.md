The bottom 40% of the frame is empty white. It reads as the top section of a web page for a harbour open day. **What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is the top section of a web page for the public. Its job is to tell people about free ferry rides at the City Harbour Open Day and get them to turn up.

**Verdict.** The type is handled competently, but the piece doesn't do its job: nobody can act on it, because it never says what day, what hours, or where the north landing is. The headline also spends all its size on mood, while the one fact that gets people out of bed, that the rides are free, is in small body text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The information people need to act on is missing (defect).**
   - The page names an event and an activity, but there is no date, no first or last sailing, and no location beyond "the north landing".
   - "Every half hour" has no start or end time. "The north landing" assumes the reader already knows the harbour. "Open Day" with no date can't be put in a calendar.
   - A reader who's interested has to go and look this up elsewhere, and many won't.
   - The empty lower 40% makes it worse. After the paragraph the eye reaches a dead end: there's no next step, no detail and no ask (Arnheim on a clear eye path from focal point to close).
   - **Fix:** use that empty space for a short block that plans the trip: the date, the first and last sailing ("every 30 minutes, 9:00 to 12:00"), and where the landing is ("north landing, by the Harbour Master's office", or a small map link).
   - The ask should name one exact thing the reader can do, such as "Catch the 9:00 from the north landing on Saturday 14 June". That works better than a generic "Find out more".
2. **The headline sells the mood and hides the offer (copy, taste).**
   - "All aboard the morning ferry" is pleasant but could be any ferry company's tagline. The part that is actually news, that the rides are free and run all morning, sits in 20 px text.
   - Keep the voice and put the fact into the biggest type. Something like "Free ferry rides all morning" with "All aboard" as the kicker works. So does keeping this headline and setting "Free, every half hour" as a strong second level directly under it.
   - The reader should get "free ferry, this morning, here" from the large type alone.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline spacing:** the capitals are tracked out well beyond the 5 to 10% Bringhurst gives for capitals, and large type wants tighter spacing, not looser (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80). "ALL ABOARD THE" breaks into separate letters and reads slowly. Bring the tracking in to a few percent so each line reads as one shape.
- **Colour and mood:** with only near-black and grey on white, the outing feels like an office notice (Itten on colour carrying the mood). One harbour colour (a sea blue, or signal-flag red on the key fact) or a photograph of the boat would match the cheerful copy. This one is partly taste.

**What's working.**
- **Clear hierarchy:** three levels (small grey spaced-out label, huge headline, readable body) with big steps between them, so there's never a doubt where to start. Keep that scale when you add the date and times; slot them in as one new level, not three.
- **Alignment:** one strong left edge at about 90 px carries every element, and the paragraph's line length (about 58 characters) is comfortable to read.
- **Body copy:** "Bring a hat and a thermos" is good writing. It's specific, it puts the reader on the deck, and it sounds like a person speaking.
