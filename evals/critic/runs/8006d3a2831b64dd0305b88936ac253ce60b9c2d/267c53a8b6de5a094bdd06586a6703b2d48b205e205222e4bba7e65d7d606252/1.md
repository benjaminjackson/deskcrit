**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: a ferry operator's news page for local passengers. It tells them about the rebuilt landing stage, and its main ask is to sign up for timetable-change alerts.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are clean, but the one thing the page asks you to do cannot work. The sign-up form asks for your name and home landing, never for an email address or phone number, so nobody can actually receive the "one short note" it promises. Second to that, the article's picture shows nothing the article describes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The sign-up can't deliver what it promises** (defect). The panel on the right says "We send one short note when the timetable changes", but its only fields are "Your name" and "Home landing". A careful reader will see there's no way to reach them and won't trust the button. A hasty one will sign up and never hear anything. Either way the main ask fails. Add the contact field the notes go to (email, or a mobile number for texts), and say which one in the helper line. Name is the field you can most easily drop: the note doesn't need it. Make "Home landing" a choice from the actual landings rather than free text. Free text invites typos the system can't match, so people would sign up and still get nothing (Nielsen 5, error prevention). Mark which fields are required.
2. **The figure shows nothing the text describes** (defect). The picture at top left is flat placeholder blocks: a grey slab, a lighter box, and a blue bar on a band of blue water. The caption promises "the new north landing, seen from the water at low tide". The paragraphs around it talk about wider ramps, a covered waiting area, benches, a kiosk, and ramps that meet the deck at every tide. None of that is visible, so the picture adds nothing and makes the page look unfinished. Use a real photo of the north landing at low tide, ideally one that shows the ramp meeting the deck. That is the article's real news for pram and wheelchair users. If this is a placeholder, it shouldn't ship.
3. **The illustration's blue bar looks like a button** (defect, small but real). The rounded bar at the right of the figure is the same blue and rough shape as Sign up. In a page with one action, that's a second thing that looks clickable, and it dilutes the one true action. If the illustration stays, take the interface blue out of it.

**Smaller things.**
- The field labels, the helper line under Sign up, the figure caption and the footer are all about 12 px in a light weight. They look thin and faint. Use the regular weight and check contrast against 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3).
- There's a dead band of white below "wheelchairs roll straight on" down to the footer, and the right column is empty below the panel. The page looks like it ran out of content. Tighten the page, or give the article its natural length.
- The footer line "Ferry timetables are a sample for a fixture image…" is fixture text. Strip it before this goes live.
- The headline "Landing stage rebuild" is a topic, not news. "The north landing now meets the boat at every tide" tells passengers what changed for them. That is the article's strongest line, and right now it's buried in the last paragraph.

**What's working.**
- There is a clear three-level scale: headline, body, small text. The headline is the obvious place to start, and the panel heading sits clearly below it.
- The body copy is good: concrete, plain, and about the passenger ("benches face the water", "prams and wheelchairs roll straight on"). Lines run at a comfortable length (about 70 characters) with easy line spacing.
- The form is set up correctly: labels above the fields, one column, a single primary button, and a reassuring line about stopping the notes at any time. Keep all of that when you add the missing field.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the fields and the button, and how the two columns reflow on a phone. Most passengers will read this on a phone at the landing.
