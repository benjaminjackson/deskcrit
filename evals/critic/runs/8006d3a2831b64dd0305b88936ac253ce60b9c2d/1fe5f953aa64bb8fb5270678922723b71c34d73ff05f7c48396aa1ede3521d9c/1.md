I've finished the walk and checked the right margin; here is the critique.

**First read.** I see three tall coloured slabs (rust, green, violet) before I read any words. Then the big white headings, left to right: "Learn to swim", "Join the lanes", "Family hour". It reads as a local pool's three programmes, friendly and plain. After three short lines the eye falls into a large empty block of colour in each card, and the read ends with nothing to do.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a section of a pool or leisure-centre website meant to get adults and families to choose a session and book or turn up.

**Verdict.** The copy and type are good, but the cards stop halfway. More than half of each card is empty colour, and none of them says when, what it costs or how to sign up, so a viewer who is interested has nowhere to go. Three loud, unrelated colours fighting for attention make it worse.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards don't give a next step or the details people need to choose (defect).** In every card the text ends about 330 px down, and the bottom 300 px, more than half the card, is plain fill. The eye reads the blurb, drops into the empty colour and stops. The copy is specific ("Tuesday evenings", "from six", "Sunday afternoons"), but it leaves out what someone deciding needs to know: exact times, the price, whether to book or just turn up, and a way to do it. The empty space doesn't give the eye a rest. It's space left over because the cards are taller than their content, and it makes them look unfinished (Ruder on gathered space versus leftover space; Tschichold on large unexplained empty areas). Fix: either shrink the cards to fit their content, or fill the lower part with what helps someone act. Pin a short facts block (time, price, who it's for) and one specific ask to the bottom of each card. "Book a Tuesday class" or "See this week's lane times" is better than "Learn more".
2. **Three vivid hues at equal size and strength (convention).** Rust-orange, forest green and violet each fill a 360×560 block, so saturated colour covers most of the content area. No one colour leads, and the colours don't mean anything. The three cards do the same job, and nothing else on the page uses colour as a key for these programmes. The colours also have nothing to do with water, calm or a pool, so the section feels like a generic template rather than this pool (Itten on colour chords: keep one hue dominant and mute the rest; Refactoring UI and Material on a small palette where each colour has a role). Fix: choose one lead colour, ideally the pool's brand colour. Give the cards one shared treatment, such as light tints, a single colour, or a photo of each session. If you keep colour-coding, use it only in small amounts (a tab or a stripe) and repeat it wherever the programme appears so it actually means something.

**Smaller things.**
- The row of cards isn't centred: the left margin is about 56 px and the right about 96 px. That reads as a near-miss rather than a choice. Centre the row or align it with the page grid. Check this in a live browser, because it may be a container width that doesn't match this viewport.
- Two headings break after a short function word ("Join the / lanes", "Learn to / swim"). The headlines would read more cleanly if the size or card width let each break at a phrase boundary, or fit on one line.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: two sizes with a big jump between them, heavy tight-set headings over a regular-weight body. You can tell heading from body at a glance. Keep it.
- The copy talks to a person, not a category. "With a coach in the water beside you" and "three speeds to choose from" each answer a real worry (am I safe, am I too slow). Write the missing details in the same voice.
- The grid is steady: equal card widths, equal 24 px gutters, and text in the three cards starts on the same baselines.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
