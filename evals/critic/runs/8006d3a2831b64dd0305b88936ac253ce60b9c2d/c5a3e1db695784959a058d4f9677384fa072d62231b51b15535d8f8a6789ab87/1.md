**Verdict.** It does its job, and it does it well. There's one clear focal point and a tidy order behind it. The copy is specific enough that it couldn't belong to anyone else. The only real weakness is where the block sits in the frame: it's pinned high, so the bottom of the card looks unused rather than planned.

**What would move it most**
1. **The block sits too high for a fixed frame** (taste, drawing on Arnheim's point about balance in *Art and Visual Perception*). The kicker is about 65 px from the top. That's less than the 100 px left margin. Below the caption there's an empty band of roughly 210 px. On a webpage that's just the page continuing. On a fixed card it makes the content feel top-heavy, as if it slid up and the bottom fifth was never meant to be there. The fix is to decide what the bottom space is for. Either move the whole block down so the top margin at least matches the left one, or put the space to use by setting the issue line or a small mark along the bottom edge. Then the empty area becomes a frame and not leftover canvas. It's the only change I'd make before shipping, and it's a small one.

**What's working.**
- **Typography with real care in the details.** The opening quote mark hangs outside the text edge, so the left edge stays straight. The "fi" in "first" is a true ligature. The quote marks are curly. The quote is set with enough line spacing to breathe but still reads as one block. Keep all of this if you change the frame.
- **A clean hierarchy with few sizes.** There's one very large serif size for the quote, then three small sans lines (kicker, name, caption). Those three are told apart by weight and colour, not by extra sizes: bold dark for the name, regular grey for the caption, bold grey for the kicker. That's the "bolder, not bigger" approach from *Refactoring UI*, and it's why nothing below the quote competes with it.

The caption grounds the scene in facts: 5:40, bicycles, bread deliveries. Together they create a small world that makes you curious about the issue, with no generic tagline.
