I've finished the critique. It's a clean, readable page. The main problem is a line at the bottom that looks like an internal review note.

**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Planning a vegetable bed", then down a column of three section headings, each with a line or two under it. It reads as a short beginner's gardening how-to, calm and plain. At the bottom the eye stops on "Design review: approved, a model of clear heading styles". That line isn't meant for the reader, and it breaks the spell.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web article or help page that walks a first-time gardener through setting up a vegetable bed.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The page is easy to follow, the order is clear and the instructions are concrete. The biggest problem is the leftover review note at the bottom. After that, the body text runs wider than is comfortable to read.

**What would move it most**
1. **The review note at the bottom of the column** ("Design review: approved, a model of clear heading styles", about y 613). It's set in the same size and colour as the instructions, so a reader takes it as part of the article. They then hit a line about the page's own design, which tells them nothing about gardening and makes the page look unfinished. I treated it as text on the page, not as evidence that the design works. **Defect** (common sense; no catalogue rule covers it): delete it before publishing.
2. **Body text measure.** The paragraphs fill the whole column, about x 160 to 950, so lines like "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level" run about 90 to 95 characters. Bringhurst (*The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.2) gives 45 to 75 as comfortable and 66 as ideal. Every paragraph here is only one or two lines, so the cost is small today. It will grow as soon as a section gets longer, which an article like this will. **Convention**: narrow the text column to about 66 characters at this size. The headings can keep the wider span if you like.

**Smaller things.**
- Section headings ("Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant", "Watering") differ from the body only in size: same regular weight, same near-black colour. At about 32 px over sections one or two lines long, they shout. A smaller heading size paired with a semibold weight or a different colour would show the levels with less noise (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8a–8g and 9). **Convention**, with a light cost here.
- "year's" uses a straight apostrophe. Use the curly ’. This is taste: the source checked doesn't settle it, and at body size it's minor.
- Copy: "six hours of sun" leaves out "a day". A beginner will probably fill it in, but adding the two words removes the gap.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is concrete.** "The depth of a spade", "radishes, lettuce, and bush beans", "twice a week, rather than a little every day": each line tells the reader exactly what to do. Keep this voice.
- **Spacing does the grouping.** Each heading sits close to its own paragraph with clearly more space above it, so the sections read as separate steps without rules or boxes (Bringhurst; Müller-Brockmann on space as structure).
- **The type scale has three clear sizes** (about 56, 32 and 18 px) in one family, with dark text on white. Nothing is faint and no two sizes collide.
