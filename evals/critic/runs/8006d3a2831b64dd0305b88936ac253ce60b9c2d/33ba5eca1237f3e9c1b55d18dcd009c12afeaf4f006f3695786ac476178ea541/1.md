**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the masthead, then skims five equal cards from left to right and stops. No story reads as the day's news, and the bottom half of the screen is blank. In version 2 the eye drops straight from the masthead onto the large footbridge picture and its big headline, then moves to the four smaller stories on the right. It reads as a local paper's front page with a lead story, and it feels calm and credible.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of a small local news site, for town residents who want to see the main story at a glance and then pick other stories to read.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job and version 1 does not. Version 1 gives five stories equal weight, so the page has no lead and wastes half the screen. Version 2 fixes both with one clear lead and a 2×2 grid of secondary stories. What still holds it back is the empty image boxes.

**What would move it most**
1. **The change that decides it: version 2 has a lead and version 1 doesn't.** (Defect in V1.) Version 1's five identical cards say every story matters equally. The reader has to read all five headlines to find the news, and the footbridge story gets the same weight as library opening hours. Version 2 makes the footbridge picture about 4× the area of the others and sets its headline at roughly twice their size. The reader gets the day's story in one glance, and the other four read as "also today". This is the contrast of scale that front pages rely on. Keep it.
2. **The empty space is gathered in version 2 and left over in version 1.** (Defect in V1, minor in V2.) Version 1 fills about the top 400 px and leaves a dead band of about 400 px below. The page looks unfinished or broken, and nothing pulls the reader down. Version 2 fills the screen to about 660 px. A blank band of about 130 px remains at the foot, plus a gap of about 90 px under the first row of side stories. If more stories follow below the fold, it's fine. If not, close the gap.
3. **The pictures are flat colour boxes in both versions.** (Defect, unless real photos load in the live page.) On a news page the photo carries half the story. A grey-blue box over "New footbridge over the Soar" tells the reader nothing, and at the lead's new size the blank box is now the largest thing on the page. If these are placeholders in a mockup, judge the layout again with real photos, because a busy picture at that size will change the balance. If they are what ships, this is the biggest problem left.

**Smaller things (version 2)**
- The lead caption (about 643 px) sits about 10 px above the right-hand captions (about 653 px). It's a near-miss on the shared bottom line, so either align them or offset them clearly.
- "Library extends Sunday / hours" leaves one word alone on the second line. Rebreak it or shorten the headline.
- The nav ("News · Sport · Business · Weather") doesn't show which section you're in. Mark the current one.

**What's working (version 2)**
- **The lead's scale jump.** One picture and headline clearly dominant, four equal secondary stories. That is the whole improvement.
- **The type.** A bold serif for headlines and a quiet grey sans for standfirsts gives two levels that differ in face, weight and colour, not just size.
- **The standfirsts.** Each one is a single plain sentence with a concrete fact ("Traders pay half rent until March"), so a reader can get the news without clicking.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the colour boxes are real images that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- How the lead-plus-grid layout reflows on a phone.
