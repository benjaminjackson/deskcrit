**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on "Departures from Corn Exchange", then drops into a nine-row timetable. In version 1 the black cage of cell borders hits next, before any of the data. In version 2 the times in the left column and the two red statuses are what pull next. Both are calm, plain information screens. Version 2 reads quieter and faster.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a stop or web departure board for bus passengers checking when their bus leaves and whether it is running.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. It keeps everything that works in version 1 and removes the full black grid, which was the only real thing in version 1's way. The remaining weakness in both is small: the board doesn't show which bus is next or how long until it leaves.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1's grid out-shouts the data (defect; this is what version 2 fixes).** In version 1, every cell has a near-black 1 px border, so the vertical rules are as dark as the times and route numbers. The eye reads a cage of 45 boxes before it reads departures, and the vertical rules chop each departure into five separate pieces when a passenger reads a row as one thing: "14:20, the 12 to Hadley Green". Version 2 drops the vertical rules and turns the horizontal ones pale grey. The column alignment and padding already hold the columns, so nothing is lost, and each row reads as one line. This is Wilke's fix almost word for word (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, 23.2): make the gridlines thin and pale or remove them, so the data is the darkest thing there. Tufte's data-ink argument says the same. Keep version 2's treatment.

**Smaller things (version 2).**
- The "Via" column is far wider than its longest entry ("Station Road, Market Square"), so there is a big empty gap before Status. That pushes the status, the column a passenger most needs, far from the time it belongs to. Narrow Via and bring Status closer.
- Nothing marks the next departure or the time left until it goes. Bolding or tinting the first row, or adding "3 min", would answer the passenger's real question at a glance. This is my taste for a departure board, not a rule.
- "Cancelled" is the most consequential line on the board but is the same size and weight as "On time". Making it bold, as well as red, would let it register on a quick scan. Common sense: it's the one row that changes what someone does.

**What's working.**
- The bold times down the left edge make the column you scan by the strongest thing in the table.
- Red is used only for "4 min late" and "Cancelled", and always with words, so colour isn't the only signal and it means only one thing.
- The title, the "Stand C · Updated 14:02" line, the column headers and the rows make four clear levels with no sizes that nearly collide. "Updated 14:02" tells the viewer how fresh the data is.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How it refreshes and whether it reflows on a phone, where the wide Via column would be the first problem.
