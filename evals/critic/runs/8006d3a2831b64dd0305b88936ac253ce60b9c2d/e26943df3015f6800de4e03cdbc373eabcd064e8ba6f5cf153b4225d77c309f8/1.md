**First read.** The eye goes straight to the large bold title, "Update the card that pays for your subscription", then down into a calm, single column of body text. It reads as a help-centre article for a billing product: quiet, orderly and trustworthy, with nothing competing for attention. After a few seconds you notice something missing: the page explains the rules around changing your card but never tells you how to do it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help article for a Ledgerly account owner or billing admin who wants to swap the card on file right now, and who arrives from search or the Billing section.

**Verdict.** It's a cleanly set page, but it doesn't do the job its title promises. There are no steps and no link to the place where you change the card, and the one passage about money and dates (the retry and downgrade schedule) can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The how-to is missing (defect).** The title says "Update the card…", but the first paragraph only says you can do it "from the billing page". There's no link to that page, no path such as "Settings → Billing → Payment method", and no numbered steps. Everything after that is eligibility, a pointer elsewhere, and what happens on a decline. Someone who came to change their card finishes reading and still has to go and find the control, which is the whole reason they opened the page. It also means the user has to remember where "the billing page" is from somewhere else (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall). Fix: put a short numbered list of three or four steps right under the title, with "billing page" linked straight to it, before any of the policy text. Then the policy paragraphs can follow as context.
2. **The decline schedule can be read two ways, and the reading decides when the user loses their plan (defect).** In "When the new card is declined": "We try a declined card again after three days and after seven days… If the card is still declined after the second attempt, the subscription moves to the free plan." "Second attempt" could mean the retry at day three (the original charge plus one retry) or the retry at day seven (the second retry). "After seven days" could count from the first decline or from the day-three retry. A reader who takes the generous reading thinks they have a week and gets downgraded at day three. Downgrades and deadlines are what people hold you to later. Fix: name the days and the outcome outright, for example "If the payment fails, we try again 3 days later and 7 days later. If the 7-day retry also fails, your workspace moves to the free plan that day." Also say what "free plan" takes away from them.
3. **The sidebar doesn't mark where you are (convention).** In "In this section", "Update a payment card" looks exactly like its siblings, and "Billing" in the top nav is unmarked too. The breadcrumb helps, but the list you'd use to move to the next billing task doesn't show your place. Its label also doesn't match the page title ("Update a payment card" vs "Update the card that pays for your subscription"), so the link back to this page is harder to match up. Fix: mark the current item with one cue, such as bold weight or a left bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist"), and make the sidebar label and the page title the same words.

**Smaller things.**
- There's no search in the header. On a help centre most visitors arrive with a question, not a category. Add a search field to the top bar (Nielsen 7; Jakob's law).
- Body lines run about 83–85 characters, a little past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column by about 10% or set the body slightly larger.
- The first paragraph doesn't say what happens if the old card fails on an invoice that's already open. Someone changing an expired card will want to know, so add one sentence.

**What's working.**
- The structure is clear and easy to keep: logo, sidebar and breadcrumb share edges and baselines. The title, bold H2 and body are told apart by size and weight. Links are blue and underlined, so you never wonder what's clickable.
- The copy talks to the reader ("you", "your workspace") and the link text says where it goes ("roles and permissions", "paying by invoice"). Keep that voice when you add the steps.
- The H2 "When the new card is declined" names exactly the situation an anxious reader is scanning for. More of the page should be chunked this way.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Keyboard focus styles on the nav, sidebar and inline links. How the sidebar collapses on narrow screens.
