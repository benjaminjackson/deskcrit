**First read.** The eye lands on the big dark rectangle top left and finds a grey-brown "default user" silhouette, the icon an app shows when nobody has uploaded a photo. It drops to the bold headline, "An evening with Ines Varga on her new novel, The Salt Year", and reads this as a bookshop event page. Then it meets the right half of the screen, which is empty from the nav bar to the bottom. The page feels unfinished, like a template whose image and second column never got filled in.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the event page on an independent bookshop's website. Its job is to get local readers to reserve a free seat for an author reading.

**Verdict.** The copy and the type are solid, but the page doesn't do its job yet. The largest thing on it is a placeholder, half the screen is empty, and the date and place a booker needs are either questionable or missing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image is a generic placeholder, and it's the first thing anyone sees.** The silhouette in the 600 × 330 block (top left) is the biggest and darkest element, so the page opens on nothing. It doesn't show the author or the book, and a dull brown shape on near-black barely separates from its background anyway. A literary evening sells on a face and a cover. Without them the event looks like a stub, and the headline has to do all the work. *Defect* (common sense: an empty placeholder in the dominant slot). If this is a picture that didn't load, the page is fine once it does. If it's the intended fallback, replace it with the author's portrait or the cover of *The Salt Year*, so the image and the headline make one idea instead of the image just sitting there.
2. **Half the screen is empty, so the layout looks broken rather than calm.** Everything sits in a ~600 px column on the left, and the right half from x≈650 to the edge holds nothing. White space that does work is gathered against something dense and shaped on purpose (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts"). This space just looks left over, and all the visual weight tips to the left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). It reads as a mobile layout stretched onto a desktop. *Convention.* Either use the right column for the practical details (date, venue, how booking works, the book itself) next to the hero, or centre and widen the composition so the space looks chosen.
3. **The details a booker acts on are incomplete, and the date may be wrong.** "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. If it means 2026, 13 November is a Friday, so the weekday and the date contradict each other. If it means an earlier year, the event is over and the button still invites people to book. Either way, some people will turn up on the wrong night or book something that already happened. Nowhere does the page say where the reading is ("Find us" is only in the nav), and there's no end time. *Defect* (copy on dates is what viewers hold you to). Check the date, add the year if events run far ahead, and put the venue address on the page next to the time.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Events" doesn't look like the current page, though that's where we are. Mark it with weight, colour or an underline (Nielsen 1, visibility of system status).

**What's working.**
- **The copy.** The headline names the person, the occasion and the book in one sentence. The body line says exactly what happens ("a reading, a short talk about the island… questions from the floor") and adds a concrete warm detail ("Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm"). Keep that plainness.
- **The ask.** "Book a free seat" names one exact action and settles the price question before it's asked. It's the only dark button on the page, so it's clearly the main action.
- **The type and colour.** There are three clear levels: a bold headline at about 36 px, body at about 17 px, and the date as a small bold label in rust. The headline's two lines are set tight enough to read as one unit. The rust date is the only accent on a neutral page, which is restrained and suits a bookshop.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the silhouette is a picture that failed to load or the real fallback. Check in a live browser with the real event data.
- How the page reflows on mobile, and whether the button has a visible focus state.
