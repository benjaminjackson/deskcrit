**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Plans for every size of business", drops to the three prices, then goes to the one blue button, "Start free trial" under Small team. It reads as a plain, calm SaaS pricing page for an accounting or invoicing tool, aimed at small-business owners. The two versions look the same at first glance. The only difference is the grey of the plan descriptions and feature lists, which is mid-grey in version 1 and a darker grey in version 2.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a pricing page: a small-business owner should be able to pick the plan that fits their size and start a trial.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job. Version 2 does it better because the text people actually compare plans on, the descriptions and feature rows, is easy to read there. In version 1 that text is faint. Nothing else changed, and nothing else in the layout gets in the way.

**What would move it most**
1. **Faint feature text in version 1 (defect, fixed in version 2).** In all three cards, the one-line description ("For one person who sends a few invoices a month") and the four feature rows are a light grey on white in version 1. They look close to or below the 4.5:1 contrast minimum for normal text (WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3). These rows are what someone reads to tell $9 from $24 from $58: invoice limits, bank feeds, how many people payroll covers. In version 1 they fade back toward the hairline dividers, so the comparison takes effort and readers with low vision may skip it. Version 2 darkens that text so it clearly stands apart from the dividers, while staying lighter than the plan names and prices, so the order of importance holds. Keep version 2's value. Measure it to confirm it clears 4.5:1.

**Smaller things.**
- Every card has an empty band about 60 px tall below its button. It looks unused rather than planned. Trim the bottom padding so it matches the top, or put a line there that earns the space, such as "No card needed" under the trial buttons.
- Copy (taste): "Access for your accountant" and "Phone support on weekdays" are clear. "Talk to us" is a vague ask. Something like "Book a call about your setup" tells a growing firm what will actually happen.

**What's working.**
- There is one primary button, the blue one on the middle plan, and the side plans use quiet grey buttons. That steers the reader toward the recommended plan without a badge.
- The type has clear levels: headline, then price, then plan name, then body, with "a month" kept small next to each price. Prices are easy to compare across the row because they sit on the same line in every card.
- The Growing firm card opens with "Everything in Small team", so readers don't have to rebuild the feature list in their heads.
