Under the message box there is only "Send message" in plain black text, and nothing tells the eye this is the place to finish. It reads as a small design studio's contact page: calm, tidy and quiet, and a bit generic.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a studio's contact page. Its job is to get a likely client to write in about a project and press send.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are competent, but the page fails at the one thing it exists for: the submit control doesn't look like a button, so the form has no visible finish. Fix that first. Everything else is minor.

**What would move it most**
1. **The submit control is plain text, not a button** (defect). "Send message" at the bottom left of the card is set in the body face, size, weight and colour, with no fill, border or underline. The blue underlined nav and footer links look more clickable than it does. A visitor who has just typed their brief has to guess whether that line is a label or a control, and the eye has no last thing to land on, so the form seems to end in the air. Users expect a submit button at the end of a form, and Norman's point about affordance applies: a control should look like what it does. Approved fix: give the main action the shape of a button, with a fill or a border, so it looks like something to click. On this page that means a filled button in one strong colour, the only filled element on the screen, sized so it's clearly a target. If the screenshot caught the page before its button styles loaded, check it in a live browser. As shown, it's broken.

**Smaller things.**
- **Nav, top right:** "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing tells the visitor which page they're on (Nielsen 1; Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist). Mark the current page with one cue, such as bold, a heavier underline or the text colour.
- **Form fields:** none is marked required or optional (Budiu, NN/g). Put an asterisk after each required label with a one-line key at the top, or add "(optional)" after the optional ones.
- **Intro text and form:** the intro asks for "a budget range and a rough timeline", but the form gives them nowhere to go, so the visitor has to remember to put them in the message. Add two optional fields, or a dropdown for budget. That's common sense, not a rule.
- **Input borders:** the mid-grey outlines on white look close to or below 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure them, and darken them a step if they fall short.
- **Grid:** the card ends at about x=1068, while the header content runs to about x=1224, so the right edge of the content doesn't match the header's margin. Align the card's right edge with the nav's right edge, or make the two columns a deliberate split of the full width.
- **Headline break:** "Tell us about your / project" splits the phrase and leaves "project" alone on the second line. Break after "about", or widen the column so the line breaks at a phrase.
- **Copy (taste):** "Tell us about your project" could sit on any agency's site. The reply promise ("we reply within two working days") is the most specific and convincing line on the page. Consider giving that kind of specificity to the headline as well, for example by naming the kind of work you take on.

**What's working.**
- **Visible labels:** each field has a short bold label above it, closer to its own field than to the one before, so the form scans quickly and is accessible.
- **The intro paragraph:** it's a good line length (about 50 characters) in a readable grey. It sets a concrete expectation (two working days) and tells people what to include, which lowers the effort of writing in.
- **Restraint:** one family, a clear three-level type scale (headline, body, labels), one link blue and plenty of space. Keep all of this when you add the button, so that the button becomes the only strong colour on the page.
