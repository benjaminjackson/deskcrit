**First read.** The eye goes to "Before you board" right away. The headline is the only bold, large thing on the page and has plenty of space around it. Then it hits three grey-black blocks of capitals that look like a warning notice or a legal disclaimer, not friendly travel information. The page reads as a ferry's passenger notes. It feels calm and orderly until the body text starts, and then it starts to feel like being shouted at.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or panel that tells ferry passengers the basics (how often it runs, bikes, winter service) before they travel.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the piece fails at its one job of being read easily. All of the running text is set in capitals, which makes three short paragraphs slow and tiring to read. That one choice is the biggest problem. The copy also leaves out the times a passenger actually needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **Body text set entirely in capitals (defect).** All three paragraphs (lines from about y=180 to y=420) are in capitals. Without the ups and downs of lower-case letters, words lose their shape. The reader has to spell their way through each line, and the even grey wall makes it easy to lose your place when moving to the next line. Hochuli says text in capitals is "particularly difficult to read." The capitals also give friendly, practical notes the tone of a warning. Fix: "Set sentences and paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for headings shorter than one line, labels, and captions" (Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps"). The "PASSENGER NOTES" label is already doing this correctly: capitals on a short line, with extra letter spacing.
2. **The copy gives no times (defect).** "Between early morning and late evening" and "in winter" are the facts a passenger plans around, and neither gives an answer. Someone reading this can't tell whether the first boat leaves at 6:00 or 8:00, or whether "winter" starts in October or December, so they could miss a crossing. The page sends them to "the shelter at each landing," which only helps someone who is already standing at the landing. Fix: give the first and last departure times, the dates when the winter timetable starts and ends, and a link to the timetable on this page. "Every forty minutes" and "about twelve minutes" already do the right thing, because they're exact and easy to plan around.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing separately.

**What's working.**
- The type levels are clear: a small, tracked, grey label, then a bold headline, then body text. There are three sizes with big steps between them, and the headline sits closer to the text it introduces than to the label above it. Keep this.
- Everything shares one left edge (around x=120), and the lines run about 70 characters, inside the readable 45 to 75 range (Bringhurst). That measure will still work once the text is in mixed case, and it will be about 10% narrower.
- The text is broken into three short paragraphs, one topic each (schedule, bikes, winter), so readers can skim to the part they need.
