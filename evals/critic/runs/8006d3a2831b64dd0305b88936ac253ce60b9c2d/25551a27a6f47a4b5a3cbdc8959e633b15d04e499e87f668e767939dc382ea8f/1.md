**First read.** The eye lands on "Before you board", which is clearly the headline, and then hits a grey wall: three paragraphs set entirely in capitals. It reads as a ferry service's passenger-information page. The top of the page feels calm and modern. The body feels like a telegram, or like a notice that is shouting.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page, or a section of one, that tells first-time ferry passengers how the service runs before they turn up. If not, tell me and I'll re-judge it.

**Verdict.** It does not do its job well. The layout is sound, but the body is the whole point of the page and setting it all in capitals makes it slow and tiring to read. The copy also leaves out the departure times a passenger actually needs to plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body is set in all capitals.** This covers all three paragraphs, eight lines from "THE RIVER FERRY RUNS…" down to "…AT EACH LANDING." In capitals every word is a plain rectangle, so the reader loses the word shapes they normally recognise at a glance. They have to read letter by letter, and the block turns into an even grey texture with nothing to grab onto. That is exactly wrong for a page someone skims on the way to the pier. It also undercuts the friendly tone the mixed-case headline sets up. *Convention, and here a defect.* Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps": "Don't capitalize whole paragraphs". Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, calls text in capitals "particularly difficult to read". Fix: set the sentences and paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for short labels like the "PASSENGER NOTES" eyebrow, which already does that well. If this is a CSS `text-transform` left on by mistake, removing it fixes everything at once.
2. **The schedule is vague exactly where readers will act on it.** The first paragraph says "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". Is "early morning" 6:00 or 8:00? Does the forty-minute cycle start on the hour? The winter paragraph says "one boat each hour" but gives no dates for winter, and then sends readers to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing". So someone reading this at home learns they have to go to the pier to find out when the boat leaves. These are times passengers plan around and later hold the operator to, so an ambiguous line here is a defect, not a style note. *Defect (copy).* Fix: give the first and last sailings and the minutes past the hour they leave, say when the winter timetable starts and ends, and link to the timetable on this page instead of only pointing to the shelter.

**What's working.**
- The structure is clear: a small tracked grey label, then a big bold headline, then the body. That is three distinct levels with clear steps between them. Keep that scale.
- The single left-aligned column runs to about 65–75 characters a line, with comfortable line spacing and clear gaps between paragraphs. Once the case is fixed, the setting is already good for reading.
- "Before you board" is a heading written from the passenger's point of view, and each paragraph covers one topic (schedule, bicycles, winter). It's well chunked.
