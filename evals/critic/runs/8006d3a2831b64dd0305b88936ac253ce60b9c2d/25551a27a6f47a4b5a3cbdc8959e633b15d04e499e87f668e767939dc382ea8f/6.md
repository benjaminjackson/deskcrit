**First read.** My eye lands on the bold "Before you board" headline, then hits a wall of capitals underneath. Those three paragraphs feel like being shouted at, or like an old telex. The small grey "PASSENGER NOTES" label above the headline hardly registers. It reads as a passenger-information page for a river ferry. The calm, well-spaced layout feels quiet and orderly until you reach the body text.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page telling ferry passengers, including people with bikes, when the boat runs and how boarding works, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, but setting every paragraph in capitals makes the one thing the page exists for, reading the details, slow and tiring. The copy also leaves out the times a passenger needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is all capitals** (defect, backed by a convention). All three paragraphs, about eight lines, are in capitals. Capitals all share the same rectangular outline, so the eye loses the word shapes it uses to read quickly. Every line has to be read letter by letter, and nothing stands out as more important. "No extra charge" and "one boat each hour" get the same weight as "in calm weather." The capitals are also at default letter spacing, which makes them denser still. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says caps suit headings shorter than one line, and to "don't capitalize whole paragraphs." Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) says text in capitals is especially hard to read. Fix: set the paragraphs in normal upper and lower case. Keep capitals for short labels like "PASSENGER NOTES," which already has the extra letter spacing that capitals need.
2. **The copy leaves out the facts a passenger acts on** (defect). "Between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing, and "every forty minutes" doesn't say from when. "In winter" gives no dates. The page then sends readers to "the shelter at each landing" for printed timetables, which they can only see once they are already standing at the landing. Someone planning a trip can't tell whether a 6:30 or a 22:00 crossing exists, and a wrong guess means a missed boat. Fix: give the first and last sailing times, the winter months, and a link to the timetable, or the timetable itself, on this page.

**Smaller things.**
- Copy, my own view (taste): the headline "Before you board" promises steps to take before boarding, but only the bicycle paragraph is about boarding. Either name what the page holds (times, bikes, winter service) or rewrite the content as before-boarding instructions.

**What's working.**
- The headline is the only large, heavy element, with one clear step down to the body. The eye knows where to start, so keep that two-level scale when you fix the case.
- The reading column runs about 65–75 characters per line, with comfortable line spacing and clear space between paragraphs. That is a good measure, so keep the column width when switching to mixed case. Lowercase text will fit more words per line, so check the line length again afterwards.
- The small, letter-spaced grey "PASSENGER NOTES" label is used correctly: capitals on a short label with extra letter spacing. It is the model for where capitals belong.
