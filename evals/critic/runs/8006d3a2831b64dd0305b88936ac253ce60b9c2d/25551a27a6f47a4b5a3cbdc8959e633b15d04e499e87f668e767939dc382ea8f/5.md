**First read.** The eye goes to "Before you board" at top left and reads it as a short notice from a ferry operator to passengers. Then it hits three paragraphs set entirely in capitals, and reading slows to a crawl. The calm, modern heading sits over body text that feels like a telegram or a warning notice. The empty right third and lower half read as an ordinary web page, not as a problem.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page or info panel telling first-time ferry passengers how the service runs before they travel. They need to scan it quickly for times, the bike rule and winter changes.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The structure is fine, but the body text is set in all capitals, which makes the only content anyone came for hard to read. And the copy leaves out the facts a passenger will act on: when the first and last boats run, and when "winter" starts.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body text is in all capitals.** All three paragraphs, from "THE RIVER FERRY RUNS…" to "…AT EACH LANDING.", are set in caps. Capitals all have the same height and outline, so readers can't use word shapes. Every line has to be read letter by letter, and the three paragraphs blur into one grey slab where nothing stands out to skim. The caps aren't letterspaced either (the "PASSENGER NOTES" label above them is), so they look cramped as well. *Convention, and here a defect*: Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "All caps" ("Don't capitalize whole paragraphs"); Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*: text in capitals "is particularly difficult to read and needs a large amount of space." Fix: set sentences and paragraphs in upper and lower case, and keep capitals for the one-line label at the top. That also removes the need to letterspace them.

2. **The copy is vague exactly where passengers will act.** "Between early morning and late evening" doesn't tell anyone whether they can catch a 6:15 boat or a 22:00 one. Those are the two times people plan around and later hold the operator to. One reader assumes 7 am, another 6, and someone gets stranded. "In winter the timetable thins to one boat each hour" has the same problem, since it doesn't say which months. "Printed timetables are kept in the shelter at each landing" then sends the reader to the pier for the answer this page should give. *Defect.* Fix: give the first and last sailing times and the winter dates, or link straight to the timetable. Say "every 40 minutes" in figures so it can be scanned.

**Smaller things.**
- The bike paragraph says what riders do but not what it means for the reader. Speak to them directly, e.g. "Wait until foot passengers have boarded, then wheel your bike on…", so it reads as an instruction they can follow.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clean: three levels (small tracked label, bold heading, body) with big steps between them. Each size has an obvious job, so keep it when you reset the body text.
- The column is a good reading width, with lines of roughly 68 to 75 characters, comfortable spacing between lines, and clear gaps between paragraphs. It will read well once the text is in mixed case.
- "Before you board" is a plain, useful heading that says when to read this.
