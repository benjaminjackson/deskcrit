I've read the help-page screenshot (1280 × 800, so no crops were needed) and run the full checklist.

**First read.** The eye lands on "Changing your payment card", then runs down three bold subheads. It reads as a plain, calm help-centre article for a SaaS customer whose card is expiring or has failed. Nothing gets in the way. It feels quiet and trustworthy, close to a standard docs template.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article that a paying account owner opens while they change their card in another tab. They need to finish the task, and know the consequences, without contacting support.

**Verdict.** The typography is clean and the page mostly does its job. But the article skips the step the title promises: making the new card the one you're charged. And its one line about a deadline, what happens when payments fail, can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**

1. **The steps that "change" the card are missing.** The intro says "The new card is used from your next invoice". But "Add a new card" only adds a card. "Remove an old card" then says a card can be removed "once another card is set as the default", and nothing on the page says how to set a default. A reader who follows the page from top to bottom adds a card. They can't tell whether it will be charged, and they get stuck when they try to remove the old one. That's the exact task in the headline. **Defect.** The fix:
   - Write the whole path: add the card, make it the default, remove the old one.
   - Say plainly whether a newly added card becomes the default by itself.
   - Because the reader is clicking along in another tab, set these as numbered steps with the button names in bold ("**Settings** › **Billing** › **Add card**"). Prose makes them hunt for their place each time they look back. That last part is **taste**, argued from Krug's point that help should be scannable.

2. **"If a payment fails" is unclear about time, and time is what the reader will act on.** "After a third failed try the account moves to read-only" first reads as "a third failed try the account". There's a comma missing after "try". More importantly, it has two readings:
   - The original charge counts as the first try, so read-only comes after about six days.
   - It means three retries, so read-only comes after about nine days.

   An owner who plans around the wrong one loses access earlier than they expected. "Read-only" also stops at the fact and doesn't say what it means for them. **Defect.** The fix: give the date in their terms ("If your card is still failing 6 days after the first attempt, your account becomes read-only: you and your team can view everything but can't …"). Also say what lifts it ("as soon as a payment goes through").

3. **The subheads float halfway between sections.** At "Add a new card", "Remove an old card" and "If a payment fails", each heading has about 50 px above it and about 50 px below. It doesn't clearly belong to the text it introduces, so the page reads as an even stack of bands rather than three sections. Scanning for "my payment failed" takes a beat longer than it should. **Convention**, partly supported: Bringhurst, section 2.2.2, allows "more space above than below" for subheads, and every one of his worked examples does it. The fix: roughly halve the space under each heading and add that space above it.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line length:** body lines run about 78 to 84 characters ("We place a hold of one pound … three working" is about 84). That's just over Bringhurst's 45 to 75 range. Narrow the column by about 10% to get nearer 66.
- **"days." sits alone** on the last line of the hold paragraph, at about y 376. A small change to the width or wording pulls it up.

**What's working.**
- **The type hierarchy.** It's one sans family in four clearly separate levels: a large bold title, bold subheads, regular body, and a grey breadcrumb. Headings differ from the text in weight as well as size, so nothing needs a second look. Keep that scale.
- **Answers up front.** The intro states the two things an anxious payer wants to know before anything else: when the change takes effect, and that past payments aren't touched.
- **Exact facts.** The hold amount, the release time and the retry interval are all given as specific facts rather than "a small amount" or "shortly". Keep that habit when you rewrite the failed-payment line.
