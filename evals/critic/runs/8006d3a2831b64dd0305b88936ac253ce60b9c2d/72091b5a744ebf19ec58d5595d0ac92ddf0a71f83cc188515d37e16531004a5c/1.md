**First read.** The eye goes straight to the black headline, "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY". Then it stalls, because the letters are so tight they run together: "ALL" looks like one shape, "NI" in MORNING merges into a single dark stroke, and "RR" in FERRY touches. After that the eye drops to the grey sentence, then stops, with half the frame empty to the right and below. It reads as a hero section or a poster for a free harbour ferry ride, and it feels calm, confident and a bit cold.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming it's a hero or poster announcing free ferry rides at a City Harbour open day, meant to get the public to turn up.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound: one clear focal point, a clean left edge and a sensible size order. But the headline is tracked so tight that its letters collide, so the most important words on the page are the hardest to read. The piece also never says when the open day is, so a reader who wants to go can't.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline letters collide (defect).** In "ALL ABOARD THE MORNING FERRY", the two Ls fuse, R and D touch, the stems of N and I merge in MORNING, and the R pair in FERRY touches. The spaces between words have shrunk almost to the gaps between letters, so "ABOARDTHE" nearly reads as one word. The reader has to decode each word's shape instead of taking the line in at a glance, which is the one thing a headline must allow. Tightening large type is right (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, p. 80; Bringhurst 2.1.1), but this has gone past tight into touching. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 30) says "individual letters should not touch," and a small hole is less bad than a collision. Bringhurst (2.1.6) goes further and says strings of capitals should get extra letter space, not less. **Fix:** open the tracking until every pair has a visible gap, especially LL, RD, NI and RR. Bring the word spaces back too, so the four words read as four words. It can still be tighter than text, just not touching.
2. **There's no date or time to act on (defect, copy).** "City Harbour Open Day" with "every half hour from the north landing" tells the reader how often and where, but not which day or from what time until what time. This is the line people will plan around and hold you to. As it stands, "morning ferry" and "every half hour" leave them guessing whether rides run only in the morning or all day. **Fix:** add the date and the hours in plain words, for example "Saturday 14 June, rides every half hour from 8 to 12." Put them in the kicker or the body line, where they sit next to the headline.

**Smaller things.**
- The kicker "CITY HARBOUR OPEN DAY" is small capitals set tight. They should be letterspaced, roughly 5–10% of the type size (Bringhurst 2.1.6), so the label reads as letters rather than a grey smudge.
- Taste: the empty right half and the lower third look like space left over rather than space planned. A grey-on-white, all-neutral palette for a "morning ferry" invitation feels more like a software landing page than a day out. Neither breaks a rule, but nothing here sets this event apart from anyone else's page.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear three-step order: a small kicker, a huge headline, then a quiet body line. Each level is plainly different in size and weight, so nobody wonders where to start. Keep those three steps.
- Everything hangs from one strong left edge at the same x, with generous margins. That keeps the page calm and is worth keeping through any revision.
- The body copy is concrete and human: "Bring a hat and a thermos" says what the morning will feel like better than a generic invitation would.
