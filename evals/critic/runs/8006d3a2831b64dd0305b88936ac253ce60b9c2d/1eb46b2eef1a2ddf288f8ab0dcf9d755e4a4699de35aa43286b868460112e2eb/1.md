**First read.** The eye lands on "About the trust" in bold. Then it meets a twelve-line block of grey-black text with nothing to grab onto. I skim the first line, "footpath, woods, quarry", and stop. The piece feels calm, competent and plain: well set, but with nothing to do.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the About section of a small conservation charity's website. It's for local people, and beyond explaining the trust, it should recruit volunteers and members.

**Verdict.** The type is well set, but the section doesn't do its main job. The one thing a reader could act on, the open invitation to the work days, is buried in the middle of a single paragraph and has no way to act on it. And the one date it gives can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The invitation is buried, and there's nothing to act on.** The sentence "Work days run every second Saturday, and anyone is welcome…" starts at the end of line 7. It's the only thing on the page addressed to the reader, and it has the same weight as "It was set up in 1987". A skimmer reads the first line or two and leaves without ever learning they could come along. There's no time, no meeting place, no sign-up link and no contact, so even a reader who finds it can't act on it. *Defect.* Fix: pull the work-day offer out as its own short block with a sub-heading that speaks to the reader, such as "Come and help on a Saturday". Give the time, the place, what to bring (nothing), and one specific next step, for example "See the next work day and where to meet". The "nobody needs any experience" and "we provide tools, gloves, and a hot drink" lines are the real persuasion, so keep them right next to the ask.
2. **"Every second Saturday" is ambiguous, and it's the line people will plan around.** It can mean every other Saturday or the second Saturday of each month. Someone who reads it the wrong way turns up on a day with nobody there, which is the worst first contact a volunteer group can have. *Defect* (copy). Fix: say it one way that can't be misread ("the second Saturday of every month, 9am to 12") and ideally show the next date.
3. **One unbroken block mixes four topics.** The 12-line paragraph covers what the trust looks after, its history, its size and the volunteer offer. Readers on screen scan rather than read, so a dense block gets skipped and each fact gets lost in the others (Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web"). *Convention, and it costs something here.* Fix: break it into short paragraphs by topic: what we look after, how we started, who we are, and come and help. Putting the volunteer offer last and setting it apart as in finding 1 does most of the work.

**What's working.**
- **The type is calm and readable.** There's one family, with a bold heading against a regular body, and two clearly different sizes. Lines run about 60 characters, with generous leading and an even ragged right edge. Keep the measure and the spacing when you split the text.
- **The copy is concrete and local.** "Eleven miles of footpath", "the old quarry above the village", "cutting back gorse where it has grown across the path" sound like a real place and real people, not a template. Keep that voice, especially in the new ask.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The right 40% of the frame is empty. That's fine if this is the site's reading column, but check whether a sidebar or picture was meant to load there.
