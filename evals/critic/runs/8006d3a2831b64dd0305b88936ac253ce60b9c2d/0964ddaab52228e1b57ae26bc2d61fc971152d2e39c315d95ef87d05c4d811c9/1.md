**First read.** The eye lands straight on "River ferry timetable", a bold dark heading in the top-left, and expects a schedule underneath. What it finds is a smudge of grey lines about the size of a postage stamp, then a white field covering roughly nine-tenths of the frame. It reads as a page that failed to render or was zoomed out by mistake, not as a timetable. The feeling is that something is missing.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: a public web page where ferry passengers look up when the next boat leaves.

**Verdict.** It fails. The heading promises departure times, and the page has none: just three paragraphs of prose set so small that no one can read them. You can't use it to catch a ferry.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no timetable** (defect). Under a heading that names a timetable, the page has no times, no stops, no table and no next departure. Someone who came to find out when the boat leaves has nothing to look up, and the heading becomes a promise the page breaks. From the fragments I could make out, the prose talks *about* the service (roughly "the river ferry runs from … every forty minutes … each crossing takes about …") without listing departures. Even read out loud, that makes passengers work out the schedule in their heads. The page needs a table of departures by stop and time, with tabular figures in aligned columns. The next sailing should be the first thing the eye finds, and the prose should shrink to a short note under the table. If a timetable is meant to load in the empty space, this screenshot shows it missing; check the live page.
2. **The body text is unreadable** (defect). The three paragraphs under the heading look about 4 px tall, roughly a tenth the height of the heading. Even at full resolution the letters can't be told apart. That is far below any reading size: the usual floor for body text is about 16 px on the web, and Apple gives 11 pt as the smallest text on iOS. Whatever these paragraphs say (service changes, how weather affects crossings) is lost, and on a transport page that is often the part people need most. The jump from a ~40 px heading to ~4 px text is not a type scale. It looks like a missing style or a broken scale factor. Set the body at normal reading size (around 16 to 18 px) and keep the heading as the one large level.
3. **The layout is stranded in one corner** (defect, made worse by 1 and 2). All the content fits inside about 480 × 200 px in the top-left, and the rest of the 1280 × 800 frame is empty. This is leftover space, not space chosen to set off a block of content (Ruder, *Typography*, "Contrasts"). The page also tips hard to the upper left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The viewer reads it as broken. Once a real table and readable body text are in place, most of this fixes itself: give the content a proper column and let the table take the width it needs.

**Smaller things.**
- I can't judge the body text's contrast, stroke weight or widows at this size. Recheck all three once the text is at reading size.

**What's working.**
- The heading is good. It's plain, bold, tightly spaced, dark on white, and says exactly what the page should be, so it gives the eye one clear place to start. Keep it as the single top level.
- The heading and the paragraphs share one sharp left edge and a sensible margin. That gives you a grid line to build the table on.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the timetable and the body size are missing because of a capture or rendering fault (failed data fetch, a CSS scale or zoom bug) rather than the design itself. Load the page in a live browser and check the computed body font size and whether a table element exists.
