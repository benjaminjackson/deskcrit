I've finished the checklist and the reference reads, and I'm writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye goes to the bold serif headline, "Notes for the summer fete", then gets pulled across the body by five emphasised phrases (two in italic, three in bold) before it has read a whole sentence. It reads as a short, calm, bookish notice from the organisers. The type is good and the page is quiet. But the emphasis pattern is the first thing you notice after the headline, and it doesn't add up to anything.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a notice from the organisers to stallholders and volunteers (and maybe visitors), giving the practical rules for the day: when to arrive, cakes, raffle, parking.

**Verdict.** The typography is sound. But as a notice the piece doesn't do its job: the emphasis is random, so the reader can't skim it for the deadlines, and the copy leaves out the date and place and words one of its deadlines so it can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The emphasis follows no system, so skimming it gives you the wrong picture.** *Defect.* There are three deadlines on the page. "arrive before ten" and "three o'clock sharp" are italic, but "collected by four" is bold. Bold also goes on "labelled with their ingredients" and "on the field only", which are rules, not times. A reader who skims the bold, which is what bold invites, gets "labelled with their ingredients / collected by four / on the field only". That misses the 10 o'clock arrival, the one thing every stallholder has to act on first. The rule: emphasis of one kind should mean one thing everywhere (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: a programme, not one-off choices). Five emphases in about 60 words also means none of them stands out. Fix: choose one signal for "things you must do by a time", put it on all three times and nothing else, and drop the rest of the emphasis. Even better for a notice like this, pull the times out as a short list (10:00 arrive · 15:00 raffle draw · 16:00 prizes claimed), so the reader finds them without reading the paragraphs.

2. **The raffle deadline can be read two ways, and the wrong reading costs people their prizes.** *Defect (copy).* "All tickets must be collected by four or the prizes go back in the box." Reading 1: winners must claim their prizes by four. Reading 2: everyone has to hand in or pick up their tickets by four, which is impossible anyway, since the draw is at three. A ticket-holder who reads it the second way, or doesn't link "tickets" to "prizes", will turn up late and lose. The cut word is the verb that holds the meaning together. Say who does what: "If your ticket wins, collect your prize by 4 pm, or it goes back in the box."

3. **A notice about a day doesn't say which day or where.** *Defect (copy).* There's no date, no venue, and no time the gates open, even though "by the time the gates open" depends on that time. "The trestle by the door" assumes the reader already knows which building. Insiders will cope. Anyone new, which is exactly who reads notes like these, has to ask someone. Fix: put the date and place right under the headline as one line of supporting text, and say which door. This also gives the headline something specific to say. "Notes for the summer fete" could be about any fete.

**Smaller things.**
- The two paragraphs mix audiences and topics (stallholder setup and cakes, then raffle and parking for everyone). Grouping by who it's for ("If you're running a stall" / "Everyone") would let each reader skip what isn't theirs. This is taste, but it's cheap to do.
- The bold phrase "on the field only" breaks across lines, so "field only" sits alone on the last line. A minor rag issue that goes away if you drop the emphasis.

**What's working.**
- The type is set with care. A sturdy text serif, a heading that differs from the body in both size and weight, lines of about 70 characters, and generous leading make the body easy to read. Keep all of that.
- The restrained layout: one column, flush left, with clear margins and open space below. That suits a short practical notice, and anything you add (date line, time list) has room.
