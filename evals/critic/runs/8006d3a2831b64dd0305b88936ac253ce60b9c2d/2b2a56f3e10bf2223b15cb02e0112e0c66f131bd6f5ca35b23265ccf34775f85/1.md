**First read.** In version 1 the eye skips the headline and lands on the three card titles, "Perennials / Fruit trees / Workshops". They are set bigger than "Hardy plants grown on our hillside", so the page reads as a category menu with a caption above it. In version 2 the eye lands on the headline first. It reads as a small, calm, local nursery's home page, and the cards come after it as the way in. Both feel quiet and earthy, and both look unfinished because of the flat colour blocks where the photos should be.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page of a small plant nursery, and it should tell a visiting gardener what makes it different (hardy, grown outdoors locally) and send them into the shop or the workshops.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is the headline size. In version 1 the headline (~26 px) is smaller than the card titles (~28 px), so the most important line on the page comes second. Version 2 makes it the largest thing on the page by far, and the page now reads in the right order. Version 2 is still held back by the empty image tiles and by having no call to action.

**What would move it most** (for version 2)
1. **The three tiles are flat colour blocks, not pictures.** *Defect.* For a nursery the plants are the product, and the 340×150 tinted rectangles leave the visitor nothing to want. The pale sage, grey-green and khaki also look like a page that hasn't finished loading. If these are real images that didn't load, check in a live browser. If they are the design, use photos: a perennial bed, a fruiting dwarf tree, hands grafting. Then the tiles and the headline's claim ("hardy, grown on our hillside") show one idea together.
2. **Nothing on the page asks the visitor to do anything.** *Defect.* There is no button. The cards aren't styled as links (no arrow, no "Browse perennials →"). The only actions are the small nav links, top right. A visitor who believes the headline has nowhere obvious to go. Add one primary action under the intro that names a concrete next step, e.g. "Shop 300+ perennials", and make each card's title or tile visibly clickable.
3. **The copy states the advantage but doesn't land it on the reader.** *Taste.* "Ready for a cold garden on the day they arrive" is the best line on the page, and it is buried in grey body text. Say it to the reader ("…so they survive your first frost, not just ours"). Or compare with what they already know: plants from a garden centre's heated greenhouse that sulk once planted out. Then the visitor judges the value on terms they already trust.

**Smaller things.**
- The card 1 description leaves the widow "pots." alone on a line. Trim it ("Over 300 kinds, in 1 and 2 litre pots") so it fits on one line like the other two. "Litre" also needs a unit form, since "one and two litre pots" reads oddly.
- The nav doesn't show which page you are on. Mark the current section.
- The lower ~180 px of the frame is empty. This is less of a problem in version 2, because the larger headline pushes the content down, but a next section or a footer peeking in would show the page continues.

**What's working.**
- Version 2's headline: big, heavy, tightly set over two lines, with a clean drop to the 18 px intro and the 28 px card titles. That is a real type scale with obvious steps. Keep it.
- One grid throughout. The logo, headline, intro and first card share a left edge, and the three cards have equal gutters, with the last card's edge matching the nav's right edge.
- A restrained green-on-off-white palette that fits a nursery without falling into cliché.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the tiles are unloaded images or deliberate placeholders. Whether the cards are clickable (hover states).
