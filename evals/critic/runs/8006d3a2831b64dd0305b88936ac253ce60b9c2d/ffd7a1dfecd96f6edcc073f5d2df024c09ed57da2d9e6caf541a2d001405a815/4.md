**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "Notes for ferry passengers," then drops into three even paragraphs of grey-black prose. It reads as a calm, tidy information page, like a help article or a noticeboard posting. It feels quiet and competent. Nothing pulls the eye after the title, so you read it top to bottom like a letter.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page telling ferry passengers (current or planned) when the boat runs, how long it takes, and the rules for bicycles, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and the typesetting is sound. But the page fails at its actual job. A passenger can't plan a trip from it, because the copy never gives a single clock time and every schedule fact is buried in prose.

**What would move it most**
1. **The schedule facts are vague exactly where the passenger has to act on them** (defect). Look at these phrases:
   - "Between early morning and late evening" (paragraph 1)
   - "every forty minutes" (from when?)
   - "In winter" (which months?)
   - "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" (paragraph 3)

   These are the lines a passenger plans around and later holds the operator to, and each can be read more than one way. "Before dusk" could mean 15:30 or 17:00 depending on the date. "The quay" could be the market quay only, or either landing. A rider who reads it the wrong way is stranded on the wrong bank. The page also never says whether boats leave the market quay on the same 40-minute pattern, or only from the north landing.

   The fix is to state times as times: the first and last departure from each landing, the interval, the winter dates and winter last boats. If they really do vary, give the rule plus where to find today's times. Right now the page sends people to "the shelter at each landing," which is useless to someone who isn't there yet.
2. **Facts a passenger looks up are set as continuous prose, so finding one means rereading everything** (taste, argued; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: scannable pages). Someone checking "when's the last boat?" has to read all three paragraphs, and the answer is in the last sentence of the last one.

   The page is short enough that this is not a layout defect, but the content is reference material, not narrative. Pull the timetable into a small block of its own, near the top, with times in tabular form. Then keep the bicycle and luggage rules as one short paragraph. The title would also do more work if it said what the reader gets, for example "Ferry times and bicycle rules."

**Smaller things.** None worth listing; the typesetting holds up.

**What's working.**
- **The reading column.** Lines run about 60 to 72 characters, leading is generous, and the left edge is shared and flush with a soft rag. It reads easily, so keep the measure and spacing when restructuring.
- **The two-level type scale.** One bold title at about twice the body size, plus regular body text. It's clear with no near-miss sizes. If you add a timetable, introduce it with weight, not a third size close to these two.
- **Restraint.** No boxes, rules or ornament. The empty space sits in the right and lower areas, not scattered.
