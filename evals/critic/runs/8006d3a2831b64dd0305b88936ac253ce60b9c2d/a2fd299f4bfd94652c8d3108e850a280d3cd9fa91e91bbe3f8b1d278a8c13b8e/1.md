**First read.** I look first at the tallest purple bar, Growth at 4.0%. Next I see that Starter looks tiny next to it, about a third of its height. Then the eye goes to the table on the right. This reads as a clean, calm SaaS analytics page, and the first message it gives is "Growth converts far better than Starter."

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal analytics view that helps product and revenue people compare trial-to-paid conversion across plans for Q3 2025.

**Verdict.** The page is tidy and well set, but the chart overstates its own finding. The bars start at 2.5%, so differences of a fraction of a point look like multiples. A viewer comes away with the wrong size of the gap, and that is the one thing this page exists to show.

**What would move it most**
1. **The truncated bar axis makes the gaps look several times bigger than they are** (defect, convention). The value axis on the left starts at 2.5%. Growth (4.0%) looks about 2.5 times as tall as Starter (3.1%), but in the data it is only about 1.3 times as high. Enterprise (3.4%) looks far above Starter when the gap is 0.3 points. A reader who decides pricing or sales effort from bar heights will overweight the plan differences. Bar length encodes value, so bars must start at zero (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 17.1; Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"; Tufte's lie factor). Fix: start the axis at 0%. If the small differences are the point, switch to dots on a non-zero scale, which is allowed for position encodings when it is labelled.
2. **The definition and the subtitle give two different cut-offs** (defect, copy). The subtitle says a trial converts if it pays "within 30 days". The Definition says a trial converts "when the first invoice is paid" and counts trials still open on 30 October as not converted. That reads as one fixed cut-off date. Take a trial that started on 1 July and paid on day 45. Under the subtitle it is not converted. Under the definition it is. These rates are the numbers people will quote and hold the team to, so pick one rule and state it the same way in both places.
3. **The page doesn't say what the numbers mean** (taste, plus Few on metric context). "Trial to paid conversion by plan" names the topic, not the finding. Nothing tells the viewer whether 4.0% is good, whether it went up or down from Q2, or whether it beats a target. The viewer can see the ranking but can't act on it. Fix: give each rate a comparison, such as the previous quarter as a faint mark beside each bar or a target line (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Then use the title to state the takeaway in words.
4. **Small samples are presented as firm differences** (convention: Wilke, ch. 16). Enterprise is 38 paid out of 1,120 trials, and Growth is 118 paid. At these counts, a gap of a few tenths of a point between plans may be noise, but the chart presents every gap as solid. The table already holds the counts, which is good. Add a range or a short note on sample size so nobody reorganises around a 0.3-point gap.

**Smaller things.**
- The table gives trials and paid accounts but not the rate. Adding a "Rate" column would tie it directly to the chart.
- The grey axis tick labels and the source line look light at their small size. Measure them against 4.5:1.
- The lower right quarter of the page is empty. That's fine for now, but it's the natural place for the comparison data from finding 3.

**What's working.**
- Restraint. The page uses one purple, neutral greys, hairline gridlines, and direct value labels on the bars. Nearly all the ink in the chart is data. Keep this when you add a comparison: use grey for the earlier period and keep purple for the current one.
- The table is well set. It has tabular figures, right-aligned numbers, and light rules, and it gives the raw counts that let a careful reader check the rates. I checked all four and they match.
- The page is honest about its terms and source. A Definition block and a source line with exact dates are rare on dashboards and worth keeping, once the cut-off contradiction is fixed.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the period can be changed: no date or plan control is visible. If this is a live view and not a fixed report, people will need one.
