**First read.** You see three big KPI numbers along the top, then a large, clean three-line chart that rises gently left to right. It looks like a calm, well-made SaaS analytics overview. On a second look the chart has nothing written on it: no axis values, no dates, and no clue which line is which channel. The green deltas say everything is up, but one of them doesn't add up.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing or growth lead checks this weekly overview to see whether traffic and sign-ups are improving and which channels drive them.

**Verdict.** The layout is tidy and the hierarchy is fine, but the page doesn't do its job. The biggest element, "Visits by channel", has no axes and no line labels, so it can't answer which channel is which. And the conversion-rate card contradicts the two cards next to it.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart can't be read: there are no series labels, no axis values, and no dates.** It takes up about 60% of the screen. Its three lines (blue, orange, green) aren't named anywhere: no legend, no labels at the line ends. The y-axis has no numbers and the x-axis has no weeks. The viewer can see that "something" grew, but can't tell which channel, by how much, or when the dip around week 8 on the blue line happened. The empty strip left of the plot and the empty band under it are exactly where those labels belong. Since color is the only thing telling the series apart, a color-blind viewer is worse off still. Orange and green also differ mostly in hue, though their lightness differs a little. **Defect** (Tufte, *Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, on clear labelling; WCAG 1.4.1 *Use of Color*). Fix: put the channel name and current value at the right end of each line, as Tufte recommends, rather than in a legend. Add a few y-axis values with units and week labels on the x-axis. Once the y-axis has numbers, the viewer can also tell whether it starts at zero, which matters for judging how big these slopes really are.

2. **The conversion-rate delta looks wrong, and it's shown in green.** "Visits +6.1%" and "Sign-ups +2.4%" mean visits grew faster than sign-ups, so conversion must have gone down. Working back from the cards, last period was about 2.20% and this period is 2.12%, a drop of about 0.08 pts. The card says "+0.1 pts vs previous 12 weeks" in good-news green. A growth lead will act on this number and repeat it in a meeting. Either the calculation is wrong, or the metric is defined differently (for example, an average of weekly rates) and the card doesn't say so. Either way, a viewer who checks the cards against each other stops trusting the whole dashboard. **Defect** (common sense: numbers the viewer will act on must agree with each other). Fix: check the calculation. If the definition really is different, say so on the card. If conversion actually fell, show it in the "down" color.

3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** "Visits by channel" tells the viewer what the chart is, not what happened, so they have to work out the story themselves, and right now they can't (see 1). **Taste**: an approved source doesn't settle this, but on a weekly overview the viewer comes for the takeaway. Fix: a title or one-line caption that states the result in the viewer's terms, e.g. "Organic drove most of the growth; paid is flat". Label the week-8 dip if it matters.

**Smaller things.**
- Green has two jobs: it means "up/good" in the KPI deltas, and it's also the color of the bottom chart line, which can read as a "good" series. Pick a non-status hue for the series.
- The type scale is muddled: I count about seven sizes. The ~13 px deltas, ~14 px KPI labels and ~15 px "Last 12 weeks" sit within a pixel or two of each other, and "Overview" (~24) is close to the KPI figures (~28). Keep roughly four sizes and separate the small text by weight or color instead (Bringhurst, scale; Refactoring UI).
- If "Last 12 weeks" in the header is meant to be a date-range picker, it looks like plain text, with no chevron or button shape. Make it look clickable, or leave it alone if it really is just a label.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards: name, large figure, then a comparison against the previous period on each card. That's exactly right for a dashboard (Few), and the three cards line up on a clean grid with even gutters. Keep that structure.
- The chart's ink is restrained: faint gridlines, no frame inside the card, flat 2 px lines. The data is the darkest thing in the plot. Keep that quietness when you add labels.
- Everything important fits on one screen, top to bottom.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart shows values on hover. Even if it does, a weekly overview shouldn't make you hover just to find out which line is which.
