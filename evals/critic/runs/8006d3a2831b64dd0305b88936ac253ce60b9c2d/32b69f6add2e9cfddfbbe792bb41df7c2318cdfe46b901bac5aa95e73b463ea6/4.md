**First read.** The eye goes to the three big numbers along the top: 184,220, 3,912 and 2.12%. Then it drops to the chart's three coloured lines. It reads as a marketing-analytics overview: calm, tidy, and a little anonymous. Then you try to read the chart and hit a wall: you can't tell which line is which, how big anything is, or what week you're looking at.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an at-a-glance traffic and sign-up dashboard. A marketer or founder should be able to see how the last 12 weeks went and which channel drove it.

**Verdict.** The KPI row does its job. The chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, doesn't. It's titled "Visits by channel" but names no channel, has no values and no dates. As drawn, it shows only that three unnamed things went up a bit.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart has no series labels, no axis values and no time axis.** (Defect.) In the "Visits by channel" panel the blue, orange and green lines carry no names, there's no legend, the y-axis has no numbers and the x-axis has no weeks. What the viewer experiences:
   - They can't answer the one question the title promises: which channel is growing?
   - They can't tell whether the gap between blue and green is 2× or 20%.
   - They can't tie the dip in the blue line at about week 8 to a date.
   - They can't check that the lines add up to the 184,220 above.

   Even with a legend added, colour alone would still exclude some people. To many colour-blind viewers the orange and green lines look much alike, which breaks WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color). Tufte (*The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2) and Few (*Show Me the Numbers*) both require labelled axes with units, so the reader never has to guess what a mark measures.

   Fix: put the channel name (and ideally its latest value) at the right end of each line, where the eye already is. That fixes identity and colour-blind access in one move, with no legend to look back and forth to. Add a few y-axis values (visits per week) and week labels or dates along the bottom. The empty gutter left of the plot and the blank band under its baseline look like space kept for exactly these labels. If the labels exist in the live product and failed to render in this capture, check them in a browser. Either way, this screenshot shows none.

2. **The chart's title names a topic, not a finding.** (Taste. No approved source states this, so I'm arguing it on its own terms.) "Visits by channel" makes the viewer do the analysis themselves. On a dashboard whose job is a quick read, a line like "Organic drove most of the 6.1% growth; paid flat since week 6" (or whatever the data actually says) is what the viewer came for. If the title has to stay generic because it's a live template, give the panel a one-line change summary per channel instead. This only works once finding 1 is fixed.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type sizes.** I count about seven sizes between roughly 13 and 28 px. "Brightpath Analytics" (~18) and "Visits by channel" (~17) are nearly the same size, and so are "Overview" (~24) and the KPI numbers (~28). Close sizes like these read as accidents (Bringhurst). Cut to four levels and separate the near-twins by weight or colour, not by another size (Refactoring UI).
- **"Last 12 weeks", top right.** It reads as a passive label. If it's the date-range control, which a dashboard viewer will expect to change, it needs to look pressable: a border or chevron and a pressable target.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards.** Each has a label, a big number and a comparison to the previous period. The conversion card correctly says "+0.1 pts" rather than a misleading percent. That's what Few asks of a headline number, so keep the pattern exactly.
- **Restraint.** The gridlines are pale, nothing is decorated, and the data lines are the darkest marks in the chart. Once labelled, it will read cleanly.
- **The layout.** One grid, even gutters, and the cards and chart share edges. The page holds together without effort.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether hovering the chart shows values. A tooltip would help exploration, but it wouldn't replace the labels for an at-a-glance read.
