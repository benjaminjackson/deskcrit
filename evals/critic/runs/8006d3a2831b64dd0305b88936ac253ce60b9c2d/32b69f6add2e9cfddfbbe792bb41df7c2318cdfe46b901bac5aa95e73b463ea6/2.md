**First read.** The eye lands on the three bold numbers in the top row (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%) and reads them easily: each has a label, a period and a green change. Then it drops into the big chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, and stops there. Three coloured lines climb gently, but nothing says what they are, how many visits they stand for, or which weeks they cover. It reads as a tidy, generic SaaS marketing dashboard. It feels calm and competent, and it says nothing once you reach the chart.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a weekly overview for a marketing or growth team, to show how traffic and sign-ups are trending and which acquisition channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** The top row works, but the chart, the largest thing on the page, can't be read. It has no series names, no values on the y axis and no dates on the x axis, so the one question it exists to answer ("which channel is growing?") has no answer. Until it's labelled, the dashboard tells you less than the three cards above it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart is unlabelled, so it carries no information.** *Defect.* In the "Visits by channel" panel, the blue, orange and green lines have no names (no legend, no direct labels). The left gutter (x ≈ 50–120) has no y values, and the empty band under the baseline (y ≈ 685–758) has no week labels. The viewer can see that "something went up" but can't say which channel, by how much, or since when. They can't even tell whether the gaps between the lines are 1,000 visits or 50,000. Tufte and Few both require labelled axes with units and a readable scale. Fix: name each series at the right-hand end of its line (Tufte and Wilke prefer labels on the data to a legend, so the eye doesn't shuttle back and forth). Put visit counts on the y axis, put week dates along the x axis, and use the space already reserved for them. That space currently reads as leftover padding.
2. **The series are told apart by hue alone.** *Defect (WCAG 1.4.1, Use of Color).* Blue, orange and green are the only difference between the three lines. Orange against green is the pair people with red-green colour blindness most often confuse. Direct labels from finding 1 fix this as well; no separate change is needed.
3. **Neither the page nor the chart states what the data shows.** *Taste, argued on its own terms.* "Overview" and "Visits by channel" are topics. The viewer still has to compare three slopes and work out the story themselves. Here the story is probably "the blue channel is driving most of the +6.1%", or "orange is the most volatile". A dashboard for a weekly check-in should tell the viewer what changed. Fix: a one-line chart title or caption that states the finding in plain words, such as "Organic search drove most of this quarter's growth". Only label it once it's labelled.

**Smaller things.**
- "Overview" (about 24 px bold) and the KPI numbers (about 26 px bold) are nearly the same size and weight, so the page heading competes with the figures it introduces. Make "Overview" clearly smaller or lighter, or drop it, since the brand bar already says where you are.
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top-right corner looks like plain text. If it's the date-range selector, it needs a visible signifier (a chevron, a border or a button shape). If it's only a label, it's fine.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards.** Each one has a label, a big tabular figure and a comparison with the previous period. Conversion uses "pts" rather than "%", which is correct and easy to get wrong. Keep that pattern exactly.
- **Restraint in the chart.** The gridlines are pale and thin, there's no frame, no shading and no 3D, so the data lines are the darkest marks. Keep that once the labels go in.
- **One clean grid.** The three cards and the chart panel share 48 px margins and even gutters. The layout is calm and fits on one screen.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether hover tooltips on the chart show values. Even if they do, the at-a-glance read still needs the labels.
