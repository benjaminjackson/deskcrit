**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, then gets pulled right into a bright field of lime and emerald ovals and stays there. It reads as a garden-centre homepage: friendly, competent, a bit stock. The words promise shade and ferns, but the picture feels like sunny spring confetti.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the homepage hero for a local plant nursery. It should get nearby gardeners who have a shady spot to browse the stock and then visit or buy.

**Verdict.** The type and layout are tidy and the headline lands first, but the hero never shows the product. A generic texture of bright ovals stands in for ferns, and it contradicts the shade the headline is selling. The only button is too weak to be the next step.

**What would move it most**
1. **The image doesn't show what's being sold, and it says the wrong thing.** The right two-thirds of the hero is random leaf-shaped ovals, with no frond, no plant and no corner of a garden. A viewer who came for ferns doesn't see a fern. The bright, saturated limes on the right read as sunlight, the opposite of "shade plants", so picture and headline pull against each other instead of making one idea. It also looks procedural and templated, so it could be any brand with a green colour scheme. That costs a small nursery its main advantage: real plants grown on a real hill. Fix: use a photo of their own ferns in a dim, cool corner, so the image does the half of the job the headline can't. If you keep a texture, make it recognisably fern, and darker and cooler. *Defect.*
2. **The call to action is the weakest thing on the screen.** "Browse the greenhouse" is a thin white outline with no fill. It carries less weight than both the headline and the subhead, and the busy texture makes it easy to miss. It's the only action, so it should be the second thing the eye lands on, not the last. Fix: make it a solid filled button in a colour that stands apart from the greens (Refactoring UI, Material: primary actions get the strongest treatment). *Convention, with a real cost here.*
3. **The copy is close but generic.** "For every corner" could be on any nursery's site, and "Browse the greenhouse" asks the viewer to grade the whole stock rather than solve their own problem. The subhead is the best line on the page ("Grown on the hill… this weekend" is specific and local), but it sits under a stock phrase. Fix: lead with the viewer's own shady spot, and point the button at that case, e.g. a "plants for a north-facing border" link, so they can judge the value against the problem they already have. *Taste, argued.*

**Smaller things.**
- Headline breaks: "shade / plants" splits the noun phrase and "corner" sits alone on the last line. Rebreak to "Ferns and shade plants / for every corner".
- The bright right half competes with the copy for the eye. Even if you keep the image, darken or desaturate it.

**What's working.**
- There's a clear type hierarchy in one family. The heavy, tightly set headline dominates without fighting the subhead. Keep the big step between headline and subhead.
- The left-hand darkening keeps white text readable over the texture. The headline, subhead and button share one clean left edge. Keep both when you swap the image.
- The header is quiet and conventional: wordmark left, four plain-language nav items right. It stays out of the way.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states: a thin white outline on texture needs a strong focus indicator.
