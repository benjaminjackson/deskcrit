**First read.** The eye goes straight to the white headline, "See where every dollar goes this month", then down to the subhead and the white "Try it free" button. It reads as a consumer budgeting app's landing page: calm, trustworthy, competent. Then the eye drifts right into a large empty navy field and finds nothing there. The page feels unfinished rather than confident.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the first screen of a marketing page for a personal budgeting app. It has to get someone who handles household money to connect their bank accounts and start a free trial.

**Verdict.** The words and the type are in good shape, but as a sales page it is weak. It promises "see where every dollar goes" and shows nothing, and the right half of the hero is an empty slab that makes the page tip left. Second, the one fact that would get a wary person to connect their bank, that the app can never move money, sits in small grey text in the third column.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero promises something to see and shows nothing** (defect). The right half of the navy hero, roughly x 800–1216 by y 62–462, is empty. All the weight sits on the left (headline, subhead, buttons), so the block looks unbalanced and leftover rather than open on purpose (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"; Ruder on space that does work). It also costs you the sale. The headline is about seeing your money, and someone deciding whether to hand over bank access wants to see what they'd get first. With no product view, the page is just a dark hero, two buttons and three features, and it could belong to any finance app. That last point is common sense, not a published rule. Fix: put the product in that space. Show the one view the headline promises, such as this month's categories with a limit and a "heads-up before you go over" alert, set in the grid's right columns. If an image was meant to be there and didn't load in this capture, check it in a live browser. If the empty half is a choice, it is the wrong one for this page.
2. **The trust argument is buried** (defect, copy). The subhead opens with "Connect your accounts", which is exactly what a cautious viewer fears. The answer to that fear, "The app can see balances and history but can never move money", is 15px grey text in the third feature column, read last if at all. People hesitate at the bank-connection step, and this line is what gets them through it. Fix: move the read-only promise up next to the ask, as a short line under the buttons or folded into the subhead ("…read-only: we can never move your money"). Make it say what it means for the viewer: their money can't leave their account through this app.
3. **"Try it free" can be read two ways** (defect, copy). It could mean free forever or a free trial that turns into a charge, and it doesn't say whether a card is needed. People remember a promise about money and hold you to it. If it's a trial, a visitor who thought "free" meant free forever will feel tricked when the first charge arrives. Fix: say exactly what they get, with a line next to the button if needed, for example "Free for 30 days, no card needed". Better still, an ask that lets them judge the value on terms they already trust: "See last month's spending in 2 minutes" sets the product against their own bank statement instead of asking them to take your word for it.

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and spoken to the viewer: a concrete outcome ("every dollar") with a timeframe ("this month"). It's set big, bold and tightly leaded, so it reads as one unit and clearly comes first. Keep it.
- The subhead names three actions in plain order (connect, set a limit, get a heads-up) and gives the product's loop in one sentence, at a comfortable line length.
- Clean structure: one left edge at 64px runs from the logo through the hero to the first feature column, and the three feature columns are evenly spaced. "Try it free" (filled) is clearly the main button and "Watch the tour" (outline) clearly the second. Bold headings sit tight to their body text.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the empty right half of the hero is an image that failed to load (check it live). Also the hover and focus states on both buttons, which matter here because "Watch the tour" is white-on-navy outline only.
