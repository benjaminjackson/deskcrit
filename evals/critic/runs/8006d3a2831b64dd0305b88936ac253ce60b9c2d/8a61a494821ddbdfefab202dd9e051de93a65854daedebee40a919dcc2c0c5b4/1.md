**First read.** The eye lands straight on the big serif quote, and that's right. It reads as a warm, quiet newsletter feature: cream paper, a rust kicker, a bookish serif. Then it stalls on *'old'*. The single quotes hint at a story, maybe a renaming, that the page never tells. The actual news is in small grey type at the bottom, and you reach it last.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a single item in a local library's newsletter, meant to tell regular patrons that the reading room reopens on Monday and to make them want to come.

**Verdict.** The layout is calm and well judged, but the words don't make the case. The headline is a puzzle with no setup, and the news patrons need to act on is the weakest thing on the page. The 95px straight quotes and the hyphen make it look like default output rather than an edited piece.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline sets up a story the page never tells, and the news is buried. (Defect, copy and hierarchy.)** A stranger reading *"It's the 'old' reading room"* asks: old as opposed to what? Was it renamed? Is there a new one? Nothing on the page answers that, so the quote reads as an in-joke. Meanwhile, the only line a patron will act on, "reopens on Monday", is in the smallest, palest text, last in the order. The second-strongest slot (bold, black) goes to the attribution instead. "Monday" has no date, so if this is read a week late, or forwarded, it points to the wrong day. Two fixes. First, have the text pay off the quote: say what changed, and if there's a new official name, say it, because that's the joke. Second, give the reopening the weight the attribution has now, with a date and, ideally, hours ("Reopens Monday 2 December, 9am"). Keep the quote as the lead, because it's the hook.
2. **Straight quotes and a hyphen at display size. (Convention, plus common sense.)** At about 95px, the straight `"` and `'` marks and the spaced hyphen in "room - nobody" are among the most visible shapes on the page. They look like typewriter marks inside an otherwise careful serif, and they cheapen it. Use curly quotes and apostrophes throughout ("It's" has a straight apostrophe too), and replace the hyphen with a true dash (Bringhurst §5.2.1 covers dashes; no checked source covers curly quotes, but at this size it's common sense).

**Smaller things.**
- The opening quotation mark isn't hung. "It's" starts indented from "room" and "anything", so the headline's left edge is ragged. Pull the quote mark out into the margin.
- The headline breaks "reading / room", which splits the very name the quote is about. Rebreak so "reading room" stays on one line, for example after "'old'".
- The attribution floats about as close to the body text below as to the quote above. Tighten the gap to the quote so "said a volunteer…" reads as the tail of the quote's sentence, not a separate deck.

**What's working.**
- There's a single clear focal point. The quote is dominant by a wide margin, and serif against sans gives each level a distinct voice. Keep that contrast in scale and face.
- The one-edge layout with generous, gathered space to the right and below gives the page a calm, unhurried pace that suits a library.
- The palette is restrained: cream, near-black, one grey and one rust accent. The rust is used only on the small kicker, so it marks the section without competing.
