**First read.** My eye lands on "Low-light houseplants", drops to the subtitle, then runs along the row of four cards. Each card goes pot drawing, name, price, then a teal button. In about two seconds this reads as a small, calm plant shop's category page. It is tidy and friendly, but it is also anonymous: the four pictures look like the same clip-art plant drawn four ways, so nothing in the row stands out.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an online shop's category page where someone with a dim room picks one of four plants and adds it to their cart.

**Verdict.** The structure works: the grid is clean, the purpose is clear in one line, and the action is obvious. But it fails at the one thing a plant listing must do, which is show the plant. The drawings don't show the products, so the buyer is choosing on price and pot size alone.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures show placeholder plants, not the products** (defect). The four drawings in the top half of the cards are the same two- or three-leaf shape in one green, in the same terracotta pot. The snake plant doesn't look like a snake plant. The pothos, which the card says "trails to 6 ft", is drawn as two upright leaves. A plant is bought on looks: leaf shape, colour, how it spreads. Here the viewer can't tell the four apart, can't picture any of them in their room, and has no reason to prefer one beyond price. The drawings also make the page look like a template, so it could be any shop. Fix: show each actual plant, photographed in one consistent way (same backdrop, light and crop) so they still read as a set. If you stay with illustration, draw each species accurately, including the pothos trailing over the pot's edge.
2. **"6 in pot" can be read two ways, and it sits next to the price** (defect, copy). The spec lines read "6 in pot, grows to 2 ft" under a picture of a terracotta pot, on a site that sells Pots separately in the top nav. One reading: $24 buys the plant and the terracotta pot shown. The other: it buys the plant in a 6-inch plastic nursery pot. Someone who expects the first and receives the second feels misled about what they paid for. Fix: say what arrives, e.g. "Arrives in a 6 in plastic nursery pot" or "Includes 6 in terracotta pot". If the pot isn't included, don't draw one that suggests it is.
3. **The cards give no reason to pick one plant over another** (taste, argued). The subtitle makes a good, specific promise: "Four plants that do well more than six feet from a window". But each card then shows only size and price, and all four already meet the low-light need. A buyer choosing among them needs the next fact from their own home: Is it safe for my cat? How often do I water it? Will it trail or stand? One short line per card, such as "Water every 2–3 weeks · Toxic to pets", would let them choose for their own situation instead of picking the cheapest.

**Smaller things.**
- The type sizes are too close together. I count about five: H1 around 30, price around 20, name and logo around 18, nav and button around 15, spec around 14. The name (around 18 bold) and the price (around 20 bold) are almost the same size and weight, so they compete in each card. Pick a clear step between them: for example, set the price in the body size and weight, or make the name clearly larger.
- Nothing in the top nav marks "Houseplants" as the current section. Mark the current item with one clear cue, such as a bolder weight, an underline or a background (Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist", NN/g).

**What's working.**
- **The grid is exact.** The logo, the H1 and the first card share one left edge at about 48 px. The cart link and the last card share the right edge. The gutters between cards are equal. Keep this when you change the pictures.
- **The subtitle is specific copy.** "More than six feet from a window" is a measure the buyer can check against their own room. Most category pages would just say "for low light".
- **There is one clear action per card.** The filled teal button sits in the same place on every card with the same label. That is the right pattern for a product grid.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- About 195 px of blank space runs below the cards, with no footer. Check in a live browser whether the page really ends there or the capture cut off before a footer loaded.
- Hover and focus states on "Add to cart", and whether "Cart (0)" updates once something is added.
