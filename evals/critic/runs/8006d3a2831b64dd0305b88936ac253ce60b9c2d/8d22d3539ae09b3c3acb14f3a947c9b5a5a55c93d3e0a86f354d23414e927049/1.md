**Verdict.** The type is well set and easy to read, but the screen doesn't do its job. A timetable page shows no times, and the one schedule fact it does give leaves out what the rider needs to plan with. The words are the biggest problem, not the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **No time a rider can act on (defect).** The eyebrow says "Spring timetable" and the headline promises "crossings by daylight", but the screen has no first sailing, no last sailing, and no dates. "By daylight" is the actual schedule here, and it can be read two ways: from sunrise to sunset, which changes every week from spring into summer, or a fixed set of daytime hours. A rider who guesses wrong reaches the north landing after the last boat. "Every forty minutes" without a starting time doesn't tell anyone which minute to show up. If the full timetable sits below this screen, nothing here hints at it. The bottom 200 px is empty, so the page looks finished. Fix: state the first and last crossing for the season, or a table of sailings, or at least "first boat 7:00, last boat at sunset (times below)". Put real dates on "spring through early summer". If the table is below, let its top show above the fold.
2. **The headline spends its size repeating the eyebrow (taste).** "Spring timetable" and "spring through early summer" say the same thing twice. Meanwhile the biggest type on the page, about 80 px, is a noun phrase with no verb, filling four lines. Seasonal wording is the least useful thing to give that much space. Fix: let the headline say what the rider gets: the route and the rhythm, for example "The quay ferry runs every 40 minutes, sunrise to sunset". It should also come out shorter. The paragraph can then cover direction and crossing time.
3. **The direction is unclear (defect, copy).** "Runs from the north landing to the market quay" reads as one way only. A rider at the market quay can't tell whether there's a return, or whether "every forty minutes" counts departures from each side. Fix: say "between" if it runs both ways, and say which side the forty minutes counts from.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline line breaks: "early / summer" splits a phrase, and "summer" sits alone on line 4. Rebreak the headline, or shorten it, which item 2 already does, so each line is a whole phrase.
- Lede runt: "weather." sits alone on the last line of the paragraph (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Adjust the measure slightly or rewrite so the last line carries more than one word.
