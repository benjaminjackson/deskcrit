**First read.** The eye goes straight to the bold headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", then drops down the column to the blue button. In about three seconds it reads as a local ferry's landing-page hero, and it feels calm, clean and competent. Then you notice the right half of the frame and the bottom third are empty. The block looks like it is waiting for something that never arrived.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a ferry operator's homepage on desktop. Its job is to get local people and day-trippers to choose the boat and check when it leaves.

**Verdict.** The type and the single action are solid, but the page doesn't yet sell the crossing. It promises a calm trip across a harbour and shows nothing of it, never names the places, and leaves half the screen blank. Right now it reads as a well-set template rather than this ferry.

**What would move it most**

1. **Half the frame is empty, and the experience being sold is never shown.** All the content sits in a column on the left, from about x=160 to x=790. Everything to the right of it, and everything below the button, is blank white. All the visual weight is packed into the top-left with nothing to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"), so the page looks unfinished rather than airy. Worse, the copy sells something you see: "watch the harbour slide past from the open deck." The viewer is asked to imagine that instead of being shown it. **Defect.** If an image is meant to sit on the right and failed to load in this capture, check it in a live browser. If nothing is planned there, add one strong picture of the boat on the water, or the view from the deck, in that right-hand area. A route map would also work. Either one fills the frame and makes the headline's "calm" believable.

2. **The copy never says where, when, or how much.** "Cross the bay" doesn't name the bay. "The north landing" doesn't say north of what, and the page never says where you end up. "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" makes the viewer guess the first and last boat. There is no fare at all. A stranger can't tell whether this ferry goes where they need. The people who already know the route are the ones who least need this page. "Twelve calm minutes" is the strongest line here, but it has nothing to be measured against. **Defect** for the missing place names and times. **Taste** for the comparison, which is my view. Name both landings in the headline or just under it. Give real times ("first boat 6:40, last 22:00") and the fare. Set the twelve minutes against what the viewer does now, for example the drive round the bay, so they can see the saving for themselves. "See the timetable" is a good, exact ask. Keep it.

3. **The three feature lines look like leftovers.** "Bicycles travel free / Seats on the open deck / Hot drinks on board" are set at about 18 px, under a paragraph at about 20 px. That size gap is too small to read as a separate level, so it reads as a mistake (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI: separate levels with weight or colour, not a near-identical size). The lines have no markers, so they look like a paragraph that broke apart. Two of the three repeat the paragraph ("bring a bicycle", "the open deck"), so they add almost nothing. **Defect** for the near-identical sizes, **taste** for the repetition. Either cut the list and let the paragraph do the work, or make it clearly different and useful. A short row of hard facts would do that: fare, bikes free, step-free boarding. Give it its own weight or icons, not just a slightly smaller size.

**Smaller things.**
- The brand line "Coastal Ferry Co." is small blue text above the headline. It works as an eyebrow label but is weak as the only sign of who runs the boat. Consider a real logo in the page's header.

**What's working.**
- **The headline.** It's large, bold and tightly leaded. The two lines hold together as one unit, and it promises a specific benefit in concrete terms (twelve minutes, calm).
- **One clear action.** There is a single blue primary button, at a comfortable size (about 210×55 px), with a label that says exactly what you get.
- **The paragraph setting.** Lines run about 60 characters with relaxed leading, set flush left in plain dark grey on white. It reads easily.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether an image or other right-hand content is supposed to load there. Check in a live browser.
- The focus state on the button.
- How the column reflows on a phone.
