**First read.** The eye lands straight on the bold black headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes," then drops through the paragraph and the three short lines to the blue "See the timetable" button. It reads clearly as the top of a small ferry company's homepage. It feels calm and tidy, but also bare: everything is packed into the upper left, and the right half of the screen is empty white, as if a photo was meant to go there.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I was told nothing: this is the first screen of a local ferry's website, meant to make a would-be passenger want the crossing and then plan a trip by opening the timetable.

**Verdict.** The message and the order of reading work. The headline is the best thing here, and the page has one clear action. What holds it back is that it doesn't feel like a trip across water and doesn't answer "when can I go?" The empty right half leaves the screen lopsided and placeless, and the copy promises a schedule without giving any of it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The screen is lopsided and gives no sense of the place.** *Convention plus taste.* All the weight sits in a column over the left ~60% of the width and the top ~70% of the height. The right half and the bottom are blank, and nothing on that side balances the dark headline (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). This doesn't look like chosen empty space. It looks like a missing photo, and for a product sold on "calm" and "watch the harbour slide past," the missing picture is the selling point. Fix: put something on the right that carries the crossing, such as a photo of the open deck or the bay, or a simple map of the route between the two landings. That would fill the space and make the promise concrete. If the white space is meant to stay, make it look chosen: widen the column or set the block on a clear proportion of the screen so it doesn't read as unfinished.
2. **The copy promises a timetable but leaves out every fact the viewer will act on.** *Defect.* "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" has no times, "the north landing" names no place, and nothing says which bay or what a ticket costs. A visitor can't tell whether they could catch a boat this afternoon without leaving the page. Fix: state the hours and the route directly, for example "Every 40 minutes, 6:40 am to 10:20 pm, from North Landing to [the other side]". Better still, show the next departure beside the button so the page answers the question before the click. "See the timetable" is a specific ask, which is good; showing the next sailing next to it lets viewers judge for themselves against their own plans.

**Smaller things.**
- The paragraph (~20px) and the three-line list (~18px) are the same face and weight at sizes only a couple of pixels apart. They read as a mistake rather than two levels (Bringhurst, "Don't compose without a scale"). Set them at the same size, or make the list clearly different by weight or colour.
- The three features have no bullets, icons, or other marker, so they read as stray lines of body text rather than a list of perks. *Taste.* A small marker or a bit of tint would make them scannable.
- "Bicycles travel free" and "Seats on the open deck" repeat things the paragraph just said ("Bring a bicycle", "the open deck"). *Taste.* Tighten one or the other so each line adds something new.

**What's working.**
- The headline names a concrete benefit in plain words ("twelve calm minutes"). It is by far the largest and darkest thing on the screen, so the eye knows where to start.
- There is a single primary action: one solid blue button with a verb-led label, in the same blue as the small brand name above, so the colour system is simple and consistent.
- The body text is well set: about 60 characters per line, generous line spacing, flush left, and strong contrast.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half is where a picture failed to load or the intended design. Check it in a live browser. If an image belongs there, finding 1 shrinks to making sure it loads.
- The button's hover and focus states.

Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 12 yes, 1 no (visual weight balances)

Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
