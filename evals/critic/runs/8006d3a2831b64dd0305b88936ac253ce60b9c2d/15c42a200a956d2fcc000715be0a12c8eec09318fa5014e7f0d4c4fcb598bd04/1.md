**First read.** The eye goes to the bold title "River ferry timetable", then straight into the table. It reads as a clean, calm schedule for one route. The look is quiet and well-made, and you can read it at a glance. Then the line below the table says "heavy black lines around every cell make this timetable a mess". The table has no such lines, so the page contradicts itself.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a rider going from North landing to Market quay needs to find the next ferry that runs on the day they travel.

**Verdict.** The table is well made and mostly does its job. The biggest problem is the caption under it, which describes a table that isn't there. The real weakness is something else: the table is sorted by time of day, but riders need it sorted by the day they travel.

**What would move it most**
1. **The caption is wrong (defect).** The sentence under the table says heavy black lines surround every cell. The table actually has thin, pale horizontal rules and no vertical lines. A reader either stops trusting the page or goes looking for a problem that doesn't exist. It's left-over or wrong copy, and the line about finished copy covers it (common sense; no catalog entry). The fix: delete it, or replace it with a note riders can use, such as fares or where to board.
2. **The day filter is the last thing you read (defect).** Three of the seven sailings don't run every day: 12:40 and 17:10 run on weekdays only, and 19:30 on weekends only. That information sits in the right-hand "Runs" column, in the same weight as "Daily". A Saturday rider has to check every row and skip the ones that don't apply. They could easily miss that 17:10 doesn't run and the 19:30 does. The day is the first thing a rider filters on, so build the table around it. Use either two columns, "Weekdays" and "Weekends", with a mark in each, or two short lists. The "Period" column could then go (see below).
3. **"Spring season" has no dates (defect).** The subtitle is the only place that says when this timetable applies, and it's the information a rider acts on. Without dates, a rider in late May or early June can't tell whether these times still hold. Add the start and end dates, for example "Spring season, 21 March to 20 June".

**Smaller things.**
- Period column: "Morning, Morning, Midday, Midday…" repeats what the times already say and takes a quarter of the width. Remove it, or turn the periods into light group labels, and give the space to what riders need.
- "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are cut-down labels. Spell out "Leaves North landing" and "Arrives Market quay" so a stranger doesn't have to work them out.
- Departure and arrival sit about 300 px apart, so the eye travels a long way along each row. Bring the time columns closer together. Every crossing takes 12 minutes, so one line saying so could also replace the arrival column.
- The times use proportional figures: in the crop, 11:00 and 17:10 are narrower than 06:40, so the colons don't line up. Switch on tabular figures (Bringhurst 4.4.3).

**What's working.**
- Restraint with the rules: only thin, pale horizontal lines, with a slightly heavier one under the header. Each row is easy to follow and the lines don't compete with the times. Keep it, whatever the caption claims.
- A clear, small type scale. One bold title, a grey subtitle, and the body size used for both the header (bold) and the rows (regular). Each level shows at once.
- A table is the right form here: riders look up exact times, and a table shows them directly.
