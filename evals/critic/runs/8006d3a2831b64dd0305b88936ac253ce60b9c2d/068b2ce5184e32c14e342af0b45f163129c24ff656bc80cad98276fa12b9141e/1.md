**First read.** The eye goes to the big "Garden show gallery" title, then to the large red Rose arch tile, then to the smaller tiles on the right and along the bottom. It reads as a photo gallery for a garden show, a featured exhibit plus five others. It looks tidy and calm, but there are no actual gardens in it. Every picture is a flat block of colour with a pale circle. The last thing you read is a grey line saying "Design review: failed".

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page where visitors browse photos of the exhibits at a garden show, with one exhibit featured.

**Verdict.** The layout works: one tile clearly leads and the grid is consistent. But as a gallery it doesn't work yet, because there's nothing to look at. All six pictures are placeholders, and a leftover internal note at the bottom tells visitors the page has "failed".

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders, not photos** (defect, common sense; no approved source covers it). All six tiles are flat fields of colour (red, green, blue, brown, mustard, purple) with a pale semicircle sitting on the bottom edge. In a gallery the pictures are the content. A visitor sees no roses, herbs or pond, only the captions, so the page can't show them anything about the show. The large Rose arch tile has nothing in it to earn its size. If these are deliberate flat illustrations rather than images that failed to load, they're still a problem: they show nothing about the place each caption names. Put real photos in every tile, cropped to the tile shapes the layout already uses. Then check the Rose arch photo can carry the largest tile.
2. **A stray review note is published on the page** (defect, common sense). The line under the bottom row reads "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size". Visitors aren't meant to see a team note, and the word "failed" makes the whole page look broken. The note is also wrong: the Rose arch tile is about twice the width of the others and has its own larger caption. Remove the line.

**Smaller things.**
- Caption spacing in the small tiles: each caption ("Herb bed / Thyme, sage and chives.") looks about 10 px below its own picture but only about 14 px above the next tile. That's too close a difference to show clearly which picture the caption belongs to. Make the gap between tiles clearly larger than the gap between a picture and its caption, for example 24 px or more, so space shows the groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on grouping by proximity).
- Title copy (taste): "Garden show gallery" could head any show's gallery. Name the show and year so visitors know they're in the right place.

**What's working.**
- **A clear focal point.** The Rose arch tile spans two of the three columns and has its own large bold heading, so the eye knows where to start. That's the strong contrast of large against small Hofmann describes (*Graphic Design Manual*), and it's worth keeping once real photos go in.
- **One consistent grid.** Three columns with the same gutters throughout, the same picture proportions in every tile, the same caption pattern under each (bold name, then one grey line), and equal 60 px side margins. The page reads as a single system.
- **Specific captions.** "Climbing roses over the east gate" and "Tomatoes in late July" each tell a visitor what and where in a few words, so keep that voice.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (image and headline make one idea; every element does a job; copy finished)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 14 lines, 13 yes, 1 no (space inside groups smaller than between groups)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 11 yes, 1 no (every image shows its content)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: skipped, no visible controls
