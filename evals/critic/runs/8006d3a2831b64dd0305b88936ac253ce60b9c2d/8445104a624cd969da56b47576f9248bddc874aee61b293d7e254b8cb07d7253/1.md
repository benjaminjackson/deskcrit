**First read.** My eye lands on the bold "Crossing the river" at top left, drops to "Early boats", and reads down the left column. Then it jumps to the top of the right column. It reads as a short feature or travel piece set as a two-column page. It feels calm and bookish. The serif and the steady grey of the text suit that.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short editorial piece, a magazine-style spread or a typesetting study, meant for continuous reading.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clear, the measure is comfortable and the type is set with care, so it reads easily. What keeps it from looking finished is that the two columns have no shared rhythm: their lines don't line up across the gutter, and the opening paragraphs are indented when they shouldn't be. Those are craft problems, not structural ones.

**What would move it most**
1. **The columns don't share a baseline grid** (convention, Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). Look across the gutter: each line in the right column ("Stallholders arrive…", "In the evening…") sits level with a gap between two lines in the left column, all the way down. The right column's first line also sits about 5 px above the baseline of "Early boats". It's close enough to look like an attempt to align and far enough off to look like a miss. The reader sees two blocks floating on their own, not one page built on a grid. The cause is the space around the headings, which isn't a whole number of lines. To fix it, make the space above and below each heading add up to a whole number of body lines, about 25–26 px each. Then start the right column on a body line that is level with a left-column line. Keep more space above each heading than below.

**Smaller things.**
- The first paragraph after each heading is indented ("The first ferry…", "The market quay is…", "In the evening…"). The heading already marks the start, so set these flush left and indent only the paragraphs that follow (Bringhurst, 2.3.1). The indents also look closer to 2 em than 1 em. A shallower indent would sit quieter (taste).
- "flowers." sits alone on the last line of the market-quay paragraph, at the foot of the left column. Re-break or edit the paragraph so its last line holds more than one short word (Bringhurst, 2.4.2).
- The columns end about four lines apart, at "flowers." and "the last boat.". Once the baselines line up, balance the columns so they end on the same line, or end the left column on a section break.

**What's working.**
- **The type scale is clean:** a bold title, bold subheads and roman body, three sizes with big steps between them. You can tell every level at a glance, so keep that restraint.
- **The measure and leading are right:** about 60–65 characters a line, with open but not loose line spacing. It's comfortable to read for long stretches.
- **The subheads are plain labels** ("Early boats", "The market quay", "Going home") that follow the arc of a day. They tell you what each section holds and give the piece a sequence without needing anything else.
