**First read.** The eye goes to the bold title "Trial to paid conversion by plan", then drops to the tallest purple bar, Growth at 4.0%. Then it goes right to the table of raw counts. It reads as one clean page of an internal SaaS analytics dashboard: calm, sober, and trustworthy. It reads more like a report than a dashboard.

**What it's for.** My assumption is that this is an internal analytics page where product, growth, or finance people check how well each plan's trials convert, and decide whether a plan needs attention.

**Verdict.** It's well built and honest: the bars start at zero, the numbers are labelled directly, the source and definition are stated, and the table's counts match the chart (286/9,210 = 3.1%, 38/1,120 = 3.4%). What it doesn't do is answer "so what?". With no earlier period, no target, and no sense of how firm each rate is, a viewer can't tell whether 3–4% is good or bad. They also can't tell whether the gaps between plans are real.

**What would move it most**
1. **The rates have nothing to compare against.** Every number on the page covers Q3 2025 only. A viewer sees 4.0% for Growth and can't tell whether it went up or down from Q2, or whether it beats the target. On an analytics screen, that's the question the page exists to answer. This is a defect: the viewer can read the numbers but can't judge them. Few ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design") flags metrics shown without context. The fix: put the previous quarter next to each bar (a paired lighter bar or a tick mark), or add a change column like "▲0.4 pp vs Q2" to the table, plus the target line if one exists.
2. **The 0.1-point precision suggests a ranking the data may not support.** Enterprise's 3.4% rests on 38 paid accounts and Growth's 4.0% on 118. At those counts, a few accounts either way moves a bar by several tenths of a point. The Growth vs Enterprise gap, and Team vs Enterprise, may be noise. Labels like "4.0%" and "3.4%" with no range invite someone to say "Enterprise underperforms" in a meeting. This is a defect for anyone acting on the plan ranking (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, on showing uncertainty). The counts in the table help only if the viewer does the arithmetic. The fix: add an interval to each bar, or at least a note on the low-count plans, so the viewer can see which differences hold.
3. **The title names the topic, not the finding.** "Trial to paid conversion by plan" tells me what the chart is, and the subtitle is a careful definition. Neither says what happened. If the point is "Growth converts best; Starter lags at 3.1%", say so, and let the current line become the subtitle. This is taste, not a published rule, but on a page people glance at between meetings, a title that states the result saves everyone a second read. Wait to write it until point 2 is settled, so the headline doesn't claim a gap that's noise.

**Smaller things.**
- The table gives Trials and Paid but no rate column. A viewer who wants the exact rate for Enterprise has to cross back to the chart or work it out. Add a "Rate" column so the table stands alone.
- The definition note "Trials still open on 30 October are counted as not converted" reads oddly next to "within 30 days". A stranger may wonder whether trials still running were left out or counted as failures. Say it plainly: "A trial that hasn't paid by day 30 counts as not converted; the last trials started 30 September, so the window closed 30 October."

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest and lean: the axis starts at zero, the gridlines are faint, the values are labelled directly at each bar, and there's one colour with no legend. Keep all of that when you add the comparison: a lighter tint of the same purple for the previous quarter would keep it that way.
- The definition and source line are exactly the context analytics viewers ask for. They say what "converted" means and which dates are covered, so nobody argues about the numbers before discussing them.
- The type is restrained: one family, a clear jump from the title to everything else, and right-aligned tabular figures in the table.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Keyboard focus on the top navigation tabs, and whether the chart has a text alternative for screen readers.
