**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "Trial to paid conversion by plan", drops into four identical purple bars, and stops on the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. Then it moves right to a tidy table of trials and paid counts and a definition. It reads as a clean, calm internal analytics page for a product or growth team. It looks competent and quiet, and it leaves you with no conclusion.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal dashboard view that tells a product or growth lead how each plan's trials convert, so they can decide where to act.

**Verdict.** It is well made and honest, but it shows numbers without saying what they mean. The bars make a 0.9-point spread look like a ranking. The page gives no way to tell whether the gaps are real or how they compare with last quarter, so the viewer can't act on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The differences look more certain than the data supports** (defect). The bars rank Growth > Team > Enterprise > Starter, and the labels give one decimal place. But Enterprise rests on 38 paid accounts out of 1,120 trials (table, bottom row). At that size, 3.4% could plausibly be anywhere from about 2.5% to 4.5%. That covers Team's 3.6% and most of the other bars. A viewer reading the chart will believe Enterprise underperforms Team, and nothing on the chart warns them. The sample sizes are in the table, but nothing connects them to the bars. Wilke (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16) asks for uncertainty to be shown with the estimate. Add error bars or intervals to each bar, or at minimum put "n = 1,120" under each plan label, so the viewer can see which gaps are real.
2. **No comparison, so the viewer can't tell good from bad** (convention, Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Every figure is a single quarter with no previous quarter, no target, and no overall rate. Is 3.1% for Starter a problem or a record? Showing Q2 next to Q3 for each plan answers that, as does a reference line for the all-plans rate (627 / 18,420 ≈ 3.4%) or the target.
3. **The title names the topic, not the finding** (taste). "Trial to paid conversion by plan" plus a definitional subtitle leaves the reader to work out the point. Once 1 and 2 are in place, the title should say what you now know: for example, "Growth converts best; Starter trails, and the rest are within noise". Then the viewer leaves with a decision instead of four numbers.

**Smaller things.**
- Source line (bottom left) says trial events "1 July to 30 September". The Definition counts payments through 30 October. Say both windows, e.g. "trials started 1 Jul–30 Sep; payments to 30 Oct", so a reader doesn't think October payments were left out.
- Definition: "Trials still open on 30 October" leaves unclear whether a trial can run longer than the 30-day window in the subtitle. State the trial length so the two rules obviously agree.

**What's working.**
- The bars start at zero and the value axis stops at 5%. The scale is honest, with no exaggerated gaps. Keep that when you add intervals.
- One hue for one series, with direct value labels and faint gridlines. The data is the darkest thing in the chart.
- The table and definition sit right next to the chart. This page already holds the raw counts behind every rate and the definition of "converted", which most dashboards hide. Make them work harder (point 1) rather than moving them.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the bars or nav have hover or focus states that show exact counts.
