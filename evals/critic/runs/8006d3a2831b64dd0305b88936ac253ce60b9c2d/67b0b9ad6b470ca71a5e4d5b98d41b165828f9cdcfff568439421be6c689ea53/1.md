**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "Trial to paid conversion by plan", drops to the four purple bars, and stops on the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. Then it goes right to the table of counts and the definition. It reads as a calm, well-made internal analytics page for a SaaS product team. It looks trustworthy. But it ends in "so what?": I can see four numbers, and nothing tells me whether any of them is good, bad, or changing.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: an internal dashboard page where product and growth staff check how well each plan's trials turn into paying accounts, and decide whether to act.

**Verdict.** The craft is clean and honest, but the page doesn't do its job yet. It shows one quarter's rates with no previous quarter, no target and no overall rate, so the viewer can't tell whether 3.1% to 4.0% is a problem.

**What would move it most**
1. **None of the rates has a comparison** (defect). All four bar labels and the whole chart show Q3 2025 alone. Someone opening the Conversion tab wants to know whether conversion went up or down and whether it's on target. Here they can only rank the plans against each other, and they'd have to open last quarter's page and remember its numbers to judge. Few names this pitfall directly: a metric with no comparison can't be read as good or bad (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). The fix is to put the change from the last period, or the target, next to each number. Here that means a Q2 marker or a paired bar for each plan, or "+0.4 pt vs Q2" under each label.
2. **There's no overall rate** (defect). The page's main number is missing. The table already has everything needed: 18,420 trials, 627 paid, about 3.4% overall. But nowhere does it say "overall, 3.4% of trials converted." The viewer would have to add up the columns to get the one figure most of them came for. Add a total row to the table, and show the overall rate with its comparison as the first number on the page, or as a reference line across the chart.
3. **The title names the topic, not the finding** (taste; no approved source states this rule, so I'm arguing it on its own terms). "Trial to paid conversion by plan" describes the chart. It leaves the viewer to work out the point, and they'll probably conclude "Growth wins." That's only half true. Enterprise's 3.4% rests on 38 conversions, so it could easily sit anywhere from about 2.3% to 4.5%, and it can't really be ranked against the others. Once the comparison exists, write a title that states what happened, for example "Growth converts best; all plans within 1 point of each other." Add a note that Enterprise is a small sample, so no one acts on a gap that might be noise.

**Smaller things.**
- **Table heading:** "Trials by plan, Q3 2025" in the right column also holds the Paid counts. Rename it "Trials and paid accounts by plan, Q3 2025" or similar.
- **The two time rules can be read two ways:** the subtitle says "within 30 days," but the Definition says "trials still open on 30 October are counted as not converted." One reading is that every trial gets 30 days. The other is that every trial had until 30 October, so a July trial would get about 90. That second reading would make the rates mean something else. State one rule, such as "paid within 30 days of the trial starting," and add the year to the date.

**What's working.**
- **The chart is honest.** The axis starts at zero, the gridlines are pale, there's one flat colour, and each value sits on its bar. Keep this restraint when you add the comparison: a second, paler bar or a marker, not a second strong colour.
- **The counts and the definition sit next to the rates.** You can check that 286 / 9,210 = 3.1% without leaving the page, and the definition says exactly what "converted" means. That's what makes the page trustworthy. The figures are right-aligned, tabular and line up.
- **The type scale is tight and clear.** I count about four sizes: page title, chart-table heading, body and labels, small source line. Each has an obvious job, and the gaps between them are wide enough to read as levels.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The focus and hover states on the top navigation.
