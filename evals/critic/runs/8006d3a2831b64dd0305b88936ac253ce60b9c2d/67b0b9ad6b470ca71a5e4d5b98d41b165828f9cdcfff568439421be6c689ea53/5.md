**First read.** My eye goes to the bold title, then the four purple bars, and stops on the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. Then it crosses to the table on the right. It reads as a clean, calm internal analytics report about how trial users convert on each plan. It looks competent and quiet, and in the end it doesn't tell me anything.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard view where a growth or product team checks whether trial-to-paid conversion is healthy and which plan converts best.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid and the chart is honest, but it fails the reader's real question. Four bars between 3.1% and 4.0%, with no previous quarter, no target and no sense of how solid each number is, can't tell anyone whether this is good, bad or meaningful. As it stands, the most likely takeaway, "Growth converts best", isn't supported.

**What would move it most**
1. **The numbers have nothing to be compared against** (defect; convention per Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Each bar shows Q3 2025 only. A reader can't tell whether 3.1% for Starter is up, down or below target, and on a conversion page that's the first thing anyone asks. Put a comparison next to each rate. Usually that means the previous quarter as a paired, greyed bar or a tick on each bar, plus the change in points. Add a target line if one exists.
2. **The small differences look as solid as the large samples** (defect; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). Enterprise's 3.4% rests on 38 conversions out of 1,120 trials. By my rough arithmetic, that's give or take about a point, so it can't be told apart from Team or Growth. Starter's 3.1% rests on 9,210 trials and is much tighter. The bars draw all four with equal confidence, so a reader will rank plans on differences that may be noise. The counts sit in the table across the page, where nobody will connect them to the bars. Show the uncertainty on the bars themselves: error bars or a light range, or at least "n = 1,120" under each plan name.
3. **The title names a topic, not a finding** (taste: argued here, not from an approved source). "Trial to paid conversion by plan" makes the reader work out what the chart means. Once 1 and 2 are fixed, the title should state what is true, for example "Growth converts best; every plan sits between 3% and 4%" or "Conversion fell on every plan except Growth". Write it in a way that tells the reader what to act on.
4. **The definition can be read two ways, and it defines the metric** (defect). The subtitle says a trial counts if it paid "within 30 days". The Definition block says a trial converts "when the first invoice is paid" and counts trials "still open on 30 October" as not converted, but never mentions the 30 days. Reading 1: a July trial that paid on day 50 is not converted (30-day window). Reading 2: it is converted, because its invoice was paid before 30 October. Under reading 2, July trials get up to three months to convert and September trials about one, so the rate depends on when the trial started. Anyone reproducing or comparing these numbers will get different answers. State one rule in one place, e.g. "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice is paid within 30 days of the trial starting."

**Smaller things.**
- Table, right column: "Trials by plan" also holds Paid counts, and it has no rate column, so the table and chart only connect through plan names. Either retitle it "Trials and paid accounts by plan" or move n under the bars and drop the table.
- Nav tabs (Acquisition…Revenue): text-only links. Check that each click target is at least 24 px tall (WCAG 2.5.8).

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest and lean. The axis starts at zero, gridlines are faint, every bar has its value printed on it, and a source line is included. Keep that restraint when you add comparison bars. Grey for last quarter and purple for this one is enough.
- Plans are ordered by tier (Starter → Enterprise) rather than by value. That's the right call here, because readers think of plans in that order.
- The figures check out: 286/9,210 = 3.1%, 185/5,140 = 3.6%, 118/2,950 = 4.0%, 38/1,120 = 3.4%. The table's numbers are right-aligned, so they're easy to read down the columns.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the page lets you change the quarter or plan filter. If it doesn't, the missing comparison (finding 1) matters even more.
