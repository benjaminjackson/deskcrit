The page reads clearly as a weekend sale, but it gives you nowhere to shop it: the hero has no button, the loudest element is a sticker that goes nowhere, and the product row shows no sale prices. I worked from the whole image (1280×800, one screen, so no crops).

**First read.** The eye lands on the tilted blue SALE sticker at the far right, crosses back to the black headline, then drops to a row of four cards with empty grey picture boxes. It reads as a home-goods shop running a weekend sale. It feels calm and competent until the sticker, which looks like it came from a discount store. Nothing tells you what to click.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the home page of a small home-goods shop during a weekend sale, meant to get visitors to start shopping the discounted items.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. It announces a sale clearly, then gives you no way into it: the hero has no action, the loudest element is a sticker that leads nowhere, and the products below show no discounts. The page tells you about the sale but doesn't help you buy anything in it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no action, and the sticker fakes one.** *Defect.* The only saturated, button-shaped thing on the page is the rotated blue SALE block on the right. It looks pressable (filled, rounded, high contrast), but it's almost certainly decoration, and there's no "Shop the sale" anywhere in the hero. A visitor either clicks the sticker and gets nothing, or has to work out that the "Sale" link in the nav is the way in. Norman (*The Design of Everyday Things*) says things that aren't interactive shouldn't look interactive, and things that are interactive should. Putting the next action in front of the visitor is common sense for a sale page. Fix: put one clear primary button under "Up to 40% off through Sunday." that names where it goes, such as "Shop linens and cookware". Then either make the sticker that button or remove it.
2. **The sticker wins the page and repeats the headline.** *Defect, plus taste on its tone.* Saturated cool blue, a tilt, and heavy capitals make the sticker the first thing you see. All it says is "SALE", which the subline already says more usefully, with a number and a deadline. So the eye spends its first moment on the least informative element and is pulled to an empty right edge. The electric blue and the tilt also clash with the warm linen band and the quiet type. My view as a designer: that pushes the page from "nice linens" towards "clearance bin". Fix: make the offer itself the dominant element, "Up to 40% off" big or set in the accent colour next to the headline, and give the right half of the hero a real product photo of linens and cookware.
3. **The products don't show the sale.** *Defect.* "Popular this week" sits right under a 40% headline, but the cards show one plain price each ($34, $89, $52, $68). There's no original price, no discount, and no sign whether these items are in the sale. A visitor can't tell if the duvet at $89 is a deal, and the strongest proof the offer is real goes unused. Fix: if these items are in the sale, show the old price struck through next to the new one and retitle the row, for example "On sale this weekend". If they aren't, swap in items that are.
4. **Every product image is an empty placeholder.** *Defect, common sense.* All four cards show a flat tint with a pale rounded rectangle. On a shop page, the pictures are what sell linens and stoneware. Without them the row looks unfinished and the cards can't do their job. If this is a capture of unloaded images, check in a live browser. As it stands, it's the biggest craft gap.
5. **The headline is ambiguous.** *Defect in the copy.* "Linens and cookware for the weekend" can be read as a weekend-themed collection rather than a time-limited discount. The offer, the line people act on, is set small beneath it. Fix: say it in the headline itself, for example "40% off linens and cookware, this weekend only", or make the subline as big as the headline.

**Smaller things.**
- The hero text starts about 8 px to the right of the logo, the "Popular this week" heading and the card edges (≈56 vs 48 px). Put it on the same left edge.
- No nav item is marked as the current page. Give the active item a weight, underline or colour cue (Nielsen Norman Group, Laubheimer's "Menu-Design Checklist"). Consider styling "Sale" in the accent colour while the sale runs.

**What's working.**
- The quiet type system: one sans in a few clearly separated levels (wordmark, headline, section heading, product name in bold, price in regular). Keep it when you add sale prices.
- The warm off-white hero band separates the offer from the catalogue without needing rules or boxes.
- The four-card grid is even, with consistent gutters, and the name and price are grouped tightly under each picture.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards and the sticker are clickable, and their hover and focus states.
- Whether the empty picture boxes are the design or images that hadn't loaded yet.
