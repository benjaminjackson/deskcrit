**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours", then drops down three tidy bold branch names. It reads straight away as a plain hours notice for a small public library: calm, clear, a bit bare. You only hit the problem when you try to use it. Looking for "is it open at 5 on a Saturday?" means reading sentences, and the third block gives no times at all.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a web page or notice where local residents check when and where they can visit each library location.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound, with clean hierarchy, grouping and spacing. The copy lets it down: the times are written as words with no am/pm, which makes them slow to scan, and the mobile library entry has no times, so one of the three services can't actually be planned around.

**What would move it most**
1. **The mobile library entry has no times.** "Wednesday at the market square / Thursday at the school gates" (third block) says where and which day, but not when. Someone who turns up at 4 pm doesn't know whether they've missed it by hours, and "the school gates" doesn't say which school. The viewer can't act on the one entry that most needs exact times, since a van stays only a few hours. Fix: give each stop a time window and a specific place, matching the format of the branch lines. *Defect.*
2. **Times are spelled out as words, so you have to read them instead of scanning.** "nine to eight", "ten to four", "ten to six", "ten to two" (all branch lines). People come to an hours page with one question: is it open at a given time on a given day. Numerals can be matched at a glance, but words have to be read and converted. "ten to four" also reads as 3:50 in British English, and with no am/pm, "nine to eight" leans on the reader to assume a 12-hour day. Hours are something viewers act on and hold the library to, so a line that can be read two ways counts as a defect. Fix: use numerals with clear meridiem or 24-hour times and a proper en dash, such as "9 am – 8 pm". Consider setting days and times in two aligned columns so the times line up. *Defect.*
3. **Closed days are left for the reader to work out.** Main branch lists no Sunday, and Harbour lists no Sunday or Monday. The reader has to notice the gap and guess that it means closed rather than an omission. Fix: state "Closed Sunday" (and "Closed Sunday and Monday" for Harbour) as its own line. *Defect,* minor next to 1 and 2, but it is the second most common question on an hours page.

**What's working.**
- The three levels are clear and economical: one large bold title, then branch names that differ from their lines by weight alone, not by another size. Keep that. Adding sizes would only add noise (Refactoring UI: bolder, not bigger).
- Spacing does the grouping. Lines within a branch sit close and the gaps between branches are about twice as large, so each name clearly owns the lines below it, with no boxes or rules needed.
- One left edge and dark text on white make it fully readable with nothing to cut.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the page reflows on a phone, and whether it sits inside a site with navigation or an address and map. If there's no address anywhere, branch names alone won't get a first-time visitor to the door.
