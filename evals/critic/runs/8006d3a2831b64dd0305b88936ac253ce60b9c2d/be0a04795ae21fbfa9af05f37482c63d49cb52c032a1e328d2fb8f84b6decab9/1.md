**First read.** The eye goes to the big headline, "Bring your bike in for a service", then across to the coloured "Book drop-off" button. This is a booking page for a local bike workshop, and it feels calm and clear. On a second look, the price list on the left doesn't feed into the booking panel on the right. I wasn't sure which service I'd be booking, or which day was selected.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a bike shop's repairs page that gets a customer to book a drop-off slot for a service.

**Verdict.** The two versions are the same design. The only change is the colour of the "Book drop-off" button: teal in version 1, bright blue in version 2. Both buttons look well above 4.5:1 for their white label, both are clearly the one primary action, and neither colour clashes with or misreads on this neutral page. The colour doesn't change how well either version does its job. What gets in the way is the same in both: the booking form doesn't capture the service or show a selected date. Neither button colour fixes that.

**What would move it most**
1. **The service isn't part of the booking.** The three services and their prices ($25 / $70 / $160) sit in the left column as plain rows. The booking panel on the right asks only for a day. The customer picks a date, presses "Book drop-off", and the shop doesn't know if they want a $25 safety check or a $160 rebuild. The customer can't confirm what they're committing to either. The decision is split across two boxes, so the user has to keep the service in their head (Nielsen 6, recognition rather than recall), and the control sits away from what it sets (Norman, natural mapping). *Defect.* Fix: put the service choice inside the booking panel as the first step, with the price beside each option, so the panel holds the whole booking. The list on the left can stay as information or be removed.
2. **The date rows don't read as choices, and none is selected.** "Tuesday 28 October", "Wednesday 29 October" and "Friday 31 October" are white boxes with pale borders that look like text fields with values already typed in. There's no radio mark, no selected state and no default. The viewer can't tell whether to click one, type in one, or whether one is already chosen. That matters because the button commits them to a day (Norman, signifiers and feedback; Nielsen 1, visibility of system status). *Defect.* Fix: make them clearly selectable options, with a radio or check and a filled selected state, and preselect the earliest day so the button always books something visible.

**Smaller things.**
- The borders on the date rows look far below 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Darken them, or let the selected-state styling carry the shape.
- The copy promises a call "before doing any work that costs more than the quote", but the page never says where the quote comes from. Name it ("the price you booked", or a quote at drop-off) so the promise can be held to.
- There's no year on the dates and no hours beyond "before 10 am". Add the collection time ("from 5 pm") so "the same evening" is a commitment, not a guess.

**What's working.**
- The headline and the one-line promise underneath ("drop it off before 10 am and collect it the same evening") say exactly what the customer gets, in their own words. Keep that plain voice.
- There's one clear primary action in both versions, and the type scale is tight: headline, panel heading, body and rows, with no near-miss sizes.
- The price list has right-aligned figures and thin rules, so it scans instantly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus visibility on the date options and the button, and what happens after "Book drop-off" (confirmation, and where the service and contact details get captured).
