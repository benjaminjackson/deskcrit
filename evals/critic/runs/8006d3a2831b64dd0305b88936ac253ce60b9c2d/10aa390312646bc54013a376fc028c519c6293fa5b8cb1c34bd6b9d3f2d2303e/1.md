**First read.** The eye lands on "The Wexley Gazette", runs down the double rule, then stops on the three bold headlines, which all have the same weight, so it picks one more or less at random. It reads at once as a small local paper's front page or news site: calm, bookish, trustworthy. On a second look the body text has visible gaps between words, and the page ends halfway down the frame.

**What it's for.** No brief was given, so I'm assuming this is the front page of a local news site or paper, and its job is to let residents scan the town's news quickly and see what affects them.

**Verdict.** It is a clean, coherent start, and the type system is sound. But the body text is badly set: justified text in narrow columns leaves holes between words. And the page gives no sign of which story matters most. Fix the text setting first, because it is in every line the reader reads.

**What would move it most**
1. **Justified text in narrow columns leaves holes between words.** *Defect.* Lines run only about 38–40 characters, and they are forced to a straight right edge without hyphenation, so the spaces stretch to fill them. You can see it in column 1 at "months  of  repairs  to  its  arches.", in column 2 at "the  old  corn  exchange  from  next" and "a  stage  for  local  bands  on  the  first", and in column 3 at "a  regional  prize  for  the  vegetable" and "feeds  the  school  kitchen  two  days  a". The grey of each column turns blotchy, and the eye trips on the gaps, in the part people actually read. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*, p. 34) says that below about 50 characters per line, even word spacing becomes "difficult, if not impossible". Bringhurst warns that short justified lines produce "white acne" and suggests 40 to 50 characters for multi-column text, and these columns sit at the bottom of that range. **Fix:** set the text ragged right. That is the simplest fix and suits this measure. Otherwise, turn on hyphenation, or widen the columns toward 45–50 characters.
2. **No lead story: three equal headlines compete.** *Convention (Tschichold, Hofmann), and in news it is also the reader's main cue.* The three stories are identical in head size, weight, column width and treatment. A reader can't tell whether the bridge reopening, which affects everyone who drives, outranks a school garden prize. The page reads like a row of equal cards, not edited news. **Fix:** make one story clearly lead, probably the bridge, with a larger headline and a wider column or more text, and set the other two back. The order of importance should be visible before anyone reads a word.
3. **No date anywhere, while the stories run on relative time.** *Defect.* The masthead has no date or issue line, yet the copy says "from next month", "in April", "from March" and "the first Saturday of each month". Dates are what readers act on. Without a dateline, a reader who lands on this page later can't tell which month "next month" means, or which April. The market story has the worst version of this: it never says the day the move happens. **Fix:** add a date line under the masthead and give the market move a date ("from Saturday 6 June"). The bridge story is clear enough once the page is dated.

**What's working.**
- **One serif family with three clear levels.** The masthead (about 34 px), the bold headlines (about 24 px) and the regular body (about 18 px) each have an obvious job, and weight rather than size separates the headlines from the text. Keep this scale when you add a lead story; make the lead bigger, not a fourth size squeezed between the existing ones.
- **A tight grid.** The three columns share a top edge and equal gutters, the body lines sit on the same baselines across all three columns, and each headline sits close to its own text.
- **Concrete copy.** Details like "two of the five arches", "eighteen months" and "room for sixty stalls" read like real reporting, not filler.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The content stops at about the halfway point and the lower half of the frame is blank. That's fine if this is the top of a longer, scrolling page. If this is the whole page, check it in a live browser, because it will look unfinished.
