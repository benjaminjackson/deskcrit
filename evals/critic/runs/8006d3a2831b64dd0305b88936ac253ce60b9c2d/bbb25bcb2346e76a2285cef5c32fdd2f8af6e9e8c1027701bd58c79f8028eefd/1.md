**First read.** In V2 the eye lands right away on the bold "Maps of the inland sea", then drops down the two bold subheads to "Opening times". In V1 the title is quieter and the two subheads blend into the serif text, so the column reads more as one prose block. In both, the big tan rectangle on the right pulls the eye next and gives nothing back. It reads as a calm, bookish museum exhibition page.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an exhibition page that gets a visitor to understand what's on, check when they can come, and decide to visit.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. Its bold sans headings make the page scannable, so "Opening times" can be found at a glance. In V1 the headings differ from the text only by size and sink into it. Both versions share a bigger problem: the hero image is an empty gradient, so a page selling an exhibition of maps shows no map.

**What changed, and why it matters.** The only change is the heading type. V1 sets the title and subheads in the body serif at regular weight, so they differ from the text by size alone. Dair, *Design with Type* (chapters 8a–8g and 9), calls this the mark of average work. Visitors scan this page for "when can I come". In V1, "Opening times" is a slightly larger line of the same texture, so they have to read rather than spot it. V2 changes structure (sans against serif) and weight. Each subhead now works as a signpost, and the title clearly sits on top. The bold sans also matches the "Calder Museum" wordmark and the nav, so the change ties the page together instead of adding a third voice. V1's restraint has some period charm, but it costs findability for no gain.

**What would move it most (V2)**
1. **The picture is empty** (defect; common sense, no approved source). The 420×560 panel on the right is a flat tan-to-brown gradient. The viewer is promised "forty charts… hand-coloured" and gets a beige box, which takes up almost half the screen. It looks like a placeholder or an image that didn't load. If it's a load failure, it isn't a design fault, but check it in a live browser. Either way, the fix is to show one real chart, cropped close enough that the hand colouring and coastline detail read at this size. It's the one thing that would make someone want to come.
2. **There's no ask** (defect, copy). The page tells you what, when and that it's free, then stops. "Tickets" is a small plain link in the nav, and since entry is free, it's unclear what that link is for. Add one specific next step under the opening times, such as "Plan your visit: free, no booking needed" or directions to the ground-floor gallery. That turns "nice to know" into "I'll go on Saturday".

**Smaller things.**
- The nav ends at about x=1184 and the image ends at about x=1220, a near-miss. Align the nav's right edge with the image's right edge.
- The nav doesn't show that you're in "Exhibitions". Mark the current section.
- The apostrophe in "pilots' notebooks" looks like a straight tick. Use a curly ’.

**What's working.**
- V2's heading system: bold sans heads over a serif reading face give real contrast in both structure and weight, and they echo the wordmark. Keep it.
- The copy is concrete and plainly spoken: "a brass sounding lead found in the harbour mud in 1958". "Until 2 March", "Entry is free" and "ground floor" answer the visitor's practical questions without padding.
- The text column runs about 70 characters with comfortable leading, and each subhead sits tight to its own paragraph, so the three groups read clearly.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the gradient panel is an image that failed to load (check it live), and how the two columns reflow on a phone.
