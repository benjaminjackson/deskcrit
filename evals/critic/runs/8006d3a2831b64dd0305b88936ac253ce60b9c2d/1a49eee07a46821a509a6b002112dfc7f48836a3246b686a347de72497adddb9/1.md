**First read.** The eye goes to the big dark rectangle first, finds a grey person-shaped placeholder, and drops to the bold headline. After that the path is clean: date, headline, one line of detail, one dark button. It reads as a bookshop's page for one author event. It feels quiet and competent, but the most prominent thing on it is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it's the event page for a free author reading at an independent bookshop, aimed at local readers deciding whether to book a seat.

**Verdict.** The typography and the ask are good, but the page doesn't yet do its job. The biggest block on the screen is a stock avatar silhouette, and the details someone needs before committing to an evening (where it is, what year, how long) are missing or contradict each other.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image is a placeholder.** It's a grey head-and-shoulders avatar on dark brown in the 600×330 block at the top. It is the largest and darkest thing on the page, so it takes the first look and gives back nothing: no author face, no book, no island. The visitor's first impression of the event is an empty profile picture. The low-contrast brown-on-brown silhouette also makes it look unfinished rather than moody. Defect (common sense, no catalog source). This is fine only if the real image failed to load in this capture. Check that in a live browser. Fix: use the author's portrait or the cover of *The Salt Year*. Better still, use an image of the island, which the copy already sets up. Then the picture and the headline make one idea (a person, a book, a place) instead of the picture just meaning "someone".
2. **The facts a booker acts on are incomplete, and the date may be wrong.** "Thursday 13 November, 7 pm" has no year. 13 November is a Thursday in 2025, but a Friday in 2026, the current year. So either the page is offering seats to an event that's already over, or the weekday is wrong. Both are bad: people turn up on the wrong night, or book something that's gone. There's also no venue on the page. "Find us" sits up in the nav, but a visitor booking a seat needs to see "at the shop, 12 X Street" next to the date, not go looking for it. Defect: dates and places are what people later hold you to. Fix: add the year (or check the weekday), and add the venue and an end time or length to the date line. If seats are limited, say how many are left, because that is what makes "book" urgent.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav, top right: "Events" isn't marked as the current section, so a visitor who came from search can't see where they are on the site. Underline it or set it in the darker text colour (Nielsen, visibility of system status).
- Headline: the book title "The Salt Year" is set the same as the rest of the sentence. Italicising it would show where the title starts and stop the line reading as "her new novel, the salt year". Taste, but it's the usual way to set a book title.

**What's working.**
- The ask: "Book a free seat" names the exact action and the price in four words, and it's the only filled button on the page. Keep that wording.
- The text stack under the image: a small rust-coloured date, a bold two-line headline set tight, then a 16 px body line and the button. That makes four clear levels with no near-duplicate sizes. The gaps are right too: about 32 px above the date and about 10 px between the lines, so the block reads as one unit.
- The supporting line does real work. "A reading, a short talk about the island…, and questions from the floor. Wine and soft drinks from 6.30 pm" tells a stranger exactly what the evening involves and when to arrive.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the hero image is really the placeholder or just didn't load in this capture: check in a live browser. Also how the page reflows on a phone, and the button's focus state.
