**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a public notice or web page that tells ferry passengers when boats run in winter, plus the practical details of making the trip.

**Verdict.** It's set well, but it fails at its main job. It's called a timetable and contains no times, so a passenger can't plan a crossing from it. The unfinished bottom edge makes it look broken as well.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times in it** (defect). The title promises a schedule. What follows is "opens at seven", "every ninety minutes", "the first crossing is usually the quietest" and "until the last boat has left". The page never says when the first boat leaves, when the last one leaves or when they come back. The reader can't work out the 10:30 sailing from "every ninety minutes" because there's no starting point. A heading should name what its section holds (WCAG 2.4.6), and this one names something the page doesn't contain. Departure times are exactly what people act on and later hold the operator to, so leaving them out is the costliest gap on the page. Fix: add a real departure table under the title, with columns for harbour → island and island → harbour and times in tabular figures. Make that table the second thing the eye reaches. The prose then becomes supporting notes. Two smaller copy fixes help too. "Usually the quietest" and "may skip a sailing" both need a concrete instruction for the reader: check the board by the gate, or a phone number or web page for cancellations. And "a rough one closer to thirty-five" should say "minutes".
2. **The bottom of the page is unfinished** (defect). At lower left, "Fares and cards" sits on a rule with nothing under it. At lower right, the same rule appears with no heading above it. To a reader, a section that opens and then shows nothing looks like a page that failed to load. Fares are the second thing a passenger needs, so leaving them out costs almost as much as leaving out the times. The rule without a heading also breaks the pattern the left column sets, where each rule sits under a heading (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*: repeated elements follow one program). Fix: fill the section with the fares and season-card prices. If this is a crop of a longer page, check the live page. Either give the right-hand rule its own heading (for example "Times and cancellations"), or run one rule across both columns.
