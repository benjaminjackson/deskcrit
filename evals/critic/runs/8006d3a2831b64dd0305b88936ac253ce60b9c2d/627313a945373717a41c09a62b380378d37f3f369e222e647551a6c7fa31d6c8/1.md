**First read.** The eye lands on "Create your account", runs down a tidy stack of six fields, and stops on the solid blue button. Within a second it's clearly the sign-up screen for a notes app called Quillnest. It feels calm, competent, and anonymous: it could be any SaaS product's sign-up.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a person who has already decided to try Quillnest should be able to make a free account as fast as possible, without dropping out.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The structure is clean and nothing is confusing. The biggest problem is that the form asks for more than a free notes account needs, and one of those asks (the referral dropdown) is a blank box that looks broken. Cutting the form back would do more for completion than any visual change.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form is longer than the job.** Six fields stand between a free user and their notes: first name, last name, email, password, workspace name, and "How did you hear about us?". The last two serve the company, not the person signing up, and both are optional. So the user is reading and deciding about fields that don't matter to them, which is exactly where people stop. Ask only for email and password, plus at most a single name field. Move workspace name to the first screen after sign-up, or prefill it from their name. Ask the referral question later or not at all. *Defect* (form length costs completion). It's also *convention*: Nielsen 8, aesthetic and minimalist design, and progressive disclosure.
2. **The "How did you hear about us?" dropdown is an empty box.** At 436–844 × 594–636, the select shows nothing but a chevron. It reads as a control that failed to load, and the user can't see what the options are without opening it. Give it a visible prompt ("Choose one (optional)") or replace it with a short free-text field. If item 1 is fixed, this goes away. *Defect.*
3. **The password field gives no rules.** If there's a minimum length or a character rule, the user only finds out after they submit and fail. Put the rule in one line of helper text under the field ("At least 8 characters"). If there truly are no rules, this point doesn't apply. *Defect*, Nielsen 5 (error prevention) and Nielsen 6 (recognition over recall).

**Smaller things.**
- The "Sign in" link in the bottom line is the same grey as "Already have an account?", with no underline. Returning users on the wrong page have to guess where to click. Underline it or put it in the brand blue (WCAG, link distinguishable, G183).
- Input borders are pale slate on white and look under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border colour against #FFF and darken it until it clears 3:1.
- The subhead "Start a free workspace for your notes." could be on any notes app's page. One concrete line about what Quillnest does differently would remind a hesitant user why they came. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- There is one clear primary action. The full-width filled button is the only strong colour on the card, so nobody wonders what to press. Keep the blue only for that button.
- The labels sit above the fields and tight to their own inputs, with even gaps between groups, so the form scans in one pass. Required fields get both an asterisk and a legend, so the meaning isn't carried by red alone.
- First and last name are the only fields side by side, which is the right exception to a single-column form.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings and error states (whether a failed field names itself in words and says how to fix it) need checking in a live browser.
