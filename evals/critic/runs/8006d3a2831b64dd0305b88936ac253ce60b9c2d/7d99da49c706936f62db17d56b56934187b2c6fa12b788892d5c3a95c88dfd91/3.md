**Verdict.** It doesn't yet do its job as a page people scan. The questions are the same weight and colour as the answers, and look slightly smaller, so a reader can't pick out their question without reading everything. Two answers that people will hold the bakery to, the cancellation deadline and what to show at pickup, can each be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The questions don't read as headings.** In all four groups, the question line ("Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else"…) looks about 17 px. Its answer looks about 18 px, in the same regular weight and the same dark grey. So the label you scan for is the weaker line. Each group reads as one block, and someone looking for "Cancelling" has to read their way down instead of jumping to it. On top of that, nothing stands above the four groups: there's no title saying what this list is, so the page has no starting point. *Defect.* A heading one size step away from its text, or the same as it, looks like an accident (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8), and a page needs one element seen first (Tschichold). Fix: make each question differ from its answer in a way you see at a glance. Usually that's semibold at the same size or one clear step up, possibly with the answers a shade lighter. Add one clearly larger page title above the list, such as "Your order: common questions".
2. **Two answers people will act on can be read two ways.** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead": ahead of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time before the day of pickup. Someone who reads it the second way and cancels at 9 pm for a 10 am pickup will think they're within the rules and get charged or refused. "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name" could mean the collector's name or the name on the order. Get it wrong and a friend goes home without the bread. *Defect.* Deadlines and pickup conditions are the lines customers hold you to. Fix: give the exact cutoff ("You can cancel until [time] the day before your pickup") and say exactly what the collector must give ("…and the name the order was placed under").
3. **The allergy answer sends people elsewhere at the moment they need the information.** "Every loaf has a card by the till" helps only someone already standing in the shop. A reader online deciding what to order is told nothing they can use now. *Taste, close to a defect for anyone with a serious allergy.* Fix: link to the ingredient lists on the site, or say where online to find them, and keep "ask the baker" as the backup.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the single-line answers ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") run about 95 characters, beyond the 45 to 75 Bringhurst recommends. Narrow the text column to about 600 px.
- The allergies answer ends with "anything." alone on its last line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrowing the column will likely fix this; if not, rebalance the break.
- "Open your order": the copy doesn't say where. Name the place, such as "Open your order from the confirmation email" or "…under My orders", and say what happens after the two-hour hold.

**What's working.**
- The spacing already shows the groups. The gap between a question and its answer (about 10 px) is clearly smaller than the gap between groups (about 35 px). Keep that ratio when you make the headings stronger.
- One plain sans, flush left, one colour on white, and a direct, human voice ("the bread has already been baked for you"). It's calm and readable, and it suits a small bakery. Fix the hierarchy with weight, not decoration.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Open your order" or any other phrase is meant to be a link. Nothing on the page is styled as one. Check on the live page.
