**First read.** My eye lands on the top-left line and then reads straight down. Nothing stops it, because all eight lines have the same weight, colour and roughly the same size. It reads as a bakery's help or FAQ section about click-and-collect orders. It feels calm and honest, but it is flat: you have to read every line to find the question you came for.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help section on a small bakery's ordering site or order page, where a customer who already has an order quickly finds the answer to one question.

**Verdict.** The copy is mostly clear and the spacing is sound, but the headings don't work as headings. They are no bigger, bolder or darker than the answers, so scanning for "my question" turns into reading the whole page. The cancellation line also has an ambiguous deadline that customers will hold the bakery to.

**What would move it most**
1. **The questions look the same as the answers.** This applies to all four headings ("Changing your pickup time" and the rest). They look about 18 px and the answers about 19 px, in the same weight and colour, so the headings are, if anything, slightly smaller than the body. Space is the only thing that marks them as headings. Someone who comes to find out "can I cancel?" can't skim the four questions. They have to read paragraph by paragraph. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8) says a heading must differ from its text in a way that is obvious at a glance, not by a step or two. Here the difference is close to none, and in the wrong direction. Fix: keep the size and make the questions semibold, or make the answers a lighter grey and keep the questions dark. Either works better than making the questions bigger. *Defect.*
2. **"Up to a day ahead" can be read two ways, and it's a deadline.** Under "Cancelling an order", it could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time on the day before. A customer with a 9 am Saturday pickup who cancels at 8 pm on Friday is fine under one reading and too late under the other. They will remember the line and argue over it at the counter. Fix: state the exact cut-off, for example "You can cancel until 6 pm the day before your pickup." *Defect.*
3. **No title, so nothing tells you where you are.** The first thing on the page is the first question. With no "Questions about your order" (or similar) above it, the section has nowhere to start. If it is shown alone, it doesn't say whose bakery this is or what it covers. Fix: add one title clearly above the question level. That gives the page three levels (title, question, answer), which is all it needs. *Taste, argued: it costs little and settles both where the eye starts and what the viewer is looking at.*
4. **Two answers leave the reader to fill a gap.** "Along with a name": whose name, the person collecting or the person who placed the order? Write "...along with your name." The allergy answer only helps someone already standing at the till. Someone ordering online who has a nut allergy can't check the card before they pay. Fix: say where to see ingredients before ordering, or give a way to ask the baker now (phone or email). *Defect for the allergy line, because it affects safety; convention-level for the name.*

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the single-line answers (Collecting, Cancelling) run about 90 to 95 characters, past Bringhurst's 45 to 75. Narrow the column to about 600 to 640 px.
- Runt: "anything." sits alone on the last line of the Allergies answer. Rewording or narrowing the column (as above) will reflow it.

**What's working.**
- Grouping by space. Each question sits close to its answer, with clearly more room between pairs, so the four items separate cleanly with no rules or boxes. Keep these gaps when you add weight to the headings.
- The voice. Answers like "After that the bread has already been baked for you" explain the rule in human terms instead of just stating a policy. Keep it while you tighten the cut-off wording.
- Plain, readable setting: a single sans, near-black on white, flush left, with comfortable line spacing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether this is a short page or part of a longer page. If more questions follow, the lack of scannable headings costs more.
