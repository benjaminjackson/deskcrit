**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the big serif title, "Leek and potato soup with brown butter", then drops through a lot of empty space to the two columns. Right away this reads as a calm, editorial recipe page. Version 1 feels warm and kitchen-like. Version 2 is the same page on white, and it looks a little clinical. On white, the rules between ingredients show up as a mix of pale yellow and pale grey lines.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a recipe page on a food site. It should let a home cook see what they need, then follow the steps at the stove.

**Verdict.** The two versions are almost the same, and both work: the title is clearly the main element, and the ingredients and method are easy to read. Version 1 is slightly better. Its cream background fits the warm, homely mood of the recipe. It also hides rule colours that don't match, and on white in version 2 those rules show up as alternating yellow and grey. The biggest problem, in both versions, is the empty space between the title and the recipe.

**What would move it most**
1. **The serving info floats by itself (defect, in both versions).** "Serves 4 · 45 minutes" sits about 100 px below the title and about 100 px above the columns, so it belongs to neither. The cook passes over it, and the page opens with two empty bands that look left over rather than planned. Group things by spacing: tight space inside a group, more space between groups. Move the serving info up under the title so they read as one header block, and keep one larger gap before Ingredients and Method. That also brings the method higher on the screen.
2. **The ingredient rules don't match in version 2 (defect).** On white, the lines under "3 large leeks", "1 litre vegetable stock" and "A small bunch of chives" look cream or yellow, and the others look grey. The list looks uneven, as if some rows meant something different. The rules seem to have been coloured to suit the cream page. If you keep white, use one neutral grey for every rule. Version 1 doesn't show the problem because the cream hides it.

**Smaller things.**
- Step 4 ends with "chives." alone on its last line. Shorten the step or adjust the wrap so the line holds at least two words.

**What's working.**
- Big serif title, bold sans section headings, serif steps and sans ingredients: each kind of text has its own clear look, and there are only about four text sizes.
- The steps run about 74 characters per line with relaxed line spacing, and the numbers sit outside the text, so a cook glancing back from the pan can find their place.
- The ingredients and method sit side by side under matching headings, so you can see the whole recipe without scrolling.
