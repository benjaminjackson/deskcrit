**First read.** The eye lands on the inbox tray illustration, drops to "No open conversations", then to the helper text and the "Open customer list" button. It reads at once as the empty state of a customer-support inbox: calm, tidy, nothing urgent. The two versions read identically. The only difference is the tray's fill, pale green in version 1 and pale blue in version 2.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the empty Inbox view of a support or helpdesk tool. It should tell an agent there's nothing waiting and give them a sensible next step.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job, and they do it equally well. Swapping the tray from green to blue changes nothing the viewer understands or does. The biggest weakness is shared by both: the sidebar doesn't clearly show which view you're in.

**What would move it most**
1. **The sidebar doesn't show where you are** (defect; Nielsen 1, visibility of system status). "Inbox" at top left differs from the other items only by bold. It has no selected background, no indicator bar, and sits at the same indent. That makes it as easy to read as a section heading over "Assigned to me / Waiting / Closed" as the view you're on. If an agent lands here from "Waiting" and sees "No open conversations", they can't be sure which list is empty. Fix: give the current item a clear selected state, such as a tinted row or a side bar, in both versions.

**Smaller things.**
- "Open customer list" is the only next step on the screen, but it's styled as a quiet outlined button, and its grey border looks pale against white (probably under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for). Darken the border. For taste, consider giving it the primary fill, since it's the one thing the screen asks the agent to do.
- The helper text breaks after "You", so the second sentence starts at the end of line 1. Rebreak it so each line holds one sentence, or narrow the measure so "You can" moves down together.

**What's working.**
- The headline says exactly what state you're in ("No open conversations", not "Nothing here"), and the helper text explains both how this list fills and what you can do meanwhile. The button then turns that second sentence into one concrete action. Keep this chain from what's happening to what you can do to the button.
- The hierarchy is clean: one illustration, one bold headline, one body size, one button. There are three clear levels and nothing competes.
- The tint is muted in both versions. Green slightly supports "all clear"; blue is neutral and closer to the slate outline. Either is fine. Choose whichever matches the product's accent colour, not on the strength of this screen.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The button's hover and focus states, and the keyboard focus order between the sidebar and the button.
