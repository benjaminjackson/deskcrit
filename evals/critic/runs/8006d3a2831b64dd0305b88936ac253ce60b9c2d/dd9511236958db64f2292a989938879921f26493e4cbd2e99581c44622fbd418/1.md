**Verdict.** It's tidy and pleasant to read, but it doesn't yet get anyone through the door. The visiting details are vague and buried in the middle of the story, and the picture and text aren't set on a shared alignment, so the layout looks placed by eye.

**What would move it most**
1. **The visitor details are too vague to act on.** *(Defect.)* Paragraph 2 says "open to visitors on summer weekends" and paragraph 3 says tickets are sold "at the cottage by the car park". The reader can't tell which months, what hours, what it costs, or where the car park is. Those are the facts a visitor plans around and later holds you to. "Summer weekends" can mean June to August or Easter to September, and the person who gets it wrong turns up to a locked door. The best line on the page, about climbing to the gallery and following the ferry across the bay, sits in the middle of a paragraph and is never turned into an ask. To fix it, give dates, hours, price and location as plain facts the reader can scan, and end on one specific ask built on that view, such as which morning to come.
2. **The picture and the text don't line up with each other.** *(Convention: Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on near-miss placement.)* The illustration (roughly y 230–573) looks centred vertically on the frame, while the text hangs from the headline at about y 145 and ends around y 497. So the image top sits about 20 px below the first body line and misses both the headline and the body, and its bottom runs about 75 px past the last line. Neither edge is clearly aligned or clearly offset, so it reads as a mistake and the two halves don't feel like one unit. Pick one relationship: align the image top to the headline cap height or to the first body line, and let the space below collect in one place.
3. **The illustration just repeats the headline.** *(Taste.)* A stock flat lighthouse says "lighthouse" and nothing about *this* lighthouse. The copy promises an estuary mouth, ninety steps, a gallery and a ferry across the bay, and the picture shows none of them. The tower even stands straight in open sea with no point, gallery or lit lamp. As it stands, the picture could sit on any coastal page, so it adds no reason to visit Gull Point. Make the picture carry something the headline doesn't: the view from the gallery, the ferry, the point itself, or at least the lamp lit.
