**First read.** The eye lands on the big tan box at the top, finds nothing in it, and drops to "Good morning, Dana". Then it hits the pass paragraph. In version 1 that paragraph is a block of capitals, so the eye snags on it and slows down. In version 2 it reads in one easy pass. After that the eye goes down to two classes and a dark "Book a class" button. It's a calm, warm booking home screen for a yoga studio member.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a signed-in member's mobile home screen that tells them what's left on their pass and the booking rules, and gets them into today's class.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only change is that the pass-and-rules paragraph is no longer in all caps, and that paragraph carries the numbers and deadlines a member acts on. Both versions share the same bigger problems: the top third of the screen is an empty picture box, and the booking button doesn't connect to the classes listed above it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The paragraph in all caps (version 1 only; version 2 fixes it).** Five lines of capitals hold the session count, the expiry date, the two-hour booking rule and the 8 pm cancel cutoff. Capitals take away word shapes, so people read them more slowly, and capitals at text tracking crowd together (Butterick; Bringhurst on letterspacing capitals). These are the lines a member will later hold the studio to, so harder reading here costs real misreads. Defect. Version 2 is in sentence case, about 45 characters to the line, with comfortable spacing between lines. Keep that.
2. **The empty image box (both).** A gradient box takes about a quarter of the screen and the first glance, and shows nothing. If a photo failed to load, the finding is about the load. If the box is meant to look like this, it's decoration that pushes the classes and the button down. Defect: show a real studio or class photo, or drop the box and lift the classes up.
3. **"Book a class" doesn't name a class (both).** Right above it are two specific classes with times and spaces left, but the rows don't look tappable. The button asks the viewer to start over instead of booking what they're already looking at. Defect: make each row the thing you tap (for example, "Book Slow flow, 7:00"), or have the button name the next class.

**Smaller things.**
- The class list has no heading, so it isn't clear whether these are today's classes. Add "Today" or the date.
- About 150 px of empty space sits below the button. The image box is what's using up that room.

**What's working.**
- The serif greeting against sans-serif text gives one clear place to start, and it makes the screen feel personal.
- The class rows are clean: name in bold, time and room below, spaces left on the right. You can scan them in a second.
- The muted warm palette and the single dark button fit a yoga studio, and the primary action is clear.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the image box is a photo that failed to load. Check it in a live build.
