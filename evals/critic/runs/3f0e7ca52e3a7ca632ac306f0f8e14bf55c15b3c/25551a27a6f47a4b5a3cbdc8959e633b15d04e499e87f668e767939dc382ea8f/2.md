**First read.** My eye lands on "Before you board", then drops into three paragraphs set entirely in capitals. They read as one gray, even block that seems to be shouting, so I slow down and start picking out single words. It reads as a help or FAQ page for a local river ferry. The heading and the small label feel calm and well judged, but the body undoes that calm.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page telling ferry passengers the route, how often boats run, the bicycle rules and the winter changes, so they can plan a trip.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but setting all the body text in capitals makes it hard to read. That is the single biggest problem. Next is the copy: it gives no real times, so a reader still can't plan a crossing from it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The body is set in capitals** (all three paragraphs, nine lines). Capitals take away the word shapes that readers scan by. Every word becomes the same height of rectangle, so reading drops to letter by letter. The text also reads as loud, which is the wrong tone for calm practical advice. Butterick (*Practical Typography*, "All caps") says caps suit headings shorter than one line and that whole paragraphs shouldn't be capitalized. Hochuli (*Detail in Typography*) calls text in capitals "particularly difficult to read." **Fix:** set the paragraphs in normal upper and lower case. Keep capitals only for the "PASSENGER NOTES" label, where they already work. *Defect.*
2. **The copy leaves out the facts people act on.** "Between early morning and late evening" has no hours. "In winter" has no dates. "One boat each hour" doesn't say whether that's on the hour or from a set time. These are exactly the details a passenger plans around and later holds you to. As written, someone could show up at 6:30 or 7:30 and miss the first boat, or arrive in early March not knowing whether the winter timetable still applies. The page also sends readers to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing," which means they have to be at the landing to find out when to get there. **Fix:** add the first and last departure times and the dates the winter timetable starts and ends. If the timetable lives elsewhere online, link to it. Only you have these facts. *Defect.*

**What's working.**
- **Heading and label.** "Before you board" is bold, dark and clearly the largest type, so the eye knows where to start. The small, gray, letterspaced "PASSENGER NOTES" label sits above it without competing. Keep both as they are.
- **Text column.** Lines run about 70 characters, the line spacing is comfortable, and the paragraphs are separated by space, not indents. Once the case is fixed, this column will be easy to read.
- **Copy structure.** Each paragraph covers one topic (route and timing, bicycles, winter), and the bicycle paragraph tells riders exactly what to do and in what order. That's good practical writing.
