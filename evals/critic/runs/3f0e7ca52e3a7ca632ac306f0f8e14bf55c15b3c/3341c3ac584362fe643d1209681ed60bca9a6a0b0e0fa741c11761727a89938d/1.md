**First read.** The eye lands on "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes," drops to the paragraph, and then down the left column to the blue button. Within two seconds it reads as the landing page for a small local ferry. It feels calm and tidy, but also sparse and unfinished. Everything sits in the left 60% of the frame, the right side is blank, and nothing on the page shows a boat, the water, or the place.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the first screen of a ferry operator's site, for locals and day-trippers, meant to make them want the crossing and send them to the timetable.

**Verdict.** It works at a basic level: the message is clear and the one action is obvious. But the page holds back what a ferry visitor came for: where the boat goes, when the next sailing leaves, and what it costs. Half the screen sits empty while those facts are missing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page hides the facts a rider needs** (defect). The paragraph says "every forty minutes from early morning until late evening." A rider planning a trip can't act on that. They still don't know the first and last sailings, when the next boat leaves, or the fare, so the only way forward is the button to another page. This is common sense (no approved source covers it): put the facts people came for on the first screen, such as the next departures, the actual first and last times, and the price. "See the timetable" can stay as the route to the full schedule. The empty right half is the obvious place for them.
2. **The copy never says where the boat goes** (defect). "Cross the bay" and "the north landing" name the start but not the destination. A visitor from out of town can't tell whether this boat reaches the place they want to go. Name the destination landing or town in the headline or the first sentence of the paragraph, using whatever the real place is.
3. **The layout tips to the left and the right side looks unused** (convention). All the dark type sits in one column ending around x≈790, and nothing on the right balances it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). It doesn't come across as chosen white space. It looks like a missing image or panel, and it adds to the "template, not yet filled in" feel. Either give the right side something that earns its weight (the departures from finding 1, or a picture of the boat and the harbor the paragraph promises) or rebalance the column. Leaving it empty on purpose is the weakest option.

**Smaller things.**
- The feature list ("Bicycles travel free" etc.) looks about 2 px smaller than the paragraph above it, so the two read as a near-miss rather than two separate levels (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1). Set the list in the same size as the paragraph and set it apart by weight or a marker, or make it clearly smaller.

**What's working.**
- The headline is specific and makes a promise ("twelve calm minutes"). It names a benefit and a number, and its tight two-line setting holds together. Keep it.
- There's one clear primary action, and the button label says exactly what happens when you click it.
- The restraint helps: one blue for the brand line and the button, near-black type, one left edge from top to bottom, and a comfortable paragraph line length of about 60 characters.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- There's no header, navigation, or logo in the frame. If this is the top of the real page, a visitor has no way to reach anything else on the site except the timetable. Check this on the live page.
