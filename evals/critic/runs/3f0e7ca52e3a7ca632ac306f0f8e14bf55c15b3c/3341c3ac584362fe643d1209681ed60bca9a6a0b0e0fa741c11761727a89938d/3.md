**First read.** The eye lands on the big black headline, "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops through the paragraph, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. That takes a couple of seconds and nothing gets in the way. It reads as the landing page for a small local ferry, aimed at day-trippers and locals with bikes and kids. It feels calm, clean and trustworthy. It also feels half-finished, because the whole right side of the screen is blank.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: the hero of a ferry operator's website. It should make a casual visitor want to take the crossing and send them to departure times.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are competent, and the headline does real work. The page fails at the moment of decision: it never says where the ferry goes, when the next boat leaves, or what it costs, so a visitor ready to go still has to click through to find out. The empty right half makes it look like a picture failed to load.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy holds back the facts the visitor came for.** *Defect.*
   - **No destination.** "Cross the bay" and "the north landing" say where you board, not where you get off. A stranger can't tell whether this boat goes where they're going.
   - **No real times.** "Every forty minutes from early morning until late evening" sounds friendly but gives no time anyone can plan around. It leaves the viewer to guess at the first and last sailings, and those decide whether they can get home.
   - **No price.** "Bicycles travel free" implies there are fares, but no fare is shown.
   - **The button is fine but carries everything.** "See the timetable" names a specific action, yet it has to do all the work the page skipped.
   - **No comparison.** The headline's "twelve calm minutes" is the strongest line, but it isn't set against anything the visitor already knows. The obvious candidate is how long the trip around the bay takes by road. The maker has that fact; I don't.
   - **Fix:** say the destination in the headline or the paragraph. Replace "early morning until late evening" with the actual first and last sailings, or show the next departure. Add the adult fare. Add the comparison above if it's true. (Judgment and common sense: a visitor shouldn't have to hunt for what they're likely to need next.)
2. **The right ~40% of the frame is empty, so the page tips left and looks broken.** *Defect, if the space is meant to be empty.*
   - **What I see:** every element sits in a left column ending around x=790. Nothing at right, from the top of the frame to the bottom, balances it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
   - **What the viewer may think:** it looks like a missing hero image, so they may assume the page hasn't finished loading.
   - **If an image is supposed to be there:** this is a capture or loading problem. Check it in a live browser.
   - **If it's deliberate:** either center a narrower column, or give the right side something worth seeing. This is a page selling a view of the harbor ("watch the harbour slide past") and it shows no water at all. A photo from the open deck would also do what the copy is trying to do.

**Smaller things.**
- **Headline line break:** it breaks after "twelve", which splits the number from "calm minutes" (Bringhurst 2.4.6 covers keeping a number with its unit; applying it to headings is my judgment). Break after "bay" or "in" so "twelve calm minutes" stays on one line.
- **Paragraph vs. list size:** the paragraph (~19 px) and the three list lines (~18 px) look like almost the same size, which reads as a slip rather than a choice (Bringhurst 3.1.1). Use the same size and separate them by spacing, or make the list clearly smaller.

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** it promises an experience ("calm"), gives a concrete number ("twelve minutes"), and is set big and tight enough to carry the page alone. Keep it as the one dominant element.
- **The three features:** short, plain and true to what a day-tripper wants to know (bikes, seats outside, hot drinks). Better as a list than as more paragraph.
- **Restraint:** one blue, used only for the brand name and the button, so the button can't be missed.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **The blank right half:** it may be a hero image or illustration that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- **How the page reflows on a phone:** a ferry page will often be read on a phone at the pier.
