**First read.** My eye goes first to "184,220" and then across the three number cards. After that it drops into the big chart, which is where the read falls apart. There are three rising lines, but nothing says which line is which channel, there are no numbers on either axis, and there's an empty band under the chart where the week labels should be. It reads as a tidy, calm, fairly generic analytics overview. The top half informs you and the bottom half only decorates.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a growth or marketing team opens this to see how traffic and sign-ups did over the last 12 weeks and which channels drove the change.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The chart takes up about 60% of the screen and can't be read: you can see that three things went up, but not what they are, how big they are, or when anything happened. The number cards also contradict each other on the one metric a growth team cares about most.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no legend, axis values or week labels** (the whole "Visits by channel" panel). This is a defect.
   - **What the viewer gets:** three colors with no key, so they can't tell which channel is blue, orange or green. The gridlines have no values, so they can't tell whether the gap between lines is 2,000 visits or 50,000, or whether the y-axis starts at zero. With no x labels, they can't place a dip, like the one at the blue line's seventh point, in a week. The question in the title, "which channel?", goes unanswered.
   - **Why it matters:** every chart needs labeled axes with units and a readable scale, so the reader never guesses what a number measures (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). Without a scale you also can't check that the scale is honest. The colors alone carry the series, and an orange/green pair with no labels fails WCAG 1.4.1. The unlabeled gridlines and the empty strip under the axis are ink and space doing no work.
   - **Fix direction:**
     - Put the channel name at the right end of each line instead of in a separate legend (Tufte; Wilke).
     - Add y-axis values, and week or date ticks along the bottom.
   - If the labels exist and just didn't render in this capture, that has to be checked live. As shown, the chart is unreadable.
2. **The conversion card contradicts the other two cards** (top right card). This is a defect.
   - **The math:**
     - Visits went up 6.1% and sign-ups went up 2.4%. If sign-ups grew more slowly than visits, sign-ups per visit fell.
     - Back-of-envelope: 1.024 / 1.061 ≈ 0.965, about a 3.5% relative drop. From 2.12% now, that's down roughly 0.08 points.
     - The card says "+0.1 pts" in success green.
   - **What it costs:** anyone who does that quick math will stop trusting the dashboard. Anyone who doesn't will believe conversion improved when the other two numbers say it slipped.
   - **What would make it fine:** conversion may be calculated differently (for example, an average of the weekly rates). If so, the card has to say how. If not, the delta or its color is wrong.
   - This is the number people act on, so a reading that can go two ways is a defect, not a style note.
3. **The chart title names a topic instead of saying what happened** ("Visits by channel"). This is taste.
   - A team opens this screen to learn which channel moved the 6.1%. Right now they have to work that out themselves, and with no labels they can't.
   - **Fix direction:** once the series are labeled, let the title or a short caption say the finding in words, using a fact only you have (which channel led, by how much). That turns the biggest panel into the answer instead of the raw material.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks", top right, is set as plain gray text. If it's the date-range picker, it doesn't look clickable: give it a border, a chevron or button styling. If it's only a label, it's fine.
- "Overview" is a generic heading that could sit on anyone's dashboard. That's fine for navigation, but it's spending the second-largest type on the page without saying anything.

**What's working.**
- **The card row.** Each number has a quiet label above, a big bold figure, and a delta against a stated period ("vs previous 12 weeks"). That's exactly the context a headline number needs. Keep this structure.
- **The type scale.** I count about four sizes (page title, card figure, label, delta), each with a clear job, plus weight carrying the finer distinctions. Nothing collides.
- **The restraint.** A neutral gray ground, white panels, one consistent grid and gutter, and saturated color kept to the data. Once the chart is labeled, it will be easy to read against this background.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the chart's axis labels and legend exist but failed to load or render. The empty band under the x-axis suggests they might; check in a live browser.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a picker, and how the cards and chart reflow on narrower screens.
