**First read.** The eye lands on the three big numbers in the top row (184,220, 3,912, 2.12%), then drops to the big chart, where three colored lines climb from left to right. Then it stalls. Nothing says which line is which channel, what the values are, or which weeks run along the bottom. Overall it reads as a calm, tidy, fairly generic analytics overview, and the most expensive thing on the page tells you almost nothing.

**What it's for.** Nothing was given, so I'm assuming this is a marketing or growth dashboard for someone checking each week whether traffic and sign-ups are healthy and which channels are driving them.

**Verdict.** The three-number row works. The chart takes up about two thirds of the screen and can't be read: it has no series names, no axis values and no dates. The viewer learns "three things went up" and nothing about the one question its title, "Visits by channel," promises to answer.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart doesn't say which line is which channel.** The blue, orange and green lines in "Visits by channel" have no legend and no labels. The title promises channels, so the viewer has to guess whether blue is organic or paid, and there's no way to check. The chart can't answer its own question. Every line needs a label (Tufte, *Visual Display*; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). The usual fix is to put the channel name at the right end of each line, next to the last point, where there's already free space. That also takes care of color-blind viewers: color is currently the only thing that separates the series (WCAG 1.4.1), and the orange and green lines look close in lightness. If this is a capture where the labels failed to render, check it live. As shown, it's a defect.
2. **There's no scale, so the shapes can't be measured.** The y-axis has no values, the x-axis has no week labels, and the faint gridlines have no numbers. The viewer can't tell whether blue is twice orange or 10% above it, whether the baseline is zero, or which week the dip around the middle falls in. Every chart needs axis values and units (Tufte; Few, *Show Me the Numbers*), and without them the scale can't be checked either (Tufte). The fix is to put values on the y gridlines and week dates on the x-axis. The empty band of about 60 px above the plot, about 70 px below it and the wide left gutter are where those labels should go, so this costs no layout. *Defect.*
3. **The chart title names a topic instead of a finding.** "Visits by channel" tells the viewer what the chart is about but not what happened. The KPI cards above give a verdict ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"), and the chart gives none. Once lines are labeled, the title or a one-line caption should say which channel drove the change, using the maker's real figures. *Taste* (no approved source states it). The cards already set this standard, and the chart is the one part that falls short of it.

**Smaller things.**
- The green delta lines under each KPI ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks") look small and mid-green on white. Their contrast looks borderline for 4.5:1, so measure it and darken the green one step if it falls short (WCAG 1.4.3).

**What's working.**
- **The KPI row:** one label, one big number and one comparison against the previous period per card. That's exactly the right shape. Every headline number has a reference point, and "+0.1 pts" correctly uses points rather than a percent for a rate. Keep this pattern.
- **A tight type scale:** about four sizes (page title, card number, chart title and labels, deltas), each with a clear job. Bold carries emphasis instead of extra sizes.
- **Restraint:** a neutral gray and white ground with color only on data and deltas. Once the chart is labeled, the page will be easy to read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- "Last 12 weeks" in the top right looks like plain text, but it's probably the date-range control. In a live browser, check that it looks clickable. If it changes the range, it needs a button or dropdown cue.
- Whether the chart shows values on hover. Even if it does, the still view still needs axis labels and series names.
