**First read.** My eye goes to the three big numbers across the top (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%). The small green deltas under them tell me it's a quiet, healthy dashboard. Then it drops to the large chart and stalls: three colored lines climb gently, but nothing says which channel is which, what the numbers are, or what dates they cover. It feels clean, calm, and competent, but the biggest thing on the screen tells me almost nothing.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a marketing or growth analytics overview for someone on the team. They want to see in a few seconds how traffic and sign-ups are trending over the last 12 weeks and which channels are driving it.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job and the bottom half doesn't. "Visits by channel" takes up about 60% of the screen but has no channel names, no values and no dates. Right now it's a picture of three lines, not a chart.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no series labels, axis values or time axis** (*defect*). There's no legend and no direct labels, so a viewer can't tell which channel is blue, orange or green. That breaks the one promise the title makes: "by channel." There are no y-axis values, so you can't read a gap like "blue is about twice green" as a number. You can't even tell whether the axis starts at zero, so the gentle climbs could be big or tiny. The x-axis has no week labels, so the 12 points can't be lined up with the "Last 12 weeks" range in the header. Tufte and Few both make labeled axes with units a basic requirement, and WCAG 1.4.1 says color can't be the only thing that tells series apart. Blue, orange and green only by hue also put people with color-vision deficiency at risk.
   - **Fix:** Put each channel's name right at the end of its line (there's room on the right; Tufte and Wilke favor direct labels over a legend). Add y-axis values on the existing gridlines and week or date ticks along the baseline.
   - The empty gutter to the left of the plot (x≈71–121) and the empty band under the baseline (y≈685–755) look like the space those labels were meant to fill. Filling them also fixes how the plot sits in its card: right now its left edge lines up with nothing above it.
2. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding** (*taste*; I can't cite an approved source for this). "Visits by channel" makes the viewer work out the story from the lines. On a dashboard meant to be read at a glance, the title or a one-line caption should say what changed. Draw that from the data the maker has, for example which channel drove the +6.1%. This only matters once finding 1 is fixed, because until then there's nothing to read the finding from.

**Smaller things.** None worth listing separately. The alignment issue is part of finding 1.

**What's working.**
- **The KPI cards.** Each pairs a large figure with a quiet gray label and a comparison to the previous 12 weeks. The comparison is what turns a bare number into news (Few). Keep that structure.
- **"+0.1 pts" on the conversion rate.** Using percentage points rather than "+0.1%" is the honest, unambiguous unit. It's the kind of copy detail that usually gets wrong.
- **Restraint.** One neutral range, color saved for data and status, and a type scale of about four sizes with clear jobs (page title, card figure, label, delta). The page reads as one calm whole.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Last 12 weeks" is a date-range picker. If it is, it currently looks like static text and needs a visible signal that it can be clicked. Hover tooltips on the chart can't be judged from a still either. Even if they exist, they don't replace the labels above.
