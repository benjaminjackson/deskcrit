**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, drops to the "Create your link" button, then slides into the four blue-outlined cards. Everything below the headline is blue too, so the eye doesn't pick out one more thing; it moves evenly across the card row. Then it lands on the right half of the hero, which is empty. It reads as a clean but generic SaaS landing page for a scheduling tool. It feels tidy and safe, and it looks like a template.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home page of a booking-link product aimed at small service businesses (clinics, tutors, coaches), and its job is to get them to create a link or try the live example.

**Verdict.** The page is clear about what it is, and the headline and main button work. It is weak at making anyone want it. Blue is spent on every element, so the main action has nothing to stand out from, and the page never shows the product: half the hero is blank where the guest's booking page should be.

**What would move it most**

1. **Blue on everything, so blue means nothing.** The same strong blue is on the headline, the nav links, both buttons, the "See a live example" link, the 2 px borders of all four cards, every icon, every card title, every badge, every "Learn more", and the full-width rule above the customer names. The "Create your link" button is the one thing that should stand out, and it sits among about thirty other blue items. The four heavily outlined cards add up to more blue than the hero, so they pull the eye down past the call to action. This breaks a convention (Apple HIG and Material Design on accent color), and here it costs the main action its pull. The fix: keep the blue for the main action and at most one highlight. Make the headline near-black. Draw card borders, icons, and titles in neutrals, or drop the card borders for a pale tone or plain space. Then the button is the only saturated thing in the hero. *Convention.*

2. **The hero has an empty half where the product should be.** All the weight in the top ~380 px is in the left 650 px, and the right half is blank white above a dense row of cards. The layout looks unfinished and tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The bigger problem is what's missing. A booking link is something the visitor's own customers will see, and the page never shows it. The visitor has to take "fill your calendar" on trust, or click away to "See a live example" to find out what they'd be sending people. The fix: put the product in that space, ideally the same booking page the live-example link opens. Then the visitor can judge it against how they handle bookings now, without leaving the page. If the blank area is an image that didn't load, check the live page; if it's by design, it's a defect. *Defect.*

3. **The customer names don't read as proof.** "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" sit in small gray text under a blue rule, with no heading or label (WCAG 2.4.6, headings and labels). Without a label they could be footer links, sample data, or a list of locations. This row is the page's only evidence that businesses like the visitor's use the product, and it doesn't say so. The fix: give the row a short label saying these businesses use Slotwise. Showing their logos would help too, if the maker has permission. *Defect.*

4. **The supporting line stops short of the payoff.** "Keep every meeting in the right place" makes the reader guess what "the right place" is: their calendar? the right staff member? the right room? The headline makes a promise ("fill your calendar") that almost any booking tool could make. Nothing in the hero says why this one, or what the visitor stops doing (the back-and-forth over times) once they use it. The fix: say concretely what "the right place" means, and finish the line with what the visitor no longer has to do. The cards already hold the material for that, for example "nobody has to do the math" and "cut down on no-shows". *Defect (copy).*

**Smaller things.**
- Badges: "New" and "Beta" are status labels, but "Auto" and "Email" describe features, and all four use the same pill. A reader can take "Email" as a status. Keep badges for status only, or drop the other two.
- Card 1: the "New" badge squeezes "Shared calendars" onto two lines. That pushes its body text and "Learn more" about 30 px below the other three cards, so the row loses its common baselines (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Put the badge on its own line or above the title so every title fits on one line.
- "Start free" (nav) and "Create your link" (hero) are both solid blue primary buttons with different labels. A visitor can wonder whether they are two different things. Use one label, or step the nav button down to an outline (Material Design, button emphasis).

**What's working.**
- The headline really dominates. It's the only big size, it's bold, and it's set tight, and below it the type scale has clear steps (headline, subhead, card title, body, small gray). Keep that scale when you change the colors.
- "See a live example" is the right second action. It lets the visitor judge the product for themselves instead of taking the claim on trust, and it correctly sits a step below the filled button.
- The card copy is concrete and speaks to the visitor: "so nobody has to do the math" and "to cut down on no-shows" say what each feature does for them. The subhead should do the same.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the nav links, buttons, and the four "Learn more" links. Whether the empty right half of the hero is an image that never loaded; check it in a live browser.
