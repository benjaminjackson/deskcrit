**First read.** The eye goes to "Tell us about your project" at top left, then right to the white form card, then down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads right away as the contact page of a small design or creative studio. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: apart from the wordmark, nothing on the page says anything about Fernwick in particular.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this page should get a prospective client to send a project inquiry that gives the studio enough to reply to.

**Verdict.** As a form it works. The order is clear, the labels are good, there's one obvious button, and the text reads well. The main problem is that the intro asks for a budget and a timeline but the form doesn't, so the inquiries the studio cares about most will often arrive without them. The page also looks like a default template, which costs more on a studio's site than almost anywhere else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The budget and timeline request sits outside the form.** "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them" is in the left column. The field it applies to, "Message", is an empty box about 450 px away with no prompt of its own. Someone who skimmed the intro, or forgot it by the time they reached the box, will type "Hi, we need a new website" and send it. The studio then has to write back to ask for exactly what this line asked for. This is a **defect** (Nielsen 6, Recognition rather than recall; Nielsen 10, help belongs next to the step it concerns). The fix is to put the request where the typing happens: hint text under the Message label, or two optional fields of its own (budget range, timeline). Either way, the visitor should see the request at the moment they write.
2. **The form doesn't say which fields are required.** None of the three labels has an asterisk or "optional". Visitors can't tell whether they can skip anything, and they only find out by submitting. That matters more if you add the budget and timeline fields from point 1, since those are clearly meant to be optional ("if you have them"). This is a **convention** (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). The fix: put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once at the top of the form, or put "optional" after each optional label.
3. **It could be anyone's contact page.** This is **taste**, and I'd argue it hard for a studio. The system-style sans, the gray page, the white card and the stock-blue button are the defaults of a thousand SaaS settings screens. "Tell us about your project" is a headline that could sit on any agency site. A visitor deciding whether to hire a studio reads this page as a sample of its work, and right now the sample says "template". The direction is to give the page something only Fernwick would do: its own type, color or layout move, plus a headline or intro line that only this studio could say. The copy is the place to get specific, and the line that already does that is "reply within two working days". Keep it and build on that kind of concreteness. I'm not going to suggest what the identity should be; that's yours.

**Smaller things.**
- The nav doesn't mark the current page. "Contact" is styled like Work, Services and About, so give it one cue such as a bolder weight or an underline bar (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- The card's right edge (around x=1068) doesn't line up with anything. The nav ends at about 1224, so there's a dead strip on the right and the layout looks placed by eye. Either extend the card to the right margin or set it on columns that the header also uses (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- The empty space isn't gathered into one deliberate area. There's a large void under the left column, another below the card, and the strip on the right, so it looks left over rather than designed. Putting the card on the grid fixes most of this.
- The input borders look like a light gray on white, which may fall below 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border color against white and darken it if it's short.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clear: one bold headline, plain body text, bold labels close to their fields, and one solid primary button. Nothing competes, and the path from headline to Send is obvious.
- "We read every message and reply within two working days" is specific, sets an expectation the visitor can hold you to, and reassures them that the message won't vanish. Keep it.
- The form itself is right: one column, visible labels above each field (not placeholder-only), a roomy message box and a button label that says what happens.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button. These matter here because the whole page is a keyboard-fillable form.
- Validation and error messages, and what the visitor sees after pressing Send.
- How the two columns stack on a narrow screen.
