**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves right to the white form card, and stops on the blue "Send message" button. It reads as the contact page of a small design or dev studio. It's calm, tidy and competent, and it's also anonymous: with the wordmark covered, it could be any studio's page, or a form template.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: this is a studio's contact page, and its job is to get a prospective client to send a useful first inquiry.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The path from headline to form to button is clear and nothing gets in the way. What holds it back is small: the form asks for less than the page wants, because the budget and timeline request sits apart from the field where you'd type it, and no field says what's required. The layout also leaves a dead strip on the right, so it looks unfinished.

**What would move it most**
1. **The useful instruction is in the wrong place.** The left column asks for "a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them", but the Message box (center, ~y 362–482) gives no hint of this. Visitors read the intro and then start typing in the box, and by then the request is out of view. Many won't include either, which costs you a round-trip email. Put short hint text in or right under the Message field, near the step it concerns (Nielsen, heuristics 6 and 10). Keep the line on the left as reassurance if you like. *Defect.*
2. **Nobody can tell which fields are required.** Name, Email and Message all look the same. A visitor can't tell whether they can skip their name or send only a message, and finds out only from an error after pressing Send. Put an asterisk after each required label and explain it once at the top of the form, or write "optional" after the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms"). *Defect.*
3. **The copy could be on anyone's page.** "Tell us about your project" is the stock headline of every agency contact page. Nothing here tells a hesitant visitor why to write to this studio rather than the next tab. The left column has a lot of empty space under the intro (~y 300–580) where a reason could go: what kinds of project you take on, or what happens after someone writes. Only you have that information. "Reply within two working days" is the one specific line, and it's good. Keep it. *Taste, argued: on a contact page the copy does the persuading, and here it only does the logistics.*

**Smaller things.**
- **Current page not marked:** In the header nav (top right), "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing confirms where you are. Mark it with one cue, such as a bolder weight, a bar or a color (Laubheimer, NN/g, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- **Form card stops short of the page edge:** The card ends at about x 1068, but the nav and header run to about x 1224, leaving a ~155 px strip that lines up with nothing. Either widen the content to the header's right edge, or make the split between the two columns a deliberate proportion. Right now it looks like it was placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- **Field borders look faint:** The mid-gray borders on white look close to 2.5:1, which is below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on input edges. Measure them, and darken them if they fall short.

**What's working.**
- **The reading order is plain.** The bold headline is clearly first, the form is second, and the single solid blue button is the only primary action, so nobody wonders what to do.
- **Labels sit above their fields.** They're bold, short and close to their own field, and the form is one column. Keep that.
- **"We read every message and reply within two working days."** It's a concrete promise that answers the visitor's real worry about whether anyone will reply. Protect it in any rewrite.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and button.
- Hover and pressed states.
- Whether Email uses an email input type, and how errors appear after submitting.
