**First read.** Nothing catches the eye first. It starts at the top-left line because that's where reading starts, then moves down a gray, even block of text. The four short lines that should be headings ("Changing your pickup time", etc.) look a little *smaller* than the paragraphs under them, with the same weight and color, so they come across as lead-in sentences rather than signposts. It reads as a bakery's pickup/order help page: calm and clean, but flat. There's no title saying what page this is.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a help or FAQ section where a customer with a bread order finds the one answer they came for (change pickup, send someone else, allergies, cancel) and acts on it.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The copy is mostly clear and the spacing is sound, but the headings don't work as headings, so a customer can't scan for their question. They have to read the whole page. The fix is simple, and it's the thing that matters.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings are no bigger or bolder than the answers, so the page can't be scanned (defect).** The four question lines at left (y ≈ 70, 183, 269, 382) look about 18 px, regular weight, same near-black as the body text, which looks about 19 px. Someone who came to cancel an order has to read every paragraph to find the cancel section, because nothing marks where each answer starts. Dair's point applies directly: a contrast has to be obvious at a glance, and here it's zero or slightly backward (*Design with Type*, ch. 8). Make each heading clearly different from its text in at least one way: heavier weight is enough at this scale, or a clear size step up, so the eye can jump from question to question. That also gives the page a starting point, which it doesn't have now (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences).
2. **The page doesn't say what it is (defect).** There's no title above the four items. Arriving from a link or a search, the viewer has to piece together "bakery order help" from the answers. Add a page title that names the subject, and make it the one dominant element. That solves the "where do I start" problem in the same move.
3. **Two answers the customer will hold you to can be read two ways (defect, copy).** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" could mean up to 24 hours before the pickup time, or any time before the day of pickup. Whoever reads it the generous way and gets refused will feel cheated. Say ahead of what, and whether "a day" means a deadline time or 24 hours. "Show it at the counter along with a name": whose name, the person collecting or the person who ordered? Get it wrong and someone gets turned away at the counter. Put in the missing word ("your name" or "their name"), whichever is true.
4. **Lines run too long for comfortable reading (convention).** The full-width lines ("Give the person your order number…", "Orders can be cancelled…") look like they run about 95 characters, well past Bringhurst's 45–75 (*Elements*, 2.1.2). With such short answers the cost is small, but each return to the next line is a longer trip than it needs to be, and the column covers ~820 px of a 1280 px screen for no reason. Narrow the text column to aim for about 66 characters.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer. Reflow or reword slightly so the short last word isn't stranded (Bringhurst, 2.4.2).
- Allergies (copy, not layout): the answer sends the customer to a card by the till, but someone ordering online can't see it before paying. If ingredient info exists anywhere before pickup, say where. That's the maker's information to supply.

**What's working.**
- Spacing groups the content correctly: each heading sits about 10 px above its answer, and the groups are about 38 px apart. Once the headings get weight, the structure will read immediately. Keep those gaps.
- The answers are short, use the second person, and give a reason ("After that the bread has already been baked for you"). That line tells the customer why the rule exists instead of just stating it. Keep that voice.
- Body text is a comfortable size, dark on white, and set flush left in one plain sans. Nothing to fix there.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- "Open your order" suggests a link or account page. Check that it's linked from here in the live page and that the link looks like a link.
