**First read.** The eye goes to the headline first, then to the one brown tile (M4) in the shelf panel, then down to the brown "Add your studio kiln" button. It reads as a landing page for a booking tool for shared pottery studios, aimed at studio owners or managers. It feels calm, warm and earthy, and it plainly belongs to the trade.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since none was given: this is a marketing home page meant to get a community-studio owner to set up their kiln in Kilnshare. The sample firing is a secondary path.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The headline names a real problem in plain words, the panel shows the product working, and there is one clear thing to click. The biggest thing in the way is the shelf map, which is the evidence for the whole pitch: its color code is reversed from what people expect, and nothing on it explains which spaces are free.

**What would move it most**
1. **The shelf map's color code reads backwards and has no legend.** *Defect, plus a convention.* In the panel, 15 spaces are sage green and three are white with a dashed outline (T3, M6, B5). Green usually means "open" or "OK" (Apple HIG and Material, on status colors), so a viewer's first guess is that the green spaces can be booked and the white ones are gaps. Only the small gray "18 shelf spaces, 3 free" in the corner settles it, and only if the viewer counts. The dashed outline that is supposed to be the second cue is close to invisible against both the white fill and the tinted panel. It looks well under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on component edges, so measure it. That leaves fill color as the only signal for free versus booked (WCAG 1.4.1). This is the image that sells "without double-booked shelves", so a viewer who misreads it doesn't see that point land. Direction: make booked spaces a neutral or muted tone and free spaces the ones that look open. Give free spaces a cue that isn't color, such as a visible outline or a word in the tile, or label the states directly on the panel.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Kiln posts" band appears only between the M and B rows, so it reads as a stray label. A potter will wonder why there are no posts between T and M, and a stranger won't know what it means. Either draw posts between every shelf level or take the band out. *(Taste and common sense.)*
- The feature heading "Bisque and glaze apart" has no verb, so the reader has to rebuild what happens. The body line supplies it ("Book bisque and glaze firings on their own calendars"). Restoring a verb in the heading, as an editorial edit, would make it read like the other two.

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific to this world. "Double-booked shelves", "the technician loads the kiln from one list", "Six mugs, cone 6" and "$18 paid" could sit on no other product's page, so a studio owner sees their own problem straight away. Keep that voice when you revise.
- The ask names one exact thing to do: "Add your studio kiln", not "Get started". The secondary "See a sample firing" is styled clearly lower, as an underlined link.
- Color restraint: one brown accent is used only for the selected space and the primary button. That links "this is your booking" to "do this" and gives the eye a clean path from headline to panel to button.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** The free and booked spaces may have hover or tooltip states that explain themselves. If so, the map would still need to read correctly before anyone hovers.
