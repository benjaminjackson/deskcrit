I've finished the checklist and the closer reads, and I'm writing up the critique now.

**First read.** My eye lands on the headline "Know which rack every boat is on". It then jumps right to the large warm-gray box, finds only a generic image icon, and comes back to the navy "Map your boathouse" button. This reads at once as a SaaS landing page for rowing or sailing clubs, aimed at whoever runs the boathouse. It feels calm, plain and competent. The empty right half makes it feel unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the hero of a marketing homepage that should get a club captain or boathouse manager to start mapping their boathouse, or at least open the sample.

**Verdict.** The words and type do most of the job. The headline is specific and the ask is concrete. But half the hero is an empty placeholder where the product should be, so the page asks people to map their boathouse without ever showing them a map. Fix that first. After that, one money line in the features can be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero image is an empty box.** This is a defect, and also a template tell. The right half of the hero (about 548×350 px, at x 652–1200) is a tinted panel with a stock image icon and nothing else. The headline promises "know which rack every boat is on", and the paragraph says "the captain sees every boat at a glance". The one thing that would prove it is the rack plan itself, and that spot is blank. Two things follow for the viewer. Their eye is pulled to the biggest shape on the page and gets nothing back. And a generic box in the hero is a move they've seen on countless template sites, so the page looks like anyone's at the exact moment it should look like Slipway (Sokolovsky, unslop: "a product preview in code or an image from the world"). Put a real preview of a rack plan there: racks with boat names on them, club boats and private boats told apart, and maybe a fee status. That would make the image and the headline one idea. If this is an image that didn't load in the capture, check the live page. Even so, as captured it is the weakest part of the page.
2. **"Send every rack holder the yearly fee… with a card link" can be read two ways.** This is a defect in the copy. It's in the middle feature column, and it's a line about money, which is exactly the kind of line people hold you to. As written, "send … the fee" can mean that you pay the rack holder, when the intent is clearly that you bill them. "With a card link" also leaves the viewer to work out that it means a link to pay by card. Restore the missing verb and object so it says who pays whom. An editorial edit would be something like "Bill every rack holder for the yearly fee on one day, with a link to pay by card." "Rack fees each spring" is a heading with no verb, so the body line has to carry the meaning, and right now it doesn't.
3. **The ask doesn't set the product next to what the club uses today.** This is taste. "Map your boathouse" is a good primary ask: it names one exact thing to do. "See a sample boathouse" is a good secondary ask, because it lets viewers judge for themselves. But nothing on the page compares Slipway with how the captain tracks racks and fees now. The page states the value and leaves the viewer to take it on trust. A line or a visual that shows the current way next to the Slipway plan would let them judge the value on terms they already accept. The maker has to supply what that current way actually is.

**Smaller things.**
- In the hero paragraph, "glance." sits alone on the last line, a one-word runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Adjust the measure or rebreak the line so the last line carries at least two words.

**What's working.**
- **Specific copy.** The headline names a concrete outcome in the audience's own words ("rack", "boathouse", "captain"), and the primary button says exactly what happens. That is rare. Keep it.
- **Clear hierarchy.** There is one dominant headline, then a clearly smaller paragraph, then one primary button with a plainly lower underlined text link. Weight and color, not extra sizes, separate the feature headings from their body text.
- **A restrained palette.** It is neutral, with one navy accent kept to the primary button, so the action is the only colored thing on the page.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the hero image failed to load or is a placeholder in the design. Check the live page.
- Focus states on the button and links.
