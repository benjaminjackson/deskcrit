**First read.** The eye goes to the row of four big numbers first. They are all the same size, so it scans across them without stopping on any. Next it drops to the map, pulled by the two frosted tooltips and the one blue dot, then to the blue Friday bar, and only last to the gray table at the bottom. It reads as a calm, tidy operations dashboard for a city bike-share. It feels finished and quiet, maybe too quiet for a screen whose real news is that a station has had no bikes for 40 minutes.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a daily operations screen for the people who run the Wendmouth bike fleet. It should tell them how today is going and which stations need bikes moved now.

**Verdict.** It's clean and well built, but it doesn't put the urgent item first. An empty station with a van on the way is the one thing an operator must act on, and it's split across a tooltip, an unlabeled dot and a bottom-of-page table, while the 3,184 rides figure takes the top spot. Fix the ranking and the link between map and table, and this is a strong screen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The problem that needs action is ranked last.** The four KPI cards are the same size and weight, so nothing on the page stands out. The page's only alarm is Canal Basin with 0 bikes, empty for 40 minutes. It appears in three places, all quiet: a frosted tooltip, a calm blue dot, and the first row of a plain gray table at the very bottom. An operator glancing at this screen learns that rides are up before they learn that a station is empty. Direction: put the restock state at the top of the order, either as the first thing on the page or by giving the "Stations to restock" block and the empty station the strongest treatment. Then set the KPI row back. *Defect*: it slows the one decision this screen exists for. Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through differences of size.

2. **The map and the table don't connect.** The "Canal Basin" tooltip floats in the top-left corner with no pointer, so you can't tell which dot it describes. The only blue "Empty" dot sits mid-map, well away from it. "Van route 2" is described in a tooltip, but the van has no mark of its own on the map. The table lists Hartley Park gate (2 bikes) and Market Cross (3 bikes), but the map doesn't mark them, and its legend only has "Docking station" and "Empty". So the operator has to match names to dots from memory. Direction: anchor each callout to its dot, give the van a mark, and give low stations a map state that matches the table, so one glance links the two. *Defect*: it makes the user remember things across panels (Nielsen, heuristic 6: recognition rather than recall).

3. **Blue means two different things, and alone it carries the alarm.** The same blue marks "Empty" on the map and the highlighted Friday bar in the chart, so the eye pairs an alarm with a good day. Blue also reads as calm or informational, which is the wrong signal for an empty station. And on the map that dot is marked by color alone, with no label on it. Direction: give "empty" its own warning color plus a second cue, such as a label or a different shape. Keep the chart's highlight color for the chart only. *Convention*: semantic color (Apple HIG and Material Design, Color), and color alone carrying meaning (WCAG 1.4.1).

4. **The comparison against "last Monday" doesn't match the date.** The header reads "Today, 24 November 2026", which is a Tuesday. Every KPI subline compares with "last Monday": "2,960 last Monday", "16 min last Monday", "41 last Monday". Either these are yesterday's figures with the wrong label, or today is being compared with a different weekday, and weekday ride counts differ a lot, as your own chart shows. An operator who acts on "+224 rides" may be reading a gap that doesn't exist. Direction: make the comparison period say exactly what it is, and make it the same day of the week, for example by restoring the missing word if it means the same day last week. *Defect* (common sense): it's a number people will act on, and it can be read two ways.

5. **The frosted-glass tooltips are a template move.** The two map callouts are semi-transparent glass cards: the road shows through "Van route 2", and they have soft, blue-tinted, blurred shadows. Viewers have seen this on many products, so it makes the map look generic. Practically, it also lets map lines run behind the text. Direction: make them plain, opaque callouts with a hairline edge, like the cards on the rest of the page, which are already plain. *Template tell* (unslop, Damir Sokolovsky).

**Smaller things.**
- "Rides by weekday" has no period (this week? an average?), no y-axis and no values except Friday's 3,410. Say the period in the title and label the bars, or add a light scale (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2).
- The chart highlights Friday but never says why. If Friday is the finding, a short caption that says so would help. If it isn't the finding, highlight today or nothing. (Taste.)
- The "Van route 2" tooltip sits on the station dot at the bottom edge of the map and covers part of the River Wend. Move it clear of the marks it would hide. (Common sense.)

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with a comparison and a unit ("of 600 in the fleet", "16 min last Monday"), so each number is readable without guessing. Keep that pattern, just fix the period.
- The restock table is set right: numbers right-aligned in tabular figures, light rules, plain column headings. Only its rank on the page is the problem.
- One sans family, a small and clear type scale (page title, KPI figures, card titles, body, small subline) and a restrained gray palette. The page has a clear structure, which leaves room for a real alarm color to stand out once it's added.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the map callouts follow hover or always show, and whether the map updates live. "Stations now" implies it does, and that decides whether 40 minutes empty is current.
