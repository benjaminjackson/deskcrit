**First read.** The eye lands first on the row of four big numbers at the top, then moves to the map, its two white callouts and the single blue dot, then to the blue Friday bar. The table at the bottom comes last. It reads as a calm, tidy operations dashboard for a city bike-share. Nothing on it feels urgent, even though one station has had no bikes for 40 minutes.

**What it's for.** Assumption: it's the home screen for a fleet or operations manager at Wendmouth city bikes. It should tell them how today is going and where to send the rebalancing vans now.

**Verdict.** It looks clean, but it puts the urgent job last. The one thing an operator must act on, an empty station and two nearly empty ones, sits in the lowest panel and the weakest color. The KPI row on top only reports. The headline numbers are also compared against the wrong day.

**What would move it most**

1. **The restock problem has no priority (defect).** The four KPI cards are the largest type on the screen and are all the same weight. "Stations to restock" is a plain table at the bottom, and Canal Basin, empty for 40 minutes, is just its first row in regular text. On the map the empty station is a small dot in the same calm blue used to decorate Friday in the chart. The screen never offers a next step: there is no "send van", "assign" or "restock" control anywhere. So the operator reads four status numbers before finding the work, and nothing marks that work as urgent. Fix: make the restock list the first thing seen, by position, size or both, and set the KPIs back to supporting context (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance by differences of size). Give the most urgent row a clear state and put the action the operator takes on it next to it. Which action that is, the maker knows.

2. **The map can't answer "where do I send the van?" (defect).** Neither callout points at anything. "Canal Basin, 0 bikes" floats at top left, a long way from the blue dot that presumably is Canal Basin. "Van route 2" has no marker at all, so you can't tell where the van is, and the legend doesn't mention vans. The callout also covers a road and touches a station dot at its lower edge. Hartley Park gate (2 bikes) and Market Cross (3 bikes) are in the table but look the same on the map as every healthy gray dot. The map and the table describe the same stations but aren't linked, so the operator has to match them up in their head. Fix: anchor each callout to its point. Give the van its own mark, labeled at the mark. Show low stations on the map with a cue besides color, such as a label or count (WCAG 1.4.1; Tufte on labeling at the data). Tie each table row to its place on the map (Norman, natural mapping).

3. **The KPI comparisons are against the wrong day (defect, copy).** The header says "Today, 24 November 2026". That date is a Tuesday, yet all four cards compare to "last Monday". Either the date or the comparison label is wrong. Even if "last Monday" means yesterday, Tuesday against Monday is not like for like. It's also unclear whether "3,184 rides" is today so far or a full day. If it's so far, "+224 on last Monday" is a false win. These are the numbers a manager repeats upward, so they must read only one way. Fix: state the period of each figure ("so far today") and compare to the same weekday or the same time of day. Restore the missing words, such as "vs" and the time window, so each caption says what is being compared.

4. **The weekday chart can't be read and doesn't say why it's there (convention).** Only Friday (3,410) has a value. There is no scale, so Monday through Sunday are just heights. The title doesn't say which week, or whether these are averages. Friday is highlighted in blue without saying why, and it is the same blue as "Empty" on the map, so the same color means a highlight in one panel and a fault in the other. Fix: label the bars or add a light scale, and state the period (Tufte; Few: the reader should never guess what a number measures). Make the title say the point. Use a different treatment from the map's alert color.

**Smaller things.**
- "Docks" in the restock table and "24 docks" in the callout don't say whether they mean free docks or total docks. That number decides how many bikes the van drops, so say which.
- Nav icons: Rides uses a shopping cart and Settings uses a sun-like glyph. Both misdescribe their section, so pick icons that match what's behind them (taste).

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards are disciplined. Each has one label, one number and one comparison line, in a single family with tabular figures. Once the comparisons are fixed, this pattern is worth keeping.
- The map is quiet: muted ground, the river and park only lightly tinted. Any alert you add will stand out against it.
- The grid is consistent: card edges, gutters and the table's right-aligned numeric columns all line up.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether map points and table rows are clickable and linked, whether the data refreshes live, and keyboard focus on the nav. All three matter for a live operations screen.
