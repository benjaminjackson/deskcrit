**First read.** The eye goes to the headline, "Let members book a desk before they leave home". It then moves right to the floor plan and stops on the one dark teal desk, E4. Within about three seconds I could tell this is booking software for coworking spaces, sold to the people who run them. It feels calm and competent. It's quiet, with a warm palette and no stock tricks. The one place I had to stop and decode was the floor plan.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the home page hero for Deskwell, for coworking operators. It should get them to add a floor or try the sample.

**Verdict.** It does its job, and it's better than most pages like it. The headline, the body text and the button tell one story, about members booking ahead and the paper list going away. The one thing that weakens it is the floor plan, which is meant to be the proof: its colors read backwards and nothing on it explains them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The floor plan's colors say the opposite of what they mean (defect, and convention).** In the plan on the right, 14 desks are filled with soft green and three (D3, E6, F5) are white with a faint dashed edge. The header says "18 desks, 3 free", so green must mean booked and white must mean free. But on almost every booking screen, and by the usual meaning of green (Apple HIG and Material: green means success, available, go), a viewer reads green as "open". A first-time viewer sees a floor that's mostly available. The page meant to show a floor that's mostly taken, with three seats left. Booked, free and the selected desk E4 are told apart only by color, with no legend or label, which breaks WCAG 1.4.1. The selected desk is also the same teal as the "Add your first floor" button, so it looks like part of the button styling rather than "this desk". Fix: make booked desks the quiet neutral and free desks the ones that stand out, then add a small key or a label on the free desks. The plan should read correctly before the viewer gets to "3 free".
2. **The demo has no date, so its numbers don't add up (defect, copy).** The body text promises "the desks booked today". The panel's own caption says E4 is "Booked for Tuesday". The header "Mill Yard, second floor · 18 desks, 3 free" doesn't say for which day. A viewer can't tell if the plan shows today or Tuesday, and the "3 free" figure has nothing to anchor it. This plan is the product's whole argument, so it should be exact. Fix: add the day the plan shows to the header, so the count and the booking line refer to the same day. That's an editorial edit using a fact only you have.

**Smaller things.**
- The dashed outlines of the three free desks look well below 3:1 against the panel (WCAG 1.4.11). They're the only cue apart from fill color, so strengthen the edge or add a label.
- The headline breaks "book a / desk", which leaves the article hanging at the end of line one. Rebreak it so "a desk" stays together, for example by narrowing the text column.
- Near-misses on alignment: the panel's bottom edge (about y 505) sits a few pixels below the bottom of the button (about y 500), and its top sits just above the headline's cap height. Either align them or make the offset clearly deliberate (Arnheim).
- "$25 day pass" in the hero of a page with a Pricing link could be read as Deskwell's price instead of the member's price for that desk. It's minor, but a price is something people hold you to. Consider making clear whose price it is.

**What's working.**
- **The copy speaks to the operator about their own day.** "The front desk stops keeping a paper list" compares the product to what the operator already does, so they can judge the value on their own terms. "Add your first floor" is an exact ask, not "Get started". The secondary "Walk through a sample floor" gives a low-commitment path, styled clearly below the primary button.
- **The hero shows the product, not decoration.** The floor plan is specific: named desks, a kitchen band, one desk with a price and a day. Once the colors are fixed, it does the explaining a stock illustration couldn't.
- **The type is restrained.** About five sizes with clear jobs, headings that differ by weight as well as size, body lines of about 55 characters, and three feature columns with no icons or emoji. Keep this restraint when you revise.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the nav, the button and the text link. How the two-column hero stacks on a phone.
