**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing", then to the teal-bordered Marina card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads straight away as the billing settings page of a marina-management app, for a harbormaster or office manager. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, close to a Stripe-style settings page. Nothing on it stands out as a problem at first glance. The problems only show when you try to act on what it says.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marina's office staff use this to check which plan they're on, change it, keep billing details current, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and conventional, and it mostly works. It falls short on money: the two lines the user commits to (the plan change and the autopay checkbox) don't say what will happen, and the invoices can be seen but not retrieved. Those are fixable, but on a billing page they are the job.

**What would move it most**

1. **It's unclear what "Save changes" commits, and nothing says what a plan change costs or when it applies.** The one Save button sits under Billing details, below a rule that separates it from the plan cards. A user who clicks "Harbor group" can't tell whether Save also moves them from $69 to $149. They also can't tell whether the change applies now or next cycle, whether it's prorated, or what lands on the next invoice. When a control sits far from what it changes, the user has to guess its reach, and here a wrong guess costs money (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping). Help should sit next to the decision it concerns (Nielsen, heuristic 10).
   - Fix: either give the plan its own confirm step, or pull Save out below both sections so it plainly covers both.
   - When the selected plan differs from the current one, show one line by the cards stating the price change and the date it takes effect. The maker supplies the billing rule; don't make the user infer it.
   - **Defect.**

2. **"Charge the card on file *before* the first of each month" can be read two ways, and the card isn't shown.** One reading: the card is charged a few days early, so the charge lands before the 1st. The other: payment is taken in advance for the coming month. The date the money leaves the account is exactly what a small business plans around, and the invoices all say "1 Nov", "1 Oct", which suggests charges actually land on the 1st. Separately, "the card on file" points at a card that appears nowhere on this screen ("Payment terminal" in the sidebar is probably something else). The user has to remember which card it is or go hunting for it (Nielsen, heuristic 6).
   - Fix: say the actual charge date in the line, with the day or how many days before. Restoring the missing detail is an editorial edit; only the maker knows the real rule.
   - Show the card ("Visa ending …") next to the checkbox with a way to change it.
   - **Defect.**

3. **The invoice table can't be used to get an invoice.** "HL-1185" and the other numbers are plain gray-black text: no underline, no link color, no download icon, no row action. People come to this table for two things, a PDF for the bookkeeper and proof of payment, and neither is visibly possible. If the numbers are links, they don't look like links (Nielsen Norman Group, Moran, on flat UI elements drawing less attention); if they aren't, a download is missing.
   - Fix: give each row a visible way to view or download its invoice, either links styled as links or a download action at the end of each row.
   - **Defect.**

4. **Italic emphasis scattered through the interface copy.** "*one* dock", "*no* booking fees", "as shown on *every* invoice", "*before* the first". This is a stock template move (Sokolovsky, unslop). It makes the labels sound like they're nudging the reader, and four italic words on one screen cancel each other out, so none of them gets the emphasis it was meant to have. Worse, the one that matters, "*before*", gets the same treatment as the throwaway "*every*".
   - Fix: remove the italics. In the plan cards, where a difference between plans really matters, let the words carry it; any emphasis that's left should be weight or color, used once.
   - **Template tell.**

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and Currency share a row, but they aren't a related pair. It reads as a two-column form, so the eye zigzags across it. Stacking them, or pairing Currency with the plan, would read more cleanly. This is my taste, not a rule I can cite.
- In the plan cards the price is set in the same size and weight as the description under it. Price is what decides between plans, so a step of weight on "$69.00 a month" would let the three be compared at a glance. Also taste.
- The plan names "Harbor" and "Harbor group" are close to each other and to the product name, Harborlist. A quick reader may take "Harbor group" for a bigger version of "Harbor" rather than the multi-marina plan. Worth a look by the maker. Common sense.

**What's working.**
- **The invoice table.** Amounts are right-aligned with tabular figures and consistent "$69.00" formatting, the rules are light, and there's no decoration. It's the right form for looking up exact values. Keep it that plain when you add the download action.
- **The single teal accent.** It does three jobs: the selected plan, the checked box and the primary button. So the color means "this is the active choice" everywhere it appears. Keep it to those jobs.
- **Clear wayfinding.** The top nav marks Settings in bold, and the sidebar marks "Plan and billing" with a tint. The user always knows where they are.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the whole plan card is clickable or only the small (roughly 12 px) radio dot. If it's only the dot, it falls under WCAG 2.5.8's 24 px minimum. Check in the browser.
- Focus indicators on the fields, the plan cards and the buttons.
- What Save does on success or failure, and whether it confirms a plan change.
