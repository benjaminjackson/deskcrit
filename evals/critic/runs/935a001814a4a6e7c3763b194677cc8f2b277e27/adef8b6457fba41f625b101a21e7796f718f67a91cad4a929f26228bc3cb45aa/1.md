**First read.** The eye lands on the "Listens" title and runs straight along the row of four big numbers. Then it jumps to the solid purple Monday bar on the right, the only block of strong color on the page. This reads as a podcast analytics dashboard: a host checking how November went. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and also a lot like many other SaaS dashboards.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a monthly overview for a podcast's makers, so they can see at a glance whether the show grew and what drove it.

**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The numbers are easy to find, every figure is compared with October, and the layout is clean. What stands in the way is that the two charts don't say what they claim. "Daily downloads" doesn't show daily data, and the chart colors change meaning from one chart to the next.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Daily downloads" is not daily.** The line chart in the middle left has 11 points across 30 days, about one every three days. A viewer who reads the title takes each kink as a real day. The bigger cost is that sampling every three days lands on a different weekday each time, so it hides the weekly rhythm. The weekday chart next to it says that rhythm is the main story: Monday is clearly the biggest day. Plotted daily, the line would show the Monday peaks the right-hand chart reports. **Defect.** Plot all 30 days, or, if the data really is grouped in threes, change the title to match. Also check the x-axis ticks: 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov are uneven steps (9, 9, 11 days). Ticks at even intervals are a convention (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*).
2. **Purple and gray mean one thing in the left chart and another in the right.** In "Daily downloads," purple is November and gray is October, decoded from a legend in the top right. In "Downloads by weekday," purple is Monday and gray is the other days, all of them November. A viewer who has just learned the left chart's key reads the right chart as "Monday is this month, the rest is last month." Then they have to work out that the purple only means "biggest day." Monday is marked by color alone, and the only other cue is its value label. **Defect** (and the convention in WCAG 1.4.1, Use of color). Label the two lines directly at their right ends ("November", "October") instead of using a legend (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). Then give the Monday bar a highlight that can't be mistaken for a series color: a darker neutral, or the same gray with the label doing the work. Purple then means "November" everywhere on the page.
3. **All the text is in tints of one indigo.** The nav, the KPI labels, the date range, the axis labels, the table headers, the episode titles and the footer are all set in near-identical indigos. So color does nothing to separate labels from values, headers from rows, or links from plain text. The episode titles and guest names look like the nav items, so they may or may not be clickable. A viewer has seen this "everything lavender" look on many other dashboards, which makes the page look like anyone's product. **Template tell** (unslop, "Many near-identical tints of one hue for text"). Set the text in three roles from one neutral gray ramp (primary, secondary, muted). Save the indigo for data and for things that really are interactive.

**Smaller things.**
- Weekday chart: it has no value axis, and only Monday's bar carries a number (9,860), so Tue to Sun can't be read or compared exactly. Add a light axis or label every bar, small and gray (Tufte; Few).
- The gray weekday bars and the gray October line look well under 3:1 against the white card (WCAG 1.4.11). Darken the gray until the marks clear 3:1 while still reading as context.
- The chart titles "Daily downloads" and "Downloads by weekday" only name the topic. The page's real news is that November beat October on every measure and that Monday drives downloads, and the viewer has to find that themselves. A title that states the finding would help. This is taste, not a cited rule.
- "1 to 30 November 2026" at top right looks like plain text. If this is where the period gets changed, it needs to look like a control (a button shape, a border or a chevron). If it isn't a control, there's no visible way to look at another month. The same applies to "The Long Table podcast" in the bottom-left corner, if the account has more than one show (Nielsen, heuristic 7).
- Copy: "Average listen 31 min" doesn't say per what (per download? per listener?), and "Finished an episode 62%" doesn't say 62% of what. Add the missing word so these are the figures the host remembers correctly.

**What's working.**
- Every headline number has a comparison under it ("44,310 in October"), so the viewer can judge each figure instead of taking it on trust. Keep that.
- The two right-hand table columns are right-aligned in tabular figures. That makes Downloads and Finished easy to scan and compare down the list.
- The layout sits on one clear grid with even gutters and consistent card padding. The sidebar marks the current page with a quiet tint. Nothing fights for space.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the episode titles and the date range are clickable, and what hover and focus states look like.
