**First read.** The eye goes to the big black headline, crosses to the stock table on the right, and stops on the red "Baby formula" row. Then it drops to the green button. It reads as a landing page for a stock-tracking tool for food banks, written for the pantry coordinator. It feels calm, practical and trustworthy, with no hype. That is the right tone for a charity audience.

**What it's for.** My assumption: to get food-bank coordinators to sign up ("Set up your pantry") by showing that the tool tells them what will run short before the delivery comes.

**Verdict.** This is a strong, honest page, and it mostly does its job. The headline is specific and the table is real proof, not decoration. The biggest weakness is that the table doesn't yet prove the headline: the item that's running out sits in the middle of an unsorted list, and only its color marks it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The table shows stock, but it doesn't show the alert the headline promises.** The rows on the right run 3 weeks, 2 weeks, 4 days, 2 weeks, 9 days, so the one urgent item is third of five. The only thing that singles it out is red text. A viewer who scans the table sees an inventory list and has to work out for themselves that this is the "what will run short" view. A color-blind viewer gets no signal at all beyond reading every "Lasts" cell.
   - **Defect.** Few and Wilke both say rows should be sorted by value unless the reader is looking up named items. WCAG 1.4.1 says color can't be the only cue.
   - **Fix:** sort the rows by "Lasts" so the short items come first, and give the flagged row a second cue (a word or an icon) besides red.
   - **Taste:** a one-line caption over the table that states the finding, rather than only the pantry name, would make the table say what the paragraph says. Use only what the table already shows, such as how many items run out before the next delivery.
2. **The feature cards have uneven copy, and the middle one has a gap a stranger can't fill.** "Counts from a phone" and "Dates checked for you" are clear. "Orders sized to the gap" has no clear subject: the gap between what and what? Its body line ("The weekly order lists only what will run out before Friday") is clearer than its title.
   - **Defect (copy).** A coordinator skimming the bold titles gets two of the three ideas.
   - **Fix:** rebuild the title from its own body text, so it says what the order does (for example "Weekly orders list only what runs out"), and leave the wording to you.
3. **The ask has no comparison with what the coordinator does today.** "Set up your pantry" is a concrete next step, and "See a sample stock list" lets the viewer judge the product for themselves. Both are good. But nothing sets Pantrylog against what the coordinator uses now, whether that's a paper sheet, a spreadsheet or a walk round the shelves. So the value rests on the viewer taking the claim on trust.
   - **Taste.** One line that names their current method and what this saves them would make the case on terms they already accept. Only you know what that method is, so supply it yourself.

**Smaller things.**
- Short last lines: "arrives" sits alone on the third line of the headline, and "week." sits alone at the end of the paragraph (Bringhurst, 2.4.2). Adjust the measure or the line breaks so the last lines carry more than one word.
- Grid: the table's left edge (about 652 px) lines up with none of the three card columns below it (461, 819, 843 px), so the hero and the card row look placed separately. Line the table up with a card column, or make the hero columns follow the same grid.

**What's working.**
- **The headline is written in the reader's world.** "Before the van arrives" is a moment every pantry coordinator knows. Keep this voice; it's what makes the page feel unlike generic SaaS.
- **The product appears as a real table with believable data.** Harbour Road, Tuesday 10:40, "Pasta, 500 g" and right-aligned counts are not a stock hero image, and they show the product doing its job at first glance.
- **There's one clear primary action.** The solid green button is the only strong color apart from the red alert. The secondary link is underlined and lighter. The type scale is tight, with a big headline, 18 px body text and small labels, and the weights are restrained.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- The empty band below the cards may be the end of the screenshot or the start of the next section. Check it in a live browser.
