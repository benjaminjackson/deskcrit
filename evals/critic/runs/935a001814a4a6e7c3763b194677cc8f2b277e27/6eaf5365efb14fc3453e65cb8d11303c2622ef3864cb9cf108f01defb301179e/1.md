**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing", then straight to the teal "Save changes" button, the only strong color on the page, and then across the three plan cards, where the selected "Garden" card is set apart only by a thin teal outline and a filled radio. It reads as the billing settings for a community-garden membership tool, used by a committee volunteer or treasurer. It feels calm, plain, and trustworthy, a well-made form.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a garden committee member checks or changes the plan, keeps the billing contact up to date, and finds past invoices, often to send to a treasurer.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid and the page reads cleanly. Where it falls short is the part that costs money. It isn't clear what "Save changes" and "Cancel" apply to, the plan cards can't be compared on the same terms, and the invoice list shows 3 of 12 invoices with no way to open or get any of them. The single biggest problem is the unclear scope and wording of the actions on a page where clicking can change a charge.

**What would move it most**

1. **The Save/Cancel pair is ambiguous where money is involved (defect).** The buttons sit under "Billing details", below a rule that cuts them off from the plan cards. A user who clicks "Network" can't tell whether "Save changes" also switches the plan, or whether clicking the card already did it. Nothing on the screen says what a switch costs today, whether it is prorated, or when it starts. "Cancel" on a billing page has two readings: "throw away my edits" and "cancel my subscription". A committee member who means the first may hesitate, and one who expects the second may think they canceled when they didn't. Controls should sit next to what they change (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). Fix: either give the plan change its own confirm step, with the charge and its start date shown at that point, or put Save/Cancel visibly in charge of both sections. Rename "Cancel" to say that it throws away edits ("Discard changes"), so it can't be read as ending the subscription.

2. **The plan cards describe each plan along a different axis (defect).** Allotment says "One site, up to 40 plots". Garden says "Up to 150 plots and a waiting list" but says nothing about sites. Network says "Up to 10 sites, one member list" but says nothing about plots or a waiting list. The user can't tell whether Allotment has a waiting list. This garden has 12 people on its waiting list (sidebar), so a downgrade might quietly drop them, and the card doesn't say. The price, the thing the user commits to, is set in the same gray regular weight as the description. Fix: describe all three plans on the same terms (sites, plots, waiting list), say what is lost on a downgrade where it applies, and give the price more weight than the description. The maker has to supply the facts; this is not about adding marketing copy.

3. **The invoices section counts 12 invoices, shows 3, and gives no way to reach them (defect).** The "12" next to "Invoices" promises more than the table shows. There is no "view all" and no pagination. The invoice numbers (WF-0212 and the rest) look like plain text, with no link, download, or PDF icon. Getting an invoice is most likely the reason a treasurer or committee member opens this page, so it should be in front of them (Nielsen, heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall). Fix: make each invoice openable or downloadable with a visible cue, and add a way to reach the other 9.

**Smaller things.**
- "Send the treasurer a copy of each invoice": the screen doesn't say who the treasurer is or what their address is, so the user has to remember it from another screen (Nielsen 6). Show the treasurer's name or email in the line, or link to where it is set.
- The space between sections is about the same as the space inside them. For example, there is about 14 px from the plan cards to the rule, and "Billing details" sits about as far from that rule as from its first label. The hairline rules are doing all the grouping (Arnheim, proximity). Open up the gaps between sections so that the rules become optional.

**What's working.**
- The color discipline: teal appears only on the primary button, the selected card, the radio, and the checkbox. Because of that, the eye finds the one action and the current choice at once. Keep the accent that scarce.
- The invoice table is set correctly: amounts are right-aligned with consistent decimals, headers are quiet, rows are separated by light rules, and plain "Paid" text needs no colored badge.
- Location is clear: the sidebar shows "Plan and billing" on a tint, and the page title repeats it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether selecting a plan card applies it right away or waits for Save. Check this in the live product, because it decides how serious finding 1 is.
- Focus states on the cards, fields, and buttons.
