**First read.** The eye lands on "Quote a bathroom from the doorway", jumps to the itemized quote on the right, then drops to the brown "Write your first quote" button. It reads at once as quoting software for tilers who work for themselves. It feels calm, warm, and credible, like a tradesperson's tool rather than a startup pitch.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the landing page for a quoting app, meant to get independent tilers to start a first quote.

**Verdict.** This is a strong page. The hierarchy is clear, the copy is specific, and the sample quote proves the claim instead of decorating it. The one real problem is that the picture contradicts the headline: the copy says you quote on site, but the picture shows desktop software.

**What would move it most**
1. **The quote mockup is dressed as a desktop window, but the promise is "from the doorway."** This is about the card on the right, and the three gray traffic-light dots in its header are what turn it into a desktop window. The headline, the body copy ("measure on site", "before you leave the house") and the third feature ("reads the quote on a phone") all say this happens on site, on a phone. The only picture on the page says "app on a laptop." A tiler standing in someone's bathroom is the exact person you want, and they get a mixed message about whether this works where they are. The dots do no other job: the card has no frame, so they float on the page ground as decoration. Fix: show the quote the way the copy describes it, as it looks on the phone in the tiler's hand or as the customer receives it. At minimum, drop the window dots so the card reads as a document rather than a desktop screen. Kind: defect (the picture and the headline make two different claims). Matching the picture to the headline is common sense; no approved source states it.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Quote a bathroom from / the doorway", which splits "from the doorway" across the lines. Breaking after "bathroom" keeps the phrase whole and puts the hook on its own line (Bringhurst on line breaks; the heading case is my judgment).
- The quote card's left edge (about x 652) lines up with nothing in the three-column row below (columns at about 80, 469, and 858). The hero and the features use two unrelated grids. Put the card on the second column line, or set the hero split from the same grid (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).

**What's working.**
- **The quote is real proof, not filler.** It has real-looking lines with units (6.2 m², 9 bags, 3 days), prices that add up to the total, right-aligned tabular figures, and "Sent 14:12, opened 14:30", which quietly shows a customer opening the quote 18 minutes later. Keep this level of specificity whatever frame it ends up in.
- **The copy speaks in the tiler's own world.** "So you do not run short on day two" and "You get a text when they do" both end on what the tiler gets. "Send it before you leave the house" sets the product against the evening paperwork tilers already do, so they can judge the value on their own terms. "Write your first quote" names one exact action.
- **The restraint.** One warm neutral ground, one brown accent used only on the primary button, one typeface, and about four clear type levels. Nothing on the page is a template move.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The gray small text (the "Line / Amount / Price" headers, "Sent 14:12, opened 14:30", the quote title) looks readable, around 5:1. Confirm it reaches 4.5:1 against the cream ground in the browser.
- Focus states on the button and the link, and how the two-column hero stacks on a phone, which matters for a page whose audience is on phones.
