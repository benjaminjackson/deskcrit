**First read.** My eye goes to "Plan and billing" first, then to the outlined Salon card in the middle of the plan row, then down the form to the green Save changes button. It reads as the billing settings page of a booking tool for pet-grooming businesses. It's calm, tidy and trustworthy, and quiet in a good way. The first question I had was whether Salon is the plan I'm on or just the one that's selected, and nothing on the page answers it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a salon owner or front-desk person comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The visual craft is solid, but the page doesn't do the billing job safely. It mixes a plan change, which costs money, with editing an address under one generic "Save changes" button. It never says which plan you're on now, and it leaves out what people open billing pages to find: what card is charged, when the next charge is, and how to get an invoice.

**What would move it most**
1. **You can't tell whether Save changes also switches your plan, or when the new price starts.** The plan cards sit above a rule, in their own section. Save changes and Cancel sit lower down, right under the invoice-email checkbox, so they look like they belong to Billing details only. Someone who clicks Salon group may leave thinking they upgraded when nothing was saved. Or they may press Save to fix a typo in the address and also commit to $95.00 a month without meaning to. This is the price line, the one people later hold you to, so the vagueness is a **defect**. The fix: either give the plan change its own action next to the cards, named for what it does (moving to the selected plan and what it costs), or make Save's label and placement plainly cover both sections. The control should sit by the thing it changes (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). A plan change also needs one short line near the cards saying when the new price takes effect and how the current month is handled. That fact only you have, so it's yours to write (Nielsen, heuristic 10).
2. **Nothing marks the current plan.** Salon has a teal border and a filled radio button. That's how a choice looks, not how "this is your plan" looks. Once someone clicks Mobile van, the page no longer shows what they're paying for. The only clue left is the newest invoice row at the bottom. **Defect** (Nielsen, heuristics 1 and 6: show the state, don't make people recall it). Fix: put a fixed "Current plan" label on the current card that stays put when the selection moves.
3. **The things people come to a billing page for are missing.** The page shows no payment method, no next charge date or amount, and no way to open or download an invoice. The invoice numbers (CL-3307 and the others) are plain text, not links. A bookkeeper asking for last month's invoice gets a dead end. **Defect**, argued as common sense; the source behind this check is off my approved list. Fix: add the payment method and the next charge to the Plan section, and make each invoice row open or download its invoice. If invoices are already reachable somewhere else, at least point there from this table.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and Currency share a row, but they have nothing to do with each other. Put Currency on its own line, or next to the plan prices it affects. This is taste: the usual source for one-column forms isn't on my list.
- No field is marked required or optional (Billing email, Invoice address). Add an asterisk with a one-line legend, or "optional" after the optional labels (Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group).
- "Up to 3 salons, one pet list" leaves the reader to work out what "one pet list" means. Spell out what it does for them, such as the pet and owner records being shared across salons, using your own wording (Nielsen, heuristic 2).

**What's working.**
- The restraint. One teal for the selected state, the primary button and the checkbox, with everything else in grays. The eye only ever has one colored thing to look at, so the Save button clearly reads as the main action. Keep that discipline when you add a "Current plan" label: make it a neutral tag, not a second accent color.
- Three plan cards in a row with the same internal order (name, limits, price) make comparing them effortless.
- The invoice table is right for its data. Amounts are right-aligned with figures that line up, the rules are light, and Paid has no badge chrome.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What Save actually commits, and whether a plan change asks for confirmation. Check this in the live flow.
- Focus indicators on the radio cards and fields.
