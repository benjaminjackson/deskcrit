**First read.** My eye lands on the "Plan and billing" title, drops to the teal-outlined "Full gym" card, then goes to the teal "Save changes" button. The invoice table reads as a quiet footer. It looks like a calm, well-ordered settings page in an admin tool for a climbing gym, with nothing shouting.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this: it's the account settings screen where a gym owner checks their current plan, changes it, keeps billing details up to date, and finds past invoices.

**Verdict.** The page is clean and mostly solid, but the plan picker fails where it matters most, because it involves money. It doesn't say which plan you're on now, which button commits a change, or what a change will cost and when. A second gap is that there's no way to get an invoice out of the invoice list.

**What would move it most**

1. **The plan picker doesn't say what you have, what you're changing, or how to commit it. (Defect.)** The three plan cards sit in their own section under "Plan". "Save changes" and "Cancel" sit lower down, inside "Billing details", right after the invoice-email checkbox.
   - **Current plan isn't marked.** "Full gym" is selected, but nothing says that's the current plan. Once the owner clicks "Gym group", the screen no longer shows what they're paying today.
   - **The commit step can be read two ways.** In one reading, clicking a card switches the plan and starts the $159.00 charge right away. In the other, nothing happens until Save. An owner who reads it the first way may never touch Save and believe they upgraded. One who reads it the second way may change the email, hit Save, and not realize a stray click on a card went through too.
   - **Why it happens.** The button is placed with the billing fields, not with the plan it may change (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, natural mapping). The page also doesn't show the current state (Nielsen, heuristic 1).
   - **What a change costs isn't stated.** No line near the cards says when a change takes effect or how the switch is billed.
   - **Fix direction.** Label the current plan in words on its card, not only with the radio. Then either give the plan change its own confirm step next to the cards, or make it plain that the one Save covers both sections. Add one short line by the cards on timing and charges, using facts only you have.

2. **The invoice list is a dead end. (Common sense.)** Under "Invoices", the numbers BS-2093, BS-2047, and BS-1998 are plain gray-black text. There's no link, download icon, or action column. The usual reason anyone opens an invoice list is to get a copy for their records. As drawn, the owner can see that an invoice exists but can't do anything with it. Fix direction: make each row, or its invoice number, open or download the invoice, and style it so it clearly looks clickable.

3. **Everything is a full pill. (Template tell, and my taste.)**
   - **Where.** The plan cards, both text fields, the currency select, both buttons, and the active item in the left nav all have fully rounded ends.
   - **Why the cards suffer most.** On cards about 85 px tall, the curve eats into the corners. The radio and text end up crowded against a bulging edge, and the cards read like oversized buttons rather than panels to compare.
   - **What it costs.** Viewers have seen this all-pill look on many products, so Basalt looks like anyone's admin screen.
   - **Fix direction.** Set the corner radius by element size. Cards and fields get a small radius, and full pills, if you keep them at all, go only on small elements like buttons or the nav highlight.

**Smaller things.**
- The borders on the unselected plan cards and on the text fields and select look pale against white, probably below 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure them and darken them to at least 3:1.
- Inside each plan card, the price has the same size and color as the description ("$79.00 a month" vs "Unlimited members"). The price is the thing being compared, so give it more weight than the description (taste).
- None of the billing fields is marked required or optional. Mark the optional ones, or put an asterisk on the required ones with a one-line legend (Budiu, NN/g).
- The currency select keeps the browser's default chevron inside a custom pill shape, so it looks out of step with the other fields. Use one control style.

**What's working.**
- **Location is always clear.** "Settings" is darker in the top nav, "Plan and billing" has a tint in the side nav, and the page title repeats it. Keep all three cues.
- **The invoice table is set correctly.** Amounts are right-aligned with matching decimals, there are no heavy gridlines, and statuses are short words, not colored chips. It reads at a glance.
- **The form basics are right.** Every label sits above its own field, and the labels use the owner's own words ("Staff and route setters", "Email a copy of each invoice to the billing email"). The type scale is a clean four sizes: title, section heading, label, and body.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**

- The focus states on the cards and fields.
