**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing", then to the teal-bordered Garden card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads as the settings page of a small SaaS tool for running community gardens, made for a volunteer committee member or treasurer. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, and nothing on it shouts.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the screen where a garden's organizer checks or changes the subscription plan, keeps the billing contact and address current, and finds past invoices.

**Verdict.** It's a competent, well-ordered settings page that mostly works. The biggest problem is the one thing that costs money: you can't tell what "Save changes" commits to, or whether picking a different plan card changes your plan.

**What would move it most**
1. **The scope of "Save changes" and "Cancel" is unclear, and these buttons commit money.** The two buttons sit under Billing details, below the gray rule that closes off the Plan cards. A viewer who clicks Network can't tell whether "Save changes" will move them to a $60.00-a-month plan, or whether the plan needs its own step. Nothing says when a switch takes effect or how the first charge is worked out. On a billing page, "Cancel" also reads two ways: "discard my edits" or "cancel my subscription". A nervous treasurer may avoid it, or click it expecting the wrong outcome. **Defect** (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping: a control sits next to what it changes; Nielsen heuristic 10, on short help placed at the step where it's needed). Direction: make it clear what each action commits to. Either give the plan change its own action next to the cards, or put the buttons below everything they save. Put a short note by the plan cards saying when a change starts and how billing is handled; the maker has to supply those facts. Rename "Cancel" so it can only mean discarding edits.
2. **The current plan isn't labeled.** The Garden card is shown only as "selected", with a radio and a teal border. That looks the same as a choice the user just made and hasn't saved. As soon as someone clicks Allotment or Network, the page no longer shows what they're paying now, except indirectly in the invoice table. **Defect** (Nielsen heuristic 1, visibility of system status; heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall). Direction: mark the current plan with words, in a way that doesn't move when the radio selection moves.
3. **The Invoices section shows 3 of 12 and gives no way to get any of them.** The count badge says 12, but only three rows appear, with no "view all". The invoice numbers (WF-0212 and the others) are plain gray text, with no link or download. A treasurer usually comes to this table to get a PDF for the books, and they leave without it. **Defect**; this is common sense, since no approved source covers it. Direction: make each invoice open or download, and give a visible way to reach the other nine.

**Smaller things.**
- Emoji inside the count badges (👥 38, 🌱 12, 🔔 3, 🧾 12 in the sidebar and next to Invoices) is a stock template move. It makes the page look like anyone's tool and adds nothing to the number. Show the plain number, or use an icon from one consistent set (template tell; Sokolovsky, unslop).
- The input borders and especially the unselected plan-card borders look very pale gray on white, likely under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks for on control edges. Measure them and darken any that fall short.

**What's working.**
- The page has one clear structure: a left column of settings, then a single content column with full-width rules marking off Plan, Billing details and Invoices. Field labels sit above their fields, close to them. Keep that.
- Each plan card says what you get and what it costs, in the user's own words ("Up to 150 plots and a waiting list", "$22.00 a month"). Prices and the invoice amounts are right-aligned with matching decimals, so the numbers are easy to compare.
- Color is held back. There's one teal, used for the selected plan, the checkbox and the single primary button, so the eye's path is set by color rather than by decoration.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus styles on the plan cards, fields and buttons.
- Whether choosing a different card shows a price change or confirmation before anything is charged.
