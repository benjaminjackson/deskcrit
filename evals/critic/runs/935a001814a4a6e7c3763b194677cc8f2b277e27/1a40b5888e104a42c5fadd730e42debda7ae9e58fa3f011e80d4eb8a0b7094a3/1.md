**First read.** The eye goes first to the "Listens" title and the row of four big numbers, then jumps to the one purple bar (Monday, 9,860). From there it goes to the purple November line, and the table comes last. It reads as a monthly analytics page for a podcast's host or producer. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, but it reads more like a report than a briefing: it gives you every number and tells you nothing about them.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the monthly overview for the person running "The Long Table" podcast. They want to know at a glance whether November went well and why.

**Verdict.** It is a clean, competent dashboard that is honest with its data. But it leaves the viewer to do the analysis. Every number sits next to its October value with no change shown, and no chart says what it found. The single biggest problem is that the page never answers "was this a good month?"

**What would move it most**

1. **The stat cards make you do the subtraction.** In the top row, each card puts this month's number over October's raw number ("48,920" over "44,310 in October"). To learn that downloads grew about 10%, followers about 6% and the finish rate 4 points, you do four sums in your head. All four went up, which is the month's main story, and the page doesn't show it. Few's dashboard rule asks for the change from the last period next to each headline number, not just the earlier value (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). This also explains why nothing stands out: the four numbers have equal weight and nothing tells the eye which one matters. Show the change itself (+4,610, +10%) as the second line, with its direction marked by a sign or arrow as well as by color. *Defect.*

2. **Purple means two different things.** In "Daily downloads", purple means *November* and gray means *October*. Twenty pixels to the right, in "Downloads by weekday", purple means *Monday* and gray means *the other days*. Anyone who has just read the legend will read the weekday chart as Monday-in-November against the rest-in-October, which is wrong. One color should keep one job across the screen (Apple HIG, "Color"). Either give the weekday highlight a different treatment, or use purple there only if it still means November. *Defect.*

3. **The charts are labeled by topic, not by what they found.** The titles "Daily downloads" and "Downloads by weekday" name the topic and leave the viewer to find the point. The weekday chart has the bigger gap: Monday is highlighted with no reason given (biggest day? release day?), it has no value axis, it doesn't name its period, and only Monday has a number. Sunday is clearly second, but you can't say by how much. Have each title or a short caption say what the chart found, in words the piece can back up, such as which day leads and why. Give the weekday bars either a value axis or a value on every bar. This is my judgment (the "takeaway title" rule has no approved source here). The missing axis and period breaks Tufte's and Few's rule on labeling. *Defect.*

**Smaller things**
- **"Finished an episode 62%"**, top right card: read cold, it doesn't say 62% of what (listeners? downloads?). Since the table's "Finished" column means something per episode, add the missing word: a percentage of whom.
- **Line chart legend**, top right of "Daily downloads": label the two lines at their right ends ("November", "October") instead, so you don't have to look back and forth to match colors (Tufte; Wilke).
- **Type scale:** the "Listens" page title and the four KPI numbers look about the same size (around 24 px), so they compete instead of reading as two levels. Make one clearly larger, or set the title back.
- **Grid:** the top row splits into quarters, the charts split about two-thirds/one-third, and the table columns follow neither. No column line runs down the page. A shared column set would make the page feel quieter still (Müller-Brockmann).

**What's working**
- **Restraint in the charts.** October is shown in gray, there's no chartjunk, gridlines are faint, and the line chart's axis starts at zero. Keep gray as the color for context.
- **The table is set correctly.** Numbers are right-aligned with figures that line up in columns, the rules are light, and the "Finished" column turns a list of downloads into a quality signal.
- **Every number has a period.** The date range at the top right and the October comparison under each card mean no figure is left without context. Item 1 only asks you to show the change instead of leaving it as arithmetic.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether "1 to 30 November 2026" is a period picker or a fixed label. If it's a control, it currently looks like plain text.
- Exact contrast of the gray secondary text and axis labels. It looks acceptable, but measure it against 4.5:1.
- Whether "Daily downloads" really plots daily values. Each line has only about 11 vertices across 30 days, so check whether the data is sampled or smoothed.
