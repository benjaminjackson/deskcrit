I've checked everything and am writing up the critique now.

**First read.** My eye goes to "Plan and billing" first, then to the teal-bordered "Small fleet" card, then down to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads right away as the billing settings for a SaaS tool that food trucks use to take orders. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy: plain type, one green accent, nothing shouting.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a truck owner or manager comes here to check or change their plan, update billing details and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** It's well-made and easy to scan, but it doesn't handle the one thing on this page that costs money. A plan change gets committed by a generic "Save changes" button that sits under the billing fields. Nothing says which plan is current or what switching will charge, or when. The page also leaves out the payment facts people most often come here for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan change has no clear commit or consequence.** "Save changes" sits under the Billing details fields, with a horizontal rule between it and the Plan cards. By position it belongs to the email and address fields, not to the plan (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping: a control sits near the thing it changes).
   - Say a user clicks "Commissary." The only sign of what they're on now goes away, because the radio and teal border mark the selection, not the current plan. They then hit a button that doesn't say it will move them from $59.00 to $129.00 a month.
   - They can't tell whether the change is immediate, prorated or from the next cycle. They have to find out after they've committed (Nielsen, heuristic 1, visibility of system status).
   - **Defect.** A charge the viewer will later hold you to is ambiguous.
   - **Fix:** mark the current plan as current, apart from the selection. Give the plan change its own commit, next to the cards, that names the change and when it takes effect. Leave "Save changes" to the billing fields. The effective date and proration rule are facts only you have.
2. **The facts people come to a billing page for are missing.** There's no payment method on file and no next charge date or amount. To answer "when will I be charged, and on what card?" the user has to work it out from the invoice dates. **Defect** (common sense, not a cited rule): this is the most likely question on this screen. **Fix:** put a short summary of the current plan, next charge and payment method at the top of the section, above the cards.
3. **The plan cards don't describe the plans on the same terms.**
   - "One menu, 300 orders a day" gives a menu count and an order limit.
   - "Up to 5 trucks, shared menu" gives a truck count and no order limit.
   - "Up to 20 trucks and one kitchen" gives a truck count and a kitchen.
   
   Someone weighing an upgrade can't tell whether Small fleet still caps orders, or how many trucks One truck allows. They have to guess at the one choice on the page that costs money. **Defect.** **Fix:** give each card the same attributes in the same order: trucks, menus, order limit and kitchen, with the real values for each.

**Smaller things.**
- Billing email and Invoice address have no required or optional marker. Mark the required ones with an asterisk and explain it once (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- In the Invoices table, nothing shows how to open or download an invoice. TR-4471 and the others are plain text. If they're links, give them a link cue. If they aren't, add a download action, since a receipt is a common reason to come here (Norman, signifiers).
- The input borders and the unselected card borders look pale, maybe under 3:1 against white. Measure them against WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 minimum for component edges.

**What's working.**
- **Restraint with color.** Teal appears only on the selected plan, the checkbox, the radio and the one primary button, so color marks what's chosen and what commits.
- **The Invoices table is set properly.** Amounts are right-aligned with matching figures, the rules are light, and it has a plain Status column. It reads at a glance with no decoration.
- **Clear location and grouping.** The gray fill on "Plan and billing" in the sidebar and the bolder "Settings" in the top bar show where you are. Thin rules split the page into three named sections, each heading sitting close to what it introduces.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What happens after you pick a different plan: whether a confirmation, a price preview or a proration note appears.
- Focus rings on the plan cards and fields.
- Whether the invoice numbers are already links on hover.
