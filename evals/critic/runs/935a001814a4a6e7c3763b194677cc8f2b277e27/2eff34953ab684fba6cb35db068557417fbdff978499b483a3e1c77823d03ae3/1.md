**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan and billing," then goes to the teal-outlined Co-op card, then to the teal "Save changes" button. It reads right away as the settings page of a small SaaS tool for a beekeeping co-op, set up to choose a plan, edit billing details and look at past invoices. It feels calm, plain and competent. Nothing fights for attention.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this lets a co-op's treasurer or admin check and change their subscription plan, keep billing details current, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** The layout and type do their job, and this is a solid, quiet page. The real problem is that the one action costing money is ambiguous. Nothing marks which plan you're on now. Nothing says whether "Save changes" also commits a plan switch, or when a new plan and its price take effect.

**What would move it most**
1. **You can't tell what changing the plan does, or when.** *Defect.* The three plan cards at the top are radio buttons. "Save changes" sits under Billing details, two sections down and past a divider, so it seems to belong only to that form. Several things are left unclear:
   - Does clicking "Packer" switch the plan to $84.00 a month at once?
   - Does it wait for "Save changes"?
   - Would "Cancel" undo it?

   The page reads two ways, and the wrong one costs money. One reading is that picking a card changes your plan now, so the user may upgrade by accident. The other is that it does nothing until saved, so the user may leave thinking they changed plans when they didn't. On top of that:
   - Nothing labels Co-op as your *current* plan. The selected radio could just as well be a pending choice. Only the invoice table below hints at it.
   - Nothing says when a change starts or how it's billed (this cycle or next, prorated or not). The invoices show this co-op has upgraded once before, so this is a real decision for them, not a rare edge case.

   Norman's natural mapping says a control should sit next to what it changes. Nielsen's visibility of system status says the current state should show. Nielsen 10 says to put short help where the task is complex. Direction of the fix:
   - Label the current plan in words on its card.
   - Give the plan section its own explicit commit, kept apart from the billing form. Or say plainly that "Save changes" applies to both.
   - Add one line near the cards saying when a plan change takes effect and how the first charge works. That fact has to come from you.

**Smaller things.**
- **Packer card:** "Unlimited members and batch labels" wraps, which leaves "labels" alone on its second line (Bringhurst 2.4.2). It also pushes "$84.00 a month" about 6 px below the other two prices, so the three prices nearly line up but don't (Arnheim on near-miss placement). Shorten the line or reserve two lines of description in every card so the prices line up.
- **Plan card copy:** the cards measure different things. Backyard is "10 hives, one seller," Co-op is "25 members," and Packer is "unlimited members." So the viewer can't compare like with like, for example how many hives Co-op allows. Describe each plan on the same terms. Which terms is your call. (This is a copy point; the checklist has no line for it.)
- **Invoices table:** "PB-0912" and the other invoice numbers look like plain text, with no way to view or download the invoice. A treasurer usually comes here to get that document. Give each row a visible view or download action. (Common sense; the Tognazzini source is off the approved list.)
- **Tax number checkbox:** "Add the co-op tax number to every invoice" refers to a number that appears nowhere on this screen, so the user has to remember it, or go check it under Co-op details (Nielsen 6). Show the number in the label, or link to where it's set.
- **Field borders:** the borders on the email, currency and address fields look light gray on white. With a white fill, the border is the only thing marking each field. Measure it against 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11) and darken it if it falls short.
- **Required fields:** none of the three fields says whether it's required or optional. The impact is low on a page where every field is prefilled, but the next empty field will raise the question. Mark them as Budiu (NN/g) recommends: an asterisk with a one-line legend, or "(optional)" after the optional labels.

**What's working.**
- **Navigation:** "Settings" in the top bar is bold and "Plan and billing" in the sidebar has a gray background, so you always know where you are with no extra chrome. Keep both.
- **Restraint with color:** teal appears only on the selected plan, the checkbox and the primary button. So the one colored button really is the primary action, and "Cancel" clearly comes second.
- **Invoices table:** the amounts are right-aligned in tabular figures, dates and amounts each use one format, and the column rules are light. It reads as a clean lookup table with no clutter.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- What happens when you click a plan card (instant change, confirmation dialog, or pending until saved). This is the point of finding 1, so check it in the live product.
- Focus indicators on the cards, fields and buttons.
