**First read.** The eye lands on the row of four big numbers, then jumps to the one solid blue block on the page, the Saturday bar, and then to the rising blue line. It reads as a monthly bookings report for the owner of a small pottery studio. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, and quite plain: a standard light dashboard layout.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the owner's monthly check on how a pottery studio's classes are selling, meant for spotting what to change. The page doesn't say, so correct me if that's wrong.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, well-behaved dashboard, but it reports without telling the owner anything. On top of that, the main chart doesn't seem to match the headline number, and that does more damage than any layout problem. Fix the numbers first, then make each panel say what it found.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "Daily bookings" chart doesn't seem to match "Seats booked 386."** By eye, the November line plots about 11 points, not 30, so it isn't daily. Those points run from about 13 to 23, which works out to roughly 540 a month if they really were daily. The weekday bars, read by eye, add up to about 370, which is close to 386. An owner who checks one against the other will stop trusting the whole page. The chart also never says whether it counts bookings or seats, and one booking can hold several seats. This is a **defect** (Tufte: label what a number measures). Fix: make the title, the unit and the sampling match what's plotted ("seats" or "bookings", and daily or every N days), and make the totals reconcile. If they're different measures on purpose, say so on the chart.
2. **No panel states what it found.** "Daily bookings", "Seats booked by weekday" and "Fullest classes" name topics. Each chart holds a finding the owner would act on: November is ahead of October all month, Saturday sells about three times what Tuesday does, and one beginners' class brings in more than the next two put together. Right now the owner has to work these out each month. This is **taste**, argued from the job: a monthly review exists to show what changed and where to act. Fix: let the titles or a short note on each chart state the takeaway, using only figures already on the page.
3. **"Fullest classes" doesn't show how full anything is.** The table ranks by seats sold (96, 72, 54), but "fullest" suggests fill rate, and "Seats filled 88%" up top sets that meaning up. A 96-seat class run across many sessions may be less full than a 54-seat one. The owner could cut or add sessions based on the wrong reading. This is a **defect**, because it's a number the owner acts on and it can be read two ways. Fix: either rename the table to what it ranks ("Top classes by seats booked") or add a fill-rate column and sort by that. The 88% headline also needs a definition, such as seats booked out of seats offered.
4. **The weekday chart shows a value for only one bar.** Only Saturday says "92". The other six bars have no scale and no values, so you can't tell whether Tuesday is 25 or 35. That matters if the question is whether a Tuesday class is worth running. This is a **convention** (Tufte; Few: a readable scale or direct labels). Fix: label every bar with its value, small and in gray, and keep the blue highlight on Saturday.

**Smaller things.**
- The page title "Bookings" and the KPI numbers look like nearly the same size, about 24 px each. Make one clearly bigger, or set the title in a heavier weight so it reads as a different level (Bringhurst: sizes come from a scale).
- The x-axis ticks on the line chart (1, 10, 19, 30 Nov) have uneven steps of 9, 9 and 11 days. Use even steps, such as weekly (Tufte).
- The November/October legend sits apart from the lines. Label each line at its right end and drop the legend (Tufte; Wilke).
- The same blue means "November" in the line chart and "highlighted day" in the bar chart, which suggests a link that isn't there (Arnheim on similarity). Give the highlight its own treatment, or make the link real.
- The split between the two charts, near x 900, lines up with none of the KPI column dividers above it. Put the chart split on one of those lines so the page reads as one grid (Müller-Brockmann).
- "1 to 30 November 2026" looks like plain text, so there's no visible way to change the period, and that's the obvious next thing a monthly review needs. If it's a picker, make it look like one (Nielsen 7).

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with last month's figure underneath ("352 in October"). That's exactly the comparison an owner reaches for. Keep it, and keep it quiet and secondary.
- Color is used with restraint. The page is gray apart from one blue, which goes on the thing that matters in each chart: this month's line and the best day. Keep that discipline as you add labels.
- The table is set right: numbers right-aligned in tabular figures, thin rules, and the column headers in a lighter gray than the rows.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the date range and the nav items show focus and hover states. Also whether the chart data for 25–30 Nov is booked-ahead data or a mock-up: today is 24 November, so check in the live app what those last days are counting.
