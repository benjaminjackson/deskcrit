**First read.** The eye lands on the big white headline, top left, and goes next to the dark card on the right. It's clearly a tide and current app for open-water swimmers, and the specific copy ("before you leave the car", "Porthgwidden") makes it feel local and honest. The card stays vague, though. It shows a row of gray bars and a list, but nothing in it tells me when the safe window is.

**What it's for.** You didn't tell me, so I'm assuming this is the marketing homepage, and its job is to get sea swimmers to trust it and tap "Add your beach".

**Verdict.** It's close. The headline, the hierarchy and the restraint are solid. The biggest problem is the hero card: it's meant to prove the promise, but it never shows the safe window. So the headline makes a claim and the picture next to it doesn't back it up.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero card doesn't show the product's main promise.** *(Defect.)* The headline and body promise "the safe window" and "the current". The card on the right shows unlabeled gray bars plus four rows where "Swim" and "Avoid" are the same gray in the same weight. The only emphasized word is "Next". A swimmer looking at it has to read every row and work out the window themselves, which is exactly the job the product claims to do for them. The chart can't help either. It has no time axis, no height or unit, no "now" marker, and nothing linking the bars to the 06:10/12:25/18:40 rows, so you can only guess that the bars are tide height (Tufte, *Visual Display*: say what the marks measure; label at the data). The current, which the headline is about, isn't drawn at all. Fix: make the safe windows the most visible thing in the card. Mark them on the chart and tie them to the times, and make "Avoid" look clearly different from "Swim" through weight or position, not color alone. Add a time axis so the bars line up with the rows. Whether the card should show current instead of, or alongside, tide height is your call, but it should show what the headline promises.
2. **The prices are in dollars on a product made in Penzance.** *(Defect, copy.)* "$0" and "$4 a month" sit next to "car park" and "made in Penzance". A UK swimmer could read the price as US dollars, and the amount on their card statement wouldn't match, or as a template leftover. Either way it weakens trust on the one line they'll hold you to. Fix: use the currency you actually charge in. If it really is dollars, say so.
3. **The plans have no way to choose one.** *(Common sense.)* "Two plans, cancel any month" ends on two descriptions and no action. Anyone convinced by the pricing has to scroll back to the hero to find "Add your beach", and that button doesn't say which plan they'd get. Fix: give each plan its own action, or say near the button that it starts on Dipper. Also, "cancel any month" next to a $0 plan reads oddly. Saying that it's the paid plan you can cancel would make the heading land.
4. **It looks like the stock dark template.** *(Template tell; unslop, "Neutral dark template look".)* A near-black gray ground, white text in a neutral sans, and the amber button as the only color: visitors have seen this on many other products. For something about a specific Cornish cove, the page has nothing of the sea, the place or the light. Fix: tint the neutrals toward the product's world, and carry the amber (or a color taken from the water) into the safe-window marking from finding 1, so the color does a job too.

**Smaller things**
- The pricing columns don't follow the three-column grid of the "From the car park to the water" section. The second plan starts at about x 464 and the second feature at about x 469, a near-miss of about 5 px that looks like an error (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Put both sections on the same columns, or make the difference obvious.

**What's working**
- **The headline and the "From the car park to the water" copy.** They name one concrete moment in the swimmer's own world, which no competitor's homepage could say. Keep that voice.
- **The secondary action.** "See a sample morning" sits clearly below the amber button. It's the right second ask: it lets swimmers judge the product on a morning they know before they commit.
- **The hierarchy is clean.** It has four clear type levels, generous gathered space, readable line lengths, and only one primary action.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus states on the nav, button and link.
