**First read.** My eye goes to the cream serif headline, then across to the shelf card, where the amber "Book" on Shelf 3 pulls it in, then down to the amber "Set up your studio" button. Within a few seconds it reads as a booking tool for a shared kiln in a pottery studio, aimed at the studio owner or technician. It feels calm and warm, like a well-run workshop. It doesn't feel like software.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the marketing landing page for Kilnroom, and its job is to get pottery studio owners to sign up.

**Verdict.** This is a strong, specific page that mostly works. The hero card shows the product doing exactly what the headline claims. The biggest gap is at the bottom: a viewer who reads down to the prices has nothing to click, and the page never compares Kilnroom with however the studio books the kiln today.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pricing section has no action.** The two tiers ($12 and $29 a month) end in a short description, then the footer. Someone who has read the whole page is ready to decide right there, but the only way to act is to scroll back up to the hero button. Neither tier says which plan the hero button starts you on, or whether there's a trial. Fix: put the primary action under the prices, either one per tier or one shared button tied to the plan choice, in the same amber style as the hero button. *Defect* (common sense: the next step should be where the decision gets made).
2. **The page never sets Kilnroom against what the studio uses now.** "Set up your studio" is a clear, concrete ask. But the case for it rests on describing features ("Members pick a firing and a shelf height from their phone"). Nothing puts that next to the way the studio books the kiln today, so the owner has to take the benefit on trust. The hero card already shows the after. What's missing is the before, the current method the reader would recognize. The lines also stop at the fact without landing it on the reader. For example, "The technician sees what is going in, what cone it needs, and who to tell when it comes out" never says what the technician no longer has to chase. Fix: name the method studios use now and finish one or two lines on what that means for the owner or technician. Only the maker knows what that current method is. *Taste*, argued: a comparison the reader already accepts sells better than a list of features.
3. **"Up to four kilns, classes, and members without limit" can be read two ways, and it's a price line.** One reading is four kilns, plus unlimited classes and members. The other is that "up to four" also covers classes. People hold you to what a plan includes, so the wrong reading means either a lost sale or a later complaint. Fix: split the line so each limit stands on its own, e.g. "Up to four kilns. Classes and members without limit." *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- **A drawing in every section.** The vase, the stacked bowls, and the kiln sit in the right column of the three lower sections. They're well drawn and on theme, but none of them tells the reader anything, and one per section is a stock template move (Sokolovsky, unslop: "one visual where it helps, usually only the hero"). They make the page look like many others and take room from the content. Keep one where it does a job, or replace one with something that shows the product.
- **Hero headline break.** "Every shelf of the kiln / booked before the / firing" splits "the firing" across lines and leaves one word on the last line. Rebreak it, or set the measure so the break falls after "kiln" and "booked".
- **"One price per studio" sits above two prices.** I assume it means "not per member", but a stranger reading it next to $12 and $29 may stop for a second. Make it say what is flat.
- **The pricing columns are on a different grid.** They start at about x = 80 and 424, while the three steps above start at 80, 349, and 619, so the two sections look placed by eye (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Put them on the same columns.
- **The currency.** Prices are in $, but the footer says the company is in Stoke-on-Trent. If UK studios are the audience, they'll wonder whether they're paying in dollars. Check that the currency matches the audience.
- **"Studio notes" in the nav doesn't say what it opens** (blog, guides, changelog?). Name what's behind it (WCAG 2.4.4).

**What's working.**
- **The hero card is the best thing on the page.** "Glaze firing, cone 6 / Loads Friday 16:00", named members with real loads, and one shelf left to book. It proves the headline instead of repeating it, and it uses the studio's own words (bisque, glaze, cone, shelf). Keep it specific like this.
- **The palette and type have a character of their own.** The deep green ground, cream type, and one amber reserved for the things you act on (the button, "Book") feel like clay and fire, not a template. The serif headings over sans body text give clear levels without needing many sizes.
- **The three steps.** "Book a shelf / Label the work / Collect when told" are short verb-led headings, each with one concrete sentence ("a number to write on the foot") that only this product could say.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Book" in the hero card is live or just an illustration, and the focus and hover states of the nav, button, and sample-firing link.
