**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Know which rack every boat is on", drops to the one navy tile (M4) in the panel on the right, and ends on "Map your boathouse". Within two seconds it reads as software for rowing clubs to manage boathouse racks. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, a little like a clubhouse notice board in a good way. The panel reads as "a grid of filled slots", though, not "boats on racks".

**What it's for.** Assumed: the landing-page hero for club captains and boathouse managers. Its job is to get them to start mapping their own boathouse.

**Verdict.** This is a sound, well-made hero with a clear promise and a specific ask. The biggest thing in the way is that the product picture doesn't show the promise. The headline says you'll know which boat is on every rack, but the plan only shows which racks are taken, and it colors them in a way a quick glance reads backwards.

**What would move it most**
1. **The rack plan doesn't show what the headline promises.** *Defect.* The headline and the paragraph both promise boats ("which rack every boat is on", "the captain sees every boat at a glance"). In the plan, 14 of the 15 taken racks show only a code (U1, M2, L4…). The one boat you learn anything about is M4, and only as "single scull" in the footer. Feature 1 below says the plan shows club boats and members' boats apart, but nothing in the plan does. A captain looking at this sees a parking map, not their boats, so the main claim stays something they have to take on trust. Picture and headline should be two halves of one idea. Here the picture is a weaker version of the headline (common sense: no approved source states this). Fix: put the boat on the rack, using the kind of label the real product shows (a boat name or a hull type). Mark club and private boats differently, so the picture proves both the headline and feature 1. If tiles get too small for that, show fewer racks rather than drop the boats.
2. **Green means "taken" here, which is the reverse of what green usually means.** *Convention, with a real cost.* The taken racks are sage green and the free ones (U3, M6, L5) are white. Green usually means available or success (Apple HIG, Material Design: color roles), so a member scanning for a free rack is pulled toward the green tiles first, the wrong way round. The sage is muted, so the pull is mild, but the panel sells exactly this lookup ("Members claim a free rack themselves"). Fix: make "taken" a neutral or the brand tone, and give "free" the color or the stronger mark.
3. **Free racks are the faintest thing in the panel.** *Defect (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11).* The thing members are told they can claim is marked by a pale dashed outline. It looks about 1.3:1 against the cream panel, well under 3:1, plus a white fill that barely differs from the cream. At a glance the three free racks look like gaps, not slots on offer. Measure the border color against the panel to confirm. Fix: give free racks an outline or fill that passes 3:1. Together with finding 2, they should be the second thing the eye finds after the selected rack.

**Smaller things.**
- Grid: the panel's left edge (~652 px) lines up with none of the three feature columns below (80 / 469 / 859). Run the hero split and the feature row on one set of columns (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- "glance." sits alone on the last line of the hero paragraph (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Rebreak or retune the measure so the last line carries at least two words.
- Copy: the ask has no comparison. "Map your boathouse" is specific, but nothing sets the product next to what a club uses today. Say what kind of current method this replaces and leave the wording to the maker.
- Copy: "with a card link" can be read two ways (a link to pay by card, or a link to some "card"). Restore the verb so it says what the rack holder does, e.g. "a link to pay by card", if that's what it means.
- Copy/content: if U/M/L mean upper/middle/lower tiers, "Doors to the slipway" sitting between the middle and lower rows will look wrong to anyone who has stood in a boathouse. Check that the plan matches how a real bay is laid out, since this audience will notice.

**What's working.**
- The hero copy is concrete and in the club's own words: rack plan, private boats, yearly rack fees, captain. "Members claim a free rack themselves" names a real chore it removes. Keep that voice when you edit.
- There's one clear primary action and a secondary that is honestly lower. "See a sample boathouse" is an underlined text link, and it's a well-chosen second option for someone not ready to commit.
- The panel footer ("Rack M4, single scull · Held until 31 March 2027 · $95 paid") is the most convincing thing on the page. It shows a real record with a date and an amount, not a vague claim. Give the rest of the plan more of that.
