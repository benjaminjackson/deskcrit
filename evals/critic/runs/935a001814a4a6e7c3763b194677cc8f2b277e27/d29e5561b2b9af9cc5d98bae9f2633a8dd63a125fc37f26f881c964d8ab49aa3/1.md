**First read.** My eye goes to "Plan and billing" first, then to the Marina card in the middle because it has the teal outline and the filled radio button. After that it drops to the teal "Save changes" button. This is the settings page of a marina-management product, used by the person who pays the bill. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, which suits a billing page.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming the job is to let a marina's office manager see their plan, switch plans, keep billing details correct and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** As a layout it is clean and it works. As a billing screen it falls short: the user can't safely change their plan. The plan cards can't be compared, nothing says what switching costs or when it takes effect, and it's unclear which button commits the change.

**What would move it most**

1. **It's unclear whether "Save changes" also changes the plan, and nothing says what a switch does.** The only commit button sits under Billing details, with a rule and a section heading between it and the plan cards. A user who picks Harbor group can't tell whether "Save changes" moves them to $149.00 a month now, at the next cycle, or not at all. They also don't know whether the price is prorated or charged in full. Norman's natural mapping says a control should sit next to what it changes. Billing makes this worse, because a change that moves money is exactly what the user will later hold the product to. Fix: make the plan change its own clearly labeled action, attached to the plan section. Next to that action, say in a short line when the new plan starts and how the first charge is worked out. Only the maker knows those facts. **Defect** (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*; Nielsen heuristic 10, help placed at the step it concerns).

2. **The three plan cards describe different things, so the user can't compare them.** Harbor lists slips and docks. Marina lists slips and booking fees. Harbor group lists marinas and a boater list. Does Harbor charge booking fees? How many docks does Marina allow? How many slips does Harbor group include? The user has to guess, and they are guessing about a decision that sets their monthly bill. The bold text doesn't help either: "**one** dock", "**no** booking fees", and nothing bold on Harbor group. The cards look like matching elements but follow no shared pattern (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Fix: describe each plan on the same attributes, in the same order, so the differences show up line by line. If bold is kept, it should mark the same attribute in every card. **Defect.**

3. **"Charge the card on file before the first of each month" refers to a card the page never shows.** The user has no way to check which card will be charged, or whether it has expired, without going somewhere else (maybe "Payment terminal"?). "Before the first" also gives no date: one day before, or a week? This line commits the user's money, so it needs to be exact. Fix: show the card (brand, last four digits, expiry) with a way to change it, right next to this checkbox. State the charge date as a specific day. **Defect** (Nielsen heuristic 6, recognition rather than recall).

**Smaller things**
- The invoice table is a dead end. HL-1185 and the other invoice numbers aren't styled as links and there's no download, yet viewing or downloading an invoice is usually why someone opens this table. Give each row a way to open its PDF. Common sense.
- The input borders and unselected radio rings look light gray on white. Measure them against the 3:1 minimum for component edges (WCAG 1.4.11) and darken them if they fall short.
- "Billing email" and "Currency" sit side by side even though they aren't a related pair, so the eye zigzags across the form. Taste: one column, or pair only fields that belong together.

**What's working**
- The type scale is tight and clear: a page title, section headings, labels, and body/table text, with the steps between them done by weight rather than extra sizes. Keep it.
- The invoice table is well made. Amounts are right-aligned with figures that line up, rules are light, and status is a word ("Paid"), not just a color. Keep that restraint when you add download links.
- The current plan is marked twice, with a filled radio button and a teal border, and teal is saved for the selected state and the primary button. Because the accent is used so sparingly, the important things stand out.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether selecting a plan card asks for confirmation, or shows the price change, before anything is charged.
- Focus rings on the cards, fields and buttons.
