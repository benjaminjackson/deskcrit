**First read.** The eye lands on "Overview", drops to $24,815.60, then jumps right to the blue Saturday bar, the only solid block of color on the page. It reads at once as a small wine shop's monthly sales dashboard, made for the owner. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, until you try to use the numbers.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this: the shop owner opens it to see at a glance how November went compared with October, and where sales come from.

**Verdict.** The layout is sound and the restraint is right for the job. What lets it down is the numbers. Several figures don't say what they count, a few are formatted carelessly, and one chart appears not to add up with the headline total. On a dashboard, numbers you can't trust undo the calm look.

**What would move it most**

1. **The charts and two KPI labels don't say what they count, and one may not reconcile.** This is a defect.
   - **Bottles by weekday:** nothing says whether each bar is a month total, an average per weekday, or one channel. By eye the seven bars add up to about 700 bottles, roughly half of the 1,384 under "Bottles sold". An owner who notices will either stop trusting the chart or stop trusting the KPI. Check this against the data.
   - **Web shop visitors:** the line has about 11 points across 30 days, and nothing says whether 700 means visitors per day, per 3-day bucket, or something else.
   - **Wine club share 27%:** share of revenue, of orders, or of customers? Each one would change what the owner does next.
   - Tufte and Few: the reader should never have to guess what a number measures.
   - **Fix:** name the unit and the aggregate in each chart title or axis, the way "Bottles sold" already does, and finish the KPI label so it says what the share is of. If the weekday bars really are a subset or an average, the title has to say so. If they are meant to be totals, the data is wrong.

2. **Numbers of one kind appear in different formats.** This is a defect, and the "09" is unfinished copy.
   - In the Best-selling wines table, Barolo shows "09" bottles. The leading zero looks like a bug or a placeholder, and it lands on the row an owner will remember.
   - Revenue in the same column runs $1,512, $1,159.50, $1,128.00: no cents, then cents, then cents of zero.
   - In the KPIs, Average basket shows $38 next to "$36.25 in October".
   - A mix like this is a stock template tell (unslop, "Numbers in mixed formats"). More to the point, it makes the table look generated rather than checked, which feeds straight into finding 1.
   - **Fix:** one format per kind of number. Either whole dollars everywhere or cents everywhere, and no zero padding.

3. **The KPI comparisons make the owner do the arithmetic.** This is my taste, argued here.
   - "$21,970 in October" sits under "$24,815.60". Giving the previous period is right; it passes the checklist.
   - But the reader has to subtract four times to learn the one thing they came for: up or down, and by how much.
   - **Fix:** keep the October figure, and lead the line with the change and its direction, worked out from numbers already on the page. That way the answer is there at a glance, which is the whole point of a KPI strip.

**Smaller things.**
- **Line chart legend:** November/October sits in a legend in the top-right corner. Label the two lines at their right ends, where there's already room after "30 Nov" (Tufte, Wilke).
- **Grid:** the three rows use three different column structures. The KPI dividers are at about 511, 756 and 1002 px, the chart split at about 891/908 px, and the table columns at 618 and 1033 px, so no vertical line carries down the page. Put the charts' split, and ideally the table's numeric columns, on the KPI columns (Müller-Brockmann).
- **Table title:** "Best-selling wines" is sorted by revenue, not bottles. That's why a 9-bottle wine ranks third, which reads oddly under "best-selling". Make the title match the sort, or the sort match the title.

**What's working.**
- **One accent hue, used for focus only:** the blue marks only "this period" and the peak day, and everything else is gray. Keep that discipline as features get added.
- **The KPI strip leads, and each figure has a comparison:** the hierarchy is clear: page title, then four figures, then supporting charts, then the detail table.
- **The table is set properly:** numbers are right-aligned in their columns, the rules are light, and there is no zebra striping or other noise.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- **Date range:** "1 to 30 November 2026" sits top-right where a period picker usually goes, but it looks like plain text. If it's meant to change the period, check in the live page that it shows it can be clicked.
