I've finished the deep read and am writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big glossy orb on the right, the largest and most colorful thing on the screen. Then it crosses to the headline, "Share the kiln without double-booked shelves," and down to the brown button. Once you read the words, this is clearly a booking tool for a shared pottery-studio kiln. Until then it could be a page for any startup. It feels calm and tidy, and the type is pleasant.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing home page that gets studio owners or kiln technicians at shared ceramics studios to add their kiln, or to look at a sample firing first.

**Verdict.** Close to working. The copy and the type are doing the job. The biggest problem is the hero visual: a stock glossy blob takes the most valuable space on the page, pulls the eye before the headline does, and shows nothing about shelves, firings or bookings.

**What would move it most**

1. **The hero blob takes the eye from the headline and says nothing about the product.** It's about a quarter of the first screen, on the right. It's the most saturated and highest-contrast thing on the page, so the viewer looks there first and finds a shapeless orb. The headline makes a precise promise (no more double-booked shelves), and the picture gives no evidence for it. The viewer has to take the claim on trust when the space could have shown it to them. This one choice causes several failures:
   - **It looks like everyone else's page.** A glossy 3D blob as the hero visual is a template tell (Sokolovsky, unslop). Viewers have seen this orb on many AI and SaaS pages, so this page reads as anyone's at the moment it should read as a tool for potters.
   - **Image and headline don't make one idea.** The picture and the words don't connect (common sense).
   - **It adds colors that appear nowhere else.** Orange, crimson, olive and teal sit beside a palette that is otherwise neutral plus one brown (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*, "Define your shades"). At this size they compete with the brown button, which should be the only strong color on the screen (Apple HIG, Material Design 3 on accent colors).
   - **It doesn't match the rest of the page.** The specular highlight and soft drop shadow fake a 3D material that the flat, quiet type and button don't share, so it looks pasted in from another page (taste).

   Fix: put the product itself in that space. The obvious candidate is the thing the copy describes: a firing's shelves with members' work booked on them, or the technician's loading list. That matches what "See a sample firing" already offers. If the real screen isn't ready, use one concrete picture of kiln shelves rather than an abstract shape. Either way the headline gets back the first look.
   *Defect + template tell; palette is convention.*

2. **The page never compares itself to what the studio does now.** The copy is specific and good, but it describes Kilnshare in isolation. Studios that share a kiln already manage bookings somehow. The page doesn't set its loading list or cost split next to that, so the viewer can't measure the gain on terms they already trust. "See a sample firing" is a strong secondary ask because it lets the viewer judge for themselves. The primary path asks for commitment before that comparison is made.

   Fix: name the current method in one line, in the body copy or a feature column, and say what it costs the viewer today. Only the maker knows which method their audience actually uses, so that fact has to come from them.
   *Taste.*

**Smaller things.**
- "Bisque and glaze apart" (middle feature heading) has no verb, so the reader has to work out who keeps what apart. The body below it fixes this, but a restored verb in the heading would read faster, for example "Keep bisque and glaze apart."

**What's working.**
- **The headline:** specific, in the potter's own words, and it names the pain ("double-booked shelves"). It breaks cleanly at "without." Keep it exactly.
- **The calls to action:** one strong primary ("Add your studio kiln") and one clearly lower secondary ("See a sample firing"). Both say exactly what happens, in the audience's terms. Keep that pairing.
- **Type and spacing:** a tight scale (headline, body, feature heading, feature body) with weight doing the work between levels. The hero paragraph runs about 60 characters a line, and the three feature columns sit on one clean grid with a single divider. It's quiet and readable, so don't add decoration when the blob is replaced.
