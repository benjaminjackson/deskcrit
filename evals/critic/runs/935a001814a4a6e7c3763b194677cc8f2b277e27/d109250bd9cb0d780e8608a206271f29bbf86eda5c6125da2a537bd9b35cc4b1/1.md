It reads as the monthly sales page of a small fabric shop that sells online. It feels calm, competent and quiet, more like a well-kept ledger than a sales pitch. **What it's for.** My assumption, since you told me nothing: a sales dashboard for the owner or staff of a small fabric shop, used to check how the month went against last month and what is selling.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid: a clean grid, a restrained palette, and an October comparison under every figure. But the figures don't agree with each other, and the money may carry the wrong currency symbol. For a page whose whole job is trustworthy numbers, that matters more than anything visual. After that, the page makes you work out the story yourself instead of telling you.

**What would move it most**

1. **The numbers don't add up, and the charts hide how they were built.** *Defect.*
   - $27,640 revenue ÷ $46 average order is about 600 orders. The weekday bars add up to about the same, roughly 600 by eye.
   - The "Daily orders" line averages about 27 a day, which comes to roughly 800 for the month.
   - That line also has only 11 points for 30 days. So it isn't daily: it is sampled or bucketed, and the title doesn't say which.
   - Its x-axis ticks fall at 1, 10, 19 and 30 Nov, which are gaps of 9, 9 and 11 days. That breaks even tick spacing (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*).
   - "Orders by weekday" adds up raw totals across unequal numbers of days. November 2026 starts on a Sunday, so it has five Sundays and five Mondays but only four of every other day. Sunday and Monday get a free extra day.
   - What the owner experiences: the first time they cross-check two numbers, they stop trusting all of them.
   - Fix direction: make the line truly daily or retitle it to say what each point is, and put ticks at even steps. Show weekday as an average per day, or say how many days each bar covers. Then check that orders reconcile across the KPI cards and both charts.

2. **Dollars on a Norwich shop.** *Defect, unless the shop really sells in dollars.*
   - The footer says "Bolt & Bobbin, Norwich". The copy uses British spelling and date style ("Metres", "1 to 30 November").
   - Yet every money figure carries "$": the KPI cards, the October comparisons and the Revenue column.
   - If the shop takes pounds, every money figure on the page carries the wrong symbol, and money figures are what the owner acts on and quotes to others.
   - Fix: use the shop's own currency symbol. If sales really are in dollars, say so once, near the date range.

3. **Nothing tells you how the month went.** *Defect for a glance-and-go dashboard; the chart-title point is taste.*
   - The page title "Sales" and the four KPI figures look the same size and weight, so nothing leads. The loudest mark on the page is the Saturday bar, a side fact.
   - Each KPI gives "this month / last month" as two raw numbers, so the owner has to subtract four times to learn that everything went up. (Revenue is up about 15%.)
   - The chart titles are topic labels ("Daily orders", "Orders by weekday"). They don't say what the chart found.
   - Fix direction: show the change itself on each KPI card (direction and amount) so the story reads in one pass. Make one figure lead, probably Revenue, by size, and let the other three step down (Tschichold, *The New Typography*). Let a chart title or a short note say what the chart shows; the gap between November and October in the line chart is the obvious candidate.

4. **"Orders by weekday" can't be read beyond Saturday.** *Defect.*
   - Only Saturday has a value (126). There is no value axis, so Sunday against Friday, or Monday against Wednesday, is a guess from bar height alone.
   - Fix: put the value on each bar, the same way Saturday has one. That would also drop the need for an axis (Few; Tufte).

**Smaller things**
- In "Daily orders", the two series are told apart only by color plus a separate legend at the top right. Label "November" and "October" at the right ends of the lines instead (Tufte; WCAG 1.4.1).
- The October line in "Daily orders" uses October's day numbers against November dates. Say that in the legend or a note.
- The "Daily orders" y-axis has no unit or label. "Orders" is implied by the title, but say it once.
- The gray weekday bars and the pale October line look below 3:1 against white. Measure them and darken them if they fail (WCAG 1.4.11).
- "Repeat makers 54%" doesn't say 54% of what: orders, or customers? Add the missing noun.
- The Settings icon in the sidebar looks like a sun or crosshair rather than the usual gear. Use the standard gear icon.

**What's working**
- Every KPI card shows a comparison with October. That is the right idea, and the cards only need it turned into a change you can read at a glance.
- The palette has one blue for emphasis and gray for context. It keeps the charts honest-looking and quiet, so keep it when you add emphasis.
- The "Best-selling fabrics" table is set right: numbers right-aligned and evenly spaced, light rules between rows, and no fill on the table itself.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the "1 to 30 November 2026" date range is a control for changing the period or just static text. If it can't be changed, comparing other months has no way in.
