**First read.** The eye goes first to the row of four big numbers at the top, then to the blue line chart, then to the one blue bar, F. The table of faults at the bottom comes last. It reads as a monthly operations dashboard for a community solar co-op: calm, tidy and competent. All five cards have bright cyan outlines that look like focus rings.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the co-op's operators and board use it to see how the month went and which arrays need work.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are sound, but the numbers contradict each other. A reader who checks one figure against another will stop trusting the whole screen. That is the biggest thing to fix. After that, the charts don't say what they show, and the one actionable part, the faults table, gives the reader no way to act.

**What would move it most**

1. **The figures don't agree with each other.** *Defect.*
   - In "Daily output (kWh)" the November line is above the October line every day. But the KPI row says November produced 38.4 MWh against 51.2 MWh in October. Both can't be true.
   - In "Output by array", F is labeled 2.3 MWh and the other bars are shorter, so the seven arrays add up to roughly 13 MWh, about a third of the 38.4 MWh headline.
   - "Panels online" is 1,184, down 6 from 1,190. Yet the table lists Array D's 64 panels behind an inverter that has been offline for 2 days.

   On a dashboard people use to judge payouts, one visible contradiction makes every number suspect, and the member payout of $4,860 is a figure people will hold the co-op to. Each of these may have an explanation outside the image: a different date range, a per-day average, or a snapshot taken at a different time. If so, the screen has to say it. Put the period or basis on the chart and the card ("average per array per day", "as of 30 Nov"), or fix the data. Whatever the cause, the reader shouldn't have to work out why the numbers disagree.

2. **The charts don't say what they're showing.** *Convention (Tufte, Visual Display, ch. 2: clear labeling), plus taste on the titles.*
   - "Output by array" has no y-axis and only one value label. F is picked out in blue, but nothing says why: best performer, selected, or something else. The reader can't read the other six bars or tell whether the highlight means good or bad. That leaves color as the only explanation for the emphasis (WCAG 1.4.1).
   - "Daily output (kWh)" plots about 13 points across 30 days, so it isn't daily. Its ticks (1, 10, 19, 30 Nov) are unevenly spaced.
   - Both titles name a topic, not a finding. My view as a designer: a dashboard's charts should say what they show, such as which array led or where November stands against October. That needs the maker's facts, so I won't write it.

   Direction: give the bar chart an axis or a value on every bar, label why F is highlighted, and make the line chart's point count match its title.

3. **"Arrays needing a visit" is the only part that asks someone to do something, and it's last and inert.** *Taste / common sense.* It's the one place an operator turns the screen into work: three faults and 765 kWh lost. It sits below the fold of attention, styled exactly like the summary cards. There's no way to schedule, assign or open a fault, and no sign of how long each has been open, except D's "2 days". The reader learns there's a problem and then has to go elsewhere to act on it. Give the table more weight than the summary cards, and give each row its next action.

**Smaller things**
- Neon cyan borders on all five cards (a template tell, unslop): they add a second accent color that fights the navy data blue, and they look like focus rings. Use a neutral hairline or no border on the gray ground.
- The legend on the line chart ("November / October", top right): label the two lines at their right ends instead (Tufte; Wilke).

**What's working**
- The KPI cards each pair the figure with last month's value in a quieter gray line ("51.2 MWh in October"). That gives every number context without clutter. Keep that pattern.
- Restrained color in the charts: gray for context and one navy for the series that matters is the right instinct. It only needs the labeling to back it up.
- The table is set well: right-aligned figures with units on "Lost output", plain-language faults ("Soiling on lower rows", "One string open"), and light row rules.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the cyan outlines are a focus or selection state caught in the screenshot rather than the resting style. Check in a live browser.
