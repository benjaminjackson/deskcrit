I've finished the checklist and reference reads, and I'm writing up the critique now.

**First read.** The eye goes to "Plan and billing", then drops straight to the Studio card, the only thing on the page with a colored outline. From there it runs down to the teal "Save changes" button and then to the invoice table. It reads as a calm, tidy account settings page for a tattoo studio's booking software. It feels trustworthy and plain, close to a standard SaaS settings template.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a studio owner comes here to check or change their plan, update where invoices go, and find past invoices.

**Verdict.** As a settings form it's well made: clean, aligned, legible, with one clear primary action. It falls short on its most important job, which is changing what the studio pays. The plan picker doesn't say which plan you're on now or what happens when you pick another. It also shares one generic "Save changes" button with the invoice email and address fields.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan change has no commit of its own, and the button that commits it is in another section.** The plan cards sit above a rule, under their own "Plan" heading. "Save changes" sits under "Billing details", two sections lower, next to the email, currency, and address fields. If an owner clicks "Multi-studio", they can't tell whether "Save changes" will move them to $89.00 a month or only save the address. If they edit the address and save, they can't tell whether a plan click they made by mistake goes through too. The rule between the sections says "separate groups", but the single button treats them as one. Defect, against Norman's natural mapping (a control sits next to the thing it changes) and common region (one box or section holds one group; the button here spans two). Fix direction: give the plan change its own action inside the Plan section, and keep "Save changes" for billing details only. Or make the Plan section clearly part of the form that the button saves.
2. **Nothing marks the current plan or says what a change will do.** Studio is selected, and the invoices suggest that's the plan being paid. But the card doesn't say "your plan", and a selected radio looks the same whether it's the current plan or a choice you haven't saved yet. Before an owner commits to a different monthly charge, they need to know when the new price starts, whether this month is prorated, and when the next charge happens. None of that is on the page, and there's no payment method either, which is odd on a page titled "billing". Defect: it slows and worries the person at the exact moment they're committing money. This is common sense; the anticipation entry's source isn't one I can cite. Fix direction: label the current plan on its card, and when another plan is picked, say in words what will be charged and when. The maker has to supply those facts.
3. **The three plan descriptions compare different things, so the owner can't compare plans.** Guest spot gives a booking limit ("One artist, 30 bookings a month"). Studio gives an artist count and a feature ("Up to 6 artists, deposits online"). Multi-studio gives a studio count and a feature ("Up to 4 studios, one client list"). Someone reading across can't tell whether Studio has a booking cap, whether Guest spot can take deposits online, or how many artists Multi-studio allows. A reader who doesn't know the product has to fill those gaps, and may fill them wrongly when deciding what to pay for. Defect, about copy. Fix direction: describe each plan on the same points, in the same order, so the differences line up. Which points matter, and their values, are the maker's to supply.

**Smaller things.**
- The billing email and invoice address carry no required or optional marks; mark the required ones with an asterisk and explain it once (Budiu, NN/g).
- Input borders and the unselected card outlines look pale gray, possibly under 3:1 against white (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the field border color, and darken it if it falls short.
- Billing email and Currency share a row even though they're unrelated. Taste: a single top-to-bottom column reads more surely in a short form like this.
- The invoices have no visible way to open or download them, so an owner who needs one for their books has nowhere to click. If the invoice numbers are links, give them a link style.

**What's working.**
- Restraint with color: teal appears only on the selected plan, the checkbox, and the primary button. So the one colored outline does mark the choice, and "Save changes" is clearly the main action, with Cancel clearly secondary.
- The invoice table is set well: amounts are right-aligned in tabular figures, rules are light, and there's no boxed-cell clutter, so it scans in one pass.
- One grid holds everything: sidebar, title, cards, fields, and table all share the same left edge at 300 px and the right edge at 1120 px, which is why the page feels calm.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus states on the plan cards and fields, and what happens after a plan is picked (confirmation, error, or a message once saved).
