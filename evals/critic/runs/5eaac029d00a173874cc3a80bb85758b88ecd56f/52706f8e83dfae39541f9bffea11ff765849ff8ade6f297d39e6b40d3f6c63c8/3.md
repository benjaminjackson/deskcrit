**First read.** The eye goes first to the big stacked-area chart at the bottom, because it's the only large block of colour on the page. Next it goes to the bold heading "Fossil fuels accounted for about 58%…", and only then to the small grey graphic at the top. This is a government energy explainer (EIA), and it reads as plain, reliable and dated. The text is easy to follow. The charts make you work.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a public explainer for curious non-experts and students. Its job is to show where US electricity comes from and how that has changed.

**Verdict.** The prose does its job. The two charts, which should be the clearest part of the page, are the weakest. The top graphic is a small picture of text you can't read, and its bands don't seem to match its own numbers. The area chart hides the main story, coal falling and gas rising, under a stack you can't read across.

**What would move it most**

1. **The top graphic can't be read at the size it's shown, and its bands look out of proportion.** It's a raster image that fills only the left half of the column. The breakdown table (wind 10.5%, hydro 5.6%…), the "total=4.43 trillion kilowatthours" line and the whole note block look about 5–6 px tall, which is unreadable. "Click to enlarge" is a sign of the problem, not a fix for it. The bands also look wrong for their values. Renewables at 24.1% looks about the same height as nuclear at 17.7% and coal at 16.6%. Natural gas at 40.6% is about twice as tall. So the picture tells readers the three are roughly equal, when renewables are about 40% bigger. On top of that, a faint wall-socket drawing sits behind the data. *Defect* (Tufte's lie factor; WCAG images of text; captions too small). Fix: rebuild it as a real chart in live text. Scale each band to its value, show the renewables breakdown at full size beside its band, and run it the full column width. Drop the socket drawing.

2. **The area chart's layout hides the trend the page is about.** Only coal, at the bottom, sits on a flat baseline. Natural gas rides on top of coal's falling edge, so its steep rise from about 2005 looks much flatter than it is. Nuclear and renewables can't be compared over time at all. The stack is decoded through a legend under the axis, so you look back and forth five times. Nuclear red sits directly against renewables green, which is a red-green pair. Coal brown and petroleum tan are close in hue. *Defect* (position on a common scale beats stacked area; label directly; don't rely on colour alone, Wilke, Few, WCAG 1.4.1). Fix: plot the sources as separate lines on one axis, or as small multiples, and label each line at its end. If the total has to stay, keep it as one grey shape behind the lines. Make the title state the finding, e.g. "Natural gas overtook coal around 2016", instead of naming the topic.

3. **The numbers don't add up between the text and the chart.** The top chart shows "petroleum and other 0.7%". The text says petroleum *alone* was 0.7%, then adds "other gases and other sources… 0.2%". Readers can't tell whether the small slice is 0.7% or 0.9%. The text also switches between "% of U.S. utility-scale generation" (gas) and "% of U.S. electricity generation" (coal, petroleum), which leaves people wondering whether the two bases differ. The two charts also cite different sources and dates (EPM Feb 2026 vs MER Mar 2026 / EPM Jan 2026). On a reference page people quote these figures, so a mismatch costs trust. *Defect.* Fix: use one breakdown and one base everywhere, and say once that all shares are of utility-scale generation.

**Smaller things.**
- Body text runs about 85–95 characters per line, which is past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Narrow the main column or raise the type size a step.
- The sidebar headings ("Learn more", "Also on EIA.gov") look bigger than the main heading, so the side panel competes with the article. Make the article heading the largest size in the content area.
- In the sidebar, wrapped two-line links sit as close to the next link as to their own second line, so "Energy storage for electricity / generation" blurs into its neighbours. Add space between items.
- The current-page marker ("▸Electricity in the United States") is mid grey and reads as disabled rather than "you are here".
- Inline links in the Petroleum paragraph are blue only, with no underline. Seven links in four lines make it read like a link list. Underline them, or cut the less useful ones.
- The thick grey rules above each chart and the thin rule below the first one carry no meaning and don't match each other. Use one treatment, or just space.
- The second "Click to enlarge" floats about 50 px below the chart's source note, cut off from it. Attach it to the chart.
- The charts use Arial/Helvetica while the page uses a geometric sans. A small mismatch, but it makes the charts look pasted in.

**What's working.**
- The section heading states the finding ("Fossil fuels accounted for about 58%…") instead of naming the topic. That's the model the chart titles should follow.
- The prose is plain, gives numbers with units and context (1 MW = 1,000 kW; 4.43 trillion kWh), and starts each paragraph with the source as a link. That makes it easy to skim.
- The area chart includes units, a source and an axis that starts at zero. Keep that when it's rebuilt.

- My request to crop the image at full resolution was denied, so I judged the small chart text at the size it's displayed. Before acting on finding 1, check the top graphic on the live page at native size to see whether the note text is actually legible.
