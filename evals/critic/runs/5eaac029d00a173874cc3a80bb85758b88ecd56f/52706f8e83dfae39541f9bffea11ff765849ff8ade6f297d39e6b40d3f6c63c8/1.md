**First read.** The eye goes first to the coloured area chart at the bottom. It's the biggest, most saturated thing on the page. Then it jumps to the bold heading "Fossil fuels accounted for about 58%…". The pastel infographic at the top barely registers: it's a small grey-edged box with print too small to read, sitting in a mostly empty column. Overall this reads as a sober, trustworthy government reference page (EIA's Energy Explained). It's clear, but flat.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a general-audience explainer for students, journalists and curious citizens, answering "where does U.S. electricity come from, and how has that changed?"

**Verdict.** The text and the long-run chart mostly do the job. The page's main figure doesn't. The 2025 breakdown is a shrunken picture of text that can't be read without clicking, and its colours contradict the chart below it. A reader who tries to connect the two will misread one of them.

**What would move it most**

1. **The two charts give the same fuels different colours.** In the top infographic, nuclear is light blue, coal is pink and natural gas is grey. In the area chart below, nuclear is red, coal is brown and natural gas is blue. A reader who learns "blue = nuclear" from the first figure will read the bottom chart's biggest, fastest-growing band as nuclear. That's a wrong reading of the page's main story. Each fuel should have one colour across the whole page, and every EIA chart on this topic should share it (WCAG 1.4.1 also argues for direct labels as the second cue). **Defect.**

2. **The 2025 breakdown can't be read where it sits.** The "Sources of U.S. electricity generation, 2025" graphic is a raster image of text. At this size the renewables table (wind 10.5%, hydro 5.6%, solar 6.7%…) and the six-line note look like 5–6 px type, and the segment labels are pale grey on pastel. The image fills only about two-thirds of the column and leaves a dead block of white on the right. The one figure that answers the page's question needs a "Click to enlarge". Fix: render it as real text (WCAG 1.4.5, images of text) at full column width, with labels at body size. Move the note into an HTML caption beneath it. **Defect.**

3. **The numbers in the text and the graphic don't match, and the reader has to do the maths.** The heading says fossil fuels were 58%, but no figure shows 58%; the reader has to add 40.8 + 16.6 + 0.7. The text gives petroleum alone as 0.7% and "other gases and other sources" as a further 0.2%. The graphic labels "petroleum and other" together as 0.7%. "Coal was the third-highest source" is also ambiguous: the graphic shows renewables at 24.1% and nuclear at 17.7%, both above coal's 16.6%. That makes coal fourth unless you count wind separately, which the text doesn't say. On a reference page, these figures are what people quote. Name what each percentage includes, make the two sources agree, and show the 58% as a bracket on the figure. **Defect.**

4. **The stacked area chart hides the story its own page tells.** Only coal, the bottom band, sits on a flat baseline. Natural gas rides on coal's wavy top edge, so its rise looks like a band changing thickness rather than a line climbing. The real event, gas overtaking coal around 2016, is invisible. The title also names a topic ("…by major energy source, 1950–2025"), not a finding. Fix: use a line chart per source, or put gas and coal on the baseline. Title it with the takeaway, e.g. "Natural gas overtook coal as the top source in 2016". Annotate that crossover. Wilke and Few both favour common-baseline positions for comparing series. **Convention, and it costs something here.**

**Smaller things**
- The body text runs to about 95–100 characters per line ("Small-scale systems have less than 1 MW (1,000 kilowatts)… In 2025,"), well past Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the measure or raise the size.
- In the area chart, nuclear (red) and renewables (green) are adjacent bands, which red-green colour-blind readers can't tell apart. Label each band directly at its right end and drop the legend.
- The two brown bands, "petroleum and other" and coal, look alike. Give petroleum a more distinct value.
- In the sidebar, the current page ("Electricity in the United States") is shown in pale grey with a tiny triangle. It looks disabled rather than selected. Give it a dark, bold style.
- A heavy grey rule sits above each figure and a hairline below. That's two rule weights doing one job. Pick one.
- There's a large gap between the area chart's source note and "Click to enlarge", which detaches the link from its chart.

**What's working**
- The section heading states a finding ("Fossil fuels accounted for about 58%…") instead of a topic. That's the right instinct; carry it into the chart titles.
- In the text, each fuel name is a link at the start of its own short paragraph, so the page scans by fuel. Keep that structure.
- The source notes are thorough and sit with their charts, which suits a statistical agency.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- What "Click to enlarge" opens, and whether the enlarged infographic is actually readable.
- What the area chart's menu (☰) offers. If it includes a data table, that partly makes up for the colour-only encoding.
