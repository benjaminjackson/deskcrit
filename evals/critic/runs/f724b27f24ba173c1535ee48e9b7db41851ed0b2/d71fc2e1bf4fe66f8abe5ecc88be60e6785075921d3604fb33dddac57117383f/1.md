**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the week with your whole team," drops to the subline, then jumps right to the blue "Create account" button. It reads at once as the sign-up page of a small-team planning SaaS. It feels calm, tidy and competent, but also anonymous: apart from the word "Tallyboard," it could be the sign-up page of any of a hundred products.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop sign-up page that turns a visitor who arrived curious about Tallyboard into someone with a free workspace.

**Verdict.** The form itself is solid and easy to fill in. The page around it does almost nothing to persuade: the left half stops after three lines and leaves a large dead area, and the one strong offer ("free for up to five people") is not where the decision gets made. As it stands it collects sign-ups from people who had already decided. It won't win over anyone who hadn't.

**What would move it most**
1. **The left column runs out of things to say, and the page tips right.** The headline and subline end at about y=360, but the card runs to about y=665. That leaves the lower-left quarter of the screen as empty space that wasn't planned, opposite the heaviest object on the page. The viewer gets a promise ("plan the week") and nothing that shows it: no glimpse of the board, no example week, no proof. So the page asks for name, email, password and company before it has shown what they get. This is a balance problem (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight), and in my view it is also the reason the page feels templated. Fix direction: give the left side something with real weight that shows the product doing its job, such as a cropped view of a week planned in Tallyboard, or two or three concrete outcomes. Size it so the left block fills roughly the card's height. *Defect (persuasion); convention (balance).*
2. **The password rule lives only in the placeholder.** "At least 10 characters" disappears the moment the user starts typing. Anyone who uses a shorter habitual password finds out only from an error, and has to remember a rule they can no longer see. The labels are done right, so this is the one field where the hint does a label's job (Nielsen, heuristic 6 "Recognition rather than recall"; heuristic 5 "Error prevention"). Fix direction: move the rule to persistent helper text under the field, and leave the placeholder empty or let it repeat the rule. *Defect.*
3. **The ask doesn't carry the offer.** The best line on the page is "Start a free workspace for up to five people. You can invite the rest of the team later." It sits far from the button, and the card repeats "Create your account" / "Create account", which names a chore, not a benefit. At the moment of committing, the viewer isn't reminded that it's free or what they'll have afterwards. Fix direction: have the button or the card title name the outcome, e.g. "Create your free workspace," with a short "Free for up to 5 people" line by the button. Read it as a stranger would: "up to five people" means five including you, which should be clear before they invite a sixth. *Defect (copy at the point of commitment).*

**Smaller things.**
- **Input borders:** the borders on all five fields look like a pale grey on white, likely under the 3:1 that WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 asks for component edges. Measure them, and darken them if they fall short.
- **"Sign In" link:** it is the same grey, weight and size as "Already have an account?" with no underline, so returning users have to guess that it's clickable (WCAG technique G183). Underline it or give it the brand blue.
- **Required fields:** no field is marked required or optional. "Company" in particular reads as possibly optional. Mark the optional one(s) "(optional)", or drop Company from sign-up (Budiu, NN/g, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- **"Sign In" capitalisation:** it is title case while every other label and button is sentence case. Use "Sign in".

**What's working.**
- **Form layout and labels:** every field has a persistent bold label sitting tight above it, the group spacing is clearly larger than the label-to-field gap, and the only side-by-side pair is first and last name. Keep this structure exactly.
- **One action:** there is a single filled primary button in the only saturated colour on the page, which repeats the wordmark blue. There is no doubt where to click.
- **Type scale:** headline, card title, body, labels and the small footer line are clearly separate levels in one family, and the headline is set tight and breaks on a phrase.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and button, error and validation messages, and how the two columns stack on a narrow screen.
