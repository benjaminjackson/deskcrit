**Verdict.** The typography is well made, but the screen doesn't do the job it labels itself with. It says "timetable" and gives no times. The headline takes the space where the first and last sailings should go, and the one line about schedule, "by daylight", doesn't let anyone plan a trip.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times on its first screen.** The "Spring timetable" label and the headline promise a schedule. The visible screen gives an interval ("every forty minutes") and nothing to anchor it to: no first sailing, no last sailing, no departure times. Meanwhile the headline uses four lines at roughly 80 px on a phrase, and the bottom quarter of the screen is empty. Someone who came to check whether they can still catch a boat gets nothing they can act on and has to scroll and hope. This is a **defect**. It would be acceptable only if the table starts right below this screen *and* something here signals it. Even then, the first and last sailing belong above the fold. The fix: cut the headline to one or two lines ("Quayside ferry: spring timetable") and use the space you get back for the facts people act on. That means the first crossing, the last crossing, the 40-minute interval, and the start of the table or a clear pointer to it.
2. **The copy leaves passengers guessing about times.** "Crossings by daylight, spring through early summer" can be read two ways: the ferry runs only while it's light, or it simply operates during the daytime. Neither reading says when it stops. Daylight gets longer between March and June, so the last crossing presumably moves too, and a passenger who guesses wrong is stranded at the quay. "Every forty minutes" doesn't say from when, and "from the north landing to the market quay" doesn't say whether boats come back the other way. This is a **defect**: times are what people act on and later hold the operator to. The fix is to state them in plain words, both directions included. For example: "Boats leave both landings every 40 minutes, from 7:00 until the last crossing at sunset. See the table for this week's last boat."

**Smaller things.**
- Headline: "early / summer" splits one phrase across lines, and "summer" is left alone on the last line. Break after "crossings by daylight," or shorten the headline so the problem goes away. This is common sense: my sources don't state the rule for headings.
- Body: "weather." sits alone on the last line (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.4.2, no short word alone on a last line). Change the measure slightly or reword so the last line carries at least two words.
- The "Spring timetable" label names something this screen doesn't show (WCAG 2.4.6, Headings and labels). It's fine if the table follows immediately. Otherwise relabel it or bring the table up.

**Where it could go further.**
1. *Taste.* Make the time the hero instead of the title. The page exists to answer "when's the next boat?", so a large "Every 40 min · 7:00 to sunset" in the headline slot would serve the passenger better than a descriptive sentence. Tschichold's argument in *The New Typography*, that the form comes from the job, points the same way.
2. *Taste.* Give it one thing that belongs to this place. Right now it's Inter on white and could be anyone's page. A single colour taken from the river or the ferry's livery, used only on times or the route line, would make it recognisably this service's timetable without adding clutter.
