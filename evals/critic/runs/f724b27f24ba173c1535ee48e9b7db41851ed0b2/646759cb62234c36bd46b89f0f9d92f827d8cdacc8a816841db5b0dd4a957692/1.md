**First read.** The eye lands on the big headline, "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the grey subhead, then stops on the dark green "Get started" button. After that it drifts right into a wide empty beige field and finds nothing. The page reads as the hero of a landing page for a group trip planner. It feels calm and tidy, but it also feels unfinished, as if half the hero is missing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing hero for a consumer app that helps a group plan a trip together, and its job is to get first-time visitors to sign up.

**Verdict.** The type and the order of reading are clean, but the hero doesn't do its job yet. Half the frame is empty, so the visitor never sees the product. The headline is generic, so the one specific promise, ending the chat-thread chaos, is buried in grey subhead text.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half of the hero is empty, so the product is never shown (defect).** All the content sits in the left half, from x 80 to about 680 px and y 190 to 500. The right half and the bottom 300 px are blank beige. Three things follow from that:
   - The frame tips up and to the left, with nothing on the right to balance it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
   - The empty area isn't shaped against anything, so it looks like leftover space rather than a deliberate field (Ruder, *Typography: A Manual of Design*, "Contrasts").
   - After the buttons the eye has nowhere to go.

   Most of all, a visitor is asked to "Get started" with a tool they haven't seen. Nothing shows a shared itinerary, flights and stays together, or a group agreeing. If an image or product shot was meant to sit there and didn't load, that explains the gap, so check the live page. Either way, the fix is the same: put a picture of the product in use in the right half. Ideally it shows one trip with several people's choices already settled, so it completes the headline instead of repeating it. If the page really is meant to be type only, commit to that. Scale the headline up to fill the width, or move the block down to the middle of the frame, so the empty space looks chosen.
2. **The headline could be anyone's (defect, from the copy).** "Plan the whole trip in one place" fits every travel app and every itinerary tool, so it gives the visitor no reason to pick this one. The thing that sets this product apart is in the subhead, set smaller and grey: "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads." That line names a pain the visitor already has and compares the product to the tool they use now, the group chat. That's the strongest argument on the page, and it's the quietest. Bring the group and the chat-thread contrast into the headline itself, said to the visitor, so their own messy group chat becomes the point of comparison.
3. **"Get started" doesn't say what happens (defect, from the copy).** The primary button is styled correctly: it's the only filled button, and "See pricing" sits clearly below it as an outline. But the label doesn't say what the visitor will do. Name the first concrete step in the visitor's terms, such as starting a trip and inviting the group, so the click feels like the first step of the plan rather than a sign-up form.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "Plan the whole trip in / one place", which leaves "in" hanging at the end of the first line. Break it as "Plan the whole trip / in one place" so each line is a whole phrase. Bringhurst's rule here covers numbers and units, not headings, so this is common sense.

**What's working.**
- The type scale is clear: headline, subhead, then nav and buttons, three sizes with big steps between them. The headline is heavy and tightly spaced, so there's never a question about where to start.
- The palette is small and consistent: warm off-white, near-black ink, and one muted green that appears only on the logo and the two buttons. Because the green is rare, it pulls the eye to the action.
- Everything is strictly flush left on one edge at x 80. The subhead line, at about 60 characters, is a comfortable length.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image failed to load on the right. Check the live page.
- How focus looks on the nav links and buttons.
