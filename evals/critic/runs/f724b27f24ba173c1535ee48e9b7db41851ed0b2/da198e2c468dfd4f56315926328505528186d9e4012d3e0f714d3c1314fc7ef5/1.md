I compared the two versions with the critic checklist. They are the same pricing section except for one thing: the grey of the plan descriptions and feature lists. In version 1 that text is a pale grey that falls short of the minimum contrast for readable text. In version 2 it is a dark slate grey that reads clearly. Version 2 does the job better.

**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Plans for every size of business", then drops to the three prices ($9, $24, $58), then to the one blue button on "Small team". It reads as a calm, standard SaaS pricing section for small-business accounting or invoicing software. In version 1 the cards look washed out below the prices, as if the details don't matter. In version 2 the same text reads as solid, usable information.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is a pricing section that helps small-business owners pick the plan that fits them and start a trial.

**Verdict.** Version 2 is better. The details in each card are what someone actually uses to choose a plan, and version 1 sets them too pale to read comfortably. Version 2 fixes that and changes nothing else. Version 2 does its job, and nothing large stands in its way.

**What changed, and why it matters**
1. **The description and feature text is too faint in version 1 (defect, WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3).** This is the one-line description under each price and all twelve feature rows ("Up to 20 invoices a month", "Payroll for up to five people", and so on). In version 1 they look like mid grey on white, roughly 3.5:1. Text at this size needs at least 4.5:1. This is the text that decides between plans: invoice limits, bank feeds, payroll headcount. Making it the faintest thing on the page makes the comparison harder for everyone and excludes readers with low vision. Version 2 darkens it to a slate grey that looks well above 4.5:1. The prices and plan names still clearly outrank it, so the hierarchy holds without the details fading away. Check the exact value with a contrast checker on the live colour.

**What would move it most** (for version 2)
No finding would noticeably change how well version 2 does its job.

**Smaller things.**
- "Start free trial" doesn't say how long the trial is or whether a card is needed. The button commits the viewer, so say it on or under the button (for example "Start 14-day free trial", if that is the term).
- "Talk to us" names no channel. "Book a call" or "Get a quote" would tell the viewer what happens when they click.
- There is about 60 px of empty space inside each card below the buttons, which makes the cards look slightly unfinished. Tighten the bottom padding to match the top.

**What's working.**
- The prices are large and bold, with "a month" small beside them. Scanning across $9 / $24 / $58 takes a single glance.
- There is one primary action. Only the middle plan gets the blue button, so the recommendation comes through without shouting.
- The plan names say who each plan is for ("Sole trader", "Small team", "Growing firm"), and the descriptions repeat that in plain words. A viewer can find themselves without reading the feature lists.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Make the recommended plan say it's recommended (taste). Right now only the button colour marks "Small team". A short label, or a slightly stronger border on that card, would tell an undecided buyer "most businesses pick this" instead of leaving them to read the colour as a hint.
2. Put the differences in the viewer's own terms (taste). "Three bank feeds" and "One bank feed" are facts. A line like "Connect up to three bank accounts" shows the owner what they get and makes the jump from $9 to $24 easier to justify.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the three buttons, and how the three cards stack on a phone.
