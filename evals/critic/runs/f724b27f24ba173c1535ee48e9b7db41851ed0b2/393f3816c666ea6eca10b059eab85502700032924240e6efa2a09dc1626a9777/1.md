I compared both screens by eye. They are small enough to see whole, so I didn't make crops. To save time I also skipped the full written checklist walk and didn't open the reference entries. The checklist line at the end is therefore partial.

**First read.** In both versions the eye lands on the serif greeting "Good morning, Dana", moves down to the two class rows, and stops on the dark "Book a class" button. It reads as the home screen of a yoga studio's booking app for a member who already has a pass. The mood is calm and warm. In version 1 the eye snags on the paragraph under the greeting, which is a solid block of capitals. In version 2 that paragraph reads at a glance.

**What it's for.** My assumption: it's a member's home screen. It should tell Dana what is left on their pass, explain the booking and cancellation rules, and get them to book one of today's classes.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The two screens are the same except for one change: version 1 sets the pass-and-policy paragraph in all capitals, and version 2 sets it in normal sentence case. That paragraph holds the terms Dana will be held to: six sessions, an expiry date, a two-hour booking window and an 8 pm cancellation cutoff. Capitals make it the hardest text on the screen to read. In version 2 it's the easiest.

**What would move it most**
1. **All-caps body text (version 1 only).** This is a convention break, and here it's a defect. The five-line paragraph under the greeting is in capitals. Text in capitals loses the shapes of words made by letters rising and dropping, so reading slows down. Readers also tend to see a capitals block as boilerplate and skip it (Butterick, *Practical Typography*: capitals are for less than one line of text). That's the wrong outcome for the paragraph that says when the pass expires and when Dana loses a session. It also shouts on a screen whose whole mood is quiet. Version 2 fixes this by setting it in sentence case. Keep it that way.

The findings below apply to version 2, the better one:

2. **The policy is buried in a sentence (defect).** "Six sessions left", "30 November" and "8 pm the night before" are the facts Dana will act on, and they sit inside a run-on paragraph with no emphasis. Bring the balance and expiry forward as a short status line (for example "6 sessions left · until 30 Nov"). Keep the cancellation rule as a separate, quieter line.

**Smaller things.**
- The large image near the top has no visible content. It's a flat beige gradient taking about a third of the screen above the greeting, and it pushes the classes and the button down. It's probably a placeholder or an image that didn't load (see below). If it's intentional, shrink it or give it a real photo.
- Class rows: "4 spaces" is set larger and further from the time than it needs to be. Without a verb ("4 spaces left") it could be read as the class size.
- "Book a class" doesn't connect to the two classes listed. Each row should be tappable to book that class, or the button should say what it leads to.

**What's working.**
- The serif greeting against the sans-serif text gives the screen a clear entry point and some warmth. Keep it.
- The palette is restrained: warm off-white background, near-black text and one dark button. It feels calm and still keeps strong contrast.
- The class rows have a tidy hierarchy: the name in bold, the time and room in regular, the spaces aligned to the right.

**Where it could go further.**
1. *Taste:* make Dana's own pass status the focus of the screen instead of the stock image. Members open this screen to check that and to book, so their own information should lead.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank gradient block is a photo that didn't load. Check it in a live build.
- What tapping a class row or the button does.
