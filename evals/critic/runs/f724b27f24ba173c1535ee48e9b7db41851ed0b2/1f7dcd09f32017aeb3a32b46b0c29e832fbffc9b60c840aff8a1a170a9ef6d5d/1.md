**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline "Savings deposits rose every month", drops to the tall June bar labelled "$1150k", then crosses to "$4.82M" at top right. It reads as a credit union's member report: calm, orderly and trustworthy, if a little generic. The first impression is "deposits nearly doubled."

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public report page that tells members, honestly and at a glance, how savings deposits did in the first half of 2026.

**Verdict.** The craft is solid and the chart is drawn honestly. The bars match the axis, and the six months add up exactly to the $4.82M total. The story it tells is not honest, though. The headline and chart suggest a surge, while the page's own card says the half was only 6.1% up on last year. Fix that, and this is a good page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline and chart oversell the growth, and the page contradicts itself.** The bars climb from $612k to $1150k, which looks like 88% growth in six months, and "rose every month" backs that up. The first card then says the half was "Up 6.1%" on 2025. So most of the climb is the usual seasonal pattern plus a one-off: the footnote admits "June includes the mid-year bonus payroll". That caveat sits in the smallest grey type on the page, under the chart, and it qualifies the most striking mark on the page, the June bar. A member who believes the chart comes away with the wrong idea. A member who notices the 6.1% starts to doubt the whole report, and for a credit union, trust is what the report is for. *Defect* (it misleads). No approved source in my references covers this; it's common sense about charts that only show a window. Fix direction:
   - Show last year's months as pale grey bars or ticks next to this year's, so the seasonal climb is visible as normal.
   - Put the bonus note on the June bar itself.
   - Rewrite the headline to say the actual finding, e.g. "Members saved 6.1% more than in the first half of 2025".

   "Rose every month" is true, but it is the wrong thing to lead with.
2. **The comparison lines on the cards are clipped.** "Up 6.1% on Jan to Jun 2025", "Up $11 on Jun 2025" and "Up 402 on Jun 2025" all drop the words that would make them read as sentences. "On" works as British shorthand for "compared with". This is a US-dollar credit union, though, and the line can also be read as "up on [the date] Jun 2025". The figures are the ones members will quote back, so they shouldn't take a second read. *Defect* (a minor one, but it sits on the numbers). Fix direction: "Up 6.1% from the same months in 2025" and "$11 more than in June 2025". Better still, say what it means for the member, such as "the average member who saved put away $11 more".

**Smaller things.**
- **Number formats don't match.** The chart uses "$1150k" and axis labels like "$1200k" with no thousands separator, while the card uses "$4.82M". Pick one format, e.g. "$1.15M" and "$1.2M" on the chart, or at least add separators: "$1,150k".

**What's working.**
- **Direct value labels on every bar, plus pale gridlines.** The data is the darkest thing in the chart, and no one has to read values off an axis. Keep this when you add the 2025 comparison (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 23.2).
- **Every card number comes with a comparison.** Each headline figure has its comparison line directly under it, coloured green and also stated in words, so colour isn't carrying the meaning alone.
- **A clean three-level type scale.** Serif headline, sans subhead, bold sans figures. Each level has an obvious job, and the serif headline gives the institution a voice without decoration.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the year-on-year comparison the main subject, not a footnote to it** (taste). Members care whether things are getting better, not about a seasonal curve they can't interpret. A paired-bar chart (2025 grey, 2026 blue) would make the 6.1% visible and believable. It would also give the page a real idea instead of a stock dashboard layout (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, on comparisons as the point of a graphic).
2. **Speak to the member** (taste). Cooperative ownership is what makes a credit union different from a bank. One line in the subhead, like "your deposits", "members saved", would set the page apart, where right now it could belong to any institution.
