**First read.** The eye lands on the bold title "Notes for ferry passengers" at top left, then drops into three centred paragraphs underneath it. It reads as a plain information page for people taking a river ferry. It feels calm and tidy, but a little off: the text sits as a column pushed to the left, the right ~40% of the frame is empty, and the centred paragraphs don't line up with the flush-left title above them.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a practical web page that a passenger scans to find out when the ferry runs, how long it takes, and what to do with a bike. Correct me if it's something else.

**Verdict.** It's clean and easy to read, but it doesn't do its job well. The few facts a passenger will act on are buried in centred prose and some are vague. The centring also gives the text no left edge to read from. Neither is a disaster, but together they make a simple reference page slower to use than it should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The facts people act on are hidden in sentences, and some are too vague to use (defect).** Frequency ("every forty minutes"), crossing time ("about twelve minutes"), service hours, the bike rule and the winter change all sit mid-sentence in paragraphs that look the same. A passenger checking "when's the next boat?" has to read all three paragraphs to find it. Some of the key lines can also be read more than one way:
   - "between early morning and late evening" gives no first or last sailing.
   - "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" doesn't say what time that is, and dusk moves by more than an hour across a winter.
   - "the quay" is unclear, because the page names two ends, the north landing and the market quay.

   If someone reads these wrongly, they miss the last boat. The fix has two parts:
   - Pull the numbers out so they can be scanned: first and last sailing, frequency, crossing time, and the winter timetable with real times or a pointer to where they're posted.
   - Name which landing the last winter boat leaves from.

   The title has the same problem on a smaller scale. "Notes for ferry passengers" names a category. Something like "Crossing times, bikes and winter service" tells the reader what they'll find.
2. **Centred paragraphs under a flush-left title (convention, and it costs something here).** All three paragraphs are four-line blocks centred on an axis around x≈440. The title starts flush left at x≈120.
   - **No fixed starting point.** Each line starts somewhere different, anywhere from about 125 px to 305 px, so the eye has to hunt for the start of every line.
   - **Wedge-shaped paragraphs.** The short last lines ("the tide turns against the boat.", "changes for the coming week.") turn the first and third paragraphs into wedges.
   - **Edges that nearly line up.** The longest paragraph lines start a few pixels to the right of the title's left edge. That reads as a near-miss, not a choice.

   Tschichold and Bringhurst (2.1.3) both keep centring for a few short lines. Fix: set the paragraphs flush left on the title's left edge, so the page has one strong left edge.
3. **The column is parked on the left with nothing balancing it (convention, moderate).** All the content sits in x≈120–760. The right ~500 px and the bottom third are empty, with no nav, image or sidebar there. It looks like a two-column layout whose second column didn't load, not like space left open on purpose (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). Fix: either centre the reading column in the frame with matching margins, or give the right side a job. That side is the natural home for the timetable summary from finding 1.

**Smaller things.**
- The paragraphs' left edges and the title's left edge come from no common grid. Once the text is flush left this goes away: put everything on one left edge (Müller-Brockmann).

**What's working.**
- **Type and colour:** one sans-serif family, two sizes, and dark text on white, with high contrast throughout.
- **Size steps:** the bold title is clearly a step above the body, set by both size and weight, so there's never any doubt where to start. Keep this restraint.
- **Line length and spacing:** body lines run about 60–65 characters with generous leading, which is comfortable to read.
- **Paragraph spacing:** paragraphs are separated by space alone, with no indents, and each covers one topic (times, bikes, winter). That split is the right starting point for the scannable version.
