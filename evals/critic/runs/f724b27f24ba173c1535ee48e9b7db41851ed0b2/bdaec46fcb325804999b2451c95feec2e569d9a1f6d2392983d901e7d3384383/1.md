**First read.** In version 1 the eye goes to the wide warm-grey block on the left (the library), then down to "Coldharbour Lane library", then across to the two narrower projects. The big light "Recent work" heading frames all of it. It reads as a calm, confident portfolio home page for an architecture practice. In version 2 the eye has nowhere to land. Three equal blocks sit side by side, and the small "Recent work" heading is about the size of the project titles, so the page reads as an evenly spaced list rather than a showcase.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home or work page of a small architecture studio. Its job is to show prospective clients the practice's best recent buildings and get them to open a project.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. It picks a lead project and makes it twice as wide, and it gives the page a heading level that sits clearly above the project titles. Version 2 takes away both, which leaves three equal tiles and two text sizes that nearly match. Version 1 still has one weakness: the biggest type on the page is a generic label rather than anything about the work.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Grid.** V1 is 2 + 1 + 1 (the library spans two columns). V2 is three equal columns. In V1 one element clearly dominates and the rest follow, which is Tschichold's ordering by differences of size and Hofmann's "much against little". In V2 three blocks of the same size and strength compete to be seen first. This is a **defect** in V2: the studio's lead project gets no more weight than a canopy, and the visitor gets no signal about what the practice is proudest of.
- **Heading.** In V1 "Recent work" is about 56 px in a light weight, a clear level above the roughly 22 px project titles. In V2 it shrinks to about 22 px, the same size as the titles, so the page heading and the card titles read as one level. That collision is the kind Bringhurst warns against ("Don't compose without a scale", 3.1.1). This is a **defect** in V2: the page loses its top and the hierarchy flattens to two tiers.

**What would move it most (version 1)**
1. **The largest type on the page is a label, not the work** (taste). "Recent work" at about 56 px is the strongest text on the screen, but it tells a visitor nothing about this practice. Meanwhile "Coldharbour Lane library", the project you've chosen to lead with, is set the same size as the two minor titles. The visitor's first verbal read is a category name. Give the lead project's title more presence than the other two titles so the text hierarchy matches the image hierarchy. Then either quiet the page heading or make it say something only this studio could say.

**Smaller things.**
- All three images are flat colour fills in both versions. If that's the shipped state, the page shows no buildings, and an architecture portfolio with no buildings can't do its job. They need real photographs before either version can be judged on its main content.

**What's working.**
- The double-width lead image in V1. It's the one decision that gives the page a focal point. Keep it.
- A restrained, consistent caption system: a title, then one grey line of type, size, and year, set at the same distance under each image. It's quiet and easy to scan, and it suits the practice's tone.
- A sober palette of warm off-white and muted earth tones with dark grey type. It's one mood that fits architecture and lets the photographs carry the colour.

**Where it could go further**
1. Commit harder to scale contrast. The lead image could take the full width or run deeper than the other two, so the first screen says "this is our building" rather than "here are three". This is taste, after Hofmann's much-against-little contrast.
2. Use the empty band at the bottom of the first screen. About 190 px of nothing sits under the captions. Let the grid start lower with a bigger lead image, or show the top of the next row so visitors know there's more to scroll. This is taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the flat image fills are placeholders or photos that hadn't loaded. Check in a live browser.
- The size of the click targets on the nav links. They look small, so measure them against 24×24 px (WCAG 2.5.8).
