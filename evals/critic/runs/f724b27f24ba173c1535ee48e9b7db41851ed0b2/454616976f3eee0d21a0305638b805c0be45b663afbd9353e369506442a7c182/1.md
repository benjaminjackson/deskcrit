**First read.** My eye goes to "Revenue overview", drops to the large $4.82M, and then gets pulled down into the block of blue bars, which takes up about half the page. It reads as an internal SaaS finance dashboard. It's calm and competent, with no surprises. Nothing tells me where to look once I'm past the KPIs.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing screen of a billing or analytics product, where a founder or finance lead checks how revenue is doing this year and trusts the numbers enough to repeat them.

**Verdict.** It's clean and mostly well made, but it fails the one thing a finance screen can't fail: the numbers don't add up. The plan table claims to show 2025 revenue but repeats the ARR figure, while the chart's own months add up to roughly $330K less. Next to that, the layout issues are minor.

**What would move it most**
1. **The plan table and the chart disagree, and the label hides why** (defect). The right-hand table is headed "Revenue by plan, 2025" and totals **$4.82M**. That's exactly the ARR in the first card ("Annual recurring revenue, Dec 2025"). Read by eye, the twelve bars in "Monthly revenue, 2025 … recognised revenue" add up to about **$4.49M**. A reader who checks (and finance readers do) finds two different "2025 revenue" figures on one screen. They can't tell which to quote, and they'll start doubting every other number. Most likely the table is ARR by plan with the wrong heading. Make the heading say what the table holds (for example, "ARR by plan, Dec 2025"). If it really is 2025 revenue, make it match the chart. Either way, every figure on this screen should state its basis: ARR, recognised revenue, or bookings. This is the heading-describes-content rule (WCAG 2.4.6), but here the cost is trust, not navigation.
2. **The biggest element on the page says the least** (taste, common sense). The chart takes up half the screen, but its title, "Monthly revenue, 2025", only names a topic. The reader has to work out the story themselves: steady growth from about $310K to about $447K, roughly +43% over the year, with small dips in April and July. Nothing in the chart compares the year with anything, either: no 2024 and no target. The KPI cards above it each have a comparison, so the chart is the odd one out. Put the finding in the title or subtitle, and either add a muted 2024 series or plan line or annotate the two dips. That turns half the screen from decoration into an answer.

**Smaller things.**
- The lower row ignores the three-card grid. The chart ends at about x960 and the table starts at about x1012, but the cards break at about 907/925. Put the chart/table split on the card columns (two cards wide plus one) so the page reads as one grid (Müller-Brockmann).
- The two section headings miss each other's baseline by a few pixels: "Monthly revenue, 2025" sits at about y243 and "Revenue by plan, 2025" at about y247. Put them on one baseline.
- The navigation has both "Overview" (selected) and "Revenue", but this page is titled "Revenue overview". That blurs what the Revenue page is for. Either call this page "Overview" or make clear what the Revenue page adds (Norman, conceptual model).
- The Total row in the plan table has the same weight and spacing as the plan rows. A heavier rule above it, or a bold label, would set the sum apart from what it sums (taste).

**What's working.**
- Every KPI card gives a metric, a period, and a comparison ("+18% vs Dec 2024", "+3 pts vs 2024"). This is exactly what Few asks of headline numbers. Keep it.
- The chart is honest and quiet: one hue, zero baseline, faint gridlines, units in the subtitle ("Thousands of US dollars"), and a source line naming "closed invoices only". That's good data-ink discipline (Tufte).
- The type scale is tight and clear. There are about four levels: page title, KPI figures, section headings, and body/labels, with grey for secondary text. Hierarchy is carried by weight and colour, not extra sizes.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Give ARR clear priority (taste). For a revenue overview, ARR is the number people come for. Right now it's one of three equal cards. Making it visibly first, for example by giving it a wider card or a larger figure, would tell a returning user where to look before they've read a label.
2. Own one accent (taste). The only colours are default blue and status green, so this could be any SaaS product. One brand colour used only on the current-period data (December's bar, the ARR figure) would make the screen recognisably Ledgerly's and point the eye at "now" at the same time.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the "Jan to Dec 2025" pill is a date-range control or a static label. If it's a control, it needs a visible cue such as a chevron.
- Focus states in the sidebar.
