**What would move it most**
1. **Remove the "Design review: approved…" line, or set it clearly apart.** At about y=613 it uses the same face, size, weight and colour as the paragraphs, and it is placed right under "Watering". So the reader takes it as the article's last paragraph. They read it looking for gardening advice, get a comment on the page's styling, and the article ends on a note that undercuts its own authority. *Defect.* Every element should serve the reader's task (Nielsen, heuristic 8). Things that look alike are read as one group (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*), and this line looks like the article. If it's an internal note, delete it. If it has to stay, take it out of the article's styling and its position. Whatever it says about the design is content, not evidence, and I haven't counted it as either.
2. **Headings differ from the text only in size.** "Preparing the soil", "Choosing what to plant" and "Watering" use the same light-to-regular weight and the same near-black as the body text. They are just larger, at roughly 32 px against 18 px. They read as somewhat bigger text rather than signposts. A reader scanning back for "how often do I water?" has to compare sizes instead of seeing an obvious marker. The same flat weight makes the title look pale for its size. *Convention:* Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9, "multiplying the contrasts". The approved fix is to pair a smaller size step with a change of weight, of form, or of colour. A semibold or medium heading at a slightly smaller size would mark the sections more clearly than the current large light ones.
3. **The text lines are too long.** The paragraphs run about 90 characters a line, for example "Dig to the depth of a spade and mix in compost from last year's heap. Rake the surface level". That is well past the 45–75 range and the 66 ideal (Bringhurst, §2.1.2). On the two-line paragraphs the cost is small. Once the article grows past a few sentences per section, the eye will lose its place on the way back to the next line. *Convention.* Narrow the text column to about 65 characters, which is about 600–640 px at this size. You can leave the title wider. Measure it in the browser to confirm.
