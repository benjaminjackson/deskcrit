**First read.** My eye goes to the three bold numbers in the top row (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%), reads the green "+%" under each, then drops to the big line chart. The chart takes up most of the screen, but once I'm there I just see three lines going up. It reads as a marketing analytics overview for a small team, and it feels calm, tidy and familiar. It's also anonymous: it could be any analytics product's default screen.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard where a marketer or founder checks traffic and sign-ups for the last 12 weeks and decides which channel needs attention.

**Verdict.** It's clean and readable, and the numbers are easy to find. But the chart's figures don't add up to the headline figure, and the chart never says what it shows. A careful viewer will stop trusting it, and a quick one will leave with nothing more than "things went up".

**What would move it most**
1. **The channel chart doesn't add up to the Visits number, and it doesn't give its unit.** This is a defect. If you read the three lines off the 0–8k axis week by week, Search totals about 71k, Social about 44k and Email about 23k, so roughly 138k. The Visits card directly above says 184,220. About a quarter of the visits are missing from a chart titled "Visits by channel". Either some channel (Direct, Referral, Other) was left out, or the numbers disagree. Nothing on the screen tells the viewer which. The y-axis also never says these are visits *per week*, while every number above it is a 12-week total. A stranger has to work out the unit, and anyone who checks the sum will find the gap. The rule is that a reader should never have to guess what a number measures (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). The fix: show every channel, with a grey "Other" line if needed, or retitle it "Top 3 channels" and state the share they cover. Also put the unit on the chart, for example "Weekly visits by channel".
2. **The chart makes the viewer decode it and draw their own conclusion.** This is part convention, part taste. The series are named only in a legend above the plot, so to know which line is which you look back and forth between the coloured squares and the lines. The labels rely on colour alone, and the orange and green lines may be hard to tell apart for viewers with red-green colour blindness (WCAG 1.4.1). Labels at the right end of each line would fix both problems; there is room if the plot ends a little earlier (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). Separately, the title "Visits by channel" names the topic, not the finding. The finding is in the data: Search grew steadily from about 5k to 6.8k a week, Social is noisy, and Email is small but rising. That's taste, since no approved source covers it. Still, a title that states the finding ("Search drives most of the growth") gives the viewer the conclusion the chart exists to support, so they don't have to work it out themselves.

**Smaller things.**
- **Type scale is muddled.** I count about eight sizes. "Overview" (~24 px) and the KPI figures (~27 px) are almost the same size. The brand name (~18 px) and the chart title (~17 px) nearly match. The labels, deltas and axis text sit at ~14, ~13 and ~12 px. Cut it to four levels and separate the small sizes by weight or colour instead (Bringhurst; Refactoring UI).
- **Near-miss inset.** The text inside the KPI cards starts about 21 px in, but "Visits by channel" starts about 23 px in, so the left edges just miss each other. Use one inset for every card.
- **"Last 12 weeks" in the top right.** If it's a date-range control, it looks like a plain label: it has no border, caret or underline. Give it the look of a control. If it's static text, this is fine.

**What's working.**
- Every KPI comes with a comparison ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"), so each number arrives with its context. The conversion rate uses "+0.1 pts" rather than a misleading percentage-of-a-percentage. Keep both.
- The chart's gridlines are very faint, it has no fills or shadows, and line is the right form for a weekly trend. Almost all the ink is data.
- The three KPI cards are equal in weight, and that's the right call here: three peer metrics read as a row. Above them the arithmetic checks out: 3,912 / 184,220 = 2.12%.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the top row a funnel, not three separate cards.** This is taste. The point of this screen is visits turning into sign-ups. Showing Visits → Sign-ups → Conversion as connected steps would make that relationship visible, and the deltas would read as a story ("more traffic, same conversion") rather than three green ticks.
2. **Mark the previous period on the chart.** This is taste. Every card compares against "previous 12 weeks", but the chart shows only the current 12. A faint grey line for the previous period behind each channel would let the viewer see where the +6.1% came from, using a comparison the page already relies on.
3. **Give the product one thing of its own.** This is taste. Right now Brightpath could be any analytics tool's default screen. One owned accent colour, or a short summary sentence at the top in place of "Overview", would make it a screen with a point of view rather than a template.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** I couldn't tell whether "Last 12 weeks" opens a picker, or whether the lines have hover tooltips. Tooltips would partly ease the legend problem, but they wouldn't fix the unit or the totals.
