**First read.** The eye lands on six equal coloured blocks, not on the title. It scans across them like a paint-chip card and never settles anywhere. It reads as a gallery template for a garden show. The mood is cheerful and orderly, but there is nothing to look at: every "picture" is a flat colour with a pale circle in it.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web gallery page: it should let visitors to a garden show browse the displays and pick one to look at more closely.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. A gallery is made of its pictures, and here all six are placeholders. On top of that, the page has no focal point, even though its own footer line says it has one.

**What would move it most**
1. **There are no pictures (defect, common sense).** All six tiles in the 3×2 grid are a solid colour with a centred pale circle. That reads as a placeholder or as images that never loaded. The captions promise climbing roses, thyme, reeds and plum trees, and the visitor sees none of them. The page has nothing to offer until real photos are in. If this is a capture problem (lazy-loaded images that failed), check it in a live browser. As shown, it fails. The fix is real photography, cropped to one ratio and one grade so the set holds together. The photos will also replace the six unrelated saturated hues, which only work as stand-ins.
2. **Nothing leads, and the footer says something does (defect).** Six identical tiles have the same size, weight and colour strength, so no tile is seen first (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*). The line under the grid, "The large feature tile gives this gallery a clear focal point.", describes a tile that doesn't exist. Either the feature tile was dropped from the layout, or the note is a design comment that ended up as page copy. Either way, a visitor reads a sentence about the page that is false. Decide which display should lead, perhaps the show's highlight or the newest one, and make its tile clearly bigger, for example two columns wide in the first row. Delete the footer sentence whatever you choose: it is a note to the designer, not to the visitor.
3. **The Rose arch caption is out of line (defect).** In the first row, the "Rose arch" title and its caption sit about 2–4 px higher than "Herb bed" and "Pond edge". The caption also sits nearer its title than the other two captions do. The baselines don't run straight across the row, and the near-miss reads as a mistake, not a choice (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*). That tile is breaking the pattern the others set (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*). Give every card the same title and caption spacing so the baselines line up across the row.

**Smaller things.**
- The captions are only labels ("Plum and pear trees."). They don't say where the display is or why to visit it. If the gallery is meant to send people to displays, add a location or one specific hook, such as the stand number or "east gate".
- The pale circles sit exactly on the bottom edge of each tile, which looks accidental. This goes away when real photos replace them.

**What's working.**
- The grid is sound: three equal columns with even gutters, and a clean left edge shared by the title, tiles and captions. Keep it when the feature tile is added.
- The spacing groups things properly. Each caption sits tight under its tile and the gap between rows is generous, so each card reads as one unit.
- The type hierarchy is clear: a bold title, bold card names, and grey captions in the same sans. That gives three distinct levels with no near-duplicate sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the tiles are links, and what their hover and focus states look like.
- Whether the placeholder tiles are images that failed to load. Check in a live browser.

Repeated elements consistent: counted in the whole piece above, as 1 of its 4 no (Rose arch card)
What matters first: 3 lines, 2 yes, 1 no (one element dominates)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (near-miss alignment; baselines across columns)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
