**First read.** The eye lands on "River ferry timetable", drops to the header row, then runs down the left column of Morning / Midday / Afternoon. It reads as a plain, tidy web timetable for one ferry route. It feels calm and trustworthy, a bit like a spreadsheet, and it doesn't help you find your boat.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a public web page where a rider checks which ferry they can catch from North landing on a given day and when it reaches the quay.

**Verdict.** It's clean and legible, but it's set up for reading every cell rather than for answering "which boat can I take today?" The days each boat runs are the facts most likely to strand someone, and they're the hardest thing on the page to see.

**What would move it most**
1. **The day exceptions are hidden in plain sight.** Defect. In the Runs column on the far right, "Weekdays" (12:40, 17:10) and "Weekends" (19:30) are set in the same weight and colour as the four "Daily" entries. A Saturday rider reading the times on the left has nothing telling them to check the far column. If they don't, they plan around the 12:40 or 17:10, which won't run, or they never see that the only evening boat is weekend-only. The fix: make "Daily" the quiet default and make the exceptions stand out. You could drop "Daily" to the grey of the subtitle, or leave those cells empty and note "Runs daily unless marked", and set the exceptions in bold or with a marker right beside the departure time. A Weekday / Weekend toggle or split would also work.
2. **The table is stretched across the full 1080 px for four short columns.** Defect. The gap between "06:40" and "06:52" is about 300 px, and "Daily" sits another 240 px further right. The departure time and arrival time are one fact (this boat) but sit as far apart as unrelated columns, and the eye has to carry each row across empty space to reach Runs. That's where a rider misreads a row on a phone or at a glance. Things that belong together should sit closer together than things that don't (Arnheim, on proximity). Size the columns to their content and keep Leaves, Arrives and Runs close together, so each row reads as one line.
3. **"Spring season" has no dates.** Defect. The subtitle is the only thing that says whether this timetable is in force today, and it gives no dates and no year. A rider can't tell whether this is the current timetable or one from before a change, and they'll hold the operator to whatever they read. Give the dates it covers, for example "Spring timetable, 1 March to 31 May 2026".

**Smaller things.**
- The times use proportional figures: "11:00" and "11:12" are visibly narrower than "06:40" and "12:52", so the colons don't line up down the columns. Switch to tabular figures (Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 4.4.3).
- The Period column repeats what a 24-hour time already says, and "Morning" and "Midday" each appear twice. Common sense: either drop it, or turn it into group subheads that span the rows, which also frees width for finding 2.

**What's working.**
- The row rules are thin and pale, and the header rule is only slightly heavier. The structure is there without a cage of lines, which is exactly how a table should be ruled (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*). Keep that.
- The type hierarchy is quiet and clear: one bold title, a grey subtitle, a bold header row and regular cells. You know where to start and what each level does, using weight and colour rather than extra sizes.
- The header labels "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" are plain-language and specific to the direction. Keep that wording when you add the return trip.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the four columns reflow on a phone. At this width the table will either squeeze or scroll sideways, and the Runs column is the one most likely to fall off-screen, which would make finding 1 worse. Check this in a live narrow viewport.
