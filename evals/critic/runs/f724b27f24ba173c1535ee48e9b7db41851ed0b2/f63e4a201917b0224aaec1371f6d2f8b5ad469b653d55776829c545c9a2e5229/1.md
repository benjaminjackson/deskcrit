**First read.** The eye lands on the black headline, "Hold a real conversation in ten weeks", and then gets pulled three ways: the orange button, the purple block right under it, and the big teal panel. It reads as a landing page for a language-learning app aimed at adults starting out. It feels clean and friendly but templated. The purple "Keep your streak going" block looks like a second button and speaks to someone who has already signed up.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the top of a marketing landing page, and its job is to get a first-time visitor to start the free week.

**Verdict.** The headline and the button do their jobs, but the piece doesn't close the sale. Three strong colours split the attention. The block right under the button speaks to the wrong person. The one image adds nothing to the promise. The biggest problem is the purple streak card: it competes with the call to action and confuses a new visitor at the exact moment they should click.

**What would move it most**
1. **The purple streak card is the wrong message, styled as a second button.** *Defect.*
   - It sits 28 px under "Start your free week" and copies the button's language: a solid fill, the same rounded corners, white bold text. So it reads as a second action, but clicking it does nothing.
   - Its message is for retention ("Keep your streak going"), but this visitor has no streak. They have to stop and work out whether it's meant for them, right where they should be clicking.
   - One view should have one dominant filled action and nothing at the same weight next to it (Material Design 3, Apple HIG, NN/g on button emphasis).
   - Fix: cut the card. Or turn the fact into proof aimed at a newcomer, something like "Five minutes a day is enough. Learners who practice five days a week finish twice as often." Set it as plain neutral text, not a filled block.
2. **Three unrelated loud colours, so no single colour means "act here".** *Convention.*
   - The page uses orange (the button), purple (the card), and teal (a 400×300 panel), all at full strength. That splits attention and leaves the piece without one colour it owns.
   - The teal panel is the biggest coloured area on the page, so it pulls more of the eye than the button does.
   - Refactoring UI and Material's colour roles call for one or two brand hues on a neutral ramp. Apple's HIG says to keep the accent for the main action.
   - Fix: keep orange for the action and at most one highlight. Move the panel to a neutral or a pale tint, and drop the purple.
3. **The picture just repeats the headline, and it sits off the grid.** *Taste, plus a convention for the grid.*
   - Two speech bubbles say "conversation", which the headline already says. The page's real difference is the claim "practice with a tutor that listens" and "feedback on each word". Nothing on screen shows it, so the viewer has to take it on trust.
   - The panel also stops at about x = 1076. The nav and the third step column run to 1224, which leaves a dead 150 px strip on the right. The hero's two-way split also doesn't line up with the three columns below (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
   - Fix: replace the icon with the product doing the thing, such as a lesson screen marking which words you said well. Run the panel to the right margin on the same columns as the steps.

**Smaller things.**
- The orange button: white 18 px bold on that orange looks like about 4.2:1. Bold text counts as large only from 18.66 px, so it needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3). Measure it, then darken the orange slightly or make the label larger.
- "In ten weeks" is a promise people will hold you to, but it has no reference point. At how many minutes a day, and what counts as a "real conversation"? Put the effort next to the promise ("10 minutes a day").
- "A tutor that listens" can be read as a person or as software. Visitors who assume a human will expect a different price and a different experience. Say which it is.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes a concrete outcome promise, and its size and weight make it the clear first read. Keep that dominance when you calm the colour down.
- "Start your free week" names an exact, low-risk action, and it echoes the "ten weeks" framing.
- The 1-2-3 strip (Listen, Speak, Use it) is clear, evenly spaced, on a real three-column grid, and makes the method understandable in five seconds.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- The button's hover and focus states.
- Whether the empty band at the bottom is the end of the viewport or a real gap in the page.
