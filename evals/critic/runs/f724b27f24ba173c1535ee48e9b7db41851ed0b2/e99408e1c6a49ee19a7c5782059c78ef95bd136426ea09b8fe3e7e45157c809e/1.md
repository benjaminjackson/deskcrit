**First read.** The eye lands on the title, then gets caught by the grid. The black 2px border around every cell is the loudest thing on the page, louder than the times it holds. It reads as a plain, competent timetable for one ferry crossing, but it feels like a form to fill in more than something to glance at on the way to the landing. The times are spread so far across the width that I read each column top to bottom instead of reading each sailing across.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a public timetable (web page or printed notice) that lets a rider find the next sailing from north landing on the day they're travelling, and when it arrives.

**Verdict.** The content is right, and a table is the right form for it. But the presentation gets in the way of the one question a rider asks, "which boat can I catch today?" The heavy grid, the stretched columns and the hidden weekday/weekend rules make the rider dig for an answer that should take a glance.

**What would move it most**

1. **The grid is louder than the timetable (defect, convention).** Every cell in the table sits inside a near-black border about 2px thick, the same darkness as the times themselves. Your eye stops at every line, and the page reads as a cage of boxes rather than a list of sailings. Lupton puts it exactly: the rules in a data table "should illuminate the relationships among data, not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell" (*Thinking with Type*, p. 157). Tufte and Wilke make the same point about non-data ink. Fix: take out the vertical rules completely. Mark the rows with hairline pale-grey lines or a faint alternating tint, and keep at most one firmer line under the header row. The times should be the darkest thing in the table.

2. **The table is stretched until the rows break apart, and the one fact that can strand someone is buried (defect).** Each cell holds about 5 to 9 characters, but the table spans about 1080px. That leaves about 250px between "06:40" and "06:52" while rows are only about 57px apart. The space groups things vertically, so the columns hold together and each sailing falls apart (Arnheim on grouping by proximity). Most of the space is empty cell interior rather than open space with a purpose (Ruder). Worse, the "Runs" column sets "Weekdays" and "Weekends" in exactly the same weight as "Daily". So someone on a Saturday has to read every row to learn that the 12:40 and 17:10 sailings won't come and that only the evening boat is weekend-only. Fix: narrow the table to fit what's in it, and keep "Leaves north" first and strongest, since that's the column people scan. Make the exceptions stand out against "Daily", for example by setting the exceptions in bold or giving "Daily" a quieter tone, or by splitting the times into weekday and weekend lists. The Period column repeats "Morning", "Midday" and "Afternoon" in pairs and adds nothing the times don't already say. Either drop it or turn the periods into group labels that span their rows. That gives the space back to the times.

3. **"Spring season" has no dates (defect, copy).** The subtitle tells a rider this timetable is valid for a season, but not when that season starts or ends. A rider standing at the landing in late May or early June can't tell whether these times still apply, and dates are exactly what people hold a timetable to. Fix: state the date range ("1 March to 31 May") in the subtitle. Also say whether the return crossing is printed elsewhere: the page only covers north to quay, and someone wanting the trip home has no pointer to it.

**Smaller things.**
- The times use proportional figures: "11:00", "11:12" and "17:10" are visibly narrower than "06:40", so the colons don't line up down the columns. Turn on tabular figures so each column reads as one aligned block (Bringhurst 4.4.3).

**What's working.**
- Type scale: a bold title, a smaller grey subtitle, bold column headers and regular body text. That's four distinct levels with nothing that nearly collides, all in one sans family. Keep it.
- The column headers name what happens, not a category. "Leaves north" and "Arrives quay" tell the rider the direction and the event in two words each. Keep that wording.
- 24-hour times in chronological order, one sailing per row. That's the correct structure; the fixes above are about presentation, not about the data.
