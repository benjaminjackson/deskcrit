**First read.** Both versions open on "Before you sign a tenancy," then work down three short sections. In version 1 the bold, near-black headings stand out from the grey body text, so you can see all three tips (deposit, photos, notice) in a second. In version 2 the headings are regular weight and the same slate grey as the body. The page reads as one even column of text, and you have to read the copy to find where each tip starts.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a short guidance page for renters about to sign a tenancy. It needs to be skimmed: a reader should be able to pick out the three checks and remember them.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only difference is heading weight and colour. Version 1 gives a reader the three checks at a glance. Version 2 takes away the one signal that makes the page skimmable.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's headings differ from the body only in size** (convention, and here a real defect). "Check the deposit," "Take photos on day one" and "Know your notice period" are about 24px regular in the same slate as the 16px body. In version 1 they are bold and darker. Without the weight and colour change, the headings look like large paragraph text and the three-step structure disappears into one grey block. Someone skimming before signing can no longer pick out the three checks. Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8–9) says a heading should differ from the text in more than size, through weight, form or colour. Version 1 already does this: keep it. The H1 in version 2 suffers the same way: it still leads on size, but it's lighter and greyer, so it feels quieter than a page title should.

**Smaller things**
- The paragraph lines in both versions run to about 85–88 characters, for example "A tenancy is a contract … explain any." That's above Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column by about 15%.

**What's working** (version 1)
- Bold, near-black headings over grey body text. This is the change that makes the page skimmable.
- Each heading sits close to its own paragraph and well away from the one above, so the three tips read as separate units.
- The copy is plain and specific ("within thirty days," "one month from you and two from the landlord"), and every heading tells the reader what to do.

**Where it could go further**
1. Number the three checks, or set them as a short checklist (taste). The page is a list of things to do before signing, and a number makes "three things" easy to remember and come back to on the day.
2. Make the one hard deadline stand out (taste). "Within thirty days" is the line a renter could lose money over. A bit of weight on that phrase would make sure a skimming reader catches it.
