I've finished the critique. The piece is a garden centre homepage with no way to act on it, so that's the headline finding.

**First read.** The eye lands on the white headline over the dark leaf pattern, drops to the three cards, then looks up at the nav for somewhere to go. It reads as the homepage of a local garden centre. It feels calm and tidy, but it could be almost any plant shop's template. And nothing on it asks you to do anything.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a local garden centre's homepage, meant to get nearby people to come in, or to shop or book the shade plants and workshops it features.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't do its job yet. It makes a clear, specific offer ("ready to pot this weekend") and then gives the visitor no way to act on it. There isn't a single button, and the eye stops at the bottom of the three cards. That missing next step matters far more than anything else here.

**What would move it most**

1. **There is no ask anywhere on the page.** *(Defect.)* The headline offers "Plants for shade and small spaces" and the subhead says they're ready to pot this weekend, but there's nothing to click: no "See shade plants", no "Book a workshop", no directions. The workshops card says there are "ten places in each class", which makes you want a spot, then gives no date, no price and no way to book. The same problem shows up two ways:
   - Nothing on the page is styled as the main action, against the usual guidance on button emphasis (Material Design, Apple HIG).
   - The eye path dead-ends at the cards.

   Fix: put one filled button under the headline and subhead, naming the exact thing, such as "Shop shade plants" or "See what's ready this weekend". Give the workshops card its next Saturday date and a "Book a place" link. A visitor who arrives wanting ferns should be one click from them.

2. **The hero image doesn't show what the headline promises.** *(Taste, but it costs something.)* The headline names shade and small spaces, and the subhead names ferns, hostas and compact shrubs. The picture is a flat pattern of generic oval leaves and dots, with no fern, no hosta, no shady corner and no balcony. The picture and the words don't add up to one idea, so the largest area on the page tells the visitor nothing about this nursery. That's why the page looks templated: a nursery that grows its own plants has a real advantage here, and the hero hides it. Fix: use a real photo of the shade stock, ideally in a small, shady space like a north-facing balcony or a narrow side yard. Keep it dark enough at the headline, or add a scrim, so the white type still holds.

3. **The headline is cut off from its subhead and sits off the grid.** *(Convention.)* There are two problems at the same spot:
   - **Grouping:** The headline is inside the hero box and its subhead ("Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs…") is outside it, on white. The subhead sits closer to the cards than to the line it explains, so it reads as the intro to the cards, not the second half of the headline. This breaks common region and proximity (Palmer; Arnheim on proximity).
   - **Alignment:** The headline's left edge sits about 16 px to the right of the logo, subhead and cards (roughly 72 px against 56 px). That's too small to look deliberate and big enough to look like a mistake (Müller-Brockmann on one grid; Arnheim on near-misses reading as unfinished).

   Fix: move the subhead and the new button into the hero under the headline. Set the headline on the same left edge as everything else.

**Smaller things.**
- In the opening hours card, "until 8 / pm on Thursdays" splits the number from its unit across lines. Use a non-breaking space ("8 pm") so a time someone acts on reads at once (Bringhurst 2.4.6).
- The headline breaks as "small / spaces", splitting the phrase. Break it as "Plants for shade / and small spaces", or widen the measure so it holds on one line.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific where it counts.** "Grown at our nursery", "ready to pot this weekend", "Free within ten miles on orders over $60" and "until 8 pm on Thursdays" are concrete and checkable, and they're what makes this business different. Keep that voice when you write the button and the workshop details.
- **The type plan is clean.** A green serif wordmark, one sans for everything else, and a clear scale (headline, then subhead, then bold card headings, then body), with each step obvious. Don't add sizes.
- **The three cards are well judged.** They hold equal content at equal weight, on equal gutters, with quiet borders that group without shouting. Hours, workshops and delivery are what a local visitor wants second.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards or nav items have hover and focus states.
- How the hero and headline reflow on a phone, where the "small / spaces" break and the headline's position over the pattern will change.
