**First read.** The eye lands on "Plan the whole trip in one place", drops to the grey subhead, then stops at two green buttons and has to decide between them. Then it drifts right into a large empty cream field and finds nothing. It reads as a SaaS landing page for a trip planner. It feels calm and tidy, and anonymous.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the first screen of a consumer landing page. It should make people who plan group trips understand Wayfold and start using it.

**Verdict.** The type and spacing are clean, but the first screen doesn't do its job yet. The visitor is told about the product and never sees it, the headline could belong to any travel app, and the call to action is offered twice under two names. The biggest problem is the empty right half. It's the space where the visitor should see what "one place" looks like.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half of the screen is empty, so the product is never shown.** Everything sits in the left 55% of the frame, upper middle. The right half and the bottom third are bare cream. If a hero image failed to load, this is a capture problem: check it in a live browser. As designed, though, the visitor has to imagine "flights, stays, and day plans together" on their own. Nothing on screen shows that the group-chat mess goes away, so the claim rests on trust. The page also tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The empty space is not a deliberate counterform set against something dense (Ruder). It's just what's left over. *Defect.* Fix: fill the right side with the thing itself, such as one shared trip itinerary with a flight, a stay, and a day plan, ideally with a couple of group members' votes or comments. The picture then finishes the headline's sentence instead of decorating it.
2. **Two buttons, one action.** "Get started" (filled) and "Start now" (outlined) are plain synonyms placed side by side. The visitor stops to work out what the difference is, and there isn't one. The duplicate also weakens the primary action (Nielsen heuristic 8; Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*). Neither label says what actually happens: does it open a sign-up, a new trip, or an invite? *Defect.* Fix: keep one primary and give it a concrete label tied to the viewer's own situation, such as "Start planning your trip". Then either drop the second button or give it a different job, such as "See a sample trip" or "How it works". A sample trip would also let the viewer judge the product on terms they already know: their own group chat.
3. **The headline could sit on anyone's page.** "Plan the whole trip in one place" is the default promise of every travel app, and it says nothing about groups. The real angle is buried in the subhead: "so a group can agree on a trip without a dozen chat threads". That line names the viewer's actual pain, but it's set in mid-grey at a fifth of the headline's size, and the scanning eye skips it. *Defect (copy).* Fix: move the group-agreement idea into the headline and speak to the viewer, for example something about getting your group to agree without the chat chaos. Let the subhead carry the "flights, stays, day plans" detail.

**Smaller things.**
- The headline's first line ends on "in", which splits the phrase "in one place". Break it as "Plan the whole trip / in one place", or rewrite it per finding 3 (Bringhurst §2.4; this is mostly taste).

**What's working.**
- There is one strong left edge at x≈80 that the logo, headline, subhead, and buttons all hang on. The structure reads instantly. Keep it when you add the image.
- The type scale is clear: a big, heavy, tightly set headline; a lighter grey subhead with a comfortable line length (~70 characters); small nav. Each size has an obvious job.
- The palette is restrained: deep teal on warm cream, with the teal kept to the logo and buttons, so the action colour means something.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image or animation should fill the right half and failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- Focus indicators on the nav links and buttons.
