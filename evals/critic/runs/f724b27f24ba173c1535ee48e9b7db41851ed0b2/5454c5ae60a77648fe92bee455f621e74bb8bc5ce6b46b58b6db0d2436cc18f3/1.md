**First read.** The eye lands on the leaf pattern, not on the words. The white headline is there, but where it crosses the pale yellow and lime leaves it breaks apart: "small" and "spaces." fade into the leaves, and the line needs a second, effortful read. Then the eye drops to a calm intro line and three tidy cards, and the page stops. It reads as a friendly, small local garden centre. It feels lush and cheerful, but loud at the top and done at the bottom.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of a local garden centre, meant to get nearby gardeners to come in or browse its shade and small-space plants.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The headline, which is the offer, loses to its own background, and once you've read it the page gives you nothing to do: there's no way to see the plants or plan a visit.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headline is lost in the illustration.** The white 56 px-ish bold at upper left sits straight on a dense field of pale yellow, lime and white leaves with no scrim. "small", "spaces" and the full stop look close to 1:1 against the yellow leaves behind them, so the viewer pieces the line together instead of reading it. Contrast has to hold at the worst point behind the text, not on average (WCAG 1.4.3, failure technique F83). Because the pattern is the same busy texture edge to edge, nothing in the hero is the figure, and the texture outshouts the one element that should come first (Tschichold: one element dominates). This is a **defect**. Fix: put a dark scrim or a solid panel behind the headline, or calm the illustration where the text sits (fewer, darker, lower-contrast leaves) so every letter stands clear. Check the result by measuring the white against the lightest leaf behind it, aiming for at least 3:1 at this size.
2. **No next step.** There's no button anywhere. "Ready to pot this weekend" is the most persuasive line on the page, but it leads nowhere, and the three cards are dead ends (the hours don't lead to directions, the workshops don't lead to booking, delivery doesn't lead to the shop). The eye path runs headline, intro, cards, then into about 120 px of empty white. A visitor who is interested has to go back to the small grey nav and guess between "Plants" and "Visit". This is a **defect**: the page makes its case and then lets the visitor drift. Fix: one primary action right under the intro, naming the exact thing, such as "See this week's shade plants" (Material/Apple HIG: one primary action per view). Then give each card a small text link to its next step ("Get directions", "Book a place", "Delivery area").
3. **The picture doesn't show the idea.** "Shade and small spaces" calls up a dim corner, a balcony, a fern under a tree. The illustration is a bright, sun-dappled, wall-to-wall field of leaves: no shade, no small space, no fern or hosta you'd recognise. So the image adds noise, and it argues against the headline's promise when it should be half of it. This is **taste** (common sense, no approved source): a picture of the actual setting or the actual plants would make the offer obvious before the words are read, and would sell the nursery's own stock instead of a generic leaf pattern.

**Smaller things.**
- Headline left edge (x≈72) sits about 16 px inside the logo, intro and card edge (x≈56). It's a near miss, so it looks like a mistake. Align it to 56 or move it in clearly further (Arnheim).
- "spaces." sits alone on the headline's second line. Rebreak it to "Plants for shade / and small spaces" so each line is a phrase (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The saturated green and yellow cover about half the first screen. Toning down the hero would also help finding 1.
- The nav doesn't mark the current page. If this is the home page that's forgivable, but give the active item a bolder weight or an underline on inner pages (Laubheimer, NN/g).

**What's working.**
- The three cards answer the three questions a local shopper actually has (when are you open, what's on, do you deliver) in plain, complete sentences with real numbers: "until 8 pm on Thursdays", "ten places in each class", "orders over $60". Keep that copy and its specificity.
- The serif logotype in dark green next to an Inter-style sans for everything else is a clean two-face plan. It feels rooted and local without being twee.
- The intro line is good selling copy. "Grown at our nursery" sets this place apart from a big-box store, and "ready to pot this weekend" gives a reason to come now. It just needs an action under it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the illustration is a stand-in for photography that didn't load, and whether the hero animates or scrolls behind the text. Check both in a live browser before fixing finding 1.
