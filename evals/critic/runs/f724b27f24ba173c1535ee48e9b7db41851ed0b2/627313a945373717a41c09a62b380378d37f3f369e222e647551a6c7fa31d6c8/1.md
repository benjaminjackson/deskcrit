Checklist done. I've matched the few failures to their reference entries and am writing up the critique now.

**First read.** My eye goes to "Create your account," then down the stack of fields to the solid blue button. It reads as a standard sign-up card for a note-taking app called Quillnest. It feels calm, tidy and competent, and you could swap in any SaaS logo without changing anything else.

**What it's for.** My assumption: new users of a notes product create an account here, and the page should get them in with as little friction and as few failed tries as possible.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The hierarchy, the single column and the labels are all sound. What gets in the way is that the form doesn't tell people what they need to get through it on the first try, and it asks for more than sign-up needs.

**What would move it most**
1. **The password field gives no rules.** The Password field has nothing under it, and there's no space for a hint. If there's a length or character rule, people find out only after they press Create account. Then they have to guess, retype and submit again, at the moment they're most likely to give up. That's a **defect** under Nielsen's heuristics: error prevention (No. 5), and help placed next to the step (No. 10). **Fix:** put a short line of hint text under the field that states the rule before anyone types ("At least 8 characters"). A show-password toggle would also help. If there really is no rule, leave it as is.
2. **"Sign in" doesn't look like a link.** In "Already have an account? Sign in" at the bottom of the card, "Sign in" has the same grey, weight and lack of underline as the words before it. Returning users who land here by mistake are the people this line is for, and they get no visible way out. That's a **defect**: links need more than colour to stand out, and this one doesn't even differ in colour (WCAG technique G183; NN/g on flat UI). **Fix:** make "Sign in" the brand blue and underline it, or at least underline it.
3. **The form asks for more than sign-up needs.** "How did you hear about us?" is a marketing question, and it sits between the user and the button. "Workspace name" is optional but looks just as important as the required fields. Together with separate first and last names, that's six fields when email and password would create the account. Every extra field is one more reason to quit. This is **taste**, backed by Nielsen on progressive disclosure. **Fix:** cut the form down to the fields that are actually needed. Ask for the workspace name and the attribution question after sign-up, or at least label them "(optional)" and move them below a divider.

**Smaller things.**
- The input borders (pale grey-blue on white) look close to or below the 3:1 non-text contrast in WCAG 1.4.11. Measure them, and darken the border if it falls short.
- The "How did you hear about us?" dropdown is an empty native select with nothing in its first option. Add a "Choose one" prompt so it doesn't look broken.
- "Start a free workspace for your notes." could sit under any notes app's sign-up. One concrete line about what makes Quillnest different would give people a reason to finish the form.

**What's working.**
- Every field has a bold label above it, close to its own field and well clear of the one above, and required fields are marked with both an asterisk and the "* Required field" note. Don't let anyone swap these for placeholder-only labels.
- There's one unmistakable primary action: a full-width filled button, the only strong colour inside the card. Keep it as the only filled control.
- First and last name side by side is the right exception to the single column: a short, related pair.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and button, and how errors look and read after a failed submit. Both matter on a form, so check them live.
