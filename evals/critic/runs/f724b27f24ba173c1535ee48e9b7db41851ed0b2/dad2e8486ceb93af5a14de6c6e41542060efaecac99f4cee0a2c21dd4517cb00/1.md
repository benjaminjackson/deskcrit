**First read.** The eye lands on the bold question "Save changes to the shared calendar?", reads the line under it, and drops to the filled blue "Save changes" button at the bottom right. Then it stalls on the row of four actions. "Export as file" and "Discard changes" look like the same kind of thing, two quiet blue links, but one exports the edits and the other throws them away. It reads as a clean, calm, competent confirmation dialog from a calendar or community-group app.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a modal that interrupts someone editing a shared calendar. Its job is to have them publish their edits, keep editing, or throw the edits away, without making a mistake they can't undo.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job well: the question is plain and the safe main action is obvious. The action row lets the dialog down. The one action that can't be undone, "Discard changes", looks just like a harmless extra ("Export as file") and sits right beside "Keep editing", so it's the easiest button to hit by mistake.

**What would move it most**
1. **"Discard changes" doesn't look or sit like a destructive action.** *(Defect.)* It's the second item in the action row: blue text with no fill, the same style as "Export as file" and the same blue as "Save changes". It sits about 40 px from "Keep editing". Someone who means "not yet" and aims for the escape button can wipe out the new rehearsal times, and nothing in the styling warns them. Discard also gets the same visual tier as Export, so it reads as a side option, not a loss. Apple HIG ("Alerts", "Buttons") and Material ("Dialogs") both call for a destructive action that is set apart from the safe ones, styled as destructive (usually red), and never the default. Raskin ("Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo") argues for undo after the fact over a warning before it. Fix: give Discard a destructive style, red text or a red outline, and move it away from Keep editing and Save, usually to the far left of the row with clear space between it and the safe pair. Also name what is lost: "Discard new times". If you can, offer undo after a discard.
2. **"Export as file" crowds a decision that needs three answers at most.** *(Defect.)* This dialog asks one question: publish, keep working, or throw away. Export answers none of them, and it's a rare action that belongs in the editor's menu (Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"). As the first item in the row it gets read first, adds a fourth choice to a moment that should be quick, and is the main reason Discard looks like "just another option". It's also unclear on its own: export the changes or the whole calendar, and to what format? Fix: take it out of the dialog and put it in the calendar's own menu. If it has to stay, it should say what it exports.

**Smaller things.**
- "Export as file" doesn't say what is exported or in what format. If it stays anywhere, label it with what you get, e.g. "Download calendar (.ics)" (WCAG 2.4.4).

**What's working.**
- **The headline is a real question with its object named.** "Save changes to the shared calendar?" says what is being decided and how far it reaches. Keep the question form and the word "shared".
- **The body line lands the consequence on people.** "Will be shown to everyone who follows this calendar" tells the user who will see the change before they commit. That's exactly what this dialog needs to say.
- **One clear primary action.** "Save changes" is the only filled button. "Keep editing" is outlined and the rest are text, so the safe path is unmistakable. The type also stays clean: one family, two or three sizes, the heading set apart by weight.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the buttons say what happens, not just name the operation** *(taste)*. In a shared calendar, the cost of saving is that people get told. "Publish to followers" or "Save and show to everyone" ties the button to the consequence the body line just set up, so the user confirms that consequence, not an abstract "save". Apple HIG's "Alerts" guidance favours button titles that name the specific outcome.
2. **Show what is about to go out** *(taste)*. "The new choir rehearsal times" is still a description the user has to take on trust. One compact line with the actual change ("Thu 7:00 pm → 7:30 pm") lets them check the edit against what they meant before everyone sees it. Publishing to a group is where a typo costs the most.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- There is no dimmed backdrop behind the dialog. It floats on a blank white page. This could just be the capture, but in a live browser, check that the page behind is dimmed and blocked so it's clear the decision has to be made first.
- Which button has focus when the dialog opens. It should be "Save changes" or "Keep editing", never "Discard changes".
