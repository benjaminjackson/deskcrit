**First read.** The eye goes straight to the tilted blue "SALE" slab on the right, the only strong colour on the page. It's hard to read because the red letters shimmer against the blue. Next the eye goes to the headline on the left, then down to four cards whose pictures are empty pale rectangles, and then it runs out of places to go. It reads as a calm, tasteful homeware shop with a cheap sticker slapped on it.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage of a homeware shop, and its job is to get shoppers to act on a weekend sale and start browsing.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page announces a sale but gives the shopper no way into it, and the loudest thing on the page is a badge you can barely read. The quiet base (type, spacing, cards) is solid. The two places that should sell, the hero and the product pictures, are the two that fail.

**What would move it most**

1. **The "SALE" badge takes the focus and is hard to read** (defect). It's in the top-right of the hero.
   - **What goes wrong:** It's the only saturated colour on the page, so it wins the first glance over the headline. But its red letters sit on a blue of about the same lightness, so the edge between them shimmers and the word is hard to read (Albers, *Interaction of Color*; Itten, *The Art of Color*). The contrast looks far below WCAG's 3:1 for large text; check it with a colour picker.
   - **Why it matters:** The shopper's first look is spent decoding a sticker that says less than the subhead already says ("Up to 40% off through Sunday").
   - **Taste:** Its saturated primaries, slanted angle and heavy wide capitals belong to a discount warehouse, not a linen-and-stoneware brand. It makes the whole page feel cheaper.
   - **Fix:** Make the offer itself the focal point. Either drop the badge and give "Up to 40% off" real size and weight in the hero, or redraw the badge in the brand's palette with strong light–dark contrast between the text and its background.

2. **The hero has no action, and the page never leads into the sale** (defect). The hero states an offer and a deadline, then stops.
   - **What's missing:** There is no button or link, and the badge doesn't look clickable. Below it, "Popular this week" shows full prices with no sign of what's discounted. A shopper who wants the 40% off has to find "Sale" in the small nav text at the top right.
   - **Fix:** Add one clear primary button in the hero that names the specific thing the shopper gets, for example "Shop the linen and cookware sale". Better still, show sale prices against the original prices in the cards, so shoppers see the saving for themselves against a price they can compare. A bare "40% off" claim asks them to take it on trust. Material Design and Apple's Human Interface Guidelines both call for one dominant primary action per view.

3. **None of the product pictures show anything** (defect). All four cards show a tinted panel with an empty white rounded box, which is placeholder art or images that didn't load.
   - **Why it matters:** For bowls, duvet covers, pans and throws, the photo is the product. The shopper can't judge any of them, and the row reads as unfinished.
   - **Fix:** If these are real images that failed to load in the capture, there's nothing to fix in the design; check the live page. If not, these need real product photography, shot with one consistent lighting and crop style.

4. **The shop header is missing what shoppers look for** (convention). It has only the logo and four category links: no search, cart or account.
   - **Why it matters:** On a shop, people expect a cart at the top right and a search box (Nielsen, "Jakob's Law"). Without them, people can't check what they've picked or jump straight to an item they came for.
   - **Fix:** Add search and a cart, in the usual places.

**Smaller things**
- **Hero text doesn't line up with the left edge.** The headline and subhead start at about x=56, while the logo, "Popular this week" and the cards start at x=48. That's a near-miss that looks like a mistake (Arnheim). Put them on the same left edge.
- **The hero has no image, so the headline works alone.** "Linens and cookware for the weekend" would land harder next to a picture of those linens and that cookware. The badge doesn't fill that role. This is taste.

**What's working**
- **The type scale is clean and limited.** The headline is large, the section heading clearly smaller, and the card title and price are split by weight rather than by another size, which is what Refactoring UI recommends. Keep this as you add a button and sale prices.
- **The neutral base fits the products.** The warm linen hero band, the soft card tints and the dark grey text feel calm and domestic. It's the right stage for homeware, and the reason the badge looks so out of place.
- **The card grid is orderly.** Four equal columns, equal gaps, consistent padding, and a tight title–price pair. It scans instantly.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- **Product images:** I couldn't tell whether they're placeholders or failed loads. Check the live page.
- **The "SALE" badge:** I couldn't tell whether it's real text or a picture of text, or whether it's clickable.
