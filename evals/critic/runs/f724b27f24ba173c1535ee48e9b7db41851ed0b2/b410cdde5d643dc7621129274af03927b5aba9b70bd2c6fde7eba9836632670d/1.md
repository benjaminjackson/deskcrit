Version 1 is better. Both versions have the same copy, sizes, colours and button, and the only change is that version 2 centres everything. Centring makes this hero harder to read and doesn't buy anything in return.

**First read.** The eye lands on the big dark headline, drops through two short paragraphs, and stops on the green "Plan my first week" button. It reads as a home page for a meal-planning app for home cooks: calm, plain and trustworthy. In version 1 the eye runs down one straight left edge, and the logo sits on that same edge. In version 2 every line starts somewhere new, so the eye has to find the start of each line. The page feels more like a template and less like a product.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a home-page hero that gets home cooks to understand the offer and start planning their first week.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job. It's a clear, well-ordered hero where the headline, the explanation and one exact next step come in the right order. Version 2 loses that by centring three-line paragraphs and a three-line list on one axis. That makes the reading slower and cuts the hero's tie to the logo. The size, weight and colour of the text didn't change, so the alignment is the whole difference.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2: the centred axis costs reading and structure. Go back to flush left (convention, plus defect).**
   - **Where:** the paragraphs that open "Tell Weekplate who you cook for…" and "Swap any meal…" are centred. Each line starts at a different x position (306, 312, 537…).
   - **What the reader meets:** after each line the eye has to search for where the next one begins. These two paragraphs are the case for the product, so the slowdown lands exactly where the reader decides whether to care.
   - **The list:** the three lines "Recipes tested…", "Portions for…" and "Lists you can send…" lose their shared left edge. That edge was the only thing marking them as a list (there are no bullets), so they now look like loose captions.
   - **The grid:** the centred column no longer lines up with the "Weekplate" logo at x=100. The page now has two unrelated axes: the full-width header and the centred hero.
   - **Rule:** Tschichold (*The New Typography*) rejects central-axis setting as a habit. Keep centring for a few short lines, and set paragraphs of several lines flush left (Bringhurst 2.1.3).
   - **Why it isn't justified here:** nothing on this page needs symmetry. There's no centred picture or ceremony.
   - **Fix:** go back to version 1's flush-left column. If you want a centred look, centre only the headline and the button, and keep the body flush left.

**Smaller things** (both versions unless noted)
- **Headline break:** "minutes" sits alone on line 2, so the promise splits right at "ten / minutes". Break after "dinners" or make the measure a little wider so the number and its unit stay together (taste).
- **Version 1, empty right half:** the text column takes the left 60% and nothing answers it on the right. If an image was meant to sit there, check that it loads. If not, the empty right half is acceptable, but a product shot (a sample week, or a sorted shopping list) would show the promise rather than only describe it (taste).
- **The list:** the three lines are the same size and colour as the body text, with no bullets or ticks. Even in version 1 they read as a third paragraph. Give them a small marker or a slightly different treatment so they scan as features.

**What's working**
- **The copy does the work:**
  - The headline is a concrete promise: a week of dinners in ten minutes.
  - The first paragraph says exactly what you tell the app and what it gives you back.
  - The paragraph ends on a consequence for the reader: "less food goes to waste by Friday".
  - The button names one exact action, "Plan my first week". Keep all of this.
- **Clear order of importance:** a heavy, tightly set headline sits well above calm grey body text. One saturated green is used only for the brand and the single primary button, so there's never a doubt where to click.
- **Version 1's left edge:** the logo, headline, paragraphs, list and button all hang on x=100. That one line is what makes the page feel organised. Don't lose it.

**Where it could go further**
1. **Show the result next to the promise (taste).** The page asks the reader to take "ten minutes" and "shared ingredients" on trust. A sample week beside the text, with seven dinners and an aisle-sorted list, would let them judge the value against their own Sunday planning. Version 1's empty right half is exactly the space for it.
2. **Make the ask compare against what the reader already does (taste).** A line under the button such as "Takes less time than writing tonight's shopping list" would put the product next to a chore the reader already knows. Then they don't have to take the "ten minutes" claim on faith.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the button and nav links, and how the hero reflows on a phone.
