**First read.** The eye lands on the top-left block and then just reads, line after line, at one even pace. Nothing stands out. Only on reading the words do you see that the short lines are questions and the longer lines are answers. It reads as the help or FAQ section of a small bakery's order-and-collect site. It's calm, clean and quiet to the point of being flat: a plain-text document, not a page someone designed.

**What it's for.** My assumption, since I wasn't told: a help page for customers who have ordered bread online. They come to find one answer fast, about pickup time, someone else collecting, allergies or cancelling.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. Someone scanning for "how do I cancel" has no headings to scan, because the question lines look the same as the answers. The cancellation deadline, the one line people will act on, can also be read two ways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The question headings don't read as headings.** "Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients" and "Cancelling an order" are in the same weight and colour as the answers below them. They also look the same size or slightly smaller (about 18 px against about 19 px). The only thing marking them is spacing. A visitor with one question can't skim the four topics and jump to theirs; they have to read the page from the top. This is a **defect** for a help page. Dair: a difference meant to be seen has to be obvious at a glance, and one size step, or none, looks like an accident (*Design with Type*, ch. 8). Fix: make each question clearly different from its answer, most simply by setting the questions in a heavier weight and a step larger than the answers. You could also lighten the answers' colour a little. The spacing between groups already works, so keep it.
2. **The cancellation deadline can be read two ways.** "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead" could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time on the day before. Someone with a 9 am Saturday slot who reads it the second way and cancels at 10 pm Friday finds the bread already baked and the order unrefundable. The bakery then has a complaint about a rule the customer thinks they followed. This is a **defect**, because a deadline is what people hold you to. Fix: state the exact cutoff ("up to 24 hours before your pickup time", or "until 6 pm the day before"). The second sentence also stops at a fact. Finish it in the viewer's terms: what happens to their money after the cutoff?
3. **Several answers leave the reader to fill a gap.** This is a **defect** in the copy.
   - "along with a name": whose name, yours or theirs? The person collecting can get it wrong at the counter, which is exactly what this answer exists to prevent. Write "your name" or "their own name".
   - "Every loaf has a card by the till": someone ordering online can't see the till, so the allergy answer only helps them once they're already in the shop with a paid order. Say where they can check before ordering, or how to reach the baker before ordering (a phone number or an email address), not just "ask the baker".
   - "Open your order": say where, for example "in your confirmation email" or "under My orders".
4. **The lines are too long.** "Give the person your order number…" and "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead…" run about 105–110 characters. That is well past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.2), so the eye has trouble finding the start of the next line. This is a **convention**, and it costs something here because these are the answers people came to read. Fix: narrow the text column to around 65 characters (about 600 px at this size). The page has plenty of empty width to spare.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer. That's a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrowing the column will probably fix it; if not, reword.
- There's no page title, so nothing tells a visitor arriving from a link that this is the bakery's help page. Add one heading above the four questions so it is the first thing seen (Tschichold).

**What's working.**
- The spacing does the grouping right: about 10 px between each question and its answer, about 35 px between topics, so each pair reads as one unit. Keep this rhythm when you strengthen the headings.
- The voice is plain and human ("the bread has already been baked for you"). That suits a small bakery better than policy-speak, so keep the tone while you add the missing specifics.
- Dark grey on white in a plain sans with comfortable line spacing makes the text easy to read.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Open your order" is meant to be a link. If so, nothing in the image marks it as one.
- How the column behaves on a phone, which is probably where many pickup questions are asked.
