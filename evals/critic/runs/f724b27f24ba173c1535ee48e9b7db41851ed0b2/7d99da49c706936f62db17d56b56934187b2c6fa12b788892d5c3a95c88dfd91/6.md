**First read.** My eye lands on the top-left corner and then reads straight down, because nothing else pulls it. It took me a second to see that "Changing your pickup time" is a heading and not the first line of a paragraph. Once I saw that, it read as a bakery's pickup FAQ. It feels calm, plain and friendly, but flat, like an unstyled draft.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help or FAQ section on a bakery's ordering site. Customers who already have an order scan it to find the one question they came with.

**Verdict.** It doesn't yet do its job of letting someone scan for their question. The headings look the same as the answers, so the eye can't jump from question to question. The wording of the cancellation deadline and the pickup ID rule also leaves gaps that will cause trouble at the counter.

**What would move it most**
1. **The headings look like body text** (defect). All four headings ("Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", "Allergies and ingredients", "Cancelling an order") look about 18 px, regular weight and the same near-black as the answers below them. The answers look about 19 px, so the answers are actually slightly *larger*. Someone looking for "cancelling" has to read every line, because the headings don't stand out on the way down. That defeats the point of a FAQ. Only the spacing tells you where one item ends and the next begins.
   - Dair (*Design with Type*, ch. 8): a difference meant to show must be obvious at a glance. A one-pixel or one-step difference looks like an accident.
   - Fix: make each heading clearly different from its text in a way you see at a glance. A semibold or bold weight is the usual move; a larger size or a darker colour against greyer answers also works. Keep it to one strong difference, not three weak ones.
2. **The cancellation and pickup rules can be read two ways** (defect: these are the lines customers will hold the bakery to).
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead." Ahead of what: the pickup time, or the day of pickup? Is that 24 hours, or before midnight? One customer reads "the day before, any time" and tries at 9 pm. The bakery means 24 hours before pickup and refuses. The answer also never says *how* to cancel. "Changing your pickup time" does: "Open your order…".
   - "Show it at the counter along with a name." Whose name, the person collecting or yours? Is it just spoken, or do they need ID? The person collecting will be the one stuck at the till.
   - Fix: state the exact cut-off ("You can cancel until 24 hours before your pickup time, from your order page"). Name what the person collecting must bring ("your order number and your name").
3. **The answer lines run too long** (convention). "Give the person your order number…" and "Orders can be cancelled…" each run about 95–100 characters on one line. That's well past the 45–75 Bringhurst gives (*Elements*, 2.1.2). On a longer FAQ, the eye will lose its place on the return to the next line. Narrow the text column to about 65 characters (roughly 600 px at this size).

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrowing the column will move the break, so check it again then.
- There's no page title above the first question, so nothing says "pickup questions" or "help" before the list starts. Add one so it is clearly the top of the page and the four headings become a level below it.
- Allergy copy: "Ask the baker if you are unsure" only works in the shop. Someone ordering online with a nut allergy is told to check a card they can't see yet. Say how they can ask before ordering, or link the ingredient lists.

**What's working.**
- The spacing does the grouping: each heading sits about 10 px above its answer and about 35 px below the previous answer. Even with no styling, the four items read as four pairs. Keep that ratio when you restyle the headings.
- The voice is plain, warm and short: "After that the bread has already been baked for you" gives the reason for the rule without lecturing. Keep that tone when you tighten the cancellation and pickup lines.
- It sticks to one typeface, one colour and black on white, so the page reads as one piece with nothing competing.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the column reflows on a phone (it should wrap nicely at that width, but check). Whether "Open your order" is a link somewhere on the real page.
