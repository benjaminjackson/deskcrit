**First read.** The eye has nowhere to land. It goes to the top-left line, "Changing your pickup time", and reads down as if it were all one paragraph. The question lines only start to stand out on the second pass, and only because of the gaps between them. It reads as a plain help or FAQ block for a bakery's pickup orders. It feels tidy and calm, but flat, like a document rather than a page.

**What it's for.** Assumption: a help section on a bakery's ordering site that answers common pickup questions. A customer should be able to scan for their question and get an answer they can act on.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job well yet. The four questions are set like body text, so a customer can't scan for theirs. Several answers leave out the one fact the customer will hold the bakery to.

**What would move it most**

1. **The questions look the same as the answers.** This covers all four question lines ("Changing your pickup time", "Collecting for someone else", and the other two). They are in the same face, weight and colour as the answers, and they look a pixel or two *smaller*. A scanning reader can't pick out the questions, which is the only reason an FAQ is easier than a paragraph. The only thing separating them is the space: about 40 px above each question and about 10 px below it. That grouping is right, but it isn't enough on its own. **Defect.** Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8: when two elements are meant to differ, the difference must be obvious at a glance, and a one-step difference looks like an accident. Direction: set the questions apart from the answers in more than size. Use weight first (semibold at the same size or one clear step up), and colour or a darker value as well if you like. Keep the spacing.

2. **The answers leave out the fact that matters, and one line can be read two ways.** Prices, deadlines and conditions are what a customer remembers and holds you to.
   - **"Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead."** Ahead of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or by the end of the day before. A customer who reads it the generous way and cancels the morning of an afternoon pickup gets refused. The answer also never says *how* to cancel. Name the cut-off exactly ("until 24 hours before your pickup time") and the action ("Open your order and tap Cancel").
   - **"…show it at the counter along with a name."** Whose name, theirs or yours? A friend who gives their own name when the counter expects yours gets turned away. Say "your name".
   - **"The bakery holds orders for two hours after the chosen time."** This stops at the fact. The customer needs what happens next: is the order lost, is it refunded? Finish with what it means for them.
   - **"Every loaf has a card by the till."** This answers someone already standing in the shop. Someone ordering online, who is the person reading this page, can't see the till. If ingredients are on the product pages, point there.
   
   **Defect** (copy). The fix is to write each answer so that it says who does what, by when, and what happens if they don't.

3. **The answer lines run too long.** The full-width answers ("Give the person your order number… along with a name.", "Orders can be cancelled… baked for you.") run about 95 characters a line, well past the 45–75 range (Bringhurst, *Elements*, 2.1.2). Here that matters less than usual, because most answers are one or two lines. Still, the text column is about 820 px wide while everything to its right is empty. **Convention.** Direction: narrow the text column to roughly 600–650 px at this size. That also tightens the block so it reads as one designed thing rather than text poured into a container.

**Smaller things.**
- "anything." sits alone on the last line of the allergies answer (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrowing the measure will probably fix it. If not, rewrite or use a non-breaking space.
- There's no page title above the first question, so nothing names the section. A heading such as "Pickup and orders: common questions" at a clear step above the questions would give the eye a starting point.

**What's working.**
- The spacing does the grouping correctly. Each question sits much nearer its answer than the answer before it, so the pairs read as pairs even without type contrast. Keep these spacing ratios when you add weight.
- The questions are worded the way a customer would think them ("Collecting for someone else", not "Proxy collection"). Keep that voice.
- The text is dark and easy to read on white, the left edge is crisp, and nothing is decorative or extra.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether this block sits in a larger page with a header and navigation. The empty right side and bottom suggest it might, and that would change how much the missing title matters. Also whether "Open your order" links anywhere. If it does, the link should look like one.
