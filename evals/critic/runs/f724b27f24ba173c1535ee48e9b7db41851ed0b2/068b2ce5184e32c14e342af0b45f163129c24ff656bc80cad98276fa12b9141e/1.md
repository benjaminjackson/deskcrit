**First read.** The eye goes to the heavy black title, then straight to the big red tile and "Rose arch". From there it moves across the five smaller tiles, which read as one sorted set of colour swatches. The last thing it reaches is a grey line at the bottom saying the design failed. It reads as a tidy, well-built gallery template that has nothing in it yet. It feels calm and orderly, and empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web gallery page for a garden show, where visitors browse the exhibits and see what each garden looks like.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The layout is sound, but the gallery shows no gardens. Every tile is a flat colour block with the same pale half-circle, so the one thing a visitor came for is missing. A stray review note in the footer would also make the page look broken to anyone who reads it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders** (defect). All six tiles are a solid fill with a centred circle cut off by the bottom edge. A visitor can't see a rose arch, a pond or a herb bed, so the captions describe things the page never shows. That leaves a list of names with coloured backgrounds. The title and the images can't work as one idea when the images carry nothing. If these are loading stand-ins for real photos, then the live page is fine; check it with the images loaded. If they are meant to be the artwork, replace them with photographs of each exhibit, cropped the same way, so the tiles show gardens instead of colour codes. Once real photos go in, the large Rose arch tile has to earn its size with the strongest picture on the page.
2. **A leftover note sits in the footer** (defect). At the bottom left it says "Design review: failed, every tile in this gallery is the same size". It's an internal comment that ended up in the content, and it isn't even true: the Rose arch tile is about four times the area of the others. A visitor who reads it will think the site is broken or unfinished. Delete it, and if the page needs a footer, use it for something a visitor can use, such as the show's dates or where it is held.
3. **The copy doesn't say whose show this is, or why one garden leads** (taste, argued). "Garden show gallery" could head any garden show's page. A visitor can't tell which show, which year, or whether these are winners, entries or highlights. The Rose arch is made the largest tile with no stated reason, so the hierarchy looks arbitrary. Name the show and year in the title or a line under it. If the Rose arch is the featured piece, say why ("Best in show", for example) so the size difference reads as a judgement and not as a layout accident.

**Smaller things.**
- Spacing: each image sits about 9 px above its own title but only about 15 px below the caption above it. The captions in the right column and bottom row float between tiles. Widen the gap between tiles, or tighten the one between image and title, so each caption clearly belongs to its own picture (Arnheim, grouping by proximity).
- Baselines: "Climbing roses over the east gate." and "Reeds and water lilies." look a few pixels off level across the column gap. Put the bottom captions on the same baseline (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- Colour: six unrelated saturated hues (red, green, blue, ochre, yellow, purple) cover about half the page, with no system behind them. This mostly goes away once real photos replace the fills. If colour fills stay, take them from one restrained range.

**What's working.**
- The grid: two-thirds plus one-third, with steady 14 px gutters and generous outer margins. The big tile spans exactly two bottom columns, so the page feels solid. Keep it when the photos go in.
- The hierarchy is clear. Title, then the featured tile, then the set of small tiles, set apart by size and weight with no muddled steps. The large "Rose arch" title with a lighter grey line under it echoes the small captions at a higher level, which is the right way to mark a featured item.
- The captions are plain and concrete ("Runner beans on canes.", "Tomatoes in late July."). They name what you'd see, in the visitor's own words.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the colour blocks are images that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken; check in a live browser. Whether the tiles are links to larger views, and what they do on hover or focus. How the layout reflows on a phone.

What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (lines align across columns; space shows the groups)
