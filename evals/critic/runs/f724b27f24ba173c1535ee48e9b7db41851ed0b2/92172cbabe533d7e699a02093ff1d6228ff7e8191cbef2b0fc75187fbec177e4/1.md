**First read.** The eye goes to the title, then straight to the tall blue Organic search bar, then moves down the staircase of six bright bars. It reads as a clean internal growth report: one chart of Q3 sign-ups by channel, with a short note on the right. It feels tidy and trustworthy, and a bit generic. The colours draw attention without telling you anything, and the actual story sits in the small sidebar, which the eye reaches last.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal growth dashboard page. It shows the team and leadership where Q3 sign-ups came from and explains a budget decision for Q4.

**Verdict.** The chart is honest, well sorted and well labelled, so it does its basic job. But the story lives only in the sidebar. The chart's colours point nowhere, and the comparison with Q2 that the sidebar relies on is never drawn. Someone reading the chart alone gets the ranking but not the point.

**What would move it most**
1. **Six bright hues encode nothing, and they hide the two things the note is about.** Each bar has its own saturated colour (blue, orange, red, teal, green, purple), but the channel name is already under every bar. So the colour carries no information, and every bar competes equally for attention. Worse, the colours work against the message. Red on Paid social and green on Podcast ads read as "bad" and "good". Yet the sidebar says Podcast ads are being cut and doesn't mention Paid social at all. The reader picks up a verdict the page never makes. **Convention** (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design", pitfall 12; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 19.1) **and defect** (the red/green reading). Fix: draw all six bars in one muted colour, and use a single accent for the point you want the viewer to see. That's Organic search, and possibly a second, quieter mark on Podcast ads and Events as the two being cut. Then the chart and the note point at the same bars.

2. **The finding is in the margin, not on the chart, and the comparison it rests on is missing.** The title "New sign-ups by channel" only names the topic. The takeaways ("a third of all new accounts, up from a quarter in Q2" and "the events budget moves to referrals") sit in a 15 px sidebar the eye reaches last. The chart shows only Q3, so "up from a quarter" can't be checked or seen. The viewer has to take it on trust, and anyone who skims the chart and skips the note leaves without the point. **Defect** (Few, pitfall 2: a number needs a comparison such as the previous period). Putting the finding in the title is **taste**: no approved source states it, but a page built to justify a budget move should say the move where people look first. Fix:
   - Show Q2 on the chart, for example as a pale ghost bar or a tick beside each Q3 bar.
   - Let the title state the finding, e.g. "Organic search brought a third of Q3 sign-ups, up from a quarter in Q2".

**Smaller things.**
- The sidebar heading "What changed this quarter" (top at about y 174) lines up with nothing on the left. It sits below the subtitle (145) and above the chart's top gridline (195). Align its top with the chart top or the title so the two columns read as one spread.

**What's working.**
- The data is honest and easy to read. Bars start at zero, are sorted largest to smallest, and carry direct value labels. The labels add up to the 5,400 total in the source line, so a careful reader can check them.
- The source line does its job: system, exact date range and total in one sentence. Keep it.
- The sidebar copy is good writing. It's specific, it shows cause and effect ("after the guide pages were rewritten") and it ends on a decision ("The events budget moves to referrals in Q4"), not just a description. It just needs a more prominent place to sit.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the page argue for the Q4 decision (taste).** This page exists to justify moving the events budget, so build the chart around that. Group or mark Events next to Referral so the viewer sees 260 against 1,210 directly. Then the budget move looks obvious rather than something you have to accept on trust.
2. **Give the page one colour it owns (taste).** The neutral grey type and white page are well judged. Choose a single brand accent for "the thing to notice" and use it only for that, on every Growth chart, so readers learn that this colour means "look here".

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty bottom fifth of the frame is just the end of the viewport or the end of the page. If more charts follow below, ignore it.

Pictures: skipped, no pictures beyond a text wordmark
