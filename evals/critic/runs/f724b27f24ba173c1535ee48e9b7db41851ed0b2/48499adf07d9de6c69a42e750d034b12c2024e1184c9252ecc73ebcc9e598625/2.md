**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline, drops to "Create your link", then slides into the row of four blue-outlined cards. It reads at once as a scheduling SaaS homepage for small service businesses. It feels clean and competent but generic. Because blue is on nearly everything, nothing after the headline pulls harder than anything else, and the empty right half of the hero feels like a picture that never loaded.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage of a booking-link product, meant to get small-business owners (clinics, tutors, coaches) to create a link or try the live example.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and readable, but it doesn't make a case: there's no picture of the product, the headline could belong to any scheduler, and blue is spread so evenly that the call to action has to compete with card borders and titles. The biggest problem is that the hero tells but never shows. The right half, where a viewer would see what a Slotwise booking page looks like, is blank.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has no visual, so the page tips left and never shows the product.** The headline, subhead and buttons all sit in the left half above the cards. The right half, from the nav down to the cards (about 570 × 330 px), is empty white. The viewer gets a promise ("fill your calendar") with nothing to look at that makes it real, and the layout looks unfinished, like an image slot left blank (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). The piece already has the right fix: "See a live example" is the strongest ask on the page, because it lets the visitor judge the booking page themselves instead of taking the headline on trust. Put that example in the empty half: a real booking page with slots in the guest's time zone. It balances the hero and makes the claim visible. *Defect* (balance) plus a copy/concept point (taste).
2. **Blue is on everything, so the button doesn't stand out and plain text looks clickable.** The same saturated blue colours the headline, the four nav links, "Start free", "Create your link", every card's 2px border, every icon, every card title, every badge and every "Learn more". Nothing marks the main action, and the blue card titles ("Time zones", "Reminders"…) look like the blue nav links, so visitors may click titles that do nothing (Apple HIG, *Color*; Material Design 3, *Color roles*: keep the accent for the main action and interactive state). Keep blue for the primary button and real links. Draw the headings, card titles, icons and card borders in neutrals (a dark ink headline, light gray borders or none). The headline can keep its size and weight; it doesn't need the colour. *Convention*, with a real cost: a weaker call to action and false link cues.
3. **The words and the proof are anonymous.** "Booking links that fill your calendar" is a line any scheduling tool could use. It doesn't say what Slotwise does that the visitor's current tool or email back-and-forth doesn't. The customer row at the bottom ("Harbor Dental", "Northfield Tutoring"…) is small gray text with no label, so it reads as a footer or nav row, not as "businesses like yours use this". Make the headline say what's different, in the visitor's terms: what they stop doing or what they get. Give the proof row a label and real marks, or a single number, so it works as evidence. *Taste*, argued: copy that could sit on anyone's page gives the visitor no reason to choose this one.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1: the right-aligned "New" badge forces "Shared calendars" onto two lines. Its body text and "Learn more" sit about 30 px lower than in the other three cards, so the row's baselines break (Müller-Brockmann). Put the badge after the title as in the other cards, or let the title run full width.
- The badges mix two meanings: "New" and "Beta" are status, while "Auto" and "Email" just restate the feature. Keep status badges only.
- The 2px blue borders on all four cards are heavier than they need to be. A hairline gray border or a light tint would separate the cards without competing with the button.
- The headline breaks as "…fill your / calendar", which leaves one word alone on the second line and splits "your calendar". Break after "links" or adjust the measure.
- Four identical "Learn more" links. They work in context, but "How time zones work" and similar would read better and scan faster.

**What's working.**
- The hero copy stack: a short headline, a one-sentence subhead with real verbs ("Share one link, let people pick a time… keep every meeting in the right place"), then one filled button and one text link. The order is clear and the subhead is at a good measure (about 60 characters).
- The card body copy lands on the viewer: "so nobody has to do the math", "to cut down on no-shows", "so the first minutes are useful". Each feature ends on its benefit, not just a description. Keep that voice.
- A consistent four-column grid, one typeface family, and a clean type scale (headline, subhead, card title, body, small) with clear steps between levels.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states on the nav, buttons and "Learn more" links. With blue already on everything, check that focus is clearly visible. Also how the four cards reflow on a phone.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (headline and visual make one idea; every element does a job; repeated elements look the same)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 10 yes, 3 no (baselines across columns; only related elements look alike; balance)
Text: 29 lines, 27 yes, 2 no (clean paragraph breaks; heading breaks at phrase boundaries)
Color: 9 lines, 8 yes, 1 no (saturated colour kept to a small share)
Pictures: 12 lines, 12 yes
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 31 yes, 2 unsure (focus indicator; focus not obscured)
