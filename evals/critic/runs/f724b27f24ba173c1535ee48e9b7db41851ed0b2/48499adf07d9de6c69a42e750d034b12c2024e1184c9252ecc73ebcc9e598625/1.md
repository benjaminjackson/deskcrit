**First read.** The eye goes straight to the big blue headline, then to the blue "Create your link" button, then along the row of four blue-bordered cards. In about two seconds it reads as a scheduling SaaS landing page for small service businesses. It feels tidy, calm and competent, and also anonymous: everything is the same blue, so after the headline nothing pulls the eye. The right half of the hero is blank, which reads as a missing picture.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the homepage hero of a booking-link product. Its job is to get small practices (dentists, tutors, physios) to create a link or try the live example.

**Verdict.** It does its basic job. The offer is clear, and the ask is concrete and comes with a way to test it. But it looks like a template. The blue is spread over every element, so the main action has no colour of its own, and the empty right half of the hero leaves the page with nothing that shows the product.

**What would move it most**
1. **The hero has nothing on its right side.** The headline, subline and buttons sit in the left half, and the right half from y≈100 to 400 is empty. The page tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). More importantly, the space where a visitor expects to see the product is blank. "Booking links" is a visual promise: what a guest sees when they open the link is the whole product. Without it, the viewer has to imagine it or click away to "See a live example". If an image didn't load in this capture, check it in a live browser. If the blank is intended, put a real booking page there: a week of open slots in the guest's time zone, with one slot being picked. That makes the headline and the picture one idea, and it's the strongest reason to click "Create your link". *Defect.*
2. **Blue is on everything, so the button has no colour of its own.** The headline, the four nav links, the four card titles, the 2 px card borders, the four badges, the icons, the five links and both buttons are all the same saturated blue. The accent stops meaning "act here". The headline reads almost like a link, and the cards pull as hard as the primary button. Apple HIG and Material 3 both say to keep the accent for the main action and interactive state. The fix: set the headline and card titles in near-black, draw the card borders as light grey hairlines (or drop them and use a tint), make the icons neutral or a muted blue, and let only the buttons and links carry full blue. Then "Create your link" becomes the obvious next step. *Convention.*

**Smaller things.**
- **Two filled primary buttons with different labels.** "Start free" in the nav and "Create your link" in the hero are equally heavy and look like two different actions. If they do the same thing, use one label. If they don't, give the nav button a lighter style (Material 3, button emphasis).
- **The first card breaks the row.** "Shared calendars" wraps to two lines to make room for the "New" badge, so its body text and "Learn more" sit about 30 px below the other three (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Shorten the title to "Team calendars", or put the badge above the title in every card.
- **The badges look alike but mean different things.** "New" and "Beta" are status. "Auto" and "Email" describe how the feature works. All four use the same pill, so the viewer reads them as one kind of label and is left wondering what "Auto" means. Keep pills for status only and drop the other two.
- **The icons don't match.** The calendar is an outline, while the clock, envelope and form icons are solid. Pick one style (my view as a designer, no approved source).
- **The customer strip has no label.** "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" sits under a blue rule as plain grey text with no heading, so it reads like footer links rather than proof. Add a line such as "Used by 2,000 practices like yours", or show their logos.

**What's working.**
- **The copy under the headline is specific and active.** "Share one link, let people pick a time that works, and keep every meeting in the right place" says who does what. The card bodies land on what the reader gets ("so nobody has to do the math", "cut down on no-shows", "the first minutes are useful"). Keep that voice.
- **The ask is a good pair.** "Create your link" names one exact action. "See a live example" lets the viewer judge the product for themselves instead of taking it on trust. Keep both.
- **The type is clear.** There are three or four sizes, with a strong jump from the headline to everything else, and every block starts on the same 64 px left edge.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the guest's view the hero (taste).** The product is a page that someone else opens. Showing that page, the way a guest sees it, sells it better than any sentence. Treat it as the piece's one strong image, not as a decoration next to the copy.
2. **Pick one colour the product owns, and keep it for the moment of booking (taste).** If the blue only shows up on the buttons and on the selected slot in the booking picture, the page and the product teach the same thing: blue means "book here". Albers, *Interaction of Color*, shows how much a colour gains when it is held back.
3. **Turn the customer strip into proof in the visitor's own terms (taste).** One concrete result, such as "Kestrel Physio cut no-shows from 1 in 8 to 1 in 20", next to the names, lets a practice owner compare it with their own diary.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the blank right side of the hero is an image that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- How the four cards reflow at tablet widths, where card 1's title wrap will spread to the others.
