**First read.** The eye lands on the big blue headline and then drops straight to the four blue-outlined cards, which pull about as hard as the "Create your link" button. The right half of the hero is a blank white hole. Almost everything is the same blue: nav, headline, buttons, card borders, card titles, icons, badges and links. It reads as a clean, competent, templated SaaS homepage that could belong to any scheduling tool.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage hero for a booking-link product aimed at small service businesses (the named customers are a dental practice, a tutoring service, a physio and so on), and its job is to get them to create a link.

**Verdict.** It's tidy and the copy is clear, but it doesn't sell. One blue on everything means nothing stands out, and the product is never shown, so the empty half of the hero carries no proof. The single biggest problem is that the accent colour no longer tells the viewer what matters or what's clickable.

**What would move it most**
1. **The blue is everywhere, so it marks nothing.** The headline, all four card titles, the icons, the badges, the 2px card borders, the nav links, the "Learn more" links and both buttons all share one saturated blue. Two things follow. First, the "Create your link" button has no colour of its own; it competes with four big blue rectangles right below it, and those rectangles cover more area. Second, non-clickable text looks the same as clickable text. The blue headline and blue card titles sit next to blue nav links, so "Shared calendars" reads as a link, and the real "Learn more" links read as just more of the same. This is a defect, and it also breaks a convention: keep the accent for the main action and interactive states, and don't use it to decorate content (Apple HIG, Color; Material Design 3, colour roles). Fix: set the headline and card titles in a dark neutral, draw the icons and card edges in grey or a pale tint, and keep full-strength blue for the buttons and links. The CTA then becomes the one thing on the page in that colour.

2. **The hero never shows the product, and its best ask is buried.** The headline promises "booking links that fill your calendar", but there's no booking page, no calendar and no picture of a slot being picked. Instead, the whole right half of the hero (about x 680–1216, y 100–390) is empty. The page tips left: all the weight is stacked in one column and nothing balances it (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). That space looks left over, not gathered on purpose (Ruder, "Form and counter-form"). The copy doesn't make up for it. "Booking links that fill your calendar" and "Share one link, let people pick a time that works" could sit on any competitor's page, so a visitor can't tell what's different here. "See a live example" is the strongest ask on the page because it lets the viewer judge the product for themselves, but it's a small underlined text link. Defect. Fix: put a real booking page in that right half, ideally for one of the named customers (for example Harbor Dental's link, showing guest-local times). The headline then has a picture that proves it, and "See a live example" has something to point at. If an image was meant to be there and failed to load, check in a live browser. As captured, it reads as an empty half.

3. **The feature cards are heavy and noisy.** Each of the four features sits inside a 2px blue box with rounded corners, an icon, a pill badge and a "Learn more". Together they form a solid band that outweighs the hero above. The borders hold no information that the column gaps don't already give you (Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, on over-ruled grids; Tschichold on ornament). The badges also mix two meanings: "New" and "Beta" are statuses, while "Auto" and "Email" are just attributes. A reader learns that the badges mean nothing in particular and stops reading them. On card 1, the badge also pushes "Shared calendars" onto two lines, so the badge floats beside the title and card 1 ends up out of line with the others (see below). Defect for the badges, convention for the boxes. Fix: drop the borders or swap them for a pale tint, and keep a badge only where it states a status the viewer cares about ("New" and "Beta"). That gives every title room for one line.

4. **The customer names read as footer links.** "Harbor Dental … Orchard Vets" is a row of small grey text under a blue rule, with no label. With no "Used by" line and no logos, it looks like secondary navigation, and the social proof is lost. Defect. Fix: add a short label that says what these are (for example "Booking with Slotwise:"), and consider one line of result from one of them.

**Smaller things.**
- Card 1's two-line title pushes its body text and "Learn more" about 30px lower than in the other three cards, so the rows don't line up across the cards (Müller-Brockmann). Fix this by stopping the title from wrapping.
- The icons mix two styles. The calendar is an outline, while the clock, envelope and form are solid. Pick one style. This is common sense: a set side by side should read as one family.
- There are four identical "Learn more" links. Name each one ("More on reminders") or make the whole card the link (WCAG 2.4.4).

**What's working.**
- The type scale is disciplined. There's a bold 48px-ish headline, a regular subhead about 20px, card titles about 20px bold, body about 15px and small badges, and each level is plainly distinct. Keep this when you change the colours.
- The copy speaks in the viewer's terms. "So nobody has to do the math", "cut down on no-shows" and "so the first minutes are useful" each end on what the feature does for the viewer. Keep that voice.
- There's one clear primary action ("Start free" and "Create your link" are the same action, styled the same) with a secondary text link beside it. That structure is right; it only needs the colour room described in finding 1.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half of the hero is an image or animation that hadn't loaded when the screenshot was taken. Check in a live browser.
- Hover and focus states on the cards and links.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (visual and headline together; one look per element; headings name sections)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 8 yes, 5 no (alignment; baselines across columns; accidental groups; gathered white space; balance)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 8 yes, 1 no (saturated colour kept to a small share)
Pictures: 12 lines, 9 yes, 3 no (image shows content; one rendering style; icon stroke)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 31 yes, 2 no (controls look like what they do; link purpose)
