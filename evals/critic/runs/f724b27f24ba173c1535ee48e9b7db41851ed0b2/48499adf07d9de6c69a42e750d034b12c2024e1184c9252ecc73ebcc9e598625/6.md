**First read.** My eye goes to the blue headline, drops to the "Create your link" button, then gets pulled straight down into the four blue-outlined cards, which are almost as loud as the hero. It reads right away as a scheduling-link SaaS homepage for small service businesses. It feels clean and competent but templated: blue type on white with a row of feature cards, and nothing that's clearly Slotwise's own.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the hero of a marketing homepage that should get small service businesses (clinics, tutors, coaches) to create a booking link.

**Verdict.** It mostly works. The message is clear, the ask is specific and there's one obvious primary action. Two things hold it back. Blue is on nearly every element, so emphasis means nothing below the headline. And the hero never shows the product, so its right half is an empty hole where the proof should be.

**What would move it most**
1. **The accent blue is everywhere, so nothing below the headline stands out.** It's on the headline, all four nav links, the Start free button, the Create your link button, the "See a live example" link, four 2px card borders, four icons, four card titles, four badges and four "Learn more" links. Because the cards carry the same blue as the button, they compete with "Create your link" for the second look, and the eye leaves the main action early. *Convention* (Apple HIG and Material: an accent marks the main action and interactive state, not decoration). The fix: keep blue for the two buttons and the links. Set the card titles in near-black, the icons in a neutral or muted tone, and the card borders as a light gray hairline or no border at all. The headline can stay blue or go dark. Either way, the button should end up the only strong blue block in the hero.
2. **The hero shows no product, which leaves the right half empty and makes "fill your calendar" a claim you have to take on trust.** From x≈660 to 1216 above the cards there's nothing, so the page tips hard to the left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The bigger cost is the missing proof. A visitor never sees what a guest sees: the booking page, the time slots, a confirmed meeting. "See a live example" is a good ask, but it's only a text link, and only the visitor who clicks it sees the evidence. *Defect.* The fix: put a real booking page in that space, ideally one of the listed customers' pages with slots in the guest's time zone. That pairs the picture with the headline: the words promise a full calendar and the image shows how it gets filled. If this capture missed an image that didn't load, check it in a live browser.

**Smaller things**
- **Badges:** "New" and "Beta" are statuses, but "Auto" and "Email" just repeat the card title, so the same pill shape means two different things. Keep the status badges and drop the other two.
- **Card text out of line:** the badge forces "Shared calendars" onto two lines, which pushes that card's body and its "Learn more" about 30px below the other three. Give the badge its own line, or keep titles to one line, so all four rows line up (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- **Four identical "Learn more" links:** out of context they all read the same (WCAG 2.4.4). Use "How shared calendars work" or similar.
- **Customer names have no label:** plain gray names under a rule look like footer navigation, not proof. Add a line such as "Booking for 2,000 clinics, tutors and vets", or use real logos.
- **Two buttons for the same action:** "Start free" and "Create your link" both start signup under different words. Use the same words on both so visitors don't wonder whether they're two different things.

**What's working**
- **The ask is concrete.** "Create your link" names exactly what you'll do, and "See a live example" lets visitors judge the product for themselves instead of trusting the copy. Keep that pair. It just needs the example to be visible on the page.
- **The card copy talks about the reader's own day.** "So nobody has to do the math", "cut down on no-shows" and "the first minutes are useful" each end on what the visitor gets, not what the feature does.
- **The type scale is clean.** There are about four levels (headline, subhead, card title, body/small), with big steps between them and comfortable line lengths in the subhead.

**Where it could go further**
1. **Let the customer list do the selling** *(taste)*. Harbor Dental, Kestrel Physio and Orchard Vets tell a niche story (appointment-based local businesses) that a generic headline throws away. A headline written for that audience, about no-shows and front-desk phone calls, would set Slotwise apart from every other scheduling tool. Right now the headline could sit on any competitor's page.
2. **Give the page one image that only Slotwise could show** *(taste)*. Show a week of calendar going from gaps to full, or a reminder that saved a slot. One owned visual would stop the page reading as a template.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Focus styles on the buttons and links, and how the four cards reflow on a phone, where the two-line-title misalignment will get worse.
