**First read.** The eye lands on the serif headline, then drops straight to the first card's photo: a dark, wood-panelled lounge with red leather sofas, the only photograph in a row of flat pastel illustrations. It pulls the eye away from everything else and reads as a different website. After that the page reads as a calm, friendly local home-services site with clear prices, but there's nothing to do except the small "Book a visit" button in the corner.

**What it's for.** Assumption: the home page of a local home-services company, meant to get a homeowner to pick a service and book it.

**Verdict.** It's a clean, competent page that mostly works. The biggest problem is the window-cleaning photo, which breaks the card set and shows the wrong thing. Close behind is a subhead that promises "pick a service, choose a day" when the page gives you no way to do either.

**What would move it most**
1. **The window-cleaning photo breaks the set and sells the wrong thing** (top-left card). Three cards use flat illustrations on a mint tile. This one uses a dim, busy photo of an old panelled room with leather sofas and lamps on. Its browns and ambers are the only colours on the page outside the teal, coral and mustard palette. The viewer's eye goes there first, for the wrong reason, and what they see is a gloomy room, not clean windows. It doesn't say "streak-free" at all. This is a defect, not taste. The mismatch alone is a convention break: Gerstner's point in *Designing Programmes* is that elements of one kind follow one program, and this card looks like it came from another design. On top of that, the picture doesn't show the service. Fix: draw a window-cleaning illustration in the same style as the other three (one window with a squeegee or a sparkle would do). Or, if you want photography, move all four cards to photos with one shared grade and crop.
2. **The subhead promises a flow the page doesn't offer** (subhead and cards). "Pick a service, choose a day, and a vetted crew arrives" tells the viewer what to do. But the cards have no button, link, or arrow, and there's no date picker. The only action is "Book a visit" in the far top-right, far from the cards. So the viewer reads "pick a service", looks at the cards, and can't tell whether they can click them. This is a defect: the next step the copy names isn't in front of them (common sense). Fix: make the step the copy describes visible on the cards. Give each card a clear action such as "Book window cleaning" or a "Choose a day" link, or put a booking action straight under the row. Then the instruction and the controls match.
3. **"From $24" for laundry pickup gives no unit** (third card). The service runs Monday to Wednesday, so $24 could mean per bag, per load, per pound or per week. The other prices read as per visit, so a viewer might assume this one does too. That assumption may be wrong, and a price is what people hold you to. This is a defect. Fix: name the unit ("From $24 a bag"). Check that the other three say what you get for the "from" price as well, e.g. "From $89 for up to 10 windows" (taste on the wording, but the missing unit is a defect).

**Smaller things.**
- The headline breaks as "on your / schedule", leaving one word alone on line 2 and splitting the phrase (Bringhurst 2.4.2 on a short last-line word). Widen the headline box so it fits on one line, or break it as "Help around the house, / on your schedule".

**What's working.**
- The type plan is clear. The warm serif for the wordmark and headline against a plain sans for everything else gives three or four obvious levels, and the headline dominates without shouting. Keep that pairing and scale.
- The cards follow one tight program: a tinted image tile, a bold title, two lines of description, and the price pinned to the same baseline in teal. Prices line up across all four cards, so they can be compared at a glance.
- The restrained palette (cream ground, dark slate, teal accent, coral and mustard only in the illustrations) feels domestic and trustworthy, which suits the job. Keep the illustration colours inside it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards are clickable and show a hover state, and how the four-up row reflows on a phone, where most people will book home services.
