**First read.** The eye lands on the six coloured blocks, not on the title, and moves around them row by row without settling on any one; the red and the mustard pull hardest. It reads as an image gallery for a garden show with the pictures not yet filled in. It feels tidy and friendly, but empty, because the blocks are flat colour with a pale circle and show nothing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page where visitors to a garden show browse its areas (Rose arch, Herb bed and so on) and decide which ones they want to see.

**Verdict.** As it stands, this does not do its job. A gallery is the pictures, and none of the six "images" shows the place it names, so the viewer can't see a single garden. Once real photos go in, the next problem is that the page has no clear starting point.

**What would move it most**
1. **The pictures are placeholders** (all six cards, top two-thirds of each). Each card is a flat colour field with a centred, bottom-cropped pale circle. Nothing tells the viewer what the rose arch or the pond edge looks like. The gallery's whole promise is to show the gardens, so the viewer has to choose from captions alone, and the captions are only a few words long. If these are images that failed to load, check the live page. If they are the design, they are the main defect. *Defect (common sense; the catalog has no entry for it).* Fix: put in real photographs of each area, cropped to one shared ratio and treated the same way (similar light and grade), so the six read as one set and the subjects carry the colour instead of the flat fills.
2. **The page title doesn't lead** (top-left, "Garden show gallery"). It looks about 20 px, the same as or a little smaller than the card titles below it (about 22 px), in the same bold. So the title reads as a seventh label rather than the head of the page, and the six equal cards fight for first place. Tschichold asks that the order of importance show through differences of size (*The New Typography*); here two levels nearly collide. *Convention, with a real cost: the viewer gets no orientation before the grid.* Fix: make the title clearly the top level, with a big step above the card titles, and keep the card titles where they are. Three sizes are enough: title, card title, caption. If one area is the show's highlight, letting that card lead is the stronger version of this fix.

**Smaller things.**
- Rose arch caption (top-left card): the title sits about 2 px higher and the description about 4 px higher than in the other five cards. The title almost touches the image, and its baselines miss those of Herb bed and Pond edge next to it. This is the only card that breaks the pattern, so it reads as a bug. Give it the same image-to-title gap as the others (Gerstner, *Designing Programmes*; Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).

**What's working.**
- The grid: three equal columns, even gutters, a generous left margin, and a clear gap between rows that is larger than the gap between an image and its caption. Each card holds together as a unit, and the rows read cleanly. Keep these spacing values when the photos go in.
- The card text: bold title plus a smaller grey caption gives two levels you can tell apart at a glance, and the grey still looks readable.
- The captions are concrete and plain ("Thyme, sage and chives." "Tomatoes in late July."). They name real things, not marketing copy.

**Where it could go further.**
1. *Taste.* Make each caption answer the visitor's next question. Right now it says what is there. A visitor at a show also wants to know where it is and when it's worth going ("By the east gate", "Best before noon", a stand or map number). One added fact per card turns a list of pretty pictures into something the visitor can plan a walk around.
2. *Taste.* Choose one card to lead. Six equal tiles is a catalogue. Letting the show's centrepiece span two columns or open the page, with a big contrast of scale against the rest (Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*, on contrasts of much against little), gives the page an entrance and a reason to start there.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the cards are links: there is no hover state and nothing on the cards says they are clickable. Check that they are, and that it shows.
- Whether the colour blocks are failed image loads or the intended design. Check on the live page.
- How the three columns reflow on a phone.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 5 yes, 3 no (image and headline make one idea; every element does a job; repeated elements match)
What matters first: 3 lines, 1 yes, 2 no (one element dominates; levels distinct)
Arrangement and space: 13 lines, 11 yes, 2 no (no near-miss alignment; baselines across columns)
Text: 30 lines, 30 yes
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 11 yes, 1 no (every image shows its content)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: skipped, no visible controls
