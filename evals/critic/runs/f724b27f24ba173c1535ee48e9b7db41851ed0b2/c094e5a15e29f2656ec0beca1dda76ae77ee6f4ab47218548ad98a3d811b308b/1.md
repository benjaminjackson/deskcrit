**What it's for.** My assumption: to tell local people, donors and potential volunteers what the food bank does and how people get help, so they trust it and move on to "Get help", "Give food" or "Volunteer".

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The two images are identical except for one change: version 1 centres both body paragraphs and version 2 sets them flush left. That one change decides it. Version 2 is a clean, readable section. Its biggest remaining gap is that it ends on three numbers and gives the reader nothing to do next.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- In version 1, both paragraphs (four lines each, under the heading) are centred, while the heading above and the stats below sit flush left on x≈96. This creates two problems:
  - **Hard to read.** The left edge of each line moves (for example, line 1 starts at x≈126, line 4 at x≈337), so the eye has to search for the start of every new line. That is tiring in an eight-line block. This is a **convention** break (Tschichold, *The New Typography*; Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, 2.1.3: keep centring for a few short lines).
  - **No shared edge.** The centred block shares no line with the heading or the stats, so the section stops reading as one column and looks like three things stacked up.
- Version 2 sets the paragraphs flush left on the same edge as the heading and the stats. That gives one strong left edge from the heading to "collection points", and each line starts where the eye expects. Nothing else differs.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The section ends without a next step.** The last thing the reader meets is "4 collection points". The text names exactly the things people will want to act on: a referral from a doctor, school or housing office; four community centres; home delivery. But it doesn't say how to do any of them, and the only way on is the small nav at the top right.
   - **Who loses:** someone who needs a parcel has to work out that "Get help" is where to go. A potential donor or volunteer, warmed up by "96 volunteers", has nowhere to go at the moment they're persuaded.
   - **Fix:** under the stats, add one or two direct links that name the act, such as "Find your nearest collection point" or "Drop food at Carver Road on Tuesday or Friday".
   - **Kind:** defect (common sense; no catalogue entry quite covers it).

**Smaller things.**
- **Body lines run long.** Lines in both paragraphs run about 82–88 characters, against Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the text column by about 15% (to roughly x≈96–740), or bump the size a little.
- **The stats have no reference point.** "412 parcels last month" doesn't say whether that is up, down or typical. One short comparison, such as "up from 350 in September", would make the number mean something (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design").

**What's working.**
- **One left edge (version 2).** The heading, the body and the stats all start at x≈96, so the section reads as one column. Keep this.
- **A clear type pairing.** The heavy sans for the heading and numbers against a book serif for the paragraphs makes the levels obvious without extra sizes.
- **Concrete copy.** Tuesday and Friday, Carver Road, three days of meals, a driver to your door: a stranger knows exactly what happens. Few charity pages are this specific.

**Where it could go further**
1. **Give the numbers more room (taste).** The three stats are the proof that this food bank is real and busy, but at about 32 px they sit close to the heading's weight and get read as a footnote. Making them bigger, or setting them apart in their own band, would let donors take in the scale at a glance.
2. **Address the person who needs help as "you" from the start (taste).** The second paragraph shifts to "you" only at "if you cannot get to a centre". Opening it with "You can reach us through…" would tell the reader in need that this page is for them, which is the reader with most at stake.
