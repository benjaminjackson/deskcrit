**First read.** The eye lands on "Cross the bay in twelve calm minutes", drops through the paragraph, and stops on the blue "See the timetable" button. That order is right. Then it hits the grey line under the button, "Design review: failed, everything on this page is centred", and the spell breaks. It reads as a ferry landing page that is calm, clean and quite bare. Everything sits in the left 60% of the frame, and the right side is empty white.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the landing page for a local passenger ferry, meant to get day-trippers and commuters to open the timetable.

**Verdict.** The core works. The headline is specific, the order is clear, and there is one obvious action. Two things hold it back: a stray internal note shipped in the copy, and a frame whose right half holds nothing, so the page says "ferry" without ever showing the bay.

**What would move it most**
1. **A leftover review note is published as copy.** It is the last line, under the button, set at about body size. A passenger reads "Design review: failed…" and wonders whether the page is broken or a test, which undercuts the trust the headline just built. The note is also wrong: every block is flush left and nothing is centred, so it is noise in every sense. Delete it. Defect (common sense: unfinished copy).
2. **The right ~40% of the frame is empty, and the page never shows the bay.** All the weight is stacked on the left column, so the page looks lopsided at this width (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance": a heavy side needs a counterweight). A ferry sells the crossing itself: the water, the open deck, the harbour sliding past. The paragraph describes that scene, but nothing lets you see it. If a hero image belongs there and simply failed to load, this is a capture problem rather than a design one, so check it in a live browser. If the space is meant to be empty, give it one strong picture of the open deck or the bay. Let that picture do what the headline doesn't say: show the calm, rather than illustrating the word "bay". Defect if an image is missing; taste if the emptiness is intended.

**Smaller things.**
- The body paragraph (~20 px) and the feature list (~18 px) are close in size, in the same face and weight. They read as two settings of one level, not two levels (Bringhurst 3.1.1, "Don't compose without a scale"). Set them at one size, or set the list clearly apart with markers or weight rather than a 2 px size step.
- "Seats on the open deck" repeats the paragraph's "from the open deck". Swap it for a fact the passenger needs, such as the fare or where the north landing is.

**What's working.**
- The headline makes one concrete promise, with a number and a feeling ("twelve calm minutes"). It could not sit on any other company's page, so keep that specificity.
- There is one primary action, and it names exactly what you get, "See the timetable". It is the right next step for someone deciding when to go.
- One left edge, one blue (the brand line and the button) and generous spacing between groups. The quiet system suits the word "calm".

**Where it could go further.**
1. Put the next departure on the page (taste). The page's job is to get people onto a boat. "Next boat 10:40 from the north landing" answers the question the button defers, and lets the viewer check the claim against their own clock instead of taking "every forty minutes" on trust. "Early morning until late evening" is too vague for something people plan around, so give the first and last sailing times.
2. Make the headline bigger relative to the text (taste). It is about 2.8 times the body size, and it is the whole pitch. A bolder jump would let it hold its own against a full-bleed photo of the bay, if that is where the right half goes (Refactoring UI on hierarchy through contrast).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether a hero image was meant to fill the empty right half and failed to load. Check in a live browser.
- How the left-heavy column reflows on a phone.
