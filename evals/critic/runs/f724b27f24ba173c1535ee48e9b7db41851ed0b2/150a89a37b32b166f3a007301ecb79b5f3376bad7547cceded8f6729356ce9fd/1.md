**First read.** In both versions the eye lands first on the big dark serif headline, "Hold a real conversation in Italian by summer". It then reads the short paragraph and crosses to the Italian chat on the right. In version 1 the eye then stops at three identical orange buttons and has to read all three to choose. In version 2 it goes straight to "Start your first lesson", and the two underlined links read as side options. The page feels warm, calm and grown-up, like a friendly language course rather than a game-like app.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the top of a landing page for an Italian course, and its job is to get a beginner to start a first lesson.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The two versions are identical except for the buttons. Version 1 gives three actions equal weight, so the page doesn't say what to do next. Version 2 makes one action the main one and drops the other two to links, so the next step is obvious.

**What would move it most**
1. **One action leads (this is the change between versions).** In version 1, "Start your first lesson", "See the course plan" and "Gift a course" are the same filled orange button at about the same size. A visitor ready to act reads all three and weighs them, and "Gift a course" gets as much pull as trying the product, though few visitors come to buy a gift. The three slabs are also the loudest colour on the page, so the orange gets spread across three choices instead of marking one. This breaks a convention, and here it has a real cost: one action per view should stand out, and the rest should be outlined or text buttons (Material Design 3, "Buttons"). Version 2 fixes it in the standard way: a filled button for the first lesson and underlined text links for the course plan and the gift. Keep that.

**Smaller things**
- **Chat panel (both versions):** the bottom third of the beige panel is empty under the last bubble. It looks like the conversation stopped loading or someone forgot to fill it in. Either end the panel close under the last bubble, or use the space for what the copy promises: the teacher correcting a mistake as you speak. A correction would show the product's real difference better than three ordinary chat lines. This is my own judgement.
- **Copy (both versions):** "Start your first lesson" names one exact action, which is good. But nothing near it says what the first lesson costs or how long it takes. "Free, 15 minutes" under or inside the button would remove the doubt that stops a click. This is my own judgement.

**What's working**
- The headline names an outcome and a deadline ("a real conversation… by summer"), and its heavy serif against the light sans body gives a clear two-level hierarchy.
- The paragraph is specific: fifteen minutes a day, a weekly call, a teacher in Bologna who corrects you while you speak. It's set at a comfortable measure of about 60 characters per line.
- The Italian chat beside the headline shows the promise instead of repeating it in words.

**Where it could go further**
1. Make the chat show the correction, not just the conversation: a struck-through word and the teacher's fix inline. Correction "as you say it" is the one claim another course can't easily copy, and the picture should carry it. This is taste.
2. Give the primary button a bit more weight than the links in size as well as colour, for example more height, so on a wider screen it still clearly reads as the next step. This is taste.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Hover and focus states for the links in version 2, and how the three actions wrap on a narrow screen.
