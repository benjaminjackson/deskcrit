**First read.** In both versions the eye goes straight to the big black title, "Change the card you pay with", and then to the intro. In version 1 it then steps down through three bold subheads, so you know at a glance that there are three things you can do. In version 2 the page below the intro looks like six paragraphs of the same grey text, and you have to read each line to find where a task starts. Both feel calm, plain and trustworthy, as a help page should.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a help-centre article for a customer who wants to change their payment card. They'll scan it to find the step they need: add the card, make it the default, or fix a declined card.

**Verdict.** Version 1 does the job better. The only change between them is the three step headings. In version 1 they are bold and a size up from the text. In version 2 they are regular weight at body size. That one change takes away the page's scannability, which is its main job.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's step headings don't look like headings (defect).** "Add a new card", "Make it the default" and "If the card is declined" are the same size, weight and colour as the paragraphs under them. The only thing that marks them is a slightly larger gap above. A reader who arrived with a declined card can't jump to that section. They have to read down the page line by line to find it. The page needs two to four levels you can tell apart at a glance (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Lupton). Version 1 has four: title, subhead, body, breadcrumb. Version 2 drops to three, and the missing level is the one a scanning reader uses. The fix is what version 1 already does: make the subheads bold and a step larger, and keep them tight to their paragraphs.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the intro and the "declined" paragraph run about 80–85 characters, a little past Bringhurst's 45–75. Each paragraph is only two lines, so the cost is small. Narrowing the column by about 10% would fix it.
- UI labels in the instructions ("Settings", "Billing", "Add card", "Use for payments", "Save") are set the same as the prose. Setting them in bold would help the reader match the words to the buttons on screen. This is taste, and common sense.

**What's working.** (These apply to version 1, and to version 2 apart from the headings.)
- The title is a plain task in the reader's own words. The intro answers the worry readers actually have ("nothing is charged when you make the change") before giving any steps.
- The "declined" section heads off the most likely failure and says exactly what to do ("call the number on the back of the card").
- It's one narrow column with generous space and a breadcrumb for context. There's nothing on the page that doesn't need to be there.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Number the first two sections as steps ("1 Add a new card", "2 Make it the default") and keep "If the card is declined" visibly apart as a troubleshooting section. That shows the reader that two of these are a sequence and one is a fallback. This is taste.
2. Put a small screenshot or an inline picture of the "Use for payments" menu next to step 2, since "open the menu" is the vaguest instruction on the page. This is taste.
