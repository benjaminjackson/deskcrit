**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", moves right to the white form card, then down to the blue "Send message" button. It reads as a design studio's contact page, aimed at prospective clients. It looks tidy, calm and competent, but it could be any company's contact page, and nothing on it tells you this studio's work is worth hiring.

**What it's for.** My assumption: Fernwick Studio's contact page, meant to get a prospective client to send a useful first enquiry and to feel confident doing it.

**Verdict.** The page works as a form. Nobody will be confused. It is weak as a design studio's pitch: it looks like a framework default, and the one specific thing it asks for (budget and timeline) has nowhere to go in the form. Fix those two before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The studio shows no design of its own.** The whole page is a framework default: Inter throughout, a light grey background, a white rounded card, standard link blue, and underlined nav. For a company that sells design, the contact page is the last thing a client sees before deciding to write, and here it gives them nothing to remember and no sign of taste. "Tell us about your project" is also a headline that could sit on any agency's site. The colours and type set no mood at all, so the piece doesn't carry the message "we're good at this". Direction: give the studio one thing it owns here, such as a typeface with a point of view for the headline, one colour that isn't link blue, or a headline in the studio's own voice. The underlying problem (looking like everyone else at the moment a client decides) is a defect. Which device to use is taste.
2. **The budget and timeline request is cut off from the form.** The intro asks the viewer to "Include a budget range and a rough timeline", but that sits in the left column, about 450 px away from a bare, unlabelled-for-purpose "Message" box. By the time the visitor is typing, the request is out of view. They have to remember it, and many won't, so the studio gets enquiries without the two facts it needs and has to chase them by email (Nielsen, heuristics 6 and 10: recognition rather than recall, help placed at the step it concerns). Direction: put the ask where they type. Either add two short optional fields (a budget range picker and a timeline field) or add hint text under "Message" that names both. Defect.
3. **The layout fills its frame by accident.** The headline column and the card take up the left 1068 px. The card's right edge lines up with nothing: the nav ends at about x=1224, so there's a 160 px strip on the right that belongs to no column. Under the intro and below the form, about 40% of the screen is empty grey. This isn't open space used on purpose (Ruder: space gathered against density). It's leftover, so the page tips left and looks unfinished, as if a block failed to load (Müller-Brockmann on one grid; Arnheim on balance). Direction: make the columns span the same width as the header (card to the 1224 edge, or both columns on a grid built from the header). Or, if a narrow form is the point, make the empty area clearly intentional rather than residual. Convention, and it costs something here.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" is drawn exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing shows which page you're on. Mark it with one cue, such as weight, a bar or colour (Laubheimer, NN/g menu checklist).
- Input borders look like mid-grey on white, maybe about 2.5:1. Measure them: WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 for field edges. Darken them a step if they fall short.
- Fields don't show which are required. If all three are required, one line saying so is enough. If any are optional, mark them "(optional)" (Budiu, NN/g).
- The headline's cap top sits about 4 px below the card's top edge, close enough to look like a missed alignment. Align them exactly, or offset them clearly (common sense).

**What's working.**
- The intro's first sentence, "We read every message and reply within two working days", is the best thing on the page. It's specific, it makes a promise, and it answers the visitor's real worry (will anyone read this?). Keep it, and write the headline to the same standard.
- The form itself is clean: labels sit above their fields in bold and close to them, it's one column, and there's a single unmistakable primary button. Filling it in takes no thought.
- The type hierarchy is clear. There are three sizes with real steps between them (headline, body, labels/links), so the eye always knows what's what.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus rings on the fields and the button.
- Whether the email field uses an email input type and validates on blur.
- What happens after "Send message": confirmation and error states. These matter on a page whose only job is getting the message through.
