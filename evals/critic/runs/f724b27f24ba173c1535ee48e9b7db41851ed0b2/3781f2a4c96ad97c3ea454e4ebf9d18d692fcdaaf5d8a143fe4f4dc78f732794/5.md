**First read.** The eye lands on "Tell us about your project", then moves right to the white card and down to the blue "Send message" button. Straight away it reads as the contact page of a small design studio. It feels calm, tidy and safe. It also feels anonymous: apart from the wordmark, nothing here says anything about Fernwick.

**What it's for.** Assumed: a potential client should be able to send an enquiry with enough detail for the studio to judge and answer it.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The page is clear and the form is easy to fill in. The biggest problem is that the intro asks for a budget and a timeline, but the form gives no place for them, so most enquiries will arrive without them. The layout is also slightly unresolved: the content stops short of the header's right edge.

**What would move it most**
1. **The form doesn't ask for what the copy asks for.** The intro on the left says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them." The form on the right has only Name, Email and an empty Message box, with no prompt in it. When the visitor is typing in the Message box, the request is in another column and out of mind. They have to remember it, and most won't, so the studio gets messages it can't qualify and has to write back to ask. That costs a round trip on top of the promised two working days. This is a **defect** (Nielsen 6: recognition rather than recall). Fix: put the request where the typing happens. Either add two short optional fields (a budget range, ideally a few preset bands, and a rough timeline), or put the prompt in the Message field's helper text. Preset bands also make it easier for a client who is shy about giving a number.
2. **The content block doesn't sit on the page's grid, so the empty space reads as left over.** The header runs from x≈56 to the "Contact" link at x≈1224. The content stops at the card's right edge, x≈1068, which lines up with nothing. That leaves a dead ~210 px strip on the right, a void under the short intro, and a band under the card. None of these spaces is shaped or deliberate, so the page looks placed by eye rather than built. The split of ≈440 / 560 / 210 follows no proportion. This is a **convention** (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: one grid governs the page; Ruder: white space gathered into a few shaped areas). Fix: put the two columns on the same column lines as the header, with the card's right edge on the nav's right edge, or deliberately centre a narrower block. Either way, make the remaining space into one intended area rather than three leftovers.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the page you're on. Mark the current item with one cue, such as a bolder weight or a bar (Laubheimer, NN/g "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Form: nothing says which fields are required. Put an asterisk on the required fields with a one-line key, or add "(optional)" to the optional ones (Budiu, NN/g).
- Input borders: the pale gray outlines on white look close to, or under, 3:1. Measure the border colour against #fff and darken it if it falls short (WCAG 1.4.11).

**What's working.**
- The intro copy makes a concrete, checkable promise ("reply within two working days") and tells the visitor what helps. That's far better than "Get in touch". Keep it.
- The form itself is set up correctly: one column, a visible bold label above each field, a Message box that is roomy enough, and a single primary button that clearly outweighs everything else on the page.
- The type scale is clean: three plain levels (heading, body/nav, bold labels) with real gaps between them, all in one family, plus one blue used only for links and the action.

**Where it could go further.**
1. *Taste.* Give the left column something that shows the studio. A line about the kind of projects you take on, or one small piece of recent work, would turn the empty space under the intro into a reason to write. Right now the page could belong to any studio, and on a contact page that's the last chance to reassure a hesitant client.
2. *Taste.* Make the headline less generic. "Tell us about your project" is on a thousand sites. A headline that names the visitor's situation, or the studio's actual work, would carry the same invitation and give the page a voice.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and the button, which matter here because this is a form-only page. Also whether the email field validates its input and how errors appear next to the fields.
