**First read.** The eye lands first on the three big numbers (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%) and reads the green "+" lines under them as "all good". It then drops into the large chart, which has three coloured lines and nothing else: no names for the lines, no values, no dates. It reads as a tidy, generic marketing-analytics overview. The top looks calm and confident, but the chart, which is most of the page, says almost nothing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an at-a-glance dashboard for a growth or marketing person to see whether traffic and sign-ups improved over the last 12 weeks, and which channel drove the change.

**Verdict.** The structure is sound, but the page doesn't do its job yet. Its one chart can't be read: it doesn't say which channel is which or how big anything is. And one of the three headline figures contradicts the other two.

**What would move it most**
1. **The conversion change in the top-right card looks wrong.** *(Defect.)* Visits are "+6.1%" and sign-ups "+2.4%". When visits grow faster than sign-ups, conversion has to fall. Working back from the cards, the previous period was about 3,820 sign-ups from about 173,600 visits, roughly 2.20%. So 2.12% is down about 0.08 points, yet the card shows "+0.1 pts" in green. A viewer who trusts the cards concludes the funnel improved when it got slightly worse, and that is the decision this page exists to inform. Two possible explanations: this is mock data, or the comparison isn't "previous 12 weeks" (for example, a different definition of a sign-up). If so, fix the data or the label. Either way, the three cards must agree with each other, and a fall must not be coloured green.
2. **The chart's whole meaning is missing: no series names, no y-axis values, no dates.** *(Defect.)* The title promises "Visits by channel", but nothing says which channel is blue, orange, or green. Without a y-axis, the viewer can't tell whether blue is 10,000 visits or 100,000, or whether the gaps between lines are large. Without dates, they can't tie the dip at the 4th and 8th points to anything. The lines are also spaced suspiciously evenly. With no scale, there is no way to check that the axis starts at zero or that the gaps mean anything (Tufte, *Visual Display*, ch. 2, on clear labelling; Few). Colour is the only thing that separates the series. Orange and green are close for many colour-blind viewers, so even a legend would leave some people matching colours (WCAG 1.4.1). Fix: name each line at its right-hand end, in or next to its colour. Add a few y-axis values with units and week labels along the bottom. Direct labels are better than a legend here, because a legend means looking back and forth to match colours (Tufte; Wilke). The empty left margin and the band under the axis already have room for the labels.
3. **The chart title names a topic, not a finding.** *(Taste, argued.)* "Visits by channel" tells the viewer what they are looking at, not what happened. The question this page answers is "what drove the 6.1%?" A title like "<Channel> drove most of the growth" (once the channels have names) answers it in one read, and turns a chart you have to study into one you just glance at.

**Smaller things.**
- The "Last 12 weeks" label in the top-right corner looks like a fixed label. If it's the date-range picker, it needs a control cue, such as a chevron or a border. If it isn't, there's no visible way to change the period.
- The three "vs previous 12 weeks" lines repeat the same words three times under the cards. Saying it once (for example, "Compared with the previous 12 weeks" under "Overview") would let each card show just the change.

**What's working.**
- The KPI row works: one label, one big number, one change line. Each card has a clear three-step order, and the number clearly dominates. Keep that structure.
- The layout follows one grid. The left edges of the brand name, "Overview", the cards and the chart card all line up, and the gaps between cards are even. That is why the page feels calm.
- Restraint: one neutral range, a near-black for type, very light gridlines, and colour kept for the data and the deltas. Once the chart is labelled, nothing will compete with it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive.
- Hover tooltips on the chart. Even if they exist, they don't replace axis values and series labels for a quick read.
- Focus states.
