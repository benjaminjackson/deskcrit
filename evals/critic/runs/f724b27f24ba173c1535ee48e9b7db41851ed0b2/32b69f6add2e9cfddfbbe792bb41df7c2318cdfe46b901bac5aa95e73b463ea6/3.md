**First read.** My eye goes to "184,220" first, then along the three KPI cards, then down to the three rising lines. In a couple of seconds it reads as a clean, standard SaaS marketing dashboard: calm and trustworthy-looking. Then the second read fails. I can't tell which line is which channel, how many visits a line means, or which weeks are on the x-axis. The chart is the biggest thing on the page and it answers nothing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an analytics overview that lets a marketer or founder check traffic, sign-ups and conversion over the last 12 weeks, and see which channel is driving them.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The chart has no axis values, no dates and no series names, so the panel taking about 60% of the screen is decoration. And the conversion card reports a rise that its own numbers say is a fall.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart can't be read: no series names, no y-axis values, no x-axis dates.** This is in the "Visits by channel" panel, the lower two-thirds of the screen. The viewer sees blue, orange and green lines and has to guess which is organic, paid or referral. They can't say whether the blue line is 10k or 100k a week, or which week the dip at the seventh point falls in. The title promises "by channel", and the one thing that tells the channels apart is colour, with no key anywhere. Orange and green are also the pair most likely to merge for a viewer with red-green colour blindness. Every chart needs axis values with units and series names on the chart itself (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). Meaning carried by colour also has to be carried by a label (WCAG 1.4.1). The empty gutter left of the plot (x≈48–120) and the empty band under the baseline look exactly like where tick labels should be. If they failed to render in this capture, check that in a live browser. As shown, though, it's a **defect**. Fix: label each line at its right-hand end with the channel name (and ideally its latest value), put values on the y-axis, put week dates on the x-axis, and make sure the hues still differ in lightness.

2. **The conversion delta contradicts the other two cards.** This is in the top-right card. Visits are up 6.1% and sign-ups up 2.4%. When sign-ups grow more slowly than visits, conversion has to fall: about 2.20% → 2.12%, roughly −0.08 pts. The card says "+0.1 pts" in success green. A viewer who does the sum stops trusting every number on the page. A viewer who doesn't walks away thinking conversion improved, when it slipped. That's the one figure here someone would act on. It's a **defect**, and it also breaks the status-colour convention, because green is supposed to mean good (Apple HIG: Color; Material Design). Fix: compute the delta from the same source as the headline figures, show it with the right sign, colour it by direction, and probably show two decimals ("−0.08 pts"), because at this scale rounding to 0.1 hides the move.

3. **The chart title names a topic but not a finding.** "Visits by channel" says what is plotted but not what happened. Once the labels exist, the viewer still has to work out the story from three lines. The page has room for one sentence such as "Organic drove most of the growth; paid is flat." **Taste**: no approved source states this, but on an overview screen the takeaway is the reason the chart is there.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks", top right: if this is the date-range picker, it reads as a static label. Give it a chevron or an outline so it looks changeable. If it is only a label, it's fine.
- KPI delta lines ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"): small green text on white. It looks about 12–13 px. Measure its contrast against the 4.5:1 minimum (WCAG 1.4.3), because mid-greens often fall just short.
- The chart title's left edge (x≈71) sits a couple of pixels off the KPI labels inside their cards (x≈69). Match the inner padding so the panel lines up with the cards.

**What's working.**
- The KPI cards are well built. The label is small and grey, the number large and bold, and the comparison small and coloured, so each card reads in one pass. Every headline number comes with a previous-period comparison (Few). Keep that structure.
- Lines are the right form for weekly change across channels. The gridlines are faint enough to stay out of the way, and the page has no chartjunk.
- The layout is calm and correct: one grid, consistent card radius and padding, and a single sans family in three or four clear sizes.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the missing axis labels are a rendering or loading failure. Check in a live browser.
- Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and its hover and focus states.
- Whether hovering the chart shows values. Even if it does, the static chart still needs series names and axis values.
