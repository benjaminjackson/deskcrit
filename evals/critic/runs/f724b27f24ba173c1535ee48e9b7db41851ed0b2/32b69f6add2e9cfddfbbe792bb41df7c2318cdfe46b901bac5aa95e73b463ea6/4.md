**First read.** The eye lands on "184,220" at top left, moves across the three numbers, then drops into the big chart, where it stops making sense. There are three coloured lines all going up, with no names, no numbers on the axes and no dates. You can tell that three things grew, but not which things or by how much. It looks like a clean, calm, off-the-shelf SaaS analytics overview.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal growth or marketing dashboard that tells a team how traffic and sign-ups did over the last 12 weeks, and which channels drove the change.

**Verdict.** The top half works and the bottom half doesn't. The "Visits by channel" chart takes up more than half the screen, but nobody can read it. Without channel names, axis values or dates, the one question it exists to answer ("which channel is driving growth?") has no answer.

**What would move it most**

1. **The chart is unlabelled. You can't tell which line is which channel, what the values are, or which weeks are shown.** This is in the "Visits by channel" panel (y ≈ 283–757). The three lines (blue, orange, green) have no series names, no legend and no direct labels. The y-axis has gridlines but no numbers. The x-axis has twelve points but no week dates. So the viewer can't tell which channel is on top or how big the gaps are. They also can't check whether the axis starts at zero, so the gaps between lines could be exaggerated. And they can't link a dip (like the one at point 8 on the blue line) to a date. The channels are told apart only by colour, so a colour-blind viewer is worse off still. The page's layout shows the gap: there's an empty band left of the plot (x ≈ 49–121) and another under it (y ≈ 685–757), exactly where the axis labels should be. **Defect** (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, on clear labelling; Few; WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color). Fix: put each channel's name at the right-hand end of its line, in the line's colour, so the colour cue is backed by text. Add y values with units ("visits / week") and week labels on the x-axis. Labels at the line ends do the legend's job without making the reader look back and forth (Tufte; Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*). If this capture just caught the labels before they loaded, check that in a live browser. As shown, though, it's a defect.

2. **Nothing on the page tells the viewer what happened.** The KPI cards give three totals with deltas. The chart title "Visits by channel" names a topic, not a finding. The viewer has to do the analysis themselves: Visits +6.1%, but from which channel? Did sign-ups lag because one channel converts worse? Right now the page reports and doesn't conclude. This is **taste / common sense**, since no approved source on my list states it. But for a team dashboard, the point is to save someone that work. Direction: once the lines are labelled, rename the chart to say what it shows ("Organic drove most of the growth; paid flat since week 6", or whatever the data shows). Or add one short note to the line that moved most.

**Smaller things**
- "Last 12 weeks" (top right) is plain grey text with no chevron, border or underline, so it's unclear whether it's a date-range picker or just a label. If you can change it, give it a control's shape. If you can't, it's fine as is (common sense).
- The chart's green line and the green delta text in the KPI cards are close in hue, so "green" means "good" in one place and "a channel" in the other. Pick a channel colour that doesn't overlap with the status green.

**What's working**
- Every KPI card has a comparison ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). That's what turns a number into information. Keep it. The "+0.1 pts" on conversion rate, as percentage points rather than a percent of a percent, is a nice, precise touch.
- The type plan is tight and the ranks are clear: grey label, then a large bold figure, then a small coloured delta. It repeats the same way across all three cards, so each card reads the same in one glance.
- The chart has very little non-data ink: hairline gridlines, no shadows, no fill. Once labels are added, it won't need anything else.

**Where it could go further**
1. **Link the top row to the chart.** *Taste.* The cards and the chart sit side by side but don't refer to each other. A stronger dashboard would make the chart explain the cards. For example, the Visits card could say which channel contributed most of the +6.1%, or each line could end with its own 12-week delta in the same format as the cards. Then the page reads as one argument instead of two separate widgets.
2. **Highlight one series and push the others back.** *Taste.* With three lines of equal weight, nothing stands out. If the team cares most about one channel (or the one that moved), show it in full colour and grey the other two. Then the chart makes one point at a glance (Tufte's principle of putting data ink where the message is).
3. **Give the page a reason to exist beyond the template.** *Taste.* As it stands, this could be anyone's analytics tool. A sign-ups-per-channel view, or conversion rate by channel next to visits, would answer the question this business actually has: which visits turn into sign-ups.

**Couldn't judge from a still**
- Whether the chart has hover tooltips that show values and series names. Even if it does, a dashboard that you can only read by hovering fails a quick glance. So the labels are still needed.
- Whether the "Last 12 weeks" range selector opens a picker, and what its focus state looks like.
