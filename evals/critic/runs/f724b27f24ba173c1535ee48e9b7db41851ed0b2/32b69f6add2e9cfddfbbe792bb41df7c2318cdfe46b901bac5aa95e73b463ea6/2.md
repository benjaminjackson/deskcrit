**First read.** The eye goes first to the three big numbers across the top (184,220 / 3,912 / 2.12%), reads the small green deltas under them, then drops into the large chart. The chart is where it stalls. There are three coloured lines rising gently, but nothing says which channel each line is, what the values are, or which weeks run along the bottom. It reads as a clean, calm, competent SaaS analytics overview, and its biggest panel tells you almost nothing.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a marketing or growth lead opens this to see at a glance how traffic, sign-ups and conversion moved over the last 12 weeks, and which channels drove the change.

**Verdict.** The top half does its job. The KPI row is clear, honest and well compared. The chart, which takes up two-thirds of the screen, can't be read: no axis values, no dates, no channel names. The one question "by channel" promises to answer goes unanswered. Until the chart is labelled, this is a dashboard of three numbers and a decoration.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart has no labels of any kind** *(defect)*. In the "Visits by channel" panel there are no y-axis values, no week labels on the x-axis, and no legend or series names. The viewer can see that "something went up", but not which channel, by how much, or when. They can't even tell whether the gap between blue and green means 2× or 20×. Without a y scale nobody can check the baseline or the size of any change either, which is the integrity point in Tufte (*Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2): every chart labels its axes and units so the reader never has to guess what a number measures. Two empty strips sit exactly where those labels belong: about 50 px of empty gutter left of the plot and about 70 px of dead band under the baseline. So this may be labels that failed to render rather than a design choice. Check it in a live browser. If they're missing in production too, that's the fix. Direction: add y-axis values with units (visits per week), week or date ticks along the bottom, and the channel's name at the right-hand end of each line (Tufte and Wilke both favour direct labels over a legend). Then the two dead strips do work.
2. **Channels are told apart by hue alone** *(convention, WCAG 1.4.1)*. Blue, orange and green are the only thing separating the series. Orange and green are close for viewers with red-green colour deficiency, and green is also the "good" colour of the deltas just above. Direct labels at the line ends (finding 1) fix most of this. Also make sure the three hues differ clearly in lightness, not just in hue.

**Smaller things.**
- "Last 12 weeks", top right of the header: if it's a range picker, it looks like a plain caption, with no border, chevron or underline. Give it the shape of a control, or leave it as it is if it really is static text.
- Chart title "Visits by channel" names the topic, not what the chart shows. Once the chart is readable, a title such as "Organic drove most of the growth" would tell the viewer why to look *(taste; common sense, no approved source)*.

**What's working.**
- **KPI cards:** each number comes with a comparison in plain words ("+6.1% vs previous 12 weeks"). The conversion delta is correctly given in points ("+0.1 pts"), not percent, and that's easy to get wrong. The figures also agree with each other: 3,912 / 184,220 = 2.12%. Keep that discipline.
- **Type scale:** about four clear levels: large bold numbers, page title, panel titles, small grey labels. Weight and colour, not extra sizes, separate label from value. The eye knows where to start.
- **Restraint:** a quiet grey page, white cards and faint gridlines. Almost all the ink in the chart is data, so once it's labelled it will read fast.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Tie the chart to the numbers above it** *(taste)*. The KPI row shows three metrics, but the chart only breaks down one. A viewer who sees conversion at 2.12% wants to know which channel converts. Pick one: let the chart follow whichever KPI card is selected, or add sign-ups/conversion by channel. Then the top row becomes the way in, not a separate summary.
2. **Let one thing on the page own the story** *(taste)*. Every element has equal calm weight and the same polite green. A stronger version would mark the one change that matters this period, such as the channel that grew most or the dip in week 7, with a single accent or annotation. The viewer leaves knowing something, not just having seen lines (Tufte's case for words and numbers on the graphic itself).

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the axis and series labels exist on the live page and just failed to render in this capture (see finding 1).
- Hover tooltips on the chart, and focus states, especially for "Last 12 weeks" if it's interactive.
