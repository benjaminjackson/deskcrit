**First read.** In version 1 the eye runs along a row of five identical cards and doesn't stop on any of them. It reads as a list rather than a front page, and half the screen below is empty. In version 2 the eye goes straight to the big blue block and the large "New footbridge over the Soar" headline, then moves to the four smaller stories on the right. It looks like a local paper's front page, calm and in order.

**What it's for.** My assumption: this is the homepage of a local news site. It should tell a reader at a glance what the main story is today, then let them scan the rest.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. It gives the paper a lead story, and version 1 doesn't: in version 1 the footbridge story weighs the same as the library opening hours, so the page makes no editorial call and the reader has to do the ranking. Version 2 still has one big problem: every picture is a flat colour block.

**What changed, and why it matters**
- **Version 2 has a lead.** The footbridge story gets a picture about 4 times the area of the others and a headline about twice their size. The other four drop to a 2×2 grid. Now the page says what matters most today (Tschichold: differences of size show the order of importance). Version 1 shows five equal cards, so none of them is seen first. This is a defect for a front page.
- **Version 2 uses the screen.** Version 1 puts all its content in a thin strip at the top and leaves about half the viewport blank, so the page looks unfinished and tips toward the top. Version 2 fills the first screen down to about 660 px, with only a modest margin at the foot.
- **Version 2 sets its text better.** Version 1's narrow columns leave lone words on last lines ("March.", "Farm.", "November.") and split "shoot-out" at its hyphen. Version 2's wider columns set each deck on one clean line.

**What would move version 2 most**
1. **The pictures are empty colour blocks** (defect, unless they are loading placeholders). The lead's picture takes up about a third of the screen and shows nothing. On a news front page the picture does half the selling: a photo of the new footbridge would make the story concrete in a way the headline can't. Right now the largest thing on the page tells the reader nothing. Check whether the images failed to load. If these are the real assets, the page needs real photos before anything else matters.
2. **The lead's deck is too small for its headline** (taste). "Walkers can now cross from Quay Street…" is set at about the same size as the small cards' decks (around 14 px), under a headline of about 36 px. The lead's deck is the sentence that tells readers why they should care, and here it looks like a caption. Set it one step up on the type scale (about 17–18 px) so the lead holds together as a block, still clearly below the headline.

**Smaller things.**
- The lead's deck (about 643 px) and the lower right decks (about 653 px) end roughly 10 px apart. Either align their baselines or make the gap clearly deliberate.

**What's working.**
- The masthead: a bold serif title, a quiet sans nav, and a heavy black rule. It reads straight away as a newspaper, so keep it.
- The type pairing. Bold serif headlines over grey sans decks give two clearly different levels from more than size alone.
- The muted, consistent tints and the one gutter width give the page one calm voice that suits a local paper.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Make the scale contrast bolder still (taste). A front page sells urgency, and the lead could take more width or a larger headline so the gap between lead and secondary reads as an editorial decision, not a layout preset (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*: contrast of scale gives a page tension).
2. Give the secondary stories a label such as "Sport", "Business" or "Community" in small caps above each headline (taste). It would tie the grid to the nav and let readers scan by interest.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the colour blocks are images that failed to load. Check in a live browser.
