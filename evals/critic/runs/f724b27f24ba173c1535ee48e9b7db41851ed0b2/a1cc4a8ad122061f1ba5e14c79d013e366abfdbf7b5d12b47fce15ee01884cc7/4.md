**First read.** The eye lands straight on "How can we help?", the large white serif on the navy band. It drops to the search field and then spreads across the grid of eight topic cards. It reads at once as a software product's help-centre home page: calm, orderly and trustworthy. It is also interchangeable with almost any other help centre. Nothing on the page tells you what Harbor is or what usually goes wrong with it.

**What it's for.** My assumption (you told me nothing): this is the landing page of a product's help centre. Visitors arrive in the middle of a problem and need to reach the right article, or a person, as fast as possible.

**Verdict.** It does the basic job cleanly. The hierarchy is clear, the grid is tidy, and the card descriptions are well written. But it is a directory, not help. Every visitor has to put their own problem into one of eight equal categories, because the page offers nothing about what people actually come here for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page makes no guess about why people are here** (taste / common sense; no approved source covers it). Below the hero there are eight cards of exactly equal weight, and that is the whole page. There are no popular or recent articles, no notice of a current incident (Status is a small nav link at the top right), and no visible way to reach a person except "Contact us" in the header. Someone who can't sign in, or whose sync just broke, has to decide whether that counts as "Account", "Integrations" or "Troubleshooting", then click and hunt again. Help centres get most of their traffic from a handful of top issues. Put those directly in front of the visitor, for example a short "Most asked" list of five or six real article titles under the search field or above the grid. Show a status line in the hero only when something is actually down. End the page with a plain way to reach someone ("Still stuck? Message support"), because the grid currently runs out into empty space with nowhere left to go.
2. **The hero copy could be on anyone's help centre** (defect, a small one). "How can we help?" is the stock line. The subhead "Browse a topic or search the guides" only describes the two things already visible, so it spends the second line of the hero telling the viewer nothing. The placeholder "Search for articles" gives no hint of what a good query looks like. A visitor learns nothing about Harbor and gets no head start. Keep the headline if you like, but make the subhead or placeholder concrete with real tasks in the viewer's words, such as "Try 'reset two-step sign-in' or 'track my order'". That shows what can be searched and teaches the phrasing that works.

**Smaller things.**
- **Search field label:** the field has only a placeholder, no search icon and no button. The placeholder disappears once typing starts, and nothing says how to submit. Add a magnifier icon inside the field, and a visible label or a submit button.
- **Header sizes too close:** "Browse by topic" (~18 px bold) barely outranks the card titles (~16 px bold) it introduces, and the logo (~20 px bold) is a third size close to both. Either make the section heading clearly bigger, or drop it to a small muted label so weight and colour mark the difference instead of a 2 px step.
- **One-word last lines in six card descriptions:** "your team.", "recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where." and "messages." each sit alone on the last line, making the cards look ragged. Tighten the copy so the lines break better, or allow a slightly wider text area.
- **Topic list mixes product types:** "Set up your workspace", "invite your team" and "calendars" read like a collaboration SaaS, while "Orders and shipping / track a delivery" reads like a shop. A newcomer can't tell what Harbor is. If both are real, group them or name the product somewhere; if one is leftover template copy, fix it.

**What's working.**
- The card descriptions are specific, in the user's own words, and say what you can do, e.g. "Track a delivery or start a return." or "Fix sync problems and error messages." Keep that voice wherever new copy goes in.
- Clear dominance. One serif headline on a solid navy band, then one white search field, so there is no doubt where to start. The serif display face paired with the sans text gives a bit of warmth without clutter.
- A disciplined grid and icon set: four even columns with equal gutters, identical card padding, and one thin-line icon style throughout. It feels calm and dependable, which is right for someone arriving frustrated.

**Where it could go further**
1. **Let the content set the hierarchy, not the grid** (taste). Right now eight equal cards say every topic is equally likely. A stronger help centre commits to what people actually need: one prominent band of top issues, with the full topic grid as the quieter fallback. That serves the job (fastest path to an answer) better than symmetry does.
2. **Give Harbor one thing that is its own** (taste). Navy, white, grey cards and a line icon set is the default help-centre look, so the page could belong to anyone. Making the navy band, the serif or a single accent colour clearly Harbor's would make the page feel like part of the product rather than a hosted template, and that is part of what makes support feel trustworthy.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus and hover states on the cards and the search field.
- Whether the search shows suggestions as you type, which matters a lot for finding articles.
- How the four-column grid reflows on a phone.
