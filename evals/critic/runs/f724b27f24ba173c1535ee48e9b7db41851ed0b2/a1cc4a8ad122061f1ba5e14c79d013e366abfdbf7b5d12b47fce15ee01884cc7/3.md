**First read.** The eye lands on the serif "How can we help?" in the navy band, drops straight to the white search field, then moves across two even rows of topic cards. It reads at once as a product's help-center home page. It feels calm, orderly and trustworthy, and also familiar: it could be almost any SaaS company's help center.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the landing page of a software product's support site, there to get a stuck user to the right answer as fast as possible, either by searching or by picking a topic.

**Verdict.** It does its job. The hierarchy is clean, the grid is tight and the copy is specific. The biggest thing in the way is that the first screen offers only routes, never answers: every visitor has to search or pick a category and click again before anything actually helps them.

**What would move it most**
1. **The first screen has no answers on it, only categories.** Above the fold you get the search field and eight equally weighted topic cards. Nothing shows the handful of questions most people arrive with, such as popular articles, known issues or a current incident. "Status" sits in the top nav as plain 15 px text, the same as "Guides". So someone whose sync broke this morning has to guess between "Troubleshooting", "Integrations" and "Status" before they learn anything. This is common sense, not a published rule (the catalog's anticipation entry has no approved source): a help center is judged by clicks-to-answer, and here the minimum is two. The fix is to put the likely next need on this screen. That could be a short list of top questions under the search field or above the cards, or a live status line ("All systems normal", or a link to the active incident) in the hero. I can't see below 800 px, so if popular articles come further down the page, they belong higher up.

**Smaller things.**
- **Search field (hero):** the placeholder "Search for articles" is the only label, with no magnifier icon and no submit button. Once someone starts typing, nothing in the field says what it is or how to send the search. Add a search icon inside the field, and a "Search" button if you can (NN/g on placeholder-only fields).
- **Card descriptions:** single words are left alone on the last line in five of the eight cards ("recovery.", "receipts.", "data.", "where.", "messages."). Across one row of cards this makes a ragged second line that looks accidental. Rewrite or rebalance the copy so the second lines carry two words or more (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- **Copy:** the nav and the subtitle say "guides" but the placeholder says "articles". Pick one word for the thing being searched.

**What's working.**
- **Type contrast in the hero.** The bold serif headline against a sans-serif everywhere else makes one clear entry point without needing to be huge. Keep that pairing, and keep the serif for that one moment only.
- **The grid.** The card grid lines up exactly with the logo on the left and "Contact us" on the right, the gutters are equal, and the icon/title/description order repeats without exception. The page reads as one system.
- **The card copy.** The blurbs start with verbs and name real tasks ("Track a delivery or start a return", "Set up your workspace and invite your team"), so a user can match their problem to a card without guessing what a category name means.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make search the page's one confident move (taste).** The field is 560 px of white with grey placeholder text. It's correct but timid for the main tool on a help page. A taller field with an icon, slightly larger type and example queries underneath ("e.g. reset two-step sign-in") would show users how to phrase a search and reward the eye's first stop.
2. **Give it one thing that's Harbor's own (taste).** Right now the navy, the line icons and the card grid could belong to anyone. That costs little on a utility page, but one owned element would make it feel like part of the product rather than a template: an accent colour, an illustration in the hero band, or a voice in the headline that only this product would use.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the search field shows a visible focus ring on the navy background, and whether the cards have hover and focus states that show they're clickable.
