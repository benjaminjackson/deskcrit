**First read.** The eye lands on the headline, then drops to the four purple bars and goes to the tallest, Growth at 4.0%. It reads as an internal SaaS analytics page for product or growth people, and it feels calm, tidy and trustworthy. Then the questions start: is 4.0% good, and is Growth really better, or are these four numbers within a point of each other by chance? The page answers neither.

**What it's for.** My assumption: an internal analytics page that lets someone see how trial-to-paid conversion differs by plan in Q3 2025 and decide whether any plan needs attention.

**Verdict.** The craft is clean and the chart is honest, but the page can't support a decision. It shows four close rates as if their differences were real, with no sense of how sure they are and nothing to compare against. On top of that, the definition contradicts the subtitle on what counts as "converted".

**What would move it most**
1. **The bars make noise look like a ranking.** *(Defect.)* The four rates fall between 3.1% and 4.0%, and Enterprise rests on only 38 paid accounts out of 1,120 trials. Working from the table, Enterprise's 3.4% could plausibly be anywhere from about 2.3% to 4.5%. That overlaps every other plan. Growth against Starter is the only gap that looks more than chance, and only just. The chart shows none of this, so the viewer reads "Growth converts best, Starter worst, Enterprise lags" and may act on it. Wilke (*Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16) asks that samples come with ranges or a note on sample size. Fix: add error bars or a shaded interval to each bar. At the least, put the paid count under each plan name ("38 paid"), so the viewer sees how thin Enterprise is.
2. **Nothing says whether these numbers are good.** *(Defect. The takeaway-title point is common sense.)* The title, "Trial to paid conversion by plan", names the topic but states no finding. There is no previous quarter, target or trend next to any bar, so 4.0% can't be read as good or bad. Few ("Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design") calls for a comparison next to every headline number, such as the change from last period or the target. Fix: show Q2 next to Q3 for each plan, as a paired lighter bar or a tick mark, and rewrite the title to state the finding, for example "Conversion rose on every plan except Enterprise". Use whatever the data actually says.
3. **The definition and the subtitle describe two different metrics.** *(Defect: copy on the number people will act on.)* The subtitle says "became paid accounts within 30 days". The definition says trials "still open on 30 October are counted as not converted". The first is a fixed 30-day window per trial. The second is one cutoff date for everyone, which gives a 1 July trial four months and a 30 September trial 30 days. Read the first way, a July trial that paid in September doesn't count. Read the second way, it does. Anyone who recomputes the figure or compares it with another team's will get a different number. Fix: decide which rule the data uses and make both lines say it in the same words. If it is the 30-day window, the definition should say "trials not paid within 30 days of starting count as not converted."

**Smaller things.**
- The right column stops at about one third of the page height and leaves the lower right empty. That is acceptable for a dashboard, but it is the natural place for the Q2 comparison or the per-plan sample notes from findings 1 and 2.
- The table gives Trials and Paid but no rate column, so a reader who wants to check a bar against the table has to divide in their head. Add a "Rate" column, or fold the counts into the chart labels and drop the duplicate table.

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest. Bars start at zero, there is one hue, the values are labelled directly on the bars (no legend), and the gridlines are faint. Keep all of this when you add intervals or comparisons.
- The supporting apparatus is right. The subtitle states the population and window, the table gives the raw counts behind each rate, and the source line gives exact dates. That is why I could check the rates at all: 286/9,210 = 3.1%, 118/2,950 = 4.0%, and so on, and they hold.
- The type scale is tight and clear: headline, section heading, body, small label. The nav shows the current page in bold and colour.
