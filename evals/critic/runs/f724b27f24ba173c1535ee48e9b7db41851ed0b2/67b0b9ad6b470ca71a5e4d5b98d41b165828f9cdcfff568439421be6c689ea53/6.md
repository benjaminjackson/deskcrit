**First read.** The eye lands on the headline "Trial to paid conversion by plan", drops to the purple bars and settles on the tallest one, Growth at 4.0%. It then moves right to the table. This is clearly an internal SaaS analytics page. It looks calm, tidy and competent, but nothing tells me what to conclude, so I leave with four numbers and no point.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is an internal dashboard for a growth or product team. They use it to compare Q3 2025 trial-to-paid conversion across plans and decide whether any gap is worth acting on.

**Verdict.** As a chart this is clean and honest. As a decision tool it falls short. It shows four rates as if their differences were real and meaningful, but it gives no sample context, no comparison, and no stated finding. A reader could easily act on a gap that may be noise. The craft is fine. What's missing is the judgement about what the numbers mean.

**What would move it most**
1. **The bars show differences the data may not support** (defect). The four rates sit within 0.9 points of each other, and Enterprise rests on just 38 conversions out of 1,120 trials. I estimated the 95% margins myself (they're not on the page): about ±1.1 points for Enterprise, ±0.7 for Growth and ±0.35 for Starter. On that estimate, Growth (4.0%) versus Enterprise (3.4%), and Team (3.6%) versus Enterprise, can't be told apart. The labels are printed in bold to one decimal place, which makes them look more certain than they are. A reader will rank the plans and may move budget on a gap that is noise (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). Fix: add error bars or a range to each bar. Put the conversion count under each plan name ("38 of 1,120"). Then say in words which differences hold up.
2. **The page gives no comparison, so nobody can tell good from bad** (convention). Every number is a bare single-quarter value: there's no Q2, no target and no trend. "Growth converts at 4.0%" could be a record or a collapse. Few's dashboard pitfalls name exactly this: a metric with no comparison can't be judged. Fix: show the previous quarter next to each bar (a ghost bar or a small delta label), or a target line, so the chart answers "better or worse?"
3. **The title names a topic, not a finding** (taste, and common sense for a decision page). "Trial to paid conversion by plan" tells the reader what is plotted. They still have to work out what it means, and the fourth-tallest-versus-tallest read they'll get on their own is the misleading one from finding 1. Fix: once the uncertainty is shown, write the claim you can stand behind as the title or a one-line caption. For example, "Growth converts best; the other plans are within noise of each other", or, if nothing holds up, "No plan converts meaningfully better than another this quarter".
4. **The Definition contradicts the subtitle** (defect, in the words). The subtitle says "became paid accounts within 30 days". The Definition says "Trials still open on 30 October are counted as not converted". There are two readings: (a) each trial gets its own 30-day window, or (b) everything is cut off on 30 October, which gives a July trial about 90 days and a late-September trial about 30. Under (b), any plan whose trials skew towards July looks better than it is. The source line also says the events run "1 July to 30 September", yet conversions are counted up to 30 October. A reader checking the method can't tell which rule produced the numbers. Fix: state one rule, plainly, e.g. "A trial counts as converted if its first invoice is paid within 30 days of the trial starting". Make the source dates cover the conversion window too.

**Smaller things.**
- Table, top right: it gives Trials and Paid but no rate, and the chart gives rates but no counts. Anyone cross-checking has to divide by hand. Add a Rate column, or fold the counts into the chart labels and drop the table.

**What's working.**
- The chart is honest. The axis starts at zero, ticks are even, gridlines are pale enough that the bars are clearly the strongest marks, and every bar is labelled directly with no legend. Keep all of that when you add ranges.
- The numbers are right and checkable: 286/9,210 = 3.1%, 185/5,140 = 3.6%, 118/2,950 = 4.0%, 38/1,120 = 3.4%. The figures in the table are tabular and right-aligned.
- The type is quietly well judged. There's one family, a clear step from title to subtitle to labels, the source line sits small at the foot, and the current section ("Conversion") is marked in the nav with weight and colour.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Nav link target sizes (Acquisition, Conversion, Retention, Revenue) look small as text. Measure the clickable area against the 24 × 24 px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8).
- Focus indicators on the nav links.
