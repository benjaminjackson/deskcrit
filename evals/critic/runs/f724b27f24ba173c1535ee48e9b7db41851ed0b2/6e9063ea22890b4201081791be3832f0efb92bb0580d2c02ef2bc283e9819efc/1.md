**First read.** The eye lands on "Get the weekly show list", drops to the purple Subscribe button, then to the field above it. It reads at once as a newsletter signup for a local radio station: calm, tidy and clearly organised. Comparing the two versions, the only visible change is the corner rounding on the two buttons.

**What it's for.** My assumption: to get Riverside FM listeners to subscribe to a weekly schedule email on a desktop web page, with a way to preview a past issue first.

**Verdict.** Both versions do the job, and they differ only in one detail: corner radius. Version 1 is better. In version 1 the input, both buttons and the card all share the same soft rounding (about 6–8 px), so the form reads as one family. In version 2 the buttons are nearly square (about 2 px) while the input directly above and the card around them stay rounded. That mismatch is the only thing the change introduces.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 2's buttons don't match the shapes around them** (taste): In version 2, Subscribe and "Read last week's list" have corners of about 2 px. The email field 18 px above them has about 6 px, and the card has about 8 px. Seen together, the stack has two shape languages, and the buttons look as if they came from a different kit than the field they submit. It's a small cost, but a real one: the form stops feeling like one built object. No source on the approved list covers this, so I'm arguing it on its own terms. Version 1 shows the fix: one radius for every control, in step with the card. Keep version 1.

**Smaller things.**
- The email field's border and the outline of "Read last week's list" look like pale grey on white, probably below 3:1. In both versions the field's edge is the only thing that marks it as a field (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11). Darken both borders until they measure at least 3:1, and check the hex value.

**What's working.**
- The hierarchy is clean: headline, then one purple primary button, with the secondary action clearly lower as an outlined button. The eye path from headline to body to field to Subscribe never stalls.
- The body copy states exactly what you get and when: "Every Friday… programmes, guest names, and any changes". The viewer knows the deal before they type anything.
- "Read last week's list" is a good second ask. It lets the viewer judge the email by seeing a real one rather than trusting the description.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Give the station more presence (taste). Right now the purple 12 px "Riverside FM 97.4" is the only sign of whose list this is, and the card could belong to any service. A listener subscribes partly out of loyalty to a station, so a stronger mark or a line in the station's voice would give them a reason that a generic form can't.
2. Make the headline promise something specific (taste). "Get the weekly show list" names the thing but not why it matters. Something like "Never miss a guest you'd tune in for" would put the value in the listener's own terms.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus ring on the field and buttons; error state for an invalid email.
