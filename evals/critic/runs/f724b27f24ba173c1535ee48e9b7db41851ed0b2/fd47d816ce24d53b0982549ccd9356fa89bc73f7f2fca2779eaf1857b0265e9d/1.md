**First read.** In version 1 the eye lands on the five black outlines before anything inside them. The page reads as a grid of boxes, and the content sits in them like a wireframe. In version 2 the eye goes to "Your account", then to the four bold card headings, and the frames drop back into a quiet background. Both are a plain, calm account overview for a small shop. Version 2 feels finished and version 1 feels drafted.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a signed-in customer checks their details, latest order, address and card at a glance, then jumps to the one they want to change.

**Verdict.** Version 2 does the job better. The only real change is the borders: version 1 has dark outlines about 2 px thick, and version 2 has light grey hairlines. That one change decides it. In version 1 the frames are the heaviest marks on the page and pull attention from the details the customer came to read. Version 2 is a competent, quiet page. Its remaining weakness is the empty lower half of every card.

**What would move it most**
1. **Version 1's heavy outlines compete with the content (defect; this is what version 2 fixes).** The sidebar and all four cards have near-black borders, as dark as the "Your account" title and darker than the body text. That makes the boxes the strongest shapes on the screen. The eye reads the page as five compartments before it reads "Order 48213" or "Card ending 4417", and the dark rectangles carry visual weight that the card headings should have. Lupton's point about tables applies: boxes should show how things relate, "not trap each entry inside a heavily guarded cell" (*Thinking with Type*, p. 157). Tufte makes the same point about ink that carries no information. Version 2 follows the approved fix: it keeps the grouping but swaps the dark lines for a pale edge, so the lines no longer compete with the content. Keep version 2's treatment.

**Smaller things.**
- Both versions have fixed-height cards with dead space. Each card is about 280 px tall, but its content ends roughly 170 px down, so every card has an empty block about 110 px deep. The sidebar box also has an empty lower third. This space is left over, not designed, and it pushes the page taller than it needs to be. Size each card to its content, or line the action links up along the bottom of each row so the space looks intended.

**What's working.**
- The type is plain and the hierarchy is clear: a large bold title, bold card headings, regular body text, and blue links. That's three or four clear levels with no sizes that nearly collide.
- Each card follows the same pattern: what it is, the current value, then one specific action ("Change card", "Manage addresses"). A customer can scan the four cards and act without hunting.
- In version 2, the white cards on the off-white page with hairline edges show the grouping with almost no ink.

**Where it could go further.**
1. Give "Recent order" more prominence, for example a wider card or the first full row with its delivery status. It's the most likely reason someone opens this page. A stronger version would make it the first thing seen instead of one of four equal tiles (taste).
2. Use the empty card space to show something useful, such as a tracking link on the order or an expiry reminder near the card date, rather than padding (taste).
