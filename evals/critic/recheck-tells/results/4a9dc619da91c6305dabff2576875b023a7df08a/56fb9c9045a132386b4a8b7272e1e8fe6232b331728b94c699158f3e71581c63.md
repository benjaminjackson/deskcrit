**First read.** The eye goes straight to the weekday chart on the right. Its bars are now red, orange, yellow, green, cyan and blue with fully rounded ends, and they are the loudest thing on the dashboard. The blue line chart and the stat cards beside it look calm and plain, so the chart reads like a toy dropped onto a sober operations page. After the colors, the eye lands on Tuesday's "1,140" and then on the empty gap above SUN.

**What it's for.** A monthly deliveries dashboard for a produce-box business: how many boxes went out, what they earned, and when in the week the work happens.

**Verdict.** The fixes did not help. Neither finding was touched: Sunday is still blank, and Tuesday is still the only bar with a value. Instead, the weekday chart got three template tells, and its highlight is gone. That loss is now the biggest problem: the chart no longer shows which day is the peak.

**Finding status**
- sunday-bar-missing: open. The SUN slot still has its label over an empty baseline, with no bar and no value, so "zero" and "missing data" still look the same.
- weekday-values-only-for-one-day: worse. Tuesday is still the only bar with a number and there is still no scale. The blue highlight that marked Tuesday as the peak is gone, because every bar now has its own bright color.

**New problems.**
- Weekday chart bars: one rainbow color per day stands for nothing in the data. It pulls more attention than any number on the page and suggests six categories where there is really one series. (`references/tells.md` "Rainbow, glowing, or pill-shaped chart bars"; `references/color.md` "Limit categorical colors in a chart")
- Weekday chart bars: the fully rounded ends make bar heights harder to compare and are a stock template move. (same tells entry)
- Weekday axis labels: MON–SUN in uppercase replaces the sentence-case "Mon"–"Sun". It clashes with "1 Nov" on the line chart next to it and is another stock tell. (`references/tells.md` "Rainbow, glowing, or pill-shaped chart bars"; "Uppercase labels with wide tracking")
- Red/orange next to green/cyan bars: the colors carry no meaning that needs telling apart, but red and green side by side also look like a bad/good signal that isn't there. (`references/dataviz.md` "Do not rely on color alone and avoid red-green pairs")

**What would move it most**
1. **Rainbow, pill-shaped bars wiped out the highlight** (Deliveries by weekday chart). Viewers no longer see at a glance that Tuesday is the busiest day; instead they see decoration that competes with the whole dashboard. Go back to one muted gray for every bar plus one accent for the peak day, with square or slightly rounded ends, and sentence-case day labels to match the line chart. Source: tells.md "Rainbow, glowing, or pill-shaped chart bars"; dataviz.md "Use color with restraint and emphasis". *Defect.*
2. **Sunday still shows nothing** (right end of the weekday chart). Viewers can't tell whether Sunday had zero deliveries or whether its data is missing. Show Sunday's value even when it is zero (for example a "0" on the baseline); if the business doesn't deliver on Sundays, the maker should say so. Source: Tufte, on showing all the data. *Defect.*

**Smaller things.**
- Weekday chart: only Tuesday can be read as a number. Add a small value above each bar or a light value axis, using the real figures from the data. *Taste.*

**What's working.**
- The stat strip: each figure sits above a small "in October" comparison, so the month-over-month change is readable without decoration.
- The line chart: a single blue for November over gray for October, on a muted grid, is the restraint the weekday chart should go back to.
- The extras table: numbers are right-aligned with quiet dividers, so it scans easily.
