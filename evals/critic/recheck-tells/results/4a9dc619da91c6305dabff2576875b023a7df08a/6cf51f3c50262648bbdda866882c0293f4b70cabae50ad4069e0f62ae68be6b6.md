**First read.** The eye lands on the big white sans headline at top left, moves to the amber "Add your beach" button (the only colour on the page), then crosses to the tide panel on the right. It reads as a competent dark-mode SaaS landing page. It feels flat and anonymous: near-black grey, white text, one neutral sans throughout, and no wave mark by the wordmark. Nothing on the page says sea, Cornwall, or swimming except the words.

**What it's for.** To get a cold-water swimmer to trust Slackwater's tide reading and add their beach (or choose a plan).

**Verdict.** The fixes did not help. None of the three findings was touched: the chart, the plan actions and the plan row look the same as before. What did change is the brand. The deep sea-green ground, the cream serif display type, the wave mark and the amber "Next" were swapped for a stock neutral dark template. The biggest problems are still the unreadable tide chart, plus this new loss of identity.

**Finding status**
- tide-chart-unlabelled: open. The chart is still 24 grey bars with no hour labels, no height scale and no marker for now. The amber "Next" in the table below is now plain white, so the one link between the table and "now" is gone too.
- plans-no-action: open. Dipper and Year-rounder still show only a name, price and description, with no button or link.
- plans-leave-third-empty: open. The two plans still fill about two thirds of the row (x 80–800 of 80–1200), with the right third empty.

**New problems.**
- The whole page is now the "Neutral dark template look" (`references/tells.md`, from unslop by Damir Sokolovsky). It has a near-black grey ground (#161616-like), white text in a stock neutral sans, and brand colour on one button only. The before page had a tinted sea-green ground, a serif display face and cream text, all with character of their own.
- The logo lost its amber wave mark. "Slackwater" is now just a word in the body sans, so the brand has no mark at all.
- The "Next" status in the tide table went from amber to white bold. It is now the only cue for the coming slack and is weaker, and the amber button is left as the page's only colour.
- Headings ("Know when the water…", "From the car park…", "Two plans…", the prices) moved from the serif to the same sans as the body. Headings and text now differ only by size and weight, so the hierarchy is flatter (`references/typography.md` "Contrast from more than size").

**What would move it most**
1. **The tide chart has no scale** (hero panel, right). A swimmer sees a curve of bars but can't tell which hour each bar is, how high the water gets, or where "now" is. This is the product's core promise, shown as decoration. Fix: label the hours along the base (every 3 or 6 hours is enough), mark the current bar, and tie the 06:10, 12:25 and 18:40 events to their bars. Source: Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*. **Defect.**
2. **The rebrand into a neutral dark template** (whole page). A visitor who would have met a page that looked like the sea now meets one that looks like any startup, which works against trust in a local, specific tool. Fix: bring back the sea-green tinted ground, the serif display type, the wave mark and the amber accent on "Next". Then fix the findings inside that identity instead of replacing it. Source: `references/tells.md` "Neutral dark template look" (unslop, CC BY 4.0). **Defect** (template tell).
3. **The plans have no action** (pricing row). A visitor who has picked a plan has nothing to press and has to scroll back up to "Add your beach". Fix: give each plan one action under its description, with the paid plan's as the stronger one. Source: Nielsen Norman Group, call-to-action guidance. **Convention.**

**Smaller things.**
- Pricing row: the right third is still empty. Spread the two plans across the full width, or set them on a 2-of-3 grid with intent, for example with a short note in the third column using copy the maker already has. Taste.

**What's working.**
- The tide event table (time, event, Swim/Avoid/Next) is still the clearest thing on the page. It answers "when can I swim" in four rows.
- The hero copy and the single primary button, with a quiet text link as the second action, give a clear first step.
- The three-step "From the car park to the water" row is plain and specific, with headings that say what each step does.
