**What would move it most**
1. **The weekday bar chart shows a value for only one bar.** This is the Bottles by weekday card. The viewer can see Saturday is highest but can't tell how far the other days fall behind, which is what this chart is for (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*). Put a small number above every bar in the style of the existing "148", or add a light value axis like the visitors chart has. Convention.
2. **Money and counts are in mixed formats.** This covers the top row and the table's Revenue and Bottles columns. The viewer meets "$24,815.60" next to "$38", "$1,512" above "$1,159.50", and "09" below "61", so the numbers read as careless and are harder to compare down a column (`references/tells.md` "Numbers in mixed formats"; `references/typography.md` "Figures in tables and text"). Choose one format per kind of number: whole dollars everywhere, as in the before image, or cents everywhere. Drop the leading zero from "09". Defect.
3. **The October comparison lines are small and pale.** These are the lines under the four headline figures. The comparison is what gives each figure meaning, yet it is the faintest text on the page and doesn't say whether the number went up or down. Make the line a step darker and show the direction of change. Keep the comparison against October and add a sign or arrow in green or red, computed from the figures already on the page. Taste.
