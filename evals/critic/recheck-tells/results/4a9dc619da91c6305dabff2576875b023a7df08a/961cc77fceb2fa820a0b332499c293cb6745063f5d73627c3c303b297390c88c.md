**First read.** The eye goes first to the headline, "Let the harbour book its own berths". Then two violet-to-blue gradient spots pull it: the "Set up your harbour" button and the S4 berth on the plan. The gradient makes the page read as a generic SaaS template rather than a harbour tool. The muted sage, sand and navy palette the page was built on now has two pieces of colour that don't belong to it.

**What it's for.** To get a harbour master to set up Moorly by showing that it handles their pontoon plan, bookings and fees on one page (my assumption, from the page copy).

**Verdict.** The fixes didn't help. Neither finding in the work order changed. The only visible change is the move from flat navy to a stock purple-to-blue gradient on the main button and the selected berth. That adds a template tell and takes away the page's own character. The biggest problems now are that gradient and the berth labels, which are still hard to read.

**Finding status**
- tiny-berth-labels: open. The berth numbers N1 to W6 look the same as before: small, dark grey text on pale green, and they still don't read at a glance. The only change on the plan is the gradient fill on S4, covered under New problems.
- feature-row-weak-lead: open. The three feature blocks under the rule are unchanged. Each heading is a little larger than its text, but no stronger in weight or contrast than before.

**New problems.**
- The hero's "Set up your harbour" button and the S4 berth on the plan changed from flat navy to a violet-to-blue gradient. This is the stock "Purple-to-blue or violet-to-pink gradients" template tell (references/tells.md). The viewer sees a look from many other products, and it clashes with the sage, sand and navy palette. On the plan, S4 also stops sharing the colour of the rest of the plan and starts to look like a decoration instead of "booked, selected".

**What would move it most**
1. **Gradient on the main button and the selected berth:** the hero button and berth S4. The viewer reads a stock AI-template look, and the page loses the calm, specific harbour feel that made it trustworthy. Source: references/tells.md "Purple-to-blue or violet-to-pink gradients". Fix: go back to the flat navy fill the page had before. Kind: convention.
2. **Berth labels are very small and low in contrast:** the berth numbers on the pontoon plan, top right. A harbour master looking for a berth still has to lean in to read them. Source: WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3. Fix: make the labels bigger and the text darker so the contrast is at least 4.5:1. Kind: defect.
3. **Feature headings no stronger than their text:** the three blocks below the rule. They still read as one run of small text. Source: Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*. Fix: give each heading clearly more weight or size than its body text. Kind: convention.

**What's working.**
- The pontoon plan is a specific, real-looking product view: named rows, the walkway to the slipway, and the booked berth with its fee. It shows what the product does better than any generic hero picture would.
- The two-column hero, with one main button and a plain text link, keeps the next step clear. Only the button's fill needs to go back to navy.
