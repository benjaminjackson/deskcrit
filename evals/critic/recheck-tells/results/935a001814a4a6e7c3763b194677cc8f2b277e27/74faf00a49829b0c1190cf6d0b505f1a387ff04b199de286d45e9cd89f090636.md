**First read.** The eye lands on the four big numbers across the top, then drops to the blue line in Shop visitors and the blue Saturday bar, then the table. It reads as a calm, plain dashboard for a small farm shop. The sidebar is the one thing that looks off. Its icons and labels now step in and out from row to row, so the nav looks loose next to the tidy panels.

**What it's for.** It lets the shop owner see at a glance how November is going against October: sales, visitors, busy days and the top sellers.

**Verdict.** The fixes did not touch either finding. The chart and the table look exactly as they did. The only change is in the sidebar nav, and it made things worse: the icons and labels no longer sit in straight columns. The biggest thing still in the way is still the line chart, which you can only read through its legend.

**What would move it most**
1. **Line chart read through the legend** (Shop visitors card). To learn which line is November, the viewer has to go up to the legend in the corner and back. The grey October line almost merges with the gridlines. Source: Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, direct labelling. Fix: put "November" and "October" at the right ends of the lines, drop or demote the legend, and make the October line darker or a step heavier than the gridlines. *Convention.*
2. **Sidebar icons and labels off their column** (left nav). The nav now looks unfinished, which undercuts trust in a calm, precise dashboard. Source: `references/tells.md` "Icons nudged off the column". Fix: put the icons back in one fixed-width column and start every label at the same x, as in the before image. *Defect.*
