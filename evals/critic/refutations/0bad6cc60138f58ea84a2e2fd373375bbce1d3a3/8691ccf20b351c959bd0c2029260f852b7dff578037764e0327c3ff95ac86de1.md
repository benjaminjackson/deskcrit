**First read.** The eye lands on "Changing your payment card", the biggest and boldest thing at top left. It then drops to the two-line intro and runs down the three bold subheads. Within a second it reads as a help-centre article about billing. It feels calm, plain and trustworthy, with nothing competing for attention. The right 40% of the frame is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a help-centre article for an account owner who needs to swap the card they pay with, often because a payment has just failed.

**Verdict.** The layout is clean and does its job. The type scale is clear, the grouping is right and nothing is ornamental. The weakness is in the words and in what the page leaves the reader to do. The intro is ambiguous on the one question a reader with a failed payment arrives with, and an article whose whole point is "go and change a setting" gives no way to get there.

**What would move it most**
1. **The intro doesn't answer whether a new card pays what's already overdue (defect).** "The new card is used from your next invoice, and nothing already paid is moved" (top paragraph) can be read two ways. A reader whose payment just failed can't tell whether the unpaid invoice will be retried on the new card or will stay on the dead one until the account goes read-only. "Nothing already paid is moved" doesn't help, because "moved" isn't how anyone thinks about charges. It probably means "we won't re-charge past invoices", but the reader has to guess. This is a money-and-consequences line, the kind people hold the company to, so the two readings are a real defect and not a style note. Fix: state both cases in the reader's terms. For example: "If a payment has failed, we retry it on your new card straight away. Invoices you've already paid stay on the old card." Use whichever is actually true. Then cross-reference it from "If a payment fails", which also never says that changing the card is how you stop the read-only countdown.
2. **The page tells the reader where to go but doesn't take them there (common sense; the catalog's source for this is off the approved list).** "Open Settings, choose Billing, and select Add card" is a route the reader has to remember and then retrace in another tab. The page has no link, button or action anywhere: the only interactive element is the breadcrumb. Fix: make "Settings › Billing" a link, or add one clear "Go to Billing settings" action under the intro. That collapses the instructions into a single click for someone who is logged in.
3. **The steps are written as prose, and one is missing (defect).** "Add a new card" packs three UI actions into a sentence with nothing marking the button names ("Add card", "Remove"), so they read like ordinary words. "Remove an old card" says a card can only be removed "once another card is set as the default", but nowhere does it say how to set the default. A reader trying to delete their old card hits a wall the article saw coming and didn't solve. Fix: add the "Set as default" step. Mark interface labels consistently, with bold or quotes, so the reader can match them to the screen. A short numbered list would make the sequence scannable, but if the article stays this short that is a matter of taste.

**Smaller things.**
- Line length: the body lines look like about 80–85 characters ("We place a hold of one pound to check the card, and release it within three working"), above Bringhurst's 45–75. Narrow the column by roughly 10–15% to bring it back into range.
- "days." sits alone on the last line of the hold paragraph (around y≈377). That runt is worth fixing: a slightly narrower measure or a non-breaking space in "three working days" will clear it (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- "Email the account owner each time": readers who aren't the owner will wonder whether they'll hear anything. Say who else is told, or say plainly that only the owner is.

**What's working.**
- The type scale has three clean levels: a bold title, semibold subheads and regular body, with a grey breadcrumb above. Each level is clearly distinct and none collide. Keep it.
- The spacing does the grouping. Each subhead sits tight on its paragraph and there is a wide gap between sections, so the three tasks read as three chunks with no rules or boxes needed.
- The copy is mostly plain and specific: "a hold of one pound … within three working days" gives the amount, the unit and the time. Bring the intro up to that standard.

- Whether the empty right side is meant to stay empty or holds a sidebar (contents, search, "contact support") that didn't render. Check in a live browser.
