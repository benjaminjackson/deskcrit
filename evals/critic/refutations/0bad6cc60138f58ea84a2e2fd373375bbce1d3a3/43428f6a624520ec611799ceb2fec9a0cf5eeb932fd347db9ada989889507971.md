**First read.** The eye lands on "Changing your pickup time" and then walks down four bold headings that all look the same. It reads as the help or FAQ section of a bakery's click-and-collect service. It feels calm, plain and tidy. Nothing on the screen says whose bakery this is or what page you're on.

**What it's for.** You told me nothing, so I'm assuming this is a help page for someone who has already ordered bread online and has a question about pickup, collection, allergies or cancelling.

**Verdict.** The layout and type are clean and mostly do their job, but the copy lets the reader down. The cancellation deadline, the collection rule and the allergy answer each leave a customer unsure about the thing they will actually act on. Under that, the page has no title, and the text lines run too long.

**What would move it most**
1. **The deadline and the collection rule can each be read two ways (defect).**
   - "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead." A day ahead of what? It could mean 24 hours before the pickup slot, or any time the day before. Someone with a 9 am Saturday slot who cancels at 8 pm Friday is fine under one reading and too late under the other. They will hold you to the reading they chose.
   - "They will need to show it at the counter along with a name." Whose name, yours or theirs? Get it wrong and someone is turned away at the till.
   - Fix: state the rule exactly, for example "until 24 hours before your pickup time" and "your order number and the name on the order". Then put back the line that tells the reader what happens to them: "after that we can't refund it, because your bread is already baked".
2. **The allergy answer doesn't help the person reading it (defect).** "Every loaf has a card by the till" is only useful to someone standing in the shop. This reader is ordering online and wants to know before they pay. It's the one section where a vague answer could cause real harm. Fix: link to each loaf's ingredients and allergens from the order page, or list them here. Keep "ask the baker" as a backup, and say how to reach the baker before collecting.
3. **The page doesn't say what it is (defect).** There is no title and no bakery name, so four headings of the same size and weight compete to be read first. That's fine for FAQ items, but nothing sits above them (Tschichold, *The New Typography*: show the order of importance through size). Someone arriving from a link or a search has to work out from the answers that this is help for collection orders (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*: a page should explain itself within seconds). Fix: add one clearly larger page title, such as "Collecting your order", plus the bakery's name, and keep the four headings one step down. "Open your order" has the same gap: link it to the order so the reader doesn't have to go looking.
4. **The text lines are too long (convention).** Paragraph lines run to about 90–95 characters (for example, "Orders can be cancelled up to a day ahead…" on one line). Bringhurst gives 45–75 as comfortable. The text is short, so the cost is small, but the eye has to travel a long way back to the start of each line. Fix: narrow the text column to about 600–650 px at this size.

**Smaller things.**
- Under Allergies, "anything." sits alone on its last line, which is a runt (Bringhurst 2.4.2). Narrowing the column or rewording will change the line breaks. Check it again after that.

**What's working.**
- **Spacing:** each heading sits about 10 px above its answer and about 40 px below the previous one, so the four sections separate without any rules or boxes. Keep that ratio.
- **Type scale:** two sizes with a big step between them, set off further by weight. It's an obvious system that needs nothing added beyond the page title.
- **Tone:** the voice is warm and human, especially "the bread has already been baked for you". Keep it when you tighten the wording.
