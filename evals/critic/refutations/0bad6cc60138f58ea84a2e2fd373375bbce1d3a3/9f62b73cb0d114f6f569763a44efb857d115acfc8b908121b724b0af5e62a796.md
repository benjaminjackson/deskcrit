**What it's for.** You told me nothing about it, so I'm assuming this is one slide in a board sales review, meant to show directors the seasonal pattern in quarterly shipments, with Q4 2026 as a forecast.

**Verdict.** The slide is close. The structure, headline and labelling are sound, but the chart works against its own claim. The headline is about the third quarters, yet nothing in the chart marks them. The Q4 2026 forecast is drawn exactly like the actual results. The 3D bars add ink that carries no data and blur where each value ends.

**What would move it most**
1. **The forecast looks like a fact (defect).** The last bar, Q4 2026 (560), has the same solid blue as the seven actual quarters. The only clue that it's a projection sits in the subtitle and the source line. A director scanning the chart reads 560 as a result. The likely wrong conclusion is that shipments fell 145k from Q3, when that's only the forecast. Projections should look different from observed data (Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. 16). Mark the bar itself: a lighter tint or outline-only fill, plus "forecast" under its axis label. If a range exists, show it.
2. **Draw the bars flat (convention: Wilke ch. 26.1; Few; Tufte).** Each bar has a darker side face and a lighter top face. The top face rises above the true value, so the 610 bar's top edge reads nearer 620 and the value label floats above a slab rather than at a line. A board audience reads the figures in the labels, so the 3D adds nothing. It costs some precision and makes the slide look like a default template. Draw plain flat bars so that each value is read from one length.
3. **Make the chart show what the headline claims (taste).** The headline says "peak each third quarter", but all eight bars are the same blue, so the viewer has to find the two Q3 bars by reading the axis. Make the Q3 bars the strong colour and set the rest in a muted tint. The chart then proves the headline at a glance. The headline also buries the stronger story the data shows: Q3 rose from 610 to 705, about +16% year on year. For a board, "Shipments peak each Q3, and this Q3 was up 16% on last year" makes the claim land on what they care about. As written, it states a pattern and leaves them to work out the growth.

**What's working.**
- The type hierarchy is clean. The serif headline sits over a sans subtitle, then a bold chart title with a light unit line beneath it, and every level is distinct at a glance. Keep it.
- The labelling is complete and calm. Direct value labels replace a legend, the units ("Thousands of units") are stated, the axis starts at zero with faint gridlines, and the source and forecast basis are given at the foot.
- The headline states the finding instead of naming the topic. Keep that approach when you sharpen the wording.
