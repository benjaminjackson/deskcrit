**First read.** My eye goes straight to "Notes for ferry passengers", then down the left column through the bold subheads: Boarding, Tickets, Winter service. It reads at once as a plain information page for people taking a river ferry. It feels calm and orderly, like a well-kept notice board. It also feels a bit unfinished, because everything sits in the left 45% of the frame and the right side is empty.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that tells passengers how the ferry runs, how to board, and how to pay, so they can plan a crossing and arrive knowing what to do.

**Verdict.** The typography mostly does its job: the levels are clear, the text column reads comfortably, and nothing gets in the way. Two things hold it back. The words leave out the facts a passenger plans around, and the spacing doesn't separate one section from the next.

**What would move it most**
1. **The copy is vague exactly where passengers act on it. Defect.** The opening says the ferry runs "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening". Someone planning a trip can't tell whether the first boat is at 6:00 or 8:00, or whether the last one is at 21:00 or 23:00. Missing the last boat is the costly mistake this page exists to prevent. The same gap shows elsewhere:
   - "about twelve minutes… a little longer when the tide turns" gives no upper bound.
   - Tickets lists what you can buy but not what it costs.
   - The Winter service line, visible at the bottom, starts the same way ("one boat each hour, and the last…").

   Fix: put the actual first and last departure times, ticket prices, and the dates winter service starts and ends in the text. The first-and-last times belong in the opening paragraph, which is the one everyone reads.
2. **The gap above each section heading is about the same as the gap between paragraphs, so the sections don't separate. Convention.** The baseline-to-baseline distance is about 51 px between two paragraphs and about 52–54 px from the last line of one section down to the next heading. Below each heading, the text sits about 36 px away. So each heading does attach to its own text, which is good. But the break between Boarding and Tickets looks no bigger than the break between two paragraphs in the same section. A reader scanning for "Tickets" sees one even stream of text with bold lines in it, not three blocks. Equal gaps erase the groups (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. II, on proximity). Bringhurst §2.2.2 describes leading subheads with more space above than below. Fix: roughly double the space above each heading so the gap between sections is clearly bigger than the gap between paragraphs. Leave the space below as it is.

**Smaller things.**
- Tickets, first paragraph: "kiosk." sits alone on its own line, a short last word (Bringhurst §2.4.2). Rewording would fix it, and adding the prices from finding 1 would probably fix it anyway.
- The column hugs the left edge at 120 px and leaves the right ~55% of a 1280 px frame empty, so the page tips left and looks like an unstyled document (Arnheim, ch. I "Balance"). Keep the comfortable line length. Either centre the column in the frame or use the right side for something useful, such as a timetable or a map of the two landings. That would also give the missing times somewhere to go.

**What's working.**
- **Line length.** The column holds about 55–65 characters a line, with generous line spacing, which sits right in the comfortable 45–75 range (Bringhurst). Keep that width whatever you do with the layout.
- **Type scale.** There are three sizes with big steps between them: title, bold subheads, and body. Each one has an obvious job and no two sizes nearly collide.
- **The bulleted rules under Boarding.** They are short, parallel, and each starts with a verb ("Lock", "Keep", "Leave"), so a cyclist can take them in at a glance.
