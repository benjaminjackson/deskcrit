**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours" and then drops into a stack of nine evenly spaced lines. It's clearly a plain page of opening hours for a public library. It feels calm and tidy, but finding your branch takes a top-to-bottom read rather than a glance. Reading the times then gives a second jolt: "ten to four"?

**What it's for.** My assumption: local residents check when their branch or the mobile library is open, often in a hurry, often on a phone.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job reliably yet. The page looks clean, but the times are written in words that can mean two different things. Two problems sit underneath: no grouping by space, and no table structure. Together they make the one fact people come for slow to find and easy to misread.

**What would move it most**

1. **The times can be read two ways (defect, in the copy).** In British usage, "Saturday, ten to four" means 3:50, not 10:00 to 16:00. "Tuesday to Friday, ten to six" and "Saturday, ten to two" have the same problem. "Nine to eight" also gives no am or pm. Opening times are what people act on, so a wrong reading means someone turns up to a locked door or leaves early. Written-out numbers are also slower to scan than numerals. Fix: use numerals with a clear range, such as "10:00–16:00" or "10am–4pm", and use the same format everywhere.

2. **The hours are incomplete.** Main branch and Harbour branch don't say they're closed on Sunday, or that Harbour is closed on Monday. People have to work that out from what's missing, and many won't. The mobile library lines ("Wednesday at the market square", "Thursday at the school gates") give a place but no time, so nobody can actually go. Fix: give every day a value, even if it's just "Closed", and give the mobile library a time window for each stop.

3. **Equal spacing erases the three groups (defect).** The bold subheads, the lines under them and the gaps between branches all look about 45 px apart. So "Harbour branch" sits as close to Main branch's "Saturday, ten to four" as to its own hours. The page reads as one list of nine lines, not three branches, and the bold weight alone has to do all the grouping. This breaks the proximity principle (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, on Wertheimer's rule of proximity). Bringhurst §2.2.2 also sets subheads with more space above than below. Fix: tighten the space inside each branch to roughly a line's leading, and open up clearly more space above each subhead.

4. **It should be a table (taste, but I'd argue it hard).** This is lookup data: branch × day → hours. As loose sentences, the days and times don't line up, so comparing branches ("which one is open Monday evening?") means reading everything. Fix: put days in one column and hours in a right-hand column, aligned, one block per branch. Or use a small days-by-branches grid, which would also put the empty right two-thirds of the screen to use. If it gets wider, cap it so the eye doesn't travel far between a day and its hours.

**Smaller things.** None beyond the above.

**What's working.**
- The title is clearly dominant and says exactly what the page is.
- The subheads use bold weight at body size instead of yet another size, so the type scale stays to three clean levels.
- Near-black on white with a generous left margin is legible and quiet. Keep that plainness when you add the table.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How it reflows on a phone, which is likely the main use. Check that the table version stacks cleanly at narrow widths.
