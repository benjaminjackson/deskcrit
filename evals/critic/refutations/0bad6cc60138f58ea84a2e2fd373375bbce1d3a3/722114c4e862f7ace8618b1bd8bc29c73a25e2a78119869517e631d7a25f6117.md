**First read.** The eye lands on the bold serif title, "Winter timetable at the harbour", then drops into two even columns of prose. I start looking for departure times and don't find any, so I end up reading paragraphs. At the bottom, "Fares and cards" sits over a rule, with a matching rule on the right that has no heading, and nothing beneath either. It feels calm and well set, more like a parish newsletter than something you check on the quay.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that tells ferry passengers when the boats leave in winter, how long the crossing takes, and what they can bring.

**Verdict.** The typography is competent, but the page doesn't do its job. It promises a timetable and gives no departure times, so the one thing a passenger came for is missing. Fix the content before anything else.

**What would move it most**
1. **The title promises a timetable, but the page has no times in it** (defect). "Winter timetable" sits over six paragraphs of prose. The only schedule facts are "every ninety minutes" and "the first crossing" (second paragraph, left column). There is no first or last departure, and nothing to count the ninety minutes from. A passenger can't answer "when is the next boat?", so they will phone or turn up and wait. A heading should name what its section holds (WCAG 2.4.6). The fix is to put real departure times right under the title, set as a table (from the harbour / from the island, first to last boat). The prose can follow as notes. If the times live somewhere else, retitle the page to what it actually holds, such as "Winter travel at the harbour", and link to the times from the top.
2. **The facts a passenger acts on are scattered and vague** (defect, copy). Opening hours, sailing frequency, cancellations, crossing time, and what you can bring all run together in the same paragraph weight. Waiting-room trivia sits between the sailing frequency and the crossing time. Three lines can be read two ways, and those are the lines people will hold the operator to:
   - "Opens at seven… an hour later than in summer" could be read as opening at eight.
   - "Until the last boat has left" never says when that is.
   - "Notices are pinned… before the first boat is due" tells someone at home nothing. They need to know where to check from home, such as this page or a phone line.

   The fix is to pull the key facts into short labelled chunks: Times, Crossing, Cancellations, Bikes, prams and dogs. Give each one a concrete number, and give cancellations a channel you can reach before you leave home.
3. **The headless rule on the right makes the lower section look broken** (defect unless explained). "Fares and cards" has a rule under it at y≈585, and the right column has an identical rule with no heading above it. The two read as a pair, so the viewer expects a second section and sees an empty one. It looks like a heading failed to load. Either give the right rule its own heading, or run one full-width rule under "Fares and cards". If the fares content just sits below this frame, that part is fine. The headless rule still needs fixing.

**Smaller things.**
- "bags." sits alone on the last line of the bicycles paragraph (right column, y≈375). Rewrap it so the last line holds more than one short word (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- "Fares and cards" sits at the very foot of the visible area with nothing under it. Check that some fares content shows with it on common screen heights, so the heading isn't stranded.

**What's working.**
- The type is clear and well set. There are three sizes with clear steps (title about 40 px, section heading about 24 px, body about 18 px), and the headings use bold weight as well as size. Keep that scale when you add the timetable table.
- The body text reads easily. Lines run about 60–65 characters with generous line spacing, dark grey on white, in one serif family. The two columns share baselines. Keep the measure and the leading.
- The tone is friendly and human ("the crew will always help with bags", "Dogs on a lead are welcome on every sailing"). Keep that voice in the notes under the times.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the "Fares and cards" content exists below this frame or failed to render. Scroll the live page to check.
- How the two columns reflow on a phone, where most people on a quay will read this.
