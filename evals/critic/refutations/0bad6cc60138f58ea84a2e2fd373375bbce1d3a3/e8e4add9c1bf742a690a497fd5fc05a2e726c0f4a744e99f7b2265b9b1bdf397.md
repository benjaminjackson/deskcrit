**Verdict.** The chart works and the layout is clean, but the KPI row fails at its one job. All three headline numbers lack units, a time period and any comparison, so the owner can't tell what they mean, let alone whether they're good. That row is the first thing anyone sees, so fixing it matters more than everything else here put together.

**What would move it most**
1. **The three KPI cards (top row) are bare numbers.** This is a defect. "3.8" has no %. "64.20" has no currency symbol. "12.4" next to "Active subscribers" reads as a fraction of a person, so the viewer has to guess whether it means 12.4k, 12.4%, or a bug. None of the three says what period it covers (today? September? all time?), and none has a previous period, target or trend. So even a viewer who decodes 3.8% can't tell whether that's good or bad. A dashboard's headline figures only work if they carry their unit, their period and a comparison (Few, "Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design"). Fix: write the unit into the number ("3.8%", "£64.20", "12,400" or "12.4k"). Put the period in the label or in one line under "Shop overview" ("September 2026", which matches the chart). Add a small change line under each number, such as "▲ 0.4 pts vs August". Don't rely on colour alone for that change; keep the arrow and sign. The chart's source line already names September 2026, so the data is there. The cards just don't show it.

2. **The chart title names the topic, not the finding** (taste; the source for this isn't on my approved list, so I'm arguing it as common sense). "Orders by delivery day" makes the owner read off the answer themselves: Saturday is busiest at about 1,130, Friday is close behind, and Sunday falls to about 380, a third of Saturday. That's what they would plan baking and staff around. Fix: say it in the title or a subtitle, e.g. "Friday and Saturday take 40% of deliveries; Sunday is the quietest day". You could also mark Sunday or the weekend peak on the chart itself.
