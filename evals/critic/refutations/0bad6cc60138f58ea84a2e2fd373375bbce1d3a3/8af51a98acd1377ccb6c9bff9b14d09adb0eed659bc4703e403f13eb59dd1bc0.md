**First read.** The eye lands on the white headline, "Plants for shade and small spaces". It drops to the grey lede, then across three equal cards with opening hours, workshops and delivery. Then it stops, and there is nothing else to go to. It reads as a calm, tidy local garden centre home page, pleasant but flat. It tells you what they sell and then leaves you standing there.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the home page of an independent garden centre. It should get local visitors to come in, buy or order plants, or book a workshop.

**Verdict.** It looks competent and it's readable, but it doesn't do the job of a shop front. Nothing on the page lets you act on the offer it makes. A big band of generic leaf wallpaper fills the space that should sell the plants.

**What would move it most**
1. **There is no next step anywhere on the page (defect).** The lede ends on "ready to pot this weekend", a real nudge with a deadline. But no button follows it, no "see the shade plants" link, and no directions; the cards are plain text. A visitor who is persuaded has to guess that "Plants" in the top nav is the way in, and the eye path ends at the cards. Add one primary action under the lede that names the exact thing, for example "See this week's ferns and hostas" (Material Design 3 and Apple HIG on one dominant primary action). Consider making the workshop and delivery cards actions too: "Book a Saturday place" (only ten places, so it's worth asking) and "Check if we deliver to you".
2. **The hero image doesn't show what the headline promises (taste, argued).** The headline makes a specific, useful claim: shade and small spaces. The 400 px band behind it is a uniform scatter of stylised leaves, wallpaper with no subject. It shows no shady corner, no balcony, and no fern or hosta you could buy. Half the first screen goes on texture that could sit on any green brand's site, so the picture adds nothing to the words. Replace it with a real photo of the product in the setting: a shaded courtyard or a small balcony planted with the stock. Then the image proves the claim instead of decorating it.
3. **The headline is set 16 px in from everything else (convention).** The logo, the lede and the left card all share one left edge at about x=56. The headline starts at about x=72. The gap is too small to look deliberate and too big to miss, so the page looks slightly unbuilt right at the focal point (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*; Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, ch. I on placements that are neither here nor there). Put the headline on the same edge.

**Smaller things.**
- The lede is cut off from its headline. It sits about 190 px below the headline across the hero edge but only about 40 px above the cards, so it reads as a caption for the cards, not as the second line of the pitch (Arnheim on proximity). Pull it into the hero under the headline, or tighten the gap above it.
- The headline wraps to a lone "spaces" on its second line. Break it so the lines are more even, or widen the measure so it fits on one line (Bringhurst 2.4.2).
- The opening-hours card breaks "until 8 / pm", which splits a number from its unit on the line people will act on. Use a non-breaking space (Bringhurst 2.4.6).
- The empty space is left over rather than placed: the right half of the lede row and a band of about 120 px under the cards. A primary action and a real image would take most of it.
- The nav shows no current page. That's minor on a home page, but worth having on the inner pages.

**What's working.**
- **The copy is specific.** "Ferns, hostas and compact shrubs, grown at our nursery", "ten places in each class" and "free within ten miles on orders over $60, with a plant care card in every box" are concrete, local and believable. Keep that voice when you write the call to action.
- **The type is calm and well chosen.** The serif wordmark against a plain sans for everything else, three clearly separate levels, and good body size and contrast give the page a quiet, trustworthy, independent-shop feel.
- **The three cards answer the practical questions a local visitor has** (when, what's on, can you bring it to me) without making them hunt.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the cards or nav items have hover and focus states, and how the three-card row and the long headline reflow on a phone.

**Checklist.**
The whole piece: 8 lines, 6 yes, 2 no (image and headline make one idea; every element does a job)
What matters first: 3 lines, 3 yes
Arrangement and space: 14 lines, 10 yes, 4 no (one grid; no near-misses; white space gathered; eye path)
Text: 30 lines, 28 yes, 2 no (widows and runts; line breaks between number and unit)
Color: 9 lines, 9 yes
Pictures: 12 lines, 11 yes, 1 no (subject stands apart from background)
Charts and data: skipped, no chart
Interface: 33 lines, 23 yes, 3 no (one primary action; current page shown; next need anticipated), 7 unsure (hover, pressed, focus and other states not visible; reflow; links' affordance beyond nav convention)
