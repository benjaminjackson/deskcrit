**First read.** The eye goes straight to "Help for ferry passengers", then to the two bold headings, and settles in the left column because it's longer. It reads as a plain help page for a small local ferry, made with care: calm, clean, easy to scan, and so plain it could belong to any service. Nothing on it would stop someone who is in a hurry or upset.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or printed notice for ferry passengers. It has two jobs: help them get on board without trouble, and tell them what to do when something goes wrong.

**Verdict.** It's set well, but the words let it down where it matters most. "If something goes wrong" offers a slow note-in-a-box process as the only help, and the key facts (when the last boat leaves, where to send a complaint) aren't on the page. A passenger who needs this page right now can't act on it.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "If something goes wrong" column only covers complaints, and the heading makes it sound like it covers everything.** It's a defect. Someone stranded, injured, or who has lost a child or a bag reads "If something goes wrong" and finds three steps for writing a note and posting it in a box. The heading names a situation, but the section only holds one answer, a written complaint. So the person who most needs help gets the wrong one, or none (WCAG 2.4.6: headings describe their section's topic). The fix:
   - Split it into two parts. Put "Right now: tell any crew member, or call [number]" first.
   - Retitle the current steps to say what they are, such as "Report a problem after your crossing".
2. **The page sends readers somewhere else for the facts they'll act on.** It's a defect. "The last boat leaves before dusk" is a deadline with no time on it. Dusk moves through the winter, and two passengers will read it two different ways; the one who guesses later misses the boat. In step 3, "the address on the noticeboard in the shelter" is no use to anyone reading this at home, after the crossing, which is exactly when they would file a complaint. The fix:
   - Give the last sailing as a clock time, or "about 30 minutes before sunset, check the board for today's time".
   - Print the postal or email address on the page itself.
   - Do the same for "the timetable board": add a link or a timetable, so the first tip isn't just "go and look somewhere else".
3. **The copy is generic, so nothing stands out.** This is taste. Each tip is a long, evenly weighted sentence, and the reason comes before the action in some ("so that the gangway is kept clear", "because winter sailings…"). Someone scanning on a phone at the landing has to read every word to find the one thing they need to do. The fix: start each item with the action in a few words ("Bring exact change", "Bikes board last"), then give the reason. Bolding just those opening words would make the list scannable without adding another type size.

**Smaller things.**
- The two columns share baselines for their first two lines. After that the right column's item spacing pulls it half a line off the left (around y≈257 against 271), so the rows no longer line up across the gap (convention: Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*). Set the gap between items to a whole line so both columns stay on one baseline grid. It costs little on a web page; it matters more if this is printed as a notice.

**What's working.**
- The two kinds of list are used correctly: bullets for tips that can be read in any order, numbers for steps that must happen in sequence. Keep that.
- The type scale is tight: three levels (title, bold headings, regular body). The headings are told apart by weight, not by an extra size, which follows Refactoring UI's "bolder, not bigger".
- Body text is comfortable: about 55–60 characters per line, generous line spacing, bullets hung outside the text with wrapped lines aligned under the first word, and strong black-on-white contrast.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- How the two columns reflow on a phone. If "If something goes wrong" stacks below the boarding tips, it drops off the first screen, so after the split in finding 1 the urgent "right now" line should come first.
