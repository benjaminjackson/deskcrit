**Verdict.** It mostly does its job. The structure is clear, the type is readable and the advice is concrete. Two things hold it back. The complaint steps end by sending the reader somewhere else for the one fact they need. And the lists are set so that their left edge breaks up and the items blur together.

**What would move it most**
1. **The "If something goes wrong" steps send the reader away for the one fact they need.** Step 3 says to "send it to the address on the noticeboard in the shelter". Someone reading this at home, after the trip, can't see that noticeboard, so the steps stop just before the reader can act. The column also never says what happens after they report: who reads the note, or when they'll hear back. That gives a frustrated passenger no reason to bother. *Defect* (common sense; no catalog entry covers copy). Fix: print the actual postal address and an email (or phone number) in step 3. Add one line after it saying what happens next and roughly when, e.g. "We reply within N working days."
2. **Wrapped list lines fall back under the bullet instead of under the first word.** This affects all four bullets in the left column. The bullet sits at the margin (x≈90), the first word starts about 25 px further in (x≈115), and every following line returns to x≈90. The bullets stop marking where an item starts, so the column reads as one ragged paragraph with stray dots, and the reader loses where each tip begins. The numbered list on the right does the same thing on a smaller scale: "1." sits at x≈660, the text at x≈681, and wrapped lines go back to 660. The two lists also space their markers differently (a wide gap after the bullet, one space after the number), so the page looks like two separate list styles. *Convention*, typography.md "Hanging bullets and punctuation": the source is only partly confirmed. Bringhurst §4.4.2 supports hanging markers in the margin; aligning wrapped lines with the first word is standard practice that the entry could not confirm in a source. Fix: use a hanging indent in both lists. Put the marker outside the text edge and align every line of an item with its first word. Use the same marker-to-text gap in both lists.

**Smaller things.**
- Lines across the two columns don't share baselines after the first item. From the second item on, the right column's lines sit about half a line off the left's (e.g. "2.
