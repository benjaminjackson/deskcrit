**First read.** The eye goes straight to "See where every dollar goes this month", set big in white on a strong blue. Then it drops to the subhead and the white "Try it free" button. Next it slides right into a large empty blue area and waits for something that isn't there. Last, it goes down to three short feature notes on white, and below those the page runs out into blank space. It reads as a budgeting app's landing page, and it feels clean, confident and a bit unfinished.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a desktop landing page that gets people who want to control their monthly spending to start a free trial. It also has to win enough trust that they will connect their bank accounts.

**Verdict.** The words do the job well: the headline, subhead and three features are specific, clear and set with care. But the page never shows the product. With half the hero empty, a visitor is asked to link their bank accounts to something they have not seen.

**What would move it most**
1. **The right half of the hero is empty, so the product is never shown** (defect). About 500 × 400 px of plain blue sits to the right of the headline. Either an image failed to load or nothing was ever put there.
   - **What the viewer sees:** all the weight sits in the left half, so the hero tips left (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The empty area also doesn't read as planned breathing room. It reads as leftover space, the opposite of the gathered, deliberate space Ruder describes.
   - **What it costs:** the headline promises a view of "where every dollar goes", but the visitor never sees that view. They have to take a money app on trust before handing over bank access. That is the moment a visitor is most likely to hesitate.
   - **Fix:** fill that space with the payoff, one real screen showing this month's spending by category, with one category close to its limit and the heads-up alert visible. Then the picture and the headline make one point together. If an image was supposed to be there, check that it loads. Either way, the slot can't stay empty.
2. **"Try it free" doesn't tell people what they are agreeing to** (defect in the copy). This is the button that commits the visitor, and it leaves open two things they will care about: free for how long, and whether a card is needed. It also gives them no specific first step.
   - **Fix:** name the terms and the first step, for example "Connect your first account, free for 30 days, no card". Or put one plain line of terms under the buttons. The aim is that the visitor knows exactly what clicking costs.
3. **The strongest trust message is buried as the third of three equal features** (taste). For an app that asks for bank logins, "The app can see balances and history but can never move money" is the line that removes the biggest objection. Right now it has the same weight as "Shared budgets", at the far right of the row, where the eye reaches it last.
   - **Fix:** move a short version up next to the call to action, for example "Read-only: we can never move your money". Let the feature row expand on it.

**Smaller things.**
- The feature row sits only about 47 px below the hero edge, while about 220 px of white is left empty below it. Give the row more room at the top, or fill the bottom, so the row doesn't look jammed under the hero. If that bottom band is just the end of a cropped capture, ignore this.

**What's working.**
- **Copy that says something:** the headline names the outcome in the viewer's terms ("every dollar", "this month"). The subhead gives the whole product in one sentence with real verbs: connect, set a limit, get a heads-up. Each feature line ends on what it means for the viewer ("fix any of them in one tap", "never move money"). Keep that voice.
- **A clear order of importance:** one dominant headline, then a subhead with clear steps down in size, then a filled primary button with an outlined secondary one. Nothing competes.
- **One grid:** the 64 px left margin runs through the logo, headline, buttons and first feature column, and the three columns are evenly spaced. The page looks put together even while the hero is missing its picture.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the empty right half of the hero is an image that didn't load. Check in a live browser.
- Whether the blank band at the bottom is the real end of the page or just where the capture stopped.
- Focus states on the two hero buttons and the nav links.
