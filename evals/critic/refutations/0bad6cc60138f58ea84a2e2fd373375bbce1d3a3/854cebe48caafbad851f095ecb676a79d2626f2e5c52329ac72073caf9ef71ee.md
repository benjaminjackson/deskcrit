**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading "Notes for ferry passengers", then drops into three plain paragraphs and reads straight down. It reads as a calm, tidy help or information page. The type is clean and easy to read, but the page gives no clock times, so the one thing a passenger came for isn't there.

**What it's for.** My assumption: a web page that tells ferry passengers when the boat runs and how bikes board, so they can plan a crossing.

**Verdict.** The page is well set and pleasant to read, but it doesn't do its job. A passenger can't plan a trip from it, because the timetable is described in vague words instead of times, and it is split across two paragraphs. The words and the structure are the problem; the typography is fine.

**What would move it most**
1. **The timetable has no times (defect).** Paragraph 1 says the ferry runs "between early morning and late evening", and paragraph 3 says the last winter boat "leaves the quay before dusk". Those are exactly the facts a passenger acts on, and each can be read more than one way. One reader thinks "early morning" means 6:00 and another thinks 8:00. Dusk moves by more than two hours across a winter. A reader who guesses wrong misses the last boat home. "Every forty minutes" and "one boat each hour" are also unclear: from each landing, or one round trip? Fix: state the first and last departure from each landing as clock times, say which side each frequency applies to, and give the date the winter timetable starts and ends.
2. **The schedule is split up and buried in prose (defect).** Summer times are in paragraph 1, winter times are in paragraph 3, and the bike rules sit between them. Someone who wants to know "when's the next boat?" has to read all three paragraphs and put the schedule together in their head. Fix: put the schedule first and in one place, ideally a small table or two short lines (summer / winter × first boat / last boat / frequency × each landing), then the bike note. The prose can stay for the details.

**Smaller things.**
- Balance: the text column is pinned to the top left, and the right ~45% and lower half of the frame are empty, so the page looks lopsided and a little unfinished at this width. Either centre the reading column or give the right side a job, such as the timetable box or a small map of the two landings (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance").
- The heading "Notes for ferry passengers" (taste) could sit on any page. A heading that names the content, such as "Ferry times and bikes", tells the reader they're in the right place before they read a line.

**What's working.**
- The measure is about 65–75 characters with comfortable line spacing, so the paragraphs read easily (Bringhurst's 45–75 range). Keep it if you add a table beside or above them.
- The type hierarchy is clean: one family, a bold heading clearly above regular body text, and space between paragraphs rather than indents. The page doesn't need another type size.
- The bike paragraph is good copy: it says who does what, in what order, and where ("Riders wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded, and lock them to the rail"). Bring the timetable lines up to that level of precision.
