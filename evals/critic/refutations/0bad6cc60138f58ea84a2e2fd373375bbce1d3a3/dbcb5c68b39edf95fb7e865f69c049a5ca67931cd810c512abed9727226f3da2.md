## Crit: Quillnest "Create your account" sign-up screen

**First read.** My eye goes to "Create your account" first, then straight down the stack of fields to the navy "Create account" button. It's obviously a sign-up form for a notes product. It feels calm, tidy and trustworthy, but it also feels like every other SaaS sign-up card. Nothing on it says why this notes app and not some other one.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a first-visit sign-up screen on desktop web, and that its job is to get someone who is curious about Quillnest to finish creating a free account.

**Verdict.** It works: the hierarchy is clear, there's one obvious action, and the craft is clean. What it lacks is mostly in the words and the friction, not the layout. The password field gives no rules, the form asks for things the user doesn't need to give, and the only pitch is a line that could sit on any product's page.

**What would move it most**
1. **The password field says nothing about what it accepts.** The field (about y 425–465) has a label and an empty box. There's no length rule, no character rule and no show-password control. People will guess, press "Create account", and find out from an error, which is the most annoying moment to lose someone on a sign-up page. *Defect* (Nielsen, heuristics 5 and 10: prevent errors, and put help next to the step it's about). Fix: put the rule under the field as short helper text before they type, such as "At least 8 characters". Ideally show it being met as they type.
2. **The form asks for more than the user needs, and the extra fields sit in the main path.** "How did you hear about us?" serves your marketing, not the user. It's a blank dropdown with no prompt, so it also looks unfinished. On top of that, a last name is *required* just to make a notes workspace. That's six fields where three would start the product. Each extra field is another reason to leave before the account exists. *Defect* (Nielsen, heuristic 8: every element supports the user's main task). Fix: cut the form to name, email and password. Ask the survey question after sign-up, or drop it. Make last name optional, or use a single "Name" field. Default the workspace name from the person's name (e.g. "Ben's notes"), or ask for it on the next screen.
3. **The pitch is generic.** "Start a free workspace for your notes" names a category, not a reason. Someone deciding whether to give you their email learns nothing they couldn't get from any other notes app, so nothing pushes them to finish. Fix: replace or follow it with one concrete line about what Quillnest does that their current notes tool doesn't, put in their own terms, such as what they'll stop losing or be able to find. You could also add a short reassurance where people hesitate, like "No card needed". *Taste / copy*: I'm arguing this on its own terms, not from a source.

**Smaller things.**
- "Already have an account? Sign in" (bottom, y ≈ 731): "Sign in" is the same colour and weight as the words around it and has no underline, so it doesn't look clickable. Returning users who land here have to hunt for it. Underline it, or set it in the navy link colour with an underline (WCAG technique G183).
- The input borders are pale grey-blue on white and look close to the 3:1 minimum for component edges (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border colour against white and darken it if it falls short.
- The "How did you hear about us?" dropdown shows an empty value. If you keep it, give it a prompt like "Choose one (optional)" so it doesn't look broken. Common sense.

**What's working.**
- One clear primary action: a full-width navy button, with nothing else competing at that weight. Keep that.
- The labels sit above the fields and closer to their own field than to the next one, so every field reads as one unit. Required fields have an asterisk and a legend ("* Required field"), not colour alone.
- A restrained palette (navy, slate, white on a pale blue ground) and a tight type scale: the heading, the subtitle, bold labels and small helper text. Each level is obviously different, which makes the screen feel calm and credible.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus rings on the fields and button, the error states after submit, and whether the password rules show up live as the user types.
