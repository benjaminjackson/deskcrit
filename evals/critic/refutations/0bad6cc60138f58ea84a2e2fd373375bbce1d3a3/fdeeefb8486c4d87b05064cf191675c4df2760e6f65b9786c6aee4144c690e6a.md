**First read.** My eye goes straight to the tilted electric-blue SALE sticker at top right. It's the only saturated colour and the only thing at an angle. Next it drops to the headline, "Linens and cookware for the weekend", then down to four product cards whose pictures are grey placeholder tiles. It reads as a calm homeware shop running a weekend sale. It feels tidy and generic: it could be any shop's sale page.

**What it's for.** Assumption: this is the home page of a homeware shop (Marlowe & Finch) during a weekend sale, and its job is to get shoppers to browse the discounted items.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The sale is the whole message, but nothing below the banner shows a discount, no product has a picture, and the banner gives the shopper no way into the sale. The two biggest problems are that the products show no sale prices and that the hero has no action.

**What would move it most**
1. **The cards don't show the sale (defect).** The headline promises "Up to 40% off through Sunday". Yet the four cards under "Popular this week" show one plain price each ($34, $89, $52, $68), with no original price, no percent off and no sale tag. Shoppers can't tell whether these items are discounted, so they have to go hunting, through the "Sale" link in the nav, for the very thing the banner told them about. That's common sense, not a cited rule. Fix: show the sale on the products themselves, with the was-price struck through next to the sale price or a "–30%" tag on each discounted card. Then the claim in the banner pays off on the same screen. If these four items aren't on sale, swap in ones that are.
2. **The hero has no action, and the thing that looks like a button probably isn't one (defect).** The banner has a headline, a subline and a blue rounded rectangle. Blue, filled and rounded is how this page would draw a primary button, but the rectangle is tilted and says only "SALE", so it reads as a sticker. A shopper either clicks it and gets nothing, or ignores it and has no next step. Material Design and Apple's HIG both expect one clearly primary action per view. Fix: put a real button under the subline that names the destination, for example "Shop the sale" or "Shop linens and cookware". Then either make the sticker the link or drop it.
3. **The SALE sticker wins the first glance, and all it says is the generic word (defect, plus taste on style).** It outweighs the headline through colour and angle, so the first thing a shopper reads is "SALE", which any shop could say. The details that matter (what's on sale, how much off, until when) sit in the headline and in a small 18 px-looking subline. The sticker also only repeats "Up to 40% off", so headline and image don't combine into one idea. My view as a designer is that the sticker's style also clashes with the rest: a cool electric blue on a warm linen-and-stone palette, and a loud slapped-on angle against an otherwise quiet, square layout. Fix: make the offer itself the dominant element, e.g. "Up to 40% off linens and cookware" set large, with "through Sunday" close by. If the sticker stays, give it the real numbers ("40% off") and a colour from the brand's warm range. The hero's right half also wants a real product photo; just now it's empty beige.
4. **No product pictures (defect, unless this is a capture problem).** All four card images are flat colour tiles with a pale rounded box in the middle, which looks like placeholders. On a shop page the picture does most of the selling, and without it a "Wool throw" for $68 is just text. If the images were still loading when this was captured, check in a live browser. If they're placeholders, this can't ship.

**Smaller things.**
- The hero text starts at about x=56, while the logo, "Popular this week" and the cards start at 48. An 8 px near-miss like that looks like a mistake, not a choice. Put the hero text on the same left edge (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- The headline breaks as "Linens and cookware for / the weekend", splitting "for" from what it introduces. Break before "for", or rewrite the line.
- "Popular this week" sits about midway between the hero and the cards. Move it closer to the cards so it clearly labels them (Bringhurst 2.2.2: more space above than below).

**What's working.**
- The cards are clean and consistent: bold name, regular price, the same padding and the same light border on all four. Keep that structure when you add sale pricing.
- The type is in one family with clear weight steps (bold logo and headings, regular body text), and the hierarchy below the hero is easy to scan.
- The warm neutral banner tint suits linens and cookware. It's the right base mood, so pick the accent colour from that palette.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether the product images failed to load or are placeholders. Check this in a live browser.
- Whether the SALE sticker or the cards are links, and their hover and focus states.
