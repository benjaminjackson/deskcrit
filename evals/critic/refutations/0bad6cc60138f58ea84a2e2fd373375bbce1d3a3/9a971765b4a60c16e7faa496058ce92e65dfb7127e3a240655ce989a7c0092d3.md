**First read.** The eye lands on the bold "Tell us about your project" at top left, moves right to the white card and down its three fields. Then it stops: there is nothing at the bottom of the form to press. "Send message" sits there as plain black text and looks like a caption, not a button. Otherwise it reads as a calm, tidy, rather generic contact page for a small studio.

**What it's for.** My assumption: the contact page of a small design studio, where a potential client sends a project enquiry. Everything here hangs on that one submit.

**Verdict.** It doesn't do its job yet. The page has one action, and that action doesn't look like something you can click, so the person who just wrote out their project brief is left to guess how to send it. The layout and type are clean enough, but that doesn't count for much while the submit is missing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The submit isn't a button (defect).** "Send message" at the foot of the card (around x 541, y 526) is set in the same face, size, weight and colour as the body text. It has no fill, no border and no underline. Even the nav and footer links get blue plus underline, so the page's most important control is the only interactive thing with no styling at all. The viewer fills in three fields, reaches the end, and has to wonder whether that line of text does anything. Some will hesitate and some will leave. It also ends the eye's path in a dead end instead of a destination. Its clickable area is probably only the height of the text, but measure that in a browser against the 24 × 24 px minimum (WCAG 2.5.8). Fix: give it a button's shape, filled in the page's link blue with white text and a comfortable height. Make it the one strongest element inside the card, so it is clearly the primary action (Material Design and Apple HIG on button emphasis; Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on signifiers).
2. **The intro asks for information the form doesn't help with (copy/UX; my judgment, no approved source).** The left column says "Include a budget range and a rough timeline if you have them", but the form has only a single open "Message" box. The viewer has to remember that instruction while typing, and many won't. The studio then gets the vague enquiries it was trying to avoid. Fix: add an optional Budget field (ranges in a select) and a Timeline field. Or at least put that prompt as helper text right under the Message label, where the person is actually typing.

**Smaller things.**
- Nav: "Contact" looks exactly like Work, Services and About, so nothing marks the page you're on. Give the current item one cue, such as bolder weight or a bar (Nielsen; NN/g Laubheimer, "Menu-Design Checklist").
- Required fields aren't marked. Presumably all three are required; if so, say so once ("All fields required"). If not, add "(optional)" after the optional labels (NN/g, Budiu, "Marking Required Fields in Forms").
- Input borders are light grey on white and look close to the 3:1 line. Check them against WCAG 1.4.11 and darken them one step if they fall short.
- The card's right edge (~1068 px) lines up with nothing. The nav and page margin run to ~1224 px, so the form column stops short for no clear reason and the page leans left. Either extend the card to the right margin or size both columns from one grid (Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems*).
- The copy is fine but could be anyone's. "Tell us about your project" over a stock reassurance line says nothing about Fernwick specifically. One concrete line about the kind of work the studio takes on would help the right clients feel they've come to the right place.

**What's working.**
- The intro copy is specific where it counts. "Reply within two working days" sets a real expectation that the studio can be held to. Keep it.
- The labels sit above the fields, bold and close to their inputs, in a single column. The form is easy to scan and fill in.
- The type is restrained: one family, about four clearly separated sizes, a headline with tight leading, and an intro line length of about 45–50 characters. Keep that discipline when the button goes in.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Focus styling on the fields and the submit. With an unstyled submit, check in a browser that keyboard focus shows on it at all.
- Whether the Email field is a real email input that validates.
- What happens after sending: is there a confirmation and an error state?
