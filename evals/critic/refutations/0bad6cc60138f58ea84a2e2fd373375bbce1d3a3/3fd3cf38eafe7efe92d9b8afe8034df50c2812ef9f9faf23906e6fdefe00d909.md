**First read.** My eye goes first to the three big numbers across the top, starting with 184,220 on the left. Then it drops to the blue line climbing across the chart. It reads as a marketing-analytics overview for a team checking traffic and sign-ups. It feels calm and tidy, and could be almost any SaaS dashboard.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is the home screen of an analytics product, where a marketer or founder checks in a few seconds how traffic and sign-ups did over the last 12 weeks, and which channel drove them.

**Verdict.** It's clean and competent, but the numbers don't add up and the page never says what matters. The chart's three channels add up to well short of the Visits total, and nothing tells you what to look at or do next. That's the biggest thing in its way.

**What would move it most**
1. **The chart and the Visits card disagree, and the chart doesn't say what it measures.** The y-axis (0–8k) has no unit or period. The points are a week apart, so it's probably visits per week, but the viewer has to work that out. Worse, the three lines add up to roughly 140k visits over the 12 weeks (Search ~6k, Social ~3.6k and Email ~2k a week on average). The card above says 184,220. A careful viewer will find the ~45k gap and start doubting every number on the page. Either the chart is missing an "Other / Direct" series, or the figures are wrong. *Defect*, and a *convention*: every chart names what its marks measure (Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, ch. 2; Few). Fix: label the axis "Visits per week", and either add the missing channels (an "Other" line in grey is enough) or say plainly that the chart shows the top three channels and how much of the total they cover.

2. **Nothing says what happened or what to do about it.** The cards give three small green deltas (+6.1%, +2.4%, +0.1 pts), and the chart title only names its topic ("Visits by channel"). The viewer has to find the story alone. In fact the story is in the chart: Search is growing steadily, Social is jumpy, and visits grew faster than sign-ups. That last point means the extra traffic is converting worse at the margin, which is the one thing here someone might act on. *Taste/common sense* (the takeaway-title rule has no approved source here). Fix: give the chart a title that states the finding ("Search drove most of the growth; Social swings week to week"). Consider one line under the cards that links the numbers, so the screen answers "so what?" before the viewer has to ask.

**Smaller things.**
- **Legend:** the legend sits at the top left, far from where the lines end on the right, so you look back and forth to decode the colours. Label each line at its right end instead (Tufte; Wilke).
- **Date range:** "Last 12 weeks" in the top bar looks like a static caption. If it's the date-range picker, give it a control's look, such as a chevron and a border (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, ch. 1: signifiers).
- **Repeated comparison text:** "vs previous 12 weeks" is written out three times. Say it once, for example "Change vs previous 12 weeks" under the Overview heading, and let each card show just the delta.

**What's working.**
- **Three plain cards:** the cards are quiet and equal, with the label, then the big number, then the green delta, so all three figures can be read in about a second. Keep that.
- **Chart restraint:** the chart uses faint gridlines, three clearly different hues, and lines that never cross, so each series stays easy to follow even without colour vision.
- **Consistent comparison:** every headline number is compared with the previous period in the same way, and the conversion rate's change is correctly given in points rather than percent.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether "Last 12 weeks" is interactive, and whether hovering over a point on the chart shows its exact value.
