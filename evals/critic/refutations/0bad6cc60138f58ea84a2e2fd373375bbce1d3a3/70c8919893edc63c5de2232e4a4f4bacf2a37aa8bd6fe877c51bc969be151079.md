**First read.** The eye lands on "Library opening hours", then drops down the three bold branch names. It reads straight away as a plain public-service hours page. It feels calm, tidy and a bit sparse: one narrow column hugging the left edge, with about two-thirds of the frame empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page or panel for local residents who want to know when a branch is open so they can plan a visit.

**Verdict.** The layout and type do the job. The copy doesn't. The hours are spelled out in words that are slow to scan and can be read two ways, and the mobile library gives days with no times at all. So a visitor can't reliably answer "is it open when I can get there?", which is the one question this page exists for.

**What would move it most**
1. **The mobile library has no times, and no closing days are stated anywhere (defect).** "Wednesday at the market square" and "Thursday at the school gates" tell the viewer where the van will be but not when. Someone who turns up at 4 pm may find it gone. In the same way, Harbour branch never says it's closed on Mondays, and neither branch mentions Sunday. The reader has to work that out from what's missing, and a missing line could just as easily be an oversight. Hours are exactly the kind of thing people act on and hold the library to. Fix: give every stop a time range, and state the closed days in words ("Closed Sunday and Monday").
2. **The hours are written in words, so they scan slowly and can be misread (defect).** "Nine to eight", "ten to four" and "ten to two" have to be read as phrases, not glanced at. "Ten to four" can also be read as 3:50, so the Saturday line literally has two meanings, and "nine to eight" doesn't say morning or evening. On a page people check in a hurry, often on a phone at the door, figures are what the eye looks for. Fix: set times as numerals with an unambiguous form ("9 am – 8 pm" or "09:00–20:00", with a true en dash). Putting the days and times in two aligned columns would let people compare branches at a glance. That last part is my taste: the content is a small lookup table, and lining it up makes it read like one.

**Smaller things.**
- Balance (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"): the whole column sits in the left third of a 1280 px frame, and the right side and bottom third are empty, so the page looks unfinished rather than deliberately spare. Either cap the frame or container width so the block is the page, or use the space, for example by putting the three branches side by side as columns, which would also help comparison.

**What's working.**
- The grouping is right. Lines within a branch are about 33 px apart and the gaps between branches are roughly twice that, so each bold name clearly belongs to the two lines beneath it. Keep that ratio.
- The hierarchy is clean and uses few means: one large bold title, then bold for branch names and regular for detail at what looks like the same size. Weight does the work instead of extra sizes, which is the right call (Wathan and Schoger, *Refactoring UI*). Keep the scale this tight.
- Dark text on white with a single flush-left edge is plain, readable and fitting for a public service. Don't decorate it.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** How the layout behaves on a phone, which is likely where most people will check the hours.
