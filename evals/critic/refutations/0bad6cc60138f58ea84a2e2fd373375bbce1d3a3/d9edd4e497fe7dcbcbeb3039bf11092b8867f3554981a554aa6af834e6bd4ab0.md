**First read.** The eye lands on the bold heading, "Notes for ferry passengers", then drops straight into three paragraphs of grey-black prose. It reads as a plain, calm information page for a small river ferry, with nothing else on it. It feels tidy and quiet, and a little bare: the right half and the bottom third are empty.

**What it's for.** I'm assuming this is a web page that tells passengers when the ferry runs, how long the crossing takes, and the rules for bikes, read by people planning a trip or standing at the landing.

**Verdict.** The typesetting is sound, but the page doesn't do its job: it describes the timetable without giving a single time a passenger can act on, and it sends them to a printed sheet at the landing instead. The weak point is the content, not the layout.

**What would move it most**
1. **The page has no times on it** (defect, copy). The first and third paragraphs say "every forty minutes between early morning and late evening", "one boat each hour" and "before dusk". A passenger reading this can't answer the only question they came with: when does the next boat leave, and when is the last one? "Every forty minutes" with no start time can't be used to work out a departure. "In winter" doesn't name any months. And "the last crossing leaves the quay before dusk" can be read two ways. It could mean the market quay only, or both ends. It could be a fixed time, or a time that changes daily with sunset. If someone reads it wrong, they get stranded on the wrong bank. The page then sends people to "printed timetables… in the shelter at each landing", which means you have to be at the ferry already to plan the trip to it. Fix: give the first and last sailing from each side, the departure minutes (for example, ":00, :40, :20"), the winter dates, and the actual winter last sailing. Write the numbers as numerals ("40 minutes", "12 minutes") so the eye can find them.
2. **Three topics are written as one story, so nothing can be scanned** (taste/common sense). The paragraphs cover three separate jobs: the timetable, bikes, and winter changes. They have no labels, so someone checking only whether they can take a bike has to read everything to find the second paragraph. Fix: give each paragraph a short subhead, or open each one with a bold run-in label ("Timetable", "Bicycles", "Winter"), and put the times in a small table. That would also give the page a second type level that does a real job, instead of one heading over plain prose.

**What's working.**
- The text is set well. It's in one plain sans serif (it looks like Inter), body text looks about 18–19 px with comfortable leading, and lines run about 65–78 characters. The text is easy to read once you're in it, so keep this setting when you add structure.
- The hierarchy is clear: one bold heading and the body text, with real weight contrast and no extra sizes competing.
- The copy speaks in plain words a passenger would use ("wheel them on after foot passengers have boarded"), with no internal jargon. Keep that voice when you add the times.
