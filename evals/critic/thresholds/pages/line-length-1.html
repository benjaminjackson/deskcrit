Why the second day of a stew is better

By Tomas Reyes, January 9

A stew that rests overnight in a cold pantry tastes rounder the next evening, and the reason is mostly gelatin. As the pot cools, the collagen that melted out of the shin and the bones sets into a loose jelly that holds the fat and the aromatics together, so when you warm it again the sauce coats the spoon instead of running off it.

The flavors do not really marry in the way cookbooks like to say. What happens is quieter: the onions and carrots keep giving up sugar, the salt spreads evenly into the meat, and the sharp edge of the wine softens as some of its acids react with the starch from the flour you browned at the start.

So cook it a day early when you can. Lift the hard fat off the top before reheating, add a splash of water, and bring it up slowly with the lid on, stirring from the bottom so nothing catches on the pan.
