Repairing a dry stone wall after a hard winter

By Ellen Marsh, March 14

Frost does most of its damage out of sight. Water gets into the core of the wall, freezes, and pushes the face stones out a few millimetres at a time, until one spring morning a whole section sags toward the lane.

Before you move a stone, walk the full run of the wall and mark every bulge with a stick. A bulge that has not yet fallen is the cheapest repair you will ever make, because the stones are still sorted in roughly the right order.

Take the section down to below the bulge, laying the stones out on the grass in the order they came off. Rebuild with each stone set deep into the wall, fill the middle as you go, and set a through stone every metre.
