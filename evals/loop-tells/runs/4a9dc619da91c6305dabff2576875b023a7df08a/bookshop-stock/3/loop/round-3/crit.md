**First read.** The eye lands first on the three-line headline. It then drops to the brown "Start a 30-day trial" button and crosses to the "Reorder this week" table. The page reads as a calm, specific tool for a small bookshop, with warm paper colours and no decoration that does not earn its place. On phone and tablet the table moves below the buttons. On the phone the header is now one row, but the Sign in button runs to the right edge of the screen.

**What it's for.** It gets an independent bookshop owner to start a trial, because they believe Shelfmark will tell them what to reorder each Monday.

**Verdict.** The fixes made the layout tidier: the phone header is shorter, the phone table no longer wraps, and the notes line up with the table. The biggest problem has not changed. The table still makes the owner work out the reorder decision for each title, and nothing on the page sets Shelfmark against the way a shop works now.

The owner must compare them and decide for each title. Nothing compares Shelfmark with the till report or spreadsheet the owner uses now, so they must take the value on trust. The fix: add a column that states the reorder decision for each title, and add a short comparison with how a shop works now. The maker must supply the column label, its values, and what the comparison says. Defect. Viewports: desktop, phone, tablet, widescreen.
2. The phone header. The page scrolls sideways by 5 px, and the Sign in button touches the screen edge. A page that moves sideways under the thumb feels broken, and this is the first thing on the screen. The fix: keep the logo row inside the 20 px side margins. For example, let the logo shrink instead of growing to 265 px, or make the Sign in button narrower. Defect. Viewports: phone.
