**First read.** On desktop and widescreen, the eye goes first to the large dark serif headline, "Hire a narrowboat for a week on the canal". It then drops through the short lead paragraph to the filled Search button in the white card on the right. The page reads as a calm, plain booking page from a small boatyard, and it now has a single obvious place to start. On the phone, the page is one column: headline, lead, routes, then the form, which starts a little above the bottom of the first screen. On the tablet, the left column is narrow, so the headline breaks into four lines and the route names wrap.

**What it's for.** It lets a first-time visitor book a canal narrowboat holiday directly with the boatyard. The main action is to search for a boat by base, date, nights and berths.

**Verdict.** The fixes helped a lot. The phone reflows, there is one primary action, the headline leads, and widescreen no longer splits the page across the screen. The biggest thing still in the way is unchanged: nothing shows the boat or the water, so the page sells a holiday with words alone.

**What would move it most**
1. No picture of a boat or the canal. This is in the hero of every viewport, and most of all on desktop and widescreen, where the space under the form is empty. People buy this as a holiday and want to see the boat and the water before they book, but the headline has to carry the whole idea alone. Fix: set one real photograph of the company's own boats or canal into the hero. The maker must supply it, because a stock shot does not do the same job. Taste. Viewports: desktop, phone, tablet, widescreen.

**Smaller things.**
- Tablet: let the hero text column take more width, or stack the page below about 800 px, so the route names stay on one line and the h1 breaks into two or three lines. Viewports: tablet.
- Phone: put the form before the route list, or directly under the lead, so that Search is nearer the first screen. Viewports: phone.
- Route details: say what the lock count means for a hirer. Only the company can supply that fact. Viewports: desktop, phone, tablet, widescreen.
- Type sizes: there are still five sizes (44, 24, 18, 16 and 14 px). If the logo size or the 14 px labels joined a nearby step, the scale would be tighter. Viewports: desktop, phone, tablet, widescreen.

**What's working.**
- The single filled Search button, with the old filled button demoted to a text link, gives the page one clear next step.
- The serif headline is now clearly the title. Moving "book it here with the boatyard that keeps it" to the start of the lead puts the reason to book direct in the first read.
- Putting the routes directly under the lead, level with the form, makes the list read as part of the search and not as a separate section.
