**First read.** On desktop the eye goes first to the filled dark green "See boats and dates" button, because it is the only solid shape on the page. It then moves right to the white "Find a boat" card. The gray headline reads like a subtitle, so you learn that this is "narrowboat hire, booked direct" only when you read the body text. The page feels calm and honest, but bare: there is no boat and no water, and a large empty area sits under the button. On the phone the layout does not adapt. The navigation stacks into a ragged column beside the logo, the intro is squeezed into a column about one word wide, and the form runs off the right edge.

**What it's for.** A landing page for Slowwater, a narrowboat hire company. It lets people book a week on the canal direct with the boatyard instead of through an agency.

**Verdict.** On desktop, tablet and widescreen the page mostly does its job, but it undersells itself. The headline is the weakest text on the page, the real reason to book here is in the middle of a paragraph, and two buttons compete for the one action. On the phone the page fails: it does not fit the screen, and that is the first thing to fix.

**What would move it most**
1. **The phone layout does not fit the screen.** The page is 587 px wide in a 390 px viewport, so it scrolls sideways (facts file, phone `page_width`). The desktop two-column layout stays in place. The lead paragraph is squeezed to 9.1 characters per line over 18 lines (`characters_per_line`, `p.lead`), so the right edge of the text becomes a jagged staircase. The headline breaks across 5 lines. "See boats and dates" wraps inside its button. The four navigation links stack in a column next to the logo, with "Your booking" split onto two lines. The search form, which is the actual way to book, starts beside the text and runs to the right edge with no margin, so part of it is off screen until you scroll sideways. A phone visitor sees a broken page and has to pan to find the form. Defect, against WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow. Fix: at phone width, put everything in one column (headline, lead, form, routes) and fold the navigation into the usual compact pattern for a phone. Viewports: phone
2. **The headline is the weakest text on the page, and the reason to book here is buried.** The h1 "Hire a narrowboat for a week on the canal" is 22 px, weight 400, in gray #6b7574 (`h1`). The lead paragraph under it is 20 px in dark #26302f, so the paragraph outweighs the heading, and the button outweighs both. The type sizes are 24, 22, 20, 16 and 14 (`type_sizes`). The logo at 24, the h1 at 22, and the lead and navigation at 20 are steps too small to read as separate levels (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1). The h1 differs from the body only by a paler color, which tells the eye it matters less (Dair, *Design with Type*, ch. 8–9). The copy has the same problem. The h1 names a category any hire firm could use. What makes Slowwater different is "book it here with the boatyard that keeps it. No agency in the middle, and no fee on top of the hire", and that sits in the second and third sentences of the paragraph. The "no fee" line also asks the visitor to take a saving on trust: nothing on the page shows a price or the fee they avoid, so they cannot judge the value. Defect and convention. Fix: make the headline clearly the largest and darkest thing on the page, with a real step up from the lead. Put the book-direct, no-fee point in it or right under it, and darken the h1 to the body color. If the maker has the figure, show a week's hire price or the agency fee the visitor avoids. That figure must come from the maker, so do not invent one. Viewports: all
3. **Two buttons do one job, and the wrong one is primary.** "See boats and dates" is filled dark green. "Search", the button that submits the form with the base, date, nights and berths the visitor chose, is outlined. A visitor who fills in the form then reaches a weaker button than the one they skipped. If they press "See boats and dates", they cannot tell whether it uses their choices. Convention (Material 3 and Apple HIG emphasis levels; Nielsen 8 on duplicate controls). Fix: make "Search" the one filled primary button. Remove the hero button, or give it a clearly different job. Viewports: all
4. **A holiday on the water with nothing to look at.** No viewport shows a boat, a lock or a canal. The headline and the copy have to carry the whole sale, and the visitor gets no feeling of the trip they are booking. This is my taste and common sense, not a cited rule: on a leisure booking page, a real photo of the company's own boats or water does work that the copy cannot. Fix: add one specific image of this company's boats or routes, not a stock scene, placed so it fills the empty area under the button rather than pushing the form down. Viewports: all
5. **On wide screens the page splits in two, with empty space between.** On desktop there is a large empty area between the button (bottom at 338) and the routes list (612). On widescreen the form sits at x 1516, about 850 px from the text column, so the eye jumps across empty space and the page reads as two unrelated halves (Arnheim, on balance). The list "Routes from Hartwell Junction" depends on the Base select in the form, but it sits far away in the other column, so changing the base and seeing the routes change happen in two different places (Norman, natural mapping). Convention. Fix: keep the content in one width cap so the form stays near the text. Pull the routes up into the empty space, or place them where the connection to the Base field is visible. Viewports: desktop, widescreen

**Smaller things.**
- The h1 and the gray route figures ("7 nights, 38 locks" and the others) measure 4.46:1, just under the 4.5:1 minimum (violations, `h1` and the `ul > li > span:nth-of-type(2)` entries). Darken the gray one step. Viewports: all
- On the phone the route figures split numbers from their units ("12 / locks", "61 / locks") and the route names wrap. Keep each figure on one line, which the one-column layout should give it room for. Viewports: phone
- On the tablet the lead runs only 24.1 characters per line over 8 lines (`p.lead`) and ends with "hire." alone on the last line. Give the text column more width, or stack the form under it. Viewports: tablet
- The select and input borders look pale gray on white and may be below 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Measure the border color and darken it if it fails. Viewports: all
- The start date field shows "Sat 14 June" with no year and looks like a plain text field. Near the end of a year a visitor cannot tell which June they are booking. Show the year, or make it clear that a date picker sets the value. Viewports: all

**What's working.**
- The restrained palette works: warm off-white, dark green-gray text, and one deep green accent. The Source Serif logo against Inter text feels quiet and fits a slow holiday on the water. Keep that restraint when you add an image.
- The form is prefilled with sensible defaults (a base, a Saturday start, 7 nights, 4 people), with plain labels above each field. A visitor can search in one click.
- The route list gives nights and lock count for each route. That is real information for canal hirers that a generic travel template would not have, and it makes the page specific.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the routes the center of the page, with the lock count as the number that leads.** Builds on finding 5. The list "Routes from Hartwell Junction" is the only thing on the page that a general holiday-hire site would not have. "7 nights, 38 locks" tells a canal hirer how hard the week will be. Right now it sits at the bottom in small gray type, like a footnote. A stronger version treats the routes as the main way to choose a trip: route first, then boat and dates. The nights and locks are the figures a person compares, and the route name is the label. This fits the page's purpose. A visitor booking direct with the boatyard wants proof that the people selling the week know the water, and the boatyard's own routes, with the locks counted, are that proof. Taste. Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, argues that the data should be the interface, not a decoration around it. The page already has the data, so this asks for no new content. If the maker has more routes or more figures for each route, those are theirs to add.
2. **Set the headline in the Source Serif of the logo, so the boatyard's voice carries the claim.** Builds on finding 2. Finding 2 asks for a bigger, darker headline. The commitment here is about voice. The serif now appears only in the word "Slowwater", and everything else is Inter, so the claim to be the boatyard itself, with no agency in the middle, is told in the same neutral face as the form labels. If the headline uses the logo's serif, the brand's own voice states the book-direct point, and Inter stays for the form and the facts. That split also gives the change in level a second signal besides size. Taste. Dair, *Design with Type*, names contrast of structure, meaning a change of typeface, as one of the ways to tell levels apart. Use the serif only for the logo and that one statement, so the calm restraint the crit praises stays in place.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Focus indicators on the form fields and buttons. Whether the routes list updates when the Base select changes. Whether the start date field opens a date picker.
