**First read.** On desktop the eye lands first on the dark green "See boats and dates" button, because it is the only solid color on the page. It then goes across to the white "Find a boat" card. The headline "Hire a narrowboat for a week on the canal" is gray and barely bigger than the paragraph under it, so it reads as a small line above the paragraph, not as the headline. There is no picture of a boat or of water anywhere. The page feels calm and honest but bare, and there is a large empty band under the button. On the phone the layout does not change at all: the two columns stay side by side, the nav stacks into a ragged block beside the logo, the intro runs two or three words to a line, and the page is wider than the screen.

**What it's for.** As told: the home page of Slowwater, where people book a canal boat direct from the boatyard. A first-time visitor has to understand the offer and start a search.

**Verdict.** On a laptop it nearly works: the copy is specific and the form is the right tool. Two things stop it. The phone version is broken, and nothing on the page makes a canal holiday look worth wanting. The page has no headline with weight and no picture. All it shows is two buttons that do the same thing.

**What would move it most**
1. **The phone layout does not reflow.** The phone capture is 587 px wide on a 390 px screen (`page_width`), so the page scrolls sideways and the "Find a boat" card runs off the right side. The two desktop columns stay side by side. The intro paragraph is squeezed to 9.1 characters per line over 18 lines (`characters_per_line`, `lines`, `p.lead`, phone). The headline breaks into 5 lines of about 8 characters (`h1`, phone). The nav stacks into five lines beside the logo, with "Your booking" split over two lines. In the route list, "12 / locks" and "61 / locks" split the number from its unit. A phone visitor sees a jumbled page and has to pan around to reach the form. That is the first thing to fix, because many people will plan a holiday from their phone. This is a defect (WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow; Bringhurst's 45 to 75 characters). Fix it so that below the tablet width the page is one column in this order: headline, intro, form, routes. Collapse the nav or let it wrap under the logo, and keep each route's nights and locks on one line. Viewports: phone
2. **Nothing on the page leads, and nothing makes the trip look worth wanting.** The headline is 22 px, weight 400, in gray #6b7574 at 4.46:1 (`font_size`, `font_weight`, `contrast`, `h1`). That is under the 4.5:1 minimum and lighter than the 20 px dark intro below it (`p.lead`, 12.75:1). So the headline looks less important than its own paragraph, and the eye skips to the button. The type sizes are 24, 22, 20, 16 and 14 (`type_sizes`). The top three are only 2 px apart, so they do not read as separate levels (Bringhurst, section 3.1.1). The page also has no picture of a narrowboat, a lock or the water. On a holiday site, that picture is what makes a visitor want the trip, and here the words have to sell it alone. This is a defect in hierarchy (Tschichold; Dair, chapters 8 and 9) and taste on the picture. Fix: make the headline clearly the first thing seen, larger by a real step and darker, and cut the scale down to three or four sizes. Add a real photo of the boats or the canal from these bases, placed in the layout so that it works with the headline. Viewports: all
3. **Two buttons for one task, and the wrong one looks primary.** "See boats and dates" is a filled green button. "Search" sits at the bottom of the form, where the visitor's base, date, nights and party size are, and it is only an outline. Both appear to lead to the same results. The visitor cannot tell which one to press, or whether "See boats and dates" will ignore what they put in the form. This is a defect (Nielsen heuristic 8; Material Design emphasis levels). Fix: keep one control for this action. The form's button is the obvious survivor, so give it the filled primary style. Remove the hero button, or give it a different job. Viewports: all
4. **On wide screens the layout falls apart into two edges and a hole.** The text column is fixed at 600 px on the left and the form is pinned to the right margin. On widescreen that leaves about 850 px of empty space between them. On desktop and widescreen, about 270 px of empty space under the button pushes "Routes from Hartwell Junction" down to y 612. There it lines up with nothing and sits below the form's bottom edge (580). The route list depends on the Base picker but sits far from it. So the eye goes from the button to the form and then has to jump back down-left to find the routes. This is a convention (Müller-Brockmann, one grid; Arnheim on balance; Norman on natural mapping). Fix: set a maximum content width and hold both columns on one grid. Close the gap under the hero so the routes follow straight after it, and let the routes visibly answer to the base the form has selected. Viewports: desktop, widescreen

**Smaller things.**
- The nights-and-locks figures in the route list are #6b7574 at 4.46:1 (`contrast`, `ul > li > span:nth-of-type(2)`), just under 4.5:1. Darken that gray one step. Viewports: all
- On the tablet the intro runs 24.1 characters per line over 8 lines (`p.lead`, tablet) and ends with "hire." alone on the last line. Give the text column more of the width, or stack the form under it. Viewports: tablet
- The input and select borders look pale gray on white, probably under 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). The file does not measure them, so check them and darken them if they fail. Viewports: all
- The nav does not mark the current page (Laubheimer, NN/g). This matters little on the home page, so give the current item one cue, such as weight or an underline, when the same nav appears on other pages. Viewports: all
- The "Berths" label is a boatyard word for a field whose answer is "4 people". Change the label to the word the value already uses ("People"), or make the value say berths. Viewports: all

**What's working.**
- The intro copy says what is different, and it says it in terms the visitor already knows: "book it here with the boatyard that keeps it. No agency in the middle, and no fee on top of the hire." That sets Slowwater against the agency booking the reader expects, so they can judge the value for themselves. Keep it, and give it more visual weight.
- The route list is specific to this trade: named routes with nights and locks, laid out as a quiet table with hairline rules. It reads like a boatyard talking, not a template. Keep it.
- The palette is small and calm: one deep green on a warm off-white, with a serif wordmark. That suits a slow holiday on the water.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Make the route list a way to start the search.** This builds on finding 4. Finding 4 asks the routes to answer to the base. Go one step more and let the visitor start from a route. Each route already states its length in nights, so picking "The Fenby loop" could fill in "7 nights" on the form. The piece is for a first-time visitor who has to understand the offer and start a search. Many first-timers do not know how many nights they want, but a named loop with a number of locks gives them a trip they can picture. That turns the most specific content on the page into the way in, not a footnote under the hero. Taste. Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*, on natural mapping, supports putting the choice next to the thing it changes.
2. **Let the wordmark's serif carry the headline.** This builds on finding 2. Finding 2 asks for a larger, darker headline. The new commitment is about typeface: the serif in "Slowwater" is the only type on the page with any character, and it appears only in the logo. Set the headline in that serif and keep the sans for the intro, the form and the routes. The page then has two clear voices. One says what the holiday is, and the other handles the booking. Until a photo arrives, that gives the page some of the feel of a slow trip on the water, for a visitor who has to want the trip before they search. Taste. Dair, *Design with Type*, argues for contrast of typeface to separate roles.

**Couldn't judge from a still.**
- Whether "Start date" opens a date picker or takes free text. It shows "Sat 14 June" with no year, so check in a browser that it limits the dates people can pick.
- Focus indicators on the form controls and on the two buttons.
