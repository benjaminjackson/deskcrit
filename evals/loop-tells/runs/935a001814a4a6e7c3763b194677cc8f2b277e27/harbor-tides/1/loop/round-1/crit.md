**First read.** The eye lands on the headline at top left, drops to the dark teal button, then runs into a wide blank area to its right. That area is about 800 px on desktop and 1,400 px on widescreen, so the hero looks like a picture failed to load. Below it, a tiny tide table and three text columns sit low and quiet. They read as small print, not as the thing being sold. The page feels calm and plainly written, but unfinished. On the phone the page is 676 px wide in a 390 px viewport, so the three feature columns are off the screen to the right and the visitor sees only the hero and the table.

**What it's for.** A landing page that gets harbor masters to set up Tidewell. Berth holders are the people who will later read what Tidewell posts.

**Verdict.** The page doesn't sell yet. The copy is specific and good, but the one thing that proves the product, the page of tides Tidewell actually posts, is set as 12 px small print at the bottom left, while the hero spot where it belongs is empty. On the phone the layout breaks and scrolls sideways.

**What would move it most**
1. **The empty right half of the hero, and the tide table hidden low on the page.** The hero text is 400 px wide (`h1` and `div.hero-text > p` boxes, x 64 to 464) and nothing sits to its right. The eye goes from the button into nothing, and the left-heavy page looks about to tip (Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*, "Balance"). The empty space doesn't read as a choice. It reads as a missing picture, which is the stock "empty hero visual" move (unslop: the hero visual should be a product preview or an image from the product's world). Meanwhile the strongest asset on the page, "Today at Porthmellin" with its four tides, is the smallest text there: 12 px cells, a 13 px heading. If a picture is meant to go here and did not load, check that first. If not, the direction is clear: move a large, readable preview of a posted Tidewell page into that space, with tides, gate times, pontoon depth and notices. The headline then promises times you can stand behind, and the picture shows them. Defect. Viewports: desktop, tablet, widescreen
2. **On the phone the page scrolls sideways and the feature columns are off screen.** The page is 676 px wide on a 390 px viewport (`page_width` 676, note "scrolls sideways"). The three point columns start at x 412, so a phone visitor never sees "Lock gate times", "Depth at the pontoon" or "Notices" unless they swipe sideways. Berth holders checking from the quay are exactly the phone audience the copy names. On the tablet the same columns fit, but they are 76 px wide with 6 to 7 characters per line (`characters_per_line`, `div.points > div.point > p`). That makes a one-word-per-line staircase, far below Bringhurst's 40 to 50 for multi-column text. The phone nav also wraps into a 2×2 cluster squeezed beside the logo. Fix: below about tablet width, stack the table and the three points into one column at full width (WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow). Defect. Viewports: phone, tablet
3. **The tide table doesn't say what it is or when it is, and its heading sits below its neighbors.** "Today at Porthmellin" gives no date, so a stranger can't tell whether these are live tides or a sample. A harbor master can't tell that this is what their berth holders would see. Heights like "4.8 m" give no reference level. Harbor masters will ask what the heights are measured from, so the page should name that reference. That fact is for the maker to supply. The heading is also 13 px (`font_size`, `h2`), smaller than the 15 px headings next to it (`h3`), so the section that matters most ranks lowest. Fix: label the table as an example of the page Tidewell posts, add the date and the height reference, and give its heading at least the rank of the point headings. Defect. Viewports: all
4. **No comparison with what harbor offices use now.** "Set up your harbor" is a specific, good ask. But nothing sets Tidewell next to what the office already does, such as a whiteboard by the harbor office, a printed tide table, or answering the phone. So the reader has to take "can stand behind" on trust. Direction: one line or one image that puts Tidewell beside the office's current way of posting times, written by the maker from what they know. Taste. Viewports: all

**Smaller things.**
- The table column headers "Tide / Time / Height" are 3.78:1 against white (`contrast`, `thead > tr > th`), under 4.5:1. Darken them to the body gray (#4d585e). Viewports: all
- Type sizes are 30, 19, 15, 13 and 12 px (`type_sizes`). The 13 and 12 px sizes don't read as different levels, and 19 px is used only by the logo. Merge 13 into 12 or 15. Viewports: all
- The point columns start at x 412 and the hero ends at x 464, so no column line runs down the page. Line the bottom row up with the hero's columns. Viewports: desktop, tablet, widescreen
- The last word "quay." sits alone on the last line of the hero paragraph. Rebalance the line breaks. Viewports: phone

**What's working.**
- The copy is specific to this audience. "Posted before the first boat asks" and "Enter one sounding and Tidewell shows the depth for every hour of the day" say who does what and what the reader gets. Keep that voice when you add text.
- The page uses one restrained teal for the single primary button on a quiet gray type palette. The ask is the only colored thing, and it suits a maritime service without going nautical-kitsch.
- The headline is short, two lines at 30 px with tight leading. It leads clearly, and the eye knows where to start.

**Where it could go further.**
1. **Let one tide time lead.** Builds on finding 1. Once the posted page fills the hero, don't show all four rows of "Today at Porthmellin" at the same weight. Pick one of the times already in the table, such as a high water, and set it large enough to read from arm's length. Leave the other three rows as a quiet list below it. The page is for getting harbor masters to set up Tidewell, and a berth holder on the quay is the person they're trying to serve. That person wants one answer at a glance, not four equal rows, and a preview that shows this proves the product better than any line of copy. Taste. No approved source states this point; it rests on how people use the page.
2. **Make the three point columns into notes on the posted page.** Builds on finding 1. "Lock gate times", "Depth at the pontoon" and "Notices" each describe a part of the page Tidewell posts. Right now they sit in a separate row away from it. A stronger version would place each note next to the part of the preview it explains, so a harbor master sees the feature and the reason for it in one place, instead of matching a column to a picture in their head. That way the page stops reading as "product picture plus feature list" and becomes one explained example of what the office would post. Taste. Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, argues for putting words and the picture they explain together rather than in separate places.
3. **Set the times and heights like a timetable.** The four rows of "00:20 / 1.1 m" are the only hard data on the page, and the headline promises times an office "can stand behind". Give those figures the care that people trust in a printed tide table: figures of equal width so the digits line up down the column, with the units kept the same and quieter than the numbers. The precision then shows in the type itself, and that supports the headline's claim of reliability for the harbor master. Taste. Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, on tabular (equal-width) figures for columns of numbers.

**Couldn't judge from a still.** Whether the blank hero area is a picture that didn't load or space left empty on purpose. Check this in a live browser. Hover and focus states of the nav links and the button.
