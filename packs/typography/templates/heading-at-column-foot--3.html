The old mill by the river

The mill stands where the lane meets the water, a tall brick building with a wheel that has not turned for sixty years. Its windows look out over the weir, and in spring the banks are covered in primroses.

Inside, the great beams still carry the marks of the carpenters who raised them, and the floor slopes gently toward the river side where the grain once came in by barge.

Climbing the stairs

Climbing the stairs

Visitors can climb the narrow stairs to the top floor, where the old hoist hangs from its hook above a hatch. The view over the roofs of the town is worth the climb on a clear day.

Guides tell the story of the miller's family, who lived in the cottage next door for four generations and kept a ledger of every sack they ground.

The ledgers are kept in a glass case on the first floor.

The bread fair

The bread fair

Each autumn the mill hosts a small bread fair, where local bakers sell loaves made with flour milled on the premises. The line starts early and the stalls are usually empty by noon.

Children are welcome to turn the hand quern in the courtyard and grind a handful of wheat to take home in a paper bag.

The mill is open from Wednesday to Sunday.
