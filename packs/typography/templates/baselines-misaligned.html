Harbour Lights festival

The Harbour Lights festival returns to the old market square on the first weekend of September, and this year the organisers have added a lantern walk, a pie contest, and a children's boat race on the mill pond. Stalls open at ten each morning and the brass band plays from noon until the evening procession begins. Volunteers are still needed to hand out programmes, carry chairs, and guard the cake tent against hungry seagulls.

On Saturday night the lantern walk leaves the town hall at dusk and winds slowly down to the river, where hundreds of paper boats are set afloat. Walkers are asked to bring their own lanterns or buy one from the school stall, and every lantern sold helps to pay for new books in the library. The route is flat and well lit, and a gentle pace is kept so that families with prams can join.

The pie contest takes place on Sunday afternoon in the long hall behind the church, with three judges drawn from the bakery, the farm shop, and the allotment society. Entries must be baked at home and brought to the hall by one o'clock. Prizes are given for the best savoury pie, the best sweet pie, and the pie that makes the judges laugh, and the winners are announced from the steps at four.
