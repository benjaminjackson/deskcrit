Growing tulips at home

Planting

Start the bulbs in October, in soil that drains well and gets some sun. Dig each hole about three times as deep as the bulb is tall, and set the bulb with its pointed end facing up.

Space the bulbs a hand's width apart, and cover them with soil. Water once after planting and then leave them alone until the shoots appear.

Mark the spot with a small stake so you do not dig there by mistake.

Spring care

When the green tips break the surface, the hardest part is over. Pull away any weeds that have crept in and give the bed a light layer of compost.

Water only if the weather turns dry for more than a week, and keep the water at the base of the plants.

Cut off the flower heads as they fade, but leave the leaves alone.

After flowering

Let the leaves turn yellow and die back on their own, because they feed the bulb for next year. This takes about six weeks and looks untidy but is worth the patience.

Once the foliage is completely brown, it will pull away with a gentle tug.

Lift and divide crowded clumps every three or four years.
