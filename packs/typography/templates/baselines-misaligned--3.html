A year at Elm Court

Notes from the allotments on the hill

Every garden begins with a patch of ground and a little patience. At Elm Court the plots were dug in March, when the soil was still cold and heavy, and the first seeds went in under thin sheets of fleece to keep off the frost. By May the beds were green with peas and spinach, and the long bean rows climbed their poles beside the shed. Gardeners meet at the water barrels each evening to compare their progress and to trade the extra lettuces that every plot seems to produce at once.

The sweetest corner of the garden belongs to the bees. Four white hives stand behind the willow fence, and on warm afternoons the whole bank hums with their work among the lavender and borage. The honey is collected in late summer and shared out in small jars, one for each plot, with a few left over for the autumn fair. New gardeners are always welcome, and a neighbour will happily show you where the clay is heaviest and where the soil is kind.
