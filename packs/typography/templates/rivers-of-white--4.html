The Salt Road · Chapter 3

By the time the carts reached the river the light was going, and the drivers argued about whether to cross that night or wait for morning. The ford was low after a dry month, but the stones on the far side were slick with weed, and nobody wanted to lose a wheel in the dark. In the end they made camp on the near bank, under the alders, and the boy was sent to fetch water while the men saw to the horses. He walked down to the edge with two buckets and stood for a while watching the current pull at the reeds. On the far bank a light moved between the trees, too low and too steady to be a lantern, and then it was gone. When he came back with the water nobody asked what had kept him, and he did not say.
