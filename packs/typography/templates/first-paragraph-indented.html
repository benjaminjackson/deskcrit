Crossing the river

Early boats

The first ferry of the day leaves the north landing at six, carrying bakers, nurses, and anyone else who has a long shift ahead of them. The river is often misty at that hour and the crossing feels quiet.

By seven the boat is full of commuters with bicycles and coffee, and the crew call out each stop clearly over the noise of the engine.

Late in the morning the pace slows, and the deck fills with shoppers heading for the market on the far bank.

The market quay

The market quay is a broad stone wharf with iron rings set into its edge, where barges once tied up to unload coal and timber. Today it holds a row of stalls selling fruit, cheese, and flowers.

Stallholders arrive before dawn to set out their crates, and the best strawberries are usually gone by ten.

A bench at the end of the quay looks back across the water and is a favourite spot for eating a warm pastry.

Going home

In the evening the boats run less often, and the last crossing leaves just after nine. Passengers who miss it can wait in the shelter or walk the long way round by the stone bridge.

The bridge is lit all night and takes about forty minutes on foot, which is pleasant in summer and less so in winter.

Most regulars know the timetable by heart and never miss the last boat.
