The quiet turbines that could power a harbour town

By Mara Eskin · Field report · 9 min read

Every twelve hours the water in the narrows runs out to sea, and every twelve hours it comes back. For the town of Vesk, that rhythm is now a power plant.

Four turbines sit on the seabed, each the height of a two-storey house. They turn slowly, about as fast as a bicycle wheel, and make almost no sound above the surface.

Engineers chose the narrows because the current there never stops for long, and the seabed is flat rock that holds the anchors well.

The town expects them to cover a third of its winter demand, and the harbour master says the fishing boats have not noticed a thing.
