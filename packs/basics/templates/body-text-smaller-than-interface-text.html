Starting tomatoes from seed on a windowsill

Tomato seeds want warmth more than light for the first week. Sow them a few millimetres deep in small pots of damp compost, cover the pots loosely, and keep them somewhere that stays above twenty degrees, such as the top of a fridge or a shelf near a radiator.

As soon as the first green loops push through the surface, move the pots to the brightest window you have. Turn them a quarter turn each morning, because the seedlings lean towards the glass and grow thin and weak if they stay facing one way.

When each seedling has two true leaves, the ones that look like small tomato leaves rather than the first pair, lift it gently by a leaf and plant it deeper in a larger pot. Tomatoes grow new roots along any buried stem, so a deep planting gives a sturdier plant.

Water from below by standing the pots in a tray for ten minutes, then let the compost dry a little before the next drink.
